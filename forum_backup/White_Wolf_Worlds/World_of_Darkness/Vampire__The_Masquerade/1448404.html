                          Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post
                          It's not even certain that Caine exists as a person, so any claims about his disciplines are just speculation. For all we know, Caine is simply the platonic embodiment of murder, and not even a vampire, merely the creator of vampires.
                          I agree with this take. I think Caine should be a mysterious figure, and while NPCs in the setting can have firm beliefs about the exact nature of Caine, I don't think it means anyone has any real evidence as to what his real status is. It's all speculation based on whatever assumptions that character has (which at this point is likely entirely based on someone else's speculations disguised as concrete evidence which in turn was also based on someone's speculations long past). Followers of the Path of Caine are vampires who follow a semi-religious orthodox line concerning the figure of Caine, and aren't actually trying to learn the truth, just new bits they can force into a pre-conceived notion of Caine.

                          In my chronicles, "Caine" is a placeholder name given for the progenitor of vampires. But that doesn't necessarily mean Caine himself was a vampire, or even the Biblical figure of Cain. He might have been some kind of wizard who discovered a dark secret source of power that was used on or by others (the second generation). Or some sort of strange creature that still wasn't what we would call a vampire now. It's entirely possible that prior to the Deluge, nobody was truly a "vampire". But the antedeluvians who survived were transformed as a result of changes to the world that occurred as a result of the Deluge, and thus the first vampires as we recognize them today. (not to mention what the Deluge might actually have been.)

                          I think all too often STs and game developers take a far too pedestrian approach to the concepts of Caine, the Deluge, and the antedeluvian world. Not only does it lack the grandeur of the apocryphal Book of Enoch, it lacks the creativity, majesty, and dark wonder of similar concepts like Marvel Comics origin of vampires with Varnae and the Darkhold cult in Atlantis, or Mike Mignola's Helboy's mythos with the Black Goddess, Hyperborea, the Ogdru Jahad, and the Cold People.

                          I would never use Caine as an actual character in my chronicles as it would reduce the mystery. But I do have concepts, artifacts, and lore about him and the antedeluvian world which PC Noddist scholars can encounter. Real Noddist scholars in my chronicles (primarily NPCs, as I find few PCs are truly interested in these kind of things that have little practical value) usually find enough information that causes them to greatly challenge the standard Noddist myth known in the books and held by the Sabbat. Some retreat into the certainties of the legend that has been constructed and just become a hack. Those brave enough to carry on in true scholarship quickly realize they are heretics, but keep quiet lest they be destroyed by the Sabbat for their heretical/blasphemous views. Only some of the Gehenna Cults and a few independent scholars in the Camarilla are able to explore their ideas with relative freedom.

                          In short, questions like what kind of Disciplines did Caine have or does have, is not only unimportant, it's probably one of the least interesting questions to ask from a gaming perspective.

                                      Caine has all the Disciplines. All of them.

                                      I justify this by assuming that, as the progenitor of all vampires, he can "read" the blood of his every descendant. Every (Cainite) vampire that exists, ever existed, and will ever exist descends from Caine, and thus by the Principle of Contagion, he can draw upon the potency of their blood and gain access to their powers.

                                      The moment any (Cainite) vampire, anywhere, invents a new Discipline power, Caine either knows about it or will learn about it promptly. He learns about it, shrugs his shoulders, and begins thinking of ways he can iterate upon it in ways no lesser vampire could ever accomplish (both because of insufficiently potent blood and from lack of experience with using Disciplines in general). It's my headcanon that Caine learned the Path of Blood long ago, and by now has enhanced it to the point where he need not bother feeding manually. He simply siphons particles of blood from people's bodies in minute quantities, so small they don't notice the absence, and can do so to entire crowds of people at the same time. That is how Caine feeds. He just walks through a crowd of people, and comes out the other end completely topped off.

                                      It's my contention that Caine not only has all the Disciplines, he has all of them at 10 dots. Caine has Plot Device level powers in every Discipline. This is why any rendering of his statline reads "You Lose".

                                      Magestuck: The Ascension

                                                          I've been trying to come up with an idea for the evolution of Disciplines. Using as many resources as I have read, including many that I no longer own, my best guess is that Caine would have all Disciplines mentioned in the Book of Nod, as well as those Disciplines that developed from them. This would mean Caine could have, for V20 rules, Dementation, Quietus, and Sepentis. He could also have most, if not all, forms of Blood Magic, including Necromancy. He would not have Chimerstry, which I feel was stolen from the Fae; Obtenebration, which is from the Abyss; and Vicissitude, which might come from either the Deep Umbra or the Underworld. He could also have bloodline Disciplines descended from the clan sets, but not those evolved from non-clan Disciplines. He could easily learn most, if not all, of these extra Disciplines, as he is not bound by in-Clan/bloodline costs. He is unbound by generation, so he could master any such new Discipline fairly quickly, as well as develop new individual powers for them.
