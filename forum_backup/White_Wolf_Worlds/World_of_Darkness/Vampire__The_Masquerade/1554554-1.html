                        The thing I'm just thinking of is my experience with great games. Sure, not every game can be like that. But I think it's important to understand what a great game is like. That you take a great game and hope that this is the standard to go by, rather than looking at it like an exotic mutation. For example, the way Disco Elysium opens. That game's captivating within minutes. When you see all these effective/compelling ways of storytelling in games, it's hard to come to a game like Bloodlines 2 and be impressed. It's not that the Fabien/Phyre-stuff is inherently bad. It's just that the portrayal within the game leaves a lot to be desired so far for me.

                        I haven't yet finished the game but I already felt a little bit alarmed when that first Fabien segment happened. Because you delve into Fabien's past and it's interesting because it relates to Phyre's present for some mysterious reason. At the beginning, I was like "Okay, interesting, let's see where this goes." but then Fabien uses a power and you're getting a flashback. So, you're already in a flashback-sequence of what Fabien remembers and within that sequence, Fabien uses a power... to do a flashback. I mean, come on... doing a flashback during a flashback? Honestly, that just reinforces my feeling that this game should've been about Fabien.

                        And Phyre... maybe I will have a different take once I finish the game. But it just feels to me like this is a character who's a victim of bad writing. Bloodlines 2 isn't an RPG. That's obvious. And even if they wanted to, TCR had neither the budget nor the time to turn it into one, apparently. I don't even want to imagine what this game looked like a year ago. And that it looks like this now shows that the developers put in some hard work over the last year for this project. But I wish the two voice-actors for Phyre would give Clan-flavored (depending on what you choose) responses for all (or at least most) of the things Phyre says. But that would cost time and (most importantly) money, of course, to make that happen. Phyre needs to talk a lot more. Often it's more "Fabien talking at Phyre" than "Fabien talking with Phyre".

                                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                For me, I define a roleplaying game as the ability to decide how your character reacts and makes decisions based on their personality and how you define them.

                                You can judge Phyre's backstory, his Clan, and how interactions that determine your ending.

                                It's a Roleplaying Game.
                                Uhm, absolutely not. It has to happen within the game to count as a roleplaying game. Leveling up characters, choices within the story leading to different outcomes. At upper limit, you get something like Baldurs Gate 3 and of course not every RPG has to go that hard when it comes to RPG-elements. But for example, skill-checks to unlock doors, dialogue-options or specific actions within the environment. Or combat-encounters that can be ignored because you can sneak through vents or passages. Or you can neutralize bosses through doing sidequests. Stuff like that. The point of a roleplaying-videogame is to offer a lot of options to deal with challenges. And the more free space the game offers with these options, the better it is at facilitating a roleplaying-experience. And that includes endings and hopefully multiple endings for various NPCs. Now, every game is of course limited by its budget and allowed development-time, so the scope for these things obviously isn't endless and it rarely is something on the level of a Baldurs Gate 3.

                                But with Bloodlines 2, it's easy... It's not an RPG. You have your linear narrative. There's a barebones skill-system that only affects combat. And then there's the dialogue-stuff related to the narrative - which has nothing to do with roleplaying. It's just a couple options and as far as the game is concerned, it's just "Option 1: Average answer Option 2: Bad answer Option 3: Good answer". So the challenge there is your standard dialogue-puzzle of "What is the right thing for Phyre to say in this moment?". That has nothing to do with roleplaying. That there isn't more to it is lamentable.

                                            My own review:
                                            Not that I doubt anyone cares much.

                                            My brother picked the game up and I've been watching him play it and playing a bit over at his place. It's definitely pretty mid. The Fabian sections are extremely bad. There's some potential there, but they keep having you swap to Fabian at really bad times. Further, Fabian has no travel skills so you have to walk from one of the map to the other in order to talk to someone and then walk back again and repeat this process until you finally get to swap back to Phyre. Even on the first playthru these sections are kind of a slog. I can't imagine ever replaying this game without a "Remove Fabian" mod. I think the game would have been much better if they had changed the Fabian sections to more of a visual novel setup, or something because they are not fun.

                                            I also found that the devs want you to play their way, not your way. For example, if there's a building they want you to go in, you have to go in the way they want. Like, let's say it's a warehouse and you don't want to go through the front entrance and fight a million goons. Tough luck, because that's the only entrance. There's no side doors or entrances, and if you climb up on the roof, there's no other entrance up, no rooftop windows or anything, so you have to go back down and walk through the front door.

                                            But wait!

                                            Once you try to walk through the front door, you get a message that instead, you need sneak in through the rooftop. How? There was no door on the rooftop! You just checked! But once you go up there, now suddenly there's a door there. That door wasn't there earlier, it was programmed to only appear after you try and go through the front door. So the game is completely on the rails.

                                            This was pretty disappointing. There's a lot of games that allow you multiple methods for solving problems. Like even in the original Bloodlines, just the house on Santa Monica beach with the thugs had several ways you could get inside, and several ways to complete the mission (kill everyone, sneak inside, blow up the power generator to get everyone to come outside, etc). With Bloodlines 2, there's basically no thinking or coming up with strategies that works for the player. TCR has one way to play the game, and you will play the game that way.

                                            The voice acting is hit or miss. There's some good and some bad. The combat is okay. It's not bad enough to detract from the game, but it's also not fun enough for me to want the combat sections to last longer. I feel like you actually top out on your powers a bit too quickly, and once you do leveling feels kind of pointless.

                                            You only have room on your power tray for a couple of powers, so getting a new power means you have to replace one of your old powers. I wish they had included an option to have multiple power trays so you could quickly swap between three or four different sets of powers. That would make leveling and learning new abilities feel more rewarding. Hopefully someone can make a mod allowing for quickswapping between powersets, I think that would really help make the combat more fun and leveling feel more rewarding.

                                            The lack of a manual save was definitely a choice. I imagine they didn't want to include one to push people to replay the game so that they can see the choices they missed the first time, but the game just doesn't feel worth replaying (especially due to Fabian), so after I've finished I'll just look at different endings on Youtube.

                                            Overall it feels like a solid 6/10 game. It's not particularly bad (other than what a slog the Fabian sections are) but it doesn't feel like it does anything particularly well. As a comparison I felt that Swansong was a 6.5. Bloodlines 2 definitely feels more polished than Swansong, and graphically it's really nice, but at the same time I think the core game underneath Swansong was better, and I'd probably recommend it over Bloodlines 2 to people looking for a VtM game.

                                                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                                For me, I define a roleplaying game as the ability to decide how your character reacts and makes decisions based on their personality and how you define them.
                                                Yeah, that's not how you define RPG as a video-game genre.

                                                Video-Games are interactive media. Interactivity is already part and parcel of how any storytelling in it works. Anything you do in a video-game is by definition the character reacting and making decisions. And it isn't based on their personality, also by definition, because decisions based on the character's personality happen within the fiction, not the interactivity. When it comes to your decision, you do it as you wish, and how you perceive the character is relevant only as far as you want it to.

                                                Giving options that take into consideration earlier choices is nice, as is having this affect the fiction being told, but those aren't genre-specific mechanics in a video-game.

                                                What defines RPG as a video-game genre is the presence of classic TTRPG-adjacent mechanics and their centrality to the game-play. Bloodlines was an RPG because it literally gave you a complex character sheet to fill that would affect a lot of options and results during the game, and you would improve it with XP. There are other possibilities, but the point is that this was central to the game's mechanics, it wasn't just a limited set of powers that didn't really cover most of the action.

                                                Bloodlines 2 is not an RPG. And that's not a problem, by any means (except in the proper sequel discussion). It doesn't make it less a story-driven game. It just defines its genre as a game.​

                                                        Personally I feel like an RPG needs one of two things:

                                                        1) A robust mechanical system with stats, inventory and powers/spell mechanics. Most JRPGs fall into this one.

                                                        2) A deep system of story driven choices that allow the player to choices in how the story will play out. Something like Mass Effect 2/3 or Dragon Age 2+ fall into this one.

                                                        Many classic CRPGs (Baldur's Gate, Bloodlines 1, etc) tend to comprise both of these, having fairly deep mechanical complexity as well as a lot of player driven options when it comes to seeing how the story will unfold.

                                                        I feel like Bloodlines 2 does, mostly, fit into the RPG category, mainly due to the second part (I haven't finished it yet so I'm unsure how much your choices will matter), but there is also some depth with the Disciplines (there would be more if you had the option to equip more Disciplines). It's worth noting that Steam and other distributors classify Bloodlines 2 as an RPG.
