                    Dhampirs and immortality is a bit nebulous; while in-universe characters have associated them with Revenants, there are both physiological and mechanical differences between them.

                    Revenants have extended childhoods due to aging slowly overall, Dhampirs age normally until their vampiric abilities kick in at which point their aging halts (and a point is made that it would suck if this happened in early puberty). Dhampirs are supposed to be young (under 20 at time ToTB came out) so whether this was just greatly retarded aging or its outright stopped is up in the air.

                    Mechanically, Revenants spend blood just as ghouls do... burning it is a physical process (which is why V20 limited the volume/number of points they could hold) while for Dhampirs the “blood points” are entirely metaphysical... expending all of them doesn’t change their blood volume at all (and V20 leaves them access to all 10 points as a result).

                    The result is that, depending on the role you intend Dhampirs to play in your campaign, you could reasonably set their lifespans as anywhere from Revenant to actual immortal.

                    Example 1: Gehenna is imminent, the Last Daughter of Eve is a Dhampir who will become a New Eve as both vampires and baseline humans go extinct. For this a finite lifespan (so that the new line of humans doesn’t rapidly overwhelm the environment since they never die off) makes total sense.

                    Hmmm... Remember how the first humans in the Bible were said to live for hundreds of years, the lifespans slowly descending to normal human lifespans? “All of this has happened before and will happen again.” [Insert “All Along the Watchtower” riff here]

                    Example 2: Gehenna is of the less severe cyclical variety and Dhampirs do not breed true (i.e. their children are just ordinary humans). Here you could go with the “who wants to live forever?” trope with immortality as more a curse than a blessing (you’ll forever look 13-18 years old, all your mortal friends will die, any children you have will die of old age, but you aren’t welcome in the courts of the undead nor can you sleep away the ages so that when you wake everything can be fresh and new).

                    In this case, it might be interesting to reveal the existing of elder Dhampirs. During the Dark Ages the 13th generation was thin-blooded. Perhaps those pathetic medieval thin-bloods produced Dhampirs that are now a thousand years old with the same long views as the Methuselahs, but without having been able to sleep away the ages so they’ve become as depraved as the Revenants.

                    Such fiends might actually make an interesting counterpoint to the low generation elders... that the weakest might overcome even the strongest by being able to marshal forces outside the elder’s usual command... to not be driven to feed and to walk in daylight for a thousand years with even one dot in Dominate and Presence could give them command of mortal institutions well beyond a typical elder.

                        What I've done is separate Generation and Blood Potency. Generation determines the strength of Blood Surges and healing while increasing Bane Severity, While Blood Potency, squashed into a 5 point scale, determines your ability to reroll Discipline Rouse Checks and your feeding restrictions. Generation is static without diablerie, BP increases with age but in a nonlinear fashion (BP0->1 takes about a year, BP2 fifty years, BP3 a hundred, BP4 two hundred, BP5 four hundred).

                        I find Generation too useful to throw out, but really don't like V5's 'generation dependent maximum and minimum BP' to be clunky and not what I want, so I ended up splitting the job of BP.
