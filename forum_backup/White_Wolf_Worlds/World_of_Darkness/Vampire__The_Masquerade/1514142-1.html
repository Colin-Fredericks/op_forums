        Yeah, the broader video-game audience has gotten wise to a lot of PR strategies to the point where these things are negatives now.

        Like, if you ask anyone who's favorite video game's developer was bought by Microsoft? They can warn you that the next game in their favorite franchise is now going to get released in a crappy state, and take a decade before the game is where it should have been at launch and recover fans for multiplayer to be fun if that's part of the game. Microsoft just has some really hated mandates it puts on the studios it buys because it actively makes game dev worse to help Microsoft's bottom line in the short term.

        If we look at the video games Paradox has started shopping out to other studios as they've grown out of just publishing their own work, there's some justifiable worry that part of the problem is Paradox's demands which can't be fixed by changing which studio is handling it. Some mandates from the owners that can sound very small, can turn any smaller studio's development process sour.

                        Originally posted by Gurkhal View Post
                        I may draw some flak here but while I liked Mitsoda's work in the original game, he wasn't necessary going to produce the same level this time.

                        Times and people change. The fact that the Star Wars prequals made by George Lucas himself were not very good as compared to the original episodes IV-VI tells me that people that produced good stuff 10-15 years ago may no necessarily produce the same good stuff now.

                        EDITED: And the screenshots looked pretty ok from a WoD mood kind of way.
                        Maybe Mitsoda's work was good, or not. But the problem is, they really hyped his being part of the team in the original advertising and this was a huge "buy in" for people who were deeply skeptical about some new studio making a sequel to a major cult classic that came out like 20 years ago. There were a number of people who would have panned the game, but were willing to give it a chance because Mitsoda was on it, and the company was saying that they had hired him specifically because they wanted to recreate and capture the feel of the original game. Then Mitsoda was unceremoniously booted to the curb in a way that made anyone who got on board because of Mitsoda, understandably upset.

                        Also, I don't know if Star Wars is the best example. Episodes 1-3, while they had their problems, they had a coherent storyline and were much better than Episodes 7-9. At least my takeaway from Star Wars 1-9 is that letting the original creator produce stuff, even if it's not on the same level as the original, can still end up with something better than letting new people who aren't as invested in the material mangle it and toss out garbage.
