        There seems to be a consensus that identification of a Caitiff (meaning a mechanical clanless with no clan weakness or clan disciplines, not a social outcast of a clan that shares the clan weakness and disciplines) is fairly hard outside of some obvious anomalies. If the only true way to identify a Caitiff is through a Tremere only ritual, that's not very good. The Tremere are a relatively small clan that has only been around for a thousand years for creatures that are probably at least eight thousand plus years.

        I still think some combination of observed "clan weakness" and the first Disciplines the Kindred demonstrates could be a sufficient tell tale sign. However, it doesn't seem most people think that's a strong sign.

        To me, that seems like a problem provided how the setting treats the Caitiff (as opposed to the mechanics). The setting seems to imply that Caitiff impersonating a clan is very rare. The setting seems to almost always have mechanical clanless Caitiff be recognized as Caitiff. Furthermore, the perceived "menace" of the Caitiff is something mentioned as far back as the Book of Nod, and destroying them is one of the primary (but not the only) function of the Scourge.

        I don't think this discrepancy is intentional. It's probably just the writers not thinking things through and simply defaulting that any mechanical Caitiff must be known to be Caitiff.

        If we are to prioritize the portrayal of the setting above the game mechanics, it seems to me there must be a stronger way to identify the clanless than what we're told. The alternative is to obey the mechanics and abandon the idea that most Caitiff are known as Caitiff, which means abandoning the concept almost entirely IMO.

        I wonder if a possible solution is to utilize some form of the Blood Ties/Sympathies or Predator's Taint rules in Vampire: the Requiem. Allow some vampires (perhaps based either on proximity of blood, old age, potent generation, or other marker ) to sense that the Kindred is, in fact, a true Caitiff. Or perhaps mark them as having an aura markedly different from "true born" Kindred so anyone with sufficient Auspex and training can know. It doesn't need to be a thing everyone knows how to do. If it is limited to real elders (in however capacity) word will quickly spread, at least in Camarilla society. Although I would want it to be fairly easy for Sabbat to identify as well given the internal dynamics of the Panders within that sect.

            Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
            I still think some combination of observed "clan weakness" and the first Disciplines the Kindred demonstrates could be a sufficient tell tale sign. However, it doesn't seem most people think that's a strong sign.
            I think that's kind of a misinterpretation. At least some of the time, we were focusing on the idea of a Caitiff passing as a clan vampire in another Domain, or other fringe situations. Also, it's a matter of frequency, time and how deep the charade goes. We were also unsure about the goal, if it was to build upon misidentification being common or rare.

            Observed Clan Weaknesses and first Disciplines aren't guaranteed tell tales, because a lot of special situations can apply, but they're definitely sufficient, because most of the time you don't need exceptional accuracy. Specially given that most Caitiff have glaring gaps on their understanding of "proper" Kindred society.

            A regular kindred, even one without a Sire, will be observed by others in their city way before they know about the very concept of a clan or a Caitiff, so they can't really play others here. Those observers have the benefit of time, so Weaknesses and Disciplines can definitely be noticed and contextualized. Yes, there's a lot of ways how a Weakness or Discipline may give either a false positive or negative, but most of the time it won't.

            Flaws with similar effects to Clan Weaknesses exist, first Disciplines may be hard to identify, Caitiff developing a clan's spread happens, but the frequency of all those things is on the ST. The books don't really assume they happening with frequency. In fact, characters identified as having those flaws are exceedingly rare.

            You also define how much cities share news, depending on their distance and possible relationships between their vampires. The fact that a vampire arriving in a different city won't necessarily be investigated doesn't mean they won't ever be investigated. Each Prince is also different on their paranoia, so there definitely are some that won't rest until they know who exactly is a new arrival. And that given that a Caitiff manages to travel in the first place, as this means not only risking the usual dangers other vampires face, it also means the risk of being caught by other vampires away from protected areas, and simply not knowing for real what the dangers out there are (as they hardly have access to minimally reliable information).

            Caitiff also face the problem with how persecuted they already are. Being caught trying to impersonate a clan-vampire is a very efficient way to piss off everyone and give them an excuse to kill you in the process. So, on top of amassing all the conditions necessary to pull this off: fake a Weakness, have at least 3 dots in "in-clans" and preferably more, know the ins and outs of Kindred in general and the clan in particular, get to a far away place where no one will reveal you, possibly have to make an ally that vouches for you, on top of all that a Caitiff needs the courage to go ahead with this crazy plan.

            To make things even worse, any Caitiff caught doing this will put everyone on high alert. It will cause a ruckus in the Domain, and possibly in any Domain that gets wind of what transpired. This will, among other things, make things even harder for other Caitiff to try it.

            What comes to mind on the setting is that, while it is consistently stated that Caitiff always happened, old or low-gen Caitiff are really rare among canon characters. It's up to the ST if this means that old Caitiff eventually impersonate a clan, or if they eventually become clan-vampires somehow, or if they simply die too soon, or even if they just remain unremarkable, but are still there in the expected numbers.

            Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
            The setting seems to imply that Caitiff impersonating a clan is very rare.
            Sometimes it does, sometimes it doesn't, although it really leans more on the former especially as editions go.

            But how much ease or hardship do you need for this rarity? And how much you need it to be viable, even if rare?

            A more straightforward method for identifying Caitiff may make it actually unfeasible, even for a prepared person.

            Instead of frequency, you should think about how hard you want it to be for a character to actually pull it off, including the need for specialized supernatural powers, so you don't end up with a character facing more trouble than worth for the story.

Without a well-established patron that can cover for them, a typical Caitiff would still have a very, very hard time passing for a clan vampire.

            And while it is possible, one thinking to be a clan-vampire while actually being a Caitiff would be exceedingly rare, maybe not something you see every century.

                    Now I have a firmer grasp on what you want, so I'll keep it brief:

                    I personally think the current mechanics are enough. There's room for a Caitiff to masquerade as clan, but overall the actual circumstances make it hard, and most of them are indeed publicly identified. The fact that impersonating is possible remains, but only goes so far.

                    That said, a feeling of unease upon meeting them isn't a bad idea if you're thoroughly disinterested on working a impersonation plot. It may even reinforce the themes of dread and apocalyptic signs associated with them.

                    What I would suggest them is to start focusing on the frequency of weird Disciplines. While from an OoC perspective the player buys Disciplines as they wish, IC Caitiff have little say on what they develop without a teacher, especially the first dots. Those Disciplines don't necessarily have to be possible for others to learn, so on making unique ones for NPCs you don't need to worry too much about balance, most of them would be somewhat weaker, but benefit from the surprise factor, while some may be strong. Outside that, Disciplines unheard of outside specific Bloodlines and Clans may also be common for Caitiff to spontaneously develop.

                    Alone that would help already with both identifying them and making them scary without being necessarily stronger. Other than that I would lean on the idea of dread and unease. Instead of active, the perception of a Caitiff being around could be diffuse, but disturbing. It could come when Discipline powers are used, as minute changes in them that don't change mechanics or pinpoint the clanless, but that definitely worries the user about the (further) corruption of their Blood. I particularly picture Auspex sensing something in the area as an impossible sensation whose origin can't be determined.

                    My suggestion on making it diffuse isn't to ease impersonation, though, but to play on the theme of unease. In practice, with Caitiff being generally removed from "proper" company, the mere presence of such tell tales would be enough to weed out them soon most of the time, as those symptoms would be considerably rare in a vampire's nightly activities or Elysium. It would also raise tensions when a Caitiff is brought to Elysium and make perfectly sense.

                            Speaking as an ST, here's my advice:

                            You can impersonate a Clan as a Caitiff but I view this as a "high risk, high reward" scenario in terms of Caitiff unlife.

                            1. Most Caitiff don't get the chance because they don't know the system well enough to pull it off so they can't called a Caitiff before they identify themselves. After all, they usually can't identify their sires and if they can, their sire is a Caitiff or someone who denies them. When they can identify their sire, the mystical lack of a link is usually obvious (i.e. your sire is a Nosferatu and you don't look like a monster)

                            2. A Caitiff who is impersonating a member of a Clan has to probably make up a plausible lineage or have someone willing to lie on their behalf. "Hi, I'm Dave Johnson. I'm a 13th level Toreador and my sire is Lily Rose. She's from the Anarch Free States. She's dead now and I want to join the Camarilla. No, you wouldn't know her."

                            3. It's easiest to impersonate Brujah, Toreador, Ventrue, Ravnos, Ministry, Banu Haqim, or Malkavians. All of them have ways of figuring it out, though. The Malkavians may actually just instantly know eventually. Gangrel might kill you outright if you don't get the animal parts needed but you will be permanently the enemy of a clan in a city if you are found to be an imposter and killing you is probably something most Princes would overlook (if they don't call a Blood Hunt on their clan's behalf)

                            4. God help you if the clan notices you're a little too sedate, you feed off of the wrong vessel, or you pass by an Andy Warhol original without the appropriate reaction. You also probably might have suspicions about your Disciplines. No one is going to accuse you of being a Caitiff on a whim unless they're an Elder but if it's an Elder, they can call in a Tremere to check you or some other test that will almost certainly fuck you up.

                            5. On the other hand, if you do know enough Kindred lore, you can probably pull it off. The higher generations are enough that a plausible story about outside of town will cover most bases. Even if it is conveniently unverifiable. "The Prince had me Embraced to fight something called the Sabbat. I was let live because I survived or my sire died fighting in the war for New York." The thing is to learn that kind of lore, you probably have to have been a Kindred for a while and people will recognize you.

                            6. Being a Caitiff is social suicide in the Camarilla but not USUALLY suicide-suicide. While the usual scapegoats, the Anarchs treat them more or less fine and the Camarilla doesn't go out of their way to destroy them unless they need a scapegoat and they do that to Neonates, Thin Bloods, Anarchs, and each other anyway. Still, some will feel the desire to do it anyway because they can't deal with being an outcast.

                            In my Chicago games, Gordon Keaton was actually "adopted" by the Tremere as a Lost member of their Clan by Nicolai's order and taught some basic thaumaturgy despite a Brujah sire and being a Caitiff--it was because Nicolai absolutely intended to use him as a disposable tool but he let him play Tremere for a time. After all, Gordon would do ANYTHING to avoid being exposed.

We should see canon characters where this confusion comes into play, but I don't remember ever seeing that.

                                If Lodin sires a Caitiff, how will he know it's a Caitiff? Some Ventrue have exceedingly generous feeding restrictions. If they happen to develop their first Discipline as one of the classic Ventrue Disciplines instead of one of the others, that should be all they need. Yet we don't seem to have any canon characters who are "born" Caitiff accepted as a clan member. The setting seems to just assume any Caitiff is discovered very quickly. Are there any actual canon characters who are Caitiff successfully impersonating a clan, or don't know they are really Caitiff?
