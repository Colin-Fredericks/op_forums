            I've been enjoying some of the ideas thrown out in this thread, figure I'll throw a few chips in.

            I think it is fair to acknowledge that some of the actions taken by the Second Inquisition strain disbelief; Vienna and London are pretty huge events, and I think assuming that those actions are successful because of other, more powerful, forces acting behind the scenes is reasonable. Or at least those more powerful forces were not intervening to prevent it. But I do find it frustrating how often discussions of the SI tend to frame the matter in very 'white room' terms of Kindred vs. Second Inquisition, like each side is collectively committed to an all-out total war. The "actual" situation is likely to be far messier than that, on both sides.

            To start, if it was "Total War" like I discussed above, the Kindred would win. If every Methuselah united under one banner to crush the kine, that would be it. Unless you take 6+ Disciplines out of the equation, or their equivalents. That is very much not going to be the situation of the struggle against the SI, however, because that same ridiculous power that would allow Methuselahs to crush the SI makes it just as easy for them to dismiss it. For them, it is just a passing phase to be ignored, if not taken advantage of. There simply aren't enough True Faith paladins out there for the SI to win a total war against the most powerful ranks of the Kindred, and the Methuselahs will almost always find a way to endure and hide in the shadows. But that means that those Methuselahs likely aren't going to be raising their god-like powers against this new "threat," especially not within the first few decades, a relatively short time for millennia-old vampires. Vienna and London are the only things that could alter that perception, but one of those has already been revealed to have been reasonably managed by an Ancient. They have bigger fish to fry, deadlier threats to concern themselves with.

            Yes, all of that kind of ignored the Beckoning. I am wont to do that, it's one thing from V5 I really haven't managed to warm up to even a bit, albeit from my second-hand mostly-interested-in-the-plot-points perspective.

            But as soon as this becomes a struggle of Elders and Ancilla versus Hunters, the playing field has been leveled significantly. The most powerful Elders may have a small handful of 6+ Disciplines, but they are no longer blood-sucking demigods that laugh at the plans of mice and men. And remember, most Kindred are not paying significant attention to what is happening beyond their particular Domain, which tends to focus around a single metropolitan area. How much do the individual Princes of cities know about the "Second Inquisition," especially in the early years? How much does the average Kindred know? Factor in the fact that the Kindred are a bunch of backstabbing bloodsucking infighting monsters that can supernaturally go off the handle under bad conditions, and how quickly do you reasonably expect them to organize any kind of collective action, especially when, again, the puppet-masters among the Kindred that tend to look at the bigger picture are a) not feeling threatened, and b) potentially looking to take advantage of the situation? The SI can be an existential threat to 90% of the vampire population precisely because the 10% that could do something about it are also the least motivated to do so.

            To begin looking at things from the other side, the main advantage the SI has is organization and common cause. Most Kindred are not soldiers, they are not dedicated to a cause and trained to work as a unit to achieve that cause. For the most part, vampire society isn't organizing to hunt the SI, but the SI is organizing to hunt them. Again, the vast majority of Kindred society is largely entangled in local affairs, while the SI has members who are used to picking up and going where they need to. Maybe 2021 or so would be the year when the Kindred would finally say "Ok, this has gone on long enough, time for some radical F-ing friendship and teamwork, folks," but I don't find it especially unbelievable that they might need a decade or two to get their shit together.

            Some of the discussion above has gone back and forth on whether or not a loose network of alphabet-agencies, inquisitors, and less official groups, could maintain the communication networks necessary for the actions attributed to the SI. But really, to question that is to question well-established premises of the setting that are included in the SI. The Society of Leopold is probably the best example, but groups like the Arcanum and Special Affairs Department may also qualify. Specifically, they are groups which have managed to maintain functioning communication networks throughout extended conflict with supernatural opposition, without being hopelessly compromised. Consider those groups the informational core of the SI. Not only would they be able to provide information that less-experienced participating groups may lack, but also the knowledge of how to effectively maintain communications and limit subversion. Are people going to be compromised? Of course. But again, at least some of these organizations have been accepted as operating in the World of Darkness for an extended period of time, and being able to endure. And from there, the rest is just slow, steady, and likely very painful, progress. And it's not likely that all information is being shared equally. But all it takes is enough information being share effectively, and those groups can become surprisingly threatening to the 90+% of Kindred that aren't millennia-old blood-gods.

            But to briefly address the original question at the start of the thread, yes and then some. No matter what, revealing the truth of the existence of the Kindred promises disruption of all sorts. While cynicism is very much at home in the World of Darkness, a more optimistic interpretation of why they avoid the disruption that would come with revealing the existence of vampires is that they hope they can end the problem without that kind of chaos ever being necessary, which makes all the more sense with the relatively solid, but by no means perfect, information that the SI has generally been depicted as having access to.

            P.S. I should admit, I do like to imagine at least a pro-Pogrom faction of the Technocracy as being a covert backer of the SI, at kind of the opposite end of the spectrum from the SoL.

                            The Second Inquisition and Supernatural Infiltration

                            A couple of fun-factoids:

                            1. If you read Project: Twilight then you'd note the SAD *IS* hopelessly compromised or was by 2001. The irony was that it was hopelessly compromised in a way that meant it kept trudging along instead of being destroyed. Its regional directors included:

                            * The second in command working for the New World Order (albeit she didn't realize it)
                            * Its third in command working for Pentex (dark money) and waiting for his cushy job post-retirement.
                            * Its Regional Directors including a goddamn Silver Fang Kinfolk who was the daughter of a previous director.

                            The Camarilla wasn't able to get a handle on the SAD because a bunch of other supernatural factions were already there and were cancelling each other out to let the SAD basically do its own thing.

                            This isn't the way I'd do it for the SI but it does kind of show the humor value of the fact that real life intelligence services have done the same. J. Edgar Hoover actually saved the communist party from bankruptcy in the USA by pumping in so much money to watch it.

                            2. I really liked the fact that the CAMARILLA book mentions that the reason the SI got off the ground with the NSA was because one specific vampire Elder (maybe a Justicar or Archon) was handling its manipulation of the organization through a woman named Felicity Price.

                            At one point, Price's Dominate just vanished and they had about 20 years of being a vampire stooge come back to them. Presumably because said Elder was killed or Beckoned. There wasn't an Elder there to immediately take over her handling because her master wasn't going to show their holdlings to potential rivals. The secrecy of the Camarilla undercutting it.

                            This is just one of those kind of random things that can happen in the Jyhad. The Camarilla had a lot of things under wraps but if you remove key players then the Jenga tower collapses.
