    Why is the VTM version of the sunlight weakness so harsh?

    05-25-2026, 03:18 AM
    In most vampire fiction, only direct sunlight burns vampires. The Near Dark vampires sleep in indirect sunlight, and only burn when direct sunlight hits them. The Fright Night fight normally in indirect sunlight, and only burn when hit by direct sunlight. The Lost Boys sleep in a cave that has indirect sunlight, and only burn when one reaches his hand out into direct sunlight. Langella Dracula fights in a ship's hold with plenty of indirect sunlight, and only burns when dragged out into direct sunlight. Numerous Christopher Lee Draculas fight in indirect sunlight and only burn when Van Helsing manages to expose him to direct sunlight. Angel often works during the day simply by avoiding direct sunlight.

    They also generally heal quickly when they get out of the sunlight fast enough. There are plenty of Vampire Diaries episodes where a vampire gets badly burned by direct exposure to sunlight, they escape before dying, and the damage heals in seconds. Abigail loses a hand to direct sunlight, and then almost immediately grows it back.

    Why does VTM go with this incredibly harsh interpretation of the weakness where even indirect sunlight is incredibly deadly?

                  I think it is just the game trying to achieve a "balance" in terms of benefits and drawbacks.

                  While the average new PC is often frustrated by not being able to simulate all the powers of the cinematic vampire, once you have enough XP, VtM vampires often are far more powerful than any cinematic vampire. In horror movies, ordinary humans can often defeat vampires who are awake and active in that pre-sundown time. In the VtM setting, human hunters will often quickly lose unless the vampire is immobile. Or bursting in flames because of any sunlight.

                  Any handling of that trope in any medium of fiction will be determined by one thing only: the needs of the story/plot and wishes of the author in creating their world. There's also artistic mediums that need to be considered. Movies and television shows need a certain amount of light so scenes can be captured on film and the audience can see them. So having actors move around while there is still some daylight left is absolutely necessary when in places where there would not be ambient artificial light.

                  Personally when I used Mortis as its own (heavily rewritten) Discipline instead of converting it into Necromancy, I used that power as a way for vampires to be active in the daytime albeit at the cost of losing most of their supernatural powers. At the lowest level it would make their sleeping body be a normal corpse that would not explode in flame, and at higher levels would allow the vampire to be active around sundown and sunrise while daylight was still present albeit at the loss of most powers, and finally to the level of Dracula where the vampire could be completely active and unharmed in daytime although without any use of powers. It was a way to include these kinds of cinematic and literary scenes into the game.
