        about the Tzmisce: as someone who's spent a good deal of time in eastern europe (admitedly the north) with a terrifying wife from eastern europe... I think Tzmisce as a eastern european stereotype is fun; but i dont think those stereotypes are really something we should overly attach to a specific clan. maybe we've got in our heads too much that Ventrue are 'The Romans' and thus need something very different for those alien slavs and others (nevermind the history of 'romania').

        sure, they've got the whole earth jar thing going on, they're bound to a land, but most of them are bound to the soil they were embraced in. It's a bit unhealthy that people want to see a vampire clan embody a regional stereotype instead of a class/lifestyle/profession archtype that fits in across cultures like literally every other clan/bloodline except the ravnos and noiad. The Ventrue can be the conservative elite of any culture, yet the Tzmisce should dump vicissitude so they can focus on being the conservative elite of eastern european mountain ranges specifically.

        Yeah. I don't think very highly of such an idea. There's so much more you can do with Tzmisce as a clan of surgeons and lords of the land as opposed to Tzmisce as a special regional ventrue with accents a monolingual would find funny.

            Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
            But the Cam hate the Tzmisce... Vicissitude is aliens don't you know?
            If they wanted to avoid sect locks then the Hecata....

            I think the sect locks are what drive interesting characters. The rare Lasombra Antitribu who must do their best to work within a sect that resists them whilst both being hungry for power and needing to keep a low profile in case the rest of the clan decide they want you eaten. The secret Tzmisce of the Camarilla who's clan is vilified and her practices are shunned. The lone necromancer who wants to work in another sect but doesn't really have other clan members present to act as his union.

            Vampire is set up for fear and loathing, fear of the other, enemies are everywhere, don't trust what you don't understand, It's you against your brother, you and your brother against your cousin, everyone prior against god and the world. The world of darkness is one made up of barriers. The more connected people are, the more access they have to information, the less darkness there is. No sect barriers would provide us with an illuminated age for vampires; The setting just wouldn't work with such generosity.

            Because you can still play a Hecata in the Camarilla or Anarchs, they are just largely independent. I mean, FFS, there's a TB Primogen in what was written for Coteries of NY. But there's a distinct difference between 'this Independent group that could have membership' and 'the thing that was written to be so so SO rare as to be non-existent outside the Sabbat'.

            Giving a reason and backstory for why there are Tzimisce outside the Sabbat in larger numbers, makes it easier to support playing them in Cam or Anarch games without a lot of mental gymnastics that people go through to justify their 'alien head crest and living suit, but Humane, really gais I fit the Cam!' characters.
