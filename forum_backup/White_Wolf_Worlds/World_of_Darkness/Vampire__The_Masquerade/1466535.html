    What Goals do the Camarilla Have (beyond survival/Masquerade)

    11-03-2021, 07:48 PM
    Survival is an Imperative, something that you must do (unless you are standing in field just before dawn, regretting your undead existence). The Masquerade is a function of the Camarilla's Survival Imperative - no small thing. A Goal is something that you aspire to that, if achieved, would make you or the world better and helps to define you in a concrete way. A simplistic position to take would be to say that if the Camarilla as a whole does not have a clearly defined Goal beyond survival, it isn't a valid Sect and then ask for evidence of a collectively understood Goal. That's not the direction I am aiming at in this post.

    VtM, for me, is something I left behind in terms of play, but still have this lingering thirst for that I'm trying to satisfy through informative discussion. New and mostly oblivious to V5, I had my kneejerk reaction to some V5 lore discussions, "Lasombra what?!". I used to play that Clan and it is the attempt to imagine them switching from the Sword of Caine to the Status Quo Sect that leads me to formulate this question. Rather than "Does the Camarilla...", I'm asking, Do they have Goals?

    In V5 lore, are there Camarilla Vampires significant to the setting in power or influence who are attempting to rally others in the sect to undertake some meaningful quest, whether or not there is universal or majority agreement?

    If so, what are those Goals and what progress has been made toward them?

                                          So the first thing to get out of the way is this: Hypocrisy runs rampant amongst vampires, and so when talking about the ideals and goals of any vampire sect, you are going to have to acknowledge that there is, or at least should be, a sharp distinction between what a sect wants its ideals and goals to be, and what that all the same can express itself in practice. No sect is immune to that.

                                          That said, the Camarilla.

                                          Ultimately the Camarilla's goals can be looked at from a few perspectives that were given nuance over the run of VtM (we're talking pre V5 here, because in V5 the Camarilla is frankly basically an incompetent cartoonishly evil joke in a lot of ways, and for people that liked to do Cam games, sigh. But we're getting off topic).

                                          In the main, the entire sect was founded on a belief that Vampires should be far less overt in their manipulation and control of mortals than they generally were in the Dark Ages period. Much of this was certainly from a "because otherwise we'll be frigging killed" issue. However some of it was that the Camarilla is in some ways the outright inheritor of things like the Iconoclastic movement of Antonius the Gaul, which believed that things like vampires setting up blood cults and or ruling other vampires themselves as religious figures and gods was morally wrong/outright horrifying and needed to be suppressed (which is a thing that makes me sigh about the Cam now being full of blood cults in V5, but again, we'll be here all day on that change, so). And so in that sense the Camarilla, while not certainly all that humane, does as far as what ideals and goals could be ascribed to it, like to fancy itself a check against outright monstrosity, and in some ways, sure. That the structure and society it establishes helps suppress the more monstrous aspects of vampires and prevents both them and humans from being done up into blood cults. That a variety of vampires with something like ethics and morals regarded it as the least of all evils to support (but still evil because, you know, vampires), because they believe in a state of chaos, Vampires often become monstrously self indulgent, which brings the beast to the fore, which furthers blood cults, which furthers cities going to war, which furthers vampires being exposed to and getting killed out by hunters, etc. etc. etc., all the things the Camarilla was founded as a reaction against. Which is to say, a lot of the Camarilla's goals and ideology can be looked at as bound up and founded from being reactive.

                                          And so as related, The Camarilla's great big goal is Order with a capital O. It regards all vampires as default Camarilla as its stated position because as Monteparnas noted, it seeks to have a universal vampiric society. The Beast disrupts Order, thereby the Camarilla creates law and an outright ornate social order as a check against the beast, to provide peaceable venues like Elysium where vampires can interact without opting to open warfare as a ready default. Again, the sect was founded in the face of the Dark Ages, with its Long Night and War of Princes, and in many ways the sect was still driven by reacting to the idea that those times could come again if it blinked too hard.

                                          In some ways what the Camarilla was trying to do was construct an elaborate shell game of an entire sociopolitical system against the Beast to keep it distracted. That whole "A beast I am, lest a beast I become" thing, writ large onto an entire system/society.

                                          I know people are going to look at that and go "but the Camarilla's wrong because X, Y, Z" and that is going to miss my point by a mile. The Camarilla, right or wrong, hypocritical or not, self delusional or not, did indeed have some ideals and goals going on beyond just "make it so we can live to the next night." The degree of hypocrisy, delusion, failure or simply being incorrect that underlied those goals is something else completely.

                                          (Also in any case in a larger sense all the sects are basically wrong to strong extents for all that they can get a couple things right sometimes, the whole point of redemption stories is that anyone who buys into the "Beast I am" riddle that vampires like to promote is already failing their own soul)

                                              The leaders of the Cam don't want it too powerful, because nobody wants to lose power in their own domains. Even if Inner Circle member A and B thought his position very stable and that he'd never leave the IC (unlikely, given how easy it'd be to push him out if he went into a very likely torpor) he'd have to concern himself with the other IC members and the veritable amount of powerful assassins that'd come knocking if he ever threatened the rule of princes on a large scale or if he tried to use the Camarilla to tangle with someone powerful. Most IC members probably think they're likely to retire at some point and really wouldn't want to set up a precedent that gets in the way of them ruling a small domain. From a cost/benefit analysis, it's an absolutely stupid idea for the Cam to get heavy handed.

                                              I believe the Cam was founded to deal with modernity. After all it was created at the start of the Renaissance, a time of rapid social change and technological progress. The Anarch revolt happened because too many opportunities appeared at once and neonates adapted fast enough to challenge Elders. The Cam keeps elders safe by providing some semblance of structure, backing power and legitimacy to a prince's rule, it also succeeds by appeasing the neonates a little more in quantifying rules better.


                                              So, onto the main question: OTHER GOALS the Camarilla has.

                                              -It promotes the OG 7 clans at the expense of outsiders. The other clans just aren't so good for the sect's promotion of the Masquerade and the Road of Humanity that champions the Masquerade. Assamites, FoS, Ravnos and Lasombra are inherently destabilizing clans with masquerade raping powers. Tzmisce don't make big waves but are very difficult to control, and Giovanni are.. well.. they know what they did. And then there's Caitiff...

                                              -It cracks down on Vicissitude. The source of a lot of trauma, a tell-tale sign of sabbat infiltrators, possibly a magic space disease, and ultimately a very transgressive discipline that allows one to change the body that was cursed to stay the same.

                                              -It supports the Tremere desire for a monopoly to keep Kindred safe from bad magic.

                                                  Originally posted by HorizonParty2021 View Post
                                                  That's the most convincing description so far. It is still hard to separate a System from Territorial Ambition when the former requires the latter on a global scale. World domination has drawbacks, even if it is only a means to a noble end.
                                                  The key here is to understand what the Camarilla is as an institution.

                                                  Camarilla isn't a country. It isn't a religion. It isn't a territory holder. We like to talk about Camarilla territory, but there's no such a thing.

                                                  As an organization, they have no territory at all.

                                                  What the Camarilla is more like the UN. They are definitely a political body, but have their members' sovereignty as one of their stated assumptions and goals. Their power don't extend over Princes, it derives from them. It invites every Domain to accept a body of rules for their common good and use the tools it offers to solve their issues.

                                                  If a common threat arises, a common response is organized. But they have few to no solders of their own. The members of the Inner Circle are powerful Princes in their own right, and they may use the organization to further their influence, but they don't exert overt control through it.

                                                  Justicars? As the Interpol they are empowered by acceptance. They must follow the rules of each domain, they bow to the Princes, need permission. They are the eyes of the global organization, and a Prince must take care around them to a degree, but they have no official authority over any Prince and not only once a Justicar was sent to headquarters staked by a pissed off Prince.

                                                  So a world "dominated" by the Camarilla isn't a world where this powerful über-government controls everything, but just a world where every Domain accepts those common rules to keep their own personal sovereignty.
