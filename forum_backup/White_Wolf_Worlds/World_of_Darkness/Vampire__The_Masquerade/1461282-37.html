                                    I really like the Cam and Anarchs pre-5 for one simple reason.

                                    The Camarilla is only as defined as it needs to be in order to get the setting to work and the core themes on the table. Why the Masquerade? Camarilla. Why aren't people taking things out of hand? Camarilla. Want an international conspiracy? Camarilla. Want a self contained city that's indicative of the wider vampire world? Camarilla. But it doesn't stop much, only enough to keep the setting together, a safety net. Want a Tyrant with all the power? Camarilla. Want a fragile council to rule? Camarilla. Want feuding bloodlines? Camarilla. Want Dave and his wife Sally? Camarilla.

                                    Not being well defined is a strength. Storytellers pre-5 have the optimum amount of leeway to do whatever they want with their cities whilst the Camarilla functions as just-enough-string for a safety net with a good dose of verisimilitude. It's a big sandbox with good walls. The Anarchs naturally fit into whatever you really want to do with the camarilla, they're intuitive resistance that don't need much defining.

                                    The Sabbat offer a smaller sandbox, but with very colourful sand and a lot of good non-sand props to work with
                                    '
                                    V5 tries too hard in defining the Camarilla. It's an even smaller sandbox with a few colours and a few mediocre non-sand props to work with. you just don't have the freedom and the context around the Cam (the SI) is very restricting.

                                                    Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post

                                                    So how do you remove a superior from a position of power the Sabbat? Which does occur.
                                                    On a Pack level, it occurs via monomacy. A truly awful pack leader is going to face challenge after challenge until he either loses, or the whole pack just gets fed up and quietly offs him. On a larger level, leaders need the support of multiple packs to keep/ascend to power. So the potential leaders court the favor of the most powerful packs in the region, likely resulting in mini-civil-wars until someone is victorious. The very top guys seem to be elected Pope-style by elites, but those electing them need to consider whether that election would spark a larger civil war, so that election must at least keep in mind largescale appeal, meaning it won't be too far away from who would win a human style democratic election.
