    Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post
    Personally I've always got the impression you don't usually rely on your disciplines for control anyway. Especially if you're a masquarade friendly clan like the ventrue. What's wrong the more conventional methods such as corruption, coercion, legitimate ownership and non supernatural violence afterall?

    I mean the fiends had an entire sub species of people who they bred to do stuff for them afterall.
    Indeed, but while I don't agree on MWIS' extreme take on the role of Disciplines, there's still a link between Discipline spread and theme/style of a clan. Most successful Ventrue do not rely on either Dominate or Presence a lot of the time, yet it fits them to send a message and work as a fallback plan for their main strategy when the usual methods fail. Otherwise they would also be better with any other spread, maybe all Physical, because they don't need Dominate and Presence for their shtick. It also help to keep the clan's themes and methods when you have a member that isn't personally that good at their thing (of course, they'll also not be that good with the Discipline rolls, but they'll at least have a clear advantage over mortals).

    Were the Tzimisce regularly competent with personally commanding people, I would at least find it thematic to give them Dominate. Were them expected to be regularly hypnotizing and doing BS to other's minds, Dominate would make a lot of sense. But as lords that mostly live isolated in their havens and most receive their orders through messengers and servants, that you obey not because of their wonderful or fearsome personal presence, but because you know you belong to them even if you never saw them personally, then I don't think Dominate fits in well.

    And again, some will do any or all of those things, they can and should buy Dominate as OoC, the clan probably has reasonably easy access to it.

    Originally posted by Kharnov View Post
    There's an arbitrary snobbishness to declaring Protean "beneath" the Tzimisce without really clarifying what your reasoning is. Is it because the Gangrel have it and they are inferior? Is it because it turns you into animals? You're just making a declaration here that really seems like it needs a solid explanation to make any sense at all.
    I second this. While I'm fine with Vicissitude, Protean also fits them really well and the "beneath" commentary is odd at best.

    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    Frankly, this is just outright wrong. The Tzimisce are actually one of the MOST unified clans since there was a Council of Voivodes that mean that unlike other clans, the Tzimisce actually had a central structure.
    Also second this. While I don't think they're as organized as the Ventrue, and they're certainly not as organized as the Tremere, those are very high bars on themselves. It is just that their organization is predicated in law and tradition instead of personal leadership and hierarchy (they have one, but are mostly left to their own devices instead of constantly answering to superiors).

    I don't think their style of organization meshes well with Dominate, but saying they're not highly organized is simply plain wrong.

    Originally posted by Vilenecromancer View Post
    Don't some believe Gangrel to be a Baali bloodline, or vice versa? This is the best part of VTM, the speculation!
    Some believe each and every single clan to either be a Baali bloodline or vice-versa.

    Originally posted by Amethyst View Post
    Important question about the TzimisceWhat is Kupala’s relationship to the Tzimisce? Friendly? Hostile? Somewhere in between?

    Also, what is Kupala? A Bane? An Earthbound demon? A Titan from Scion (probably not 😊)?
    Somewhere in between. They have some mutual understanding, but at the end of the day the Tzimisce freed Kupala to an extend, but keep it bound tp another, and Kupala knows, so they're in a complicated dance.

    A lot of later material gives more credence to Kupala being an Earthbound, but that's mostly because Demon was the last line before the end of the world, so it is actually in the air if it is a very powerful Earthbound, a very powerful Wyrm Head, a very powerful Dream God, or some weird other option that includes any, all or none of those.

    Originally posted by Kharnov View Post
    The Tzimisce often made heavy use of the Blood Bond, which means that regardless of how terrible they were as rulers, they did often have plenty of servants that would literally lay down their lives to defend them. While they lived in a castle. A random angry mob of villagers with pitchforks and torches is going to have a hard time breaching their defences, and if they don't succeed before sundown, they are in trouble. And assuming they *did* succeed, they'd better be able to rouse every other village in the region to their side, or the Tzimisce and their minions would leave their flayed corpses hanging from the rafters as a warning to the rest. If they were lucky; if they were unlucky, the Tzimisce would leave them in a state that would make them *wish* they were dead, as a warning to the rest.
    Not to say that for most villagers of the time there was no such a thing as an actual vampire lord. There was the lord of the castle, and the fact it was a vampire was both unknown and meaningless. To them there was the Voivode and the next Voivode, all of them equally ruthless and disinterested on the commoners' personal affairs. Why bother? That when the vampire lord was known in any capacity, as most probably ruled from the shadows through their living kin (something people forget the Tzimisce used to do back then similarly to how the Giovanni operate today, Revenants or not).

    It is like Strahd in Ravenloft. People in Barovia call their lord the Devil Strahd, but they don't really think he's a vampire, they think he's a lineage of ruthless but ultimately mortal lords. And to most of them it would change little if it actually was the case, many people with actual claims to Strahd's throne should he been eliminated wouldn't be any less evil, maybe just less competent and ultimately worse for Barovia.

    Originally posted by Sergeant Brother View Post
    Though, how bad are the Tzimisce as rulers really? Would their subjects just live in constant misery? It seems like the honorable traditions of the Old Clan would cause them to be protective of loyal subjects. Of course, disloyal ones would suffer horribly. Kind of a carrot and stick situation.
    Throwing your people in misery in the Middle Ages usually means throwing yourself in misery with them as they become far less productive. Imagine if on top of that you need them healthy for your own dietary needs.

    While they can be terrifying for anyone facing them, the Tzimisce, or any vampire lord for that matter, back then or in the modern nights, probably had the best interests of the people mostly in mind as rulers of their territory, so their territory has the means to become rich, prosperous and powerful. There are many ways you can be a good ruler for the land, and many ways you can be a terrible ruler, but they have little to do with you being a good or evil person and more to do with you being competent or stupidly self-serving (instead of pragmatic self-serving).

                                                        Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post
                                                        The Carpathians is where Tzim himself laired and periodically popped out baby 4th gens. Of course the Carpathians would have a large number of powerful, old Tzims. Just as most of the old Lasombras are from the Mediterranean, where Lasombra himself laired and periodically popped out baby 4rth gens. I've always thought that whatever region an Ante laired in being dominated by that particular clan made perfect sense. The only ones for whom that wouldn't be true would be the Gangrel and Ravnos.
                                                        The Tzimisce have more than that anchoring them to the Carpathians, it is a deeper link than what happens to most clans. It is more strongly the Region X clan than any other regardless of Antes. Even when a geographical location is cited as really important and the birthplace of a clan, they generally grew over it a long time ago.The prime exceptions are Tzimisce, Lasombra, Giovanni and FoS, and even then their dependence on their home turf varies, with Giovanni and FoS depending on it only for their central bases, while Lasombra and Tzimisce are still extremely tied to the power struggles and power bases there.

                                                        What would happen if you took that land from under them on a swift strike? For the FoS and Giovanni you would create some temporary power vacuum until their leaders showed up somewhere else and setup shop. If you do this to the Lasombra, you take down their main seats of power and assets, the clan would be greatly crippled, because its tendrils outside Italy are far weaker than their tendrils elsewhere. If you do this to the Tzimisce as written, especially before Revised, you basically nuke their power and resources, and for a lot of players their raison d'être. It also means for most that there will be no more vampires in the Carpathians even if you only took out the Tzimisce.

                                                        And this is a problem, a sure sign of ethnic coding in the setting. It is best for the clan to be untied from that specific place, for it and for the real people that actually lives there and may not be fond of the clan link to them. It is also completely doable without changing significantly the clan's history with the place, or even with Kupala (that arguably shouldn't be tied to the entire mountain range and at the same time a single clan, though. This is hugely silly).

                                                        MyWifeIsScary is right on one thing, we can't write off one issue just because the affected people are white. Prejudice against Eastern Europe is also a thing, and a strong one in the US. WoD itself is deeply guilty of depicting Europe in general as as backwaters a place as possible, especially Eastern Europe. And the Tzimisce got to be linked to a lot of this, like in the millennia old struggle between the Eastern Coded Castlevania Villains Clan and the Eastern Coded Bond Villains Tribe.
