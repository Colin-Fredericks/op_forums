    Why is VTM so allergic to a character being an inventor/visonary?

    10-20-2023, 10:13 AM
    I believe someone in the past mentioned here that inventor/visonary character builds don't see much use due to the ST's not wanting any change to the setting, and after playing for a while, that feeling seems to be somewhat common. My question is, why? As long as you are not going too crazy and making nuclear weapons or spaceships in the early 1900's, I don't see why it would be that large of an issue. I have also noticed that at times ST's will refrence real life science when talking about how it is unlikely for characters to make certain innovations.

    Which doesn't really make much sense to me. There are characters which are breaking the laws of nature using magic and disciplines, comparatively, what some of my characters were doing was much more tame. Plus, if someone has 5 in tech and computers, why can't they make robots somewhat similar to those found in cyberpunk 2077? From what I remember, having 5 in an ability makes you a vissonary in that field, and in STEM, there's always some advancement.

                  The game doesn't really support that because while you're supposed to want to make changes and improve things, you're not actually supposed to be able to do that. As such, the main driver behind whether or not something can be done isn't so much logic as it is "mood", and so the game and STs tend to take arguments from each side of the spectrum depending on how they need to head the matter off at the pass. The conclusion is that vampires (or at least player characters) make things worse and can't change the world in any meaningful way, especially for the better. The supporting arguments change as needed.

                  "Why can't I create synthetic blood for vamps so they don't have to feed?"
                  "Well, it's scientifically impossible so we're going to cleave to "realism" and rule that you can't do it."
                  "Well, if we're looking at realism, how can vampires do magic with their blood? How can we heal with our blood?"
                  "Hey, it's just a game, not a realism simulator."
                  "OK, well, can I use that magical vampire blood to cure people of terminal diseases? Seems like we could make a pretty good organization of humans willing to donate blood if we can show that we can process that blood into anti-cancer juice."
                  "Uhh...*checking notes*"

                  (These kinds of arguments get really fun when you start asking if you can replicate feats known to be possible for vamps in-universe, like making your own Road/Path or developing your own Combo Disciplines)

                  That said, early VtM 1 was somewhat about these things. Vampire Louis Pasteur was killed via political machinations specifically because he was an innovator as you describe - he canonically made a serum first to turn humans to vamps, then one to turn vamps into humans. Before the game began to solidify itself as Blood Weeping Simulator, it was quite willing to explore a multitude of themes. Early WoD was absolutely willing to get gonzo, and it's in that environment that your character might thrive.

                              Originally posted by ShadowcatX View Post
                              Because there are plenty of people in our world who would have 5 in those skills and we don't have those things. Just because you're a visionary doesn't mean you get to have technology 50+ years ahead of what is available to everyone else.
                              I completely agree with this. A character with Computer, Science or Technology 5 will be acknowledged as an industry leader in those fields, but it shouldn't allow the character to have access to any technology that does not already exist. It just means your the equivalent of one the top researchers at Boston Dynamics, HPE, Lockheed Martin, etc. Anything that is actually "super science" is best left to Mage and the Technocracy in terms of the World of Darkness. Certainly anything that can be found in a future sci-fi setting like Cyberpunk 2077, but not in the real world, should not be allowed in a VtM chronicle.

                              Certainly even if the character is capable of conceiving future technology, that does not mean the technical knowledge, industrial base, and manufacturing expertise actually exists to build it. Steve Jobs could drive the creation of the iPhone in 2007. He couldn't do it in 1983 when the best he could was the Apple Lisa. Vannevar Bush conceived the idea of a memex in the 1930s. But the fundamentals of the world wide web were not built until 1980s and did not become commercially available until the 1990s. AT&T had functional videophone technology by 1970, but it wasn't commercially practical for many reasons. We wouldn't see actual video conferencing/communication technology until the 2000s when improvements in digital technology, infrastructure, and production cost made it actually feasible.

                              Now of course many existing cutting edge technologies might give some capabilities that are hard for STs to understand how to incorporate into the game. STs can only handle so much of the unknown, and if a Player is pushing the ST to allow them to get away with something that is too disruptive, I think it's fine for the ST to say "no". No matter how much more the players may know than the STs about a certain field, it is the ST's responsibility to run the game. In the context of the game's setting, the ST is the expert of what can be done, not the player. They can't allow any players to hijack the setting to allow something they want. If that is unsatisfying to the player, then that is something that must be negotiated to find a sweet spot everyone can live with.

                                  The theme of VtM is that of hopelessness, of grim darkness, of the corrosion of the soul due to the environment. At best you are a monster hanging on to its humanity, at worst you are a monster who has decided to find new levels of being a monster. Every problem is on a scale that it is impossible for you to do anything about it, other than perhaps survive long enough to see the next problem arrive.

                                  To be an inventor or a visionary is to inherently look at the situation and say "I can change that". So already on a thematic and emotional level, being a creative character is at odds with the themes of the baseline game. Now there are plenty of creative vampire artist concepts but contrary to some people's beliefs, art by itself doesn't change anything. It may inspire or give emotional solace to another, but a masterwork sculpture or painting won't create jobs, cure a disease, or allow a war to be won.

                                  Now there is also the concept that an ST would have to map out all the changes to the setting due to a character inventing something. Invent the VtM equivalent of True Blood and practically the entire setting needs to be rewritten and every culture on the planet has to be modelled dealing with the vampires coming out of the coffin. Invent a new computer app and you have then model how all of that interacts with the online environment.

                                  This issue of having to rewrite the entire setting due to hypothetical advances is part of the reason why in many Isesaki anime/manga, the main character only "invents" things from our own world rather than creating new things, the writers can just look at our history to see how the technology affected the world. It's a lot of work to please just one character.

                                  It really comes down to the difference between simulation and story. An inventor wants to see their ideas interact with the world, thus a simulation. Alternatively, a character who made a new formulation for a type of makeup doesn't care about how the market share adjusts, they just use that as an explanation for why they have Resources 5 and a degree of Fame & Influence in their Backgrounds.

                                  Of course, this is all going on before the subject of game balance comes up. Even if you don't invent a single thing and only apply real world technologies or concepts to the setting, the game balance implodes. For example, let us say that your character has Resources 5 and a science/inventor flavor. Now your ST has to figure out if the 3D printed titainium iron man suit that is more or less immune to small arms fire can withstand werewolf claws. If it can't, the character concept is pointless, if it works then the entire power dynamic between shifters and vampires has changed because unlike trying to outfit an entire mortal army with these things, outfitting say 100 vampires with inherent fortitude (layered protection) and potence (to move the suit without a power source) is entirely viable.

                                  Hell, the game has yet to really address how the Tremere who have the Path of Technomancy, which makes them hackers second only technological mages, have not ended up running everything since they would literally be able to control anything connected to any form of internet. They even have a ritual that automatically causes all records of a location to be lost, misplaced, or otherwise deleted. This works for an entire year and can be reapplied so that the protection never lapses. Yet somehow V5 decided that Tremere chantries could be located and targeted by missile strike. The missiles use maps and GPS to hit the target as well as when to arm the warhead, the ritual would make the missile not know where the location was, thus it would just fly off or in circles until it ran out of fuel.

                                                          In general I'd steer clear of those ideas in a VtM game, but if someone wanted to explore making spaceships or robots or whatever, I'd be happy to run a Mage game where those ideas would be right at home. In general a hard sci-fi aesthetic simply doesn't really fit VtM and there are other, better games to explore those concepts. You'll have one player making death rays, robots and space ships and the rest of the group playing the night-to-night survival and personal horror that is VtM. The sci-fi player is going to be really breaking the mood of the game for the other players - the campaign could end up feeling a little bit like A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court.

                                                          You risk having that one player twist or warp the game around their character, or at least have their character constantly breaking the mood of the game, and it ends up making it hard for the other players to enjoy the game. And at the end of the day, VtM is a group game and it's important that one player's enjoyment doesn't come at the expense of the enjoyment of everyone else.

                                                          That said, if it was a solo game I wouldn't have an issue with that. Likewise, if someone was playing a Tremere or Tzimsci or such, and was using blood sorcery, homunculai or Viscissitude to create twisted robot-people hybrid monstrosities, then yeah I might be down because that at least hits the horror aspect of VtM. But it still risks seriously pushing the game in a non-traditional direction, so I'd want the other players to be on board with that before allowing that sort of thing to happen.
