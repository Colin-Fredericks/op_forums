    Ok, more stuff for this. I was planning on doing Ventrue next but something came up and I spent more time reading Lasombra material instead, thus the Clan of Shadows was suddenly next in time.

    The Lasombra in the Ancient Nights

    The Clan of Shadows is centered on the great islands, save Crete, of the Mediterranean as well as the city of Carthage, in the lands of the old Philistines and the coast of Illyria. In the old nights the Lasombra were destroyers and raiders, destroying kingdoms to feast on the dying and return home with rich spoils. But no more. Now the Romans have made that unlife hard even while the many civil wars can offer windows of oppertunity for those with their wits about them. Many young Lasombra are integrating themselves to make a place within the empire instead of a raider's unlife.

    The traditional main stronghold of the Clan is on Sicily but there are many old and cunning Lasombra spread out on the islands and dotteted about the coasts to rule their domains even while younger Lasombra are making their ways to new places in both terms of space and society. The Lasombra have spread even beyond the borders of the Empire and the waves of the Mediterranean with rumors of Lasombra who have gone north to the Saxons and Frisians and west to Hibernia.

    The Clan is riven with a great divide between the old ways and finding what should be the clan's new ways in a changing world ruled by a single empire which may or may not endure.

    [Lasombra] - Lasombra was many cruel and evil things but its said that when it wanted something, it did it, and when it didn't want something, it didn't spend time on it. That it drove its Childer as its slaves is also known characteristic. It was never content with things as they stood or with faded laurels from its Childer. Maybe that's the reason as to why [Lasombra] inspires only what loyalty the fear of it can create among its Clan?

    Similar to [Julii], [Lasombra] seems to have been a loner who early travelled west and away from its fellow Ancients and made its place at the islands of Thera and then travelled extensively along coasts and to islands to sire Childer and it would seem search for something which it presumably never found.

    Cult of [Lasombra] - Associated with destruction, darkness and pride. Many find that reflecting on [Lasomra]'s dark nature and deeds to be a way to deal with their undead conditions with a soothing acceptance. They are no longer humans and don't need to fret and shame themselves over what they do now. Merely accept their inner darkness as a power to wield in order to achive things as opposed to be hindered, scared or consumed by it.

    To offer cult honors to [Lasombra] is to say that you accept your darkness and will use it for your goals and ambitions. Getting a sense of direction and acceptance of yourself is what most find this cult to be all about.

    And naturally its widespread for Lasombra to honor their ancient as their great ancestor with cultic honors regardless of how they feel for it on a personal level.

    Factions

    Sea Shades - Raiders going back to pirates and the Sea Peoples. This is the oldest way of the Clan and many of them take pride in that they have few possessions they didn't take by force or cunning as opposed to barter or manufacture. It is also a faction which promotes activities which can get sloppy members into a lot of problem, including Final Death, but often find a suprising acceptance and silent support from Elders of the Clan. As it is now this faction has also successfully spread itself beyond the Roman Empire and into barbarian lands to continue the raider life style.

    Constantinians - . A pro-Christian faction among upper class Kindred with many different sub-factions. Its ranks boost both the faithful and faithless opportunists and has a large number of Lasombra in both categories. Some of the Clan even boast that it was Lasombra in the Egyptian city of Thebes who first practiced the miracelous arts of Theban Sorcery, which has now spread far and wide among the Christian Kindred. ​This faction is growing quickly and sinking its fangs deep into the Christian communities of the empire.

    Caesarians - A faction considered scandalous by many Elders but not without appeal to young and ambitious Lasombra who have integrated themselves in the greater Roman and Kindred society. They believe that things can and must change and that with popular support an elite group of Kindred can force through the necessary change against corrupt opposition.​

    Barcids - A faction centered on the old Punic lands that would seek a reborn Carthage free from Rome. This separatist faction is considered beyond the pale by almost all Roman Kindred and especially the Julii who forces through occasional purges of suspected members in North African cities. Thus there are many members who have been driven into the faction by a hatred for the Julii who represent Rome more than any other Clan.

    Bloodlines

    TBD but probably few or no Bloodlines for Clan Lasombra

        Its been a long time since I engaged with this project but I figure it might be time to do so again.

        I haven put all that much thought into this so consider it a first draft.

        Clan divisions

        The Clans in this setting would be differentiated, from a Roman perspective, by two axis. Roman-Barbarian and Patrician-Plebeian. Now to my understanding the division between patricians and plebeians was not as important in the Roman Empire as it had been in the earlier Roman Republic. But I figure that old ways dies slowly among the Kindred and that it also helps me to make the relative relation between the clans clearer. It should be noted that the more important divison in treatment is between Roman-Barbarian Clans while the Patrician-Plebeian division is not nearly as strict as during the Dark Ages with the High Clans and Low Clans division.

        Roman and Barbarian Clans

        Roman Clans: Julii, Toreador, Ventrue, Malkavians, Nosferatu, Mekhet, Cappadocians, Tremere, Brujah, Setite, Lasombra

        Barbarian Clans: Assamites, Ravnos, Gangrel, Salubri, Tzimisce, Daeva


        Patrician and Plebeian Clans

        Patrician Clans: Julii, Toreador, Ventrue, Brujah, Malkavians

        Plebeian Clans: Cappadocians, Nosferatu, Mekhet, Tremere, Setite, Lasombra

        EDITED: Switched places for Malkavians and Cappadocians

            Originally posted by Gurkhal View Post
            Once more I'm bored and decided to write this up as a means to get ride of some free time. This is for a possible, but unlikely, VtM game in (Late) Antiquity and the Roman Empire. This is a run down of the Clans I would use with some comments on them. As you will notice its a mix between VtM and RfR.

            I hope someone will be somewhat entertained by reading this.

            Clans

            A bit more developed Clans

            Julii - Roman Clan in the West

            Traditores Clan - Dead and gone by Julii hands

            Tremere - Created from a nightmarish pact with the Strix rather than a potion made from Tzimisce. See above for metaplot connection

            Ventrue - Rather than Roman I intend to run them as Greek and Anatolian with historical branches in Sparta, Syracuse, Makedonia, Epirus etc. And they have sunk their collective fangs deep, very deep, into Constantinople.

            Brujah - A very factionalized Clan. Big City Brujah in Rome and Constantinople and other major cities are close to modern Brujah while those active in Classical Ionian Greek areas or in Phoenicia and North Africa are a bit more in the Dark Ages direction.

            Toreador - Ancient basis in Minoan Crete and some ancient Elders were gleefully helpful to aid the Romans in overtaking Greece. Not to many further notes.

            Malkavians - Plans to associate them with the Etruscans

            Nosferatu - Pretty much as in canon with those in Rome taking a page or two from the Nosferatu in Requiem for Rome.

            Mekhet - I always felt weird that only the Setites would be active in Egypt and down the Nile and as such I decided to have two very different Clans coming from this fascinating land

            Gangrel - Pretty much standard but given that to my knowledge the "Barbarians" had advanced in most areas through centuries of interaction with the Roman Empire there would be a fair number of Gangrel who are not pissing on trees and barking at wagons.

            Clans I haven't planned much for yet

            Daeva - Coming in from Persia but haven't decided on many details yet

            Setites - Pretty much as canon so far

            Ravnos - Not many plans and such yet

            Cappadocians - Kind of like canon with a investment in early Christianity

            Salubri - Not as hunted by the Tremere but still not very common or popular

            Tzimisce - A serious number in the Balkan provinces. Relation with the Roman Empire varies wildly between Tzimisce broods and even individual Tzimisce.

            Assamites - Not many plans here.

            Lasombra - Not many plans yet but probably involved with the early Christians

            EDITED: Added part of the Ventrue with Constantinople.
            This is a perfect opportunity to create your own "Now Extinct" unique clan and characters. The world is yours. Go for broke.

                        Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
                        Regarding the Setites, there was, apparently, an ancient Italian goddess named Angitia, associated with snakes, snake-charmers, and witches, as well as healing. Her worship was centered among the Marsian peoples in the Central Apennine mountain region (central Italy), and the 2nd century BC Roman historian Gnaeus Gellius describes her as being the granddaughter of Helios and the sister of Circe and Medea. However, because she was so heavily associated with healing, her cult might also make an interesting angle for Roman Salubri.

                        Meanwhile, in Iberia, you have the Basque god Sugaar, a storm/thunder deity usually pictured as a dragon or serpent. Unfortunately, there doesn't seem to be a lot of surviving information about him.

                        And in Greece, you have a number of possibilities. One is the story of Ophion/Ophioneus ("serpent"), who ruled the world before Chronos came to power. This is the sort of thing that can play into the Setites' belief that they've been unjustly supplanted by other (Aeonic) gods. Likewise that of Python, the original goddess of Delphi, who was slain and supplanted by the sun god Apollo.
                        What's more exciting is that we know almost absolutely nothing about the Etruscans, except what Roman records tell us. We can't even decipher their language. We have no primer, even though Claudius the emperor could speak, read, and write it. All we know is Lucretia was raped by Roman soldiers, and it started a war.

                                The Ventrue in the Ancient Nights

                                The Ventrue have historically have had two main branches, one in Anatolia and one among the Doric speaking peoples of the Balkans and the Mediterranean areas, commonly refered to as East and West Ventrue respectively. In old nights these divisions were strict with lineages of Ventrue following a strict internal hierarchy based on location and tradition as they claimed domain. Veneration of these traditions remain but in the Roman Empire these have lost most political importance even while they serve as a point of pride and a social network for mutual support. Thus while in olden nights you had Hittite, Luwian, Makedonian, Lakedaimonian, Syracusean and so on lineages of Ventrue based on geographic and cultural domains this is no longer the case, even if the lineages continue to operate and the sire-childer relations is given great importance the Ventrue have become alot more cosmopolitean in these nights even while socially conservative and lineages often honor pre-Roman traditions to some degree.

                                Unlike the republican-minded Julii the Ventrue are culturally disposed more towards a monarchical style of rule. Not necessarly by kings but most found the rule of an imperator more to their taste than a more consul, replaced after a year, would be.

                                In the current nights the Ventrue are most often found around the Aegean and Anatolia and they graviate towards high office and civic and military affairs while most often staying clear of priesthoods, augurs and similar positions. Given the cosmopolitan nature of the Roman society the old ways of Embracing from a selective human cultural community is no longer possible but many old or conservative Ventrue prefers humans who are natives to their area of residence. Others Embrace from more individual choices even if these Ventrue fledglings have to work harder in order to gain status in the Clan.

                                [Ventrue] - One of the more known Ancients, [Ventrue] is the subject of many myths and an entire library of wisdom literature which is a key foundation within Clan Ventrue's traditions. According to sympathic myths [Ventrue] was wisest and most noble of the Ancients and elevated its Clan in that image. Less favorable myths speak of it as the Great Tyrant who also shaped the Ventrue in its image. Regardless most tales say that [Ventrue] ruled a great city, which is no more, after having left the company of the other Ancients and little more is said about it after its rule. The exact way in which that rule supposedly came to an end is not agreed upon. Most of the mentioned wisdom literature is said to have come from [Ventrue]'s time as a ruler over its city or when dwelling among the other Ancients.

                                During its time with the other Ancients [Ventrue] was often in the center of events and impacted the rest in one way or another. In stories told by the Ventrue, [Ventrue] was constantly trying to give advice and help others overcome their pettiness and set things right and well for everyone while less flattering tales tell of a [Ventrue] trying to lord it over the rest.

                                Cult of [Ventrue] - Associated with mastery, rule and governmental wisdom the cult of [Ventrue] is popular among the Kindred having or seeking authority and a public office while less so with those who don't like figures of authority. Regardless if a tyrant or hero, or both at the same time, many find that the wisdom literature of [Ventrue] contain alot of insight into how to order the public affairs of the Kindred in working way and as such its popular among those who would do so. Further cementing [Venture] as a object of cult and interest among high society Kindred outside of the Clan Ventrue.

                                Given the Ventrue Clan's love of traditions failure to honor [Venture] as their ancestor can lead to severe sanctions within the ranks of the Clan, regardless of standing. Something which however don't mean much for Ventrue who are not part of the higher echolons of society.

                                Factions

                                Caesarians - A faction considered scandalous by many Elders but not without appeal to young and ambitious Ventrue who have integrated themselves in the greater Roman and Kindred society. They believe that things can and must change and that with popular support an elite group of Kindred can force through the necessary change against corrupt opposition.​

                                Spartiates - A faction which primarily exist in the hinterlands of the Peloponnessos and Anatolia. Here small groups of ,often highely militant, Ventrue lord it over small communites of humans in a very brutal manner and keep the kine under iron yoke of oppression and misery.

                                Medonians - Less of a faction and more of a name for Ventrue who allow power to get to their head and are destroyed or reduced by mistreated and underestimated servants, not seldom ordinary humans.

                                Constantinians – A pro-Christian faction among Julii and upper class Kindred with many different sub-factions. Its ranks boost both the faithful and faithless opportunists.

                                Diocletians – A basic conservative faction that seeks reform of the Kindred community to make it work better than it currently does. Given they often, but not always, find more merits in centralization of power in specific individuals than in institutions they often come into conflict with more republican minded Kindred.​

                                Philosophers of Power - These Ventrue tries to act as advisors and philosophers on matters of power and the state and they marry, or try to, Greek philosophy and ancient wisdom literature, most importantly that associated with [Ventrue] into a system of enlightened rule. They seldom hold office themselves but cultivate influence to make Kindred who do hold offices carry out their responsibility with a more enlightened consideration for what is the public good.

                                TBD but probably few or no Bloodlines for Clan Ventrue

                                    Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
                                    Carthage always seemed like an odd hill for the Brujah to decide to die on. I realize that it's a result of it being 1992, the two Sabbat clans being nothing more than a pair of "blink and you'll miss them" names buried in the text, and someone deciding that the Punic Wars sounds like an explanation for why the punk rebel idealist Brujah have always hated the conformist corporate suit Ventrue, but hindsight only makes it weirder.

                                    As you both say, given how the Lasombra were given ties to the sea, especially the ancient Mediterranean, were so strongly associated with Iberia, Sicily, and Northern Africa (all Carthaginian territories), and the Carthaginians coming from the ancient sea-fairing Phoenicians, they make a much more logical choice of the clan most associated with Carthage. Thus the Punic Wars then become a logical explanation for the long running rivalry between the Lasombra and Ventrue, with various Lasombra holding grudges which carry on through the Roman Empire and into the Dark Ages and beyond. (At that point, you probably have a bunch of things like rivalry between Iberian and German branches of the Hapsburg dynasty/empires, Spanish Catholics verses German and English Protestants, and so forth just adding to it.)

                                    In contrast, given their ancient reputation as intellectuals and philosophers, the fact that two of the big name clan members in Chicago by Night were from Ancient Greece, and how much the Brujah section in Dark Ages's Keepers of the Word focuses on Ancient Greece, one would expect them to be primarily connected to that ancient culture and territory. In that case, the Roman conquest of Greece become a possible bone of contention between members of the two clans. Such a thing could also be compounded by Rome's mythical connections to ancient Troy via the Aeneid, perhaps with certain Roman elders having ties that lost Bronze Age (Hittite?) city state/kingdom, and still holding grudges about it. (This ironically flips the traditional narrative on its head, with the Ventrue being the ones bitterly pining for a lost cause.)
                                    It’s possible that Carthage became the Brujah hill to die on only after the Romans chewed up Ancient Greece, but it’s equally possible that the Carthaginian Brujah were the ones who refused to do business with the Ventrue (Whilst their Hellenistic cousins quietly accommodated the Roman conquest of the Hellenes as the price for getting some peace from the Diadochai and their inveterate quarrelling, only for their pro-Roman, pro-Ventrue policies to bring them down, possibly with the Republic and certainly in the estimation of posterity after some historic disaster).

                                    It’s also possible that while the Brujah were the loudest and proudest residents of Carthage, the Lasombra were the OG Kindred of the city, who quietly decided to up stakes after that connection started to become more of a liability than a blessing (whilst the Brujah made the city their own and made a real mess in the process).

                                    As with any history, Kindred history presumably recalls the broad picture whilst forgetting many of the nitty-gritty details.
