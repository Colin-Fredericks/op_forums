                                Eh, I think you're making a bigger deal out of pronouns than they really are. I mean, a Prince doesn't have much obligation to remember a neonate's name either, but somehow, they often manage (and many modern names did not exist in medieval times either!). Compared to the eccentricities that dealing with many Malkavians requires, remembering one out of three words doesn't seem particularly hard.

                                As for "twitter mobs", remember that Vampire has always been among the first. When GenCon was a massive sausage fest, the vampire booth had female fans clustered around it. When D&D was completely heteronormative, Vampire had gay, lesbian and bisexual NPCs. When dressing in women's clothes was something other RPGs only ever contained for comedic purposes, Vampire had several transgender NPCs in the clan books. The representation was often clumsy by today's standards, but if you're complaining that Vampire goes outside of the bounds of heteronormativity, you may be between 20 and 30 years too late.
                                A video game decoupling the choice of gender from the choice of body shape is really... such a small change that I can't quite fathom how many people are up in arms against it. But it means the world to some people who feel that games see them and their lives now.
