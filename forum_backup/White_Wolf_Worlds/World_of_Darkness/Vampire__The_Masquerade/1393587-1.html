                            My favorites, in descending order:

                            1 - Requiem 2e: I know it's not VtM, but I like the execution of Celerity a lot. For those who don't know it, it has both active and passive benefits that scale with your dots. Passively, add Celerity to Defense, even against firearms. Actively, spend one blood to jump to the top of initiative for one round, multiply your speed by (Celerity+1) for one action, or pre-empt someone else's action; this last one can only be used a number of times per scene equal to your Celerity.

                            2 - V5: I like some of the lower-level powers (like automatically succeeding at balance checks), but I think it went too far in nerfing the mess that was V20. Needing five dots to pre-empt someone else's action is a bit too weak for my tastes.

                            3 - MET: Struck a good balance between "multiple actions dominate everything in combat" and "Celerity is useless without that". Each dot unlocks a new power (just like with Dominate or Auspex or whatever); at five dots you can spend blood to get two extra actions per round, among other benefits, and there's no way to get more actions than that.

                            4 - DAV20: Made some progress toward fixing V20's version, but still feels awkwardly in-between in terms of power level.

                                                    Shameless Plug: My homebrew Celerity Rules. Maybe you, gentle reader, can find something in here that you like and adapt it to your own game, whatever version of the rules you use.

                                                    Note: The mechanics of this won't be immediately apparent -- combat in my game is handled with a contested roll between opponents using their "Combat Values" (basically the same number as their attack or dodge dice pools) and then if someone lands a blow, a follow-up contested roll of the victorious character's Damage Total (DMT -- depends on weapon, skill, strength/potence if applicable) against the losing person's Soak roll to determine if "HIGH" or "LOW" damage is inflicted.

                                                    Celerity Zero: "Combat Celerity"
                                                    (Combat)
                                                    By spending a Blood Point the user of this power can act with superhuman speed for a single combat turn, benefiting from an opponent’s comparative sluggishness. This is the “basic” advantage gained by those with the Celerity discipline, and the following rules apply:

                                                    • In hand-to-hand combat (Brawl or Melee), or when evading, the user gains Advantage on CBT or DMT if he or she has one more level of active Celerity than the relevant opponent.
                                                    • If the power user has 2 or more levels than the opponent, he or she gains Advantage on both CBT and DMT (if attacking).
                                                    • Combat Celerity does not affect ranged combat unless the power user is evading a ranged attack, in which the same rules apply (i.e., Advantage on the EVADE CBT only).
                                                    • In multi-person combat, the power user may split his or her active levels of Celerity between opponents. Once that allocation has been made, compare the numbers allocated with each opponent’s respective Celerity (if any) then treat as above.

                                                    Note that unless specified below, separate Blood Points must be spent to activate Combat Celerity and another Celerity power in the same round.

                                                    ● Flash
                                                    (Combat)
                                                    The vampire with this power reacts with superhumanly fast reflexes, and may spend a Blood Point to react to any action or movement that they can reasonably sense. This power does not, however, allow a reaction if the power user is caught completely unawares. In terms of mechanics, the power user may spend a blood to negate a Surprise Attack or turn an Ambush Attack into a Surprise Attack. The power user is still helpless when Blindsided.

                                                    As a fringe benefit, use of this power also allows the power user to aim a firearm (and receive aiming bonuses) in effectively zero time for the cost of a Blood Point, provided they are not engaged in hand-to-hand combat.

                                                    ●● Dead Time
                                                    (Fair Escape)
                                                    By expending a Blood Point, the user of this power moves so fast that most others simply cannot react. While moving this fast they may only make very simple actions - such as running in a straight line, drawing a weapon, open-ing an unlocked door, or moving an object that no one is holding. For the power user, the world appears to stop. They may take simple actions for (subjectively) 6 seconds; this power may not be used in Combat except for purposes of escaping. For everyone else, no time seems to pass. Those without either Auspex or active Celerity are unable to see the power user - they are effectively invisible while in Dead Time. Those with Auspex will see a brief blur.

                                                    ●●● Blur
                                                    (Combat)
                                                    When using Combat Celerity, the power user moves at such a rapid pace that they appear to blur. The vampire is able to move and roll with even the swiftest sword strikes, minimizing damage. Thus, the power user gains Advantage on his or her Soak rolls, as well as the other Advantages given by Combat Celerity. This does not cost any blood beyond the initial cost of activating Combat Celerity.

                                                    ●●●● Foresight
                                                    (Combat)
                                                    The power user has developed their reflexes to such an extent that it almost seems that he or she can see a few seconds into the future. He or she may burn a Blood Point to react to any physical action her or she can sense — in any way — before that action completes. In terms of mechanics, the power user cannot be Ambushed or Surprised, but may still be Blindsided. However, if there is any advanced warning at all -- even the breaking of glass from a sniper's bullet coming through the window -- the power user may treat Blindsides as Ambushes.

                                                    When using Combat Celerity, the power user may wait to hear what any character who does not have Foresight is doing, before deciding his or her own actions. (This does not take priority over Auspex 5B’s similar power.) Additionally, if the power user does nothing but Evade, this power grants an additional claim to Advantage on all Evade rolls, and relieves the power user of the necessity to seek cover while evading ranged attacks. (You are so fast you can dodge bullets.)

                                                    ●●●●● Abattoir
                                                    (Combat)
                                                    [5A] The possessor of this fearsome power has the ability to become a whirlwind of death and destruction in close combat. This power offers two distinct benefits:

                                                    • Celerity ranks need not be split between multiple opponents: every opponent is allocated the power user’s full discipline rank. Normal multi-party combat rules will still apply, however, except as described below.
                                                    • The power user may also “spend” ranks of Combat Celerity each round to simply ignore the multiple opponent rules and attack/evade additional opponents without any penalty. A vampire with Celerity 5 could thus "spend" three ranks to engage up to four opponents without penalty, while still preserving two ranks to claim advantage against each opponent. This aspect of the power cannot be used while the power user is being grappled.

                                                    ●●●●● Untouchable
                                                    (Combat)
                                                    [5B] In any combat contest in which the power user is Evading an attack and has activated Combat Celerity — including those for which he has an Evade ranking of “Zero” or in multi-party combat — the power user may add the difference between his or her activated Celerity ranking and the attacker’s activated Celerity ranking to the Evade roll.

                                                    ●●●●●● Blink
                                                    (Fair Escape)
                                                    [6A] This power is essentially identical to Dead Time, but the power user moves so quickly, that he or she has twenty seconds of subjective time available to perform actions. This is an immense amount of time — enough to run over to a desk, scrawl a hasty note saying that you are sorry you had to leave, and to run out the door and down the stairs. It is enough time to run over to the window, carefully open the curtains to the sun, and run out the door, locking it behind you.
                                                    Seriously. It’s a lot of time. And a lot can happen in twenty seconds, change a vampires’ life…

                                                    ●●●●●● Between the Moments
                                                    (Combat)
                                                    [6B] By burning a Blood Point, the power user may gain a “free” round of combat activity between normal combat rounds during which only he or she can act. The power is activated during one round, and the power user receives the "free" round after that round ends. Opponents may still contest using their Brawl or Melee CBTs (as appropriate) but will not cause any damage, and unless they possess either Celerity (at any rank) or Auspex (at rank 4 or higher) their combat values are zero. The power user gains the benefits of his or her Combat Celerity during this “free” round, without the necessity of spending any additional blood.
