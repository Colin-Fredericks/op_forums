    The Red Rose pack were wasted potential

    06-12-2023, 07:15 PM
    The Red Rose pack were first introduced in Sins of the Blood and can be found on pages 105-108. They are a large pack of Sabbat infernalists that would make deals with demons on behalf of their clients (who consist of both vampires and mortals) in return for a hefty payment. They promised that since the client in question isn't dealing with the demon directly they didn't need to worry about any infernal taint.

    Of course, this was all a lie. Regardless of dealing with demons directly or indirectly, the fact of the matter is that the client in question intentionally chose to gain power from a demon. So even though someone else decided to deal with the demon on their behalf, the fact that they accepted power from a demon, still damns their soul to hell. Something interesting about this pack is that they have created an enterprise out of this practice and have learned how to cover their tracks, so well in fact that the Sabbat Inquisition has trouble tracking them. They have also spread their business across multiple states and seem to still be growing.

    I genuinely feel that they could have been an enemy that would force a truce between the sects. Here you have a large group of kindred who are ruthless, clever, and crafty in equal measure. Whose actions, threaten the existence of all vampires. They are very similar to how the Baali were during the Baali Wars, but unlike the Baali they are far more subtle in their actions and seem to have a lot more success in corrupting others. This is a great setup for another war, one that could put even the Baali Wars to shame.

    What do you think?​

      Vampires facilitating demonic pacts sounds cool but also like a trick you could only play on neonates. Openly advertising demon connections is a no-no. Perhaps the only exception is if you had it bound, but even then it's dangerous

      Damnation is a tricky topic. I'm of the opinion that God in the WoD is humanity's jailor, at best he keeps us safe from whatever's outside. When things are that grim, damnation of your immortal soul shouldn't be so easy. Reincarnation seems to be how things work, so such easy damnation would cause too many problems. Anything short of the caul or a pact where you have full awareness of what you're doing and you have enough willpower (6+) to be considered spiritually developed enough to make such a deal shouldn't be considered true damnation. Otherwise all demons would have armies of really edgy kids at their disposal.

      You can throw the word damnation around to upset whoever will believe you though. In for a Penny, in for a pound kind of tactics are good.

          It'd make for an interesting chronicle, though I certainly think it shouldn't be the direction the metaplot develops in, (I say, as somebody who thinks the only direction metaplot should go is straight in the bin). Plus even if the Sabbat and Camarilla won't play nice directly, the Setites, Ravnos and Assamites all have a bone to pick with infernalists too.


          On the topic of damnation as a fate within the WoD:
          Changeling and Mage both support reincarnation, and while the Garou aren't human souls, they also reincarnate. So let's assume that by default sapient beings are just stuck in a loop, living again and again, trapped in the World of Darkness. (Which is a damnation all on its own, tbh)

          Annihilationism also appears to be fact within the WoD; unless Oblivion actually simply resets your soul and chucks it back in the cycle, falling in there is the end.
          (I'll be honest, given the option between having to live through that hellhole again, and simply being erased, I would probably willingly jump into the Well of the Void. Therefore, Oblivion =/= Annihilation in my games, because it's more depressing that way.)

          For a more hellish Hell we have the Labyrinth and Harrowings. The Shadow-Eaten are consumed by their own hatred and pain, incapable of seeing the world as anything but hell. - that one though is more of a temporary damnation and a self-inflicted fate based on one's own will and perception, where one gives up and succumbs to nihilism. It still ends in annihilation.
          - So vampiric damnation, flavour No.1 [Annihilation]: Your soul is simply consumed by Oblivion and erased from the cycle after the final death.

          There's also Yomi, which may be divinely appointed, but it's still basically a little pocket dimension that only souls ascribing to a specific paradigm get kidnapped to, rather than something that affects everyone by default. The Baali and their demons seem to be connected to the Earthbound, although there are similarities with the Neverborn Malfeans, imo, so you're probably looking into an eternity as a servant of those. Hell, as in a jail of eternal torment, doesn't seem to a default fate awaiting the worst denizens of the WoD by default; but let's assume that the embrace or making a pact involves signing up for stuff that;
          - Vampiric damnation, flavour No.2 [Fire and Brimstone Hell]: Your soul is taken back to a Hell dimension to suffer - probably somewhere in Malfeas or the Labyrinth to be used to power something horrible.

          There's the Abyss that held the Fallen is just going insane of boredom in absolute nothingness for all eternity. I don't think that was a fate ever intended for a human (or former human) soul though.
          - But if you'd like, Vampiric damnation, flavour No.3 [Traditional Separation from God Hell]: Nothing, forever.

          Prone to hyperbole

                                          As far as infernalism goes, don't forget that destroying souls isn't actually something most "demons" in wod are capable of.

                                          Fallen can't actually keep souls very long unless they master one of the most jealously kept earthbound lores in the setting(the only one know to wield this power is an Archduke)

                                          Banes can't eat a soul without first chewing through all the person's autonomy, and if they die before then the bane is out of luck. Even then, the soul isn't destroyed, the bane and the soul just fuse(which might mean eaten souls might be released to reincarnate if the bane is killed)

                                          Spectres don't actually destroy souls when they make deals, and it's ambiguous if even the mightiest neverborn actually destroys souls when they "Eat" a wraith given how oblivion works(heck, neverborn might just be collections of the soulless plasm that ghosts shed with their memories when they reincarnate)

                                          Yama kings are still part of the cycle of reincarnation. They may live in a stagnant part of the cycle that makes escaping them within the next few kalpas infinitesimally unlikely, but it's a big theme that eventually the bill will come due on them as well(demon lords in book of madness seem to function similarly).

                                          The only demon type that calls this into question as a group are the uncategorized demons that make more generic infernal investment based on a 1-10 tiered pact system. Nothing is really known about these and no one knows what happens to the souls they claim.

                                          So in total, we have 1 demon that can actually eat souls, a group of unknowns that may or may not bring souls out of the reincarnation cycle, and everyone else is LARPing eternal punishment.
