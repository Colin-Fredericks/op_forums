    The Ravnos; Paradoxical Rogues

    09-22-2018, 04:41 PM
    Note: This is a preamble. If you just want to see the writeup, which I'm doing in the style of a V20 Clan writeup, then you can skip to the next post :-)

    So I've done this before, and I've gone about it the wrong way. I decided to read V20's presentation of the Ravnos, carefully, and figure out exactly what it is about it that means I don't use it at my table. To fix something you need to understand what's wrong with it.

    Ravnos are characterised as rogues, tricksters and outsiders. Hated and despised by the other clans. Seemingly more so than the Setites and Caitiff, indeed! It's strongly implied that they do lots of stuff to piss off the powers that be, but the V20 writeup doesn't devote that much space to explaining what that is; the implication is that it's theft.

    - Tricksters
    - Thieves
    - Rogues
    - Despised Outsiders
    - Strong links with the Romani
    - Nomadic

    On its own, this is kind of lackluster, and really superfluous. Caitiff are your scapegoats. Gangrel are your nomadic outsiders, carrying tales with them. And "trickster" isn't a Clan concept, it's a Path concept really. A clan of tricksters isn't a functional concept.

    So my idea was to reform their lore somewhat. Starting with Vampiric society, you have the Traditions. And in most Domains in the Kindred world, those matter a lot. So rather than having them be "thieves", let's have them be a clan which never really got on board with the Traditions. The smallest of the 13 clans, their behaviour has been shaped by their Methuselahs and Elders. The least organized, but paradoxically the most united.

    My goal is to revise the Ravnos to be:

    - "Thieves" and outlaws by the standards of Kindred, rather than by the standards of humans
    - Outsiders in a different sense to the Gangrel; they're not nomadic and animalistic, they're decidedly nomadic in a human way
    - Place more emphasis on links with "travelers" of all kinds than with the Romani in particular

    I'm trying to do this in a way which works with the existing concepts and lore, rather than completely revising them or just tacking on random new stuff like "they're basically the Camarilla in India".

      I think the treatment in Ravnos Revised more or less eliminates any need to "fix" the Ravnos. The best part of that book is that it fixed not only the Ravnos but multiple other problems with the bloodline as a whole.

      1. The over-reliance on Western vampirism myths. By adding Rakshasa to the Kindred mythology, they have expanded to include one of the most prevalent monsters in Indian mythology with a rich cultural history.

      2. The expanding of the Ravnos beyond "gypsy" stereotypes and instead incorporating the entirety of Indian mythology and one of the largest ethnic groups in the world getting their own clan. The fact Romani are Europeans of Indian descent also means the ludicrous stereotype neatly slides into their role as an effect of Kindred racism.

      3. The fact corruption, vice, temptation, and frigging illusion is all stuff which Rakshasa are known for in mythology.

      4. Highlighting that Ravnos culture is actually extremely traditionalist, classicist, and brutal with the people we meet tending to be the Outcasts (literally) of their race.

      Suddenly their Discipline has a historical basis as does their Clan flaw.

      So, yes, it was, to me at least, as simple as making them the "Indian" vampires versus the Romani vampires.

          The Ravnos are the smallest of the clans, and they move through the night with a freedom other Kindred can't begin to understand. Forget the Traditions. Forget Clan Hierarchy. Forget Primogeniture, Princes, Bishops, Elders... the Ravnos try to move past all of it. "Anarchs" in the most literal and puerile sense, they reject most of the basic foundations of Kindred society. They "poach" by feeding in the domains of others, they skip town with the item the whole court was fighting over, they disrespect the authority of Sect and society. To the Ravnos, these things are profane, and they take joy in disregarding them.

          In a society as structured and paranoid as that of most Kindred, this simply is not acceptable. The Ravnos are not polite outsiders who behave and follow the rules to avoid pissing off the local rulers. Here they may be distinguished from the Gangrel, and other Independent Clans and Bloodlines. They aren't even Rebels; they're Rogues. The Camarilla despises the Ravnos as a result, and has levied many resources towards maligning and scandalizing them, building on truth and lie to warp their reputation. Some Kindred historians believe that this has even shaped human history; the Ravnos are human-like nomads, and they travel with Travelers for protection, blood and companionship. So 1000s of years of propaganda from Princes may have warped the way kine perceive these traveler communities; when Roma were thrown out of villages with bloodied noses, perhaps once Kindred were pulling their strings.

          The simplest way to explain the Ravnos's place in Kindred society is to talk about the sort of effect they have on a Domain. There's an order to things. In-fighting, politicking, backstabbing, they're all ongoing. Everyone has a few skeletons in their closet, and the city is abuzz over one issue or another. By the time the so-called Deceiver has left town a lot of strands in this delicate web have been torn and tangled. Old alliances are bent out of shape, and Kindred find themselves with strange bedfellows. It is the Ravnos's self-appointed duty to shake things up and undo the stasis of Kindred society.

          For the Ravnos, the greatest values are those of community and independence. This seems paradoxical to some, but the clan is full of contradictions. They disregard the Masquerade in theory, and yet for the most part they keep it. For them it tends to be a personal matter of privacy rather than some grand intoxicating secret. Their human companions tend to know who and what they are, and they are not always ghouls.

          While a Ravnos is staying within a Domain, they tend to hunt however they wish, and use this as a chance to stake out the local Kindred society. "Mischief" is an oversimplified way of putting it, but many do seek to throw a spanner in the works. What can they do that would cause the most disruption to this ordered fascist shit-show?

          Some among the Ravnos follow the Path of Paradox, which codifies the Clan's culture and beliefs as a system of morality. These mystics hold their own secrets, and practice rare forms of blood magic.

          Clan Ravnos is well known for its signature Discipline of Chimerstry; using it they can create illusions and warp perceptions, and with its greatest powers they begin to warp reality itself.

          Nickname: Deceivers

          Sect: The Ravnos, as a general rule, are Independent. There are few exceptions due to their reputation, and the concerted efforts of Elder Ravnos and Elders within the Camarilla and Sabbat. Some Ravnos ally themselves with the Anarchs, albeit in an informal manner.

          Appearance: Ravnos tend to embrace out of traveling communities, such as the Romani and Irish Travelers. However those who choose nomadic lifestyles and give up on structured society may be an enticing prospect to a Ravnos; as a result they may hail from any background. Many of the Elders of the clan have Egyptian, Eastern European and even Indian heritage.

          Haven: Most Ravnos live on the road. Caravans, RVs, motels, trailer parks... if a Ravnos is staying within a Domain for a few weeks or months they may set down some roots and find themselves nice accommodation, but their home is rarely truly fixed.

          Background: Ravnos tend to embrace self-sufficient people who exist on the edges of society, especially people who find society absurd or defy it in some way. Punks, bikers, travelers, the homeless, refugees and immigrants; the Ravnos prefer to embrace those who need no lesson in what is wrong with ordered societies like the Camarilla.

          Character Creation: Indepedent concepts are common. Ravnos have diverse and fluid backgrounds, rather than being fortified in one place. Some allies here and there, some money in international accounts. Physical and Social attributes are often primary, and Talents and Skills are often predominant.

          Clan Disciplines: Chimerstry, Presence and Fortitude

          Weaknesses: The Ravnos are impulsive, having a kind of rebellious individualism in their very blood. Every Ravnos has some kind of "virtue" or "vice" which can manifest as compulsive behaviour. Ravnos must roll Self-Control, difficulty 6, to avoid sating their impulse when tempted by an opportunity. Those on Paths which use Instinct separate themselves from the restraints which hold them back from impulse, and therefore do not roll to resist the temptation towards virtue or vice. Examples include an urge to punish abusive people, urges to help defenseless young people, urges to scam others or urges to inflict emotional pain.

          Organization: The clan is fragmented yet strangely united. Some postulate that the power of their Methuselahs (or even their Antedeluvian!) is felt through the blood via the power of Presence, leading to a relatively cohesive clan philosophy. Others simply point out that the Ravnos are left in a position where their only choice is to go with the grain and follow the clan's culture of chaotic and paradoxical rebellion. The opinions of the Sects and the other Clans don't leave room for them to avoid this.
