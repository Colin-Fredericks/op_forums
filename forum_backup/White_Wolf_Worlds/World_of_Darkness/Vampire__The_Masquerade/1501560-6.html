    Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
    One did, however, learn the interesting fact that at least one theatre of the First Sabbat Civil War actually began in New England (Under the cover of Shay’s Rebellion in AD 1786)
    This is the first time I have ever heard of this. I reviewed my old copies of the original Sabbat Players and Storyteller's Guides and then the Revised era Guide of the Sabbat. Scratching my head, I finally looked up the White Wolf Wiki's entry on the Sabbat Civil Wars, saw it mentioned, and looked up the reference that put this in Giovanni Chronicles III (published in 1998)!

    When it comes to the Sabbat Civil Wars, we're definitely at the point where we need to ask are we on Earth-II where Alan Scott is Green Lantern with a magic lamp, Earth-I where it is Hal Jordan as part of the space faring Green Lantern Corps, post-Crisis where both exist, or one of the other resets of the DC universe with its own continuity, because the details of the Sabbat Civil Wars are all over the place.

    The original Sabbat guides had little details on how many civil wars occurred or when they happened. What little we do have implies there was a civil war during the great Camarilla-Sabbat Jyhads in the "early years" (likely during the 17th century or so, because we must remember it took quite some time before the Anarchs who rejected the Convention of Thorns to turn into the Sabbat) which resulted in the original Code of Milan, another one that lasted around 1800s to early 1900s (roughly) that resulted in the revised Code of Milan of 1933, and the "last" war which saw the creation of the Panders in the 1950s. But it's not clear if these were the only civil wars.

    Guide to the Sabbat introduced the Revised era's canon of three civil wars. The first started in the late eighteenth century and lasted around 30 years ending with the Purchase Pact. The second lasting seventy years between 1863-1933, and a third civil war lasting 100 days in 1957. The two timelines are not actually compatible, but do bear some similarities. (This is not unusual as the Revised era saw a lot of writers creating backstory but didn't seem to read previous sourcebooks very closely or perhaps purposefully retconned stuff.) So STs will need to pick what they like as they create their own history for their games.

    Personally, I like the earlier version which seemed to describe a situation where the newly formed Sabbat grew large, tried to take on the Camarilla directly in the 17th-18th centuries, got almost destroyed, and the survivors either escaped to distant areas of Europe like northern Scandinavia, retreated to certain strongholds like Milan, or fled to the New World. They then lost hold of much of the New World from a concerted Camarilla expansion, and this lead to another civil war that saw the sect centralize in response. But everyone will have their own preferences for the timeline.

    Giovani Chronicles III's idea of the Sabbat Civil War beginning from Shay's Rebellion doesn't hold for the original canon. Not only is the timeline different, but the history of Dark Colony (and geography) would not allow for a Sabbat civil war to erupt in western Massachusetts. But if you want to reconcile the two, perhaps some feud between the Lasombra and Sabbat at that time would eventually lead to the second civil war. The big problem I have with this idea is that this period saw the Camarilla expanding in the New World, New England was a Camarilla stronghold as several clans conspired to remove the local Ventrue from leadership, and the Sabbat would not really become a threat until post Civil War 1860s-1890s. The main Sabbat danger to New England before the Civil War likely came from its "control" of Canada, particularly Montreal, and having lots of nomad packs of Country Gangrel in the wilderness. Sticking the Lasombra and Tzimisce in western Massachusetts that somehow survived so they could start a Sabbat Civil War is kind of silly IMO. It creates more problems than it solves, and it isn't necessary to include the Sabbat in the history of New England.

    Now Dark Colony's own history is not perfectly aligned with the general Sabbat outline in the original guides either. Their period of greatest threat to the New England Camarilla (and takeover of New York City) for a thirty year period post-Civil War is when both versions of the timeline place a huge civil war that almost destroys the sect (and won't end until around 1933). But maybe we could attribute it to Dark Selina's manipulation (while torpored in Switzerland!), as blow back from the civil war as losing Sabbat seek to find a safe place for their side by conquering New England, a plan by the Black Hand to reunite the sect by leading a successful crusade, or an indication that the Sabbat of NYC and Canada were among the first centralizers and imposed some kind of order among the local Sabbat and attempted to conquer New England to showcase their strength.

    That's how I would approach it, but everyone will have their own preferences on what tidbits, lore, and trivia to include. We all have different tastes and different things inspire different people.

                Saladin is the Western transliteration of Salah ad-Din, the actual name of the historical figure. "Baladin" is a French word meaning wandering performer, and it's a rare French last name. There are, of course, other varieties of Baladin out there. It is an ordinary Turkish last name as well. Most likely the original intent of Dark Colony was for a French origin Tremere.

                Setting contradictions between editions always leads to ST decision whether to embrace one version or the other, to split the difference, or create new information that explains why different versions exist. That can be a fun part of being the ST.

                    It is, in fact, one of the great pleasures of working in any shared universe - or, indeed, working with History (Indiana Jones is an exaggeration for effect, but I think it’s safe to say that any real historian worth their salt loves to solve mysteries quite as much as they love to collect and share old anecdotes, artefacts & Trivia.

                    I wasn’t aware that ‘Baladin’ was a French surname, but given that Saladin et al are Medieval French versions of Arabic names (I sometimes think history ought to be described as one long game of ‘Spanglish Whispers’ rather than ‘Chinese Whispers’) that actually makes a good deal of sense.

                    In all honesty I do like the notion that the Tremere Baladin is someone using a French name to obscure a still more exotic (By the standards of Federalist-era New England) origin: In fact making this character someone born a Turk who adopted a French name and wound up associated with the Winchester chantry before heading out to the New World suggests a pretty darned intriguing history (Of the sort that can very reasonably be associated with an elder vampire).

                    One assumes that, according to this version of events, ‘Baladin’ might have been born (or lived most of his mortal life) in the Ottoman Sultan’s European possessions (Possibly of mixed parentage), was recruited by the Tremere and (Either as a result of that transformation or through the very actions that made him an attractive subject for The Embrace) made his homeland too hot to hold him, entered Christian Europe and moved to an area of same where being a Turk was less inherently dangerous (So France after Francis I made an alliance with the Sultan would be as likely a place as any) and from there made a hop across the English Channel.

                    Whether he was originallyTurkish or not, I do like the idea that Baladin was a childe of Meerlinda and himself the sire of Madeleine Coventry (With the latter being the first vampire he embraced): it helps make his promotion over Coventry at least somewhat politic, rather than a crude insult of the sort I don’t appreciate with the Tremere (and also positions Baladin himself as an ‘Advance Party’ and proxy for Meerlinda in New England, which would help explain why the latter seems to have set up somewhere else in the United States.

                    This version of things does beg the question of why Meerlinda didn’t intervene during the Chantry War: my best attempt to win a ‘No Prize’ would be to suggest that Meerlinda preferred to avoid drawing attention to this unedifying spectacle by intervening directly, was too busy with more productive labours to get caught up in this sorry business or was patiently allowing Baladin a chance to settle his own affairs (Intervening only after the murder of her Childe and then only covertly).

                                I just read some interesting trivia on canonical New England vampire characters in an unlikely source. I was checking Necropolis: Atlanta for something, and in the section on the vampires of Atlanta saw some characters had ties to New England.

                                Aunt Bedelia (6th generation Malkavian vampire embraced in Brighton, England in 1760) and her sire Alabaster moved to Boston area relatively soon after her embrace. She didn't like the city, broke free from her sire, and then moved down to Atlanta with a Ventrue ancillae named Herbert. Elsewhere it says she began her journey from Plymouth, MA so it's likely she moved to there after breaking things off with her sire, probably taking her "boy" Herbert (Bedelia's derangement is fixating on young men and becoming an overprotective mother to them.)

                                She arrived in Atlanta when it was still known as "Terminus", so her arrival was somewhere between 1836-1845. So she and Herbert might have been in New England from around 1760-1840.

                                We don't know what happened to Alabaster, but he isn't mentioned in Dark Colony. But a 5th generation Malkavian isn't someone you just forget. He'd also be the first of a large number of exceptionally potent Malkavians to be in Boston. While canonically William Biltmore did not migrate to Boston until 1895, in this thread I moved him there pre-American Revolution. So I'd just make him, not Alabaster, as Bedelia's sire and adjust her generation accordingly.

                                There's also the possibility that she could have sired one or more vampires. Biltmore is 6, so Bedelia would not be 7th. We know Skarvan was originally 8. Perhaps Skarvan was Bedelia's first childe. Perhaps Skarvan's antics was a reason Bedelia and her sire split.

                                Since the Ventrue ancilla Herbert is a cipher (he only exists to demonstrate Bedelia goes nuts and destroys her "boys" if they become too independent), we don't have anything to go by. But willingly going with a Malkavian fixated on him indicates a level of desperation. So I'd have him be the childe of the deposed Ventrue Prince of Boston overthrown in 1783. That still gives him about 50-60 years still living in New England, but if Aunt Bedelia became fixated on him prior to the revolution, it's possible her sire - and new Prince of Boston - Biltmore would have accepted it. It would also provide a reason why Bedelia broke with Biltmore if eventually he considered Herbert's survival to be a threat to him.

                                Herbert was said to be Ancilla by the time he and Bedelia arrived in Boston, so he was embraced prior to 1730-1740 or so. Boston was first settled in 1625, so it's quite possible he was a colonist in Boston embraced by a newly arriving Venture looking for a local person to assist his rule. I would keep him separate from the Rowlands lineage of Ventrue in Connecticut, but might plug him into the lineage of any Ventrue characters left in Boston or perhaps our Barnabas Collins analogue.

                                Then there is also "Thelonious" an African slave originally brought to Virginia who was then bought and emancipated by abolitionists. He was educated, became involved in the Underground Railroad, and eventually embraced in 1859 by the Brujah Isaac in New York. He soon rose in power among the New York Brujah before moving to Atlanta to help the freedman after the end of the Civil War. Unfortunately, this history is quite a mess. Thelonius was 26 when he was embraced meaning he was born in 1833. However, the US abolished the international slave trade in 1808 meaning Thelonious could not have directly from Africa. His parents would need to be existing slaves in the US if we want to keep to the timeline. It's a minor change, but wanted to mention it. It's also a bit weird to have the character be tied to Boston but then have his embrace happen to New York. There are several canonical Brujah in Boston around this time that could serve as logical sires. Thelonious is 8th generation, but if we wanted Crispus Attucks or Pug Jackson to be his sire, we would adjust that to be a weaker generation. Thelonious would be merely a curious part of Boston's history, but it would help establish that underground railroad network Crispus Attucks established while he was still in Boston and give Pug Jackson another out of state contact to have that an ST could use if ever needed.

                                Another bit of trivia is that Boston hosted a conclave in 1850 which sent several Archons down to the American south to investigate the rumors about the Southron Lords (an alliance of powerful vampires ruling from plantations in the South rather than the cities). All but one Archon failed to return. There's no mention of an 1850 conclave in Dark Colony, but no reason why we should not have had one and make it part of the revised history of New England.

                                                I found some other mentions of New England in other Vampire sourcebooks. Finally have time to post it here.

                                                In New Orleans by Night, we are introduced to several Tremere with history in the New England region. It describes three renegade Tremere - Antonio Calbullarshi, Nigel Poter, and Sebastian Wolcott - who arrive in New England in the late 1700s before they eventually move to New Orleans. I think the best way to integrate them into the history of Dark Colony is that these three would have joined Madeline Coventry's Salem Chantry since it was already on the outs with the clan, and thus they would have participated in its war with the Boston Chantry. Antonio would quickly disappear, but Nigel and Sebastian assisted Madeline Coventry in the hopes of establishing themselves as accepted Tremere should she win. In 1831 the Sabbat overrun the Salem Chantry. Madeline Coventry disappears. Nigel Porter and Sebastian Wolcott are among the survivors and flee south and disappear.

                                                Antonio would eventually return to New England after the Anarch Free State was established. There he embraced the awakening mage Marie Richet in 1991 and presented her to the Hartford Chantry (which in this thread we moved to Providence) in the hopes an embraced mage would fix things with the clan and welcome him back. Instead it got them both blood hunted. Marie's backstory is very complicated.

                                                I am a bit surprised that all the Tremere in New Orleans are 1) essentially all renegades and 2) tied to New England. It must have been a deliberate design choice.

                                                The next sourcebook is Giovanni Chronicles IV which provides more detail on the Milliners. Now I must say I do not like GCIV as a sourcebook. It internally contradicts itself, contradicts​ the Giovanni clanbook, and does things differently than what I would have them. But in one spot it establishes Francis Milliner's embrace in 1954 (though in another section it moves this to 1959). 1954 is a good match for the clanbook's original description of mid-fifties. Jason Milliner, Francis's nephew, is embraced in 1959 although in GCIV, his sire is not Francis but the same sire as Francis which I think is just silly. Again a family of aristocratic Venetian financiers are portrayed as simply Sicilian mobsters. How the Giovanni embrace these people without permission by Prince Quention King and never suffer any blowback is never adequately explained. Also despite the clanbook clearly stating ALL the Milliners are generation 10 and above, both Francis and Jason start as 9th generation.

                                                So in my version once the Giovanni decide on embracing Francis, the Giovanni struck a deal with Quentin King. They'll make up influence and business support he lacks from the Connecticut Ventrue's rivalry. Quentin agrees up front that Francis will be embraced in 1954 (10th generation), and that Francis can create one or two more children which he does in 1959 by embracing his nephew (11th generation). The Milliners officially keep out of Kindred politics per the Promise of 1528, but secretly provide Quentin some support that he needs.

                                                The last thing is Chicago by Night 2nd edition which has some trivia. The 11th generation Caitiff Victoria Longwood is said to come from an old money family in New England. Torrence, a 10th generation Toreador, embraces her in 1984, but being annoyed by him she manages to stake Torrence and then flee, eventually running into some anarchs and she eventually makes her way to the Free State.

                                                So this rather throwaway text gives us some nice things. We have the mortal Longwood family that is extremely old and powerful family in New England. We have a known 10th generation Toreador vampire named Torrence (who could easily have survived his staking though CbN shows him as destroyed). We could place the Longwoods anywhere, either in Boston or Connecticut depending on whether we wanted Torrence to be in either city.

                                                Dark Colony only gives us Elsa Linden, but Swansong provides various Toreador that we could use in Boston. So just to cover all our bases, I'd probably put Torrence and the Longwoods in Connecticut to be part of Upton Rowland's domain. Torrence might be unrelated to any existing vampires, or we could make him part of a lineage that goes back to Elsa Linden.

                                                    This is a more speculative post.

                                                    In Milwaukee by Night, we are told that Edward, the Black Prince of Cornwall embraced Akawa in Missouri in 1770. Otherwise we are given very little about his past between his embrace and his current status in Milwaukee.

                                                    Presumably, Edward being in Missouri is a result of St Louis (founded in 1764). He does not have Protean or any other Disciplines which makes overland wilderness travel easy, so presumably he made safe passage through the existing French fur trade routes in the Midwest (though by 1770 French North America had been ceded to either Britain or Spain). How did Edward get there though? Missouri is a long way from Cornwall.

                                                    Although not detailed, Dark Colony says there were early Brujah in New England, but that the Ventrue arrival in Boston and Connecticut pushed them to northern New England, and that eventually Sabbat attacks in turn pushed them back south which lead to fights that erupted during the American Revolution. We also know from World of Darkness 1e that the Brujah were one of the lesser clans of England/Great Britain. The Ventrue, lead by Mithras, dominated, and their only real challenges came from the Tremere and Toreador.

                                                    So Edward, being a Brujah, could very well have left England early for more opportunities in North America. Or if he stayed longer, he might have been one of the Brujah who rallied to Pendragon's struggle against Mithras in the 18th century. In either case, he would have left the region some time before the actual War of Independence. Edward arriving in New England, as opposed to Virginia or Pennsylvania, would have easier access to New France which would allow him to follow the trade routes to St Louis. He could have arrived some time in the first half of the 18th century (or earlier) and then made his way down the Ohio River after the end of the French and Indian War.

                                                    Edward would not have been any real factor in New England's history, but he could be an alternative source of a Brujah lineage in New England that does not issue from Pendragon or the Anarchs (MacNeil or Foccart) that fought during the revolution. Someone, after all, needed to sire some of those Brujah who fled north. Some of the might still be around, though individuals could easily be followers of Pendragon, Anarchs, or independent Camarilla.
