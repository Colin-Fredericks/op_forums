                      Romance in VtM tends to be either a parasitical emulation or if it is real, then it is something the PCs are actively trying to destroy between NPCs.

                      This only gets more complicated when you consider that in the modern era romance can't exist between individuals with different levels of power, status, wealth, or experience. Such things are seen as being predatory or taking advantage of the lesser individual. With such things as an age gap and a successful career being seen as an immoral power imbalance, things like Blood Bonding, Dominate, Presence, the promise of the embrace, etc. just kind of make it an automatic non-starter.

                                          Romance is part of the Gothic Horror genre, but I usually find most traditional RPG players are uncomfortable roleplaying it unless done at an arm's reach (establish things more OOC than IC roleplaying it, and avoid intimate details). So it's important how an ST handles it. Of course, many players of Vampire are not traditional RPG players and are more open to it, especially those who came into the hobby out of a love of vampire literature where that is very prominent. So an ST's approach needs to cater to both the general group needs and specific to each player.

                                          I let players do it however they want to approach it. It's their character. But I use some general guidelines as to how I approach it as an ST for NPCs.

                                          First, the capacity of actual love is dependent on Humanity. That is different from lust or more selfish desires. Anyone can have those, and most vampires who discover "love" for a particular person is more likely just driven by some kind of desire rather than love. Higher Humanity means the vampire is more capable of love, lower Humanity means it is not. Whether or not a character's "romance" actually turns into real "love" is dependent on various factors, so of course there is a spectrum. You can have romantic tropes, but not actually do romance in terms of an actual love story. Lots of vampires are skilled in appearing romantic, but that is only to groom their victims and those the opposite of actual romance.

                                          Second, actual love/romance is best done through vampire-human interaction, not between vampires. Vampires can have a relationship with one another, but over time they tend to break down into dysfunction because each of their Beasts tend to find feelings of love to be a weakness, and thus a danger to themselves that must be eliminated, and having both partners experience that doesn't lead to happy endings (I am probably influence heavily here by Michael and Sharon Payne in Chicago By Night). Mutual blood bonds and such can overcome to that some degree, but the blood bond just simulates love and is probably an anti-thesis of it. So if a player wants to experience it, as ST I would want to introduce a living human being to be their partner.

                                          Third, love is redemptive and can be a source of a vampire regaining more Humanity and increasing their Virtues. As long as it actually drives changes in behavior.

                                          Those are just general tendencies. There's nothing wrong with an elder vampire of low Humanity - or even a Path rating - somehow rediscovering true love and begin the process of clawing back their Humanity (albeit they may not succeed). That can be a great story. You can also have a high Humanity vampire reject love and begin a descent. And even inter-vampire romance can be done appropriately. Khalid having repressed love towards Elzbieta Jurofsky is something that can make the game interesting. These are just exceptions to the general rule.
