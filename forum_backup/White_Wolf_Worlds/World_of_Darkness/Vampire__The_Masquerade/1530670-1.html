        Originally posted by Matt Corleone View Post


        I do like your ideas and I wonder how you feel about my ideas about the Convention of Charleston, and Operation Country Roads, where a previously beleaguered Sabbat gets a second wind and unexpectedly claps the Ashirra's cheeks throughout the 2010's?
        Oh, i loved those. Joseph Pander has been one of the more interesting figures in the bits of internal politics and history of the Sabbat as a sect and the fact he went mostly ignored for the whole of VtM first run felt like a huge disservice. Then comes Rites of the Blood, to change everything we thought we knew about the Panders as a movement and show Joseph in a whole new light in one ritual, that does not even get a level and all its ramifications (on an aside, it was through BJD, the dialogue between Beckett and Benezri, that i learned about the lore update/upgrade on the Panders). So, while i'm not a fan of the whole "rock falls, Gallbraith dies" subplot from Revised per se, seeing it solved with Joseph as impromptu Regent to really shake things up is awesome indeed.


        Incidentally, in my Milwaukee plans he's also one of the major leaders in the siege - deeply to the frustration and distaste of Vykos, who has much less of a free hand than he would prefer due to constant crosschecking and interference from him, Dominique (now a Salubri antitribu, much to Adonai's equal distaste - more on that later), Parovich (who clearly has some bizarre agenda but is the Sabbat's top grapevine into the city's happenings) and Dimitri (actually Lasombra of the Amici Noctis in my games). They are all galling but Joseph is the worst of the lot, constantly questioning his decisions and keeping tabs on his motives. Vykos has no doubt the detestable upjumped rabble would attempt to topple him in the bat of an eyelash if he learned that Vykos' true aim is obtaining Carna's copy of the Book of Grave War.

        What is doubly ironic, since this distrust and rivalry between both Prisci also results in Vykos never learning Joseph Pander already knows and possesses a copy of the book, that indeed was a key component in devising the rite that breaks one blood's ties to their founders!

        As an aside, my version of Joseph also thoroughfully detests Thin-bloods, what has led to him influencing the adoption of some peculiarly conservative policies within the sect and particularly this war campaign, in relation to them (also, my version of Milwaukee has become something of a duskborn refuge in the years between the "Psychomachia killings" and the Siege, for a number of reasons).

        [I have sort of tinkered with the idea of Thin-Bloods in my most recent game, partly inspired by BJD's sub-chapter on them in Anarch Freefall, coming up with some points of divergence - and conflict, as you can guess from my comment on Joseph's feelings on them, for example - from Caitiff, among other things]

        Plus, he might be actually kind of related to someone not exactly that far away from local events....


        Operation Country Roads is a good one too - truth be told, using the Gehenna War to comment upon/explore the last two decades of chaos in many muslim countries is kind of a natural. Some people might take issue with that as culturally insensitive like hell but action movies & spy thrillers are among the most anything-insensitive popular genres ever anyway, so whatever. Kudos to you.

        That said, my take of things is a little more pulverized in some ways, with thoroughfully Black Hand-infiltrated PMCs in Afghanistan and Iraq, Anarch cabals involved in all too many pies in the movements of the Arab Spring, a dizzying mess of factions on the nightmarish fighting spilling between Iraq, Syria and other neighnoring countries.

        Also, the Ashirra are more of a regional politico-religious kindred alliance restricted to the Arabian Peninsula proper (a good chunk of the Arab-speaking having formed ties to the Ivory Tower thanks to Toreador diplomacy of centuries past) and possible origins quite unlike their appearances might indicate....

(To a lesser extent the Ravnos "Jati" could also fit here). In light of a Cyclical Gehenna, they may represent Clan recreations that were allowed to exist concurrently, survive, and most importantly be known. Kindred in-universe may only know them AS members of the same Clan, because their creators permitted it.

                                Conversely, it's an established possibility that Saulot created the Baali (deliberately or accidently), and they got out of hand (again, deliberately or accidently).                                 How many times have Clans or Bloodlines come into existence, and the later purge was simply more successful?

                                        In terms of cyclical Gehenna, it's important to note that in the 1e Corebook mentioned a 2300 year cycle which implied the antedeluvians devoured almost all their descendants at the end of these cycles, and that the last time this occurred was around Alexander the Great's wars 2300 years ago (setting up that sometime soon there would be another devouring). I believe the 2300 year cycle was inspired by the Book of Daniel whose 2300 day prophecy was though to be one day = one year, and this 2300 years.

                                        That would seem to be rejected in 2e and later renditions, but its interesting as an insight into the original thinking behind Gehenna. The idea of a cyclical Gehenna is not inherently against the VtM mythos. Personally I don't think there is an objection between recycling the concept of a cyclical Gehenna and a final Gehenna that results in an apocalyptic end times.
