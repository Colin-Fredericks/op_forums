    I feel capadocians have passive/defensive disciplines does well to give them low numbers and makes them a perfect metaphor for how old-fashioned, scholarly loners will get wrecked by agressive big-business (The Giovanni come into power during the late medieval/early renaissance, where banks and merchants are getting their big break; it's not a coincidence)
    Also, I imagine virtually all capadocians as very penitent monks, i'm sure some weren't, but it's the archtype that comes to mind most. Auspex, Fortitude and Necromancy just become the most -this fits the character- disciplines we have. Auspex and necromancy for -scholarly monk- and Fortitude and Necromancy for -I must suffer- monk living.

    Making necromancy an out-of-clan might work, but some people are very attached to -clan of death- idea and wouldn't ever want that.
    Giving up fortitude? No, we must applaud their masochism.
    Giving up Auspex... well, they are rather delusional... it's the lesser crime I think. You don't need auspex to find ghosts if you have necromancy, but the other issue is that the cappadocians are idiots and put zombies above ghosts so they often didn't have sephulcre; that could do with a retcon.

    But what to replace it with?

    Animalism- The guy plays with bodies all day. Probably reeks of embalming fluid. No.
    Celerity- There's the quick and the dead. A guy who hangs around with corpses all day shouldn't be embracing the quick.
    Dominate- Gives the impression they're well oranized, and they aren't, the giovanni are. Conflict is more interesting narratively when two sides hold directly opposing views.
    Obfuscate, by far the most beneficial for what I think a capadocian playstyle should be, but their corpse-palor would need to become Samedi-ugly or else they'd be cheating.
    Potence- It could work well, Interesting juxtoposition to the 'defensive' stereotype of capadocians. Maybe a little redundant with fortitude (Where you'll still win fights against mortals for feeding, they'll just take longer and maybe involve more noise... hmm...)
    Presence- Hahahaha no.

    So, I'd say best options would be Necromancy, Fortitude, Obfuscate, (Samedi called) or Auspex, Obfuscate, fortitude (Like the toughest malkavian you'd ever seen, you'd never kill them all) or you tread on the Lamia's toes with a potence replacement. If you gave them obfuscate, a weakness fix (that isn't appearance 0) would be to have the -looks deathly, can't blush of life- show effects no matter how they use mask of a thousand faces. Necromancy as an -out-of-clan- discipline in general isn't a terrible idea (The Samedi and Kiasyd do it so) because it's reallly no good at getting you food...

    After all that brainstorming, I still think I'd stick to Auspex,Fortitude, Necromancy and the same clan weakness. Having a poorer spread is a feature, not a bug, when you're doomed to fail anyway.

                Originally posted by AkatsukiLeader13 View Post
                As long time Giovanni fan and someone that generally likes the Hecata and what has been done to them, I thought I'd share some of my own thoughts on stuff that's being talked about.

                With regards to the Giovanni and the Harbingers, their whole thing has always been off. The earliest write-ups of them have always been vague on why exactly they hated the Giovanni. But it has also been noted that the Pisanob bloodline in particular had been receiving a lot of their attention. Now the first thing that comes to mind in that is that Pisanob are the Giovanni based in South and Central America, where the Sabbat are strong. Obviously many Harbingers would target them first due to their proximity to Sabbat strongholds but there are two things that put an interesting spin on that. First in V5 it's noted that the Harbingers had brought the Bloodline to near destruction until their founder and leader offered his life up in exchange for the survival of the rest of the Pisanob. An odd thing for a Giovanni Bloodline to get more attention than the main family. Until we factor in the second thing, the Pisanobs were vampires before the Giovanni found them. Their founder and leader, Pochtli was long suspected to be a Cappadocian.

                And that piece might just be the thing to explain it all.

                Now this is pure speculation on my part but I think there are two things that drove their aggression against the Giovanni (well aside from the fact that as rival Necromancers they are a threat to them), the first being vengeance by proxy. They can't get revenge against Cappadocious anymore but they can go after the ones that still carry his Blood, Augustus and those Giovanni embraced before Cappa's death. The second reason is to go after those Cappadocians that defected to the Giovanni to save their own hides. Like I said, Pochtli was almost certainly a Cappadocian but there's also been vague talk over the years that some of the Premascine within the Giovanni were of Cappadocian Blood but not Giovanni stock. To a Harbinger, they would be worse than the Giovanni. They abandoned their Clan mates to their fate to save themselves just as so many Cappadocians abandoned them during the Feast of Folly.

                It would help explain why they were so against the Giovanni yet also willing to take part in the Family Reunion and join the Hecata. Those that were focus of their ire were by that point dead or subdued while they are now a leading voice in the Clan of Death (something they likely never had with the Cappadocians) as well as regaining their ability to Embrace. In fact of all the Bloodlines, the Harbingers have gained the most from the Family Reunion. They've even eclipsed their Cappadocian kin in terms of power and prominence.

                Finally, Lazarus and the Harbingers. On the surface of things, I could see why some would think that the Harbingers should hate him but if the material from V20 on the matter holds true in V5 (which in this case I don't see why it wouldn't) then the generally it would be reverse. In the V20 Black Hand book it was revealed that the Capuchin is a trio of Cappadocians (rather two Cappa and the wraith of one), Japheth, Byzar and Lazarus. And the Capuchin has long been a shadow player in the Giovanni as well as potentially one that freed at least some of the Harbingers. In fact one in Lore of the Bloodlines claims that the Capuchin unmasked himself to them, revealing himself to be Lazarus and that he gave them their name, the Harbingers of Skulls. Of course another Harbinger claims otherwise, muddying the situation. But generally their chapter presents Lazarus as a respected figure among the Harbingers, even those that dispute him saving them acknowledge him as someone to respect.

                And given the Capuchin was involved in the Family Reunion, with it being suggested that he gave them their new name, it wouldn't surprise me if the Capuchin was the driving force behind the Reunion and that to do he unmasks himself as Lazarus to get the Harbingers and Cappadocians to general play ball. Or even the aforementioned trio to reveal themselves as the Capuchin. Which actually now that I say that out loud I realize that the reveal of the Capuchin being three people would play into the trinity motif that is present in the Hecata.

                As for the Hecata and the other Sects, there are some points in V5 Cults that I wish to bring up. First is that the book notes that not all Cappadocians rescued from Torpor aren't okay with joining up with the Giovanni and that they are looking to join the Camarilla. The other being that most outside the Hecata don't know that the Hecata are what they are and are using that to their ends. At the moment, the Cam and Anarchs think that the Giovanni has just reorganized and rebranded themselves. And with the Giovanni continuing to be the face of the Clan, others are offering their services as alternatives to the Giovanni when in reality they're buying the services of the same group.

                More importantly, the book very much takes a stance that I've long held with the Giovanni in my games, that neutrality does not equal inactivity. In my games, I've long had the Giovanni be a fixture of local politics, regardless of whether the city's Cam or Anarch. Giovanni reps frequently appear at Elysium and other major events because the Princes and Barons don't trust them but also can't afford to alienate them lest they decide to quietly send aid to their rivals and foes or worse.

                And with the uneasy new politics of the Hecata, there's plenty of room to get non-Hecata dragged into their schemes and games. Hell, that the Hecata chronicle within the book includes a sample coterie with non-Hecata in it is proof of that alone.
                I like your theory here about the HoS. It makes sense, though I think there are some other things potentially going on.

                I was also going to point out the same thing with Lazarus--he's been connected to the Harbingers since they appeared. They're nicknamed the Lazarenes, Unre/Constancia attributes the return of the HoS to the Capuchin, and the description of the Capuchin in CB:GioRev implies it's Laz (the first letter of each line spells out his name).

                Interestingly, older descriptions of Unre say she and her sire worked on different sides of the Shroud to return the Harbingers to the world. Now that we know Japheth is a wraith, however, it seems they were on opposite sides to the sides we previously assumed (Unre, as a Risen, would have been in the Skinlands, with her sire in the Shadowlands). Previously we assumed that Japheth was still undead and Unre was trapped in the Underworld. This way around makes more sense of their given histories, though.

                There was one more reason for Unre's hatred of the Giovanni, which might also apply to the Harbingers, and that was that they had taken the Sargon Fragment and the Anexhexeton (which she herself sought). I think competing for limited resources is a valid source of conflict, and would certainly aggravate (or be aggravated by) all the nasty business of usurpation.

                If we consider the V20 lore, the Cult of the Capuchin was also looking to bring about Gehenna. So it's entirely possible Laz and co were playing everyone off each other to this end--it doesn't really matter if the rivalry made sense, but he was in the perfect place to stoke it (on both sides).

The gangsters of their family can feed on rivals they kill, or people who won't attract attention if they go missing. Legitimate business men can run casino resorts which can attract lots of people who also won't draw attention, and whose rooms you can monitor at night. Rich bankers can host fancy social events for their family, and identify which people are the ones who are so attracted to the Giovanni wealth that they willingly ignore any danger signs and put themselves into a position where they can be fed upon. Whether they have Dominate or Potence or perhaps something else, can adjust their tactics accordingly. It'll still work for them even if they only have one of those Disciplines and not both.

                                        It's perfectly fine if none of this is to your taste. But I think it's too dismissive to say the clan couldn't be made to work simply because they are not your cup of tea.

In some hospice care services, the caregivers drive out to the homes and take care of their clients there. And not all people receiving hospice care are in pain. They have other medical and care needs. Nor do all patients receiving pain medication get them all the time, or are allowed the option to self-administer. So there can be a period of time when a vampire could feed yet not suffer the effects of drugs in the bloodstream.

                                                    I can definitely see certain vampires feeding on hospice care patients and losing Humanity. They see it as something to exploit. Most vampires would be there. But I also see certain vampires feeding on them and not losing Humanity at all. A very humane vampire who actually cares about the patients might even raise their humanity in certain situations. It all depends on the context of how the vampire treats them and his motivation for doing so. Now I think in practice, vampires won't be raising their Humanity at all, and the Beast could easily take over in that situation. Even reasons that were originally altruistic can easily become self-serving. But for those PCs who want to retain high Humanity, it should still be an option in this scenario.

                                                    So your scenario is plausible, but not the only one.
