                                            Chalk me up as another supporter of 'mobile Genius loci'. I never cared for the idea that she morphed into a living planet (it was slightly less absurd power-wise than what Tzimisce became) and it really upset the idea of any serious power struggle between the Antes (Ennoia would either just swallow them up where they were buried, including Tzim... or eat whatever Tzim hadn't grabbed up by their own nigh-absurd transformation.) I don't mind the idea of a living, city destroying earthquake (seems more in line with Ante power given Raveos AND it reminds me of Mr Oliver from the third Anita Blake novel.)

                                            The idea she's not some automatically some living planet also sets up some interesting narrative contrats with the Garou and Gaia that could play out any number of ways: Ennoia subsumes Gaia (ultimate atrocity), Gaia subsumes Ennoia (a possible way to redemption/Golconda, or just simply a way the Gangrel are ultimately eradicated) or something in between (a war between the spirit of earth and this vampiric interloper which can fall anywhere between those two extremes.) It adds an extra dimension to the larger Gehenna conflicts between the Antes themselves. .

                                                            Yeah, the Gangrel antediluvian as it appears in the Crucible of God scenario is basically a god that still sometimes concerns itself with vampire drama out of habit. Even with a more down-to-earth approach analogous to how the antediluvians during the Anarch revolt were depicted, this particular one is going to be one of the handful of most physically resilient beings on the planet, be able to shapeshift into any animal form she can conceive of, become air or the ground underfoot at will, neutralize the shapeshifting powers of any werewolves within her reach, and command all animal life for miles around at minimum. That ain't a fight that's going to work out well for anyone picking it with her.
