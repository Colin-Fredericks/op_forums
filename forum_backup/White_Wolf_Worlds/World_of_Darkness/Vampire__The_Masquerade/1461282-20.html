                                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                        The key to Paths for me is this phrase:

                                        "A Path will free you from the guilt and self-loathing of being a murderous predator. It will destroy your life in every other way."

                                        At my table the important part of Path followers is they're every bit as miserable and self-hating as other vampires. They just have it for different reasons. You can't escape the Curse of Caine.

                                        Yeah, that seems about right, or at least close to it. At the core of the game is the vampire fighting against the Beast. That's the eternal struggle that every vampire engages in every night, whether they're a shovelhead who crawled out of the grave thirty minutes ago or an Antediluvian. Being on a Path doesn't change that - vampires on Paths still struggle against their Beast. They still regret their mistakes and feel guilt for their failings. Of course their guilt and failings are different from vampires who still cling to their humanity but they're still there.

                                        A vampire on the Path of Death and Soul might feel incredibly guilty after killing someone, not for killing them but because they had to kill them in self-defense and then flee and didn't have the opportunity to properly observe their death. A vampire on the Path of Power and the Inner Voice might not feel bad for killing, but would feel a great deal of guilt about the time he allowed another to gain power over him. A vampire on the Path of Honorable Accord would feel himself losing to his Beast for breaking his word.

                                        Vampires on Paths still struggle, still feel guilt, still experience personal horror. They do so differently from humans but they've embraced the fact that they're not human. And any vampire on a Path is struggling. There's no "Path of Doing Whatever I Want Whenever I Want." Any vampire who tries that will quickly fall to their Beast. Any vampire player who tried to take a Path to sidestep problems wouldn't get very far since the ST would simply introduce scenarios that would be a problem for that Path follower.

                                        And all that isn't getting into the fact that some Paths are harder to follow than Humanity. And for many it isn't even all that much easier to to kill or such - in many Paths murder or casual killing are still in the Sin 1-4 range at around the same level as Humanity.

                                            Originally posted by Taggie View Post
                                            That would have been Chivalry, and it was one of the Humane Roads.
                                            Right. It’s probably the least “alien” of the paths in the modern setting, to the point that I could see certain actual mortal humans on that path. There are plenty of real world professions and cultures that place honor and duty ahead of compassion for others (which seems to be the core sin of Humanity).

                                            And I do think honor and duty probably better resist the Beast than compassion, because its right there in the word Com- (share) -Passion (powerful feelings). It’s a lot easier to flip a positive passion into a negative one and end up doing something horrible in the heat of passion than to move someone emotionally detached to act.

                                            Frankly, in the vampire corner of the WoD the only ones who are really safe on Humanity are ghouls, revenants and dhampirs precisely because they can’t lose that last dot. Similarly, if werewolves had to be on Humanity they’d wassail in a week… something like Path of Harmony would pretty much be required to keep them from becoming a mindless beast.

                                            As much as I prefer Humanity (there’s a reason I only play Dhampirs on the rare occasions I get to actually play) I don’t think “Humanity is for Humans” is even that hard of a sell for vampires. They’re objectively not human and so long as the alternative has some sort of alignment with the vampiric condition (some of the the paths are clearly more propaganda driven; ex. Typhon).

                                            My personal feeling is that “Harmony” is probably the most ‘natural’ for vampires as it embraces their predatory nature and even includes a practical morality that can appeal to former human psyche of avoiding killing humans (because a dead human can only be fed from once).

                                            Honorable Accord is probably next best as it has less issue with the violence that predation of humans will inevitably result in, and has better tools in its focus on duty and honor over feelings for resisting the passions of the Beast.

                                            Humanity is probably number three on my list, mainly because the general principles are natural to who the vampire used to be, making it a great “starter set” (and that is all some people actually need). However, I think it is, bar none, the STUPIDEST system of morality to hold onto in conjunction with the Masquerade; a system that requires you to feed in ways that violate others because allowing them full consent would be a Masquerade breach and which requires manipulating and even killing others in order to protect it if there is a breach.

                                            Humanity would work fantastically if vampires were allowed to assemble fully consensual human herds (it is pleasurable after all) or to buy blood (eliminating the theft aspect) from consenting mortals. But that’s a Masquerade mess and so the sect most likely to be ON Humanity is also the sect whose policies are the most corrosive TO Humanity.

                                                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post

                                                I'm not sure why "this book should have been front and center, but wasn't," means it gets an improved rating.
                                                It's a correction of a massive flaw in V5. I would not be the huge V5 fan if not for OPP fixing most of the problems but they couldn't handle the Sabbat issue. Now most of my issues with V5 are fixed.

                                                But, it's the 5th edition of an RPG, not a novel. Besides the fact that it's ridiculous to say not to judge something on what it isn't without any context (if I book sells itself as a horror story, and isn't scary, are you going to give it a high rating simply because the prose is still good, and ignore it failing to be what it says it is? And novels that exist as parts of a series get judged negatively all the time for things they lack when it comes to how they interact with the larger setting they're part of), it exists as an update to existing material into a new edition. It's not just there to be read, but to be used by people that want to have the Sabbat in their V5 games. RPG books are tools, not just coffee table books. A beautiful car that can't make it to the end of a block without breaking down should be judged for being a bad car however good it looks, because you're not just reviewing something with form, but with function.
                                                It's a case of where they said what the book was going to be at the start and didn't deviate from that. They said it was an antagonists book and never said otherwise or implied otherwise. So I think I have to judge it on the context of a Sabbat antagonist book versus a Sabbat player book.

                                                Which is... kinda useless as a reviewing system? "4.5/5 stars because I don't include flaws in my ratings," doesn't actually tell people how good the book is in total. What's the point of a review that isn't critical (in terms of thinking, not in terms of negativity) and only looks at the positives?
                                                The rating is just a shorthand for whether I liked the book or not and how much I enjoy it. The actual point of the review is the text.

                                                            Originally posted by Taggie View Post
                                                            So for me Sabbat didn't have Humanity at all until Revised forced it in because certain devs hate the Sabbat.....
                                                            For the same reason that not giving Paths in the V5 Sabbat book hurts the game, not giving Humanity in the Sabbat before Revised hurt the game in the past. It was one of the best decisions of Revised, among many dubious ones, because it increases options for Players and Storytellers.

                                                            Bringing out of the character the argument that vampires shouldn't exist on Humanity is simply stupid. Vampires are fictional characters that were human in their formation years in-character and played by humans out-of-character. It is perfectly possible to play on Humanity and have a successful character as many players can attest to it, and indeed it is impossible to even say what a truly inhuman mindset would even look like.

                                                            Originally posted by Chris24601 View Post
                                                            Imagine what the Camarilla might look like if it had determined, say, the ancestor of Honorable Accord* as it’s foundation that fledglings were indoctrinated in
                                                            They would fail.

                                                            Humanity isn't the primary Path because fledglings are indoctrinated on it. It is the primary Path because we live in a humanist society. EVERYONE is indoctrinated on it. It is in our education systems and media. Some parts of it are the natural wiring of our brains (like not killing another human). Most social norms, family values and religious teachings gravitate toward it even when they don't follow it to the letter.

                                                            So it is at least familiar to everyone and already natural to most. People that come from life already wired for another Path are a minority, sizable but a minority. So in this sense Humanity as a Path is a logical, pragmatic decision by the Camarilla. Going for anything else in modern society would be too ineffective as a strategy.

                                                            Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                                                            And for many it isn't even all that much easier to to kill or such - in many Paths murder or casual killing are still in the Sin 1-4 range at around the same level as Humanity.
                                                            That's another important aspect. Many Paths don't exactly let you ignore Humanity, they give you pretty narrow exception cases. You can do stuff when fulfilling the Path's obligations, not whenever you fancy it. And many are indeed harder, because they lack "accident" clauses, you're never allowed to do that no matter what. And even if you're low in the Path, the social pressure for you to follow the higher standards is far greater than in Humanity. You're not seen as evil, you're seen as weak, a failure, so you're pressed always into following at least the equivalent to 6 or 7 to the best of your ability.
