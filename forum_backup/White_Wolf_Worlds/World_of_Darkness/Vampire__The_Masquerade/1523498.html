    Lineages X Clans

    10-03-2023, 11:34 AM
    Some ask what is the difference between a clan and a lineage in VTM, I never stopped to think about that exactly, all I know is that as a general rule, clans are much more numerous and lineages, apart from perhaps the variants , you rarely find one, so much so that many kindred go centuries without ever seeing one and even seeing them as legends or rumors. That said, in your opinion, what differentiates a lineage from a clan?

      As far as we know, it's nothing but a social convention. Kindred are culturally attached to the idea of thirteen bloodlines due to their long history and probably due to the desires of methuselah, antediluvian and possibly other entities.

      One could say the rule is to have an antediluvian, and that the Giovanni and tremere took that position with diablerie, and that's certainly why in the middle ages the tremere were finally acknowledged as a clan.

      But that rule no longuer works whe' we see that salubri survivors are still mentioned as being part of a clan, and that the ravnos are still considered one despite losing their own (I don't count lasombra a'd tzimisce, since their antediluvian, according to their propaganda, were diablerized by one of their own).

      Also, the ventrue had for centuries the story that their founder was killed by the others.

      So in the end, it depends. One can say any bloodline will be acknowledge as a clan if they become proéminent enough, although very old vampires may only accept that if the total number of clan becomes 13

          Clan is a term used by vampires to describe the major groups of Cainites who share common characteristics passed on by the blood and more often than not are descended from an Antediluvian
          Lineage is the Kindred term for their direct blood relation to fellow clan members. The line of their sire and of their sire's Sire and so on. Many Fledgeling are expected to know and speak their line of Lineage back as far as it is known during introduction. With most clans, their Lineage often grants prestige and boons or opens doors that most newly created Kindred would not have a chance to go through early in their unlife.
          The older vampires used/uses it to depict what Clan a being may be from.
          Bloodline is a term used to describe a group of vampires who share a common lineage. They are basically a clan within a clan, but have some inherent distinction from the thirteen traditional clans of Caine, be it in their weaknesses, Disciplines, or both.

              My viewpoint is that clans are more numerous and have a respectable amount of influence over mortal and immortal society and can generally trace themselves back to a member of the 3rd generation and share common powers and weakness. Now you have some lineage variants within the clan such as Old Clan Tzmisce who don't have visscuitide as a core discipline but are very much like there parent clan or a mortal family that a clan likes to embrace from as they have been proven benifits to them. Or castes like Banu Haqim that have there own focus but otherwise don't differ all that much.

              Bloodlines are much smaller and exist on the fringes of mortal and immortal society and don't always trace themselves to a member of the 3rd generation such as the Nagaraja who got turned into Kindred without being directly embraced and seem that the most powerful signature Discipline level in the old setting was seven dots. Or the Ravnos and Salburi suffering such enormous losses that they will never really recover from.

                                      Originally posted by Lysander View Post

                                      That is a good question and I don't think if it was ever addressed by the creative teams and maybe we will get a answer of sorts soon.
                                      On the contrary, i expect them never to - bloodlines exist to represent an element of uncertainty in the world of the kindred, mystery & doubt amidst the supposed "facts" and "truths" of the lore.
                                      Where do they come from, how numerous or organized are they, what sort of agendas might such possess or not. They are the unusual pieces in the tableau of the Jyhad that not everybody, in fact almost nobody, has much of an idea of how are supposed to play out.

                                      - How many are fallen clans like the Cappadocians, Salubri or others less spoken of like (perhaps) the True Brujah?
                                      - How many of them are groups of mortals "ascended" into kindred-hood through secret arcane methods without the embrace like the Tremere, Nagaraja - and in at least one myth, the Assamites/Banu Haqim?
                                      - How many stories, admited to varying degrees, are there of a bloodline or lineage within a clan rising to full clan status through collective diablerie, like with Tremere, Giovanni and to a lesser degree, Brujah, Lasombra & Tzimisce? Should one expect similar treachery/ambition from groups like the Samedi, Daughters of Cacophony or Kyasid/Maeghar?

                                      How do they, or Gargoyles & Blood Brothers, fit or not in the equation, for that matter?


                                      Honestly, their point, imho, is how bloodlines relevance, individually or as a whole, is customizable according to the convenience of the individual table, to be tweaked in role, numbers and influence according to what ST - and players deciding to make characters belonging or connected to these 'lines - whims.


                                      In my own games bloodlines can be somewhat numerous, though in a haphazard, pulverized manner, where one has cells of individuals with the same "starting package" in different parts of the world, but those groups might claim different histories and origins, sometimes not even recognizing each other as "kin" when individuals from very divergent groups bump into each other.

                                      Like, for a quick example of ingame crypto-lore, a nordic Dokkalfar bumping on one of the Moon Eyed People outside of their Appalachian warrens, even though mechanically they would both simply count as Kyasid. And that's just the tip of the iceberg, when it comes to bloodlines. The slope is ever so slippery when it comes to lore, metaplot and what i'll hack apart for taste.
