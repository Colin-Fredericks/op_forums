    Just to be clear, I wasn't really making a suggestion as much as translating something that got lost when the V5 creators ported Loresheets from Weapons of the Gods to V5: that the individual perks you can buy from a Loresheet were coupled with extensive knowledge of the broader subject the perk is involved in. WotG was also far more narrative over mechanical for the perks which made the pricing of them easier: how big was the narrative impact going to be? Putting the minimum into, "Former member of a criminal organization" means you get a small perk that will help out in probably once every five or six game sessions. Putting in for a max perk makes that organization a major part of the story and whatever perk you have means they're going to be around - a lot - for possibly good and ill for the group.

    That said, it still had significant problems with balancing perks (even if you get away from a progression) simply because you were putting character resources to ensure narrative-significant results that might have been easier to achieve by just buying character traits instead. You could use a Loresheet to ensure your character finds their one true love... or just buy good social stats and romance a NPC your character develops rapport with.

    My actual suggestion for making Loresheets better would be to import the Lore rules from Exalted 3e, with Loresheets being areas you can introduce facts from, and investment in the loresheet increases how major the facts you can introduce are. This means instead of paying resources for good knowing of things and some perks that may or may not be worth it in the long run, you get something that you can always try to use with some creativity.

                                                07-06-2026, 02:47 PM
                                                Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                                I don't think this is a fair comparison and I wouldn't say ignoring sometimes is the same as being an optional rule. Optional rules are alternatives to how the system works, a rule that doesn't need to be enforced every time is context dependant.
                                                If a rule can be safely ignored most of the time, it's not really necessary. That's not the same as saying that if the circumstances the rule comes up are rare, then it should be optional.

                                                E.g., Humanity rolls don't occur every session (or don't have to), but they're still a core rule. If you can handwave Humanity rules because you don't feel like engaging with the system any more, then it's an optional system.

                                                I think Blood Resonance is more akin to the latter than the former.

                                                Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                                On the other side, freeform with scaling costs would generally cause problems, and in my experience for no meaningful gain. Freeform is the source of a lot of decision paralysis in my group, and until now I've seen no character concept that couldn't be built in it as is.

                                                With flat XP, which should have been standard, then freeform would have more benefits.
                                                Yes, I think we're in agreement. That's why I suggested flat XP costs, too.

                                                Freeform with scaling costs is a mess. But also, the system with scaling costs is already a mess because of Predator Types—RAW, they ignore the scaling costs. They can be fixed, as we've discussed, but it's just easier to use flat XP because then the base system can be used with greater flexibility.*

                                                Personally, I use the old Freebie Points costs, with a few tweaks: Clan Disciplines cost 5 per dot, others cost 7 per dot, secondary blood magic paths cost 4 per dot, Merits cost their rating, and alternate/combo-powers (I just call them Devotions) cost the same as in VTR2e: [sum of all dots/2], rounded down (min. 1). Dots 5+ cost twice as much. Most traits can only be increased once per story except with ST permission.

                                                "XP" gain is 1–3 points per session. If you want to make XP awards more granular (e.g., 1–5 per session), you can just double the XP cost relative to the freebie cost. So Attributes are 10 points per dot, Disciplines are 10/14, etc. So long as you keep the conversion strictly equal at all times (i.e., 1 freebie = 2 XP or whatever), it works fine. The problem comes in when 1 freebie equals a variable amount of XP.

                                                *I quite liked the option in the Guide to the Camarilla to build your character with 184 freebie points back in the day. Not for every character, and definitely not for new players, but if you want to make a niche concept. You just have to put limits in certain areas (the main two are: no more than 100 points in Attributes or 35 points in Disciplines).

                                                With these caps, characters have flexibility to invest in fewer Attributes and Disciplines to bump up their Abilities, or to go all in on Backgrounds, etc., but they can't go over the top on Attributes and powers.
