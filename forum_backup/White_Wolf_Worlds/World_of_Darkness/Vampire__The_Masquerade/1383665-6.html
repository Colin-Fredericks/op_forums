                    I don't like V5, but that really doesn't have anything to say about the topic at hand. Even if I did like V5, armour would still be useless to vampires. Wether or not I like V5 has no impact on the usefulness of armour in the setting.

                    It's quite clear, plain as day, that armour doesn't do anything. Let's look at the following scenario: I'll acount for V5 vampires not having the durable bodies of prior editions. Let's say there's Greg and Gale. Both are fit and have 3 stamina, but Gale is a vampire who possesses disciplines of relevance to this test. If we took a 12 guage shotgun loaded with buckshot and shot at them from three metres towards their centre of mass, and then compared them to alternate versions of themselves wearing NIJ rated IIIA armour, we'd should expect something like this

                    Greg the human shot without armour- Big fist sized spaghetti wound. Very dead.
                    Gale the Vampire shot without armour- Big fist sized Spaghetti wound. Very hurt but not about to meet final death. Probably stunned

                    Greg the human shot with armour- Hurt. Maybe a Broken rib. Probably stunned for a round or so.
                    Gale the vampire shot with armour- The armour absorbs the majority of the force and the remainder is not enough to bother someone who can withstand part of his chest turned into spaghetti.

                    But, mechanically, in v5 Gale suffers the same, spaghetti wound or not.
                    If we went medieval here, we could use a sword against a man in chain. If he wore chain, you wouldn't be able to cut through him. But whilst the mortal gets benefits from chain, again, the vampire gets nothing. Hacking an arm off is more difficult to do against an armoured greg, but no more difficult against an armoured gale.

                    In prior editions of the game. We'd have a conflict like such
                    Str+Potence+weapon+Carry over VS Stamina+Fortitude+armour. The carry over could be dealt proactively by defending, otherwise this was a rather even conflict. Infact neither stamina nor fortitude nor armour made the others redunandant: They would build on top of eachother in an entirely organic manner. But In V5 we have carry over+weapon or potence VS... Fortitude if you've got it.

                    Armour in V5 doesn't do it's job. It doesn't fit it's purpose. Steel and kevlar literally might as well be paper. I don't think it works great for mortals either, but it does still kinda work for them with the abstract combat system V5 has. But for vampires, it's a bad joke. And from a player standpoint, it's infuriating to have something work well for others but not for you solely because of someone's strange notions of balance.

Maybe the blood itself imperfectly replaces damaged tissue, as if the vampire immediately becomes part T-1000 without even rousing the blood. Regardless, vampire's superhuman soak can mean whatever the ST wants it to mean.

                                    Simply put, Kevlar armour and the like is designed to protect mortals from lethal damage. It is not designed to protect vampires from anything particularly - because vampires are clandestine, supernatural creatures and not at the forefront of the priorities for Kevlar manufacture in the mortal world. Vampires have their own supernatural defenses, and essentially make mortal-designed defenses redundant - because they are magic. If they wanted to find an alternative to mortal armour for themselves, the design would also most likely be magic. Magic trumps physics in a fictional world.

                                    Mortal armour isn’t particularly useful for zombies or ghosts either.

                                            OK, since we're still debating some core issues, anyone that would like to defend the RAW:

                                            Please explain why a mortal or a ghoul wearing riot armor vs. a mortal/ghoul with a baseball bat wearing street clothing is actually at a mechanical disadvantage.

                                            As it stands, riot armor, which is designed to protect police from frequently improvised weapons many of which would count as superficial damage even to mortals, has no impact against a baseball bat (superficial source of damage that it is) despite being designed specifically to combat such attacks.

                                            So armor vs. unarmored with equal weapons (a police baton vs. a bat) seems like a wash on equipment; which is already strange enough.

                                            However, riot armor, being the high end armor also has a -1 Dexterity dice pool penalty. So, wearing armor specifically meant to protect you from blunt trauma, in V5, impedes you instead of aids you, if you're a mortal/ghoul facing already superficial sources of blunt trauma. Mortals and ghouls are not vampires. They do not have the defense of magically powered corpse-bodies to explain this.

                                            As for as issues of relevance to the game: imagine how this plays out for the tactics of the Anarchs, Sabbat, and Second Inquisition.

                                            A Brujah Anarch that has the ability to rile up local unrest to the point of a full on riot isn't just creating a big mess that puts Camarilla assets at risk, that Anarch is generating an effective army that's mechanically better off than the riot police that show up to stop them. Unless the cops are going to start using lethal force, they're going to get steamrolled by the mechanically superior rioters. The Anarch can use the chaos of the rioters winning to torch Camarilla Havens and assets without the Camarilla having the resources of control over local mortals to help protect them. Or the Camarilla can pressure the police into turning their city into a war-zone, making it easier for the SI to infiltrate their city as the government puts civil liberties aside to reassert order.

                                            So, Anarchs stoking civil unrest, and things like Sabbat mass embrace tactics are more dangerous than ever, because mortal police forces got nerfed by way the armor rules work.

                                            If someone can present a logical reason why riot armor shouldn't protect a mortal from being punched/hit with a baseball bat/have a rock thrown at you/etc. please, present it.

                                                            Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                                            If someone can present a logical reason why riot armor shouldn't protect a mortal from being punched/hit with a baseball bat/have a rock thrown at you/etc. please, present it.
                                                            If the system is abstract, then Health levels lost through these means don’t necessarily mean a character is injured. If they are contained as superficial damage, then character will recover in the next session without any healing. It’s only when Health levels are exhausted that impediment and possible aggravated damage will be incurred - maybe because the armour is considered to be breached at this point.

                                                            If baseball bats or rocks are used proficiently, it could be argued that they can break bones and limbs, and are therefore possibly causing Aggravated damage - which would require medical attention and time to heal. As such, Riot armour would act to reduce this damage to superficial levels too. A straight punch would probably be rendered useless against riot armour, but the unarmed attack may not be a punch - but maybe a push or trip or something else. Again, the system is abstracted. What body armour does is protect the wearer from lethal, aggravated damage - not all forms of potential damage. If a riot officer was also using a large shield, as they often do, I’d also give them bonuses for blocking the attack.
