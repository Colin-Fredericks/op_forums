        Originally posted by Elphilm View Post
        I think the "traveler" component is also there to make the Ravnos foils for the Gangrel. Having two clans compete for the same resources prevents the Gangrel from fucking off forever from normal Kindred society and eking out an existence without competition. (The Lupines are another problem for nomadic vampires, but I run the Gangrel as having a "special relationship" with the werewolves... and the Ravnos stick to human groups even when traveling.)
        I handle things much the same way. The Ravnos exist - as a metagame concept - to cause competition and rivalry for the Gangrel outside the cities. So much of the clan's backstory is "your enemy are the Gangrel". So the clan needs a reason why characters are outside the city and potentially interacting with the Gangrel.

        I think the distinction between "Gangrel are hardy survivors in the wilderness" and therefore are able to handle Lupines (through various means), and "Ravnos surround themselves with humans which they exploit and then leave before it is too late" is good too. One is physically oriented, the other is socially oriented. One is generally known as straightforward and honorable, the other as deceptive and untrustworthy. Very different approaches despite the general idea that both groups move around a lot.

        Originally posted by Elphilm View Post
        Just something to think about. I get the impulse to make a clean break with the crass ethnic associations of the Ravnos, though.​
        The early game definitely overused gypsies. Gypsies were an obvious trope for the generic Überwald-type gothic horror stories of the past, They are found in the traditional horror literature and early horror movies, and they were definitely a strong influence on the game. So referencing it in some way makes sense. But using them so much treated the group much less like real people than if they were just a fictional concept. So the reaction against them was justifiable.

        Unfortunately, going the whole "Indian vampires" route didn't fix the root issue, it just transferred it to a different/larger ethnicity.

        I think a better solution would have been to just move beyond any specific ethnic group and stay with the nomadic exploiters archetype. There were always plenty of people who made their living by travelling a lot - transhumance pastoralists; merchants/salespeople; sailors; religious pilgrims; occupations on long haul trucks, trains, and airliners; highwaymen; migrant smugglers; livestock drovers; natural resource developers that exploit a natural resource and quickly move on; circus/carnival performers; perpetual vacationers/RVers; etc. Then the Roma just becomes one way this can be done, but there's plenty of others who can fit the same niche even in many different human cultures and historical eras.

        The only challenge is to explain why it is in the culture of the clan to always keep moving, and how long do Ravnos typically stay in an area before leaving. The idea of twig description that the clan has a culture of not respecting any of the traditions is something that definitely helps justify it. You just need to develop some detailed lore - probably involving the Ravnos antedeluvian and how Caine and the other antedeluvians treated him - to explain why that culture developed.
