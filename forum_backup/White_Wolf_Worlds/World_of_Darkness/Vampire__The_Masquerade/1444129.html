    A Fortitude power to represent how stakes work in "Fright Night"

    04-16-2021, 12:10 AM
    In "Fright Night", stakes paralyze rather slowly. Evil Ed takes over two minutes to "die", and almost gets the stake out before he does. Later, Dandridge is staked and just yoinks the stake out, though he is straining in a way that suggest he has been weakened, considering his previous feats of strength. At the end of the movie, Evil Ed is revealed to have survived, presumably because Peter Vincent pulled the stake out.

    So to represent this, how about this for a Fortitude power: instead of immediately paralyzing you, a stake causes you to lose 1 point of strength per round. Only once your strength drops to zero are you paralyzed.

              Fortitude 4, Stake Resistance
              When staked through the heart, the vampire may make a rouse check to resist paralyses. The vampire nonetheless loses one point of strength per round. If the vampire has not managed to remove the stake before reaching 0 strength, they are paralyzed as normal.

              Some SI have taken to using stakes lined with several large metal barbs or hooks after witnessing this power in action.

                              The Tomb of Dracula comic also had Rachel Van Helsing use a crossbow to stake Dracula as well. So there's other precedent in comics.

                              Of course, much depends on why you think staking works against vampires. It's an exercise not in logic, but magical thinking.

                              In traditional vampire lore, it wasn't just any wooden stake. It was one made of a certain kind of wood that possessed the correct magical properties. Usually it was a stake made of either ash or hawthorn. But in certain regions, alternate wood was used. And sometimes it was an entirely different means of impalement like a large iron nail or spike. Iron, of course, also being know for its ability to protect people against malevolent supernatural creatures and magic.

                              As far as I can tell, the reason for impalement is linked to the belief that by piercing a bloated body, it would let out the blood the vampire had consumed. Of course, we know the reason why dead bodies bloat now is because of the release of bacterial gasses, not because the corpse is feeding on the blood of the living. But that's the magical reasoning behind it. There's also the thinking that by staking the body, you are pinning the dead to the ground so they can't rise. That implies not just piercing the heart, but using a particularly long stake that also plunges through the body into the ground. And in some places, staking wasn't done through the heart, but the head, stomach, or elsewhere.

                              And sometimes it mattered how many times you hammered the stake into the heart.

                              So depending on what inspires you, there are numerous ways you may want to interpret staking and how it works. You can make it both harder and easier depending on what you want to use, and even combine them. Maybe a normal wooden stake can get pulled as the OP stated, but one of ash or hawthorn works as normal per the rules, but if you do use to not just pierce the heart, but actually drive it into the ground, the vampire is completely destroyed. Maybe the material of the impalement device needs to be magical and solely made of that one item without any other material "polluting" it and disappointing its magical properties. Or maybe any sharp thing will work as long as the heart is pierced. Folklore and legend are not consistent, but the game rules need to be. It's just a matter of what combination works for you.

                                          My favourite interpretation is that Blood is the "source" of a Vampire's power, and the Heart is the "core" of our circulatory system; "disrupting" the system by piercing the "core" (without fully destroying it, which would be Final Death, in VtM terms) basically stuns the Vampire until it can regenerate the damage, which can't be done as long as the Heart is still pierced by something. But by that logic, anything piercing the Heart can do the job. Probably a Hellsing influence. Definitely doesn't work in VtM, where the object in question must explicitly be wood. So there seems to be something "magical" going on, but broader than the very specific categories of wood that were believed to have spiritual powers in Medieval Europe.

I get where you're coming from, with the idea of getting weaker the longer it is in, but since it's a Fortitude power it seems more appropriate, and the idea that you're running out of "endurance" to keep fighting back seems just as appropriate as running out of "strength."
