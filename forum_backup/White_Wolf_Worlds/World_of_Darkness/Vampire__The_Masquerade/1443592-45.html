            Why would I pay money for an Edition similar to the one I just bought? An Edition needs heavy updates to justify its purchase.
            That's you. Lots of people buy new editions with a much lower bar for what needs changing than you seem to have. Look at how many people bought V20... and it barely changes anything. 1e to 2e, and 2e to Rev weren't some major overhauls either.

            And this isn't some WoD or WW specific thing, this is true of most RPGs that get a significant number of editions with the same company identity (which is, to be clear, a rarity in the broad view of things). Most new editions are incremental upgrades, not massive changes, unless there's some overriding factor like the new IP holders no longer having the rights to the old system (like Scion 2e).

            New people to existing games also tend to just by the newest edition unless they're warned away in some fashion. To new potential fans, big shake ups between editions are meaningless because they weren't invested in the previous material to care about changes. For them, a Clan changing allegiance isn't some huge new play experience, it's just setting history. For them it's the past, even if for older players it's a major change up, because they never played the other way.

                                Changes like the ones proposed by MyWifeIsScary could surely have been a good basis for a less controversial update: the resurgence of the Baali as a threat had also been widely prepared by Onyx Path with V20. I still consider all the rules changes introduced by V5 as positive, but that's a matter of tastes.

                                I feel a huge problem is that, while it is sensible that metaplot changes were presented in the Corebook, players would have tremendously benefited of the possibility of playing directly into those events: the controversial introductory adventure set in Berlin did a wonderful job, it's a pity it was buried under shitstorms over the pregen characters.

                                A series of "Fall of ..." adventures (Fall of Berlin presenting the rise of the Anarchs; Fall of Wien detailing the crumbling of the Pyramid; Fall of Mexico City or Montreal detailing the Sabbat Civil War, Fall of Venice with the birth of Hecata) would be / have been awesome, and helped people fall in love with new metaplot. Also, the Sabbat book should have come just after the two main splat books, or along with Chicago.

                                Also, a Translation Guide such as the one OPP gave us for Requiem-to-Masquerade and viceversa would have been useful.

                                Unfortunately, handling the license to Modiphius seems to have been a huge problem: "Fall of London" is really an outlier, in terms of atmosphere (dungeon crawling Ancillae escaping trenchcoat-and-katanas Inquisition agents, and fighting a God-Methuselah), and none of their other projects saw fruition.

                                I hope now that Justin and his team, working with OPP, can steer the course of this Edition for good: I really love what has been done so far, and I think it has huge potential.
