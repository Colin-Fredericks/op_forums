    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    The Followers of Set in Egypt alone are going to have people who interpret Set as a Protector of Ra's Barge, those who believe he's allied with Apophis, monotheists that believe Set to be the only God worth worshiping, Coptic Christianized Gnostics who associate him with Satan, Islamic Setites who view him as Iblis, Hellenized Setites who associate him with Gaia as well as the Greek God Typhon, and Roman ones that believe he's Ares. Now imagine that plenty of these don't much care for the idea that their version is inferior to one of the others.
    I have no doubt there are internal disputes within the Setites. However, I don't think those debates are anything like you are presenting.

    First, Set himself would have clarified any official story when he reestablished the clan in the 10th century BC after being driven out of Egypt, and since he seems to have been fairly active for the next thousand years, had plenty of opportunities to set the story straight. So most of the things theologians like to debate aren't the things the Setites might debate. You won't have esoteric debates about the importance of protecting Ra's Barge, or whether the Wyrm should really be worshipped, or the thoughts of Gnostic Christians or Hellenestic philosophers about the figure of Set. Most of that will be completed eliminated because they had Set himself explain things to his clan for a long time in the historical era. The fledgling who brings up Romans identifying Set with Mars will be quickly reminded that the Romans arrived nearly a thousand years after Set left Egypt, and their opinions are irrelevant because they are obviously wrong. (One of the neat things to do as an ST of the WoD is to determine the real, secret history of these things). Instead of sounding like an erudite theologian who's very knowledgeable, he'll be seen as an ignorant clown who needs to shut up and listen. A Setite who questions the story Set himself laid down won't survive long. (We're not told those stories since they're not things most gamers actually care about.)

    Second, there's not necessarily a problem with contradictory myths for a pagan style religion. Religious practice in the Classical World and earlier wasn't about arguing doctrine, eliminating contradictions in myths, or creating a coherent theology. It was about performing the correct rituals and rites of the religion. How do you perform correct sacrifices to the gods? How do you avoid religious miasma or pollution? What are the mysteries we don't reveal to outsiders? What things do you to avoid offending the divine? A lot of religion is still like that today especially in the non-monotheistic parts of the world. As long as the Setite religion follows those things that Set told his followers to do, things that may seem important to you from your religious background may be totally unimportant to Setite religion, and their cult adherents would be told that. And those that kept trying to force the religion to deal with issues that Set and the elders who followed him did not think important won't last long. People from a Western style religion that prizes things like theology will just have to unlearn those things in order to understand how the Setites follow their religion. That's why it is important to indoctrinate people into the cult before you embrace them. A mortal who doesn't "get" it, just gets passed over when time comes to hand out the goodies. You have a lot more trouble when your newly embraced vampire doesn't "get" it.

    Third, the longevity of the vampires and the clan structure would prevent most situations where the new guy taking over the religion hijacks things. In Scientology, once L. Ron Hubbard died, David Miscavige essentially performed a palace coup, eliminated his rivals, and later began re-editing Hubbard's texts or found new clarifications. Even he didn't make major changes, just enough to force people to buy all the books they already had again. That should be much harder to do in a vampire clan. There might still be elders around who remember Set before he disappeared in AD 33. If someone starts claiming things about Set that doesn't have overwhelming consensus, he'll be shut down quickly. Also, Miscavige could get away with a lot of shenanigans because there was relatively little other Scientology members could do. Most members had no say, and the relatively few who disagreed with Miscavige could be quietly and legally removed from power. That isn't possible when your other rivals for power can respond with unbelievable violence. You either have to risk civil war, or rule by some means of consensus. That consensus probably includes "don't make up shit." With Blood Sorcery and Auspex and all sort of other powers that the elders have, they can stay in direct communication with each other even in a pre-technological era. So some level of supervision should happen.

    This doesn't mean that the issues you talked about won't come up at all. But as a whole, I think the Setites would prevent or eliminate the vast majority of such cases. When needed, they would do so ruthlessly. That's why we don't have many canonical instances of heresies or apostates. They've been successfully suppressed. Sure, maybe this group or that group managed to survive for a while, but eventually the clan wipes them out. Who's to say after 200 years time even the Serpents of Light won't be extinguished?

    I think the things that will be disputed the most will be issues of enforcement and discipline (this person acted in a wrong way, how badly do we admonish or punish him; or she did a very good thing, how should she be rewarded?), and matters of politics (who gets to be in charge of this temple; the leader of the temple in this city wants to do this thing should the rest of us support him with resources, do nothing, or support this rival plan by this other temple leader there?). "How can we best serve Set now" is a valid question that can lead to disputes. As can "Which one of us will wield this newly discovered powerful artifact of Set, as only one can wear the Serpent Crown." Things like "what is the nature of Set" likely isn't because it is either a settled point, or considered irrelevant.

    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    As for the human factor, Followers of Set are subject to the exact same sort of longings that other vampires are and are going to be inclined to Embrace who they want when they want. Set embraced Kemintiri and that was a disaster but you can't really criticize your god. Hesha embraced Elizabeth because he was certain she was in love with him and events spiraled out of control. Maybe you're only getting permission to make a progeny NOW or they're terminally ill or you want them to be your inside vamp in another group. The Church encourages you to embrace only your hardened cultists for years as a reward but there's no rule to say you HAVE to and maybe you hold your followers in contempt (as most cult leaders do).
    This is where we disagree on the nature of the cult, or what the cult should be. I'm sure Setites embrace without permission just like any other vampire can. I'm also sure that the Setite elders ruthlessly punish those who do far worse than any Prince in the Camarilla because they don't have to worry about Justicars, Conclaves, Anarchs and what not. And furthermore, where does that illegally embraced Setite supposed to go? You can't exactly hide them from other Setites or claim it was some other Setite who embraced them when you meet up at the temple. Nor can you send them to safety to some random city far away from the rest of your clan, unless you don't mind them being Caitiff in some domain where Setites don't rule and who knows what will happen to them. That's essentially abandoning them. So I think this happens a lot less than you might think. It doesn't mean they can't happen, but I don't think it is any kind of routine problem.

    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    For me the Ministry is basically just what happened when you kicked the ball down the road a long time and doctrinal drift happened. The cults of Loki, Eris, Satan, and so on became more focused on their local variants than Set themselves. The lip service element is still there or was but the Church of Set decided to eliminate some "heretics" and, well, they realized that was a LOT more than they expected.
    This is what I disagree with. There should not be cults of Loki, Eris, Satan, and so on among the Followers. Those cults are for the mortals as a front to introduce real Set worship. If you run a proper cult, none of those people get embraced until farther down the line after they have been taught the "authentic" Set religion. That final group, carefully vetted and culled, is who you select for the embrace. And they know perfectly well it isn't some Loki or Eris or Damballah or whatever figure they originally believed in by that time, but the "real" Set they are now worshipping. There should be a lot less drift than you think. That's to prevent the entire scenario you are talking about. We can assume Set learned some things when he lost Egypt, and this is why he reorganized the clan.

    I am not saying such things are impossible and don't happen. I'm sure they do every now and again. But that is not the default of the clan. At least, I don't think it should.

    You and the developers of V5 are making certain assumptions, and I am making other assumptions. That's fine. We're all allowed to. I know you and many others see V5 as canon and therefore the truth from All High. But I see V5 as essentially a form of fan fiction. There may be good forms of fan fiction or pastiche, and bad forms. But no matter how one may like the Conan stories of L. Sprague De Camp and Lin Carter, they're not REH's Conan. Same thing for all the many stories about Sherlock Holmes written by someone who is not Doyle. Even if in some cases they were approved by the literary estate and therefore "official". I put V5 in the same category. Revised probably needs to be put there as well.
