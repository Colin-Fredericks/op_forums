    Rethinking backgrounds.

    02-02-2022, 03:45 PM

    1 Generation is taken too often
    Conventional thinking currently promotes Generation and Resources above all other backgrounds; Resources is stronger than Generation, but easier to obtain and often not desirable for "street level" games or for characters that want to get their hands dirty and don't want to solve all their problems with money (plebeians!) . Most of the benefits of Generation come with Dots 4 and above, Thus, on tables with some level of powergaming, we have many 8th gen and 9th gen fledglings running around, which is strange considering these generations are recommended for elders and most American* cities don't have scores of 7th gen elders (I find Games are disproportionately likely to be set in the Americas, probably because messier politics makes for better Vampire games)

    Generation without the power to back it up should be a trap. Without a powerful mentor supporting the vampire, a neonate with low generation should be an ever more attractive tonic in the eyes of Ancillae and high generation elders who are feeling the limits of their blood. It'd be all to easy to scheme against such neonates . Rumours of an 8th generation neonate ought to attract Sabbat packs to the city.

    2: Mentor probably isn't as important as it should be.
    Maybe I've been reading too many trashy chinese webnovels but they've really made me rethink how family and master/disciple relationships should work in Drama. As written, a Mentor in Vampire is someone who'll occasionally get you out of trouble in return for you doing services for them. V20 even goes on to cut down on the background and states that the more powerful the mentor the less care they'll give you. Perhaps anything more intimate than that would disrupt the young vs old drama VtM's got going on, but is that likely? I don't think it is. A Sire is a vampiric parent. A Childe is, at the very least, an investment, and protecting your Childer should, at the very least, be a matter of Face. A sire who can't protect their childer looks weak. It's very easy for a sire to foster a positive relationship with their childer, and it's very smart for a Childe to foster a positive relationship with their sire. Bad relationships exist of course, but you can simulate that by... not taking the background (and perhaps taking a few flaws)
    Vampires should carefully consider the might and disposition of the vampire's backing when in conflict with another vampire. If an intruder in the Domain has little-to-no backing, they can be killed no problem, it'll be a footnote in Elysium. If the intruder has a mentor that could be pose problems, It would be considered diplomatically acceptable to stake the intruder and send her home. If the Mentor is stronger? You should definitely be opening dialogue before getting physical. If the mentor is terrifying? Bend over backwards and treat them like a royal guest. (of course, your own backing is worth factoring in the equation)

    3: Status as literal titles doesn't work well.
    5 dots for Prince, 2 dots for sherrif? This implies some sort of universal structure that I don't think is meant to be there.
    Status would really work better as a level of respect in a specific kindred community. Having plenty of positive relationships, being seen as trustworthy, displaying usefulness and loyalty to the ideals of the sect, being in favour with the people who matter, being relevant at the moment. Since players are reckless by vampire standards, this should be bouncing up and down like a yoyo. Of course, people remember the things you've done wrong

    4 Some backgrounds simply cost too much at character creation for what they are.
    Arsenal is simply "resources but worse" (it works when you want to be well equiped but having 3 or more resources isn't proletarian enough for you). I think Arsenal might work well if each dot costed half a dot. Alternate Identity and Fame are also annoying backgrounds where I may want AltID or feel my concept obliges me to take fame, but they're simply of significantly less value-per dot than something like resources, allies, herd or contacts. If players could get twice as many of these lesser backgrounds, they'd be more inclined to invest and diversify


    5: Backgrounds above 3 should be discouraged at character creation
    (at least the ones that scale exponentially, like resources. lots of dots in Contacts is a great tool for STs and I wouldn't get in the way of a player who wants that)
    I am on the fence: a cap at 3, or increased costs for higher levels at character creation. Go fight about it I'm really interested in what people think. Maybe the first three powers cost 1, the 4th level costs 3, the 5th level costs 6. I'm just spitballing here I haven't calculated what'd be the best numbers for it, it's late.)

    Why?
    1: It encourages more diversity and better rounded characters at character creation. If you can't put all your dots in -clearly the best choice- then you'll have to actually think through lesser options. Going wide rather than deep leads to better rounded characters at character creation
    2: It gives players something to work towards.
    2: Balance: the 4th and 5th dots are often gamechangers. Gen 1-3 and Resources 1-3 don't mean that much for vampires, but these backgrounds at the 4th and 5th level are stronger than high level disciplines,

      A while back (yeash, at least 15+ years ago now), I decided to try to simplify my Backgrounds as much as possible. Part of this was capping everything at 5, with the optional "I am master of this" paragon level similar to that of the original Adventure! RPG. The other was narrowing it down to just 10 backgrounds. Exactly which 10 varied slightly from game to game, but for Vampire they were:
      • Allies
      • Contacts
      • Influence
      • Resources
      • Servants
      • Status
      • Haven
      • Herd
      • Patron
      • Arcane Object
      Influence is entirely one's pull among the mortal/mundane world. Status is one's general position in the city's Kindred pecking order (which may or may not come with a title). Servants are Retainers, just with the way the relationship works made more explicit. Patron is essentially Mentor but with the caveat that there is a constant expectation of quid pro quo involved. Arcane Objects are just something I tend to make use of in my games, typically unique antiques or the like which derive power from Kindred vitae or human blood. (Most of them would probably be considered cursed.)
      I stopped using Generation, largely because I felt it sucked a lot of mystery out of the setting, and by just using the Blood Potency set up from Requiem, it muddled the waters regarding just where vampires come from and how long they've been around.
      Overall, I had pretty good results from it.

          Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
          1 Generation is taken too often
          In my experience, it's either taken at the highest levels, or is used to dump a few remaining leftover points into. Characters that intend to use Dominate are much more likely to take many ranks in Generation than those that don't. Physical oriented characters often don't take it, likely because those players aren't thinking of the benefits of spending blood to boost physical traits. It can be easily overlooked. But if you have one of those playing groups that min/max a lot, then yes it is taken way too often. I haven't had that problem too much in my tabletop group though. In any LARP or online game though, I always see some kind of restriction or limit imposed because so many players in those groups do want those advantages because it isn't something that can improved by XP.

          Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
          2: Mentor probably isn't as important as it should be.
          In general I find any of the NPC dependent Backgrounds are neglected because most Players don't want to put into time coming up with good concepts for Allies, Contacts, or even Herd. Their imagination is limited to their own PC. Mentor is usually a ST/PC collaboration because players don't know enough of the chronicle setting to do it on their own, but even here they usually don't have any ideas.

          Most players seem to think of Background solely in the prescribed mechanical benefit described, as opposed to the possible benefit from roleplaying with a Mentor (often their Sire, but not always so). I think this is mainly a function of what the player focuses on and the STing style of the ST. Depending on the ST, it could either be incredibly useful or annoying.

          You're right, but the game has never meant it to be a universal structure. It is just giving examples. In the first two corebooks when they presented Gary, Modius only had Status 3 despite being a Prince, and Lucian had Status 5 despite not having any title at all.

          I disagree. It really depends on the player and character. There are lots of good (beginning player) concepts out there that would not only justify a rank of 4 or 5, but even require it.

                  Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                  1 Generation is taken too often
                  Couldn't agree more. Rules as written it's an auto 5 freebies; not only is it amazingly powerful, but it can't be purchased with XP. I don't blame players for being predictable and loading up on generation... they're only responding to overwhelming incentives.

                  I got rid of Generation entirely, using a system we developed for our MUSH in 1999 (because Generation sucked back then, too) called "Sanguinis" (rated on a scale of 1-12, with 1 being weak) which is similar to what Requiem later used as "Blood Potency." But you can't buy it; it goes up with time or diablerie. Your default Sanguinis is 1. It's possible, with GM approval, to spend 12 Freebies on the "True Childe" trait, which gives you a Sanguinis of one less of your sire's at whatever time you were embraced. That still might not be that much, although because it requires collaboration with the GM it's likely that the sire won't be a Sanguinis 2 neonate.

                  Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                  2: Mentor probably isn't as important as it should be.
                  That's a playstyle issue more than a mechanics one, but I agree with that, too. It's up to the GM to work on this and make it worthwhile.... much like social attributes, empathy rolls, etc.

                  I actually made one of the Tremere bonuses in my game 2 free dots in Mentor

                  Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                  3: Status as literal titles doesn't work well.
                  I agree with Black Fox that this isn't really meant to be taken literally -- but I also agree with your point as well. My solution is to affirmatively separate Titles and Domain control into a separate trait called "Station". Station is your *official* standing, while Status is your *social* standing (with the Camarilla and aligned vampires).

                  Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                  4 Some backgrounds simply cost too much at character creation for what they are.
                  This once again is a matter of playstyle. Fame can actually be quite potent if the GM is feeling jiggy. If a character has Fame that's relevant to something they are trying to do, I'll just have them add their Fame score to their Charisma+Intimidation (or whatever) straight up. I don't know what Arsenal is, but it sounds stupid. Sort of like Haven.

                  I think Identities is actually pretty fucking cool. I don't know what the RAW is on that background -- maybe it sucks like you say -- but I play it that every new Identity comes fully documented, with birth certificates, SS cards, passports, credit ratings/history, employment histories, credit lines, etc. In a modern game, that can be gold.

                  The point is: be a ruthless GM. Rip out the crap that seems extraneous, beef up the stuff that seems too weak, and nerf the stuff that seems overpowered. I know I probably don't need to tell you that, but it is The Way and probably can't be said too many times.

                  Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                  5: Backgrounds above 3 should be discouraged at character creation
                  Completely agree. I actually carry this further and discourage ANYTHING above 3 in Chargen (except attributes and maybe one or two abilities at most). But I'm a hardass GM in Chargen. Once play starts I'm pretty lax, but I like to get off on the right foot. Character concepts need to be really well-developed, really tight, and reflected on the sheet. You have a 5 in Medicine? Where are the Fame dots and Medical Influence dots? How did you get to be a world-class surgeon without becoming an integral part of the medical community?

                  In the end, though, I completely agree with your overall take that there are SEVERE problems with Backgrounds -- a lot more than you indicated (although you did not say your list was exhaustive). It's the sloppiest-put-together part of the game... like they just threw it in at the last minute because they were tired of sketching out rules and systems.

                  But I also think that it's an opportunity The answer (at least in my case) was to completely overhaul the system. Like No One of Consequence, I ended up with 10 core backgrounds:
                  • Cult (a very, very flexible background that works like a discount combo of resources, henchman, and influence)
                  • Fame
                  • Herd (Super potent in my games)
                  • Henchmen (Disposable and REPLACEABLE sub-retainers)
                  • Identities (1 full identity per dot)
                  • Languages (straight up 2 language per dot, total fluency)
                  • Library (useful for rituals and occult research, which is usually at a premium in my games)
                  • Mentor (Again, completely agree that this needs to be put into practice with style and vigor)
                  • Resources (My scale is a little broader than RAW -- I sometimes feel like the game designers didn't understand how wealth works.)
                  • Retainers (Dots get you "Retainer Points" which get spent through an algorithm to generate your retainer stats)

                  There are also all the status backgrounds: Status, Sabbat Esteem, Anarch Reputation, and various Clan Prestiges.

                  Then there's four special discipline-related Backgrounds: Affinities (for Animalism), Maskings (for use with Obfuscate disguises; couple with Identities for extra goodness), Emotions (for certain Presence powers), and Rituals (for Thaumaturgy -- ONLY taken at Chargen, as it just gives some extra starting rituals).

                  Finally, there's Influence, which is a whole separate subset of Backgrounds... 18 different spheres of Influence. In my game, Influence is much more powerful (although obviously more limited by sphere) -- it can be used for effecting changes, getting information, sending out feelers as to what other people in the sphere are up to, etc. Like Fame, it can just be straight up added to a roll where appropriate. The spheres are: Academic, Artistic, Church, Civil, Criminal, Education, Financial, Legal, Media, Medical, Military, Occult, Police, Political, Society, Street, Transportation, Union.

                  By both focusing/narrowing Influence AND making it more powerful, I feel like I've empowered the players' autonomy a little: they KNOW more or less the sorts of things that they are capable of doing, and their influence is in the sorts of things that their character is interested in. As written, Influence is so vague and nebulous that players either try to use it for EVERYTHING or they just sort of forget it's on their sheet. It's like Hades said -- they have to be fleshed out. But you can do that in great part with MECHANICS; you don't have to have twelve-hour long preludes. You want players actively thinking of ways to use their capabilities -- you want coteries to be capable of a wide range of flexible responses to situations.

                      The first thing I did was remove Generation as a background… instead the player attainable levels were all trade-offs (Gen 7 and lower were unmitigated power-ups without trade-offs so drinking your way up is still desirable). Gen 10 was basically the default, higher generations got more human-ish traits (more nutrition from cold/animal blood, easier blush of health, able to hold down food longer) and reduced clan weaknesses. Gen 9 got more potent clan weaknesses (ex. +/-3 instead of +/-2) and Gen 8 had that and +1 difficulty to virtue tests.

                      The second thing was nuking Resources as a distinct background. Most people I’ve played with treat it as a super background… to buy influence, to hire retainers, to buy havens, etc. So if you want to be wealthy you instead buy a mix if influence, domain/haven, retainers, etc. reflecting their particular mix of wealth. To reflect physical things like nice cars, fancy clothes, etc. I added “Possessions” which could be due to wealth, but might also be stolen, or “borrowed” via dominate, etc.

                                                      The main issue with backgrounds is that they are too abstract for many types of games, see any discussion regarding Resources.

                                                      I think the simplest way to make Backgrounds more functional is to take a page from the design of 2nd edition Exalted and its Manse background. In the core book you have the basic background which gives you a magical building that generates a hearthstone, the stone gives you a magical benefit and helps restores motes (a refilling blood pack in V:tM terms). If that is all you want a Manse to be, then you can play it as such.

                                                      Alternatively, a book was released that greatly expanded the meaning of the background. You still use the core book system to buy a Manse background at 1 to 5 points in value, but you then had a point buy system to build a custom manse where every dot in manse gave you two points to buy characteristics for the manse.

                                                      The same sort of system applied to V:tM backgrounds would give an optional system for people who want more complexity if they desire it, but it wouldn't negate the core book background system for those that prefer simplicity.

                                                      This would work well for Backgrounds like Allies, Alternate Identity, Contacts, Domain, Fame, Herd, Influence, Mentor, Resources, Retainers, and Status. But it wouldn't work as well for Black Hand Membership, Generation, or Rituals.

                                                      The main issue with doing this sort of expansion is that it breaks the Background Pooling function. But when you really look into how Pooling Backgrounds work, it becomes apparent that it was intended to be a way for starter characters to have cooler things and falls apart with late game builds or characters who went ham on backgrounds at character generation. So a revised pooling backgrounds mechanic is kind of needed as is, regardless if you add the expanded point buy system.
