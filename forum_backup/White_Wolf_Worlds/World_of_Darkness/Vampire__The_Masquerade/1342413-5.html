                                                            Originally posted by Chris24601 View Post
                                                            The take I use in my campaign is it was mostly just a case of mutual disinterest. The Gangrel had little use for anything going on in the city and the Camarilla had zero interest in expending resources protecting the territory the Gangrels actually cared about.
                                                            l.
                                                            I don't think it works like that. At all. Before we even get into the issue of "disinterest"
                                                            The Camarilla doesn't expend resources to protect one another. The Camarilla exists (on paper) only to protect vampires from mortals (and to protect old vampires from young vampires through appearing to moderate powers of each.)
                                                            There's no sect-wide proclamation that they need to defend eachother's territories. That's up to individual princes and gangrel to work out.

                                                            Now. "disinterest"
                                                            That's dangerous thinking.

                                                            To butcher some financial terms, Vampires need "Confidence" to work with one another. Near every vampire is a blood sucking sociopath, and near every vampire will implicate their peers if they were ever to be brought down by mortals. Prestation is an investment; you can only work with characters you trust, and you can only have trust with people bound by the same rules as you. Once you have that, Both parties can build up credit and collatoral and deepen their bonds: If you owe someone, you become an investment that they need to protect. If someone owes you, and they're well embeded within the system, then you have an asset.

                                                            The Gangrel leaving the Cam creates a lot of issues in this virtuous cycle for the Gangrel.
                                                            First, they're not bound by the same rules as you. The chance of someone reneging on an arrangement has skyrocketed. Even if they don't go back on their word, the value of each favour is undermined by the increased likelihood of refusing to honour an arrangement.
                                                            Second, they're outsiders, so you're doing a favour for them by even talking to them. A Gangrel has to look up to every Camarilla member they do a favour for. Nobody wants that kind of social deficit. The help of an outsider is more often than not less valuable than an insider. Questions on "why are you going to Gary the Gangrel rather than Terry the Toreador" will really harm your prestige.
                                                            Lastly, all this prevents the build up of credit and collateral. The Gangrel no longer has the confidence to build up a valuable domain near Camarilla territory; the Prince can decide the Gangrel's a risk and take back everything it's ever given to the Gangrel for the good of the sect. Because of this, the Gangrel is going to have even less collateral, so the Gangrel can't be trusted because they don't have as much to lose; You're not going to give a blood sucking sociopath a loan of a million dollary-doos if he only has land worth 10k and no steady job. The risk is too great.

                                                            The Gangrel leaving the Cam is only bad for the Gangrel. There are no positives.
