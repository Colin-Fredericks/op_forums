                Chapter Twenty-Seven: A Brief History of Beckett

                It should be noted that Beckett's exclusion from the Clan Novels was something that some fans were rather annoyed by. In a series that included everyone and everything, his absence was conspicuous. Some people felt Ramona was a poor substitute for the popular adventurer archaeologist even as I felt the Signature Characters were front-loaded with way too many Elders as is.

                Well, Beckett got his revenge by being the star of the Victorian Vampire Trilogy that I have not read because I hated London by Night. I feel regretful of that now and if Crossroad Press gets the contract for reviving the rest of the White Wolf catalog, then I'll definitely read them. It certainly seems to have the kind of sexy melodrama that I enjoy. Unfortunately, this chapter doesn't actually give a good sense of what happened and feels very disjointed.

                It opens with a romantic letter by Emma Blake that reads like a suicide note (because it is) and eventually she dies in Beckett's arms but we don't know about this because it's not specified. It does give us the sense that Beckett seduced her for less than romantic reasons, though.

                We also get a story about how Emma Blake's death was faked (she became a Tremere). Then a letter between Victoria Ash and Beckett over Emma's personal articles. Victoria Embraced Regina Blake, Emma's mortal daughter, and had a "love that dare not speak its name" in the Victorian Era with her. Beckett didn't appreciate the ultimatum.

                Unrelated to the fact Beckett once carried on an affair with the World's Nicest Revenant/Tremere is a letter from Aristotle that reveals his library has been broken into and a bunch of tremendously fantastic works have been vandalized in somewhat ridiculous as well as humorous ways. Forgeries and copies in different (dead) languages for example.

                There's a slip of paper where Beckett talks about how a woman, Mary Elizabeth had her life ruined in some unexpected way by Beckett due to her resemblance to Emma Blake. He blamed Vykos for it but, apparently, she wasn't involved.

                There's also a Freudian analysis of the vampire species that feels a bit out of place here.

                Finally, there's a chat log with Beckett's brother Atieno and Marie who apparently has an actual network of spies as well as contacts.

                Beckett expresses a longing for Anatole and says he misses him, which would imply Anatole was dead but that's since been retconned I believe. I suppose it still works with their "breakup" after Jerusalem.

                Thoughts

                There's not actually much here.

                * Beckett knew and loved a woman named Emma who committed suicide
                * Beckett studied with Freud and wrote a really useless psycho-sexual text (vampires are dead, so they have no reproductive urges)
                * Beckett knows who his sire is but isn't telling
                * Beckett Embraced Marie who is still hunting and screwing with him

                Most of this stuff is either too personal to be relevant or not particularly that interesting, IMHO. It also lacks context that would have provided some of it for or a way to follow up for it.

                We're in the final stretch now.

                    This is arguably the weakest chapter in the book even if it is not actually a bad chapter. Simply put, I do not find Beckett terribly interesting on his own. I do not find his origin, or the compulsions that drive him to be terribly interesting. If it makes sense, his actions are more interesting than Beckett himself.

                    It might be a relatively weak chapter, but that is in comparison to the other chapters. There are interesting elements to this chapter. For one, I cannot help but read the acrimonious correspondence between Beckett and Ash here, and then think about their oh-so polite meeting back in Chapter Seven – The Anarch Freefall. That scene, which otherwise felt fairly straight forewords, now reads as two people who dislike each other giving fake gins through clinched jaws.

                    The section on the Freudian analysis and stages is amusing. I will argue it does provide insight into Beckett. The Gangrel Noddist tries to be scholar and a rationalist. Over the years, he had probably encountered and embraced (for lack of a better term) theories about almost anything that might help to explain his situation. One such theory would be Freud’s development stages. When human society eventually rejected the theory, Beckett probably did as well. That said, he probably did not actually post it on-line. I am not certain he possesses the technical proficiency to do so, even if he once wrote the vampire-version of the theory. At a guess, Marie posted the material.

                    If we are bringing Freudian analysis into this to, we should remember these are vampires we are talking about. They do not so much struggle with an Oedipus Complex as an Edible Complex. They eat people, and vampires eat each other.

                    On the other hand, the material about Emma Blake does not move me. I stopped reading White Wolf novels ages ago. I read some of the early anthologies and found them to be good to decent. Then I read some of the novels and found them to be terrible and a horrible waste of my time. By this I mean, I also never read the Victorian Age novels. I get that some of the ideas here, of Beckett’s great-lost love, and that does help to shape his nature. His is simply another version of the wanderer, fated to be forever alone, fated to forever to pursue a goal he will probably not achieve. He is also tormented perpetually by his own great sin, the embrace of Marie. Perhaps unfortunately, I do not care and am not moved.

                    I do not think Anatole is correct – Beckett is not seeking “…to evolve into something else, some higher power.” Anatole might be seeking that. Beckett, however, is seeking an answer to the “why” of vampire existence and his own existence. BJD eventually gives an answer to that question.

                    As an aside, I thought Christina Rossetti was a reference to a VtM character, but she was actually a Victorian era British poet I had forgotten about. She wrote Goblin Market, a deeply sexual poem.

                    The out-of-character portion at the end of the chapter does provide a nice number of plot hooks.

                            Chapter Twenty Eight: The Drowning of Rasputin



                            The penultimate chapter of BJD and one where we finally get some answers on the South American Kindred. I was initially skeptical of the Drowned because I always felt that vampire needed less new sects, not more, but then again I've always run them as archetypes rather than European-centric clans. But that ship, she has sailed, and the Drowned are a fascinating new collection of rivals that avoid the orientalism of the Wan Kuei.

                            Basically, it starts with Beckett investigating the Telyavelic Tremere that apparently now includes Rasputin (or he's just trying to wipe them out). He was beaten to the punch by Vykos and said Tzimisce tortured as well as mutilated two local Primogen. We get a short biography of Raputin, who was a 1st/2nd Edition character that kind of was a ridiculous joke between Clan Books. He was multiple Clans and even a Tradition or two with the final "answer" apparently being that he was a Wraith with high Possession that was possessing various vampires. He is an evil mastermind that manipulates, kills, and screws with Kindred left and right.

                            I've never been entirely comfortable with adapting historical figures to Vampire: The Masquerade because it can often be disrespectful to actual history. Rasputin, in particular, is a figure that I actually have long felt was maligned by history. Indeed, I'm one of the supporters of his actual canonization (not that the Eastern Orthodox Church has any reason to listen to me) and that his murder was motivated by pure malevolent intent among the Royalist conspirators. Oddly, I think the most fair treatment of the frequently villified figure is by the late great Sir Alan Rickman who portrayed the man was a sexually active alcoholic but very spiritual holy man.

                            https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasput...ant_of_Destiny

                            Not much of that here.

                            Beckett proceeds to track down Vykos to Rio, which supposedly had an Archbishop as well as Prince. There's apparently a Wild Hunt-esque hunt of visiting Kindred, which Beckett decides to opt out of by sleeping in the ground. We also get the first of the new Drowned legacies in the Karai Pyhare who are a seductive race that, nevertheless, are never-nudes as their weaknesses.

                            Beckett observes that the Sabbat and Camarilla share the space harmoniously with Lucita pointing out the Camarilla control the poor districts while the Sabbat are in high society. Given what complete assholes the government of Brazil has proven to be according to my friends there, I'm not sure if that's a complement or insult.

                            There's one part of the chapter that doesn't make any sense where Beckett attempts to talk to someone in English while they're confused due to speaking in Portuguese. Beckett may be an idiot and a moron in some social situations but in no way, shape, or form is he an Ugly Englishman and would never try to interrogate people without speaking the local language. We also get the Lostundo who are shapechangers with either Protean, Obfuscate, or both. They cannot use their powers in the havens or homes of another, though, unless they have drunk from a resident.

                            Finally, Beckett meets with Vykos who is back to something resembling their original form which I may or may believe (I prefer Tilda Swinton for Vykos). They exchange some reasonable pleasantries. Vykos has taken up the Son of the Dracul's cause of slaying Kupala. There's also Vykos putting down how she tossed her genitals at Hardestadt during the Convention of Thorns. They admit, even while alive, they didn't think much of them.

                            Apparently, there are Kindred disappearing all over South America at the hands of the Drowned, which Vykos initially assumed was the work of hunters. This is another thing I can assume will tie into V5. The Sabbat were tremendously weakened by what was implied to be the Second Inquisition (and we know they've since hit them hard in Montreal) but it's also possible that this is after the Drowned did a number on them. So that, at least goes a long way of explaining why the Sabbat went from hero to zero seemingly overnight.

                            We get a third Bloodline in the Cipactli who have the Serpentis version of Protean (technically Crocodile) and are huge gluttonous vampires who engage in ritualistic mass murder on a regular basis that seems like it would have smashed the Masquerade quickly. Rasputin has left a few letters that highlight how afraid they are of the Drowned and how he's been chased out of Eastern Europe. There's a fourth bloodline detailed called the Kalku, who are very good at Dominate and have Blood Sorcery too. Rasputin has been writing increasingly frantic letters to The Way. which reveals that he's been a servant of the Inconnu the entire time or more specifically the Master of Ravens. Note: It seems I'm mistaken and Rasputin actually has always served the Drowned. The Way being a bunch of Andean Drowned that have been manipulating him. We get a fifth Drowned called the Unhudo who resembled desiccated corpses that can defy gravity and have Potence among other powers.

                            Finally, Beckett and Vykos catch up to Rasputin, who has been working for the Drowned since they captured him. We find out Rasputin is an even bigger son-of-a-bitch than most Kindred as he's been massacring members of the Bloodlines because the Way desires the extermination of all but the original 13 Clans descendant from the 2nd generation. Honestly, this seems to be more a Tal'Mah'Ra thing and they have Bloodlines too with the Nagaraja being their Euthanatos founders. Beckett is incensed by this and I don't blame him because of all the reasons to exterminate vampires, racism has got to be the absolute dumbest.

                            Rasputin proceeds to say the Drowned Legacies get their names from being drowned in blood, which is a metaphor I actually don't think works very well. I think something tied to the Biblical Flood would have worked better. However, their basic story is when the Europeans came and destroyed the cultures of the Americas, they decided to go to ground and pass themselves off as locals. They've been building their strength up ever since and now are striking back to form their own sect.

                            Honestly, I find the idea that the Drowned could keep themselves secret from other vampires only slightly more than the idea that vampires could keep themselves from humans and the Second Inquisition could keep themselves from vampires. However, the game is about such secrets and it fits thematically into the Masquerade.

                            Titlacauan is a final Drowned bloodline that has powers over Mirrors. It caps off a fairly decent collection of interesting Bloodlines and I hope we eventually get full write-ups for all of these guys (and similar Bloodlines for the Wan Kuei).

                            Beckett and Vykos dump Rasputin in the ocean with a stake in his heart and then depart, fully expecting the Drowned to rescue him.

                            Adventures

                            I'm a fan of the Drowned concept and rather than just review them as a whole, I'm going to say that I appreciate how they were introduced in the book as a whole. The Drowned were not merely pulled forth, full grown Athena-style from this chapter but were alluded to at the start of the book before finally revealing them in their entirety. I approve that bit of magician-style theatrics and say it made the Drowned's final appearance feel all the more natural.

                            I also appreciate the way they're treated in the Adventure section, which doesn't just present them as the Yuuzhan Vong (double SW points if you get that reference). Rasputin says he expected a bunch of tribal warriors and people with bones in their nose that Vykos mocks as stupid as well as racist. They're modern South Americans as well as ancients, including people of differing religious beliefs including some who also believe in the Book of Nod. One of my favorite lines of this chapter was, "With people who claim to be Mithras and Sutekh, why is Caine the eldest among you?"

                            It also addresses the fact that Vykos is trying to find the Telyavelic Tremere so that he can harness their extensive knowledge of Eastern European demonology (not necessarily infernalism but not necessarily NOT infernalism) to help Kupala in order to free the Tzimisce. Frankly, while I give Dracula 25 out of a 100 odds on killing Kupala since the latter tricked the former into freeing him then feeding him the sacrifice of dozens of children during the Transylvania Chronicles, I'm actually more inclined to give Vykos better odds in killing or banishing Kupala.

                            My favorite part of this section is probably the inclusion of Mictlantecuhtli as one of the Drowned. I've always been a fan of Diablerie: Mexico's "Final Boss" (I wouldn't use the term villain) and would be interested in seeing him have potentially survived. Perhaps by having substituted a fake body the same way Tiamat did in her book. Either that or just ruling that, canonically, a 4th generation vampire newly risen from torpor was able to make short work of a bunch of diablerist Neonate Anarchs.

                            As Nerissa Blackwater shows, Drowned might be Beckoned....right back to the Americas.

                            I hate the treatment of Rasputin in the book because, well, I find the Hellboy-esque villification of the guy something that makes Hollywood History of a very fascinating real-life figure that plays into both Czarist as well as Bolshevik propaganda about the man. The Nostalgia Chick did a similar joke with Rasputin calling in about how it was bullshit that he got treated the way he did in Anastasia by Don Bluth. The Rasputin in this chapter has nothing to do with the historical figure and could have been any Kindred ranging from Khalid to Leopold.

                            Thoughts

                            I really liked this chapter and think it's one of the strongest for introducting an entire new faction to the world of Vampire: The Masquerade.

                                I came to the same conclusion fairly early as well. Instead, I apply the "Johnny Fontaine Rule" in my chronicles/setting. Any important historical figure that the PCs may interact with, and thus affect, (or NPCs based on real figures) will get a thinly veiled analogue instead, much like how historical figures in Mario Puzo's The Godfather or James Clavell's Asian Saga novels are treated. In fact, I've ripped off quite a lot of names from those works (as well as other historical fiction works) for use directly in my WoD games. This gives me the benefit of having the same "feel" as the real person, but complete freedom to do whatever I want.

                                        This is one of my favourite chapters, even if (I agree with @CTPhipps) they do Rasputin a dirty.

                                        Beckett is pursuing the Telyavelic after he gets a tip from House Carna. It is not a tip about House Carna – meaning Beckett is in communication with the renegades. To hazard a guess, this occurs after his recovery of the Book of the Grave War. Bindusara turned around and sold the book, but then supplied Beckett with a copy. Beckett in turn provided another copy to House Carna. House Carna repaid this with the tip about the Telyavelic Tremere. The question of why Beckett was seeking the Telyavelic in the first place remains unanswered. The chapter does come in two sections, one in Poland and a subsequent section in South America. In terms of the timeline, the Poland chapter might occur near the Russian chapter – the nations share a boarder – but that linking would do strange things to the timeline.

                                        The situation, in terms of the Camarilla and the Sabbat, in Rio de Janeiro is interesting – it is neatly reversed from expectations. I wonder how the fan supplement Rio de Janeiro Noturno.

                                        Vykos

                                        According to the White Wolf wiki, Vykos’s "original" chosen form had a saturnine (gloomy in temperament or appearance; somber; taciturn) aspect with pale skin, long black hair, and large hazel eyes. This is distinct from how the character would be depicted for the next 800 or so year. It is interesting to speculate on how much of that was Vykos, and how much of that was Dracon and the Tzimisce founder. It is also notable that Vykos is arguably on “best behaviour” here, in so far as the character refrains from torturing Beckett to death.

                                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                        …I'm actually more inclined to give Vykos better odds in killing or banishing Kupala.
                                        I would like to have followed Vykos around as he came to this idea, after the events in Istanbul.

                                        Legacies

                                        I am almost fascinated by the Drowned Legacies. I have always preferred the setting to be dangerous on multiple levels for vampires. It is not simply other Cainites that power a danger with their violent politics, but there are other things. Out there… in the dark. And they do not like your characters. The setting, and the nature of its many terrible dangerous, should always be in motion. The Legacies nicely fulfil this narrative notch. My only disappointment here is we do not see any North American Legacies. But we can always make our own.

                                        Rasputin

                                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                        I've never been entirely comfortable with adapting historical figures to Vampire: The Masquerade because it can often be disrespectful to actual history. Rasputin, in particular, is a figure that I actually have long felt was maligned by history.
                                        They can be used, I feel, but with a light touch and rather sparingly. The idea of Capone as a vampire, for example, is not a bad one. So long as he does not take over the game, because the game should be about the PCs.

                                        And Rasputin’s posthumous celebrity status is odd in any case – I wonder where it comes from. The story about him, and his death, exceed the reality by a considerable degree. Yes, he is an interesting figure. But how pop culture chooses people to pseudo-canonize (George Washington, Thomas Edison, etc.), and the opposite (Dracula, Rasputin, etc.), is terribly interesting to me.

                                        Also, he never had anything to do with South America, so finding him down there and working for the Legacies strains my suspension of disbelief.

                                        That said, I am alright with the idea of a lot of Rasputin running around. Actually, the idea amuses me. It feel like a nice way of resolving the issue of multiple Rasputins appearing in the original World of Darkness due to an editorial error which became a running gag. What is less appealing is Rasputin being a coward, which feel both oddly insulting and reduces the characters usefulness.

                                                Chapter Twenty-Nine: The Omega part 1

                                                We're here. I was going to try to draw this out but there's plenty of subjects to talk about inside the book so there's no point. Besides, completing a big read through like this is something that we should all be proud of. The fact it was done in a little under two months is also doubly impressive.

                                                Anywho, thanks for everyone who has participated and I hope we can discuss the material for months to come.

                                                ====

                                                We open in Albertus Magnus's auction for items of interest among the Kindred. This is something that is probably doubly galling for Beckett as it seems very likely that Albertus didn't keep his copy of the Book of the Grave War but auctioned it off on the Kindred equivalent of Ebay. Indeed, from my read through, we never do find out if Beckett ever got a copy of the book or whether it was an enormous "Shaggy Dog" story where he scoured the globe, acquired a copy, and then lost it.

                                                Beckett complains about the taste of fellow collectors where he points out a draft copy of the Anarchs' Status Perfectus went for $900 and a love letter by Victoria Ash went for ten times that amount. I'm a huge fan of Anarchs but I find that reasonable since it has a multitude of potential uses up to and including vampire porn, blackmail material, a tragic keepsake, and potential seduction advice.

                                                Interestingly, Beckett has apparently made friends with Serenna as she is the person who clued him into the auction along with Mister Nobody. I wonder if this is a reference to Grant Morrison's Doom Patrol that recently used him (as played by the eternally funny Alan Tudyk) as their DC streaming service show's central villain. Some of the items on sale are Lodin's Penthouse, poetry of Helena, and Samuel Haight's ballcap. We also have the book listed, missing its second half. Which indicates that either Albertus defaced it or Marie never had a complete copy to begin with (or defacted it herself because she's a witch that way).

                                                There's no sign Beckett got his copy of the book back (I doubt Albertus Magnus cares that he purchased stolen goods--as Jack Sparrow would say if he was a vampire, "Vampire."). However, he does acquire the Sword of Null, which was the overpowered super-artifact from Diablerie: Britain. It is also linked to the demon that Tiamat was possessed by. So, in fact, Beckett has acquired his own version of the Ainkurn Sword.



                                                The irony, of course, is that Beckett has absolutely no use for the Sword of Null because he's a "slash and claw" Gangrel build. In Tabletop RPG terms (what other terms are there?), he is a Brawl centric killing machine that has no use for a Melee weapon. Even if that weren't the case, Dylan Bruce wants the sword and will kill anyone else who has it. Personally, I would give the sword as a gift to Karsh (assuming Beckett didn't murder him in this continuity), Hardestadt, Christof, Karl Schrekt (make up for that horrific betrayal), or even Vitel. Sure, it's a bit demon-haunted but that's how Kindred gifts work. Ailsing says to tell people he dumped the sword in the Mediterranean--note: not actually do it--just tell people he did.

                                                Beckett gets chased out of the Middle East by the Tal'Mah'Ra who have officially graduated to being those guys after Indiana Jones in The Last Crusade. He gets on a boat to Naples but leaves Serenna behind because the protection of the Ashirra doesn't extend to Europe. Beckett mentions he's become exceptionally fond of her and even mentions that vampires can have feeling--which is either a very subdued way of saying he's attracted to her or literally that he hasn't felt anything for almost any of his other associates through the entire book. I wouldn't be surprised if it's a little of column A and a little of column B.

                                                Apparently, there are cities all across the globe where the Anarchs are taking over. This is not entirely due to Anarch competence but a mass revolt of Revenants and Ghouls. Beckett finds a cult of them that are planning to exterminate all vampires not of the Second City line, which works out for Beckett as he's of the Beast Clan. If I were to assume who was responsible for this, I'd say the Tal'Mah'Ra again because their Revenants are independent this way but also Gehenna cultists.

                                                The Rise of the AnarchsTM here could tie to their later emergence as the 2nd Most Powerful Sect in V5.
                                                Beckett talks with Maginardo and gets some perspective on the fact that the ghouls are tearing up the world. Honestly, I think this plotline would have been better for the Thin Bloods rather than ghouls even if there's a lot more Revenants than their used to be in canon. Originally, it was just a weird Tzimisce thing but now we have Giovanni, Tal'Mah'Ra, and soon possibly Ventrue or Malkavian.

                                                From there, Beckett attends the Bloodline Ball (which isn't actually it's name but might as well be) where there's everyone from Ahrimane to Baali and Daughters of Cacophony. The party is hosted by the Stoneman, the world's oldest Caitiff that I've always been intrigued by but we've never actually gotten any information on beyond that he exists and was referenced in Outcasts (an amazing book).

                                                The Bloodlines talk about how they were responsible for the Blood Curse and have been waging a covert war against the Kindred aristocracy for years. They then talk about how they're going to empower the Society of Leopold to strike at the Sabbat. Beckett panics over this, though, to which the Bloodlines use the "Just Joking" defense. Beckett was, honestly, incredibly stupid here and should have kept his mouth shut to learn more--but then he wouldn't be Beckett.

                                                That's when a group of Alastors crash the party and they are a BUNCH of people, including one who is implied to be Lady Gaga.

                                                Huh.

                                                    Chapter Twenty-Nine: The Omega part 2

                                                    The Bounty Hunters could easily have earned their own chapter as they're the perfect gang of weirdos and miscreants the PCs could have to murder if they have a friend on the Red List or negotiate with OR compete with in hunting down a Red Lister. They've acquired multiple trophies, including a Japanese schoolgirl in Stockholm. Which I confess I'd like to know what her story was.

                                                    They're a bunch of assholes and are looking for Kemintiri who my players were certain they once killed but who managed to survive despite a fairly awesome plan that involved anti-material rifles, Helena, exploding planes, and super-charged Pavis of Foul Presence magic amulets to prevent presence from affecting them.



                                                    I admit, I don't like Kemintiri despite being the most interesting of the Red List by far and she's one of those characters I hope meets the Final Death in an upcoming supplement. Blame the fact that I was one of those players (I wasn't STing that time). However, I certainly don't want these dorks doing it and don't believe they'd be anything other than an inconvenience for her to dispose of. Kemintiri doesn't even bother here and just slips Beckett a note before departing because that's what you do when you have Obfuscate 9. The note directs Beckett to visit Castel D'Ombro and while Lucita refuses to take him, Kemintiri's name forces her to agree to make arrangements as she wants to make sure she doesn't get in.

                                                    Beckett must dig up something Kemintiri has found or she'll publish his journals throughout the Cainite world--Beckett wonders which of his friends might have betrayed the diary's location to her and the answer is, of course, "Everyone." Note: It was Anatole. Beckett also finds out he's on the short list for the Red List that we see includes Carna, Marcus Vitel, Dante Rosselini, Stoneman, and Lizette Cordoba (from "Children of the Revolution"). I think the latter was especially silly as she's not even out of Neonate status and thinking about starting to persecute the poor to make them tasty meals. It also lists the Master of Ravens and that he's been denied by the Inner Circle--mysterious.

                                                    Beckett gets permission to dig in an area and then there's a hilarious collection of vampires gathered together to do it: Aisling, Lucita, Ramona, Vykos, Beckett, Theo Bell, Talley, Khalil, Hesha, Fatima, and Okulus. It's easily the most ridiculous and awesome scene in the book, IMHO. Just watching every one of these people snarking at each other is hilarious. I'm genuinely confused about why Khalil is there but the reference to Elizabeth Dimitrios is awesome, though.

                                                    "Still bitter about your love childe?"

                                                    Then they find the tomb of Enoch of the 2nd Generation. Which everyone immediately takes as actually his sarcophagus. Honestly, I kind of regret my Noddist wasn't there as he'd be extremely upset because the 2nd Generation are supposed to be DEAD gosh darnit. (dramatic irony) If they're not dead then whose to say all the other Antediluvians aren't (dramatic irony).

                                                    This causes a panic among the diggers but there's a lot of interesting ways to imagine this might have impacted certain characters. The existence of Enosh, for example, would utterly destroy Hesha's worldview for example. He is an orthodox (if liberal) Typhonist so the proof-positive that Enoch exists repudiates the idea of Set as the First Vampire.

                                                    It may not prove the Noddist interpretation of vampirism as correct but it means the Follower of Set interpretation is (probably) wrong. Even if Keminitiri expected to find Sutekh here, it actually does a pretty good job of potentially fucking up the FoS better than her creation of the Children of Osiris (who used to control this area). You could even say that Hesha's rumors about this object could have led to the weakening of the Typhonists and helped contribute to the Ministry's creation. You know, with the fact the Fire Court was being a bunch of assholes even by the standards of an amazingly assholish bunch of assholes.

                                                    The chapter also states it could be Set and Enoch are the same, Set was sealed in Enoch's tomb, and other possibilities.
                                                    Beckett is notably afraid Aisling and Isabel would try to eat Enoch but, frankly, I'm surprised Talley didn't try to. They decide to remove the contents of the box but Beckett will, whatever the case, not remember what was in them when things go to hell. There's many possibilities for what Beckett found but it's kept mysterious and that was probably a wise decision.

                                                    Lucita gets a favor from Montano to take Beckett to the Castle of Lasombra and there's apparently a vast meeting there of Keepers. This is an interesting tidbit because Lucita is supposedly a member of the Sabbat now, if not its Regent. Given what she discovers here, it'll be interesting if it destroys her faith in the Black Hand or renews it (she already figured out that Lasombra was alive in the Trilogy that had her murdering orphan children). There's a good conversation where Beckett has decided he's going to probably meet the Final Death soon and when he does, he wants his diary distributed everywhere in the world among Kindred to strike at his tormentors.

                                                    Lucita says this is stupid and will screw over his few remaining friends.

                                                    The final part of the story involves Dylan Bruce and a gang of about six or seven Red Listers showing up including Petanique to go after the Sarcophagus. Dylan Bruce's presence there is interesting because he's an Infernalist and Petanique is the world's biggest anti-Infernalist crusader. I wonder if the presumed presence of Set was enough to dissuade her from killing him or if the Red Listers, like all vampires, are enormous hypocrites. Anyway, they open the sarcophagus and a bunch of Alastors pop out with more than they bargained for. It then goes from bad to worse as the MOTHERF***ING LASOMBRA ANTEDILUVIAN is awakened by all the bloodshed, going on a rampage throughout the castle filled with what's implied to be hundreds of Lasombra witnesses.

                                                    All of this is told via Anatole, who has acquired Beckett's Jyhad Diary and will see to its dissemination.

                                                    DUN DUN DUN

                                                    Adventures and Thoughts

                                                    This is easily the most important chapter in the book and its interesting to speculate on all the various things that were thrown at the wall for the grand finale. There's enough here for three chapters worth of material and I congratulate the author for saving the best for last. There's a good amount of stuff that can and does change the whole of V:TM's setting whiel also leaving the fate of certain characters up to the ST.

                                                    Kemintiri and many of the most powerful Red Listers may have met their own. Beckett and Lucita could have also joined the ranks of those destroyed. It's implied Lucita, at least, survived the events of the Lasombra antediluvian's rise since it's implied Talley may have destroyed her later. However, everyone else's fate is up in the air.

                                                    I've long pointed to this event as the best explanation for why the Lasombra would defect to the Camarilla. Face to face with the failure of Gratiano and the sheer power of the Antediluvian, it means that the Lasombra would be aware their plan to fight them is not only doomed but utterly pointless. Submission or servitude to the monster may be the only way to survive, which might lead them to the Camarilla in a fit of irony (dismissing them as tools of the Ancients for as long as they have).

                                                    I also mention that Hesha discovering Enosh's location could lead to a massive change in the Followers of Set or at least weakening of the traditionalist Typhonist view. Like the Web of Knives, much of their power depends on Set being a different sort of Antediluvian than the other "Aeons." If he's actually just another vampire then that would strengthen the others tremendously. It might even result in Kemintiri gaining her own Ancestor Cult (as perhaps detailed in Cults of the Blood Gods, the Children of Osiris are nothing more than her's).

                                                        Part of the fun of this very fun thread has been seeing how other readers present alternative ways of connecting the book’s dots.

                                                        I had missed the Lady Gaga reference entirely, for example. and look forward to rereading this chapter later today to see if I can find the clue that she might be one of the Alastors.

                                                        The in-text gossip blog snippet from a Nosferatu Antitribu named Pangbourne* suggests that he doesn’t attribute the recent attacks on havens to ghouls or revenants, but to the Tal’Mahe’Ra.** He mentions vampires using disciplines that he hasn’t seen before (True Brujah and Nagaraja, perhaps?), and assumes that they’re acting in the service of something powerful that’s about to awaken in the Middle East.

                                                        *Pangbourne’s given biography in this book has him as the childe of Brother Kanker, one of the three difficult bosses that players in the “Bloodlines” video game had to face in order to resolve the disease outbreak quest and remove the CDC from the city. The “plague spreading group of Cainites” that Pangbourne is rumored to be associated with are likely to be the Brotherhood of the Ninth Circle, the plague cult that Kanker belonged to. Given that Pangbourne titles himself a “Devoted Brother” at the end of his blog entry, there might be something to the supposed rumors that he’s a member, too. All of this is a lot to cram into a tiny one-paragraph bio for a throwaway character, and it’s a terrific example of what a high volume of Easter egg content this book manages to sneak in there just by hinting.

                                                        **If the Tal’Mahe’Ra were really the ones who chased Beckett after the auction, it may have had nothing to do with the demon-possesed sword he’d recently acquired and everything to do with the rumors that Beckett belongs to the Mnemosyne bloodline, which sets up his invite to the ball.
