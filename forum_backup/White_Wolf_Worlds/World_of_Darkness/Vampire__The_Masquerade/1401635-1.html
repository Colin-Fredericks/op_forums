                So, I don't know enough about D20, so this might be a bit different there.

                That said, I'd say a lot of things depend on how long the PCs stick around, who exactly the werewolves are, how organized the local werewolves are, and how stupid/suicidal the player characters are.

                So, what tribes of werewolves live in the area? If they're Silent Striders, then the characters are probably going to die. They almost certainly have someone who can do the proper rite (Descent Into the Underworld in main WtA) to transfer over into the Underworld and just straight up ask the kinfolk's ghosts who killed them. "I was murdered by vampires before I could warn my werewolf kin" is definitely some very valid "unfinished business".

                If they're someone a little less mystically canny, the characters might have a little time. All tribes probably have someone on hand who can talk to spirits, but spirits aren't all-seeing and all-knowing either. Other mystically in-tune tribes like Uktena and Wendigo have likely set spirits to watch their kinfolk, but others may or may not be more reactive and take longer to figure out the kinfolk are dead. Depends on how far ranging the local packs are and who exactly is checking in on kinfolk.

                If/when the werewolves figure out what the situation is and that it was vampires, this is likely a TPK. Werewolves aren't stupid and know enough about vampires to attack at a time to their advantage - as you say, during the day they're basically just corpses waiting for the sun. If the coterie is stupid enough to hang around where they murdered a family of kinfollk, and they know who they killed... yeah, "My sire told me to stay here so I'm staying here!" shouldn't be their response. Even if they're all full blood-bonded, at least one of them should be making the rationalization of "... but I'm also pretty sure my sire probably wants me to live" at this point. If not... TPK.

                One thing that could happen is a local vampire, likely a Gangrel, showing up mad and demanding that the coterie move off before the werewolves just straight up murder everyone. This might give them some motivation to depart and make it clear to them that this isn't a "but my sire said!" sort of moment.

                If that fails, give the characters a chance to roll some "this is suicidally stupid" checks. If THAT fails, give the players an OOC "this is suicidally stupid" talk.

                        Some things I'm surprised people have missed.

                        An impressive body of water (Lake, Raging river, Sea) is an excellent escape from lupines: The local garou are unlikely to have "spirit of the fish" and probably don't have the sensory powers for dark and murky waters. Sure, the players will end up leaving their retinue at the mercy of enraged lupines, but the players can probably survive this way.

                        If possible, the players could take the remaining kinfolk hostage.

                        The players might stand a fighting chance if they Ghouled all or most of their retinue. A defensive formation of 15 ghouls and a combat monster vampire should be able to hold off three or four fosterns assuming these people are reasonably competent. Perhaps your other players can use this defence to try their own tricks.


                        If you want to laugh at the players; They find a silver mine, one of the NPC party members develops true faith and prays away the lupines, one of the party knows some hearth lore and recognizes some plants to keep the lupines away....

                                        Originally posted by Pleiades View Post
                                        I'm wondering about those spirits of inanimate objects and plants,
                                        are they from our side of the gauntlet or are they still on the other side?

                                        their description in VtM makes it sound like they're on our side
                                        All spirits are in the Penumbra on the other side of the Gauntlet unless they specifically happen to Materialize in the Realm. Spirits present in the Realm are always the exception, never the rule. (Normally in Vampire which does not usually deal with spirit things, I would not nitpick and simply let the ST do whatever they want. But if the ST asks how it is "supposed" to operate, I'm going to tell her that.)

                                        And most inanimate objects and plants do not have spirits that are currently awake. They are usually in slumber, and a Garou would need to first wake them up with the Rite of Spirit Awakening. Needless to say, if the spirit was in slumber during that time, they have can have no knowledge of what happened regardless of what powers they have.

                                        And while the Charm Realm Sense can allow some spirits to sense what transpires in its domain in the Realm, it is a power that must be actively used. It is just a way to allow spirits to remotely do Peeking instead of being directly there. In other words, unless the spirit was using that power at the time these events occurred, it won't know what was happening. The most they can do is use it now and have access to the exact same information the Garou would when they stumble across it later. They can't use it to time travel back to the past and see what happened. They would need some sort of postcognitive ability, and that is not a standard power.

                                        The ST can arrange all sorts of things in order to have a spirit present and using its powers to sense the place at that exact time. It's not the most likely coincidence at any random place, but it could happen. But since the ST is looking for ways to give the PCs an out, why would he want to rule that way?
