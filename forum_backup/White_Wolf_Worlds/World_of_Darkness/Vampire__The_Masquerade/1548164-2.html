        Originally posted by Lysander View Post
        I'm quite impressed with all the world building you put in.
        I always overprepare my chronicles because I'm actually not very good at winging things when PCs do something unexpected (which happens a lot). It is not that I don't have ideas, but I tend to freeze while I figure out if that actually makes sense. However, if I figure out how things work beforehand (at least in a general sense), then it is far easier for me to decide that things will happen this way or that way once the PCs do something. Other STs are much better than me on winging things out of the blue and still having it make sense.

        I also like having a sandbox style game in the sense that the PCs can do things other than just follow my plot hook for the night or a medium/long term plot. If the PCs suddenly decide to go here and screw around, it helps if I know what might already be there.

        The other advantage it gives me is that lots of little story seeds/plot ideas that I write down. Whenever things die down and players are looking for something else, I can go to my list and quickly pull the next hook for the next game session.

        I don't think Los Angeles By Night is a very good sourcebook, but when combined with other sourcebooks, there is a lot I can mine from it. I don't mind keeping names the same and building on certain ideas, but some characters and concepts are going to be different than how they are depicted in LABN.

            Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
            Curiously enough, I was trying to work out where to post some thoughts on Ms. Kearney (without disrupting this thread) before seeing these suggestions: one of my key sources for LA BY NIGHT is an online read-through and commentary on the book, and at least one of the reviewers expressed confusion regarding her actual character, as well as her motives for wishing to become a Camarilla Prince and not an Anarch Baron.
            I am only going by what is written in the original Los Angeles By Night sourcebook as I dislike much of what was developed in Revised. Like most character descriptions in LABN, we're actually not given much information about the character. Most of it is just filler geared towards immediate needs. Unlike the stronger sourcebooks, we're given little motivation and detailed backstories for most characters and have to create our own details if we want them to be real characters.

            Tara isn't even given a last name in LABN. We know she was born in 1794 and embraced in 1822 so she's a product of early American independence. It makes sense that she'd be a product of the American frontier, but in her mortal days this probably meant Tennessee or similar, and she worked her way over to Texas by the 1940s.

            She's described as slightly overweight which is surprising for someone in her mid-twenties from that era. People worked hard back then and unlikely to have extra weight so young. So I imagine her as a more stocky, hardy frontier type. Probably with some weight gain from pregnancy, but hardly "overweight". Unusually for LABN, she's only average-looking. The author really liked female characters who were extremely attractive in their mid-to-late teens. So I just interpret that to mean the author didn't want Tara to be hot because she's likely one of the antagonists, and he wouldn't want PCs to take her side because of her attractiveness.

            By the time of the 1944 revolt, Tara was supposed to be a "well known anarch from Texas" so she already had a reputation. She arrived with "13 Brujah fighters, many of them veterans from the American Civil War." I'm sure part of that was some of her brood. I imagine Tara took part in the struggle against the Southern Lords and any Ventrue or Toreador Princes who ruled any of the southern cities.

            Tara lead the anarch attack on San Diego. LABN says she claimed the area as her own in "the first land grab" so she's actually the very first "baron." The other anarchs on the Revolutionary Council (that ruled the Free States until the Status Prefectus abolished it in 1945 or so) were "too busy" to argue so she got away with it. So her desire to rule her own plot of land was very early.

            Strangely, it mentions Tara arrived with the Prince of San Diego (someone named Maria) already destroyed. We're not told by who. Local anarchs who destroyed the Prince, but were destroyed by the surviving elders? Lupines? Sabbat? Hunters? In any case, being right next door to the Sabbat in Mexico would be an incentive for someone to want to hold real power. Being a much smaller domain, San Diego anarchs don't have the luxury of the immense population of the LA metro area.

            One thing I am leaning to is the idea of having "Tijuana Anarchs" and Tijuana, Mexico being a battleground between Anarchs and Sabbat. Tijuana is part of the same metro area as San Diego. Actively fighting the Sabbat there would help protect San Diego proper. And it provides another incentive for Tara to cooperate with certain Camarilla officials. She needs help protecting her domain, and the anarchs in LA proper just aren't contributing enough. I can see someone becoming resentful because of that.

            Tremere Clanbook, Revised gives us Joseph Ravenfeather, a Tremere of Indian ancestry who studied Indian "shamanism" and may have been the only Tremere in San Diego prior to the revolt. He quickly worked with Tara and gained acceptance. The character description isn't very good or coherent once you start looking at its details, but it's a good enough to work with. So I'm going to use him as another Kindred in San Diego, and someone whom Tara can rely on against the Sabbat. He straddles the fence between anarch and Camarilla loyalist, but is a conduit to his "superiors" in the Tremere.

                As a note, I tend to get the gist of various characters from that online ‘Let’s Read’ of LA BY NIGHT mentioned earlier and biographical details from the WHITE WOLF WIKI articles - assuming the characters are lucky enough to get an article to themselves, which not all do.


                https://whitewolf.fandom.com/wiki/Tara_Kearney


                As it happens, Prince Tara Kearney does, so I was able to build my mental image of the character from there and the article includes some interesting details, like the fact that she was Prince of El Paso until the Civil War - apparently building a following from other neonates, though implicitly building up a brood of her own as well - that she declined to take sides between the North & the South (Resulting in her brood scattering to follow their own opinion); that she moved to Dallas during the conflict but was unable to establish herself as Prince or in any other position of prominence there (Purportedly for being too junior, though one wonders if quitting her own city and lacking a coterie of her own might also have played a part in this - that the local Kindred kept her under scrutiny is potentially revealing); having been forced to abandon El Paso due as much to economic factors, she proceeded to familiarise herself with economics to make good her previous weaknesses in this area; that almost a century after her brood scattered she was able to call them together once again and move on San Diego with this following (Though it took a coup and her own counter-coup to consolidate this conquest).

                All of this seems to be cromulent: it’s also interesting to note that her sire, one Justin Davies, was an 8th Generation Brujah active in Texas circa the 1820s. Given he shares the same generation and Clan as Jeremy MacNeil, one could semi-plausibly suggest a connection between two of the more prominent Brujah in the Free States (One that would lend an especially interesting dynamic to LA/San Diego relations).


                - Working from your own list of facts established in LA BY NIGHT, it seems reasonable to deduce that Tara was of Irish descent (Tara being the seat of Ireland’s high king, as well as the place of their acclamation) - it’s possible that the name was adopted at a later date, but the fact she chose it all suggests it has some significance for her.

                - I definitely agree with the notion of Tara as more ‘human average’ than ‘overweight’ (Given this was a description penned by somebody working from the ‘liquid diet for a hundred plus years’ standards of Vampire Romance), hence my mental image of the character as played by Ms. Kathy Bates.

                - It seems plausible to suggest that Tara may have targeted San Diego precisely because it lacked a Prince at that point in time (She strikes me as more than shrewd enough to present herself as more a peacemaker than a conqueror and to prefer a headless enemy): whether it lacked a Prince because the first stage of the Anarch Revolt had already done her in or perhaps because she suffered some Sabbat-Anarch related misfortune - persuading Don Sebastian of LA to grow steadily more tyrannical before the Second Anarch Revolt precisely because he’d seen one Prince in the region pulled down already (Very possibly one of his own blood, given that probably-Hispanic name and the necessity of maintaining an outpost on the fringes of Sabbat Mexico to the Prince of LA) is a question for the individual storyteller to answer.


                - I do like the idea of Tijuana as THE Anarch outpost in Southern California before Los Angeles revolted and the Free States were established, as well as something of ‘frontier warfare’ zone thereafter: it makes sense that vampires in Southern California would want somewhere they didn’t have to worry about being nailed down by Don Sebastian’s boys and offering their services as hired guns against the Sabbat to Maria might have been just enough to earn them their little hole in the wall (Not to mention a useful safety valve, albeit too small to keep Los Angeles working smoothly in the long run, especially if Maria was the one who persuaded Don Sebastian to maintain that safety valve properly in the first place).

                Of course after the Second Anarch Rebellion the old three-sided duel of wits might well become more of a straightforward clash of wills between Anarch and Sabbat (Though it’s quite possible that a local tradition of Anarch and Sabbat back-and-forth against the Camarilla might complicate the local dynamic, with the new local Prince tolerating Kindred she might otherwise oust precisely because she wants to maintain the local balance of power, at least in terms of armed force).


                - Incidentally, the notion of Tara already being something of a known Anarch in Texas would definitely explain why she was kept a carefully eye on whilst in Dallas.


                - Finally, it’s intriguing to speculate on the presumably-complex relationship between OG settler matriarch Tara Kearney and Joseph Ravenfeather of Clan Tremere: any situation that can be described as a ‘land rush’ is likely to give the latter some seriously unhappy flashbacks even as the former is likely to be completely in her element.


                Please pardon this somewhat messy summary of notions: I’ll try to do better tomorrow.

                        Black Fox, I hope my posting is not too repetitious and that it’s been somewhat helpful - if only by inspiring you to come up with better ideas all your own - and I also wanted to further test your patience by advocating for the Nosferatu member of the Free States Council to be an affectionate pastiche of vampire characters from ANGEL: THE SERIES (Based, of course, on the crackpot theory that the most interesting way to work BUFFY vampires into the World of Darkness would be making them Nosferatu - since this would pay tribute to the makeup jobs in that series, whilst also highlighting the considerable differences between Kindred and some of the more famous vampires to terrorise/romance/sleuth/punk their way across Southern California).


                        I suggest this because:-

                        (A) BUFFY/ANGEL is one of the most famous depictions of vampires in any context

                        (B) The specific context iof the show is, in fact, Southern California and Los Angeles proper

                        (C) It therefore makes sense to include one or more homages to one or both of these shows

                        (D) With this in mind, ANGEL makes more sense as a model than BUFFY, because sometimes, just sometimes, even Anarchs need lawyers (Also, guilt-ridden vampire hero in LA is much more MASQUERADE than one of the most famous heroic vampire hunters in fiction who is not actually a vampire).


                        Anyway, I’m doing this on my smartphone so this has to be more of a character sketch, but here’s what I have so far:-


                        - This character is of the Tenth Generation (As a ‘Gen X’ joke)

                        - This character is of Clan Nosferatu, but hails from a branch of the bloodline that is … well to put it simply they’re a Cult. Possibly a Noddist cult, definitely descending from and, to some extent, still answering to a leader who (from the perspective of the 1990s) has only recently been unburied after suffering a rather nasty accident during the 20th century (Possibly entering a fairly brief Torpor) and taken over a small town on the outskirts of Los Angeles that had a vampire problem to start with and is now suffering a potentially ‘End of Human Civilisation in Southern California’ problem


                        Note: for the record, I’m assuming that this nameless pastiche of Sunnydale-on-the-Hellmouth is experiencing a status quo comparable to that seen in ‘The Wish’ timeline (Where no superhero vampire slayer showed up, the day went unsaved and various well-beloved supporting characters went very, very Bad as a result, with the result that an elder vampire is currently working on a device that will rip the blood out of human prey faster than a juicer sucks an orange dry).

                        Not least because this is a very WORLD OF DARKNESS status quo that would make an excellent starting point for an adventure where the slightly-less-evil vampires show up and take the problems of a small California town with some
                        interesting secrets upon themselves, however briefly.


                        - Anyway, regarding the character under construction, my mental image was of a character who combines elements from both Angel and Spike (Given that the former’s Magical Curse doesn’t really work in a VtM context and because, let’s face it. The latter’s “Let me be Evil and Fun! Aargh, Evil hurts! Therefore I shall fight some Evil and get my kicks that way. Hey, that Hero is not only hit, she and her fellow heroes provoke both thought and feelings. Wait, Evil no longer hurts, but I still want to fight some Evil and do some Good? Right oh!” character arc is, in many respects, far more compelling).

                        Where was I? Oh yes - the character would be somebody embraced because a vampire wanted somebody fun to spend eternity with; adapted well enough to being a deformed creature of the night because, honestly, they were a passionate romantic and the idea of somebody wanting to spend all eternity with them really floated their boat; didn’t get on very well with the aforesaid Cult Leader grandsire and was allowed (or encouraged) to go their own way (Possibly in the hopes that they would die alone); obstinately continued to live, fight and be ruled by their passions; eventually guilt-tripped their way into anti-heroism; experienced a really bad break up as a result; decided to get an actual job and settled on Vampire Private Eye; experienced a series of adventures that pitted them against (Amongst other things) an organisation of Evil SUPERNATURAL Lawyers and, possibly by giving said law court sharks a choice between ‘Hostile takeover’ and “First we kill all the lawyers” acquired a level of control over said Law Firm.

                        - At this point I’m not entirely clear on what their exact origin is: I’m assuming they’ve been a vampire since the 19th century , at the least; that the progenitor of their lineage was a vampire who arrived in California claiming to be acting at the behest of Don Armando de Madrid (Seeking one Alonzo Guillen, a Childe who went MIA during the Nineteenth century) and proceeded to spend much more time establishing their own power base in Los Angeles (Very possibly as the progenitor of the local Nosferatu lineages) before departing the city under ambiguous circumstances (Did he leave or was he pushed? The Hollywood Nosferatu aren’t telling).

                        - Whether the character in question followed after ‘Grandsire’ or was made in the Old World and then followed after I’m not sure: one definitely imagines him playing some role in the Second Anarch Revolt (Probably as his way of getting out from under ‘Abuello El Caudillo’ with support from his new friends and associates, but increasingly for the sake of the Anarch cause).

                        - At this point I tend to imagine his role on the Anarch Council as ‘human whisperer’ (Having built up a crew of loyal assistants, not least to act on his behalf in the daylight hours and as the ‘face’ whi deals with clients), not least in that he takes at the very least a professional interest in dealing with same - rather than tending to focus on Vampire politics as a world unto itself, a tendency not infrequently on display in the Free States Council.

                        Given his association with a law firm, it seems highly likely he takes a particular interest in identifying and countering Camarilla efforts to extend their influence into the Free States by covert daylight channels, as well as seeking to cause legal problems for the Camarilla in nearby territories.

                        Being a Nineties vampire detective he likes to keep his hand in by doing some of the legwork himself (He’s not much inclined to katanas, but he LOVES the leather duster with a love surpassing the love of woman); he also still displays a dangerous tendency to help the helpless, for fun and profit and because he feels at least a little guilt for his years as a mean *******, which will obviously keep him in trouble.

                        Oh, and I’d bet money he has some interesting history with non-vampire supernaturals (It also seems likely he has a history of intense and increasingly-dysfunctional romances, if only with his fellow Nosferatu, but quite possibly with other beings … probably at a distance, given his Clan Curse).


                        Might I please ask if there’s enough in the character to be worth developing further? Any thoughts on how to make him fit more elegantly into the World of Darkness?

                                                        This is somewhat tangential to the core of the thread but with enough connections to make it worth including imo.

                                                        I’ve never really understood the rationale for having the New Anarch Revolts (here referring to the ones which produced the West Coast Free States rather than the First Anarch Revolt, the revolts that produced the ‘Brujah Council’ of the USSR, or the contemporary revolts of the early 1990s) take place in the 1940s rather than the seemingly natural pick of the 1960s. Maybe for Gen Xers in the 90s it being so natural was the problem, it was too obvious. But I mean, it would be natural for a reason.

                                                        Southern California in the 1940s, to play Devil’s advocate, does have some things going for it tbf. You’ve got the waves of unrest produced by the Great Depression which certainly produced political ferment in real life (you had Upton Sinclair’s EPIC program, but also groups like Ham and Eggs & Technocracy Inc.). You had waves of Oakie immigrants who (of the Steinbeck tradition), alongside the early Chicano’s, could possibly provide a base. You had the New Deal programs which finally ended LA’s tenure as an anti-labor ‘Open Town’ and which gave the left room to breathe thanks to the Popular Front. You had Japanese internment which could have functioned as a ‘barrack of revolt’ hypothetically.

                                                        Yet on the other hand this is still coming in the wake of the put-down of organized labor in Southern California post-WWI and in the 1920s. This is also post the Water Wars which helped to entrench the ruling powers and the rise of Hollywood Land. Furthermore imo the conditions of Southern California as a major military camp and manufacturing hub would dampen the potential for revolt - Military MPs everywhere, anti-saboteur vigilance, the anti-strike pledge from labor, and the generalized atmosphere of paranoia (see the Battle of Los Angeles) would imo strengthen the Camarilla during this period by making clear the absolute necessity of upholding the Masquerade. Hell that’s part of what makes Hollywood so valuable - it was, alongside the news network hubs back east, a crucial wing of the state’s propaganda arm. If the toreadors couldn’t thrive in such an environment they wouldn’t be worth their bulls.

                                                        The humans would simply be too edgy to risk much going on outside the shadows. Besides that the war economy was a boon to organized crime, who were virtually given a blank check to manage the black market, so long as they kept saboteurs off the docks. In this atmosphere I can’t help but see the Camarilla thriving; fattening themselves on internment profiteering, war profiteering, and black market profiteering.

                                                        Then what about after the war ends? There was a bit of a post-war slump after V-Day, that’s true, so maybe you put the revolt there. But I still don’t really see it. The slump ends pretty quickly once the Korean War gears up. Besides that the post-war economic engine gets running pretty soon. The suburban sprawl in Southern California takes on new life in this period and the automobile nation proper is born. Plus the Military-Industrial Complex takes root and really makes cities like San Diego come to life, not to mention the sci-fi wonders coming out of places like Cal Tech. Then you get into the era of the Second Red Scare, from McCarthy & HUAC to the anti-Beatnik hysteria to the teen delinquency-hysteria, all the way to Goldwater and the Orange County suburban warriors coming into self-consciousness.

                                                        For me the Southern California of the 1930s through to the 1950s (including perhaps the 20s & early 60s), especially LA, is the prime real estate for Noir. IMO that works best, in vampire terms, by having the Camarilla still running the show.

                                                        I do think there would be a ferment however, the seeds of what would become the new Anarch Revolt. The old-old left in Southern California may have been beaten down after the era of the First Red Scare post-1917 and through the 20s, but there would be survivors, especially amongst the immortal. You had the Steinbeckian figures of the 1930s alongside the noir anti-heroes, the flakes of the nervous breakdown of the middle classes, the rising old left of the popular front and their liberal counterparts in the New Deal tangling with the rise of the Californian answer to brown shirts while exiled European intellectuals sought security in Hollywood. You had the rise of Noir proper with the 1940s and the question of the post-war order, amidst even further booms in population under the War economy (concentration that, even if they had accepted War-time Masquerade protections measures, would certainly have felt tensions building). You had the post-war answer in the Second Red Scare and HUAC which forced the comrades and fellow travelers back underground (those who didn’t flee to Mexico City or Moscow at least). You also had the run-wild paranoia and hysteria of the era produce more moral panics to provide anarch inspiration: hipster anarchs from the anti-Beatnik hysteria (paging Nick Cave), hotrod Anarchs from the anti-youth delinquency hysteria, and perhaps most significantly anarchs drawn from the rising discontent of a black and latino population chafing under Anglo rule and channeled through the rising civil rights movement.

                                                        If you combine all the potential anarchs created by the past 50 years with the booming of the population in the 1960s as the children of the war are finally grown up then I think you have a much bigger recipe for a New Anarch Revolt than you do in the 1940s. Combine that with the waves of migrants called in by 1967 with the inauguration of the Summer of Love, the international ripple effect from the Cultural Revolution in China in 1966, and the possible catalyst (or at least mask for the spark) which can be provided for LA by the Watts Uprising of 1965, then I think you’ve got a strong case for post-dating the New Anarch Revolt forward two decades.
