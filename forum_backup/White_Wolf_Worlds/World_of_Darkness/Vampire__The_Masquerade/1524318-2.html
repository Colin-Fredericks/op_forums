                Originally posted by Hello View Post
                I believe someone in the past mentioned here that inventor/visonary character builds don't see much use due to the ST's not wanting any change to the setting, and after playing for a while, that feeling seems to be somewhat common. My question is, why? As long as you are not going too crazy and making nuclear weapons or spaceships in the early 1900's, I don't see why it would be that large of an issue. I have also noticed that at times ST's will refrence real life science when talking about how it is unlikely for characters to make certain innovations.

                Which doesn't really make much sense to me. There are characters which are breaking the laws of nature using magic and disciplines, comparatively, what some of my characters were doing was much more tame. Plus, if someone has 5 in tech and computers, why can't they make robots somewhat similar to those found in cyberpunk 2077? From what I remember, having 5 in an ability makes you a vissonary in that field, and in STEM, there's always some advancement.
                In Truth, a "Tony Stark" or even a Bruce Wayne or Macguyver guys are completely unrealistic. RPGs are fantastic in nature thou... When you buy "Science Skill 5" would basically be some insane intellect that would make Einstein look illiterate.

                In real life, you have people who are experts in their fields... And pretty much nothing else. Someone brilliant (say, like Einstein) can revolutionize their field... But not improve the Science and Technology of the world for the next era. Einstein revolutionized Physics, but our GPS machines would only come almost a century later.

                Those "Visionary" technological leaps are the result of THOUSANDS (or more) of competent (and many brilliant amongst those) people building up on the work of those before them.

                Like Newton said "if I managed to look ahead, it's because I stood in the shoulders of giants".

                Instead of a single unrealistic and cinematographic "Super Genius" building automatons using just Steampunk engineering, you actually need incremental steps done by thousands of scientists, engineers and mathematicians each contributing over dozens of different fields and hundreds of different specializations.

                A better computer requires hundreds (or thousands) of microprocessor engineers, hundreds (or thousands) of programmers, and several other fields, and no single person is capable of doing it all by themselves.

                    Originally posted by Shadeprowler View Post
                    There is a True Brujah merit, called Advanced Tech (1-5 pt. Merit) [Lore of Bloodline p. 99]
                    You have a device (or possibly a few) of an advanced design. While the True Brujah are not especially good at innovation, they are good at making improvements. So this device has no special functions unavailable to its contemporaries, but it does work a lot better. It reduces the chance of a mechanical fault (such as a jam) by half and all uses of the device are made with a -1 to the difficulty. The Storyteller should decide on the cost of the Merit, depending on how many items the character has, and how powerful they are.

                    To reflect what the narrator True Brujah says about technology and science:

                    "We are not just collectors of lore, but also students of it. With the vast resources at our command, we stand on the shoulders of giants when it comes to scientific advancement. While it is too dangerous to share the fruits of our labor with other Kindred or the kine, we do release some advancements into kine society when we need humanity to catch up.
                    Now you would think that any scientist, given eternity and the best resources, would be able to change the world, cure cancer, develop true artificial intelligence, and more. Unfortunately, it has proved not to be the case. While our scientists are both dedicated and extremely intelligent, they are not innovators.
                    It seems that the spark of creation and inspiration turns to ash when we become Kindred. It is a problem that haunts many of our most academic elders.
                    That is not to say we waste our time, though.
                    While we rarely innovate, we are very, very good at making improvements.
                    Our technology does little that is new, but works much better and faster than its modern counterpart. This can often be an advantage, as it makes it easy to hide among less advanced equipment. For instance, our computers are simply faster than those available to humanity, even if they are not intelligent or organic                    ."​

                    It is nicely summarize this whole discussion
                    Even with literally all the time of the world a vampire only manage to impove, but not really innovate
                    That's exactly what I would say. Instead of creating teleporting machines, self replicating intelligent robots or trans-dimensional projectors, your visionary vampire could make things better. Imagine a vampire hacker with Computer 5 that also has a super computer with the juiciest of the bonuses possible. Imagine that your hand guns or fully automatic rifles give several bonuses; your security system is better than Fort Knox and so forth so on.

                    Sure, Hit Marks are impressive beasts, but just "simply" having what everybody else has but simply better, already is a massive advantage. Computers for instance are powerful tools, so imagine having not just the best possible model, but one slightly better than that. Get a bullet proof car with machine guns, fire throwers and a maneuverability unequal to even the best Lamborghinis around, ABS breaks that are better than anything around - basically a car that would keep alive and ALMOST eliminate the possibility of accidents for even someone suffering a drug induced psychosis episode. Or a sword made with heavy material (for example, depleted Uranium) and Kevlar, making it the ideal tool for someone with Potence and Celerity (possibly with an edge that has been sharpened with laser, turning it into a "monowire", the cutting edge is reduced to the size of atoms, or perhaps it also is a "vibroblade").

                    That's the kind of improvements that would be within the scope of the game and proper for a "super genius" type of character

                                        Originally posted by Nyremne View Post

                                        The extra diegetic reason for it is evident, the writers don't want the rational vampires to have futurist tech.
                                        ​
                                        It's no coincidence the bloodline with the time manipulation and perception discipline, is the one to have a unique merit related to possessing near-future tech. Much the same as the one with the body modification discipline, is the one to have unique merits related to body modification of any and all sorts. Just food for thought.

                                        It's also the case VtM, contrary to how it's presented in the metaplot, is best played as a street-level game which means things can't necessarily get too gonzo. If we're going with comparisons to MtAs, your Trujah is more likely to have an X-5 Protector, armor cloth business suit, or black sedan (minus the Mind and Forces stuff) than they are a plasma cannon, exosuit, or VDAS implant. Believe me when I say the three examples I mentioned as something a Trujah might have, is more than adequate. That really strikes to the bigger problem -- players tend strongly towards the wild, outlandish, impractical, and borderline-if-not-outright un-judiciable stuff when it comes to things like this.

                                        As said up-thread, they want to be Tony Stark building ridiculous and wildly impractical crap like power armor, opposed to running a chop shop which would let the PC launder, customize, up-armor, or disappear vehicles in the city. They want to be Mark Zuckerberg, not the vampire who surreptitiously dropped a kinky hook-up app on the mobile stores which lets vampires find preferred victims without needing a herd or endangering the Masquerade. They want to be Jeff Bezos, not the vampire who runs a server farm in a basement somewhere that parses local news feeds, community forums and law enforcement scanners, and algorithmically processes that to identify and track hunters, and predict their activities -- or runs a bot farm fouling up the signal-to-noise ratio on the same online spaces, to disrupt and foil hunters. They want to be Elon Musk, not a vampire who holds a few key patents, and launders royalties for Resources 3 or 4.

                                        Those are the kinds of things players should be targeting when building for high Computers, Science, or Technology ratings, not comic book-y pulp stuff. They're all lower-key, more easily integrated into a chronicle, easier to adjudicate, more practical, and more importantly deliver higher returns for PC's in the chronicle as it plays out than any more-gonzo alternative.
