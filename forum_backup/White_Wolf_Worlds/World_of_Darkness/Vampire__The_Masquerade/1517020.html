    What do [Brujah] Mean To You?

    07-02-2023, 09:26 PM
    This is the first thread in what might become a series of threads called ‘What does X mean to you?’, if people seem interested.

    The inspiration for this thread is two fold: one, as many of you probably know, I like to go on somewhat long and hopefully interesting or useful diatribes about themes and vibes in the World of Darkness setting, and I’m always interested in what others have to say in the same vein. The other inspiration is something I read X5 threads for (in part anyway): the way that those threads seem to uniquely draw people into discussing what they feel parts of the setting/game ‘ought’ to be and I’m always fascinated by the spectrum of ideas that people have and I often find them very exciting and interesting. So a part of this thread comes from the hopes that people will bring some of that energy to the discussion.

    That being said, I’ll start with Clan Brujah, first of the OG 7 clans and, I must be honest, one of my favorite since the first time I opened VtM.

    * * * * * * *

    What are the Brujah?

    The Brujah are vampires in VTM that are designed to embody certain archetypes. They are more humanistic vampires like the Toreador & Ventrue (the Brujah to me have always had a feeling akin to Spike from Buffy, Lestat from the Vampire Chronicles [More his depiction from Louis’ perspective/the rock star thing], & the Lost Boys). The Brujah are the leather jacket wearing punk rock vampires of the 1970s & 1980s; bred on urban decay chic, insatiable passion, rebellion, hard & raw music, radical politics, and the general punk attitude. They move fast, hit hard, and are rock stars; and barely beneath the surface is a roiling, murderous rage.

    Of course, that’s not all the Brujah are or became:

    •The Brujah became the vampire tag-line for unorthodoxy and rebellion: they are the loyal opposition of the Camarilla (and I imagine many of the Ivory Tower’s originally radical ideas were cooked up by ancillae & elder Brujah), they are one of the principle clans of the Sabbat (leading Founders and alongside the Ventrue & certain Lasombra are probably the Vanguard of the Loyalist faction), and throughout VtM history Brujah has basically been a byword for Anarch

    •The Brujah have a defining quality in a generational divide: one which may be caused by degeneration of the blood or natural laws. Older Brujah become carefully regimented, emulating the Philosopher Kings & Prometheans of Old Carthage, while the Younger Brujah are more typically iconoclasts and rabble with red-hot blood (and Ancillae Individualists somewhere in the middle). Whether this is in fact the degeneration of Brujah’s blood under the Curse of Troile, a natural consequence of vampiric survival mechanisms over time, or some combination; I leave to each their own

    •The Brujah have become inseparably associated with the concept of Carthage in VtM, however part of me wonders if that isn’t missing the forest for the trees somewhat. I can see Carthage as being not only a Brujah project but a Assamite or Lasombra project. What seems more in keeping with me is that Carthage is one part of a larger Brujah pattern: a tendency towards Promethean ideals (alongside the Assamites) as well as antagonistic projects against rivals such as the Ventrue (alongside the Lasombra, to a point). In this view I imagine in Dark Ages for example we have a spectrum of Brujah. Brujah of Mesopotamia who from the beginning were deeply interwoven with each state and their competitions (perhaps with Assyrian Brujah Charioteers running wild later on). Brujah of Athens conducting political experiments while the Ventrue organize Sparta and the Toreador orchestrate Hellenic culture from old Crete. Brujah would indeed be in Carthage but they would also be at the heart of Rome challenging the Ventrue & the Senate from within, not to mention in the forests of Gaul and Germania. Later you would still have Brujah everywhere - in Spain alongside the Lasombra to plague Ventrue England & Toreador France, in Anatolia for Brujah & Assamite Ottomons to terrorize Ventrue & Tremere Austria Hapsburg Ventrue, and Brujah everywhere within as well, etc.

    •I’ve never been able to settle on which specifics of the Ilyes & Troile story I like best and I’m okay with that. I suppose my sketch would be Ilyes as an antediluvian chosen to be a warrior-king philosopher type (akin to Haqim somewhat, but with a less focused vision). Troile was their counterpart, the antithesis to their thesis and an important part of Brujah’s process. Whether Troile was an imaginary interlocutor, a priestess, a lover, a prophet; I don’t know. Regardless the two were inevitably linked. As time went on they grew alienated from one another, perhaps with Troile suspicious of the sources Ilyes found to develop their Temporis (likely a Lilian source) and this would eventually culminate in shadow boxing around the Trojan War where in the profanation of the city the two forces made contact again and we are still seeing the development of their constant dialectical struggle. Whatever comes from the struggle has yet to be born in full, but the conflict of development generates the energy of the clan. The Brujah Founder is likely buried somewhere below ancient Carthage (or perhaps Troy) but while sleeping their effect is still felt. They are torn within themselves over their goal for Gehenna and the Jyhad. They use the Spirit of their Presence to contest their kin but what are their own goals? Ilyes perhaps hoped to use Lilian Gnosticism to transcend temporal bonds and overcome the Jyhad & Gehenna that way and perhaps Troile hoped to use Promethean means to establish the Third City and either erect a throne for the Dark Father to preempt Gehenna or to create a Work of such greatness as to render Gehenna unnecessary - an earthly paradise, a Promethean Republic of The Philosopher King/Queen Brujah. The struggle of these impulses may still be felt in the Jyhad of Brujah. On one hand seeking philosophical truths which transcend the limits of reality and on the other hand the philosophical truths to perfect reality.

    •Speaking of the Jyhad, a part of me has always wanted to come up with a few methuselah characters for each clan who I can use as figures in the global Jyhad to better understand what ripples of intrigue may be going on. For Brujah I imagine that a few good characters for that would be one in North Africa/West Asia (an old Carthaginian) and perhaps one either to represent Greece or Spain and be a Promethean in the ‘modern’ Camarilla sense (perhaps the ultimate backer of the Brujah Founder of the Camarilla). Of course for North America we have Menele who we can use as the representative of Brujah at the highest levels of the Jyhad in the Americas - still tangling with Helene in his sleep and extending his influence to keep the Anarch Movement a viable challenge to any pockets of Helene’s control while grooming his preferred philosopher-princes/barons to eventually arise as Napoleonic/Caesarian figures from the struggle.

    •For the Camarilla I view the Brujah as fulfilling the role of Loyal Opposition at every level. The Elders are the Ideologues who helped define the various ideological camps of the sect - from Princely Prerogative to Radical Conclavism. The Elders have a somewhat softer than average approach to dissent; viewing it as a crucible for truly great ancillae (and some probably having some level of secret respect for their kin who became leaders of the Sabbat or Anarchs). That said a hardline Brujah Elder will typically be more hardline than any other Elder, whatever that line is. Brujah Elders will likely have a unique web of policies and ideas they apply to the sect and its government, becoming either supporters or opponents of local princes based on those points. Brujah Ancillae, as their elder counterparts imagine, are the crucible for development in the sect - when allowed. Brujah Ancillae can be some of the most volatile when unable to gain purchase, often forming a vanguard of Reform-Anarch programs; not radical Anarchs but Anarchs who would be Prince and carry out their own reforms. This energy can be useful if one has it in a retainer or if it’s satisfied by the position of Harpy or Sheriff, but if not it’s a double edged sword. Neonate Brujah in the Camarilla are a spectrum. Most are probably Anarch sympathizers to some degree. Most probably are Iconoclasts or Iconoclast curious (if only by temperament and not ideology). Most will not survive and those who do will find their own camps where they can settle as rock stars and cult leaders amongst humanity. The relatively large number and volatility of Brujah Neonates makes them a double edged sword in any domain - great shock troops, but that could be good or bad.

    •Brujah in the Sabbat, I feel, have always gotten the short shrift. Brujah were incredibly prominent in the Anarch Revolts and I’d be surprised if, like the Anarchs of today, they didn’t make up a plurality. That said, with the focus on the Status Quo & Conservative factions I can understand the focus on Lasombra & Tzimisce but I’ve always felt, like in the Camarilla, the Brujah Antitribu should be well represented amongst the loyal opposition of Sabbat Politics as well with members of high standing. That said the lion’s share are probably still more with the Loyalists than anything else.

    •The Brujah and the Anarchs are practically synonymous. Rob Leeland, Tyler, Jeremy MacNeil, Salvador Garcia, etc. It’s a classic for a reason. From the numerous Brujah in every generation of Anarchs (from the peasants of the first revolt, to the liberal radicals of the French Revolution, the radicals of the 19th century, and the socialists of the 20th century) with both the Brujah Council of the USSR & the California Free State having major influences from Brujah. Hell, it’s been a long-running joke for a reason that the Brujah are basically the Ventrue of the Anarchs (or the Lasombra of the Anarchs, if you prefer). This is true and I do think it’s interesting. While I don’t think it should distract from the sincerity of many Anarchs and the reasons why Brujah would naturally predominate, it is also probably not untrue that, just as Toreador & Ventrue elders supported the Camarilla to advance themselves post-War of Princes and the Lasombra & Tzimisce supported the Sabbat, the Brujah elders supported the Anarchs.

    These are all the things that came to mind for me, off the top of my head, and while it’s by no means comprehensive I hope that it was interesting to some of you who read and that you’d be interested in adding something to the thread and sharing your own thoughts.

    Thanks for reading!

                      It means having every square inch of your existence defined by rage, which is exhausting. Brujah sort of live in perpetual flight or fight mode, except there's no flight option.

                      The fledgling stage acts as a filter as the newborns drop like flies; most will fail to get ahold of themselves and get put down as rabid dogs, while many will off themselves because they can't cope with the emotional pain and exhaustion of living in constant hate and anger. A few of the unstable disasters survive into the neonate stage outside of the Cam, but they're still liable to burn bright and fast before they piss off somebody bigger than them (be they kindred, hunters or worse...). If you survive for any meaningful length of time, then you're one of the ones with damned good self-control. Brujah tend to be introspective and proactive as a necessity; they watch what they think and feel closely, identify things that help calm them and things that have the opposite effect, and try to shape their environment appropriately. They're driven to identify problems, causes and ponder solutions as a means of survival, lending to their reputation as philosophers... also adding to this is their tendency to hate each other and feud over it for having the wrong opinions, à la Diogenes and Plato but nastier.
                      Not all of them are "activists", but even so, they'll often go out of their way to try and tailor the society they live in to their comfort, surround themselves with the "right people", and decompress by directing their anger in "appropriate" directions at people who "deserve it".

                      If the environment is your hot button, you might engage in eco-terrorism. If you think women outside of the role of homemakers and mothers is unnatural or ungodly or whatever, you may go out of your way to abuse and humiliate women who don't fit your ideal of what they should be, or ghoul them and force them into that mold for their own good. If you think [insert group here] is causing the downfall of modern society you may go out of your way to exclusively feed on (and probably kill) people of that group. Eat the Rich is a slogan one might take literally.
                      You might even be utterly indiferent to political issues and social divisions... but things such as people with poor manners, or having to take orders from others, may trigger homicide ideation.

                      Contrary to the Anarch stereotype, the clan isn't always loud about their venting. Yes, some go out of their way to break shit; others go in for being authoritarian nightmares who are only hurting you because they care and are trying to "save you from yourself/ves"; some channel it into emotional abuse and bullying.​

                      In the end the rage in their blood always overwhelms them; they can't help destroying what they build. Relationships, alliances, your reputation, prized/vital possessions... accidents will happen.

                      Prone to hyperbole

                                  I see them as a clan that wants to remake society, but don't understand the naivety and cost behind it. A clan that seems unable, or unwilling, to learn from its own mistakes, and is incapable of learning from both the successes and failures of others. A clan that seems to make everything worse.

                                  Just look at Cartage, the Soviet Union, and California.

                                  Cartage, a paradise praised by the brujah, was in actuality a hellhole. Eshmunamash a fifth-gen brujah who was a doctor and actually lived in Carthage at the time, was a firsthand witness of the human sacrifices and the Baali infestation, and when he went to Troile to report this, Troile promised to look into it, but never did. He got so disgusted that he went to Rome and informed Camilla of the defenses around Carthage and other vital information.

                                  The Brujah Council were directly involved with the Soviet Union and actually supported Stalin. They legitimately thought that Stalin was the best guy to lead it, and preferred him over Trotsky. They also intentionally ignored the millions of innocents that died under Stalin's reign, all while thinking everything was going to work out in the end.

                                  And that's before we get into California. An Anarch free state, where anarchy is enforced. Gangs pretty much roam the streets, and for the most part, are free to do what they want. There is little to no neutral grounds, and if I remember correctly, the second anarch source book goes into how much of an idiot Jeremy MacNeil is for his hand in creating it.

                                      The big thing to remember is that for Brujah as a clan, politics -- any politics -- are means to an end. That end being delusion, making believe they're anything but violent bullies driven by their Beasts. The only thing they care about politically, is how violent a given ideology already is, how easily it can be driven to violence, and how well it justifies violence, because that facade lets them lie to themselves that it's not about the violence. All vampires are corruptive, parasitic monsters, and Brujah are no different: they corrupt and parasitize ideologies and movements, and move onto the next best thing when the lies they tell themselves wear thin.

                                      Not exclusively physical violence, although that's the basest and most common form of it, and at what they excel. Verbal violence, emotional violence, psychological violence, cultural or economic violence, it's all on the table for Brujah. Destroy, destroy, destroy, but pretend it's for some higher purpose so you can sleep during the day.

                                      Some -- usually the naive or just plain stupid -- actually believe it. Then they die, or grow out of it. But mostly, they die.

                                      The followers of Entelechy just try to deny it altogether, and replace lies about violence for a higher purpose with lies about Kumbaya drum circles being the secret solution to pretending you're something you're not. Works about as well as a fishnet condom. Brujah antitribu just decided to be honest with themselves, and we see what Brujah think of Brujah who are honest with themselves.
