                                                      I'm pretty much always the GM, and I homebrew everything (including the origins of vampirism) so the oldest character I've actually statted is the oldest surviving vampire in my world -- a Gangrel named Dilgan. He is the first childe of the first childe of the first vampire (the Gangrel clan Founder, who kicked it in 300 AD). He's approximately 3,961 years old. Here's his capsule write-up:

                                                      Dilgan was a Priest in Lagash, a man of profound wisdom and bearing who was embraced by Aeliameh to be her companion (in much the same way that Namtar embraced her). This coupling, however, was far more successful, though their relationship was never romantic in nature. Rather, they were the very best of true “friends”, if such a term does not sound out of place among vampires. When Aeliameh left for Persia, Dilgan initially stayed behind, although he took a 300-year hiatus to go visit her at one point, residing in and around Estakhr from around 120-420 AD. He returned to Mosul around 420, however, confident that his sire would return and wanting to prepare her way. Dilgan is almost certainly the oldest living vampire in the world, having been the fifth vampire ever created. He is profoundly weighed down with Stigmata, suffering from no fewer than a seventeen of them. Nevertheless, he has maintained his humanity remarkably well considering the circumstances, and still feels passion and yearning and loss in a way that many five-hundred-year olds have already lost. He has three times embraced a woman with whom he was “in love” and all three times it has ended badly. He has sworn not to create any further vampires and is one of the most vocal proponents of the Third Tradition being strictly enforced. He does not consider vampirism a gift but sees it as a curse that can and should ultimately be borne. He has great hatred in his heart for the Sabbat, seeing in them the ruination of the human world and the death of his beloved sire, with whom he spent over 2,000 years. Dilgan is known among truly ancient vampires as “The Divine Star” – but it is not a name that would be known to anyone born after the death of the Christ.
