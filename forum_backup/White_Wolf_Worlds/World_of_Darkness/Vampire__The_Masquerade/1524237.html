    Intimidating werewolves into (superficially) converting to Setism

    10-18-2023, 04:45 PM
    Hello everyone,

    I'm trying to figure out how a certain thing could play out.
    My character in VDA20 (7th gen Warrior of Glycon) has Potence 5, Serpentis 4, Obfuscate 2, Intimidate 3, Road of Set 10, Willpower 10, all virtues maxed out. The starting concept was basically a setite saint. He's currently doing a suicide mission on a hill inhabited by Shadow Lords werewolves. The ideal plan is to kill them all stealthily, one by one. Please avoid comments on how unlikely that is: I am aware and have another character ready if this one dies. My character's first encounter with a werewolf ended with my killing him in one turn despite his being crinos (did a hold with a snake tail and Potence, he botched the roll to break free and I bled him dry), so from his point of view, as a trained stealth warrior, Set fanatic, riled up against werewolves due to some other things that have happened in the chronicle, going in the werewolves' turf alone to stealthily kill them all is not too bad of an idea. He has to kill some anyway necessarily to bring the heads to Vladimir Rustovich in exchange for some important items for the Followers of Set. Having Road of Set 10, the cause is worth the risks and he has not seen all that werewolves can do to fear them enough.

    My question here is about one thing in particular. The character has currently Strength 9 (and Dexterity and Stamina also 9) for the scene (reached through blood expenditure + Typhonic Avatar, hybrid form) plus Potence 5 and Intimidate 3. His snake tail has Strength 12. He has -3 to difficulty for Intimidate attempts thanks to Serpentis 1 + Serpentis 3, and another -3 if the intimidation is connected to (even forcibly) converting others to Set or glorifying him thank to his Road of Set 10 + the merit Powerful Aura. Total: -6. Intimidation attempts in VDA20 are difficulty 6, contrasted roll.
    One of my ideas to save my skin if the werewolves (there's plenty of them, 20 at the very least) find me is to try intimidating them into surrendering to Set or being exterminated before they go into frenzy. It's possible that the ST apply the modifier for multiple oppnents (max +4 difficulty) to my roll since I'm intimidating a lot at once. This would make my roll of Strength + Intimidate be difficulty 4 (6 is the base, +4 for multiple opponents, -6 for my bonuses). If I slam my tail to show its strength as part of the intimidation attempt and spend 1 blood point to activate Potence, I would have 5 automatic successes, plus roll 15 dice at difficulty 4 (no specialisation) for an average (mathematically) of 9 successes. Plus Potence, 14 successes. Excluding gifts or rites, the worst the Garou could roll is Strength 9 (crinos with Strength 5 + 4) + Intimidate 5 and an applicable specialisation at difficulty 6, for an average of 7 successes. I would still win by 7 successes, which is A LOT:

    I am aware that any Garou in frenzy is immune to being intimidated, so that's not the scenario about which I am asking. For now the werewolves are all in homid or glabro form. They've gathered for some ritual and I have stealthily killed the first of them with a tongue slice from the back. The session closed with me not having busted my Obfuscate, but there is apparently some other power going on because the ST is asking me some strange rolls to stay hidden from time to time. But they have not seen me yet, for sure, as I walked among them for a long while before going behind the last of them and killing him instantly.

    So the question, finally, is: what would happen at this point? Could the fear drive the Garou so deep into the Wyrm as to make them submit - even temporarily, though 7 successes (even just 5) justify durable results - to me/Set and be instantly corrupted? Or would they just make scared sounds, maybe go in their form of Rotshreck, and / or feign submission as long as I'm around? Like, how does their mind and spirit react to this kind of stuff when they're not raging?

          Every ST will handle things like this differently. Some will just use dice rolls. Others will adjust the dice rolls results to something they think is more realstic. Others will want a more narrative approach.

          If I were the ST, I would definitely not have the Garou be so intimidated they convert to Setism.

          Here are the points I would be thinking of in order to determine the scene.

          First, this is similar to just any kind of "convert or die" scenario. And while in general you will always have people willing to convert (at least formally, even if not really), others will always choose death. But in such cases, it is almost always a case where the people being threatened are ALREADY defeated, disarmed, etc. and are no longer capable of defending themselves. They are at the mercy of their foe. The situation above is not that. There are various things they can do to fight or escape, all of which would likely be tried.

          Second, I don't care how scary or intimidating your character is, they are not going to succumb to it until you've defeated them. Especially as they are part of a group, and each Garou will likely not want to disappoint their buddies. It's a possible a single Garou on their own might choose to surrender to spare their life at a potential threat, but it's a lot less likely when part of a group. Especially if this is their pack and you have just killed one of their buddies.

          Third, Garou typically are already heavily indoctrinated in their own religion. Even more so in the Dark Ages setting than the modern day. They hate and detest everything that Setism is. Not only would the Garou be committing blasphemy, they would be committing treason. How many people, if threatened with death if they did not commit treason and betray their country, would actually betray their country? Surrendered soldiers have sometimes agreed to do this, but usually only in unusual circumstances (like colonial troops who fought for their colonial power, and could be shown how fighting for the other side might lead to their country's independence) and after a prolonged period of propaganda.

          Fourth, the Garou are not typically known for their cowardice. I have no doubt some Garou are cowards, but Garou culture as a whole stresses the martial virtues and pride in their people. We have no reason to view some Shadow Lords performing a Garou ritual are particularly cowardly. Regardless of how well a PC might roll, I wouldn't have the Garou react in that way. Fear might cause them to flee - possibly as a result of Fox Frenzy. But it wouldn't make people do a complete turn around of their values. Then again, such fear might also result in them immediately entering frenzy as their "flight or fight" response kicks in.

          Fifth, even assuming that in some bizarre situation a Garou end up surrendering, the idea that they'll be so terrified they'll immediately become corrupted just does not make sense to me. If your life was threatened, would you immediately "become corrupted" (whatever that means) and drop everything you believed in? No. You might act very cowardly and dishonorably to save your life in the immediate circumstances, but there would be nothing behind it. No sincerity. "Corruption" isn't some magical effect. It's someone choosing to become morally impure. You are not tempting the person in anyway to succumb to their base desires or animal pleasures. You would just be scaring them.

          Sixth, as powerful as your PC might be, Garou often fight spirits and monsters that are even more powerful. Vampires can be powerful threats, but to Garou they just don't compare to say a Nexus Crawler or Thunderwyrms. Not all Garou are capable of fighting off such evils by themselves, but they are aware of them.

          Most likely if you present yourself as a terrible threat, their flight or fight response kicks in, and they either succumb to frenzy and attack you, or keep their wits and attack you. That is how I would run it as an ST.
