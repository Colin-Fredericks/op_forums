                        Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                        You all seem to be under the belief that studio executives are rational actors that have achieved their positions through merit.
                        It's still a job, and you still have to perform the job to keep it (or have someone else make smart decisions you can claim as your own anyway).

                        And we don't need to assume anything about individual executives, we can look at things like sales data to see that, for the most part, studios have internalized ideas about how to make money off of properties, and those norms do not actually include the idea that either political messages, or an R/M/etc. rating "kills sales." Executives are, if nothing else, generally good at following trends (the trend setters tend to be the exceptions after all). When they see Blumhouse turning into a juggernaut of a studio on the backs of a bunch of low budget R-rated horror flicks that had huge returns for the investment, they're at least capable of saying, "oh, so that's how you make money off of R-rated stuff, lets do that too."

                        Some of you have also insinuated that your cool, liberal minded parents are the standard for all parents.
                        What makes you think this is a liberal thing? IME liberal parents are more likely to be the ones that micromanage what their kids watch and be sensitive to maturity ratings (even if not dogmatically abiding of them). Conservative parents are more likely to just drop their kids off at the theater with enough money for tickets and food, because (esp. in the US) they consider helicopter parenting a liberal thing; and their kids know perfectly well to lie if they see something their parents would object to, it's just not about what it was rated.

                        Because I'm old, but these things haven't changed that much: I got Mortal Kombat 1 when it first came out. I got my liberal open minded mother to buy it for me, but I had to have a whole conversation with her where I had to show I know the difference between fantasy violence and reality, and so on, before she'd agree to it. One of my friends with a far more conservative father saved up their lawn mowing money, and then had their older brother buy the game for them and never bothered telling their father about it.

                        The end result is still that lots of kids watch movies/play games/etc. that the ratings systems say they're too young to handle deciding to see on their own.

                        I think you're all a little too overeager to refute me here.
                        That tends to happen when you say something that's fairly obviously wrong. The idea that Paradox (you know, the executives in all this) are afraid of political messages or a Mature rating on their Vampire video games is ridiculous.
