    As we consider the Sabbat, one thing we must keep in mind is that some of the information in Dark Colony is either contradictory or confusing. Dark Colony is an early product, and thus some of its statements do not match later canon as it was developed. While we willw ant to utilize information on the Sabbat close to New England as detailed in Montreal By Night, New York By Night, and other sourcebooks, we must understand that things will not align and STs must make certain decisions.

    First, let’s consider Dark Selina. We’re told Dark Selina is a rising power in the Sabbat. We’re even told she is a Cardinal. But we also know she is lying in torpor in Switzerland, and that her support of the Sabbat (through Jeremy Skelton) is one of a “mysterious benefactor”.

    Does this make any sense? Why would the Sabbat make a torpored vampire a Cardinal? How would they even know she still exists? Or put her in charge of New England as she is in Switzerland? It doesn’t. Pendragon is known to be Dark Selina’s childe, yet he seemed to have been accepted among the Camarilla in Europe. He was not seen as an anti-tribu. Rather than be Sabbat, I think Kindred saw her as one of those powerful Methusalehs that remained outside any sect - a menace definitely, but not a member of that sect (likely because she was so old and powerful she disdained the limitations of belonging to any sect - to her the Sabbat is just another tool.)

    Instead, it seems more likely to me that Dark Selina is not actually a Cardinal, but that she is somehow “controlling” or “manipulating” a local Sabbat Cardinal. Primarily to keep her pawn Pendragon alive. (If we want a reason why the Sabbat has not been more successful in New England, this could be it.) She's also directly manipulating Skelton by influencing his thoughts and actions.

    We know Pendragon is also being “manipulated” by Dark Selina. That is a common conceit in VtM, but one usually associated with vampires of the same lineage as the Methusaleh. Critias served Menele because he is his childe. Annabelle serves Helena because she was sired by Maria who was Helena’s childe. That implies that Jeremy Skelton, 8th generation Brujah antitribu and his (destroyed) sire French Sabbat Marcel DeBreau, is also of Dark Selina’s lineage. Skelton might be only distantly descended from Dark Selina (who must be 5th generation), but it makes sense. We’re also told that Skelton’s sire was a powerful seventh generation Sabbat feared by others. Skelton cannot be the only childe of Marcel, or that Marcel’s sire might be Sabbat as well. So there very well could be another Brujah anti-tribu descended from Selina who is a Cardinal that Dark Selina is manipulating.

        Second, we need a real organization for the New England Sabbat. Namely, who is their Cardinal and where is he based? At the time of Dark Colony publication in 1993, this was not established. Kyle Strathcona as Cardinal of Canada did not appear until 1997. I don’t think Francisco de Polonia, Cardinal of the Eastern American Territories, existed until 1999/2000 when his name began appearing in various Revised products.

        I also want to note that the history of the Sabbat in New York City as later detailed in products like New York By Night does not match what was earlier established. Rage Across New York mentions important Sabbat like Shawnda Dorrit, Priscus of Manhattan (Brujah) and Bishop Violet Tremain (Tzimisce). They are completely absent in NYbN. Under a Blood Red Moon mentions a New York Sabbat Bishop named Gustov who is a mentor/contact of Anita Cartwright. He too is not mentioned. Whether intentional or an oversight, the New York Sabbat we know from Revised does not exist in Dark Colony. How these versions are reconciled will be up to the ST.

        So who do the New England Sabbat fall under? I don’t think it makes sense for them to have their own Cardinal. New England is too small an area. And Strathcona rules the Sabbat of Canada, not America. The Cardinal of the Eastern American Territories makes the most sense, but if the Cardinal is a Brujah of the line of Dark Selina then Francisco de Polonia cannot be the Cardinal yet. When I look up Polonia’s page on the White Wolf wiki, it says he did not become Cardinal until after the East Coast Crusade in 1999, but it does not name who was the Cardinal before him. If anyone knows, it oculd be useful.

        In any case, I would put the Sabbat of New England was under the auspices of another Brujah anti-tribu Cardinal, probably based in New York City who was the predecessor to Polonia (who is only the Archbishop of New York at the time). He might be a broodmate of Skelton, the sire of his sire Marcel, or some other descendant of Dark Selina not directly in the line of Skelton. Now that we’ve established that, we can consider some other things.

            Third, who rules the Sabbat in New England? Shouldn’t there be a Bishop or even Archbishop? Dark Colony is silent on this. We know Jeremy Skelton is an important figure as leader of the Crimson Tide (which seems to be an alliance of packs, not a pack/coven itself), but he only has Status 2. From the Players Guide to Sabbat, we know a pack priest or leader is Status 1. Status 2 is “respected”, equivalent to a Templar or Paladin. Bishops and Archbishops are at 3 or 4. So Skelton clearly is not a Bishop. It is possible that Skelton is actually a Paladin to some Sabbat leader, or just considered to be equivalent in status because of the prestige of leading Crimson Tide. Pick whichever option you like best.

            Skelton’s base is in Maine and northern New Hampshire. So there should be someone with a title like Archbishop of Bangor or Portland. In Dark Colony, Portland, Maine is definitely Sabbat. I will make it a contested city in my own chronicles simply to boost the numbers of Pendragon’s Brujah. But simply because it is contested does not mean there cannot be an Archbishop there especially because it would have been solely Sabbat until Pendragon launched Operation Longbow in 1895.

            Maine does not have much of a population. Using Dark Colony’s numbers, there are probably only 30 or so Sabbat total compared to 120 or so Camarilla/Anarchs in all of New England. So the Archbishop would not have many Bishops assisting her. Maybe zero or just one. Bangor is distant enough from the Portland metro area, so it might have its own Archbishop covering 3 vampires or so. But I don’t think an Archbishop should have so few Sabbat under her, so I’d make the ruler there a Bishop who reports to the Archbishop who might be called Archbishop of Maine or even New England rather than just Portland.

            I think that is it for the terms of Sabbat organization. We need to create the Archbishop, and one or possibly two bishops (the ruler of Bangor and someone else to assist the Archbishop on other matters). Now we move to the packs.

                Fourth, we need to fill out more packs. Skelton’s Rangers and Mowgli’s Order are interesting. But we only have their pack leaders and a little general information on their packs. So there is lots of work for the ST to do.

                In the coterie chart section (which strangely comes before the NPCs, not after as was done in other books), we have a chart for the Sabbat. Unfortunately, the chart description does not refer to the things on it which hurts in usefulness. But it seems like there are three additional packs under Skelton’s Rangers. These other packs are the Flayers, the Stand, and Fugue. They are probably allied with Skelton as part of the “Crimson Tide” alliance of packs, and probably dominated by him (while Mowgli's Order is allied, but much more independent.) It could mean Skelton, while not having the title of Archbishop, may have effectively usurped his power. Or that the Archbishop defers a lot to Skelton while she concentrates on other things not dealing with the fighting of the Camarilla. Or that she is dominated by his power, possibly resentfully so.

                We’re not given any other information about these three packs, but I will be using them as the other Sabbat packs/covens in the region. So that is five packs total for around 30 vampires. That leaves us decent pack sizes for all of them with an average of six.

                I would make at least one pack a founded coven in Portland (around the Archbishop), and another founded coven in Bangor. Pick whichever name out of the Flayers, Stand, and Fugure, that you like. Dark Colony heavily implies that Skelton’s Rangers is based in Portland, but they also seem to travel a bit. Mowgli’s Order is obviously a nomad pack, but again one that stays within the region. That leaves one remaining pack for our five which I would make another nomad pack, perhaps one that does leave the region sometimes to travel to Montreal or New York City (or both).

                And of course, there is no problem if the occasional nomad pack originating from outside New England shows up briefly.

                Filling out these others packs will be a job for each ST. I would only recommend that since Jeremy Skelton is on the Path of Power & the Inner Voice, and Mowgli is on the Path of Honorable Accord, that we look for other Paths as the basis for the other packs. While packs do not need to follow just one Path, I think there should be strong representation of the Path of Caine and Path of Death and the Soul. I've always thought these two Paths define the Sabbat. And since Mowgli’s Order seems to be comprised only of Country Gangrel, we might want to put one of the city covens as having members of the City Gangrel to provide some additional conflict.

                One thing I like is that the two representatives of the Sabbat are of the anti-tribu, and not of the two main clans of Lasombra and Tzimisce. It provides some nice distinction. So while I would not exclude members of those clans here, I would probably emphasize the presence of the anti-tribu here more, especially in terms of leadership. I will note that neither Skelton or Mowgli have Vicissitude listed in their Disciplines, but both do have Obtenebration. So likely there is some Laosmbra presence at least.

                                    Another aspect of the Sabbat I push in my chronicles is the idea that almost all vampire hunters (of whatever persuasion - mortals, Lupines, Mages, Children of Osiris, etc.) hunt Sabbat and not Camarilla vampires because the Sabbat is just so much more obvious. This isn't a deliberate choice just pragmatism. So it's probably best to provide a lot more detail on Sabbat history and their current opponents so the Sabbat NPCs (or PCs if an ST goes down that route) understand what they are facing. They have a lot more opponents than just the Camarilla, and they strike during the daytime.

                                    Have some battles when some Lupines wiped out an entire pack. Create some past names and put down specific dates and locations where it happened. Garou Gifts provide a lot of the same "here's the plot for the game" stuff that Prudence Stone's visions have so shovelhead ceremonies and the like can easily bring the attention of Lupines. There are probably two separate septs - the Silver Fangs of Vermont (the Morningkill family) and a mixed tribal sept in northern Maine so the Sabbat suffer from both. There are supposed to be Black Spiral Dancers as well who help Mowgli'd Order at least. So if an ST wants to build on the relationship between the Sabbat and the BSDs (something generally ignored in VtM games in my experience), that could be used as to why the Lupines aren't more successful in wiping them out.

                                    Make Caiaphas Smith into a true boogey man for the Sabbat (the original Hunters Hunted state that Smith concentrates his hunts in New England where the Sabbat is his bitter enemy) with many instances of him destroying entire covens during the daytime in their own lairs (he need not be alone, but have various groups who help him - likely different groups arrayed to avenge the one friend or family member they lost). Maybe even have the Sabbat think this isn't the same man, but a family as it isn't possible for the same man to be hunting them for 150+ years, and provide all sorts of alternative legends about him - he's a revenant, a ghost, a clever Lupine who doesn't change. And of course, the Sabbat don't know his name, they just know a general, but vague description. Maybe the Justicar's edict of avoiding Caiaphas at all costs is less about depriving him of vitae so he'll die, and more about allowing him to live so he'll continue to wipe out Sabbat.

                                    Have the Arcanum collect a great deal of information about the various Sabbat disasters and know a lot about their activities and perhaps names of longstanding Sabbat. Maybe someone from the Arcanum is leaking to Caiaphas or others simply out of fear and self-preservation while they wouldn't betray information gathered on the more "civilized" or "interesting" vampires of the Camarilla.

                                    And given the large Catholic population of Boston and Massachusetts, the Society of Leopold should have a very active cell. Most of New England falls under the Ecclesiatical Province of Boston except for Connecticut and Rhode Island which is under the Ecclesiatical Province of New Hartford. So there might be two different experiences with the society of Leopold in those two areas assuming the cell is based in Boston and limits their activities to what falls under their Archbishop (which happens to be the Sabbat hotbeds). Boston is one of the four oldest Cenacula of the Society of Leopold.

                                    And there might be various independent vampire hunters out there. Family lore passed down from the Vampire Panic of the 19th century. Am actual Revenant who wants to avenge his family's murder at the hands of the Sabbat (and perhaps the specific vampire who lead that pack at that time). Or someone whose friend or family member was just recently threatened or turned.

                                    This can go a long way to explain why the "frontline" has so little changed in a century. The Sabbat keeps losing packs and covens and need to recover. Maybe the Sabbat of Bangor have an issue because they lose the entire coven every twenty years or so. Maybe Skelton and Mowgli are the only noteworthy Sabbat because they are among the few who actually survived during this time.

                                                I don't think the Sabbat is quite the underdog. They have a solid core of territory (which the Anarchs don't), get reinforcements from the other Sabbat strongholds, and have some experienced leaders. It's just that we need to explain why for a century there's been no progress on either side. The Camarilla isn't making any head way either. It's just that the Camarilla does not have shovelheads it can just throw at the problem. The Sabbat does, so you need a way to eliminate any shovelhead advantage and reset them to zero after a while. Lupines and hunters are one way to do that.

                                                But certainly every ST will have their own comfort level as to how the Sabbat fares against its other foes in order to explain a stalemate.

                                                I do think the Sabbat must conduct raids in Boston or in New Hampshire against Pendragon, it's just that they cannot be destroying the Kindred of the Camarilla to the extent that it causes them to seize ground. If shovelheads are destroyed, the Sabbat can just create more easily. If a Camarilla Kindred is destroyed, it takes more effort to replace numbers because such Kindred have 5-20 years under their belt. The Camarilla is on the strategic defensive here and the Sabbat on the strategic offensive even if the Camarilla has the greater local strength.

                                                I admit I find the idea of a century long stalemate and the lack of details as to what has happened to be a major weakness of Dark Colony. There's been little thought in terms of how often the Sabbat attacks, how many casualties, how losses are replaced, etc. It's a stalemate because the writer likely ran out of ideas and just froze the setting after 1895. Ideally we'd have some battles happening around the 1920s and the 1960s with certain towns being lost or regained.

                                                Or we get some details on Sabbat politics that explain the Second Sabbat Civil War devastated the New England Sabbat, or that the New England packs were part of the brief Third Sabbat Civil War against the Lasombra and Tzimisce before they got crushed by the betrayal of the Panders. Turn the reasons for the stalemate away from outsiders and more internally if you prefer that. The best approach is probably some consideration of both. In either case, the ST has a lot to do on her own.

                                                Now some of this may be due to Dark Selina purposefully sabotaging the Sabbat because she's still deciding between Pendragon and Skelton as her primary pawn. But the better solution is probably that the Sabbat is not on the continuous offensive. But as they build up their strength, they end up losing some of the resources they've been building (for whatever reason) that either cancels the attack or causes it to be not as effective.

                                                            For the record, I was thinking of THE MANCHURIAN CANDIDATE because Dame Angela is more horror-adjacent in that role (and much more blatantly Establishment-looking); it also occurred to me that Georgia Rowlands would have been embraced at a relatively young age (Since that seems to be the usual custom amongst the Kindred).


                                                            Incidentally, I’ve been operating on the assumption that ‘Aunt Rowlands’ (One imagines that she had an immense social circle of younger persons who instinctively call her ‘Aunt’ whether or not they’re actual blood relations) was embraced at some time during the American Revolution - with her sire quietly seeding New England with vampires young enough to be expendable, but shrewd enough to quietly rebuild Clan Ventrue’s position in the region after the Brujah did a number on them during the Revolution & War of Independence.

                                                            One further assumes that, while Aunt Rowlands did a yeoman job in this respect, one of her peers preferred to settle into obscurity and focus almost exclusively on the dramas of his own mortal relations, becoming the ‘World of Darkness Barnabas Collins’ mentioned in previous pages.

                                                            More on him later.
