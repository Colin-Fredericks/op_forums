                                    I think there is practical merits to keeping armor a relatively weak stat. One, it keep elders scary, because if you, say, crash an armored van into her business luncheon, the Prince isn't significantly squishier because your coterie is armored up like the Micheline Man and she's wearing a tasteful dinner dress. Two, it preserves the movie flavor of vampires in general, because vampires don't run around in heavy armor in most movies/novels. Vampires generally dress in mundane, if gothically styled, clothing, or light leather armor. The typical movie/novel vampireverse is essentially a D&D Rogueverse, where everyone is light and cunning and agile.

                                    In other words, armor is nerfed in this game for the same flavor reasons that the lighter armored classes in D&D have excuses why they can't wear heavy armor. In the case of D&D, it's generally because they can't use important class powers while wearing heavy armor. In Vampire, it's because armor itself is of limited value. Both are really just game mechanics designed to enforce a stylistic flavor via preventing the Rogues and Bards from plating up like Paladins and Fighters.

                                                Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post
                                                I think there is practical merits to keeping armor a relatively weak stat.
                                                It doesn't have to be powerful as much as useful enough to avoid breaking immersion for people.

                                                Let's take vampires out of the combat for a second (because this applies to mortals too).

                                                You send your ghoul to go beat up some mortal with a baseball bat because for whatever reason that's deemed an effective tactic for this hypothetical. That mortal played football in college and happens to still have his protective gear. Realizing he has a few moments, the mortal tries to prepare for the fight as best he can.

                                                IRL, getting all that padding on is a no brainer if you have the time. You'd much rather have a football helmet on if someone's swinging a baseball bat at you, than take in on your unprotected skull.

                                                By the V5 rules, there's no difference. It's superficial damage either way, so mortals putting on protective gear in such situations has no point.

                                                To get more hyperbolic in terms of a situation, a mortal facing a mortal opponent in plate armor can deal damage almost as effectively with punches as they can with pistols... and the only difference there is the weapon damage bonus. Punching someone covered head to toe with steel is equally effective as punching someone wearing nothing.

                                                Pre-V5 armor was already weak. The dice bonus to soak was massively outmatched by what damage pools can get to in the game, and high soak armors meant big penalties to Dexterity rolls (aka the most important combat stat). Most vampires in Pre-V5 had minimal incentive to wear more than Class One armor (aka keeping the visual aesthetics of leather clad warriors) because the help from more soak was easily outweighed by the Dex penalties, and vampires already have good soak anyway (plus as noted, it helps with the Masquerade to have some armor on to excuse you taking a bunch of hits). But at least you could understand why vampires might wear armor, and certainly why squishy humans would wear armor by default in serious combat situations.

                                                In other words, armor is nerfed in this game for the same flavor reasons that the lighter armored classes in D&D have excuses why they can't wear heavy armor. In the case of D&D, it's generally because they can't use important class powers while wearing heavy armor. In Vampire, it's because armor itself is of limited value. Both are really just game mechanics designed to enforce a stylistic flavor via preventing the Rogues and Bards from plating up like Paladins and Fighters.
                                                There's two big problems with this.

                                                First, D&D doesn't make armor useless by making it weak. It uses the same thing Pre-V5 VtM armor did to encourage these things: penalize important traits so some characters would avoid armor to avoid the penalties, and others wouldn't.

                                                Second, V5 is also going to set the mechanical tone for the rest of the games. Armor might not be a big deal for vampires, but it's a huge deal for some of their enemies (vampire hunters), and for games like Mage and CtD where characters don't have default supernaturally tough bodies. W5 is going to struggle with making effective First Teams if Pentex's hit squads can't use armor to equalize fights with Garou any more. Hitmarks are going to really struggle to pull their Terminator-but-for-mages tricks if all that internal armor they have is pointless. Etc.

                                                V5's rules aren't just preventing rogues and bards from plating up like paladins and fighters... it's preventing paladins and fighters from having the benefits from armor they'd want for giving up on the perks the others get by not wearing so much armor.

                                                    V5 isn’t D&D.

                                                    In the modern era, the population at large generally doesn’t wear personal armour. Vampires in the setting are mostly attempting to live a clandestine life hidden amongst urban, civilized populations - in the modern era - and are not going to want to draw attention to themselves by walking around in full plate armour.

                                                    The rules for armour in V5, therefore, are aimed at those people who are likely to wear it - special services, swat teams etc. In these cases, as with real life, what body armour does is convert what would be lethal damage to the body into non lethal damage. That is what the rules do. However, seeing as most lethal damage is already converted to non-lethal damage for vampires as it is - because they are magical, undead beings - the impact of wearing such armour is negligible.

                                                    Now this is the rules as they are. They are functional and logical in their conclusion. If you want to develop rules to allow armour to function in a manner that would be comparable to D&D, you may need to develop ideas further or in a different way. Good luck with that. However, V5 isn’t D&D. Never has been; never will be. Nor are the other WoD lines.
