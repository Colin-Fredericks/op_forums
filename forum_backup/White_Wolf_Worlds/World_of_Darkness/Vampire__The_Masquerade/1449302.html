          Oh the poor Tremere. One of the original clans from 1st edition but for some reason the writers could never stop messing with the clan, its back story, or stop adding twists.

          Half the backstory and design of the clan suggests rigid order and near perfect lockstep between all members, the other half says it is the exact opposite. In other words the clan was less designed and more of a plot device that changed attributes based on what the writers needed to make their stories work.

          Rebuilding the pyramid doesn't require anything beyond simply abandoning all the contrary materials added for non-sensical reasons. The partial blood bonding to the council, the clan discipline of dominate, and the fact that the Tremere are hyper selective about who gets embraced means the Tremere is a small clan, made of like minded individuals, whose shared interests and need for mutual defense ensures that they have to stick together to survive.

          Once you have that as the bedrock, you just expand the frame work while maintaining the standards.

          If anything in my opinion, starting from a point of promoting inner clan rivalry for the Tremere sounds a lot like suggesting all Brujah would agree to rebelling in the same way, it more or less misses the point of the clan. If I was going to be uncharitable it sounds a lot like turning the Tremere into Hogwarts with its house system.

          The Tremere stole the power of vampirism for themselves, they as a clan are battle hardened by being attacked for centuries by some of the more powerful clans (as in not even Vozhd can crack a chantry defense), and despite having minimal numbers they not only held their ground, they thrived enough to become a valued part of the Camarilla. Not even the Giovanni can lay claim to that level of systemic lockstep and they are blood related in two different ways.

                      Tremere are a clan where at first you look at them and think "hold on a minute, these fellows seem like OP mary sues and for some reason all the dork powergamers with no social skills want to pick them, I hate them, they need a nerf" and then when you learn a little more you realise that their backstory makes complete sense, that their power is justified, and that dork powergamers will be dork powergamers with whatever clan they like and the Tremere are a clan of choice because they provide options rather than an issue with balancing, and also they're infamously not that charismatic so it's ok to play a dork Tremre..."

                      A good chunk of writers get stuck with their initial assumption and don't look further than that. It's was a problem that began long before V5, and these ill-advised revisions for the clan became the stepping stone for the farce the Tremere are put in for V5. The V5 move is also motivated by this strange idea that clans should be more inclusive for different types of people, which is... exactly the opposite of what clans and sects are all about.

                      Ultimately, it boils down as follows:
                      The Tremere benefit immensely from their clan structure. A Tremere not in the pyramid is not going to be successful, because only 2/3 disciplines will work without either tutelage or more studying than a hungry lone neonate can really commit to. If they are a thaumaturgical talent, they may be press-ganged into serving some local lord or otherwise become an unwelcome competitor that drives prices down. The pyramid provides resources, security, and brand recognition.

                      The Tremere are going to embrace likeminded people, and their vetting process is going to be heavy, because if you're going to share a communal haven with undying folk you're going to pick folks you can all stand, or at least not cause problems, You also want people who're going to be on a similar page to teach magic too, because ideas communicate better when you're all keeping within the same framework.

                      What kind of Tremere would join house Carna? None of them. That's not who Tremere are. The paradigm is wrong, and if it was a real competitor you'd be killed for joining. HC might maybe work if you fled for the hills and embraced some fledglings just to fill a chantry, but that kind of approach will bite you in the ass. You can only reasonably conclude that the Tremere split is either A: Pure silly/wish fulfilment or B: A trojan horse designed to get Tremere into territories that hate Tremere, and perhaps an attempt to make the Tremere look less threatening so that the Ventrue don't curb their ambitions.

                      So, using reason, you can only either Ignore the split or run with the idea that it's just a ruse.

                                  I always imagined that the Regent of the local chantry decided what the local priorities of the chantry were, influenced by instruction he received upstairs from the lord (who in turn got some instructions from the Pontifex). So if one Regent only wanted to do mystical research and nothing else, that's what that chantry did absent some minimal maintenance work like making sure they built enough Influence or Resources by using Dominate, the Blood Bond, or simply their investment portfolio to pay taxes and utilities for the chantry. If another Regent in the city next door wanted to grow Influence in the mortal realm and treat the Chantry like a masonic fraternal organization, then that's what they did. If a third Regent had a very specific goal in mind like deciding who will be the next Prince, quelling the local Anarchs who tagged one of the chantries with spraypaint, or bending the domain's Primogen Council to his will, then they worked on that.

                                  If all of them reported to the same Lord, and the Lord asked them to do X, then they included that on the agenda. If that was each chantry needed to provide 5 million dollars in resources by the end of the year to the Lord (to achieve whatever plan he had), then they would do that. That might mean trading some boons. If the other Regents knew that one of them could raise all the cash by themselves, they could just ask him to do it and they owed him one. That way the next time their Lord asked for something he might rely on them to help him achieve whatever. Or if the Lord demanded each chantry control one of the major local media networks, they added that to their agenda and did their best to not have it disrupt what they really wanted to do.

                                  As long as they could keep their immediate superior happy, each Tremere did their own thing with their remaining free time and resources. And likely thinking how much they'd change things if they ever got to be the one in charge. It was just that everyone knew they needed to "work towards Vienna" - making sure they were contributing to what Vienna wanted done.

                                  I figure the hallmarks of the Tremere were obedience to the Pyramid and paranoid preparation for conflict with their many enemies. But as long as the chantry was kept adequately prepared and superiors' orders were obeyed, the Tremere could determine their own personal studies or endeavors. The Tremere Oath covers a lot of stuff, but it also allows a lot things too. So Regents usually had a lot of leeway, and the Apprentices that contributed most to his goals were the ones who got promoted most.
