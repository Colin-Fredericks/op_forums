            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
            I suspect you can draw a direct line from Point A (defining things with city books and supplements) to Point B in the Chronicles of Darkness where it's a toolbox rather than anything actually being defined.
            I feel like both styles can be valid, zig-zagging between them depending on the occasion, often in the same chronicle - or even story arc between sessions.

            But when you write a citybook to describe/define a particular place you have to be extra careful with what you present in the book, because it will be referenced - no it will be THE starting reference point for players, STs or just afficionados reading a book for fun, for good and for ill. One bad slip of improvisation because you had no time or interest in researching the place or had terrible luck with the sources used and you end with stuff that bothers or offends present or future buyers-players in a place you as writer had little familiarity or experience with. And people will still give you crap over that mistake decades later.

            Also the reason i always went "200% pulpy dark fantasy" with the clans and their roots, with emphasis on Ravnos as wanderers, rogues, ilusionists & tricksters instead of just romani (or later indian), Assamites as hashassin, zealots, political assassins & vigilantes (and in the last decade, Assassins' Creed) across the ages, the Followers of Shesha in India & much of Southeast Asia and so on, among other bits to blur geographic lines and confuse things.

                        Like many things in the WoD the "standard" approval process for the Giovanni is more of a guideline than a law in practice. There's always a way to fast-track individuals that have clear potential, and slow down those that are being given a small chance to prove themself. It exists far more to keep the younger mortal members of the family in line than it has to do with any practical matters regarding training and indoctrination.

                        Also, the Giovanni are the Clan of the Borgias (or at least the worst interpretations of their history as told by the Borgias' enemies), not just the Clan of mobsters (even if V5 didn't really help on this front). Beyond that, the history of the Italian mafia in the US shows how quickly such groups can fall apart once their "code" gets broken. One high-ish ranking member that knows enough and feels disrespected enough by those above them can lead to hundreds of mobsters in jail. There are a few dons that notoriously got overthrown precisely because their underlings saw far more money and power in making deals with the mobs of other ethnic groups than keeping the old school Cosa Nostra mindset about things. So that sort of move is hardly unprecedented in real world organized crime history.

                                                    The vast majority of the Clan - if one includes the mortal and ghoul members especially - are involved in legitimate trade and banking efforts. A few of the auxiliary families were explicitly brought into the fold to increase the Clan's mystic power by already being practitioners of mortal (not necessarily actual Numina) magic practices. Though all of the auxiliary families have some disturbing "traditions" (cannibalism, dealing in the illicit antiquities trade when they don't find actual mystic artifacts, etc.) that made them viable targets for recruitment, only one of the dozen detailed families that make up the Clan actually specializes in organized crime (rather lazily, the Sicilian one).

                                                    As Shadeprowler already noted, the Giovanni started in ancient Rome as a family of merchants. For nearly two thousand years their MO has been using their abilities to control the spirits of the dead to get ahead in business. While the Clan has never shied away from bending or breaking the law when it comes to getting what they want as business folks (esp. when it comes to swaying politicians), crime has always been a tertiary endeavor after money and necromancy. Corruption and organized crime on the scale the main Clan tends to get involved with is because why not have another edge their rivals don't rather than the core focus on the Clan's enterprises.
