    Originally posted by Herr Meister View Post
    the very existence of diablerie utterly destroy this notion that vampire power is only measured by age...
    Not true. Not in a direct, objective sense. While it doesn't negate anything else in your post, this isn't a valid assertion of fact.

    There is no actual objective and official rule on this whatsoever. The few official books that ever dared to present something of this nature are somewhat obscure supplements presenting optional rules.

    What we factually have on the RAW is that Diablerie causes the following effects and them alone:
    • Decrease of Generation by 1 if the victim was of lower generation, with further decrease strictly at the ST's discretion;
      ​
    • Others are entitled a Perception roll to few unease at your presence for X* months;
      ​
    • Aura marked by black stripes for X years (minimum 1 year);
      ​
    • Self-Control/Instincts roll to avoid "loosing control" (for whatever that means);
      ​
    • Regardless of benefits, it may cause addiction;
      ​
    • You may receive extra Discipline levels for a single scene or longer, at the ST's discretion;
      ​
    *X is Original Generation - Victim's Generation.

    And that's it. Anything that's at the ST's discretion may and should be considered unreliable at best in-game, so while some diablerists do experiment such benefits, there's no rhyme or reason to it that can remotely guarantee results. You may be 13th Gen, diablerize a 4th Gen Methuselah of 6 thousand years, and still get only to become 12th Gen and not a single second of extra powers out of the deal, and that's what you should indeed expect despite the chance of getting some extras.

    In this same section in the core it is said that legends talk about sometimes the act causing possession-like side effects that may be or may be not an actual possession, and may or may not lead to a permanent personality change. Some other books also claim that legends talk about greater, more permanent rewards from the act.

    Both claims are given in every official lore as unsubstantiated legends. There are only two case studies known to readers in greater detail, Saulot and Mithras, but those cases are also peppered with so many unknown factors and particular considerations that it isn't possible to draw definite conclusions (for starters, were they able to apply their powers while in Torpor? They're both masters in Disciplines that are geared to cause the effects saw in their Assailant-Victims).

    My point here is to remind you that, while greater rewards from Diablerie are a popular approach that many, many tables use and the lore somewhat accepts as a possibility, it is not an official fact of the setting for more objective, house-rule agnostic discussion.

                        Virtually all books in the history of the setting assuming as default that any character actually committing Diablerie won't face further effects unless the ST wants to, and usually that such effects will be temporary if they happen at all. That includes core books, Player Guides, Storyteller Companions, and every other book meant to be a staple on mechanics or cover a broad aspect of the game.

                        There are two or three exceptions, two of them being about the Tal'Mahe'Ra, which I am confident to say to be a concept of limited scope in the setting (not talking about the scope of the organization itself, which is gigantic).                         Originally posted by Herr Meister View Post
                        I feel, with all due respect, that here you're just being a "rules lawyer" and not adding much to the discussion, only adding unnecessary possible complications that only serve to dilute what I said, in other words, it looks like provocation.
                        Not my intention, I guarantee you.

                        What you said, and that I responded to, was that the idea of Diablerie goes against the notion of power coming from age.

                        And what I mean by all this is that by no means this is the intended theme of the setting. Behind those rules is a theme, and by making the standard rules give you little in the way of direct power when lowering your Generation (or being Embraced into a low Generation to begin with), the game is playing directly into the notion of the Power of the Ages, not against it. It is a central theme on itself, frequently repeated, either with or without association to Generation.

                        The game isn't binary on this, and the legends are there both to make Diablerie more tempting and give the ST the power to tweak those themes in their game, but as a rule of thumb the seizing of this power by the young is an exception, not the rule. The young can go a long way on their own or through Diablerie, but only age will give you mastery. Exceptions like the Tremere happen, but they're that, the exception, and usually go hand in hand with other special circumstances (a House of Awakened Mages and a family that has practiced Necromancy for millennia aren't exactly your standard fare for new vampires).

                        Can you apply those ideas and throw away the theme of power tied to time? Certainly. But you can't say this is RAI, the setting is built around this trope and the rules are how they are by design.

                                Originally posted by Herr Meister View Post
                                In my opinion, they only didn't conveyd it completely to the rules in the core books (giving instead, "half-rewards" in the form of temporary disciplines and the possibility of some being permanent, cheaper to buy or at the very least giving access to disciplines the cainite didn't know before, etc) in order to not give "way too big of a reward" and somehow curb players' "powerplay", so to speak
                                That's the crux of the matter: I'm not convinced this was the intention, or at least not to this extent and not exclusively (and it can't be conclusively decided from the rules alone, in neither direction). There is a theme of Power of Age, and it partially trumps the theme of stealing power through consumption, which is the reason one can't be considered decidedly more canon. This is even more relevant if we take the Highlander inspiration seriously, which would make the core rules similar to the first movie's Quickening and basically have those other options as based on the Quickening in the series and other movies (with the whole thing being less a soul steal and more of a Dark Quickening). But without the source, let's just keep this as a possible middle ground interpretation.

                                We can discuss at length what stealing only Generation means anyway. Or stealing Generation plus the capacity to develop Disciplines without further help other than XP. The fact is that on the long run, and if the game didn't had high level stats so overpriced, this would be enough for a huge advantage anyway without giving all the goodies at once, which may cover more of what we see in the lore than it seems at first. For example, there's no indication that the Tremere or that Augustus Giovanni got any additional powers from Diablerie as they were formidable people already and very advanced on the kind of thing that would become their main powers even before undeath.

                                In the end, the game has many characters that committed Diablerie and achieved considerable power, but few that we can say for sure that there's a tight link between the two things, and even less certainty that it bypassed any of the many option that we would describe as "increasing traits through XP". Add to it the possible secrets those characters hold, and the only, single character that we have that can be objectively pointed to as having absorbed permanent stats was Monty.

                                And rituals, Disciplines and other interventions have the problem of being exactly that: interventions. They're by definition not the default state of a character and not how most in the setting will have it. And it is hard to say which stance their existence reinforce as the intention of the game as a whole. Is this a sign that any character with enough dedication can acquire the means to obtain greater powers? Or does that indicates that the one with this power is a "cheater" of sorts, a threat to the world at large for its ability to bypass the limitations that are meant to be? Do they tell about how the authors wanted the setting to be, or are meant to explain exceptions not intended to become rules?

                                Even the Tremere offer us a hint: most of the clan committed Diablerie at the time wantonly, and the Inner Circle did it to Elders and Methuselah. Yet we hardly if ever have news of them showing many signs of those transfers having occurred, neither personality nor power.
