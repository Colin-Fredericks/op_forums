              I would say it really depends on the level of Dominate being used.

              A character who has an implanted command/suggestion does not have to know that it is there, only becoming aware of it once it activates. Thus, you can have some very interesting "Manchurian Candidate" style shenanigans going on. You can make a whole game of the coterie trying to figure out what the trigger conditions are and based on that they then can try to guess who implanted them.

              Likewise, since memory manipulation is a thing, you can play it out like each PCs backstory is essentially an unreliable narrator. Though I find that on both a mechanical level and a storytelling level, it works best to change details rather than large chunks of memory. This way when they go digging through their notes, the players don't always catch the change and just go with it.

              As for the first power, unless it's used in an obvious fashion like forcing someone to kneel, it can be concealed inside a normal sentence with perhaps a hint of emphasis. For example, if the Dominate user is playing poker and says to one of the other players "you should Fold this hand" the target will do so but then be confused as to why they did that.

              If you are planning on running a dominate heavy game, it might be helpful to pull the targeted player aside ahead of time to work out what might happen. This won't always make for the best role play by that player, but it does make for much better role play for the rest of the group.

                          So, yes, sure there are nuances as there are different Dominate powers, I'm inquiring more how other ST prefer to handle it.                               Subtle uses of dominate tend to go unnoticed, at least by the person being dominated.

                              More obvious uses of dominate tend to shock the victim into asking, "Why did I do that?" afterwards, but sometimes the victim might rationalize their behavior. (e.g. "I was too tired to resist." or "He has such a magnetic personality I couldn't help but do as he asked." or "You know, you had to be there, he was just so dominant.")

                              Resisted attempts of dominate (the victim spends WP to resist) almost always results in the victim realizing some supernatural power is being used.

                              Outside observers of the scene increasingly realize something is wrong if they are paying close attention, but may miss subtle uses of dominate. In much vampire media, when the vampire commands their victim, it is usually clear to the audience and other observers what is happening.

                              = = = = =
                              In terms of how I would run it as ST with its use against players, I use several ways.

                              1) If it is just a subtle use, I just have the NPC command or request the PC to do it and see if they do it anyway (many players are willing for their characters to accommodate reasonable requests). Only if they don't, do I say they are compelled but can spend WP to resist.

                              2) More outright uses of dominate against them, I go straight to the dominate rules. If the PC fails, I narrate what they do. They don't have much autonomy as they are under a kind of mesmeric spell so they can't do passive aggressive things and the like while being commanded. I do let players make OOC suggestions though about the scene if it improves the game.

                              3) I do have NPCs sometimes subsequently command the PC to forget what they just did (if they have Forgetful Mind) to again allow the player to resist. I want the player to know what happen. I have enough good, mature players now that generally they don't metagame these things. They enjoy the complications that occur. Keeping things above board and not confusing players gives more advantages than disadvantages in my experience, at least with my gaming groups.

                              4) I tend not to pass notes or take players aside anymore because the other players always suspect some form of mind control is going on. So I'm just open about it with the idea that we all want to create the best "story" together. If there is information I don't want other players to know, but one player needs to know, I do speak to them in private, but I try to limit such things.

                              5) In cases where a sire or other background elder has Conditioning, and could have implanted commands on the PC prior to the beginning of the chronicle, I work out the details in advance with the player. Typically I keep them limited to things like, "If you are involved in a Masquerade breach or observe others doing so, when you have the first safe and secret opportunity when alone you will call this number and explain what has happened and forget that you did." It is not unusual for Ventrue and Tremere PCs to start a chronicle with something like this, but it is also not unusual for them to NOT have pre-planted commands. For other clans, it really depends on what kind of backstory and preludes the character has.

                              6) In some cases where I intend the NPC will command the PC to do something and then make them forget, I will ask the player if they want to know what they did, or if they just prefer not to know so there is some mystery. Or in more unusual cases, not give them that option at all to know what they did (although I do allow resisted dice rolls).

                              In general, my principles are:
                              1) Make it known it is Dominate so they can spend WP if they so choose, and that they aren't confused by what is going on.
                              2) Quickly adjudicate the scene as players dislike not having agency so I want to get the scene quickly back to the point where they can act again.
                              3) Keep the player informed about what is going on, even if their PC is clueless.

                                          I am not up to all the V20 rules changes and TBH even 2e/Revised rules blend together in my mind, so I could be wrong on how WP can be used in terms of dominate. However, if you are worried that spending temporary WP is too powerful, then you could just require a reroll of the dice instead of an automatic resistance.

                                          I think spending temporary WP really depends on how much of a constrained resource it is. If the story chapters in the chronicle are short enough that temporary WP would never run out, then players aren't really making choices. If the chapters are long enough that it is possible for them to run out and not have it when it counts towards the end, then players will be more judicious when spending it.

                                          = = =
                                          My gaming groups are too undisciplined to not split between OOC and IC discussions. We just have too much fun making OOC comments. We just blend the two in back and forth. Usually it is easy to tell the difference, and when not, it is easy to clarify. My personal preference is to be focused on the game, but not everyone is the same and our goal is to have fun. There generally isn't any "abuse" of that because we're all honest and mature, and the rare times something happens that does cause me to raise an eyebrow, it's just better if I ignore it.
