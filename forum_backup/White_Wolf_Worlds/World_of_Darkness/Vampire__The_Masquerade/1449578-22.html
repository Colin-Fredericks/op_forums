    To an extent, of course, but it's no different than any 2e books destroyed for Revised books to get on shelves.

    The point I'm making, is that this is standard stuff in traditional publishing. Old books that don't get sold get destroyed to make room for new books. Ascribing more to it than that is hyperbolic. It isn't a sign of "trying to shove things" down people's throats," or "cleanse the old games from the world." It's just how companies deal with excess stock, that always goes up a bit if there's a new big release.

        It does seem common.

            It's absolutely common to destroy unsold books. I work in publishing and can confirm this happens all the time with big publishers, since they often have print runs of at least 10-20,000 copies a pop to keep unit prices low. That leads to lots of waste.

            Most indie and small presses print much fewer copies (e.g., 100-1,000 at a time) so there's much less waste. But that means unit prices are higher. POD is even more expensive, as you can tell from DTRPG.
