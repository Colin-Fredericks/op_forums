                Another I'd like to talk about is the role of clan culture and clans in general in the Anarch Free States. The LABN sourcebook says there is a "complete breakdown of the clan system that dominates the Camarilla." Many Kindred who arrive in the Free States have "either rejected their clan or been rejected by it". The large coteries (gangs) have replaced most of the functions traditionally fulfilled by clans. Attempts by the Camarilla to divide the Free States by appealing to one's clan loyalty falls on deaf ears.

                I take this to mean that to a certain degree that the anarchs of the Free States see "clans" as a tool of the elders to keep the young divided and unable to resist elder rule. Why should it matter to a young neonate who was the progenitor of their bloodline? What loyalties should one owe solely that five to ten generations ago you and some other vampire had the same clan founder sire your line? There's some merit to this argument.

                Now of course, this does not mean clan identity is completely unimportant, just that it is a lot less than in Camarilla domains. We do know there are certain clans that are less "popular" or Kindred from them are (at least initially) less trusted than others. But what does this mean in practice, particularly for things like clan meetings and other defining features of that clan that should appear in a traditional Camarilla domain?

                I think there is little change for Brujah and Gangrel. Brujah clan culture, to a large degree, is the political culture of the Free States. Their system of Rants basically became the defining feature of Free States "governance". The anarch rants are basically Brujah rants open to everyone. Gangrel are also not much impacted. Gangrel things aren't the same as Brujah rants, but Gangrel culture prizes the individual. And should Gangrel really need to discuss something among themselves, they probably meet in a wilderness place outside LA anyway.

                Malkavians are probably not affected either. First, I think other Kindred would just give up trying to stop them from meeting since its futile. Second, Malkavian clan meetings are already "open" to other Kindred. Malkavians in LA doing their own thing probably won't be seen as disloyal to the Free States. But what about the other clans?

                    Tremere in LA are probably already rare, but I think any that do dwell there would be advised to NOT meet and discuss "clan business". Tremere "clan business" would be chantry type stuff, and any possible ties to the Tremere Pyramid would be considered hostile and treasonous by everyone else. I am not saying that Tremere in LA cannot or don't occasionally meet up with each other. Just that it is ill advised, and they probably work hard to stay away from each other. Even Tremere in a common coterie could be seen as suspicious.

                    Likewise, I don't think the Ventrue meet up in a local board meeting. The Ventrue already possess a fairly high profile in the Free States that one would likely not guess before reading the sourcebook. Fortier is a prominent baron controlling West LA. David Geduld controls Long Beach. Even Salvador Garcia's right hand man, Jesus Ramirez, is a Ventrue, and he effectively rules La Hermandad when Garcia is away (which is often). Three major Ventrue barons probably strikes a lot of Brujah as bad. If they actually meet up like a clan, it'll raise alarm bells. For that reason, I think they don't meet, and if any new Ventrue show up and innocently ask where the clan meets, they'll be politely told they don't (and perhaps explain why). Still, there may be some aspects of clan culture that is still adhered to like the claims of sanctuary and embrace night parties. Given their own high ranks in Melee, Fortier and Edward Vignes might even have dueling as a mutual hobby.

                    I think the Toreador might also avoid clan meetings. Not only is the clan distrusted because of the unique history of LA and the rule of Don Sebastian, Toreador salon culture is at the heart of the Camarilla. Much of their culture infuses the Camarilla and its expectations of proper social behavior. Meanwhile the Free States won't even call Disneyworld an Elysium, but simply a "safety zone." LA Toreador who meet in salons and attempt to replicate the guilds, socials, and clan prestige are probably seen as people wanting to bring back all that is wrong with the hated Camarilla. Small, informal gatherings are probably OK. But I imagine LA Toreador wanting to do traditional Toreador things probably head out for an evening at the Vampire Club in San Francisco, and don't make a regular habit out of it.

                    That leaves the Nosferatu, and I am of mixed minds here. First, I think it would be too tough for anyone to try to police the Nosferatu. So they hold their clan meetings. Having said that, I also think some anarchs might be worried. One reason is that the Justicar assigned the responsibility of combating the anarchs is the Nosferatu Justicar Petrodon. Another reason (though this is much less well known) is that the Nosferatu stick so much together than even Camarilla and Sabbat Nosferatu will exchange information! So it's likely the LA Nosferatu still share information with their Camarilla loyal brethren. While some sect loyalties will still be observed (there are plenty of NPC Nosferatu shown to be anarchs loyal to their cause), I imagine there are Camarilla loyal Nosferatu in LA that their anarch brethren do not snitch out. Whatever their political loyalties are individually, the Nosferatu are still loyal to their clan first (for various reasons).

                    Last are the Caitiff. Of course there is no Caitiff clan culture, and I imagine most Caitiff have adjusted quite well in the system of "gangs". However, this is not to say there is no prejudice against them. And for that reason I'll be introducing a group of Caitiff whose "gang" are all Caitiff and trying to organize the clanless together, in a manner similar to what Joseph Pander did in the Sabbat. There are no connections between the Sabbat Panders and these Caitiff, but I think the dynamics that compelled Pander in the Sabbat also applies here.

                                I saw another relevant bit in a reread of the Anarch Cookbook. As I've said multiple times on various posts, there's discrepancies between information earlier reported about the Free States in early sourcebooks like the Cookbook and Clanbook Brujah, and what we would get in the LABN sourcebook. I think this was likely deliberate possibly because the author of LABN didn't want to beholden to what was written before. As a result, STs wanting to run a Free State chronicle will need to decide how to handle this - choose one or the other, mix the two somehow, or something else? As for me, my approach is to try to integrate the two as best I can while ultimately deciding what to accept or reject.

                                Anyway, the relevant section is called the Anarch Movement which in this book is the name by which the anarchs of the Free State govern themselves. The Movement only governs when there is a dispute. The contesting parties come before a council of their peers who are respected for their experience or power. This council is comprised of 3-5 anarchs. This council votes on the outcome. If one party disputes the outcome, they can appeal, but it is to the exact same council members. If it turns out any member or members voted unfairly, they get kicked off the council likely losing anarch status or even gaining a social Flaw like Notoriety. However, the section says that despite this method, most anarch disputes are handled by violence before any council is convened.

                                Despite not being mentioned in LABN, this can easily be incorporated into it. A call for a council to arbitrate or mediate between parties is clearly something that can happen at a Rant. Presumably one or both sides ask for a council at a Rant, or possibly a third party asks the two sides to accept one. If both agree, 3-5 volunteers agree to arbitrate, possibly selected by the other attendees of the Rant. These anarchs are probably those who have slightly more anarch status than those possessed by the disputants.


                                Newly arrived, unproven, or uninvolved anarchs aren't going to get Jeremy MacNeil, Salvador Garcia, or Smiling Jack to appear on such a council. They'll probably get Kindred like Ariane or Steve Booth who only have Status 1. Kindred like MacNeil or Garcia would probably only join if one of the powerful barons get into a dispute, and both sides are looking for a way to end things peacefully but save face. It's a combination of which anarch volunteer to join such a council, and which ones are most acceptable to the Rant attendees.

                                This probably requires the ST to determine better Status for many of the NPCs listed in LABN. There are examples of Status that does not make sense in my opinion. MacNeil has Status 5 which makes sense, but as he is a figure revered by anarchs throughout the world, it's probably more of a 6 or 7. Salvador Garcia, the author of the Anarch Manifesto and Status Perfectus, the acknowledged destroyer of Don Sebastian, leader/baron of La Hermandad, and active helper of anarchs in other domains only has Status 3. That's the same status as Amethyst and Edward Vignes who have no other claims to status than being barons (and in Vignes' case a rather minor one), and lower than Tara. I don't think that makes sense. Many characters have Status either lower or higher than I would expect. I recommend the ST adjust Status of canon NPCs so it makes sense to them.

                                I'm sure certain anarchs are considered to render the best judgments and respected for their prowess. Some of these could regularly agree to join such councils. Others might be known to volunteer only rarely. In all cases, if the dispute seems stupid, petty, or resolution could be controversial, these anarchs might refuse to join to signify they don't want to become involved. Other ambitious anarchs might regularly volunteer, but not be accepted by the Rant because they have a low opinion of them. This could be a good way to distinguish these NPCs from one another, and how the greater anarch population sees them.

                                For example, despite being respected barons, Geduld, Fortier, and Vignes might not volunteer since they know most Brujah might resent a decision coming from a Ventrue. Maybe you have a fairly minor anarch NPC who doesn't control any territory or lead a gang, but because he's well liked and respected is someone who frequently serves on councils for other low status anarchs. Maybe a respected baron volunteers to be on the arbitration council because he thinks the issue is important, or foresees more trouble down the line.

                                Like with all decisions, enforcement depends on individual anarchs deciding to do that. I expected usually both sides accept the result whatever it is. If they wouldn't, why agree to an arbitration in the first place? But that may not be universal. So if one side refuses to accept the decision, it could be a paper outcome. However, someone who first agrees to arbitration and then refuses it when it does not go their way likely loses respect/status. It also might mean that some or all of the "council" might enforce the decision, especially if they consider the other side to be unfair or that their own reputations might suffer by someone denying their judgment. (Thus not only does one want anarchs on the council who is not only wise, but sufficiently strong to help enforce their decision). It might also convince some fence-sitters to come down on one side since there is now precedent of the community supporting the decision and political cover is provided.

                                Disputes like the gang war in Anaheim probably has had other anarchs calling for arbitration, but because Tom Weaver and/or Alison Maller refuses, there is none and the violence continues.

                                Such arbitration/mediation councils are a relatively minor thing, but I think it's a good way to distinguish how things are sometimes done in the Free States versus a domain with a prince. The first time PCs show up to a Rant, I'll likely show a request for arbitration and have an NPC explain what will happen. That way the concept is introduced. Because the term "council" gets used a lot for several different things in the Free States, I'll probably just have NPCs refer to this "arbitration".

                                        Given the setting is LA and home to a tremendous celebrity culture, I wanted to do a post on Fame. I was not sure where to put it, but ultimately decided to do it here because Fame and celebrity will be a big impact on certain anarch activities. At some point, even anarchs will need to judge if certain celebrities are a threat to the Masquerade.

                                        The Fame background has always been part of character creation. It just makes hunting for blood easier, and by itself isn’t necessarily any threat to the Masquerade. It just means more people want to be near you. Actual celebrity though invites attention from the media, and that is something else. As early as Chicago By Night 1e, it was said Baby Chorus (whose two frontmen only have Fame 2 each) would be considered a “blatant violation” of the Masquerade. Prince Lodin was even planning to hold a Conclave to judge the “transgressors of the Masquerade.” (Lodin himself did not really seem to care about them, seeing it only as a pretext to call a Conclave so he could address other matters).

                                        So what about Kindred with even greater fame or celebrity? Normal character creation allows up to rank 5. Clanbook Toreador 1e had multiple templates with Fame 1 and 2, and one each for Fame 4 and 5 (the Horror Writer and Idol of Millions respectively). Both of those latter templates explicitly mention that other vampires fear they jeopardize the Masquerade.

                                        The LABN sourcebook strangely does not have any NPCs with Fame greater than 1 despite being in the film and television capital of the world and home to a huge music and media industry. In contrast, Annabelle Triabelle of Chicago has Fame 4 due to her mask as “Ellen Stanley-Greer,” the socialite wife of a newspaper syndicate baron. (Presumably Annabelle is a case where Fame does not necessarily link to a Masquerade breach because despite a certain prominence, most people would not recognize her and journalist and popular interest in her is rather low.) I would suggest that perhaps Annabelle’s description and rank of Fame does not exactly tie in together, but there’s always been a lot of that in sourcebooks.

                                        LABN even mentions that the venue A Taste of LA has hosted several “fairly well-known” Kindred bands and name dropped the aforementioned Baby Chorus and two new bands, “Topaz Jet” and the “now-defunct Vital Signs.” (The name dropping of Baby Chorus ignores Chicago By Night’s statement that Baby Chorus was only known in Chicago and the surrounding region.)

                                        However, I think the vast majority of players would expect that in any chronicle set in LA there would be multiple vampires of extremely high Fame and celebrity. Vampires who are movie celebrities, music video icons, and other paparazzi targets. I want to give that to them, but I want to work out and explore such concepts and the consequences of them.

                                                Regarding Fame in Kindred, I have two major notions:-

                                                - Firstly, that for a vampire being famous is a double edged sword at best: even when you’re not famous for being a vampire, enjoying the sort of fame that consistently attracts (or worse yet COMMANDS) attention is generally unhelpful when one is obliged to live a lifestyle uncomfortably like that of a serial killer or a mobster or a double agent.

                                                After all ‘Famous Serial Killer’, ‘Famous Gangster’ or ‘Famous Spy’ are generally synonyms for ‘They got caught’ (and if a vampire gets caught, Kindred or Cainite, Bad Things happen … and not just to their captors).


                                                - Secondly, I think it therefore makes sense to grade ‘Fame’ on a curve when it comes to vampires: for one thing, I feel that if a person is famous and celebrated, in the prime of their pomp and prosperity, they are extremely unlikely to become a vampire (Even at the end of their life) because they already have it made, so why upset the applecart?

                                                It also bears pointing out that famous and successful people are surrounded by potential eyewitnesses (Heck, by multiple circles of eyewitnesses), so even if the target is willing to go from the top of the ladder to the very rattle of the snake, it’s difficult to believe that most vampires would be able to avoid scrutiny for long enough to kill the subject and raise them from the dead without the deceased being noticed.


                                                (I may have noted this before, but I tend to feel that a solid majority of Kindred claiming to be Charlemagne or Frederick Barbarossa are either fooling themselves or lying to everyone: honestly, Heinrich Himmler is one of the more plausible high-profile recruits to the undead because that dude was on the run, out of options and quite literally suicidally desperate).


                                                Or, put another way, a vampire who enjoys Fame as a mortal must either be willing or downright eager to trade in the whole blessed lot if they want to become a successful vampire - because successful vampires attract very few eyewitnesses and leave even fewer in a talkative state of mind, especially over the long term.

                                                Now this process of shedding their fame isn’t always instantaneous (With the obvious exception of a Nosferatu ’Cleopatra’, a Sabbat ‘shovelhead’ and presumably one ir two other exception) but sooner or later a Famous vampire is going to have to shed their mortal fame if they want to outlive their first generation (No matter how obscure their place of residence).


                                                That came out a little more tangled than I meant it to: in a nutshell what I mean is that a vampire who enjoyed celebrity in their breathing days will have to give up that Fame sooner or later; a vampire who acquired some measure of Fame after the Embrace is, by definition, going to live a lifestyle too weird to achieve more than niche fame amongst mortals (Especially when the Masquerade is firmly in place); and it seems unlikely to me that extremely famous and celebrated people are going to make attractive recruitment prospects for the undead in the first place, because to my mind those who do Best as vampires are those who can work the system, but are willing to live outside it, mostly in obscurity (A profile that definitely does not cover major celebrities in any field).

                                                    Something I forgot to add - does ‘Fame’ mean ‘Vampire famous’ (Grading on that curve I mentioned) or ‘Famous period’? (In the sense that humans and vampires will have a a little bit of ‘whoa, it’s that guy!’).


                                                    Anyway, to get back to the main point, I think that it makes perfect sense for LA BY NIGHT to have relatively few vampires who are Famous-famous (As opposed to ‘vampire Famous’) because Los Angeles is one of the worst places in the world for a vampire to remain Famous - given that as one of THE major media hubs on the planet, if you’re very famous then the media will not only take an interest, they will STALK you: they will take an interest (Not necessarily a friendly interest) in every little thing about you and put it up where the whole world can not only see, but where the whole world will actually take a close interest.

                                                    Heck, this applies even when a vampire wasn’t very famous to start with or mostly tries to lie low, because so much of LA is bound up with industries that draw attention from the media even when nobody especially famous is in the vicinity - and sustained scrutiny, however casual, is something most Kindred can’t sustain for very long.


                                                    Add that sheer intensity of interest to the fairly casual organisation of the Free States (They want to maintain the Masquerade, but I suspect most Anarchs are reluctant to pursue the sort of inglorious clean-up jobs and outright resistant to the constraints that the Camarilla are fully prepared to insist upon, with unhappy consequences to those that ignore them) and I suspect that those Kindred who get too famous in the Free States will either be invited or positively obliged to do elsewhere - possibly all the way to Hell, if they get a little too big for their britches.

                                                    Anarchs enjoy their iconoclasm, but they’re not above some Tall Poppy syndrome in pursuit of their own self-interests (and as their preferred alternative to embracing the sort of quiet afterlife, full of constraints, the Camarilla insist upon).

                                                    Put another way, in LA a little fame is winked at, too much Fame will attract something other than admiration…

                                                            The most common celebrity Kindred character concept is likely that of the musician. It’s easy to justify the Fame background, and as a career it’s one which someone who is only active at night can probably handle. There may be some difficulty with rehearsals, recordings, shows, and promotions, but not enough that they can’t be addressed and dismissed by the ST and players.

                                                            Famous models can also be easily done (the nineties was the high of the “supermodel”). If you are successful enough, you can probably get away with a restricted (nocturnal) activity cycle because other people are more desperate to get you than you are for a job. (Less famous models would find their career dead unless they can quickly achieve fame through Presence and the like.) Or the model just goes into early retirement and adjusts to “famous for being famous.”

                                                            Actual film and television celebrities probably have their career ended. Too much money is at stake, and too many people are involved for even prima donnas to demand that they only appear and perform at night. Acting exceptions may apply if the performer begins doing odd independent movies that they can completely control, or if they transition to a local theatre performance. Such odd career choices may provide some satisfaction or even acclaim, but effectively they’ve now become former film and television actors. They may still have Fame and have paparazzi follow them, but their career in Hollywood is dead. This could cause some bitterness unless they really like being a vampire and are good with the exchange, or had already decided they wanted out of the biz.

                                                            There will also be certain executives, producers, and other business end types that technically count as celebrities in Tinseltown. They’ll be important people that Variety and other industry press will report on. LA is an industry town after all even if such people aren’t “famous” elsewhere. If they’re rich and powerful enough, they may not technically hold a day office job. They just make things happen and then show up to the award shows at night.

                                                            Any “retired” celebrities could also work as vampire character concepts. They could be older actors and musicians, “disgraced” creative types or those with reputations as difficult to work with and thus forcibly retired, or still relatively young sports atheletes who have already passed their pinnacle or perhaps been injured. (I’m sure there are lots of other examples that could be used.)

                                                            Los Angeles has all the major professional sports leagues (including multiple teams in the same league), lots of minor league times, is a center for many action sports, is home to lots of retired athletes including non-team sports, and has many sports venues used to host special events. Vampire professional athlete is not a usual vampire character, but it’s just as valid as many other celebrity concepts. Retired athletes tend to still be fairly young and near their prime and don’t need a day job anymore. Plus lots of reasons why they might enjoy being a vampire (fear of aging and physical decline, wanting to compensate for a disability, having blown all the money they made and wanting a way they can still enjoy existence).

                                                            There’s also what I call the fake celebrity or entourage celebrity. They’re the people around the talent. The general public might not know who they are, but the entertainment industry people recognize them, they enjoy a lot of the same benefits as their celebrity friend, they provide a means for others to access the true celebrity so they’re sought be others, and they can become notorious on their own in the right circles.


                                                            (I wish I could find a better video of Gob Bluth as “Getaway” when he was hanging around Mark Cherry in the series Arrested Development. I am very tempted to create a character based on him for the chronicle. He’s such a parasite he’d be the perfect basis for a character.)

                                                            Today there’s also influencers and YouTube celebrities, but of course they didn’t exist back when the sourcebook came out. All we had was public-access television.
