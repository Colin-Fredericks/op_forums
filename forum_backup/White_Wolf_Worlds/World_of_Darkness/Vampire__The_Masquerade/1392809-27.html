                                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                            I am hoping that they keep a lot of Dark Ages Tzimisce and update them for Modern Nights
                                            The reason why I was perplexed earlier in the thread when you set the original 2nd Edition Tzimisce and Dark Ages Tzimisce in opposition with one another is that I don't see the Dark Ages Tzimisce as a change of direction at all. When I write "Necroscope vampire," to my mind that includes all of the trappings and visuals of feudal lords that you are so fond of. Even I don't want to keep hammering home the Necroscope point any more than I already have, because I too think that the Tzimisce should not be direct ripoffs of the Wamphyri, just like the Toreador are not direct ripoffs of The Vampire Chronicles vampires. But Necroscope characters like Faethor Ferenczy and Thibor Ferenczy are proud, regal feudal lords in the exact mold of Strahd von Zarovich, Castlevania's Dracula, and other examples of Tzimisce paragons from various media that you gave earlier in the thread. In the Necroscope series, the vampires in question just happen to be medieval humans who were infected with a parasitic lifeform whose origins are wilder than they ever would have conceived. But that's just another origin story for vampirekind.

                                            Mind you, I wouldn't be surprised if that is what they do because the treatment of body modification by the public has changed greatly. It's not a discipline one should look at being any worse than another Vampire Discipline.
                                            Here is where we have to part ways, though. I don't think classic Vicissitude says anything at all about the authors' views on body modification. From my perspective, that's like saying that the alien in John Carpenter's The Thing is the misunderstood hero of the story -- it just wants to liberate the stodgy researchers from the rigid confines of the human form! You could make the argument, I guess, but you would have to change the plot, tone, characterizations, and even the entire genre of the movie to make it work. Which, in a roundabout way, brings us back to my original question. If a thing (pun intended) was never intended nor designed to support certain kinds of concepts, why take the old thing, rewrite it completely, and insist that the end result is still a continuation of the old idea? It would be so much easier to scrap Vicissitude entirely, including the name, and design a new discipline from scratch that would better support the things you want to see support for, without the considerable negative connotations that this particular discipline has. At its core, Vicissitude has always been about body horror. This is not a good starting point for positive representations of body modification culture.

                                            I have always had the impression that you are very fond of the personal horror angle of Vampire: The Masquerade. Think of how you would react if there was a push to remove personal horror from the game in favor of more positive representations of vampires. Superheroes with Fangs, some might even call it. I'm sure you can see the analogy.
