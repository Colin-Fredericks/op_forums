    The problem of people talking about this development like it was a brilliant political move from the Lasombra where they totally tricked the stupid Ventrue/Camarilla/Whatever, or the problem is a nothingburger, or what have you is that it doesn't really jive with the way that's presented in the material. It's a forced by circumstances move they're trying to make the best of. The Amici Noctis themselves are noted as realizing that they're going to lose a bunch of useful members of the clan to this, owing to the problem that it's nice that the letter of the law is "Sabbat elders", but that in actual practice, the Camarilla princes, not the Lasombra, get to decide who counts as a sacrifice to let a given Lasombra in, and who doesn't count. All the Amici Noctis can really do is believe that the stricter readings of this deal that will kill a bunch of useful Lasombra will eventually lapse when enough have died. They can say which Lasombra are supposed to be exempt, but in the end, it doesn't really get to be up to them. I'm not going to call that a dire mistake, but reading it as "the Lasombra will decide who dies anyway and it's all people they wanted to kill anyway, brilliant!" is handwaving away parts of the game material.

    The idea that only hardcore unrepentant "we were going to kill them anyway" Sabbat Lasombra are dying to this doesn't otherwise bear out. One of the first moves the clan took to prepare the ground for this deal was to straight up kill almost every Lasombra in the Chicago area, explicitly regardless of sect affiliation for no greater reason than to try and make sure they had control over the proceedings that would follow. That aside, the terms some Princes are subjecting the clan to are brutal, and there's not much they can do it about it.

    You can certainly read into the deal that it was not without advantage to the Lasombra, but the idea that it didn't come with significant cost and that they didn't give up significant things doesn't bear out.

    An example of the cost to the clan this is all coming at is that Talley himself, the Lasombra who arguably basically made the entire deal possible as far as his work to organize and put it into motion, ended up Blood Hunted by the entire Camarilla. That's the loss of one of their best people (he's still alive for now, but it's not like he's anything like an asset to anyone but himself now and otherwise having to be constantly on the run), especially in the wake of things like the Gehenna Crusade. There's only so many vampires they have of Talley's caliber and when they throw them away, that's a loss by any metric, and he's just the beginning. The Lasombra are going to suffer for their choice/are suffering for their choice and it remains entirely up in the air if it will be worth it in the end.

    It's also worth noting that for other Lasombra, joining the Camarilla isn't some ploy due to being fed up with the Sabbat, it's feeling that the Sabbat is being literally consumed from within by the methuselahs they went to fight in the Gehenna crusade, being utterly terrified, and needing to find a new sect to shelter in.

    Honestly the biggest winners in the Lasombra deal are less the clan as a whole and more the Cult of Shalim, who now have an entire sect to lurk in amidst the protections of as they spread corruption and their quietly insane end the world/raise up the horrible monster they worship plans, as opposed to being stuck with a group that was known for having an entire inquisition to kill cults like that.
