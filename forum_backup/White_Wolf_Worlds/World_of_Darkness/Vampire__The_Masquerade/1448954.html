    Narrative Dissonance

    05-19-2021, 05:16 AM
    I have almost finished reading my recently arrived physical copy of Cults (love it). I have come again across this odd phenomenon. I wanted to ask you whether you also got this impression and maybe have a better name for it.

    1. So world of darkness works like a like dark reflection. But it does not only look like it. It has a different logic to the real world, an emotional logic.

    The worst of what you feel the real world works like is true in this story world.

    Conspiracy theorists that think the world is run by a shadowy cabal are surprisingly common. It feels logical.
    - I would argue that in the real world this is mostly misguided or even dangerous.
    - In the world of darkness this is actually true and useful

    2. This principle of emotional logic is broken in many places - to me.

    - The beckoning. The old and powerful are called away to never be heard of again (so far).
    The powerful become more powerful and the poor become poorer.

    - The Sabbat are called away never to be heard of again (so far).
    A more violent factions takes over and comes to your home.

    - The big fat corporate HQ in Vienna gets blown up splintering the organisation
    Some desperate fools turn terrorist and try to blow HQ up. The powers that be go on a rampage and create more desperate fools.

    - The world was fairly secular but religion is coming back.
    The world was religious, but it is becoming secular. People feel empty and turn towards new causes.

    - Splintered family that hates and kills itself comes together (though still dysfunctional and with the occasional dead family member.)
    Everyone is 100% only pretending. Murder and theft snowball leading to sure implosion.

    I am looking in this case through the lense of emotional logic, not real logic or narrative usefulness.

    Is there any usefulness to this perspective?
    Do you have a better name than Emotional Narrative Dissonance? (Not counting hate, edition war or whining ;-)

                                  "Deep State" talk is mostly a misinterpretation of what would more accurately be called a Hybrid-Regime.
                                  I would call the most accurate term for the American system a Hybrid-Regime.

                                  The four pillars of this Hybrid-Regime are combination of corporate control over the media, first-past-the-post elections, gerrymandering, and Citizens United.

                                  Corporate control over the media allows those corporations to manufacture consent for economically rightwing policies by framing all issues from an economically rightwing perspective, and starving those disaproved of by corporations of coverage.

                                  First Past the Post elections prevent the rise of 3rd parties in response to corruption. Voting for a 3rd party in such a system effectively elects your worst opposition, which dis-incentivizes people from even trying, and blocks such parties from ever reaching a critical mass. This means that once corporations have corrupted the two main parties, that corruption is effectively eternal, as it can not cause the collapse of the party that would happen in a parliamentary system. Parties in a parliamentary system can easily collapse if they become corrupt, as people have many options that do not result in their biggest enemies gaining power.

                                  Gerrymandering allows politicians to pick their voters rather than voters picking their politicians, gradually resulting in one-party states. This means that corporations only need to corrupt one party in many states. It also means that young politicians are more beholden to party leadership than to the people. This creates a Master/Apprentice relationship between young and old politicians, rather like the Sith. In such a system, networking older politicians and donors is far more important than creating policies that appeal to the people.

                                  Citizens United legalizes bribery, making politicians more beholden to those who have bribed them than to the people.

                                  Any two of these would result in what is officially called a Flawed Democracy. Four of them together moves America firmly into the Hybrid-Regime category.

                                                  For my V20 game, I made two big decisions at the outset regarding the nature of the setting:
                                                  1.) While elements of Mage Lore apply, there are no "True" Mages or Ascension War behind the scenes of this setting. I feel that Mage's "Battle for Reality" tagline takes a bit too much oomph from the other splats and reduces them to spinoffs of the "real" conflict of the World of Darkness.
                                                  2.) Vampires influence society at almost every level but directly control very little. Fear of backlash from a reorganized Inquisition (whether from organized religion or secular authorities) discourages such activities. Ghouling a billionaire or top political, military bureaucrats etc. is not countenanced by Elders who have the greatest stake in the status quo. It would be comforting to lay the sins of history and everything else wrong with the world at the feet of immortal monsters, but in truth humans need no manipulation to establish corrupt systems of control which the Kindred are happy to use. In our increasingly connected, populist times top-down manipulation of society is harder than ever, even as younger Kindred use the fragmented forms of mass media to cultivate herds and carve out little fiefdoms of influence.
