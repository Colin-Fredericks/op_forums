    Dark Ages: customising weapons to superhuman strength realistically

    05-12-2023, 03:50 PM
    This question is less about game lore or mechanics, and more about actual weapon crafting.

    I know that in the real world one can - and would, if professional - customise the draw weight of their bow to match their strength.
    When dealing with characters with Strength + Potence 6+, is it still possible to produce bows with a draw weight suited to them, for more damage?
    In a dark age setting, would there be materials available to make composite bows for them? I have been told that the shorter the arms of the bow, the greater the draw weight, so size would not seem to be a problem for superhuman bows.

    What about thrown weapons like a javelin or hatchet? Would increases in weight or size and minimum Strength required mean greater damage? How much is too much to be realistic? I'm assuming weight can only be increased so much before one has to also increase the weapon's size, and wielding giant javelins, even if possible Strength-wise, would not seem realistic in the game, because the size would make movement inconvenient.

    Similar question for hand-to-hand weapons: what can you increase and how much before the size of the weapons makes it inconvenient to move? My character (currently Str 2 and Potence 4) currently wields a great sword using just one hand, but I might want something customised to my strength when it increases.

              Sufficiently big and heavy weapons would throw off the wielder's balance with every swing or even just lifting it, regardless of how supernaturally strong they are. So whether it's melee or thrown, a weapon can only get so large on a human frame. You'd need to be physically larger (and thus heavier) for it to be practical.                   More Potence in VDA20 always means more damage, naturally, regardless of what weapon you wield. However, a sabre requires Str 2 and does Str (which is inclusive of Potence in this edition) +2 dice of damage; a greatsword requires Str 4 and two hands, or Str 6 and one hand and deals Str + 4. Basically size and weight matter, as they add more dice to the damage, on top of what Str and Potence offer. So if a character has, say, Str+Potence=8, it's legitimate to wonder: can I make a weapon that requires Min Str 8 to be wielded without penalties, but deals Str + Potence + 8 dice of damage, i.e. 16 dice, instead of just using a greatsword and dealing 4 less dice of damage?

                  Having said that, some digging showed me that the heaviest swords, zweihanders, weighed around 2 kg (one hand sword were around 1 kg). Heavier swords, up to 9kg, are seen in some museum but they were ceremonial, not for real use in battle, because they were too heavy to be effective: swing after swing, a heavy weapon tires the arm of normal humans.
                  A vampire with 6+ Str obviously can swing more weight without problem. If the zweihander/greatsword weighs 2 kg, requires Min Str 4 and 2 hands (or Str 6 one-handed) and deals +4 dice of damage, and a sabre weighs 1 kg and deals +2, one could rule that for game purposes a sword requiring Min Str 6 two-handed or 8 one-handed would deal +6 dice of damage and weigh around 3 kg. Basically add 1kg to the weight every 2 dots of Min Str required and keep the damage equal to the Min Str. Following this, a sword for a Brujah Mathusela with Str 9 and Potence 9 would weigh just 9 kg. I read there is such a sword in some museum, i just need to find a pic, but it means it is actually very doable...

                      Ultimately there's a problem where the Disciplines aren't realistic on top of the Attributes not being realistic, so trying to modify mundane weapons realistically to them doesn't really work.

                      Can you make a bow that could take advantage of Strength 6/Potence 6 for draw strength with medieval tech? Yeah, probably. Is it a realistic weapon? That's much harder to answer. Bluecho brings up things like balance, but also things like size of the weapon, size of the individual missiles, etc. If you run things more "fantasy/superpowers" where you can lift a car with super-strength even though cars would just break from not being supported properly, you get a different answer than if you try to be more realistic about physics and just because you can lift two tons doesn't mean the two ton item can withstand being lifted by two human sized hands.

                              Part of the problem with trying to modify real world weapons for supernatural interactions is that physics and terrain restrictions don't go away.

                              In the case of a bow, it is less a question of using different materials and more a question of design. Look at even the smallest of the roman ballistas or scorpions, hand that to a kindred with a high strength+potence score and essentially they can free hand draw it, rather than using the crank systems. The problem is that those weapons are way too bulky to carry around with you.

                              Melee weapons likewise gets complicated because the materials just won't hold up to the excessive forces being put on them. This isn't Exalted with magical material Daiklaives, anything of that size and weight would probably snap its handle after a couple battles and more of a problem the insane weight would mean the vampire is walking around with a couple hundred extra pounds at all times, the dark ages were not exactly known for their building codes, so figure on wooden stairs collapsing, bridge boards breaking, etc.

                              Now the closest thing you could probably pull off is a quarter staff made out of solid iron. The modern day version of this would be a wrecking bar, the enhanced strength just means you could move it at the same speed as a wooden staff. The important thing is that since it is iron, if it gets bent, you just bend it back into the proper shape. While it is true that such a staff couldn't cut opponents, getting hit by such a thing would cave in plate armor like getting hit by a cannon ball.

                                  Lead vs. tungsten vs. steel is entirely dependent on what you're doing to it. Lead is the softest of them, so it will deform most easily. That can make it more durable since you can also bang it back into shape, and deformation can increase the trauma of a wound. But that malleability means it's less effective than steel against steel armor because it will deform on strong armor instead of penetrating and deforming in flesh.

                                  Realistically tungsten isn't great in the context of DA, because it would be obnoxiously rare, and tungsten metallurgy was still fairly limited. Without modern purification techniques, you'd have a metal like tempered glass: incredibly strong - better by most metrics than lead or steel - in one direction, and a light tap will shatter it to pieces from a different direction.

                                  Steel, though very dependent on how good your smith is with different types of steel, is in the middle of the two; one of the reason it's historically more popular in combat use besides lead being used for sling ammo. If you know your steel, it's much easier to get the right mixture of malleability and durability for whatever use you want to put it to.

                                  Metal is complicated because there's so many different ways to measure "sturdy."

                                          OK, I've done some more research.

                                          Medieval weapons weighed on average between 0.5 and 3 kg. That's including twohanded war hammers and axes.

                                          In VDA20, the heaviest weapons in the manual require Min Str 4 and two hands, or Min Str 6 and one hand. That would be our 3 kg weapons. So let's say that each dot of strength allows you to wield comfortably 0.5 kg of weapon, given the fact that you need to swing it potentially hundreds of time and keep balance. This is very different from the weight you can carry (up to 10 kg per dot of Str + Potence before your speed starts being affected) and the weight you can lift one off (which is a lot more, but I don't remember the table for it by heart) by the game's rules.

                                          This means that the heaviest weapon we may need, if we have a 4th generation with Str 9 and Potence 9, is 9 kg.

                                          Just thinking of dumbbells, barbells and kettlebells and the weights they are used for, it seems clear to me that designing a wooden or metal shaft able to manage 9 kg (shaft's weight included) should not be that crazy. It's obviously easier for shorter shafts, like those of war hammers and axes, than for long shafts like polearms, because the heavier weight on the extremity of the shaft of the polearm would increase the risk of breaking the shaft or bending it. It also makes balance harder, but I said in a previous comment that I understand that as covered by the user's Strength. So the main concern is structural, for the weapon itself.

                                          It's also clear that increasing the weight is only useful for slashing or blunt weapons: thrusting weapons like spears, for example, don't hurt more just because they're heavier (except maybe when thrown), unlike an axe or war hammer. With swords, since to increase the weight we need to "fatten" the blade, heavier blades will do more damage but will not be useful for thrusting, only slashing. In this, they become a bit similar to axes. There are, in the world, swords designed to have a broad and heavy blade that cuts on one side (I've seen a few from Asia but I don't know their proper names), for example, and that's what we would end up with unless we want to imitate the design of Cloud's sword from Final Fantasy 7.

                                          Alternatively, one can develop innovative designs suited to the extra weight: one option could be a cilindrical or spherical "glove" of metal weighing 9 kg with a cavity and handle inside, and maybe even some padding there to protect the hand and wrist from damage as they are inside the cilindrical or spherical glove. Punches from these would be pretty damaging, and they could be improved with spikes or blades to slash and thrust. The main disadvantage would be reach, though I don't think that's a big worry for a 4th generation vampire.
                                          Another is a form like a disk with a small round shield on it to add weight and protection and broad blades that come out of it, say on two or three sides. I have in mind some werewolf miniature from the game Confrontation or the Demon Hunter's weapon in Warcraft 3, or a spherical glove surrounded by a circular blade. It would require developing a special fighting style, but it would be doable. Again, one loses reach, but gains other things, including the equivalent of one or two small shields (which in VDA20 can parry at diff.4) whilst wielding a weapon.

                                          ​​​​​​This is what's working in my imagination.

                                          As for bows, bigger ones are obviously not much of an option. Draw weight, which is the measure of how hard it is to pull the string and arms of the bow, from which the strength of the arrow's impact depends, can be increased in an ancient composite bow by adding layers of more rigid (but still elastic) materials and using a string weaved from tendon and similar strong materials, without altering the size of the bow. One may also change the curvature of the bow's arms for extra draw weight.
                                          Let's also keep in mind, like with the weight of melee weapons, that it's not enough to be able to pull the bow once, but that it needs to be pulled hundreds of times. Thus, the actual draw weight of historical bows does not need to be the maximum achievable: they stopped designing heavier bows when there was nobody strong enough to wield them in combat, but the materials and techniques might still have untapped potential.


                                          My difficulty is in that I don't know how far one can go with that before the materials and techniques are not adequate anymore.
