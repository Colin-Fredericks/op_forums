    Messy Criticals and Bestial Failures

    05-30-2021, 12:56 PM
    I thought I'd make a separate thread for this, since it seems to be a particular sore spot.

    I never liked the old Frenzy mechanics. They were too all or nothing. And they resulted in scenes where your character just roleplayed being constipated while waiting to accumulate five successes on that Self-Control roll or to fail it. Just you standing there going "uuuuh must...resist..frenzy!" with an expression like you were on the toilet and hadn't eaten any fiber after a huge meal of red meat. It was bland. It was boring. It was not a fun mechanic.

    Whereas the Messy Critical/Bestial Failure mechanic has many fun results. It creates scenes that more closely resemble the moderate self-control failures that vampires in fiction seem to exhibit. For example, this scene in "Fright Night" can be perfectly explained in terms of messy crit/bestial failure results.


    Storyteller-"A bouncer blocks your path. He says 'you want chicken, man, you go someplace else"

    Jerry Dandrige Player-"I say 'out of my way' forcefully to intimidate him to move aside".

    Storyteller-"roll Manipulation+Intimidation"

    *Jerry Dandrige Player rolls a Bestial Failure*

    Storyteller-"the bouncer is not intimidated. He says 'move me', and his unbearable insolence causes you to gain the Harm Compulsion"

    Jerry Dandrige Player-"I bare my fangs, activate my Eyes of the Beast and Protean Claws, and do a called shot to his jugular with my claws"

    Storyteller-*mentally notes that activating both the eyes and claws should take two rounds, but lets it slide for Rule of Cool's sake* "Roll Strength+Brawl"

    *Jerry Dandrige Player rolls, scoring four 10's.*

    Storyteller-"you land a devastating critical hit to his jugular. The bouncer goes down, mortally wounded. Another bouncer rushes in to help him."

    Jerry Dandrige player-"I blood surge my Strength, pick him up by the throat, and throw him across the room."

    Storyteller-"roll Strength+Brawl"

    *Jerry Dandrige player rolls, scoring a messy critical*

    Storyteller-"you lift him by the throat, crushing his windpipe in the process, and hurl him across the room. Everyone on the dancefloor panics and rushes for the door. In the chaos, you lose track of Amy"

    You can't do that with the old all-of-nothing, you stand there with a massively constipated expression on your face waiting to succeed or fail on the extended self-control roll, rules.

    I adore this mechanic. It's been a "OMG awesome where has this mechanic been all my life?? :-D" experience for me both to play and run with it.

              So, yeah, basically my response last time:

              It's not a bad result if you're trying to represent a certain kind of vampire and certain kinds of vampire fiction. It's a bad result for VtM, because as restated above, breaking the Masquerade like that has insanely serious consequences in the VtM setting. When people complain about the Messy Crit/Beastial Failure mechanics, this is something they think is a negative to the system: a bad set of rolls might make a cool cinematic moment, but when you return to the reality in which that vampire exists, they're now a wanted criminal that could easily have a death sentence waiting for them.

                  I actually think in this example, the scene is not that bad of a masquerade breach. It's not hard to explain away. I've seen far, far worse.

                  Dandrige killed the man with wolf claws. But it would most likely be explained away as a guy using a knife or some other weapon somehow smuggled into the club. People likely weren't even looking at the attack except the one guy who is now dead. Leon of course saw some weird stuff regarding Dandridge's looks, but assuming he survives he might not remember it, attribute it to stress or injury, or simply won't be believed by others. Depending on the specific results of the dead guy's throat, there may be some strange forensic evidence, but it'll likely just be attributed to an unknown weapon. Without doing anything to cover it up, this will likely just get attributed to a bouncer seeing a guy with a weapon in the club, shouting for Leon to back him up, getting killed by the knife guy, and Leon falling off the stairs as he attempted to stop the attacker. Police probably get a vague description of the "attacker" that could fit Dandridge as well as lots of other guys because when fights break out like this, people panic and run away, they aren't trying to get a good look at the attacker. I really don't see anything an ST or PCs would need to do to cover it up. Just lay low for a while.

                  Certainly by sticking to the actions, but describing the scene a little bit differently than what we see in the cinematic portrayal (whose portrayal has different needs than that of an RPG game), it becomes ever less of an issue.

                  As an ST, I would not be too concerned about this as an isolated incident (as opposed to a persistent pattern that would attract more attention). It wasn't the smartest play by Dandridge and the police will be looking for the "knife guy" for the immediate future, but considering the panic there actually isn't much evidence of the supernatural. I would actually prefer this than say the PC failing his frenzy roll and then attempting to kill everyone in the club who couldn't get away, which would be a disaster the game might not recover from.

                  If this was the worst extent of masquerade breaches I've seen in game, I would have a much larger pool of players I'd be willing to play VtM with.

                  Now, I don't play V5 so I don't know the rules of Bestial Failures. If it is anything like the old Botch rules, I could see why people don't like them, as every player I known that played the Storyteller System complains about the sheer frequency botches come up (as well as many other things, my gaming groups hold the opinion that we really like the setting but think the mechanics are terrible). If Bestial Failures crop up too often, it'd be the exact same problem with botches but worse. But as something that is an alternative to frenzy (hmm, rather than roll S-C, would you rather just take a Bestial Failure for the scene?), I'd give it a thumbs up. More options in resolving a scene is generally a good thing. But again, that's not a comment on the actual V5 mechanics which I don't know. Just going by the OP.

                              I've wondered if it could be tied to Willpower expenditure like Imbalance in C20. E.g., if your temporary Willpower drops to one (or, if you like fewer Bestial Failures, 6-Self-Control), you have a Bestial Failure whenever you botch. Or maybe you pick up a Compulsion instead, and this activates automatically, rather than requiring a botch.

                              But I also like Black Fox's idea that you can opt to take a Bestial Failure instead of rolling for Frenzy. It just means you'd need to be fairly consistent in requiring Frenzy checks or you have the same problem.

                              Maybe the ExEss model for Limit Breaks works best of all: you can be subject to the Beast at a dramatically appropriate time and if everyone enjoys how you RP it, you gain a reward. In the ExEss KS manuscript, it's an extra 1xp, but you could maybe reward them with one WP point or something as well (or instead).

                                  The C20 approach is pretty easy to slot in with Bestial Failures and the base V5 mechanics: the severity of a Bestial Failure is based on how much Aggravated Willpower damage you've taken. Among other things, it would also bring back a normal botch for non-vampire characters by having one for when you don't have an Aggravated Willpower damage.

                                  You can work in the carrot method with something like "choose to upgrade a normal failure into a Bestial Failure, and convert all your current Aggravated Willpower damage to Superficial Willpower damage," as a way to encourage players to have their PCs cut lose emotionally, but have some say in it rather than it just being the dice.
