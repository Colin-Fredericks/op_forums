                            Martinelli's Ring of Clear Thought​ is what you need from the '92 Players Guide to the Sabbat
                            It is gold ring was the property of a Malkavian elder destroyed during the Anarch Revolt. A ghoul created it for her as a gift. Wearing the ring removes all fears, phobias, derangements, and any mental flaws the individual wearing the ring might have. Removing the ring brings them back twice as strong, and the wearer becomes obsessed with recovering it.

                            Or find a Salubri Healer, they have a history to tend the Malkavians like how Saulot tended his brother Malkav (finding an Unicorn is hard, but a great source of story - in the last desperate attempt hunting a near extinct bloodline member - with all the attached problems this may cause

                            Obeah •••••
                            Mens Sana (Vampire Storytellers Companion)
                            With this power, the Salubri can heal madness, quieting inner demons and bringing a soul to peace. Indeed, ancient stories of the Salubri state that Saulot used this power to bring sweet, if temporary, relief to his “brother” Malkav. Other, more recent stories claim that Saulot caused Malkav’s madness in the first place.
                            System: The player spends two blood points and rolls Intelligence + Empathy (difficulty 8). The use of Mens Sana takes at least 10 minutes of relatively uninterrupted conversation. Success cures the subject of one derangement of the Salubri player’s choice. This power cannot cure a Malkavian of his core derangement, though it alleviates its effects for the rest of the scene. A botch inflicts the same derangement on the Salubri for the rest of the scene

                            Obeah •••••​
                            Treat the Sick Mind (The Vampire Players Guide (1991) and The Vampire Players Guide 2nd ed (1993))
                            The vampire may cure a derangement of a target. If she first made a roll to sense the derangement, each success on that roll grants an extra die on the roll to cure the derangement. When the vampire uses this power, her third eye and the target both glow in gold light.
                            System: Sensing the Derangement requires a Perception + Empathy roll (difficulty 7). Remember, the Derangement is a survival mechanism and the subconscious will not be willing to let it go. Curing the Derangement requires Charisma + Medicine roll (difficulty Willpower + 3) During the cure, the vampire gets one extra die to roll for every success made on the diagnosis. During the treatment, the target's body is bathed in a soft golden glow from the third eye, and anyone in the area will more than likely notice both the glow and the eye. Also, if the target moves during this time, the difficulty of both the diagnosis and the treatment increases by one. If the target moves more than five feet away from the healer, the treatment ends.

                            Valeren (Healer Path) •••••
                            The Ailing Spirit (Dark Ages Companion and Dark Ages Storytellers Companion)
                            Mischievous demons of madness plague many souls in the Dark medieval era, but Cainites, due to their unholy nature and dastardly activities, are among the most frequent victims of madness. By invoking The Ailing Spirit, a Salubri can briefly drive off the demons that cause insane fits, and bring a subject to lucidity. This ability even works on Malkavians (albeit temporarily), for it is said that gentle Salout himself was the first to use The Ailing spirit - on his brother, Malkav. Some Cainites - not all of them under the sway of the Tremere and their allies - hear legends that Saulot used this ability to enslave his beloved brother for a time. Using this power on mortals know to be mad may attract the attention of the local Church, so the kind-hearted Salubri is warned against pursuing demons of madness too fervently.
                            System: The player spends two blood points and rolls Intelligence + Empathy (difficulty 8). Success indicates that the character has cured one of the subject's Derangements. Failure means she cannot try again that night, and a botch pulls the Derangement from the subject onto the Salubri's own psyche for the rest of the night. This ability is subject to certain limitations. First, the character cannot use it on himself; one's own demons are too slippery to grasp. Second, it can only be used once per night on a given subject. Finally, it cannot heal the permanent Derangements of a Malkavian. It can provide a Malkavian with a scene's worth of lucidity, however.

                                1- mabye an high Conscience, high Humanity vampire would be concerned enough with the people he could hurt due his insanity, and struggle harder to control himself

                                There is the option of asking a Mage - including a Tradition Mage ( for example an Ecstatic or a Verbenae Sorceress ) or a Technocrat ( for example a Progenitor or a Technomancer from the Syndicate ) for help.                                 Other Malkavians - and of course also Malkavian Ghouls - could definitely give definite, particular, optimistic, and good support. It is clear that team effort and group cooperation is an area where Malkavian Vampires tend to achieve victories and successes.

                                    I like Malks as divided mostly between -slightly off all the time- and -very crazy on rare occasions-.

                                    The Beast doesn't exist to fuck with you, it's there to keep you alive. Clan curses aren't there to fuck you, they're their to push you in certain directions and facilitate cooperation with the clan. So with that in mind the madness shouldn't drive you to suicide or be worse than other clan weaknesses. Every other clan can build themselves around their weakness, mitigating it's detriments, and the malkavians should be no exception. Malks can learn to avoid their triggers and build their strategies,abilities and backgrounds to mitigate constant-effect ailments.

                                        Part of the problem, again, goes back to the underlying systems. The Derangements themselves are all over the place in terms of what their effects are, and to cope with them.

                                        Every Brujah is more prone to Frenzy, but this mechanic is consistent for all of them even if you do something to add some personal flavor to it. If you want to avoid a gather of Brujah, or with a few Brujah in it, to avoid getting derailed by Frenzies, the same basic strategies can be applied with only minimal adjustments for individuals.

                                        The Malkavians are a grab bag because of their mechanics. Every Malkavian you add to a mix exponentially increases the difficulty of avoiding triggering Derangements.
