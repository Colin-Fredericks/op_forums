    Hate for Gaia: bring back the Lupines in VtM

    12-12-2023, 06:20 PM
    So, first disclaimer: I don't want a long discussion between VtM and WtA fans. Different games, different spirits (no pun intended), different ways of enjoy the WoD.

    Second disclaimer: cWoD crossovers are usually or sloppy or very crunchy, due to the need to join really different game lines. That's why you have in VtM core books stats for werewolves using disciplines and in WtA core books stats for vampires using gift and similar things.

    So the problem. I use sometimes werewolves in my VtM games and I'm not satisfied with how I'm using them.

    1) If I go full WtA (so a complete crossover) all the umbral and spirit part is usually lost, because my players want to play VtM and not WtA, so the themes and the plots are about really different stories. So also a lot of the social structures, the belief system, the powers and the folklore are... Not useful. They are designed from a different game.

    2) If I go full VtM, using suggestion from the various core books and storytellers handbook, I only have a big Gangrel. An interesting take was one from a drivethru product, "The Darkness Owns: The Lupines" but still I'm not completely satisfied because it still seems to me too semplicistic.

    Of course you can say "take WtA and trim everything you don't need". That is what I'm doing now. But trimming a setting of one of its core element (mainly the spirit world) it's difficult because you usually leave a lot of holes (in the belief system, relationship between septs, goals, tactics,...).

    Am I the only one in that position? How do you solve this problem? I tried searching online and in the forum without good results. I want to stress again that my players don't want to play WtA so going full Garou is not my goal: but big gangrels as suggested by VtM seems not so compelling in terms of narrative depts. I regret a bit having in the core handbooks only small chunks and some stats, and not full suggestion for involving those supernaturals in the setting.

    Of course I can write from scratch everything but really, I prefer to ask before doing something so huge guided by hubris.

      It's not a solvable problem, it's a tradeoff. You either have VTM Lupines that are essentially "suped-up Gangrel" or you have WTA garou with the host of nonsense your players won't interact with outside of encounters with them.
      You can tweak the Lupines/the Garou to be slightly more powerful/slightly less of a mess, but you can't really "fix" it entirely because that's not problem, it's a Feature(from the designer perspective) core to how they're supposed to work.

      My advice if your players don't want to go full crossover, but you want werewolves to actually be tough?
      Use full garou, but only as one-off encounters or a non-descript threat to build up to. The players and their characters don't get to talk to them in any means other than smack-talk, they don't get info on how their powers work, the why of the garou's actions/plans, or any real info on them other than immediate effects(you take x damage, you feel a wisp of power and your obfuscate drops, etc).
      Have the garou drop in, change the status quo and either die or leave as your players are left with more questions than answers.
      This allows werewolves to be intense and memorable encounters without forcing your players to read WTA to play.

          Cut down for simplicity.

          How you use a given splat can have just as much importance as what splat you are using. You haven't explained what you are wanting to accomplish or achieve with the use of the Garou other than being more complex and satisfying you. The players won't know what rules you are using so they won't notice if it is a super gangrel that can step sideways or if you are actually using full WtA rules.

          Is the story or function of the game better with one or the other, if so go with that one. Being an ST regrettably often means what is satisfying to you is secondary to pulling off the game in the first place. You are having a tug-o-war with whose interests are being served.

          My suggestion is if you absolutely must have WtA crunch in your VtM story, then use the were-spiders rather than Garou. That way they are already city dwellers and blood drinkers, which means they also interact with spirits which are in the same genre as the vampires.

          Furthermore, if you want more symmetry of themes and storyline, then make sure all the vampires are from clans/bloodlines that have some sort of connection to the spiritual side of things. Tremere, Baali, Kiasyd, Lhiannan, Tzimisce Kolduns, etc. If the game is more death and ghosts than spirits of nature and the deep umbra, then go with the necromancer clans.

                  What narrative function do Lupines have in your VtM chronicles? What purpose do they serve?

                  Lots of things about the Garou simply will never get mentioned in a VtM again because vampires will never interact with Lupines in that way. Their inner life, their approach to spirituality, their internal divisions and politics just do not matter and never will. It doesn't meant it isn't there, it just means it is immaterial.

                  For most games the Lupines are just the boogeyman who will get you if you leave the safety of the city for an extended trip in the wilderness. Their forays into the city will be rare, brief, and only for good reason (albeit likely unknown to vampires). They don't need to be anything more than that. If there is something more you want to include, that is usually something special you want as the ST.

                  If your players should meet Lupines, what are the elements you want to distinguish them from being "big Gangrel"? Gangrel usually don't run in packs, don't have other forms than just lupus, don't operate in the daylight, don't have widespread mortal networks helping them as kinfolk, don't have supernatural patrons that can help them with odd powers (pack totems), don't have tricky magic items (fetishes), don't make rites or offerings to pagan deities (spirits), etc. (Not to mention Lupines do not need to drink human blood to survive.)

                  I can think of LOTS of differences between Lupines and Gangrel that don't involve aspects that can be invisible to vampire PCs (like the umbra, internal Garou politics, etc.), but could be demonstrated in some format to vampire observers. But only you know what distinctions are important to you that you want to communicate to the PCs.

                  Figure out what are the unique elements that you want to include, and you should be able to figure out ways that they can be included. Sometimes that may be though foreshadowing and other indirect elements (newspaper articles, discussions on local radio stations, rumors by people waiting for subways, reports by ghouls of strange things they witnessed during the daytime, the PCs come across the remnants of some kind of occult ceremony in an alley way near their haven, etc.) rather than directly by the Lupines themselves.

                  The mechanics are probably the easiest to handwave. Just have the Garou do whatever you need them to do (based on what you think their powers should be from your knowledge of WtA), and save the dice rolling only for those actions that are directly opposed by your vampire PCs.

                      Generally speaking, I don't think you need to go FULL-GAROU to have the experience of using Werewolf: The Apocalypse in your games and I think CRIMSON THAW is a good example of what you can do with werewolves in the setting for a vampire the Masquerade game.

                      The premise is that the vampires have unwittingly offended the local Garou sept by one of the Tremere buying a church in heavy debt and putting up some wards. The absolute IDIOCY of this is that the church was a Garou caern and the Tremere has no idea that he's tainted the place with his blood magic.

                      It thus starts spiralling into a full-scale war as the Tremere Justicar plans to let things go to shit so he can get some Garou experimentation fodder, local Kindred be damned.

                      To the vampires, the Garou religion is INCOMPREHENSIBLE but it makes perfect sense to the players and while they'll never see spirits, the Umbra, or so on, they can still understand (if they interrogate a kinfolk or whatever), "Okay, these are nature worshiping animists with a massive amount of rage issues."

                                              Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                              The funny thing is the "Garou kills a Prince by jumping through the Umbra" thing is also a stupid misunderstanding of the rules.

                                              A Prince is going to usually be in the middle of a city in a spiritually dead sort of place with a 9 difficulty to enter and exit.
                                              Not to mention that the local Penumbra is likely to be filled with a variety of Weaver spirits and Banes that will probably exhaust if not kill the Garou before they can get to that location to attempt an ambush. But that is often overlooked by STs and players because many neglect to think about what the local Penumbra should be like. There's often an assumption that the Penumbra is predominantly empty or won't present a challenge to Garou characters because sourcebooks don't really provide detailed examples (though they do give plenty of guidelines scattered around various places). And STs often neglect that because it takes a huge amount of work to map out the local Penumbra in advance, so it is easy to just forget and handwave it. Plus the Garou has to know in advance when and where any vampire will be present beforehand in order to do the ambush which is not necessarily an easy thing.

                                              You also need to think that not only does a 9 difficulty mean you may not be able to step sideways, there's the problem that you might succeed only to not transition instantly or botch and be caught in a very dangerous part of the Umbra. It's a very high risk approach.

                                              Then if you start adding in stuff like high Gnosis Garou simply being in a city feeling the pain and suffering, and higher Rage difficulties, and other issues that are often ignored, there are even more difficulties. Then there are issues of why urban Garou would want to risk pissing off vampires when it would only mean an ongoing war with disposable vermin that will always make new ones. And wilderness Garou would be violating the domain of the urban Garou if they wanted to do it. There's plenty of reasons why Garou would want to minimize conflicts in the city. Wilderness Garou will usually avoid it entirely, and urban Garou have other options to protect their turf and come to some kind of accommodation even if it is informal and unspoken.
