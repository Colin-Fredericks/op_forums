    Why no Dark Ages game?

    12-14-2024, 01:17 PM
    I'm a huge fan of the setting and I've been storytelling for more than 2 decades (continuosly so, I need to add, as unfortunately most ST I know only ST for events, with "one shot" stories, etc, while my chronicles usually span many years, often with the players starting as mortals) and I've been into Dark Ages since it was published. While we had "Vampire Redemptiom" (which was a masterpiece, even if flawed), since then we haven't got A SINGLE game set in the Dark Ages.

    While I still play many modern stting games, more recently the graphic novels, "Coteries of New York" and "Shadows of New York" (I unfortunately didn't like the thrd game in the trilogy and asked for a refund), I naturally miss playing my favourite setting that is the Dark Ages. I'm currently playing "Parliament of Knives" and I'm loving it, but I can't understand why not a single game set in another time. I often ask myself if the Dark Ages line wasn't that successful commercially, because I struggle to understand "why on the Tellurian" is that.

    I have read many of the Dark Ages romances and every book published and we could have many interesting stories to explore there, not to mention develop new ones. I really wished we had a game in the Dark Ages line. Havve you ever wondered why is that as well? Or am I the only "Dark Ages lover" here?

          Why no Dark Ages (video) game? Because Paradox has already admitted that they massively overextended themselves in the late 2010s and branched out into a lot of IPs that are outside their core niche (many of which have not been successful). As such the current executive team is paring down on everything that isn't the traditional strength of in-depth strategy games. When they haven't even gotten Bloodlines 2 out, the idea that we're going to see any other big games from anywhere else in the larger WoD back catalogue is distant chance at best unless something major changes.

          If we see Dark Ages out of Paradox, it'll be something like an official version of the Crusader Kings mod to reskin CK as Vampire.

                                          Even if Paradox was in a position (financially and in terms of in-house technical expertise) to make a DA WoD game in the vein of the Witcher series... the problem is what's the market for Witcher, but you play as a vampire? We here, are obvious fans of the concept, but a AAA release is a huge investment by the company, and results have been really hit or miss in the industry. A single player game like The Witcher 3 had to sell ~35 million copies to break even (it sold ~50 million, so big success), because there's a lot less additional monetization available in single player games compared to game types that support more microtransactions/etc.

                                          Even with a bunch of visual novels and interactive fiction games, and some actual RPGs now, we can see that there is an audience for VtM video games... but is there a "will sell 35 million copies" market? Probably not. So what's the hook to bring in the millions of broad audience? If you weren't a WoD fan, and you were looking a The Witcher 4 (or whatever From Software does to one-up Elden Ring for a different style of dark Medieval-ish fantasy action RPG), or a new Dark Ages WoD game from a company that's never delivered this kind of game before... what's going to push you into taking that leap?

                                              Originally posted by Boldkill View Post
                                              An open-world game like Witcher 3 set in the WOD would be cool, but it looks like it will never happen.
                                              I think the part that they would need to get right for the game is the "verticality" of play rather than the open world aspect. Every vampire game might have different storylines, but they all fit into the same mold of you are a low-level character who gets caught up in everyone else's issues and bumbles one's way through them to change the status quo. But you don't change your relative power level or societal ranking.

                                              To use Skyrim as an example, you can become Thane of every hold, but never the Jarl. You can be guild master of the Companions, Mage College, Thieves Guild, and the Dark Brotherhood at the same time but never King/Queen of Skyrim. You might be Dragonborn but never the new Emperor.

                                              A good VtM game that wants to stand out has to take into account that if your character is successful enough, they will become the leader/power player simply by virtue of killing everyone who messes with you and no one else is strong enough to take the job. Bloodlines 1 allows you to choose your faction, but despite being strong enough to wipe out most of the power players, you are still just a cainite doing fetch quests. You don't become the new Prince/Baron/etc. and even if you did, the game ends without that being a mechanic to play with.

                                              But if you try to include that aspect of verticality to the game play, then you are practically forced to switch game genres to accommodate it. Rather than playing an RPG for the whole game, about halfway through it would suddenly shift into a City Builder/RTS/Management game system on top of your personal role play sections. Despite the climb for power being a central concept of the setting, I find that most people don't resonate with achieving those goals.
