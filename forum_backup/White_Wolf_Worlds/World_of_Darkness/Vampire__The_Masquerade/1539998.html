                  None. While there are some appealing rules in V5 to consider, I've found better versions of pretty much every one of them in other games to pull from instead

                  VtR1e's Atrocity Dice optional rules are something I find more appealing. BtP's feeding and Satiety system make juggling hunger and feeding a lot more engaging where the players want to engage with risks of different feeding strategies on their own instead of it just being about dice; BtP's 'some powers get stronger when you're hungry and some get stronger when you're full,' balancing act also makes concepts like predatory archetypes more organic.

                  Loresheets lifted things so directly from Weapon of the Gods that it's kinda painfully obvious they didn't really consider how to adapt them to the different setting conceits. Loresheets would be drastically improved (they aren't exactly state of the art game design in their original form) by adding the Introduce Facts mechanics from Exalted 3e with the first function of a Loresheet to be an area of the setting your character can introduce facts from (in the original WofG implementation, regardless of what perks you spend points on from your Loresheet, you automatically gain extensive knowledge on whatever the subject is, so even if you put zero points into a Loresheet, your character was still an academic/theoretical expert on the subject). Loresheets would also benefit a lot from a more standardized way to determining what they can get you so it's easier for long time VtM fans to build appropriate ones. CofD 2e's Mystery Cult Initiation, Vigil 2e's Endowments, or the Storypath Path Connections models could all do well in helping making Loresheets that feel far more in-line with what the original concept was supposed to do.

                  So, if I just had V20 and V5, I could see myself doing some pulling from V5 for V20. But having a much larger gaming library to pull rules from for tinkering, I've seen a lot more appealing stuff out there (even with my examples above not even leaving the ST family of games).

                                      Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                      ...juggling hunger and feeding a lot more engaging where the players want to engage with risks of different feeding strategies on their own instead of it just being about dice...
                                      This applies to every edition, but feeding is a tricky endeavor to adjudicate -- not difficult, just tricky -- at its best times. You have to balance what's on the session's agenda with player interest and experience with the system/setting, needs of the story, all while leaving wiggle room for complication and minor derail. How to balance "personal horror" with players simply getting bored/desensitized has been a subject discussed to death elsewhere. At the end of the day, regardless of system it's going to have to be accomplished on a per-table basis with room for adaptation as needs dictate.

                                      My old in-person group played Vampire enough to know what's up, which means "just roll and if you pooch it we'll go from there" does the trick. Lengthy feeding scenes just bored them because they'd all been there and done that, and wanted to get on with the main plot. If nothing else running a feeding scene for the sake of just doing a feeding scene, made them innately suspicious and eager to run down whatever red herring they decided (completely on their own) I wanted them to focus on.

                                      Half of my new group was new to Vampire, which left a lot more breathing room to run feeding scenes for everyone to get their heads in the game and in-character, familiarize everyone with game mechanics, and help reinforce setting and theme. It didn't take long for them to pick up on it enough to start short-handing little things, which is important to build that foundation and start going deeper on setting/theme. It was a nice change for me as a storyteller, to revisit old (to me) topics long taken for granted.

                                      Where V5 goes simultaneously wrong and right, is establishing rules and an idea springboard for the variety of ways a vampire can feed outside norms established by popular culture. And by extension/implication, how certain advantages, flaws, and disciplines correspond to feeding methods. But...the authors could have done a lot better to establish predator type isn't a restriction, it's a preference (with certain clan/merit/flaw-based constraint).

                                          Just to be clear, you quoted me talking about the CofD game Beast: the Primordial. It uses an entirely different "hunger and feeding" mechanics than any edition of Vampire has so far.

                                          One of the reasons I've been tempted to use it as the base for a vampire game (besides the struggle of finding groups comfortable dealing with Beast's baggage), is the whole concept is better oriented towards avoiding so many of the issues you brought up. BtP feeding is done at the end of a scene with one roll based on everything that happened (if you did enough to even get a roll) so it doesn't derail the scene itself to stop and figure out how much hunger you deal with in the middle of play. It's very player driven regarding how complicated things can get, so the level of horror in a given scene is on the players to decide on more than the ST.

                                          It also helps a lot by not having any "good" state to be in. There are two "bad" states and three "mixed bag" states. Characters naturally orient towards one of the three mixed bag states as their default because it best suits how the character tries to balance the needs of their hunger and how they try to justify what they do to feed themselves. There's no strong moral aspect to this either. The most hungry of the three main states is appealing to brutish monsters for how strongly it empowers the Begotten's more direct powers, but it's also appealing to characters trying to be ethical via harm reduction as it has the lowest feeding restrictions. The high satiety state lets you be subtle and cautious in your approach as you grow far more potent with powers that control people by hijacking their nightmares, but with the highest feeding restrictions these can easily be the most noxious Beasts because of how much damage they have to do each time they feed in comparison and they can be more detached from people so they can arrange their meals.
