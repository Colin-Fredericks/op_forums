    It's worth noting that CTPhipps said "mostly."

    Among the three "main" splats, Mages probably start off with the lowest "power level," but end up with the highest, generally.

    Werewolves start with the highest "power level," but have the least potential of the three, aside from the "Bigger Fish" in the Umbra that they can collaborate with when needed.

    Vampires start off in the middle between Mages and Werewolves, but rival Mages for the highest potential of the three major splats. But that's partially because the actual top limits of vampiric power have always been left purely in Storyteller fiat territory, whereas hard rules for the upper limits of True Magic's power have been put down once or twice. Go check out the thread in Mage about building to fight an Ante if you want to dig into that more.


    But it is probably fair to say that Vampires are the least-informed splat about the wider World of Darkness, simply by virtue of how little involvement they have with the various Umbra, where is where a lot of the rest of the World of Darkness operates pretty regularly. Add on that most of what the Technocracy is doing out in the open is going to be very Coincidental, and it is going to be the rare vampire that even picks up traces of what the Union is up to.

    On the flip side, there are going to be vampires that are, to varying extents, in the know, because there are going to be the rare days where a Union member is caught without their mirrorshades, and then they get milked for information with Dominate, Presence, and Auspex. Even then, the fact that members of the Union tend to operate on a need-to-know basis means that few prisoners will be able to give up much more than a single cell or organization, one piece of the network. The number of vampires that get their hands on someone with broad knowledge of the Union's operations, or continuing putting the whole thing together one lead at a time over many years, are going to be very small. And the general trend among vampires is to keep knowledge like that as a private advantage rather than share it publicly, in my opinion. The only reason I could see that changing was if they perceived the Union as an existential threat, where rallying every vampire against it was necessary for survival.

                            Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                            One problem here is that Astral Travel never got to be defined. Are Vampires that cut out of the Umbra, or did the game just silent on the matter because it shouldn't matter?
                            This has always been a major weakness of Vampire. I kept anticipating that in some future sourcebook - a Players Guide, an Storyteller Handbook, a Thaumaturgy supplement, etc. - would provide STs more details on how vampires utilize Auspex 5. What do they find there? What kind of adventures or events could happen? What other kinds of astral projectors would they meet? How can it be used as storytelling device, not just a gimmick to spy on someone in the physical world?

                            I imagine many players would just use the power to occasionally spy on other vampires or mortals and didn't need anything more. But there had to be other players than me that wanted to use the power to do more with it. Or STs that wanted to spice things up for players with that power. But we never got any guidance for it, so any ST interested would have to figure things out for themselves. Sure, we did have sourcebooks in other games, like the Book of Worlds for Mage, that addressed the astral, but nothing targeted for the needs of Vampire PCs which was what was needed. So it was going to take a lot of work. I always thought there should have been a 5 page or so section in the VtM Storytellers Handbook that addressed this. Its lack thereof in second edition is to be expected - other gamelines would be more concerned with the Astral, like Mage. But by Revised once the basics had been established, a new edition should have developed it for use in Vampire.

                            I've compiled over a dozen pages of notes I took from the Book of Worlds, what I liked in horror or fantasy stories that involved the astral plane, and some non-fiction reading done on it as an occult/magic phenomenon. So I think I'm somewhat prepared to actually ST a PC through the Astral. However, ever since I created these notes I've never run a game with a player whose PC ever learned Auspex 5 so I've never seen how it turned out in actual play.

Winning the war for the Consensus is about slowly shaping it, not outright controlling it.
