        "Who are these people?" Amy the Thin Blood asked.

            "I come from a time when travel was done by horse or by foot, when most people never left a five mile area around their home, and I crossed the whole of Europe, alone and unaided.                     My games are all text-based, so quotes are pretty convenient.

                    From one PC to another, on Vampire 101 and Camarilla 101:

                    "First and foremost, you have to understand, really understand, that the substance that René made you drink, the stuff that transformed you, that fills you, sustains you, is poison. Poison. They call it vitae, but it's poison. I'm not talking about any simple physical toxin. I'm talking on a spiritual level. And there's no antidote. None. At. All."

                    "One symptom of the poison is what they call the Beast. The thing that frenzies, the thing that begs and screams for, demands blood. It's pure, mindless evil. Without conscience, morality, or rationality. But the Beast isn't some separate entity, some thing that possesses you. It is you. It's the vitae. The Poison."

                    "Some of your kind, they try to manage the Beast. Tame, appease, or ignore it, pretend it isn't there, always there. Pretend that they're the master and the Beast is their slave, shackled tight and secure. But they're fooling themselves. There is no cure, no mastery. And it only gets worse. With each inhumane act, each time the Beast breaks free and runs wild, the Poison spreads, grows. In time, not even human blood will appease the Beast."

                    "Caroline, if there's one thing you need to remember or understand out of everything I tell you, it's this. The only way to get rid of the Poison, the Beast, is to kill it--which means killing yourself. To die again. To die once and for all. There's a reason they call it the final death."                     With a flick of his thumb, the lighter's top snaps shut.

                    "Don't forget what I said. The Beast, the poison, it's going to try to make you forget or ignore what I just said, to latch on to everything else I tell you and try so desperately to reject or forget the most important and ultimate truth shared here tonight."

                    Lou flicks open the lighter again, his thumb perched on the igniter. He then expounds on many of the inner workings of the Kindred, the Camarilla, the Masquerade, and how all three of the above operate in the Big Sleazy. Notably, he gently but firmly explains how Caroline has not only been grossly, but understandably ignorant of Kindred society, but severely, if reasonably, interpreting Kindred society through a modern mortal perspective. "You can't try to understand the culture of an immortal monster through the lens of a contemporary mortal."

                    In particular, he explains how Kindred society is a gerontocracy. There isn't even a pretense of Kindred equality. He describes the ladder: fledglings, neonates, ancillae, elders. He tries to use her father, her mortal father, as an illustrative example.

                    "Look, your pop is a U.S. senator, right? Senators are like elders, and just like senators, more seniority generally means more authority and power. But you're not an elder. Not. At. All. Fledglings are the equivalent of hobo children. Hobo kids don't waltz onto the Senate floor and try to legislate or negotiate the law. They have no say. They do not act upon the law. The law acts upon them."

                    He shakes his head. "This may sound harsh, but you're lucky it was Coco. You walked in and tried to negotiate with a primogen about the terms of her debt--an elder in her own domain, owed a boon by an unreleased fledgling who'd trespassed in that domain. Any other primogen would have likely have destroyed, maimed, or done far worse to you. Continuing to openly defy the elders' will only continue to bring down harsh reprisals until you fall in line. It's like that quip: 'Beatings will continue until morale improves.' Except, via blood bonds and other powers, they can literally dictate your morale."

                    "Along the same lines, I know you were born with a whole utensil drawer of silver spoons in your mouth. Your whole-damned generation was raised on the teat of entitlement and few are ever weaned. Being a pretty white girl, the world was your oyster, and being a Malveaux, well, I'm sure that oyster was manically coughing up pearls for you day and night."

                    "But Kindred aren't like that. Among the Kindred, you are entitled to nothing. There are no inalienable rights. You have no rights. Only the elders are entitled, if you will, and only so long as they can hold on to their crowns. The Camarilla isn't a democracy, but a fascist monarchial dictatorship. In the case of New Orleans, you can also add 'theocracy' to that list. The Camarilla doesn't exist to protect the well-being of its 'citizens' as ours purportedly does, but exists to maintain and perpetuate the power of a small ruling elite, namely the princes and other elders, which all-too often comes at the direct expense of younger Kindred, much more the 'kine.' The Camarilla and most of its ruling elders belong more to a time and culture of monarchs that ruled by divine right."

                    "It isn't fair. It never was, never will. Poison begets poison."

                    --Louis Fontaine, vampire hunter, to Caroline Malveaux, abandoned Ventrue fledgling

                    Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.

                                Jean-Philippe the Toreador taking Natalia the elder Tzimisce to a place where she can pick up a quick change of clothes after their ship sank (different ship).

                                JP: "So... I don't suppose you know what size you are, eh Nat? It's more of a modern thing. You can buy pre-made clothing now instead of waiting on a tailor, or however you did things back then."

                                Natalia: "What is this... Size? I am this size." Natalia gestures down her body, an eyebrow raised inquisitively.

                                JP: "Size, like... Your measurements. How broad around you are, how wide your hips are, how big your, uhh... ... ... Anyway, you know, that type of thing. Measurements. Size"

                                Natalia: "I am Tzimisce."

                                JP: He shakes his head and sighs in frustration, once more annoyed at the communication barrier. "Yes, I know you're Tzimisce. I don't see what that has to..." And then he gets it. "Ah. Right. Any size will do. Ok, let's just grab this and this."

                                Natalia: "What else can be bought in this place?"

                                JP: "Oh, pretty much anything you need. It's Walmart. The quality isn't the best, but it's all cheap and will do until we get back home."

                                Natalia: "This is good. Jean-Philippe, you must take me to where it is they sell swords. Mine was on the boat, and I shall need a new one if the guards come after us again. Also, I shall need the armorsmith as well. Perhaps the stablemaster too. We have ill fortune with your... Ka transports. We should find horses."

                                JP, feeling the headache starting to come on. "Ok, maybe I overstated. Not everything can be bought at a Walmart. Let's just focus on the-"

                                Nat: "And also, we should inquire at the department where mercenaries are to be rented. Perhaps also we should find the torches and rope sections, and possibly a rack where the bows and quivers are sold."

                                JP's already defeated, but he must continue onward anyway. "No, they don't sell things like that. Just stuff that- ... Hey. Department? Rack? What do you..." His voice trails off as he notices Natalia's smile. "You're fucking with me, aren't you Nat? You've been to Walmart before, haven't you?"

                                Natalia can't contain it any longer and snickers. "Jean-Philippe, I have been awake for the last fifteen years. Yes, I know what a Walmart is. I also know I shall not be wearing that outfit you picked out for me. I am no trollop."

                                                        As I dig through the old files, I found this. Not a direct quote from the players, so I apologize in advance. Thought it may be of interest. Our hero, Sam Taggart's only friend was the Toreador Jeanette LeBlanc. She turned out to be an undercover Setite. This is a handout I gave her player as the character slept.


                                                        The shimmering haze distorts and warp fathers’ distant mansion. In some ways, it seems we only yesterday arrived from France. In other ways, I know it was 147 years ago. Which version is true? What is truth, if not, like the Nile, a mutable, flowing stream?

                                                        Father’s mansion is far away, but the oasis is near. I remember the times spent here with Anwar. Gentle, strong, shy Anwar. Together, we discovered pleasures unknown and when we inevitably were discovered, he left my desperate claims of force unopposed.

                                                        He went to the gallows with only tears on his cheek, my gentle, strong, shy, sad Anwar.

                                                        A shadowy figure lies in repose by the water. Is it you, Anwar? Will you again greet me with a smile and a kiss?

                                                        “My childe.” The soft, but firm voice carries effortlessly over the dunes. It blocks out the sun and the sky. My sire. My world.
                                                        The sand should burn my knees but if it does, I feel it not. I touch my forehead to it’s yielding softness. “Sire”.
                                                        “I have watched from afar. The pitiful Camarilla appears unaware that one of the faithful walks among them.”
                                                        “Thank you, sire”
                                                        “You have already begun your holy work?”
                                                        “Yes, sire. A Brujah. He is unimportant to the rulers of the city, yet possesses great agency and bears little love for his sect. Already, he has been betrayed by the damnable Ventrue. I helped him extract… justice. I pray that in time, I will be able to show him the true glory of Set.”
                                                        The silence is all-encompassing. Is he not pleased with my efforts? I lift my head. Golden grains trickle down in gentle streams.
                                                        Kepi Elsayed, scourge of Luxor, saviour of the Ebon scrolls gazes past me, into the boundless solitude. His eyes flicker with… fear?
                                                        “You are about to be given a new mission, my childe. It will supersede and replace this one. Accomplish this task and you may return to me.”
                                                        The shock makes my head spin. Seventy years of training. Another fifty of subterfuge. All about to be thrown away? The training takes over. I stutter some platitude about existing only to serve, while my mind and heart race with the possibility of seeing Him again. Perhaps I could even bring Sam? Surely, if he could only see for himself the sights I’ve seen…

                                                        “Make me proud, Jeanette. I will spend my nights praying for the Dark Father to watch over you. Listen to the instructions you are about to be given. And… do not fail.”

                                                        With that, he warps. The features engraven on my heart give way to others. Slowly, tortuously, it stretches, straightens, uncoils.

                                                        Nunet.

                                                        Nunet the venomous. Nunet the emerald serpent. Nunet of the abyss.

                                                        It watches me impassively, gently swaying. Suddenly, its flickering tongue spears my chest. I am paralyzed with pain, yet through the agony, I hear its words flowing through me. They resonate in my bones and my blood. They tell me of a treasure, long ago stolen from the faithful, hidden in a golden effigy and smuggled far, far away. They talk of desperate, failed quests. They whisper of powerful rituals, of… sacrifices, of success!
                                                        It is here. Here in Chicago. The Eye of Hasdrubal.
                                                        It must be ours!
                                                        The Dark Father provides for his faithful. Even now, men travel to me from the southern part of the continent. They carry the white powder those in the New World price higher than myrrh, gold or purpura. Once traded, it will buy me the loyalty of many servants.

                                                        Its eyes grow ever bigger. I tumble into opaque orbs specked with gold. Darkness swallows all and I wake. My sheets are stained with blood sweat.

                                                        I look at the sculpted, ashen form of Samuel Taggart. He stirs uneasily in his slumber, tormented by his own demons.

                                                        I know what I must do.


                                                        She wound up manipulating him in order to get her hands on the Eye, upon which she left him and went back to Egypt. It was a shame,I think, in her way, she really loved him.
