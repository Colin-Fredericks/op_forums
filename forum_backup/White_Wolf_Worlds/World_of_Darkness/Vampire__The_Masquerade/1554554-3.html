                    Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                    Leaving aside questions of rating, are there any tidbits of history that fill out Seattle as a setting? (I’m especially interested in information touching on the era of my old correspondent Black Fox’s Anarch Free States game.

                    Are there any characters and setting elements worth bringing into VAMPIRE’s Consensus Reality?
                    I think they did a decent job with creating Seattle as a setting. With no spoilers about the in-game situation but spoilers for background.

                    + Lou Graham and her sister Rosalind were the children of a 6th generation vampire that Embraced them both.
                    + Lou caused the Seattle fire to wipe out much of the city's Elder Kindred and cover up the diablerie of her sire.
                    + Lou built over Old Seattle and a lot of magical caerns and places of power were buried beneath.
                    + The court of Seattle was eventually hit by a Hunter named Santiago who killed Rosalind and all the remaining court that opposed Lou ascending to Prince.
                    + Lou Embraced her ghoul, JJ Campbell, who was a 1920s police chief and made him her Sheriff before making him Prince of the City when she "retired."
                    + Lou has been living in her hotel penthouse for decades like Ms. Havisham. She doesn't even hunt but has vessels brought to her.
                    + JJ Campbell became fascinated by the Blood Trade and made a deal with a Thin Blood named Katsumi to allow Seattle to be a safe haven for Thin Bloods as long as he allowed them to make Alchemical Potions for Kindred.
                    + Katsumi is a former surgeon branded with a M on her face. She is very much a consolidator but the brand makes her hate Campbell.
                    + Thin Blooded Alchemy allows blood that is much more nourishing than regular Kine blood as well as actual superpowered blood. It's also addictive as hell and being distributed across the United States.
                    + The city is INFESTED with Thin Bloods to an absurd degree. The Anarchs are also debating making a revolt against them because the problem has gotten so huge. The blood trade means independent ghouls are more common here than anywhere else too.
                    + The seneschal, Ryong, is upset with Campbell's focus on the blood trade as he's using tens of millions of his legitimate businesses to finance the Tremere Safia's experiments with blood.
                    + Benny the Brujah Sheriff wants to purge all the Anarchs in the city and hates that JJ is making too much money to care.
                    + Benny is a psychopath with a very gentle caring Kindred father named Max.
                    + The city has accepted some Lasombra in the city but Lou is the only one who approves them and only if they pledge loyalty to her.
                    + There's no Tremere chantry but Mrs. Thorn runs a occult book shop and has gotten into the Blood Trade and magic trade herself. She calls herself "Magistar" but there's only one other Tremere in the city.
                    + Ysabella is the local Toreador Primogen who runs a cult out of her nightclub, Atrium, and absolutely DETESTS the Masquerade.
                    + The Ventrue Primogen, Fletcher, is a power in name only as Lou, Campbell, and Ryong all act without him. He runs a piano bar and basically just acts like an asshole to everyone.
                    + Banu Haqim Niko lives with Toreador Patience in a pawn shop. Niko uses his job to find stories of desperation and executes those he judges to be evil. Patience makes sculptures from bones and murder victims' items.
                    + Silky is a big Hells Angels looking guy who is actually a gay man and one of the modern Prometheans, being an incredibly well-read self-taught philosopher and anarchist who is loyal to the Camarilla. He's also lovers with a werewolf and believes Seattle has a spiritual wound that is corrupting it somewhere in the Underground.
                    + The IAO is planning a major operation in the city but has absolute shit luck finding real Kindred but a lot of luck with Thin Bloods. Its led by Mother Hen, a Inquisitor who is dying of cancer and notably deciding that alternatives to death are within reach.

                                    I just finished my review of LOOSE CANNON and I am torn on my opinion because it's extremely good content...for while it lasts. Benny Muldoon was not a character that I particularly wanted to revisit because he's a Thin Blood hating scumbag psychopath with all the depth of a puddle. I wanted more Phyre and Fabien with possibly some of the game's lost content being restored but I always secretly knew that was never going to happen. If they were going to hire voice actors or other people then they would have done it for the original announcement of the DLC to replace the cordoned off Clans.

                                    Still, I have to say this is probably some of the best content for V:TM:B2. Benny has a quality about him that separates him from 90% of Bloodlines 2's NPCs: he's actually interesting and has a personality. Benny hates the courts, hates Thin Bloods, and loves his sire but while this isn't much of a personality, it's more than most of Seattle's Kindred. We get to see his promotion to Sheriff, his reaction to Campbell's death, and what events led him to being recruited into the Sabbat.

                                    The gameplay is really the biggest benefit, though, because Benny can use melee weapons and has a gun that compensates for his lack of telekinesis. Only having the Brujah tree of powers also makes the character challenges more difficult. The ability to use melee weapons is also something that carries over to the main game. Plus, you can reload Benny's gunwiht the Anarchs you kill. I suspect the Toreador DLC will add the ability to use every variety of gun.

                                    The problem with the game is that it is still blindingly short. I finished it at about an hour and a half with no real ability to expand beyond that. You slaughter a bunch of Thin Bloods and Sabbat with a decent boss fight against a Lasombra before it's over. Is it work 15 dollars? I don't know, really, but the improvements to the main game are probably the real benefit of the game. Still, it wasn't boring and I appreciated that.

                                                    On Sunday I sat down and played the new DLC, so I want to add my two cents here.

                                                    The first thing I was thinking about while I reinstalled the game was how my experience with the main-game had been sitting with me. A couple of months has passed and you just kinda reflect on how your opinion may or may not have changed since then. With good games you just go through your day sometimes and you suddenly remember a good moment or character. With Bloodlines 2 the process has gone into the other direction actually. Honestly while I was reinstalling the game all I felt was total ambivalence. The characters, the story, the locations… honestly couldn’t care less about any of it after all these months. Although it also wasn’t such a huge disaster as a gaming-experience that it became memorable that way. Looking back on it now, the released game itself is for me the least remarkable part of this whole affair. The troubled development-history, the turning-point it created for Paradox… Pretty much everything else surrounding this game is more interesting to talk about.

                                                    Anyway, the DLC… I think giving a verdict is very easy here. The content you get from the DLC is extremely short and not that interesting. It’s absolutely not worth 15 bucks.

                                                    I played it for two and a half hours. But I played a bit around with Phyre to look at the quality-of-life-updates as well. And they’re good. There’s some nice stuff there (saving your game is better now, for example and there’s a FOV slider). Being able to hold a melee-weapon is a good addition. But on the other hand, it also does have the whiff of “too little, too late”.

                                                    As for the actual DLC… I’m really not sure why this story was picked. The main-game gave more than enough story on Benny, I think. Then again, I wasn’t a fan of the characters in general in the main-game so naturally I also wasn’t particularly curious to find out more about any of them. Maybe with good storytelling this could’ve been changed. But the story was both very short and not that interesting. Especially because the main-game already had shown how Benny’s story would end. Benny’s super-jump he uses for traversal also made me think it would’ve been really cool if the game allowed access to different ways of traversal linked to what Clan or Discipline-powers you had access to.

                                                    But yeah, on the whole… it’s just worth a shrug in my opinion. It does improve Bloodlines 2 and adds a new story. But in terms of length, it’s extremely short and neither the story nor the new mechanics are huge game-changers either.
