    Black Fox, I’d suggest mining crime dramas specifically set in LA (or at least somewhere in Southern California) rather than importing characters all the way from Maryland to the West Coast - unless part of the character’s background is time in Baltimore - given that gang culture is intensely local and that there’s a whole continent between THE WIRE and the City of Angels.

    I also like your idea of the Crypts being a gang within a gang (or possibly several gangs: it would make sense if ‘Papa Crypt’ made sure to pull his recruits from just about every gang with a suitable composition - I.e. African American ancestry), with Mohammed’s own brood representing yet another level of secrecy.

    Honestly, if you’re going with that sort of layers within layers approach, I’d argue that keeping Mohammed of the Crypts as a Lasombra makes more sense - since allowing player characters the option to expose him as a Lasombra, rather than a Brujah would give them a chance to achieve a significant victory against the character without removing him as an ongoing threat (Heck, ‘Papa Crypt’ publicly declaring himself Lasombra antitribu would be an interesting exercise in spin control that would highlight his ingenuity and resilience, while still having a dramatic impact on his own political situation and that of Los Angeles).


    Incidentally, it might be interesting to lean into the ‘half Anarch, half Sabbat’ nature of Mohammed of the Crypts when describing Sabbat activities within the Free States - I’d bet cash money that there are elements within the sect who question whether ‘Papa Crypt’ and his Sons represent their Fifth Column within the Free States or just a private fiefdom for an individual playing both sides (This interpretation would be especially apt if you run with the idea that the Crypts pull recruits from just about every African American street gang, sooner or later.


    Finally, I’ve been assuming that while Mohammed was Embraced ahead of the Big Sabbat Push in the 1960s he wasn’t part of a mass embrace: whomsoever ‘Tarantine’* may have been, he would seem to have picked the future al-Muthlim for his brains, not a meat-shield.


    *Incidentally, while the name might well have been picked by the author as an homage to Quentin Tarantino, the name itself suggests a connection to the Tarantine cavalry of Ancient Italy (Back in the days when it was ‘Magna Graecia’) suggesting either that Mohammed the Crypt’s sire was very, very old or (More probably) that he’d diablerised a very opd
    vampire and taken their name as a trophy.

        Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
        I feel that Booth's gang should include someone who is clearly a homage to Spicoli from Fast Times at Ridgemont High, as well as a mash up of Alex Winter's characters from The Lost Boys and Bill & Ted (and maybe even a nod to Death Wish 3), and a slightly clueless undercover FBI agent who fell under Booth's influence (giving a perfect excuse to get FBI-SAD entangled in all this). Perhaps also something similar to Courtney Thorne-Smith's character in 1987's Summer School, a young surfer who became infatuated with Booth as an older mentor type and ended up a vampire.
        I’d suggest throw in some homages to the BEACH PRTY movies and the GIDGET franchise (novels, films, TV) at least in terms of names and faces for the coterie - particularly the latter, since it was based on descriptions by the author’s daughter (namesake of the title character) of California surf culture from a time and place with which Steve Booth would have been quite familiar (Heck, since he was born in AD 1942 he would have been only a year younger than Kathy ‘Gidget’ Kohner and may well have been part of the youth crowd she surfed with in the Malibu area).

        Given that Steve Booth seems to have been a night surfer even before he became a vampire, he might even have been one of the inspirations for ‘Moondoggie’ (The fictional Gidget’s love interest from the novels and at least some of the adaptions) - incidentally, that nickname was apparently based on the character’s fondness for surfing at night and I can absolutely imagine Steve Booth wearing it with pride.


        Assuming that Steve is ‘Moondoggie’, one assumes that his coterie would include a ‘Big Kahuna’ (One imagines this as a native Hawaiian who’s been surfing longer than any of them, but tends to be a little less deep in the ‘Surf dude’ mindset than the rest because, well, he predates it - perhaps make him a young US Navy veteran who got made in the Los Angeles area at the time of WWII?); a ‘Sandy’ (A nod to Sandra Dee, the first actress to play GIDGET on live action) and/or an ‘Angel’ (Go ahead, just guess how a vampire from Los Angeles got the name) and possibly a ‘Professor’ (‘Prof’?), as a nod to the fictional Gidget’s dear old dad.

        Bonus points if Prof is the youngest and dumbest of the lot.


        Incidentally, I recently learned that Mark Twain himself, on a visit to Hawai’i actually tried his hand at ‘surf-bathing’ and, in his inimitable style, describes how “None but natives ever master the art of surf-bathing thoroughly” (Apparently he got the right board, the right wave and the right instructions, but lacked the right reflexes): coupled with an awareness that San Diego was something of a surfing hot-spot, this gave me the delightful mental image for a character whom I share with you now.


        GEORGE HANNIBAL GRIFFIN

        - Almost universally know as ‘Huck’ (or, if we’re feeling formal, ‘Huckleberry’), the character in question is on of Tara Kearney’s brood who started in Mississippi in the 1830s (Spent some time as a printer’s boy, as a hand on a Mississippi River boat etc) before heading west sometime in the mid to late 1850s, where he met and was Embraced by Tara Kearney because he had a way with words that she believed would be useful (and which she may even have found delightful, although it’s always hard to tell with that ‘Mother knows best’ poker face).

        - Not entirely delighted by this situation, but willing to ‘live’ with it, Huck seized the opportunity offered by the scattering of Tara’s brood into the War Between the States to get out from under her thumb and head further West (Though evidence suggests he kept up better relations than most with the Boss Lady by sending her letters concerning his own movements and on behalf of those brood-mates he ran into … especially after the War started going quite badly for some of them).

        Asked whether he fought for the North or the South, Huck would always reply “Good question. I got shot in the head, so we’ll have to ask someone else” (“Got shot in the head” remains his favourite excuse and a recurring punchline).


        - Reaching the Pacific Coast at some point after the American Civil War, he’s known to have spent time in San Francisco and to have visited Hawai’i at some point (Whether he actually took a shot at ‘surf-bathing’ while there remains lost to the ages, but he might well have been able to observe the practice, at least).

        He’s very proud to boast a number of Mark Twain stories (The two were from the same part of the world originally, though they took different courses thereafter, and still seem to share a sense of humour), some of which might even be true, but claims that it was he who originally told the famous frog story that the former Samuel Clemens used to make his name in the world of literature remain extremely doubtful.


        - While a fair amount of his career remains unrecorded (He kept sending letter to Tara in Dallas, but he probably didn’t tell her everything and she hasn’t exactly shared her correspondence) we do know that he also kept in touch with the scattered El Paso brood and wound up in San Diego, California, at some point after the rail link went in.

        He’s thought to have acted as the Brujah primogen under Prince Maria (and may well have been one of the key elements allowing her to assert praxis and survive in the first place), with the job becoming much less of a sinecure after the massive influxes of ‘Prohibition tourists’ during the 1920s.

        What role he played in the assassination of the first Prince of San Diego remains obscure, but he was most certainly passing actionable intelligence to Tara Kearney and posse’d up with the text of her brood during the Second Anarch Revolt and played a key role in Tara assuming control over San Diego (At least in the early days).


        - Somewhat marginalised as Tara solidified her control over San Diego (‘Mother knows best’ is an attitude easier to smile at from half a continent away and Huck is an Anarch by conviction, albeit somewhat casual conviction, rather than convenience) he found himself gravitating to the distraction and sense of freedom offered by the burgeoning surf culture.

        As a result, he found himself pulled into the social circles of Steve Booth and perhaps the unlikeliest member of ‘The Surf Baron’s’ extensive network of friends (Often as a friendly rival to the LA surf set, particularly as “Their man in Tara Country”).

        Rumours that he and Marguerite are working to shape Steve into an actual Baron (or at the very least seeking to turn his long list of friends into an actual network) remain unconfirmed.


        AUTHOR’S NOTE: Yes, this is a shameless effort to create a character best summed up as “What if Samuel Clemens became a vampire, not Mark Twain?” (Or, more succinctly as ‘Mark Twain, Surfer dude’), but that mental image was played by a young Woody Harrelson in a moustache and I couldn’t resist it.

        Also, for my money Tara Kearney had to have some sort of informant inside San Diego even before the Second Anarch Revolt (Arriving in California from Texas and somehow finding exactly the right city to make her very, very own domain right after the local Prince was retired, presumably with Extreme Prejudice, isn’t just a stroke of luck: either Tara Kearney had her very own magic lamp or she had an agent in the future Free States).

                        I haven’t quite worked out ‘Blood Types’ for all the canon Ventrue NPCs, but I did want to share those that have occurred to me and my basic rules for coming up with individual manifestations of the ‘Blue Bloods’ Clan Curse in PCs & NPCs:-

                        - ‘Blood Types’ should be specific, but archetypical rather than generic (To my mind ‘Rough trade’ would be acceptable, ‘Males’ would not)

                        Archetypes, after all, allow us an intuitive understanding of what appeals to the character without requiring excessive explanation (Although further explanation might give us a deeper understanding not only of what appealed to the character in the first place, but what continues to hold their interest, for good and ill)


                        - The ‘Blood Type’ should have been readily available during the character’s earliest nights as a Vampire, but not too ubiquitous (Familiarity breeds contempt, after all): it
                        may have become more or less common in the years, decades/centuries/millennia since, though

                        This rule applies more to elders than to fledgelings, but it’s common sense that a vampire isn’t going to starve themselves (Though the more adventurous might well fixate on a Type that involves a certain degree of risk - for example a vampire who was a Renaissance monk might well chase ‘Naughty nuns’)


                        - The vampire’s choice of ‘Blood Type’ should never seem entirely-innocuous, The choice should provide an insight into what gets a Ventrue’s motor running, ideally for better and for worse

                        To my mind the entire point of the Ventrue clan curse is not merely to make them look snooty: like learning a public figure’s sexual preferences, it punctures the aura of majesty and pragmatic mastery with which the Clan of Kings surrounds itself - it is, after all, a Curse


                        One last, much less essential point, is that the perfect choice of Blood Type should have a ‘Good’, a ‘Bad’ and an ‘Ugly’ reason for the vampire to have been drawn to it -

                        - For Example:

                        JESUS RAMIREZ (‘Blood Type’ Bully Boys)

                        The Good - Being the unwilling servant of a cruel master, Ramirez making a habit of drinking from the muscle meant that sometimes the boss had to repress the peasants with thugs not operating at their peak (Much to his master’s annoyance)


                        The Bad - Jesus Ramirez isn’t exactly a Herculean figure and there’s at least some evidence he targets big, burly thug-types to soothe old grudges against those better built than himself


                        The Ugly - as a former enforcer himself, Jesus Ramirez could be guaranteed a steady supply of muscle-headed bullies whether he munched his own master’s myrmidons or the Boss started a rumble with the opposition purely to keep his henchman in vitae

                                    Black Fox , your point about Ventrue ‘blood types’ is well-taken (Though for my money the one-person feeding restriction makes sense on a storytelling level, if nothing else - what better epitome of a tragically, romantically, creepily strong bond suitable for Gothic Romance and Tragedy than a vampire being literally unable to live without someone?).

                                    I admit to preferring more specific than generic feeding restrictions because they help make sense of the Clan Ventrue tendency to wind up in charge: when the very nature of your bloodline curse requires you to make very specific arrangements or perish (Whether through starvation or being caught up or caught out in diablerie), it makes sense that a lineage of vampires would develop considerable organisational competence and a vested interest in arranging the world to suit themselves (Not to mention in defending one’s ability to do so).


                                    Speaking of Clan Ventrue, I wanted to discuss your suggestion in the ‘Anarch domains’ thread that Sebastian LaCroix be a Childe of Baron Fortier: it’s an interesting idea and it certainly gives him a strong stake in Los Angeles, though it does beg the question of where Louis Fortier is during the events of the game, in a scenario where his own progeny is an embarrassment to the whole bloodline (On the other hand it’s interesting to wonder if one of his ‘Brides’ would make a logical choice for the sire of a Ventrue PC, if their ‘Blood Daddy’ were out of the picture and their brood-mate were turning into an Idiot Fail Childe).

                                    Anyway, my preference would be to show LaCroix as a Camarilla agent crafty enough to approach Baron Fortier with an offer of ‘fictive kinship’ - that is, offering Fortier recognition as the member of a prestigious Old World bloodline in return for the Baron arranging some discrete introductions and the occasional back-scratch.


                                    While this may sound like small change to some, it’s worth considering that Louis Fortier was an aristocrat in life who has absolutely no idea whether the vampire who embraced him was the stuff of legend or a Clan embarrassment - being able to ‘reconnect’ with an extremely prestigious bloodline would undoubtedly be worth serious political capital in Ventrue political circles (On top old the fact that having a slightly less hostile back-channel of communications open to the Camarilla might offer it’s own benefits).

                                    That this ‘fictive kinship’ would be of immense value to a Camarilla agent, but something of a liability to a Prince of Los Angeles (Adding Fortier’s widespread unpopularity to the inherent hostility to a Camarilla prince imposing themselves on a former Anarch domain) is a feature, rather than a bug.


                                    For the record, my characterisation of Prince Sebastian LaCroix would be as an immensely competent agent of the Camarilla promoted past his competence as a ‘parachute Prince’ in LA, most likely due to a crafty rival reasoning that no matter how much of a poison chalice Los Angeles might be, it was too juicy a prize for a seriously ambitious Kindred to pass on (Especially if the offer were made by Elders of the Ivory Tower, persuaded that it was better - at least for them - to risk an expendable agent than to risk the creation of a powerful rival by promoting some local hero or handing the crown jewel of California to a Kindred with the demonstrated ability to make a great city their own).

                                    Hence LA winding up with a Ventrue prince who was in over his head, a true successor to Don Sebastian (Whom I tend to imagine as very capable of dominating a small town or even a regional hub, but increasingly out of his depth as Los Angeles grew into a World City).

                                                    Something related to a specific LA BY NIGHT character that I only realised today is that Don Armando de Madrid (Sire of Alonso Guillen of Clan Nosferatu) becomes much more interesting when one learns that Madrid is one of the key Sabbat outposts in Europe.

                                                    Assuming that Don Armando does hail from Madrid itself, rather than using that name as an alias, it’s
                                                    possible that he is (or was) a Nosferatu antitribu - whether aligned with the Sabbat, more of an Anarch or simply Autarkis.

                                                    In any case it’s perfectly possible that Alonso Guillen was brought up outside Camarilla society - and may have sought out his sister in California seeking to escape a vampiric environment less than conducive to maintaining a solid Humanity rating (Or which scared the young fellow spitless).

                                                    Admittedly with tragic results, but that’s the sort of risk you run when you’re THE Most Classically Gothic Vampire in the LA basin.


                                                    Think about it: Alonso Guillen is the disfigured son of a Spanish aristocratic family with a beautiful younger sister who made her life a very long way off from little brother (I’m assuming that a key part of the reason a purportedly successful lady of the Spanish court wound up in a provincial backwater on the other side of the world is that her brother’s deformities were at least whispered, if not common knowledge, making it easier to make a decently prestigious match as a ‘mail order bride’); was so badly-deformed that a Nosferatu (Probably one of noble ancestry, given the title ‘Don Armando’, possibly even one embracing a distant relation) decided that becoming a vampire would improve his condition; nonetheless found himself in an underworld which so disturbed him that he deliberately sought out his presumably-beloved (or at least much-admired) sister - from which one May deduce that his parents were either dead or living a little too close to his Sire’s base of operations; and, descending on his last living relative seeking some human comfort was refused, called a monster, and nonetheless sought to make his sister as he was (Possibly hoping this change of perspective would help forge a closer connection between them); Failing quite spectacularly and killing the only witness (His brother in law) he hares off into the wilderness and lives the life of a wandering spirit (Though apparently one with a continuing desire to receive and bestow kindnesses … apparently tempered by a refusal to acknowledge this kindness and desperation for kindness, given ‘Nature: Caregiver/Demeanour: Curmudgeon’).

                                                    Honestly, this really is the stuff of classic Gothic fiction - complete with all the tragedy of a hideous monster desperate for love and lashing out when he does not (and possibly cannot hope to) receive it - and I’m more than a little fascinated by the character as a result.

                                                    It doesn’t hurt that he seems to be second only to Christopher* in length of years spent as a vampire in LA (and, while solitary by habit, far more likely to accept and benefit from gestures of friendship than the Peter Pan of Clan Toreador), which could make him a key source of information on local history (However incomplete, due to his outsider perspective and lack of fluency in any language but Spanish).


                                                    *It’s interesting to wonder how he managed to avoid the attentions of character who radiates ‘Will kill the ugly for messing up the place and also for sport’ energy: I wonder if Christopher was lazy and self-indulgent enough to have Don Sebastian (In the latter’s days as a ghoul) on the trail of whomsoever murdered two prominent local residents and the Don took one look at that mess, made a half-hearted effort to do some tracking, then decided that discretion was the better part of valour (Possibly because Alonso’s brother-in-law was a local rival whose loss was not resented) and then proceeded to stick with that policy even after he became a vampire himself (Partly due to reluctance to the difficulty involved with finding a Nosferatu who doesn’t want to be found, partly due to the risk of running into local Lupines the further out into the wilderness one goes and not least due to his being kept busy with the work of a Prince with a growing brood & growing city to handle).


                                                    -                                                     Getting intocharacterisation of Alonso himself, I tend to assume he’s somebody mostly willing to live by himself but reluctant to completely sever his his to humanity - the sort of Nosferatu who keeps out of sight, feeding on those who are about to die (Animal & human) but making a point of offering aid (Usually covert assistance) to those persons and those beasts who have been hurt, but stand a decent chance of recovering (I find it especially easy to imagine him treating injured animals and taking a close interest in following the comings & goings of nocturnal creatures of the natural world - particularly flying creatures, with the inherent freedom from Earthly confines).

                                                    He is, in other words, somebody who might well appear in the folklore of Spanish speaking California as an Angel of Death and a Guardian Angel (Possibly without the locals realising that these two are one and the same, much less one and the same with the hideous monster occasionally glimpsed when the sun is gone, but the deepest dusk has yet to settle).


                                                    - I tend to imagine that, after the supremely horrible experience of that botched Embrace, he would be deeply reluctant to risk another such - further contributing to his fundamental loneliness: I find it easier to imagine the other Nosferatu of LA descending from agents sent out by his sire (or other elements in the Clan) to seek him out.

                                                    Whether those agents actually managed to make a connection is an interesting question, given Alonso fled his own kind at least once; it also seems likely, as mentioned above, that Christopher and his brood would make existence in LA very, very difficult for Nosferatu during their heyday (Which would help explain why Alonso is the only Nosferatu specifically mentioned: I may have mentioned elsewhere that one also imagine LA as a town Gangrel have traditionally avoided, possibly due to some ugly history there but also quite possibly due to a dislike of the local Kindred culture).


                                                    - It’s interesting to note that he seems to have struck up an unlikely friendship with Henry Waters of the Vignes household (Clearly Henry lives up to that Humanity 7), but I’ve also seen it noted that Alonso has ties to those two horrible sisters and seems amused by these old serial killers (Strongly hinting that while he’s interested in keeping up human ties, he’s either indifferent or desperate enough to fall in with the worst people in preference to going on alone: also that Humanity 5 may be the latest stage in his descent from an uncommonly sensitive Kindred to someone as monstrous in nature as he is in appearance).


                                                    Anyway, after a somewhat disorganised ramble, my key suggestions are that any depiction of Alonso emphasise:

                                                    (A) His longevity in LA - if he hasn’t seen it all, he’s seen more than most (and remembers it): he’s also had time to make a major impression on LA folklore, as mentioned above, even if the stories don’t have a perfectly clear idea of what and who he is.

                                                    (B) His nature as a Classic Gothic Horror - all the tragedy, pathos and horror of his secret history & solitary lifestyle (I’m not sure what the best way to do this in writing would be, but in a visual adaptation I’d suggest artists really lean into classic compositions, particularly those from the Silent Film NOSFERATU, setting up Alonso as an almost purely Gothic contestant to the prevailing Punk influence amongst the LA Kindred).

                                                    (C) His appetite for kindnesses, both given and received, but also his painful vulnerability to the desperate desire to make some kind of human connection (With the associated risks of leaving him open to corruption by the wicked or triggering some horrifying spasm of rage if he feels slighted by some frightened innocent).

                                                    It’s easy to imagine an ongoing sub-plot of Alonso being carefully re-socialised by a party of PCs, assuming a willingness to take an interest in a potentially-dangerous stray on their part.

My own tastes and perspective are probably less useful to those who want to approach things from a solely canon perspective.

                                                            Having given a little thought to the ‘MacNeil Boys’, one tends to assume that it’s core is what they used to call a chief’s ’Fighting Tail’ (The clansmen who followed him throughout a campaign, shielded him in battle and stuck around even after the fighting was over - not quite a military household or a staff, but more than just a fighting brotherhood) - presumably his close, personal friends like Crispus Attucks and other veterans of various adventures & misadventures, including the coterie which helped to make the Second Anarch Revolt happen (and helped put down the Second Temple of Set*, as well as drive off the Sabbat in the 1960s).

                                                            It’s also quite possible that the oldest and closest comrades of MacNeil act as an example to the admirers drawn to the most famous Anarch in LA from all over the city & the world, as well as recruiters for the best of the local gangs - pulling the leading best & brightest into his orbit long enough for the MacNeil to get to know them, befriend them, provide them with the sort of training that will help them hold leadership roles across the Free States and so forth (Not to mention grow MacNeil’s own network and spread his philosophy)..


                                                            *Incidentally, my mental image of the Nicholas who played a key role in making the ‘Second Temple’ possible, but also brought it tumbling down with his hostility to a rival priestess and curious decision to bring in MacNeil, is that he may well have been a Brujah convert to the Setite cause (Possibly even one with a personal connection to MacNeil), hence his survival even after the fall of the ‘First Temple’ and his willingness to risk bringing in LAs reigning Brujah hero (Also, the curious failure to bring in some senior Setites to straighten things out).


                                                            I’m assuming that the MacNeil Boys - probably a mix of MacNeil’s own childer, his dearest friends, his most devoted admirers and the odd tourist - help make existence a little easier and more safe for their Chief to boot (Although MacNeil would likely emphasise a sense of fictive kinship and an even stronger sense of camaraderie over any sort of formal hierarchy).

                                                            It seems quite possible that while this would be a Brujah-heavy group (Probably with members who go all the way back to the Old Country and the old wars, running from LA to the Midwest to Boston to Ireland and back to the Wars of the Three Kingdoms via the Civil War, the American Revolutionary War and the Jacobite Risings) but that MacNeil would make room for colleagues from other Clans - though I’d bet that Toreador tend to be visitors, rather than part of the inner circle (Given MacNeil’s ugly history with Don Sebastian and the high likelihood of an LA Toreador being of the late Prince’s bloodline).

                                                            Above all, I think we can safely say at least one or more of these fellows will be a bagpiper: MacNeil HATES ‘Professional Scotsmen’ with a mighty hatred, but he’s too shrewd a warrior to deny himself the services of Scotland’s greatest contribution to psychological warfare…
