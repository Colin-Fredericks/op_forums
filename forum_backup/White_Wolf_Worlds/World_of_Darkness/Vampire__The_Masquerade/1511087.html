    What is Malkav's true goal?

    05-08-2023, 06:31 PM
    So, as the title suggests, I'm talking about the Antediluvian himself. I was talking with a friend who often plays as a Malkavian and he asked me this question and to be honest, it's hard to give a good answer. Naturally, we have those generic easy answers like "win the Jyhad", etc. But while we get a somewhat clearer picture of other Antediluvians like Saulot or Tremere, for instance, for me at least, despite my decades of experience as a Storyteller, it's not easy to come up with such an answer regarding Malkav, we don't see much of his actions in the setting as we see in other cases and while many regard the Antediluvian Ventrue as "dead and gone", it's not the case with Malkav.

    So, I ask here to you players and Storytellers, what do you think is Malkav's "ultimate end", what is his purpose or perhaps even more important, especially relating to what I said before, what are his "means"? How does he feel towards his madness, etc.

      I think he may have already succeeded at it long ago. He wanted to achieve true immortality by becoming a psionic being inhabiting all Malkavians everywhere. That way he can only die if every last Malkavian dies, and it is easy enough for a being with his immense level of telepathic ability to prevent that.

      In other words Malkav won, and he won a long time ago. At most, he subtly schemes against the other Antes to keep them from undoing his win by genociding the Malkavian clan. He's only interested in keeping what he has, so his schemes don't amount to much.

      I think that's also true of Ventrue, who I believe has become living Dominate. The reason he has been known to embrace new 4th gens thousands of years after his death is, he has achieved a level of Dominate so great that he can possess any random Ventrue, thicken their blood to 3rd gen for the duration of the possession, and embrace a 4rth gen. Ventrue didn't die eons ago. Ventrue won eons ago, and is now a spirit who mostly relaxes and enjoys his retirement, perhaps just occasionally engaging in a bit of low-level jyhad to prevent the other Antes from undoing his victory by completely genociding the Ventrue clan.

          In addition to the obvious, I have two theories.

          1. The web of madness acts as an escape hatch for him, so his own madness, which I imagine is much worse than in other Malkavians, is assuaged once it is shared with others, and so it continues for as long as the clan persists, the more Malkavians exist, the more his madness is mitigated and the less it gets worse, in this scenario he hopes to one day be almost sane.

          2. He is very angry with Cain for what he did to him and therefore feels a desire for revenge and thanks to his different vision he can see patterns which allows him to know how to manipulate events and obtain the desired result, the Gehenna, where he will get his revenge and the Malkavian clan is his instrument for this, that's why his visions are imprecise so that they only know what is necessary to act as he wants, that's why they have the impulse to play pranks, they are actions that seem random, and most of the time they are, but in fact they are planned and as many of them are jokes in fact nobody sees the pattern. In short, the entire Malkavian clan is a puppet of Malkav without even realizing it, as he doesn't control with orders or charisma, but with impulses that seem natural.​

              Everyone knows the Malkavians are not real. They are a collective hallucination produced by Malkav: each Malkavian is one of his personalities, which are actually psychic representations of his neurons, hence why the web of madness: that's his actual brain.

              As for goal, he's still looking for one, hence why the Malkavians contradict each other about it, some think they know it for sure, some are wholly unsure, etc.: the neurons are still at work examining possible goals.

                      I am aware that, as VtM metaplot and lore advanced, the writers went for a 3rd Generation as god's/concepts and their powers being plot devices.
                      I still like to think as them as fundamentally being people. Really old and powerful people, but just people. I also really like them to lie in deep, hidden and well protected tombs, from which they exert control over different Kindred, from their Clan or not, through advanced Auspex, Dominate and other Disciplines. As for their goals, the same as most people have: survival, power and experiences. They don't really have a master plan for Gehenna, because they don't really know even if there will ever be one. Maybe they don't even believe in it, or know that's an invention of younger, telologically inclined, Kindred.
                      As for Malkav, I envision a deeply disturbed, extremely scared and dangerous individual, who is prone to make unexpected movements in the board of the Jyhad trying to throw their rivals off.

                                          I find it hard to determine what exactly each Antedeluvian wants in the Jyhad. It's not clear Gehenna is a Highlander type situation where there can be only one. While some Antedeluvians may want to kill all the others, some may not (they may only want to destroy some, or none at all as long as they are allowed to live.) Some Antdeluvians might even be allies with one another. And until I 1) figure out how I think Gehenna might play out or even would actually look like and 2) determine how this would be relevant in how I actually run the game, I'll probably never bother to answer these kind of questions as an ST.

                                          Though I do think it's fine for players to speculate these things because these are fun kind of questions to wonder about. It's just they are not actually relevant in game.

                                          I do agree with Microcuchon that I still want them to be actual persons. I don't agree with the idea that they can somehow depersonalize and "become" this immaterial thing or that. Even bizarre things like Cappadocious going full occult bonkers in an attempt to become God in some bizarre Gnostic ritual can be understood in that way.

                                          In terms of explaining the Malkavian Madness Network, I do not hold that it "is" Malkav. Instead, my interpretation is that its existence is because of a unique Level 10 Discipline (likely Auspex) of Malkav's. Without Malkav learning that Discipline, the network wouldn't exist. (Which means for some length of historical time, Malkavians wouldn't have it.) And if Malkav was ever truly destroyed, the Network would cease to exist.

                                          I don't think Antedeluvians necessarily know any level 10 Disciplines. They simply have the potential to do so because of their Generation. Indeed, I would probably rule in my chronicles that most Antedeluvians do not know level 10 Disciplines (certainly none of the third generation who diablerized to that status know any level 10). But in terms of justifying anomalous things like the Madness Network, I think it's a good justification.

                                              Though I do think it's fine for players to speculate these things because these are fun kind of questions to wonder about. It's just they are not actually relevant in game.
                                              As a ST, these things are sometimes necessary in order to make a long term story, I've never been a fan of "one shot" stories, usually my chronicles last for years in real life (and centuries in game), so it's often important for me to know what the highest levels of machinations are really happening in the game.

                                              And if Malkav was ever truly destroyed, the Network would cease to exist.

                                              I particularly like the idea of it "surviving his destruction", like an echo.

                                              I don't think Antedeluvians necessarily know any level 10 Disciplines. They simply have the potential to do so because of their Generation. Indeed, I would probably rule in my chronicles that most Antedeluvians do not know level 10 Disciplines (certainly none of the third generation who diablerized to that status know any level 10). But in terms of justifying anomalous things like the Madness Network, I think it's a good justification.
                                              At least canonically they all have at the very least their "theme" disciplines at level 10 and some others that are not defined and are up to the ST to decide and I really agree with that stance, otherwise, at the end of the day it wouldn't make much sense for level 10 disciplines existing at all. Regarding the ones who diablerized to become Antediluvians, I don't see their powers being any less than the others(in the Gehenna scenarios, for instance, the only Antediluvian who is often stated as being weaker is Augustus, and not because of his age, but rather because he couldn't even "complete" his diablerie, Tremere, on the other hand, IIRC was able to beat other Antediluvians namely his nemesis Tzimisce, in two scenarios if I'm not mistaken), as I stated, the best way to make things in the setting make sense is the idea of a Cainite absorbing all the powers of the diablerized through the act of diablerie, and it has always been somewhat implied that it's what in fact happens on a narrative level. This way, I really love the rules provided in the V20 Guide to the Tal'Mahe'Ra that finally addressed the issue with not so many "if the ST wants, the diablerist could gain some permanent powers", etc. I find it the only reasonable explanation for Cainites acquiring a huge amount of power in a short time span (we have cases of Cainites that acquired level 9 and 8 in a few disciplines in a little more than 1 century). Otherwise, a great deal of the setting falls apart. The rules presented in The Guide to the Tal'Mahe'Ra made me rejoice for finally addressing this situation.
