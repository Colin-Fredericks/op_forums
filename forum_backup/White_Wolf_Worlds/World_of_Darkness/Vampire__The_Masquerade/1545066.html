    Alt-history; Tremere becomes liches

    10-30-2024, 04:14 PM
    So as a mental exercise, what would have happened to the metaplots of VtM and MtA if the Tremere had become liches rather than vampires?
    Say at the leadership of House Tremere (Tremere himself and his six homies) becomes liches and using Vile Magic (tm) prolong the lives of their prefered servants. But they are caught and kicked out of the Order. So, the Tremere never hunt the Salubri, but hang around in the shadows occasionally harassing and hunting mages.

    What would the modern setting of VtM and MtA look like?

      As liches the Tremere wouldn't interact with vampire society other than maybe for a research project.

      Off the top of my head the VtM setting would change as follows:
      1. Assamites would never have been cursed after being defeated, probably leading to the extinction of the clan or decimation down to a bloodline population.
      2. Gargoyles would never have been created
      3. Salubri would be a full sized clan with two internal factions
      4. Most Elders would have ratings in Koldunic sorcery ways rather than random Thaumaturgy paths or perhaps no magic at all.
      5. The Kuei-Jin invasion of the west coast would not be stopped since there would be no Tremere to ward them into a containment zone in San Francisco.
      6. A successful Kuei-Jin invasion would mean the anarch movement on the west coast would have been crushed trying to fight a two front war.

      Without the Tremere being the upstarts and troublemakers that they were, there are a few things that while not directly connected to them, the existence of the Tremere may have pushed certain things into being or certain courses of action. But these are far more speculation than factual.

      1. The creation of the Blood Brothers bloodline, inspired by the creation of the Gargoyles or as a counter to them probably wouldn't have happened.
      2. The Salubri and Tzimisce were friendly clans and given the Salubris probability to join the Camarilla, it is possible that the Domain Lords would have joined rather than being part of the Sabbat, or at least the clan would have split with the Kolduns going to the Camarilla and the flesh crafters going to the Sabbat.
      3. Without the Tremere stealing unlife for themselves, diablerizing an Antedeluvian, and holding their own against three other main clans, it is fair to wonder if the revolt that created the need for the Camarilla or the Sabbat would have even happened.

          Without Vitae/Vampirism in the mix, the Tremere never have a reason to hunt the Salubri. If anything, they're more likely to hunt other Clans with older Blood Sorcery traditions (Assamites, Setites, Cappadocians, etc) to steal their magical knowledge. The Salubri Antedeluvian, by contrast, has nothing to offer Tremere Liches, so neither he nor his Clan would even be on their radar. Not unless the Warrior Salubri decide the Liches are too vile and unholy to live, so they make themselves House Tremere's problem.

          Again, though, I don't see the Tremere wanting to exterminate the Salubri. The reason the Tremere slandered the Salubri and brought Blood Hunts against them is as a political move to keep the rest of Kindred society from turning on the obvious usurpers. The Warlocks turned from upstarts to active threats to Kindred society. Turning the blame on a weak, somewhat disliked Clan was their Refuge In Audacity. Even then, it only barely worked long enough for the Tremere to get in on the ground floor of the nascent Camarilla during the Anarch Revolt.

          If Diablerizing Saulot doesn't happen, neither does the political machinations meant to protect the Tremere. The Liches would just be a shadowy cabal of arcane rivals to existing Kindred in the immediate sense, little different from any others. Many Elders and Methuselahs would barely notice the undead wizards were even around, if at all.

          Circling back around to the Anarch Revolt, I imagine that still happens, as does the rise of the Camarilla. If only because if the Tremere weren't there, someone else would have to do the political heavy lifting of forging the alliance. And I trust that the Ventrue have that task well in hand.

          What I definitely see playing out different is the Tzimisce joining the Camarilla in the Tremere's place. The whole reason the Elders of Clan Tzimisce didn't join (aside from their independent streak, of course) is because the Tremere were founding members. The one hated the other, so occupying the same sect was never going to happen. If the Tremere become Liches, though, the Tzimisce have no beef with them. They didn't have their blood usurped. At worst, individual Voivodes might have beef with the Tremere, as competitors for arcane resources. But the Clan itself will likely not care.

          Obviously, due to the Omen Wars, the Tzimisce don't have a high opinion of the Ventrue either. But, especially in the face of their rebellious Childer, enough Tzimisce Elders/Methuselahs would be willing to tolerate the Blue Bloods enough to form an alliance of convenience. Though they would then spend the better part of a millennia going forward being rivals to the Ventrue within the Camarilla to the degree the Brujah normally are. Which would have the side-effect of preventing a single obvious trio of close-knit "Pillar Clans" from occupying the center of Camarilla unlife. Instead, you might see the Camarilla broken into a couple of major sides (Ventrue and Toreador here, Brujah and Tzimisce there, and Malkavian, Gangrel, and Nosferatu either forming their own block or just sitting as the buffer between the two groups).

          Also, the Banu Haqiim probably don't get saddled with the Tremere Curse, because the Tremere aren't involved in Kindred politics. Not unless the Tzimisce come together to inflict a Koldunic equivalent, which I don't see happening. Making the Assamites a more persistent threat down the centuries. Or at least the Web of Knives. (It's entirely possible, without the Tremere, the more moderate parts of the Banu Haqim - the Viziers and Sorcerers - elect to join the Camarilla. Granting the sect access to alternative sources of Blood Sorcery.)

                                      Loving this topic, it's terrific food for thought.

                                      Taking the "Tzimisce join the Camarilla" ball and running with it, the impact that this has on the culture of the Sabbat is colossal.                                       The Path of Blood, the Path of Caine, and the Path of Power and the Inner Voice still figure prominently in Sabbat culture, but the Camarilla aren't championing Humanity to the exclusion of all other ethoi as long as there are Metamorphosists in the Inner Circle. Paths might be a phenomenon that survives the Dark Ages to become the norm in the Final Nights, with Gangrel, Tzimisce, and Ventrue following the Paths of the Beast, of Death and the Soul, and of Honorable Accord openly.

No need to hidde in Transilvania and reject all contact

                                                  If I remember correctly using spying magick against members of the Order - while widely practiced - it's also forbidden. That being the case, one can't complain against people hidding their auras and stuff - you shouldn't be prying the secrets of other mages in the first place. I imagine this law is a good part of why the Tremere were able to hidde their vampirism fo so long as they did.

                                                  So, I would say that there's a non-zero (but admitedly low) chance that there could be a House Tremere with an undead conspiracy inside the OoH for a long time, perhaps even to the modern nigths. It could be a very dark secret of the Order, waiting to be blown up

                                                  Do they remain active players in the Ascension War? Do they tip the balance against the Technocracy or any of the setting's other metaplot villains?
                                                  This one it's interesting because the Tremere caused a whole lot of problems on their attempt to survive the OoH and the Omen War.

                                                  Most notably, the event that jumpstarted the Ascencion War was the fall of Mistridge (a chantry of very evil Hermetics) at the hand of the rebel Craftmasons. Unbeknownst to most, this victory of the young masons was made possible by a Tremere (Goroatrix?) lowering the defenses from the inside. The Tremere were blammed mostly because it happened after the start of the Massa War, and thus the Massons escaped consequences, and eventually grew to become the founders of the Order of Reason(Technocracy).

                                                  If the Tremere get exposed before the Masons muster the guts to rebel and put MIstridge to the cannon (arround 1210), they could try to resort to the same tactic of opening more fronts of war for the OoH. But they are going to end defeated. Maybe the most powerful (like Tremere himself) could flee, but their influence stops there and most of Mage remains all the same

                                                  If the Masons fail, the OoR doesn't exist as we know it, the Ascencon War could have a completely different face

                                                  It's possible we would have a more fragmented scenario:

                                                  The mirriad of technomancers that formed the Order of Reason still exist, and it's still likely they would find common ground at some point (this ain't even the first time technomancers unify). But the "pro-human" and "anti-supernatural" approach has a lot to do with the justified hatred the Craftmasons had for the abuses of mages (of the OoH) over the masses. W/o the masons as founders, it's possible the "Technocracy" would have taken a far less militant approach against the supernatural. At the same time, it's more unlikely they would've sided with the Gabrielites (extremist christian witch-hunting mages)

                                                  An OoH that would be much stronger, w/o the Massa War and the Order of Reason depleting their resources (and perhaps having one extra house that happen to be excelent manipulators and also an undead-ruled conspiracy). In the face of inquisitorial mages, some small european factions could end being forced to join the OoH, or could decide to never leave (like the Bojnaer House that defected to the Verbena)
