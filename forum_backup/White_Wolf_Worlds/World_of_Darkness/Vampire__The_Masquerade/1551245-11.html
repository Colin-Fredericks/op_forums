                        So, the key bits are probably the bullet points:
                        Originally posted by White Wolf
                        • We liked the concept of Blood Points, but not necessarily its execution.
                        • We liked the constant struggle with the Beast that Hunger Dice provided, but we’ve noted that their mechanics promoted specific styles of play over others.
                        • We liked the tiered Blood Bonds of older editions.
                        • We liked how V5 expanded the powers within each Discipline.
                        • The fact that Celerity, Fortitude, and Potence didn’t have powers in the pre-V5 editions could be improved.
                        • We felt that some Disciplines had real progression, while others felt like a single power that steadily got kind of better.
                        So, this is actually fairly promising.

                        It suggests they're rethinking Hunger Dice (a major pain point for many players who didn't get into V5), but still intend to keep something that captures that struggle. If blood points come back, they're likely to be overhauled to be more meaningful and work better with the Hunger mechanic, but I suspect they might try something different (e.g., like Conditions).

                        It suggests more choice for Disciplines, which could continue the messiness and decision paralysis of V5, but it might result in something more like VTR2e Disciplines + Devotions. Personally, I'm hoping for fixed Disciplines with "modes" for different clans (and the ability to buy additional modes and create your own), but I don't think we'll get that. But ExEss and CTL2e show it's possible and can work quite well.

                        The fact they've played each edition and Requiem as well is a positive sign to me. There's no way you can play those older editions and not fall in love with each of them, even if you don't like their rules 100%. If you create space for all those styles and moods to be played (making it more like V20 than V5), then that's a net benefit for everyone, I think.

                        Also, more hand-drawn art is a huge improvement. I like the B&W art they teased as well (I know it's just concept art, but the B&W stuff works so well).

                                    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                    My problem with Hunger Dice is that I don't like V5's idea of unavoidable degeneration.

                                    Vampire was written for the narrator, as a member of Vampire society, to repeat that BS about degeneration being too hard to avoid, but the rules actually giving plenty ways to avoid it if you take the actual effort, because hypocrisy is the point and temptation only means anything if it's a choice you can reject.

                                    In the end, the Vampire falls because it was the path they chose, and they have no one else to blame. Hunger Dice instead force you to follow clan stereotype and degenerate against your will. That's lame.
                                    This feeds into their comment that they want to give players more control of the story and more agency. I've watched the video now, so have more context on this.

                                    The video specifically covers this, suggesting things like empowering players to decide how their characters lose control, frenzy, or "complicate their lives" (they mention complications, risk/reward, and fail-forward mechanics). That all sounds good.

                                    They also talk about giving players more of a say in how they conceptualise humanity and the Beast for their characters, as well as making characters feel more unique in general. Part of this will include making clans feel more unique again, including "reinforcing the identity" of the clans by exploring how the Beast might be different for each clan (which sounds a lot like Clan Compulsions, TBH, but they may have expanded on this or gone in a new direction).

                                    A lot of the things they've learned, as it happens, won't be news to many of us here. They're the same things people have been saying for years (and sometimes even decades). Case in point: they noted that fans don't like that vampires are scared of lit cigarettes.

                                    During their research, they learned that there is a roughly equal distribution of fans for each of the clans. That kinda makes sense, because I find I'm actually quite attached to them all in their own way, and reading their lore makes me want to play each of them. The ones that come out slightly higher, apparently ("only by like 1% or something"), are Malkavians, Lasombra, Tremere and Toreador. (That the Lasombra beat their rivals, the Ventrue, tickles me no end. But it also means a lot of people must be ashamed to admit their secret love for the Tremere. 😉)

                                    The Ravnos apparently changed the most (I think I'd disagree with that, given that the Assamites have changed loads and you now have the Ministry and Hecata), and the Ventrue changed the least (they note this even carries over to Requiem for the Blue Bloods!). They reckon only five clans kept the same Disciplines through every edition, but I think they're counting things like Oblivion and Blood Sorcery as "new" or "changed" Disciplines in this, rather than saying they swapped Presence for, say, Auspex.

                                    They reiterate that they've played all the editions, including Requiem, and noted all the changes for clans, rules, and so on. They also analysed them to see how they played and what stood out. The person who led on this part of the process was only previously familiar with V20 which, for me, is a positive thing. I think someone starting with V20 will have a different viewpoint to someone starting with V5, because you go from a position of having lots of choice to suddenly having much less, and that should hopefully lead to more tolerance for a diversity of interests.

                                    I was genuinely surprised when they said they liked blood points—which, ironically, commenters on YouTube are treating as a sign of the apocalypse, meaning the V5 fans have finally become "grognards", too—but I think I get it. They said they liked the implementation of them less than the concept, which I take to mean there's a simplicity to blood points that is easier to manage than the complicated Blood Potency table and rouse checks of V5, but that they're probably less interesting than Hunger Dice. They liked the way Hunger Dice made it feel like you were dealing with the Beast, but recognised that it encouraged certain types of playstyles over others.

                                    They also found the three steps of blood bonds interesting, but that never went away, so it seems odd to mention. They did like V5's smorgasbord of Discipline powers, but they didn't like that some Disciplines only had one power that scales with each dot, compared to those that have "a lot of progression". This all made it sound like they're keeping V5's Disciplines but giving them all more "TLC" (as they put it).

                                    However, the community was apparently very divided on frenzy (they said this was an issue that went back years, across all editions) and amalgams. The former is probably to do with player control, going back to that earlier point, but they said it's also about how frenzy is such a binary state. I suspect the problem with amalgams is about the messiness of the system (prerequisites, etc.), the one-power-per-dot rule, and the smooshing together of disparate Disciplines.

                                    They said fans "really like to suffer", which... That could go either way. Like, do I want my characters to have gone through difficult and challenging stuff? Yes. Do I want to indulge in that all the time in play? No. I'm not really into trauma porn or relentless angst.

                                    Outstar talking about art, and said they all looked not just at official art, but also fan art, and art used in posters, book covers, etc., for the vampire genre more widely, as well as across the wider World of Darkness (and Chronicles). They want to show things like Disciplines in the art (Jess said she's a huge Dark Ages fan and liked the way elder Disciplines were shown/described in those books).

                                    In general, photos are out, line-drawn art is in. They also talked about how they can control the artwork a lot more now, and will be developing distinctive visual cues for individual characters, so that even if the same character is illustrated by different people in different media, we should be able to identify them based on their silhouette and other "touch points".

                                    They've also been looking at layout, and apparently have a physical library of books in Stockholm so they can compare how other games handle this. That's kinda cool and suggests we're going to get better layout and organisation this time around.

                                    As well as mood boards for art and layout, they've done the same for the splats as well. This sounds like they mean the "fatsplats" rather than just clans, since Jess talks about figuring out each of their own moods while still making them feel like they're in a shared World of Darkness. She then mentioned how she loves world-building, so we might be able to expect a bit more crossover compatibility.

                                    But they also say they want to support "all styles of play", saying "Vampire is a big game", and noting that people play it differently and the game should support that. That's the most positive part for me.

I like for example how V5 ditched the division of Vicissitude's basic Fleshcraft into 3 tissue-bound powers, which was always a very weird design, without need to create 2 new powers or something.

                                                            I do think it needs refinement, and I absolutely think VtR2nd take on designing the powers themselves is hands down superior. I think the rule for Devotions is a much needed addition and that Amalgams just got too abused in V5, a lot of powers being amalgams just for the sake of being. Just add the need for a teacher for particular powers or some other similar requisites and stop tying bloodline specific powers to specific builds.
                                                            I take the argument for alternate powers in general (as with Arcanoi, for example), but the V5 implementation was awful. If they fix that, most of my complaints evaporate. But they can't try to jam everything into a small set of Disciplines and shouldn't publish a slew of new powers with every book. Well curated lists of themed power sets is fine.

                                                            One advantage of fixed powers, however, is simplicity—you don't need to agonise over your picks and plan your builds. If you have Devotions, you can add an optional rule to let players take a Devotion in place of the main power (provided it's a single-power Devotion or you meet the prerequisites), but you can't really do the opposite without huge sidebars to explain it.

                                                            Rather than lots of narrow, specific powers, however, you could also just focus on broader, more flexible powers. E.g., Protean 2 might let you change a body part such as a head, a single limb, a pair of hands or feet, etc. Wanna grow claws? Done. Wanna grow bug eyes? Ditto. Wanna make your head look like someone else's? That's covered now.

                                                            Or—and this might be sacrilege—we could just remove the linear nature and let people buy whatever powers they want in any order, perhaps with a few prerequisites if it absolutely makes sense. It'll require a bit of reworking, but it's shown to be feasible with Loresheets. Don't want Protean 3? Fine. Skip it.
