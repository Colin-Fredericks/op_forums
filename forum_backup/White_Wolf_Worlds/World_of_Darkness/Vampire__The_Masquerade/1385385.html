    [V5] Racism, Sexism and other problematic content

    05-03-2020, 07:45 AM
    As a disclaimer: I am a huge fan of V5, I am a playtester of the game and an old-time fan, playing since 1st edition. This post is not about the rules or the lore of V5.

    Now. For the last few months, I am running a campaign in Chicago, using the Onyx Path books of Chicago By Night and the Chicago Folios. And I must say that these books are very hard to read. The writers surely have a political agenda here, but I am not sure that they even understand their own agenda. At one side characters are described as "Obnoxiously Male" and on the other side, all of the disenfranchised clans (Caitiff and Thin-Blood) consist of minorities.
    LGBTQ+ NPCs are glorified as heroes, but they prey and torture the same community they hail from, even though the writers describe them as protectors of those communities. You have "woke" characters that prey on "bigoted white woman", and "homophobes", or have an ambition to "Break the nose of the next man who tells you what to do", but at the same time, these characters are flat, with no real depth or impact on Chicago, because the city is mostly ruled by characters we know from the previous books.

    I understand that this is the world of darkness, but sometimes I don't even understand who is the target audience for these books. Should people who identify as males feel bad about themselves? why every black character in these books deals somehow with crime or drugs or both? you guys should know better. Every NPC appears to be something new and special, but when you read into it you realize that the vast majority of them are an amalgamation of stereotypes and political slogans stitched together as some bizarre fan fiction monstrosity.

    White Wolf always pushed for equality, you can see it in books like the Revised Followers of Set clanbook for example. But I never felt like they hate the reader, the customer. It never felt like they are trying to preach. How can someone even be "Obnoxiously [insert gender here]"? it's not something you usually choose. I don't get these statements.

    Anyway, sorry for the rambling, I am very emotional about it, because, to me, it seems very hypocritical, and I am sure I will see more of it in the Cults of The Blood Gods book.

      This started showing itself the later half of the 20th aniversary editions, it's not a V5 exclusive problem. Some of it made me think "is this parody?" Like I genuinely couldn't tell if the Ahrimane section in DAV20 was a joke or not.

      The World of Darkness is meant to be our world but darker. It's a satire. It's meant to exaggerate and highlight problems through the cunning use of vampires (and other supernaturals). When everything's dark and bleak except for, say, "the femenists", the reader's mind starts to wonder if "the feminists" being good and happy and strong is part of what makes the world so shit. Some of the present writers clearly don't have any genre awareness, they just want to push their power fantasy, and it's clearly counter productive for actually getting their politics results.

      I'd rather have no representation than bad representation. I'd rather provide food for thought than hand over ammunition to an enemy. That's what WoD feels like; Some neoliberal hijacked it, pushed low quality identity politics to the forefront in order to create a stir and distract from the traditional themes of the series, and made what used to be an intelligent satire into a screaming Karen with dyed hair

          I'm on the right of the political spectrum, but although I see your point to a degree, I don't feel that you're being fair. If you look at, for instance, Abraham DuSable, he's not a criminal or a drug dealer; he's a cultured, accomplished lawyer who was held back by the racism of his day.

          Many of the writers do seem to be very left-wing, and every now and then they'll show off the tolerant, open-minded inclusivity so characteristic of the modern left by saying damn' silly things like "obnoxiously male" (just out of curiosity, where was that? I don't recall seeing it). Nonetheless, I don't get a sense that they're pushing an agenda in a way that's unbalanced. They have not, to pick one example at random, tried to pretend that Bret Stryker is anything other than a grade-A 24-carat asshole just because he happens to be bisexual.

          They have acknowledged, to a degree that the older books perhaps didn't, that pervasive racism and other prejudices do exist and do blight lives, especially in cities with a majority non-white population like Chicago. To take another example, Naomi Stewart isn't, to my mind, shown as an unambiguous heroine. She's portrayed as tough, ambitious, and unwilling to to cowed or kept down by unjust prejudice, but also willing to kill in self-defence and perhaps to murder. She is an admirable character in a great many ways, (and a blessed reassurance that the whole "vampires are evullllll" schtick that pervaded the core book isn't going to blight the V5 line), but she is nuanced. She has the same flickering interplay of light and dark that make the principals in LA by Night interesting.

          Nor do I find it unrealistic or unjustified that vampire characters should target mortals who exhibit forms of bigotry from which they suffered in life. There was an episode of Dr. Who a couple of years ago, about Rosa Parks. By the end of it I was quietly seething with rage. I would cheerfully have horsewhipped some of the racist characters it portrayed to within an inch of their misbegotten lives. If a fifty-minute fictional TV programme could affect me that way, imagine someone who'd experienced it for real, day in, day out, for years, suddenly acquiring Kindred powers. Their likely reaction would make the archetypal high school list look anaemic.

          As I said, I'm on the political right, and yes, every now and then I'll come across something that makes me roll my eyes and mutter, "Mummy, look at me, I'm virtue-signalling". But those are few and far between. By and large, I think the Chicago books have excellently-written characters and plots which draw on a broader and deeper social spectrum then the previous editions did. I think they're a tremendous success which adds a lot to a setting which was already a favourite of mine.

              Welcome to the post-2015(ish) rpg scene (or nearly any hobby or subculture scene, that tend to be left-leaning, by and large). These things aren't constrained to V5, or WW, or OPP, sadly.

              Honestly, I feel the Revised material was a lot more mature and balanced on these things, frequeantly. The WoD and WW were always leftist-liberal/progressive (let's be honest, most of the core themes of the games are like that) and I never took issue with that, honestly, I consider myself to be that too and the books were pretty much in tune with my worldview. It's just, the zeitgeist of the present is ripe with overdoing everything, extremities in general, misplaced "wokeness" frequently coming off as outright hate and spite (or rose-tinted glasses and over-romanticization to the other direction), or bordering it and the whole stuff being full of inconsistencies. The OPP 20th books (especially the earlier ones) are largely good, though it depends on the writers and lead developer. For example, when I pointed out that the overwhelming majority (practically all) of the characters presented on the illustrations as "cool warrior PCs/protagonists" in the DAV20 corebook were female, which is, IMO going a bit too far, in a game, that is supposedly not has a super-equalistic fantasy world as a background, but our very history, well, people, including the writers and OPP staff didn't take it well. But that's just one example. On the other hand, I generally like Phill Brucatto's work on M20, but let's acknowledge that the Disparate Alliance is pretty much "minorities assemble against the white MAN" with precious little thought behind that. Because the Traditions weren't good enough for that. These and similar things are really the products of these times.

              On the positive side: I think the worst of this wave maybe abated a bit in the past couple of years. I definitely felt it raged the most from '15-'16 to '19 but at this point, everybody slowly getting bored/exhausted with it. Or, it's just me, who did and seeing less of it is just a by-product of not visiting forums, groups and threads which were affected by it the worst way, dunno.



              If nothing worked, then let's think!
