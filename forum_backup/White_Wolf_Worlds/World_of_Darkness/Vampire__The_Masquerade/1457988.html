    TC, pt. 19: "You walk in and see some dude with a full hog flaying a fat guy"

    08-29-2021, 01:57 PM
    Last installment's here.

    As you can probably tell by the title, it got weird. Thank the Lasombra PC.

    Thankfully not much happened, so hopefully this post will be brief. Luckily I set up the story in the last thread, so I don't have to spend a lot of time on it here -- read that one if you need the details.

    TLDR: it's 1442, the Ottomans are running buck wild in Transylvania, the PC's are in Sibiu right in the middle of the tracks on the Turk Train, and the Turk Train ain't got no brakes. So naturally, they get letters from Bulscu calling in those boons owed to him, telling them to stand their ground and fight for Transylvania and Christendom, and all that bullshit. The letter's otherwise scant on details, which leaves the coterie scratching their heads a bit.

    I should point out, the underlying theme of this whole story is "use Auspex, motherfuckers". PC's with Common Sense: zero. Uses of Spirit's Touch on the letter they were given: zero. Might've helped, just saying.

    They go to the Prince, Eyulf, and let him know their boons are being called in. Eyulf was planning on doing the smart thing: not being in town when the Assamites show up, and to hell with everyone else. The one vampire they notice isn't around is Eyulf's seneschal, but we'll come back to that in a minute. After a bit of strategizing, they come to the conclusion based on a general lack of details in the letter, Bulscu's probably sending someone to the city to make sure the coterie plans to make good on the debts they owe, and formulate a plan for holding the city. Sensible, if Assamites and Anarchs are around, it's better to not provide vital intelligence in a letter carried by a simple, vulnerable, courier.

    So if said representative showed up to an abandoned city, free real estate for the Ventrue when the blood and dust settles. Plan A (run away) abandoned, the coterie and Eyulf work out their next move. They figure out the real threat at hand isn't the Turks but the Assamites, and it's more likely than not the Assamites will be moving ahead of the Turks to sabotage the fuck out of the city's defenses, and offing key vampires, as a prelude to the attack. Because that's what the Assamites did decades prior in Brasov, leading to the city's fall and occupation by the Ashirra.

    I'll say, the player who's now playing the Tzimisce is doing a fucking baller job at it, learning to ask the right questions at the right time and figuring out things on their own. They're the one who remembered Brasov is now an Ashirra city, thought to ask how the Assamites pulled that off, figuring the Assamites would replicate the strategy. Massive leap forward for this player, who had previously just played reactive beat-stick characters who didn't do much in the long run. But more heaping praise for this player later, for flourishing under the constraint of playing a Tzimisce on Humanity, with a Creep Show demeanor, and tying that together by playing up the "menacingly polite" aspect of Tzimisce characterization.

    They're also the player that remembered, "wait, Assamites have Obfuscate which means they could be literally anywhere, even in this room!". Cue a round of paranoia, PC's looking all around them, and Perception checks with Heightened Senses up.

    Then, upon seeing this, does the city's seneschal drop his Obfuscate, revealing himself as having been in the room the entire time and straight-up telling them had he been an Assamite, they'd already all be dead. Eyulf was already entirely aware Yusuf (the seneschal) was in the room; what Eyulf wanted to know, was who had Auspex and who didn't among the PC's.

    "Use Auspex, motherfuckers" tests failed: two of two.

    Eyulf commands no vampire in the city is to be left alone, and they will at all times be accompanied by another with Auspex, as a precaution against infiltration and sabotage. Thus, they pair off: the Lasombra and Tzimisce will keep an eye out for worthy mortals to ghoul to act as "improved" defense for the city while looking out for signs of sabotage, the Setite will go with Yusuf to prepare traps ahead of time for the Assamites, and the Gangrel will scout in flight form using the keep as a base while Eyulf secures the keep from infiltrators.

    The Gangrel's part in this is quick and straightforward. He flied out, saw a wagon bearing Hungarian heraldry and a retinue of knights on its way to Sibiu, and saw it get promptly attacked from both sides of the road with flaming arrows and thrown jars of pitch. This was clearly Bulscu's representative -- his neonate childe Albrecht -- and the Gangrel noticed those attacking the wagon were Turks. The Gangrel decided to be the big hero, swooping in to rescue Albrecht from the Turkish ambush...or at least, he tried.

    While Albrecht got ganked and murdered by one Assamite warrior and a sorcerer, the Gangrel got absolutely nailed by a hail of arrows. This part was actually pretty funny, the Gangrel's player was freaking out about Quietus and blood poisons, and that the Assamite attacking him had at least Celerity 4 and good enough night-vision to spot a Gangrel in flight form swooping down. Queue the following brief conversation:


    Gangrel's player: "how the fuck did he even see me? I'm in flight form, it's night! How does he even know I'm a vampire? How much Auspex does this guy have? Jeez!"
    Tzimisce's player: "your flight form is a giant diurnal eagle that's not even native to Transylvania and animals tend to not go towards the fire."
    Gangrel's player: "...oh. Right. Never mind..."


    I mean, hilarity aside, the Gangrel's player isn't wrong; that Assamite in particular is no joke. But when all he has, is a dinky-ass shortbow, and what he's attacking is a fucking Gangrel...so despite the laughably low amount of damage being slung his way for the moment, the Gangrel decides discretion is the better part of valor. He promptly fucks off, leaving Albrecht to his fate, and returns to deliver what he learned back to Eyulf.

    And that's when things get weird. What happens next, is me getting some insanely good rolls on the part of an NPC and just rolling with it to see what happens.

    The Lasombra and Tzimisce go off, looking for "recruits". They find a couple, and one in particular happens to be a notably perceptive Szekler on the town watch. The Lasombra gets challenged, spars a bit verbally with the guard, and is duly impressed by the guard's wit. The Lasombra asks why this guy is on the town watch when it's clearly beneath his station.

    The watchman replies, it's punishment for things he did during the Transylvanian peasant revolt. Apparently, impaling peasants as a warning to not revolt is okay, but public flaying, disembowelment, and boiling in oil, is a bridge too far for weak-stomached nobility. Really for this watchman, the issue is the hypocrisy that one way to deal with stinking, revolting peasants as an example to the others is okay, while others that happen to be far more entertaining, are not.

    Mind, he's saying this shit to the Lasombra and the Tzimisce. The Lasombra, now interested in this guy's sadism, does his usual thing: lead the watchman off some place private, drop shroud on him, see if he survives. The watchman makes his Courage check, recognizes the shroud, and reaches for a torch which he obviously can't light. The watchman identifies the vampire as a "la-somme": some kind of strange demon that controls shadows. Now really interested in this guy...the Lasombra gets a shiny new ghoul to beat into submission and pit against his others Thunderdome style to see who comes out on top for his own amusement.

    The Lasombra just might have gotten more than he planned for. Something he'll learn shortly.

    Meanwhile, the Tzimisce has a look around some of the nearby storehouses and notices the powder is still there, while the other goods have been relocated to the keep in preparation for a siege. This stands out, and the Tzimisce asks the town watchman why the powder hadn't been moved. The watchman replies the order to move it had been countermanded the day prior, by one of his cousins; he offers to lead the two vampires to his cousin, to find out why the orders were countermanded and what exactly is going on.

    The Lasombra and the Tzimisce discuss briefly what the latter can do to extract information from the watchman's cousin, and of course the topic of Vicissitude comes up. Neither notice the watchman is eavesdropping, rather intently. When they get there, the watchman knocks on the door and when the cousin comes to it, the watchman immediately punches him in the gut, doubling him over vomiting. Before the two vampires can react, the watchman kicks the cousin in the jaw, breaking it; the watchman draws a flaying knife, rips his cousin's pants open, and castrates him.

    The Lasombra pulls the watchman away, intent on punishing the watchman for potentially murdering his cousin before he can be interrogated, while the Tzimisce steps in to fleshcraft the cousin's wounds. The watchman smirks at the Lasombra, side-eying the Tzimisce; then does the Lasombra realize the watchman overheard that conversation about Vicissitude, and was quite intently testing the Tzimisce to see of what she was capable.

    In the Lasombra player's words, "clever bastard". The Lasombra lets the new ghoul off with a warning; the Tzimisce interrogates the cousin, while the Lasombra murders his family and servants to leave no witnesses, getting a quick snack out of it in the meantime. They get the info they needed: the cousin was given a strange drink by a dark-skinned man, who he became obsessed with and who told him to countermand the order to move the powder into the keep. The implication is clear: the Assamites have already been in the city, sabotaged its defense, and gotten out with no one the wiser.

    Having gotten the info, they let the watchman finally flay his own cousin (his cousin was the one who ordered him to the town watch as punishment for flaying peasants). The Lasombra also notices the watchman has a fucking hard-on from flaying his cousin.

    In the meantime, the Setite goes with Yusuf, who the Setite knows is the alternate identity of his new boss, Iason. They go to Iason's haven, complete with blasphemy shrine and Book of Going Forth by Night. Iason quizzes the Setite on what he'd do in this situation, and of course his answers are all "wrong"; Iason's evil comic book villain plan is to sabotage his own city, let it fall to the Ashirra to get rid of Eyulf, while Iason lies low. The Ashirra likely won't hold the city for long, and when they're finally beaten back, whatever prince seizes Praxis in the end will likely be much more controllable and corruptible than Eyulf ever would be.

    So, Iason needs the Setite to tie up some loose ends: a ratter who released the rats he caught into the granary, a town watch captain who's been sneaking Typhon's Brew to the town watchmen without knowing what it is, and...a Szekler noble who ordered the powder be left in the stores outside the keep. The Setite warrior goes off to do what Setite warriors do best, whacks the first two, and when he gets to the third he sees...the Tzimisce PC standing guard outside the house.

    The Setite sends his ghoul to do the job by sneaking through a window. The Tzimisce catches the ghoul and intercepts him. The Setite realizes his duty may be interrupted, and he shifts into Typhonic avatar and prepares to attack. The Tzimisce's danger sense goes off, she shifts into Zulo form, and the two shapeshifted vampires face off. In the middle of the street, at night.

    The Lasombra overhears the ruckus, goes outside and sees the Silence-breaking stupidity, and just drops shroud on everyone. All three of them end up arguing for a bit, before the Setite goes in the house and sees some random dude with a full hog, going to town with a flaying knife on his target. Realizing they have the same goal, cooler heads prevail and they talk it out (for once).

    They eventually go back to court, the Lasombra's new ghoul burns his cousin's house down to destroy the evidence, and the coterie powwows with Eyulf and Yusuf. At this point they have a sabotaged city they have to hold -- somehow -- a dead Ventrue on their hands who happens to be Bulscu's childe, and a city that may or may not already be crawling with Assamites. Eyulf forbids them all from returning to their havens, and they all bunker down in the castle's underground tunnels and crypt for the day.

              Originally posted by Vendrin View Post
              Must have missed this, when did the Ashirra take Kronstadt?
              In the downtime before the 1413 story, so...three downtimes ago?

              It came as a shock to the PC's at the time, too. Brasov and the Burzenland at large had been completely no-go territory since the 1289 story: the Inquisition took it over, purged it completely of vampires, and patrolled/monitored it so heavily no vampire could really get any sort of foothold at all. All that work the coterie put in, turning it into a crusader stronghold paid off...for the worst possible party.

              In addition, when the coterie had to evacuate in such a rush, they left behind a bunch of vital intelligence about their herds, influences, and other key backgrounds, the Inquisition found and weaponized against them. Part of that was also intelligence on their enemies and rivals, not to mention full knowledge of Zelios' tunnel network below Brasov, crypts and potential havens, court, that shit about the Baali nests in the Burzenland, and the geomantic rune beneath the keep.

              Between that, Nova's shenanigans, individual blowback, and more than a century spent on the run with little time to regroup, some members of the coterie actually still have fewer background points than they started with as neonates; only the Setite has managed to preserve his background dots with any level of efficacy, and that's because he actively works to keep his cult (as the cornerstone of all his backgrounds) out of danger.

              One of the funnier moments in background fuckery was the surviving Gangrel PC. He'd worked for a century or two to build up this free company led by ghouled lieutenants that served him incredibly well...while he had them. When the Black Death hit, he contracted the plague but his childe at the time didn't; he stopped feeding his childe to keep him from becoming a plague-bearer, and had his childe feed the ghouls as he was unwilling to let his prized ghouls risk contracting plague.

              Of course, the Blood Oath wore off on his childe. His childe, who was territorial, "creatively interpreted" his orders as the ghouls and free company were his territory. Ergo, he stole the free company, went north, and made a shitload of money and prestige selling his services to the Ventrue and Tremere. The irony of a childe rebelling against an "Anarch" sire to join the "Camarilla" is just...delicious.

              The Gangrel's getting his chance for payback very soon if he plays his cards right. I'm having his rebellious childe join Matthias Corvinus' Black Army, meaning he'll be back in the area in a downtime or two.


              The Gangrel's other childe -- Tierney, that revenant Koldun from the 1289 story -- held out, because he took over Ilias' former domain. It developed into a nice, self-supporting, pagan enclave thanks to Ileana (the Ventrue PC) working with Ilias to save pagans being purged from the Burzenland, by Jurgen and the Black Cross. The mountain pass into it collapsed when the Inquisitor magus derped out and exploded his own army, triggering a huge landslide. After that, the only way in or out was through the caves previously inhabited by a literal Antediluvian.

              But, Tierney was the sole exception. Other than that, the Burzenland was a vampiric roach motel: vampires go in, they don't come out.

              In the next century, the Inquisition had become rather...lax...in its efforts to keep the Burzenland vampire-free. The coterie of the PC's sires and mentors managed to whack a bishop in 1312 who happened to be a key figure in the Society of Leopold, and they did it in Alba Iulia which happens to be the diocese of Transylvania. Between that good, hard, punch upside the noggin, and simple politics, the Inquisition changed tacts to centralize Alba Iulia and keep it secure. Which is why and how the Tremere moved in and kept the city for themselves, for the better part of a century -- they could outmaneuver the Inquisition, while the Inquisition shielded them from would-be attackers.

              Amid all that, Tremere and Ventrue managed to creep in and build up something of a court, which was wiped out by the Ashirra on the down low. So, here we are.
