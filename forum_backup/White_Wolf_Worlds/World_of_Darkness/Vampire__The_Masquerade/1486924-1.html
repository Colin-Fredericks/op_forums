                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                    I suppose the issue here is you're comparing it to Fornite, one of the most profitable games of all time, and I'm comparing it to the other WOD licensed games.
                    He's not comparing it to Fortnite, he's using Fortnite to exemplify the numbers of money pouring in. There are two distinct comparison going on here.

                    Bloodhunt is, as far as we can see, the greatest hit among WoD-based games. The best reception and the greatest gross sales to date. People plainly like it more than any of the others, give or take personal taste with game genre.

                    Yet the OP isn't just saying that, but also implying success, and that's a far trickier definition. What is success?

                    From the perspective of a player of course it is quality and a large fan base to talk with about the game. But from the perspective of the companies involved it is this and a lot of other things in a context whose end result is profit.

                    The problem is that profit doesn't depend on comparing with the rest of the IP, it depends on the size and cost of the project itself. As far as we can tell most other VtM games may have got enough net profit to at least make it even, but they're small projects whose costs are concentrated in development itself, then they just have to wait for the money to pour in without expending much more. Bloodhunt is a Battle Royal, it must be taken as a Battle Royal. It had a greater budget to pay off and has to keep servers and ongoing development. It isn't ever a finished product, it'll only stop costing good money when it stops making any money at all.

                    So its initial success may be a good sign, but it isn't nearly enough. The game didn't even paid itself as far as we can tell, and it needs to stay afloat on top of that. Every day keeps the cost going up for the profit to play catch up. It needs to gross over a year enough to pay its own basic costs at the very least, which includes at least a couple dedicated developers/staff to handle it.

                    Lets not forget: for all the (deserved) cult around it, the original Bloodlines was a blunder, not a hit. It didn't grossed enough to recoup costs before it spelled doom on the devs. And the problem was precisely that it was too ambitious a project for the time, team and budget they had. It cost a lot, took too long, ended up being released unfinished because of that and didn't made enough to secure new projects for Troika. And this is our gold standard. Bloodhunt is too big of a project to follow its footsteps, it has to surpass Bloodlines.
