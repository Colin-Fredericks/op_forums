    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    I remind you that canonically the Path of Harmony was exterminated because it wasn't evil enough to work in the Sabbat.
    But I need no reminder -- this is precisely why I wrote that the above line of thinking seems more like the way the Revised era writers wanted to represent the Paths of Enlightenment, rather than what was actually written in the original books. As with so many other Revised era "course corrections," I find the in-game "extermination" of a Path and its followers an incredibly clumsy and crude way of handling the matter.

    The idea of "following a Road" in the modern setting is essentially a non-starter for me, because I do not like mixing the Dark Medieval and Modern Nights settings with each other. The entire point of releasing Vampire: The Dark Ages was to provide a different setting for vampires to play in! I genuinely don't understand why people want to take two highly developed settings with strong and unique themes and... mash them together into one indistinguishable goop. Every time someone has the bright idea of bringing Dark Ages elements into the modern setting, I mentally sigh and roll my eyes. Cappadocians, Lamia, Salubri warriors, the Baali as a major force, Roads... All of these elements belong in the Dark Medieval, and the continual mixing only diminishes both Vampire: The Dark Ages and Vampire: The Masquerade. In my opinion, of course.

    Case in point: Retconning the Paths of Enlightenment into what essentially amounts to dumbass frat boy versions of Roads. The Paths were written first, and in the setting lore, they were supposed to have been a recent development, a product of the last 500 years as a result of the Sabbat's spiritual development. Demoting that achievement into the Sabbat having merely debased the work of their elder superiors is one of the many indignities heaped upon the Sabbat in Revised.

                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                            The Sabbat was created, essentially, to fill the vacancy of the vampires who are complete pieces of shit.
                            This was certainly their function in 1st Edition, when they essentially had no writeup of their own, only a couple of paragraphs that amounted to Here There Be Assholes. But even in the 1st Edition core, the text said that most Camarilla vampires simply don't know much about the Sabbat, and could just as well be wrong about everything. And they were wrong, to an extent, as The Players Guide to the Sabbat showed a year later.

                            Was that a retcon of the Sabbat's function? Or simply an expansion of the setting? Does the game absolutely need an entire sect of vampires who are complete pieces of shit? These are not facetious questions -- the answers are genuinely for each Storyteller to decide for themselves. I absolutely do not mind a nuanced, playable Sabbat or Technocracy, myself. The Sabbat presented in The Players Guide to the Sabbat can still have the exact same function in a Camarilla game they had from the start.

                            I would only challenge your suggestion that the Paths of Enlightenment were created solely as a hack for inflexible Humanity rules. The game originally had perfectly functional, suave, well-dressed zero Humanity vampires, as you well know if you're a fan of the original Chicago by Night. As a Storyteller, you can always just say that your NPC has Humanity 0 but isn't a ravening monster, because the needs of an NPC are completely different from those of a player character. A zero Humanity character was not originally unsuited for players because they had become a 28 Days Later zombie, but because they no longer had any inner conflict about who they were. The Paths of Enlightenment were a way to restore inner conflict to Sabbat characters, who were originally envisioned as all having zero Humanity. That's an elegant expansion of the game's concepts, not a crude hack for Humanity rules that should, in my opinion, only apply to PCs anyway.
