    Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
    Your latter point rather dismisses the possibility of old-fashioned indoctrination (You don’t need creepy supernatural powers to shape and maintain loyalty, just the right carrot and the stick - or more likely money, time and special attention) and the sort of worldly incentives available to a member of the Inner Council (Money again, power, access to the most interesting places and people etc).
    While the lack of distance might not dampen more mundane means of influence in other non-local industries, I think it might apply a lot less in Hollywood and the high end of the entertainment industry. People there are already fabulously rich, have high status. have access to sexual targets, hobnob with other rich and powerful people, have lots of esteem/validation, be in extremely glamorous surroundings, and so on. Almost anything someone outside of LA could bring them, they already have access to in LA. Most of the time such local things will probably be preferred over non-local. In that kind of industry, only parts of New York (particularly Broadway, but also some TV, fine arts, music, and other legacy media) have equal or more prestige.

    (It's interesting to think maybe Petrodon has a Toreador archon whose main purpose is to cultivate that NYC arts & entertainment influence for use against the LA anarchs. Just hope she isn't eaten by the Sabbat while doing it).

    It's not that such people still cannot be tempted, but more than nine times out of ten, if they have someone local offering it versus someone non-local, there just doing to go local. Relationships need to be maintained for that kind of thing, and proximity makes it easier to develop and maintain relationships.

    LABN makes it seem that all anarchs are simply brutish gangbangers, but that is also one of the flaws of that sourcebook. There are going to be a certain number of anarchs who are part of the Hollywood movie and TV industry, and they are just going to be better at developing most relationships with industry bigshots than someone not local.

    Not to mention that LA should have a significant number of vampires with the Fame attribute. Lots of concepts of vampires actresses, musicians, other artists, idols, and celebrities out there. Their personal connections (likely made when they were still mortal) are going to count for a lot.

    I'm not saying that means it is impossible for Petrodon and other Camarilla members to have influence there. They should to a degree, and I think such influence struggles will make for a more compelling game experience for the PCs. However, I do think Petrodon and company are under a handicap here. I think it's a smart idea to step back and consider how they can cultivate what resources they can and how to leverage it best. If they are too obvious or direct, they risk losing their agents of influence. I default to a position that Petrodon has a relative weak hand in this compared to the anarchs, but that he manages to play it well and wins more often than he should. The difficulty for me is working out how he does that.

        As a spin off to something discussed in a post on the Anarch Council thread, i want to consider the idea of a Warwick's Web type intelligence network of the Nosferatu on the Pacific Coast. Possibly based in Seattle because if the center of the web is in Seattle, it would explain why Petrodon decided to base his operations there.

        Obviously this is completely non-canon, but it's also the case that the Nosferatu methuselah Trajan must have other webs around the world. LABN does mention that the Triad fighting over New England have agents in the area. Dark Selina's is supposedly Christopher Houghton himself (somehow - how these methuselahs actually manipulate or control things is never defined), but the other two only have agents of much less power. In Trajan's case, it would make sense it would be someone working for the web.

        The Webs seem to have a simple structure. There is someone at the center, whose title/position is not given. I imagine it's something like webmaster or spider. Dark Colony just says that the headquarters of the Web is the "Lair". Maybe I'll just use that. If someone has an idea for a cooler name for this position, I'm interested in hearing it. It's not clear if these webmasters report directly to Trajan (or when Trajan was in torpor to his second in command), but I would guess so. Then we have "strand masters" who are in charge of a region. In New England, these were Home Strand, Vermont Strand, Connecticut Strand, and Maine Strand. Nosferatu responsible for an individual city or county were "district overseers". Then there were deep infiltration agents called "veins". Any other Nosferatu just collecting information are simply termed "operatives". Warwick's web comprised around a dozen Nosferatu so each Strand on the average only had three. So once we assume one Strand Master and an additional District Overseer for each Strand, there's not much room left over for veins and operatives. I assume some Strands have more and others less. It is possible that Veins are not based on individual Strands and move around at the behest of their Strandmaster or Lair.

        If we use this same structure for the Pacific Coast, we have a Lair in Seattle and Strand Masters that probably cover Washington, Oregon, North/Central California, and possibly elsewhere.

        I won't have an LA Strandmaster because I do want the Los Angeles Nosferatu to mostly be loyal to the anarchs, but I will make one be a District Overseer who reports to the Strand Master in San Francisco. There might also be one Vein who is under deep cover in Los Angeles with an alternate identity and high enough Obufscate not to be identified as a Nosferatu spy.

        Not all Nosferatu are part of the Web. Members have a special commitment to Trajan's Web. Most local Nosferatu just do their own thing. However, members of Trajan's Web do have access to a lot of juicy gossip from outside the local warren. Just as Petrodon uses it to get some insight into what is going on in Los Angeles, Nosferatu anarchs occasionally benefit from this too. Trajan himself doesn't really care about sect disputes and local politics. He has the typical elder Nosferatu concerns about Nictuku and such, and his own feuds with other methuselahs operating at his level. Petrodon is just able to utilize this Web already in place because he is a powerful and influential Nosferatu now local to Seattle. However, he does not control it, and whatever information he "takes" from it, he also "gives" an equivalent back because that is simply clan culture. Still this is an important advantage he has.

        It's likely one of the PCs will be a Nosferatu so this is something that should be discovered early on, and it'll be interesting to see how the PC reacts. Of course, the Nosferatu member on the Anarch Council also knows this exists and has figured out who the LA District Overseer is. But I intend for the identity of this Nosferatu is unknown (outside to the members of the Anarch Council) and is one of the mysteries that PCs may or may not figure out on their own.

        However, the main reason I'm including this is that I have always loved Warwick's Web in Dark Colony. The evocative titles for its agents are great. Dark Colony is an extremely flawed sourcebook, but it has a few great things, and this is one. I was disappointed when I did not see this concept utilized again in future sourcebooks and the revised Nosferatu clanbook. Trajan's Web should be a huge setting element IMO.

                Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                Also, regarding LA Nosferatu being relatively loyal to the Anarch Free State, I wonder if this could be grounded in historic persecution by the local Toreador under Don Sebastian as steered by Christopher?
                Obviously there's no canonical statement about that as the LABN sourcebook only has one Nosferatu character in it who dwells by himself in the San Gabriel hills. The entire clan is pretty much ignored just like the Gangrel (who don't even get one NPC description!).

                I made that decision for several reasons. 1) Since one of the PCs will be Nosferatu, I don't want a situation where the coterie is confronted with an entire clan being disloyal to the Free States. It would create a bad dynamic and put that PC into a real bind. 2) I assume just like any clan, Nosferatu anarchs in other cities will travel to the Free States and thus be prominent members of their clan. In turn these Nosferatu anarchs will embrace new fledglings and be raised to be anarchs. So there's obviously going to be anarch Nosferatu.

                Does that mean all Nosferatu are strictly loyal to the Free States? Of course not. You will have several groups. One are loyal anarchs. Another is a group that just accepts the domain is anarch and adjust. What does it matter to them who is in charge? It won't change how Nosferatu treat each other. A third group are Nosferatu who are not happy about the anarchs being in control, but aren't going to do anything about it. Regardless of their preference, it's the way things are. Best for all Nosferatu to get along. Probably only a small group are both upset that the anarchs are in control and willing to do something about it. In this case, help the Camarilla secretly and don't mention it to the others. So regardless of the actual demographic breakdown of the Nosferatu clan, it'll seem like they're loyal overall.

                If the PCs want to dig deeper and uncover the actual motivations and loyalties of individual Nosferatu, they need to figure that out on her own.

                Now, it's not a bad idea to say that Don Sebastian and the LA Toreador were real asses to the Nosferatu when they were in power, and as a result the clan didn't mind the anarchs wiping them out. That's a good angle to use. It's not unusual for those clans to not get along. It doesn't even need to be objectively true. Just what's told to new arrivals or embraces to explain why there may be some pre-1944 Nosferatu around. Or what attracted some Nosferatu to support the anarchs afterwards or attract more Nosferatu who harbor their own anti-Toreador prejudices.

                                    There's one obvious group of Camarilla in Los Angeles I haven't mentioned yet; the Brujah Idealists. These are the four Brujah who arrived in the Free States to study it. Some are sympathetic to the anarchs and others hostile, but all are ostensibly loyal to the Camarilla. The coterie is an interesting concept that I haven't quite figure out how to use in a chronicle. As fairly passive observers, they actually lack obvious hooks. Yet they seem like they should be something that is interesting for PCs to encounter and interact with.

                                    I've long felt that the original Brujah Clanbook's division of "iconoclasts", "individualists", and "idealists" is something more like how an anthropologist would categorize things than categories a group would divide themselves in. So I'll use this group as a way to "introduce" these concepts, since they are essentially anthropologists and political scientists. It's one thing I can do with them at least.

                                    Another thing is to make sure they are not passive scholars. Most Kindred in the Free States know about them, and treat them in the same way many countries treat Western media - a way to get out their message and propagandize. Eventually what the "Brujah Idealists" (I really need a better name for them) study is going to be reported back to many of the salons of the Camarilla. I need to have them attend Rants, interview the various barons, ask regular anarchs to complete questionnaires and such like.

                                    Everyone knows they are essentially Camarilla loyalists, but no one does anything against them because it'd be counter productive. If you are a Third World terrorist, you don't kidnap or kill Western journalists. You try to use them to your advantage to get out the story you want to tell. For many Brujah anarchs, they seem them as loyal clanmates. They might be wrong, but they are still trustworthy and worthy of respect. The non-Brujah anarchs might resent that, but there are just too many Brujah anarchs that you can't ignore their feelings on this matter.

                                    Everyone knows some (maybe everything) of what they learn is going to go to Petrodon, but so what? They know Petrodon already has agents and spies in the Free States. He's going to learn what the Brujah Idealists learn anyway. They aren't going to uncover any secrets that Petrodon won't learn on his own. So treat them with kindness and earn goodwill. Just don't tell them any actual important secrets.

                                    I wonder if it might be a good idea to split up the four different Idealists into different anarch groups/gangs/baronies. Each one studies a different group and then shares his learnings with the others and then discuss what he's learned with the group.
                                    • Sir Ralph Hamilton - as the conservative most pro-Camarilla Brujah, he'd be good to approach the least radical anarchs (which in my chronicle would be Kindred like Fortier and Geduld)
                                    • Professor Gregory Habersohn - as a non-judgmental academic, he'd be good to approach those anarchs least willing to trust the group as he could best win them over eventually
                                    • Dr. Shiraiwa Takuya - as a Stalinist Communist, he'd be a good choice for the most radical, revolutionary anarchs.
                                    • Countess Andrea Visconti - as a flamboyant gallant, she'd be good at charming many of the youngest anarchs without clear politics
                                    One or more members will almost always be at every Rant. And instead of them being together, each will be split up, hanging out with one of "their" anarch groups. Whenever the PCs visit one of the barons or other important factions, they might encounter one of the Idealists already there. They may or may not be witnesses to a conversation with the PCs, but they will be ubiquitous and often seen.

                                    Finally, since they all dwell together in the "Laurel Canyon House", it'd be good to make that an actual landmark in the Free States. Have "open nights" when any Kindred is welcome to arrive and share their story. Many anarchs might very well go there simply to vent and complain, happy that someone - anyone - is willing to hear their side of the story. Or even important individuals go to relate the propaganda they want to get out there, just like how many dictators will use the foreign press. That way it might be something one or more of the PCs decide to do themselves, or perhaps try to exploit that. They may even host certain events or salons or parties there because informal gatherings is a great way to cultivate contacts and informants.
