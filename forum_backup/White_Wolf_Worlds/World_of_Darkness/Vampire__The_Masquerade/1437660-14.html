                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                    How do you figure?

                    or it just the Humanity thing and not the Tzimisce write-up.

                    The Tzimisce write up and new discipline spread (Paths in some form being either a given, or just ignoring the morality system). Disciplines and weaknesses explain vampire stereotypes: Why do Ventrue create herds? Because prey limitations, and easy mind control make that the best option for them. Why do Toreador love art? Because Auspex and Fascination make it their crack, Why do Nosferatu spy? Because they cannot blend normally due to the curse, but Animalism and Obfuscate make finding information easy, and that role is always needed etc etc.

                    Sure you can play against those stereotypes, but the Weakness and Disciplines act to 'force' you back onto type.

                    That brings us to the Tzimisce: Why do Tzimisce make war ghouls and revnant families? Because they don't have mind control any other way so use this inefficient and weak method. Why are Tzimisce so bonded to a haven? Weakness, Animalism and Auspex synergise into a mystic link with home, by object read, by spirit sight, by the very soil and bricks talking to them, and the beasts paying them homage. Now that is gone. Replaced by the same old same old High Clan mind control. Only a complete idiot would make war ghouls now, or waste the time on revenant families, only the deranged talk to the spirits now. All that is gone.

                    All that is left is the wannabe ventrue Dracula imitator. The weaknesses and write up are laser focused on that, any other idea is badwrongfun. (Seriously read the related twitter thread, they practically issue bad fake accents)

                        I'm sympathetic to the overall point that Taggie is making, and I agree that the original Tzimisce clan disciplines better represented what the Fiends as written were (the Wamphyri clan) rather than what a subset of players seems to have wanted them to be (the Dracula clan).

                        But at the same time, I cannot help but feel like maybe the decision to bump up the costs of purchasing and raising disciplines across the board made out-of-clan disciplines too onerous for most players to consider. In 1st Edition, discipline costs used to be current level ×3 for clan disciplines and ×5 for other disciplines, rather than the ×5 and ×7 that most players are used to.

                        I feel like even with the original clan discipline spread, it should be relatively easy for Tzimisce characters to pick up a few levels of Dominate if they so choose. Certainly the Tzimisce who are powerful enough to make war ghouls and cultivate revenant families would have enough experience to buy basic Dominate dots many times over? The reasoning that the clan must make do with "inefficient" methods of control because they don't "have" Dominate doesn't quite ring true to me.

                                                        I agree. In my first tabletop games, the standard Disciplines were assumed to be learned by anyone except special things like Thaumaturgy. We even treated Protean the same way.

                                                        However, once the non-Camarilla clans and bloodlines were introduced with their own specialty Discipline, it suddenly became an issue of who could learn them. Then people started saying Disciplines could not be learn without a teacher except the in-clans that were "inherent." That was doubled downed in LARP games where certain Disciplines were much more valuable in that context than other Disciplines. Soon everyone had those Disciplines which really negated the feel of each clan being different. So barriers were put in place (which were quickly overcome because LARP involves putting 12-30 people into a confined space together, so you quickly find people who swap Disciplines.)

                                                        Unique Disciplines created an issue that didn't exist before because those Disciplines were presented as unique to those clans and unknown outside them, or at least outside the sect.

                                                            Originally posted by Elphilm View Post
                                                            I'm sympathetic to the overall point that Taggie is making, and I agree that the original Tzimisce clan disciplines better represented what the Fiends as written were (the Wamphyri clan) rather than what a subset of players seems to have wanted them to be (the Dracula clan).
                                                            I think this encapsulates the entire issue. You either want Wamphyri style vampires in your game, or you don't. Unfortunately, the depiction of Wamphyri in Necroscope is really alien to the legendry of Vampire as originally written. I am all about toolbox approaches, and the original Discipline and Umbral Disease angle is a good way to shoehorn that into the game, but it creates problems if you just don't want to have Wamphyri in your game either because you don't like them or think this shoehorning creates its own problems. (And I think many people don't as we've seen various attempts by the game to slowly remove those influences from the game, beginning with Revised eliminating the umbral disease angle.)

                                                            If you want Wamphyri, then the original Tzimisce are great. I totally understand that.

                                                            I don't so I eliminated all the Necroscope style stuff from my chronicles a LONG time ago. No Vicissitude. I gave the Tzimisce Protean and moved on. All my players liked that because none of us liked Wamphyri style vampires. As ST, I can make any adjustments to history or characters or with Merits/Flaws as needed to accommodate that.

                                                            But that is harder in the published setting because canon is out there, and the Tzimisce and Vicissitude are an old part of the game and a very recognizable part of it. You have to deal with continuity in that case. You just can't say that is Earth-1 where Vicissitude exists, but our game now takes place on Earth-2 where it doesn't. Fans get upset as we clearly see.

                                                            Instead what we have is a strange hybrid, where Vicissitude has to exist because its canon, but is treated like a red headed step child that the setting wants to ignore.

                                                            At least, that is my impression because I only have these forums to go by - I don't buy V5 products.

                                                            I think what would be best is to keep the lore as is, but in some sourcebook as an optional rule/variant setting, give rules for non-Vicissitude Tzimisce more like Dracula and provide a revised history that takes it into account. That way both groups are satisfied, but you acknowledge it's a different setting if you get rid of Vicissitude.
