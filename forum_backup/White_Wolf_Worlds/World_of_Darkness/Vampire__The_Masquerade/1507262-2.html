                                                            Originally posted by Herr Meister View Post
                                                            That's my thought as well. Unfortunately we see many instances in books where the coterie is almost predetermined to "win", Darkening Sky is a good example by the way.

                                                            Would you care to share any of your experiences with this kind of scenario?
                                                            Generally speaking, one good thing to do in these sorts of scenarios is the following five tips.

                                                            Make it clear the player characters aren't necessarily in a life or death struggle: Vampires should not necessarily be trying to kill the PCs. It's good to have player characters possibly in danger but the situation is often more likely games of cat and mouse for money, power, prestige, or position.

                                                            There's no good side: Freddy vs. Jason so to speak. If Horatio Ballard is up against Al Capone with the PCs caught in the middle, then whoever wins is going to still be an asshole. So *A* villain is
                                                            likely to be an ally.

                                                            Make the villain's victory good storytelling: Players, not necessarily their characters, don't mind their characters suffering or failing as long as it's interesting. As long as it doesn't seem UNFAIR, tragic endings are appreciated rather than derided. This is a matter of opinion but if you avoid a "cheap" ending or diablous ex machina, most players will roll with it.

                                                            Here's a short list of some things from various campaigns:

                                                            1. The System can't be Beaten: Some humans find out about the existence of vampires and the player characters try to basically tell them how much their lives are in danger or to protect them. Some of them listen, others get their mind washed. One of the more likable ones ends up trying to go to the authorities and the PCs hear later that they were killed in a hit and run.

                                                            2. Stupid idealists get massacred: Some Anarchs are determiend to declare a block of the city into a Free State and haven for Thin Bloods and Caitiff in their area. The player characters gave their protection when they were effectively running the city (Tampa Florida). When the PCs got overthrown for a time, they retuirn to find the area had been completely massacred.

                                                            3. You got Palpatined: The player characters got into a fight with another Anarch gang and the PCs never understood why he was so hateful to them. In the end, the two of them had a thrown down that resulted in the PCs triumphant, the Anarch leader dead, and a bloody triumph. The Prince then invites them to Elsyium and reveals that they would now have his territory. Because the PCs are told that the dead Anarch leader's sister had been murdered and somehow he thought the PCs were responsible (because the Prince framed them).

                                                            4. Always a bigger fish: The PCs get to witness a powerful godlike Methusaleh or Elder waltz into an area, slaughter everyone, and then walk out. The PCs are then offered positions by the figure and rewards but made clear that they are facing something simply beyond their kin.​ Basically, the PCs get offered a job by evil and thus become part of the villain's schemes.
