        For what it is worth another aspect that Necromancy tends to bring up is the play style Players and STs want to use for in game conflicts.

        Some people want the power fantasy of being the combat machine, thus their build starts and ends with Fortitude, Celerity, Potence, and maybe Protean for agg damage claws. However other people prefer the power fantasy of never having to get their hands dirty because they win before combat ever happens. This can be achieved by several different methods, social builds who pull in allies, background builds who manipulate the setting rather than the characters, or by being a summoner who uses minions. The problem is that when a player or ST wants to go one way with the game and it negates some one else's preference, the salt will overflow very quickly.

        Necromancy brings this to the forefront very quickly. Sure your brawler might be a badass, but when a ghost can set your haven on fire in the middle of the day you have no idea what happened, that an attack was coming, who is responsible, or what to do about it. This can be seen as boring to some or game breaking to others.

        But in truth, you could just as easily use Path of Conjuring to create a drone laden with explosives/incendiaries to do the same thing with a delay timer. Like wise you could use animalism to have various vermin literally undermine or destroy a building over time. Conversely a character with Influence, Contacts, and Resources could just foreclose on the haven and have it demolished. What is the vampire going to do, call the cops or try to deal with it in day time court?

        There are plenty of builds and playstyles that "win" without having to actively fight, but often people tend to forget about that style of play for players, and only use it for NPCs to punish or grief the players. From a historical perspective these win without fighting styles is a lot like bringing a sniper rifle to a knife fight, sure a knife never runs out of bullets, but it can't do much when you can snipe a target up to two miles away. But if a sniper is every finds themselves within knife fighting range, then they have made a probably lethal mistake.

                    Shakanaka and I have different interpretations of how things go in the Wraith setting. He ignores or minimizes all the things in the setting that would make it hard for his points. I take them seriously or even emphasize them. All my points stand. It depends on the ST and what the ST allows. Disciplines should be useful and fun for PCs. But if you are at the point where you think you have something that can't countered or is unstoppable, it's a good sign you are probably ignoring a lot.

                    It is very easy for an ordinary person to get in contact with someone who has Occult 3 and can perform a Warding or Forbiddance. What would be one of the first things someone does in real life if they think their house is being haunted and are at the point that they're fearful? Most of the time they are going to contact their local religious leader and ask for help. Even if the leader is unqualified, he'll have enough contacts in his religious grouping to easily find someone who can perform an exorcism or similar ritual. Even if all it is holding a crucifix and saying the prayer of St. Michael (real Vatican trained exorcists likely have Occult 4 or 5 with a specialty and likely have access to Inquisition paths). This is what happens in practically every real world case of a bad haunting.

                    There are lots of people, especially in the WoD, who will have Occult 3 - religious figures, paranormal enthusiasts, lay magicians, fortune tellers, academics, ghost hunters, wizened old men and women who remember folklore from the old country, etc. Not to mention character concepts that don't have supernatural powers in the real world, but would have real powers in the WoD - priests and ministers with True Faith, mediums who can contact the dead, minor hedge wizards who actually have Paths of sorcery. Not to mention really heavy hunters around in the Inquisition and other groups who could loan out protective amulets, good luck charms, blessed rosaries, etc.. All of that is easily justifiable and consistent with the setting.

                    I have no problem with their being bad hauntings or even the occasional person being killed in one. They make good stories (even if every example of a "real life" bad haunting like Amityville is probably a hoax, I have no problem with them happening on occasion in the WoD setting). But the idea that wraiths are simply able to kill whoever they want in the Skinlands is fraught with problems. When it happens, it's not in the spectacular manner Shakanaka seems to imply. And it's probably less efficient than what a vampire could arrange on his own with Necromancy. Wraiths can be useful to a vampire, but the more he uses them or the more obvious use of them are done, the more problems the Necromancer will create for himself because other aspects of the setting will begin to get involved.

                    In so far they may be some incredibly well trained Shroudbreaker haunter assassin somewhere in the Underworld, it'll take a lot of work for a Necromancer to learn who they are and successfully (and secretly) contact them, he won't be a "servant" but a professional selling his services, the price for those services will be extremely high, and the Necromancy will not be able to afford such services very often. The chances for failure and blowback at any point of the process is fairly high. Would it be harder or easier than finding another kind of supernatural assassin among the Assamites, Nephandi, lupines, PENTEX or someone else?

                    Would I allow a PC to attempt to do this in one of my chronicles? Of course. Would I allow him to succeed? He probably wouldn't. I'd be fair though and allow him to explore the setting, working with local wraiths to find out about secrets like the Haunters guild, avoid the secret police of the Hierarchy, navigate potential treachery and informers, make contact, negotiate price, etc. all with the possibility that something can go wrong (and likely would but depends on how smart he plays and how lucky the dice rolls are). I don't think many vampire players would be interested in that approach as it is taking them away from the Vampire setting. But if a PC is interested in that and has the patience for it, I'd be fine running it. But the end result will not be an unstoppable assassin who cannot be countered whenever he needs one.

I'd need to check the 20th ed for Wraith later) so there would be some baselines for scenarios with religious figures, like mentioned in the previous post, to really take hold of and do some critical stuff to a troublesome Wraith.
