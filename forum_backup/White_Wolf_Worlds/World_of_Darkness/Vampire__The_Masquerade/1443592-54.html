                                    No, it really wasn't.

                                    But anyway, to try to get back to VtM vs. VtM stuff:

                                    When it comes to how the different editions stress how vampires can change over time, I'm still feeling V20 offers more compelling game experiences. However much the Embrace might change you (and that's potentially quiet drastic in any of the editions), after that?

                                    The Nature/Demeanor setup offers some potential (if generally squandered as talking about changing those is a side-note in V20, and stuffed way back in ST oriented supplements previously). But the Paths of Enlightenment offer up a real place for characters to meaningfully change over time. Including Humanity itself, the Bearing rules help show a character changing as they pursue (or fail) an outlook on unlife. It's difficult and hard, but V20 right in the core book shows that vampires aren't stuck in one mode of thinking and reacting to their situation. The robust and/or overwrote Merit and Flaws also offer ways to represent changes in your character (assuming the optional rules are in play, and the optional rules about gaining/losing them are also in play).

                                    V5, even with all its optional rules as presented, still suffers a lot from no real way for vampires to change beyond the Beast vs. Man axis. The Beast can get strong or weaker. You can lose Convictions (or with house rules to back up the one unclear passage about possibly regaining them, regain them), but that's just losing a defense against the Beast. Even if V5 vampires aren't static, they're still on a single rail. If you look at a V5 character sheet at the start of play, and after a year of play, is there anything that would really show "this character has had some major emotional developments?" They lost a Conviction... but has the character really changed or just lost enough belief in an idea to use it as a bulwark against the Beast even if they're still struggling to hold on to that belief? They lost some Humanity, but since that can come from external sources and bad dice, that doesn't communicate nearly as much emotional change for the character.

                                    While a V20 character sheet isn't always going to show a story when you compare it over time, there's a lot of places where you can tell the character went through some major emotional changes just by seeing that those notations are different. V5 lacks that.

                                                    Originally posted by James_Willoughby View Post
                                                    Nature and Demeanour were the two most important traits within Vampire: the Masquerade. They should probably be the last traits to be dumped when revising the system.
                                                    Eh....

                                                    I actually think they were a vital part that needed reevaluation, and experimentation in other WW games even in the late 90s were showing there were other options that might be better.

                                                    Or, more specifically, Nature is pretty important, but Demeanor was largely vestigial.

                                                    Dumping them can be handled well, but you need something to take up the slack of helping to codify your character's emotional core. You can dump Demeanor and not even notice it because it has no actual game function. If anything dropping Demeanor helps because it allows characters to behave more naturally where they have more than two modes to pick from.

                                                    My issue with V5 here is that they were just dumped without any evidence of thought towards what fills the gap left behind.

                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                    Nature and Demeanor are like Alignment. They're not actually a thing that should exist save as a gaming shorthand, IMHO for beginning players.

                                                    If you have a three-dimensional personality, you don't need any of them.
                                                    Yeesh there's a lot of problems with this take.

                                                    First, if stuff is good for new players it absolutely needs to be in the game. People that know the games can take whatever shortcuts work for them and their group. New players need tools to understand the game the most and suffer the most when people get dismissive of rules like you've done here. If you want people to like a game you like, you want to make as new player accessible as possible, and you want to be happy about the existence of stuff that's new player friendly even if you skip it now.

                                                    Second, it is very difficult to find a sufficient argument against the "Systems Matter" concept. While lots of people have tried, it more comes down to a critique of presentation of the concept, or ends up affirming the premise in objection. By which I mean, even for people that feel that being free to ignore the rules is a good thing, you can't feel good about ignore rules that aren't there. That whole smug expression of "if you have a 'real' character you don't need these things," only exists because of an emotional validation in breaking the rules being fun (which is true for most people on some level). Disregarding any issues with the implementations of a given mechanic, concepts like Alignment, or Nature/Demeanor, have obvious value in shaping how people approach the game... even in how they house rule out those elements should they desire to.

                                                    Finally, these are aspects of the system with actual mechanics to resolve. My D&D character might have a spell that keys off Alignment, or even has differential effects based on Alignment; thus even with a "three-dimensional personality" I need Alignment or something to replace it if I don't want to completely rewrite the spell list. As noted, Nature is linked to the WP economy of the pre-V5 editions, as well as being meant to help guide how to interpret certain systems (including Dominate).

                                                    You can't value emotional change in a game, and not have something in the system to represent major emotion based changes in the character. Alignment and Nature/Demeanor might not be great systems, but they allow a representation of change that V5 does not offer. If my D&D PC goes from Lawful Good to Neutral Good, that is a major statement about my character's emotional journey. If my VtM vampire changes Nature from Cynic to Idealist, that's a huge sign of emotional change. Deriding those mechanics' flaws doesn't support any claim that V5 is better with nothing in this space.

                                                            I like the Bestial Failure/Messy Crit mechanics, but I'll admit it does seem intended for games that are more survival-horror than political-chess.
