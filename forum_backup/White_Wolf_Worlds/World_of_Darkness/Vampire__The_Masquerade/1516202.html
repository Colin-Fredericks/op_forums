    What do vampires know of other clans, in your games?

    06-26-2023, 08:41 AM
    Sometimes it feels like characters have access to the clanbooks, especially when you read the clan stereotypes sidebar, although I feel like they probably shouldn't. How about you guys?

    I think I might borrow MtA's Lore ability to represent a pc's knowledge of other clans*, honestly. You'll like learn over time the basic structure and goals of a clan in order to deal with them, and some vampires make a point to be scholars of their kind, or are ex-converts, and Elders are more worldly, but intimate knowledge of other clan cultures is not that widespread; you generally know what that particular clan wants the world to know about them, no more.

    *
    I would make the first dot free, honestly.
    So, WIP but with every clan being measured on their own, maybe like:

    •You know they exist, what they do, and some other basic knowledge and rumours
    [The Tremere are disliked, have a strict hierarchal structure called the Pyramid in groups called Chantries run by a guy called the Regent; Lasombra are social darwinists who manipulate shadows and have a connection to the Church, there seems to be bad blood with the Ventrue]

    •• You know the basics of their history and some legends, some basic grasp of their structure.
    [You can name their clan founder, their important players, their historical activities (exactly why the Tremere are hated, and by who, and stuff like the usurping of Saulot and Cappadocius, for example). You know that the warrior caste is only one branch of the Assamites. You know what the Gerousia is, and the difference between a Tremere Lord and a Regent.]

    ••• Structures fully understood
    [The Assamites have three castes and a split between the Web of Knives and everyone else; you know the role of the Ephorate and other structures in Clan Ventrue]

    •••• Finer distinctions understood
    [The Setites are a mytery cult and all their faces are just avenues deeper in; Clan Giovanni actually consists of something like 12 families, of various importance; Each rank in the Pyramid is divided into seven "circles of mystery", and the clan has secret societies]

    ••••• You know so much about this clan you may as well be a member, and probably not the stuff they like you knowing. Or maybe you're just a very dedicated convert to the service of Sutekh or Haqim, or something.
    [You know about Vienna's worm problem; The Giovanni pursue world domination via the Endless Night; the Ventrue are worried about the Daeva; the Nosferatu are being hunted by a sibling bloodline called the Nictuku]

    ​​​​​
    If you're running on basic knowledge in the Cam, six of the core seven are familiar enough*, with the Tremere being a little more secretive but not a total unknown. They have a "pyramid", they're wizards, they're useful but don't trust 'em. The two Sabbat clans are alien monsters leading some kind of apocalypse cult; Lasombra are goths with a fetish for catholic iconography who can control shadows; this doesn't sound scary, but everyone who's ever seen obtenebration in action gets this far away look in their eyes that suggests you should probably avoid them. Tzimisce are... face stealing shape-changers or demons or something with a hobby for making human furniture? (Which might be because of an alien disease?)
    * Though some deeper lore, like the Ventrue's paranoia about the "secret masters" and the Nosferatu vs Nictuku, are much better guarded secrets.​

    In the Sabbat core six are still familiar**, as are the Lasombra and Tzimisce, but the Tremere are the Usurpers; mysterious, occluded boogeymen who will kidnap you and do terrible things (which are terrible things that aren't ok, unlike the stuff everybody else does, because they're not Cainites; they're creepy wizards who basically stole vampires' faces to wear as a Halloween mask, or something).
    **Although if you got a neonate from the Sabbat and the Cam to compare notes on the Ventrue as they know them, they'd probably get a bit confused.

    The Independents are mysteries that you may not hear about until you're decades into your unlife and won't truly understand unless you join them.
    The Assamites are a group of hitmen, rumour suggests they're cannibals and religious fanatics (whether this religion is a weird vampire blood cult or a shallow stereotype of Islam depends on who you ask).
    The Setites are... New Age cultists with a thing for snakes? Fixers? Pimps? Drug dealers? Self-help gurus? Antique dealers? MLMs? Who knows! They're very friendly though...
    The Giovanni are as that old Toreador stereotype says of them; they're charming, and pleasant to do business with, but unsettling for reasons you can't put your finger on. You know they're "necromancers" (ghosts are real?) and there's unsubstantiated rumours about mafia and... weird fetishes.
    The Ravnos are compulsive liars are inherently prone to criminality, what more could you possibly need to know? (...what the hell are you talking about? "They have a clan culture"? What are you going on about demons for? Fairies!? Fairies don't exist, what are you five? Grow up!)

    And for anything else; "what's a bloodline?"​

                          Camarilla Kindred are more likely to know about how Sabbat Clans operate, if there's been a crusade on their city within their unlifetime, or not too long before. (Which could mean anything from a few years to a few centuries). An Elder or Ancillae, if they're in a chatty mood, might have shared some "war stories" about Tzimisce flesh abominations during a siege, or the shadows coiling out from a manhole cover and dragging a ghoul into the sewer.

                          Of course, even in a Sabbat crusade, the "rank and file" Sabbat fighters are likely to be shovelheads and young packs. Often from the Antitribu, as many Lasombra and Tzimisce consider it beneath them to stand at the front lines. Not unless there's a great deal of glory to be won, anyway. If Camarilla vampires encounter any Magistrates or Fiends, it'll be young licks just coming into their powers.

                          Antitribu can be illustrative, though, in the more martial applications of even "familiar" Disciplines. Camarilla society tends to disincentivize starting fights at the best of times. So Neonates may be ignorant of what Discipline powers can do at higher levels, or how they can be applied to violence or as asymmetrical war tactics. (A random mortal doesn't need to know the briefcase they've been Dominated to deliver to Elysium is actually full of plastic explosives. So their impromptu s*icide bombing technically doesn't violate the rules of the Discipline.)

                          This can apply to both Disciplines outside a PC's Clan, and even powers within it. The Gangrel PC may not be aware they can eventually possess the body of an animal. Especially if their sire wasn't around to instruct them. If their formative nights involved tangling with Sabbat Gangrel or Nosferatu, though, they might have a better idea about it. Let alone what Elders could do.

                                  I don't have much to contribute but I do have a couple of thoughts from a meta perspective.

                                  Firstly a lot of it is down to your players, if you have players who haven't read all the books and don't know all the lore then it's easier to surprise them with something. Whereas with experienced players it's down to wether or not they're capable of playing 'ignorant' characters. Good players with lots of canon knowledge can be easy to work with but players who utilise their player knowledge for an in-game edge for their character are a pain to deal with.

                                  Secondly, when adding Lore skills, remember that forcing players to put points into them is restricting their builds, but giving them free Lore ratings based on their character's knowledge/background/etc enables them to RP with that Lore.

                                      Vampires are, in fact, incredibly ignorant in my games.

                                      Most Neonates over a year old know the following:

                                      * You can probably deduce how vampires are made from context.
                                      * Sunlight and fire are instinctual. No one needs to be taught this anymore than blood is yummy.
                                      * There's the Clans of the Camarilla (Brujah, Malk, Nosferatu, Ventrue, Toreador, Gangrel, and Tremere--maybe Assamite and Lasombra depending on the year)
                                      * They're generally aware of Caitiff and Thin Bloods these days. Neonates generally get taught to treat them like trash if they're not either themselves.
                                      * They're aware Lupines exist, some are skeptical. It's a "friend of a friend" sort of thing unless you live in Chicago or Milwaukee or Vancouver.
                                      * They're aware Blood Magic exists but not magic in general (the only wizards you know exist are Tremere)
                                      * There's Princes, Primogen, and Sheriff
                                      * They all know the Masquerade
                                      * There are rules to vampire existence. You probably have to ask about them other than the Masquerade. Less the "Laws of the Camarilla" and more, "Don't make any more vampires without permission.", "Elders get pissy if you feed in their turf.", and "Introduce yourself to the boss vampire in an area if you enter a new area. Don't worry, they'll find you."
                                      * There's a big vampire Illuminati called the Camarilla but no one deals with it beyond the Prince and Sheriff level generally
                                      * There's a vampire resistance that amounts more to street gangs and constant complaining called the Anarchs.
                                      * Older People (like, over 10 years) sometimes mention the Sabbat and clam up
                                      * There's other vampire Clans out there but you'll almost never meet them
                                      * Everyone will have heard the "Caine origin" but almost no one takes it seriously in the Modern Nights and it's more like a one sentence, "Yeah, a lot of Older Vampires say Caine from the Bible was the first vampire. Sounds like a joke but there it is."
                                      * No one will have heard of the Antediluvians unless they're Tremere, Assamite, or Lasombra. Maybe Ventrue just to explain what "Generation" is.
                                      * Gehenna will NOT be spoken of
                                      * Ghouls are around constantly but unless you ask, no one will explain the Masquerade exception and generally it's like, "Yeah, they're slaves. They drink blood and are addicts. Its fucked up."

                                                      Even vampires who love to hear themselves talk are more likely to talk about things unrelated to the state of the Jyhad, like their exploits in the Napoleonic wars or something.

                                                      The World of Darkness is a setting of copious competing and concentric conspiracies. Where your baseline survival, both individually and as a faction, depend on keeping everything about you and how your kind operate as secret as possible. (Doubly so for Vampires, for whom being exposed means likely extinction). It behooves everyone within one or more of these conspiracies to keep their cards close to their chests. Both as a self-preservation matter, and because this same principle means the knowledge you carry can demand a high price.

                                                      Knowledge - actionable knowledge - is a precious commodity and bargaining chip. You don't give that away for free. Your Elders teach you because they feel they need to (a truly ignorant Fledgling will shatter the Masquerade a million different ways by accident). Or because they have something to gain by you knowing something. Whether what they tell you is accurate, and whether your Elder knows it, is another matter entirely.

                                                      A new Kindred (whether newly created or newly arrived) - unknown and unvetted - is a potential security risk. They could be a spy, future turncoat, or simply incompetent to the point of being dangerous. You don't tell them more than is needed, to minimize risks. They are told where to present themselves, and are told to fuck off when and if they stray too close to a more powerful vampire's territory. Established Elders, despite having all the time in the world, have no time to be giving the run-down about city politics to every fresh face. They have better things to do. Their underlings give out messages, and even THEY have better things to do than waste time coddling Neonates.

                                                      Most new Kindred in town won't make it to the end of the century anyway. Unless they make themselves problems or assets, it's not worth devoting attention to them until they're at least Ancillae. When they have ostensibly proven their ability to survive and play the game.​

                                                      When a Neonate shows themselves as an asset, that still makes them a potential danger. Those with enough guile, social skills, and influence to force Elders to pay attention are the very kind of ambitious go-getters that try to climb the hierarchy of vampiric power. So even though there's much to gain from handing out knowledge, it's still a risk. The young whelps could use your kind instruction against you.

                                                      Better to keep plenty of information back. Make the upstarts pay for every morsel of understanding they can get out of you. Bind them with webs of Debt and reliance on their betters. Make them play the Jyhad "the right way". Until they've been "properly" incorporated into the existing power structures (hopefully a restructured one that, with the whelp's diligent efforts, improves YOUR position).

                                                      A knowledgeable Neonate is one that's harder to control. They get ideas. If they know about other Clans, Bloodlines, and Sects, they can try to make alliances with them and not you. If they understand how the status quo functions - the forces that underpin it, and where it's weakest - they can disrupt it. They're the gutter trash. Unlike you, they have nothing to lose, and everything to gain.

                                                      And if they learn enough, they may realize how little you actually know. They may realize you're not as privy to the secrets of the Jyhad as you pretend. Which is both dangerous, and deeply disturbing to be reminded of.

                                                      No. It's better to keep those cards close. You have all eternity to take the whelp's measure.

                                                          When you consider how much info you could dump in a single hour, and that half the vampires are going to be charismatic types love the sound of their own voice/love gossip/want to appear supremely knowledgeable,. I find it unlikely that the average neonate would know so little.
                                                          Secrets are power among vampires and the majority of undead actually prefer to bargain for their information. As such, it's hard to know what's going on in another city let alone in ancient annals of Kindred history.

                                                          It doesn't help that the two Clans actually prone to showing information are the Nosferatu and Tremere who keep it all in-clan.

                                                          The Brujah used to teach everyone but they've lost their reputation as the Learned Clan.

                                                          Mind you, this isn't entirely by design. The Modern Nights have been something of a disaster in my games with how the Kindred race has evolved. A lot more vampires are created by accident, as a tool of war, or as disposable pawns only to get their freedom due to the Jhyad when their masters never intended them to be anything more than a tool under their iron fist in a velvet glove.

                                                          But the ignorance of the Jyhad has an out of game purpose as well. It encourages PCs to know exactly what they're getting out of an Occult score.​ Those who put the EXP or Skill Points into it are showing that they are willing to make the effort to learn what the books have. The person who loves to talk more than most vampire NPCs as MyLifeIsScary describes is the PCs themselves. They can share all their own knowledge in-game once they make the effort to acquire it.

                                                          It also provides an air of mystery to the setting that discovering secrets is important. The "common knowledge" of the books is incredibly valuable and it helps show why so many Elders are disdainful of your typical Lick who doesn't even know what a Salubri is.
