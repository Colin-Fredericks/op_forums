    Predator Types as a bundle of traits (Specialty, Backgrounds, Merits and Disciplines, balanced with a Flaw) should just be a kind of grouped Advantage, like Paths in the StoryPath system.

    You could give them a simple mechanical or roleplaying bonus to make them slightly better than buying everything separately (e.g., +2 dice on hunting rolls or social rolls related to your Predator Type) or just have them as a time-saving device for those who want to make characters on the fly.

    Think of Concepts in Exalted Essence, for example, and how they suggest a certain set of traits for quick character creation, but don't force anything on you.

    The easiest fix, though, is flat XP costs. Then the third Discipline dot issue doesn't matter.

That's a "Deadly mortal, a Gifted mortal with magic or other supernatural powers, or a well-armed group of Gifted mortals (a private security squad, a lawyer contingent, a Russian mafia bratva)" They're not minor.

            "Just gain so much political power that they become irrelevant" sounds simple but its like, "Financial problems? Just become a millionaire, problem solved."

            It seems like your issue is with the Flaw, not with the predator type. "Just kill your enemy" sounds simple enough but my brother in Caine, we are playing a roleplaying game. if that's the thing your character is trying to do then we are going to tell a story about that, it's not going to just happen in downtime between sessions.

            Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post
            Blood Leech, Consensualist, Farmer - the vampire loses so much Humanity, perhaps even adopts a Path, so that any squeamishness about feeding from humans becomes silly.
            For all of these the flaw doesn't go away when you lose humanity. Prey exclusion gives you Stains if you violate the exclusion, regardless of what your humanity rating is. Farmer has a Willpower tax for drinking human blood, regardless of your humanity rating. Nothing about either of these flaws requires that the flaw is a conscious, deliberate moral choice. People (alive and dead) are complicated and trauma is complicated, even if you become a hardened evil asshole.

            But again it seems like your problem here is with the Flaw, not the Predator Type. "Lol just lose enough humanity so your Flaw shouldn't matter" is (1) not how the game rules work anyway, but mort importantly (2) a crappy way of engaging with the game's premise. If you're sitting at my table trying to figure out how to game around your character's weaknesses you won't be sitting at my table anymore because you're not a good fit for the kind of game I want to run.
