    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

    There's a couple of points here that need to be addressed:

    1. The Sabbat have really taken a beating in V5 as not just a sect but as a concept: Paradox has decided the Sabbat are something they flat out either do not like or do not want to particularly see succeed as a playstyle. As such, they've decided to take the Sabbat Civil War of BJD and the "Gehenna War" to destroy the sect. As the Sabbat are my second favorite group after the Anarchs (who the Sabbat are the poor cousins of the aweseomeness thereof), this is somethin I don't like.

    However, they have REALLY gone to town on making the Sabbat collapse into parodies of themselves. I don't like it. It's not something I appreciate but they have been hammering the Sabbat collapsing for the better part of five years now.

    * The Lasombra defection
    * The Tzimisce defections
    * The destruction of some of its most popular Paths of Enlightenment.
    * Defections to Blood Cults
    * Massive casualties due to the Gehenna War(s)
    * Loss of territory

    And so on.

    I don't know who has a bee in their bonnett (forgive my Southernism) about the Sword of Caine but the Sabbat really is wrecked beyond all repair. Barring a new edition, those who have fled this sinking ship seem the best off.

    2. Some people have collective amnesia about the Lasombra Antitribu: I mentioned this above and I think people are pretending that it wasn't the case in V1,2,Revised,20 because they're so pissed off about 1#. Believe me, I get that because I find the treatment of the Sabbat in 1# to be ridiculous. It's my 2nd biggest complaint about the game and frankly, might be my biggest.

    However, the Lasombra Antitribu have always been the most powerful Bloodline in the Camarilla and always JUST SHORT of having their own seat on the Inner Council. They have a 4th generation Methuselah in the inner circles of the Camarilla after all, which is arguably more powerful than all of the other Inner Council combined.

    The idea the Lasombra need THAT many defections to go from being a bloodline to a full Clan in the Camarilla is a stretch. Remember, there were Lasombra Princes in the previous city and not just Marcus Vitel. Really, the biggest weirdness about the "defection" is acting like the Lasombra didn't ALWAYS have a steady line of defectors from the Sabbat. They weren't exactly A LOT of them and plenty did get tracked down and killed but they weren't ever unheard of either. An entire city once defected.

    3. I get the impression Justin Achilli doesn't have complete freedom here: The Sabbat depicted in The Sword of Caine are a swarm of zombies and make the previous Revised take on the Sabbat look positively enlightened. However, if you talk with Justin Achilli on Twitter or other social media, he says that the modern Sabbat are just a cover for the Elders who are sitting in their bunkers sending them all out to die.

    That the "true Sabbat" is tsill around, just waiting Gehenna out.
    1) probably, but they could just as easily soft retcon the sabbat back to power anytime they feel like. V5 is full of them anyway. I mean the Sabbat book subtly contradicts itself anyway.

    2) the lasombra antitribu were in pretty small numbers and this was something of a plot point.

    3) hard to say, were working off guesswork here.

    Its probably worth noting 1 and 3 wouldn't really change the lasombra being a borderline bloodline and 2 is pretty weak.

                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                Given there's only about 2000 vampires of each Clan with 1:100000 numbers, there's not that much difference,
                The clans are not equivalent in population. We know there is a big difference between the more populous clans and the least populous ones.

                We know the Brujah are by far the largest clan period with their promiscuous embraces and outlawry. When you combine them with the brothers among the Anarchs and Sabbat, they are probably the largest clan of vampires in the world.

                I would say the Gangrel, Toreador, and Ventrue fall in the next spot. They were fairly dominant members of the Camarilla. The Gangrel were not as well represented in the cities, but had a substantial number in the outlands and the wilderness.

                I would say the Nosferatu probably have noticeably less numbers than the last three, but would still appear substantial.

                The Malkavians and Tremere probably had the lowest numbers of the classic seven clans for different reasons.

                I am sure others have different ideas in mind, but this is what I use as general numbers although individual domains can have very different ratios.

                Anyone else would be less than these, including the Lasombra anti-tribu. Obviously rare bloodlines like the Daughters of Cacophony and Samedi would be less. But would they have a higher population than Caitiff? In almost every case the Lasombra anti-tribu are mentioned, it states that they are limited in the number of domains they are in, generally around the Mediterranean, and they sometimes get mentioned in South America. They seem very restricted in the domains they are in.

                Yes, Montano was very old and powerful, but it's not like the Camarilla was lacking in numbers of incredibly ancient and powerful vampires. Plus given his age, it was never quite sure how active he was in modern nights or whether he went through long periods of torpor. Like anything when a detailed history is not given, every ST fills in the blanks according to what they like.

                I don't think there is much evidence that the Lasombra anti-tribu were so large that they were somehow almost a peer of the classic seven. We are not given any information to think that in any of the sourcebooks that I know of. That sounds more like something an individual ST wants to house rule. As many fans like the Lasombra anti-tribu, it is only natural that they want to see them increase in number and become more ubiquitous. And that's fine, but it was not canon. If the Lasombra truly were as large as the classic seven clans, then it would have been much more likely they would have given them membership on the Inner Circle originally, buth they didn't so they weren't.

                Of course, as I often mention we have many different versions of canon now as the idea that there is one continuity between the different versions of the game are becoming increasingly unrealistic. The 1e/2e, Revised, V20, and V5 are pretty much their own worlds using the same concepts but making lots of changes that create incompatible versions.

                        -The seven clans are of course going to be more populous than the other six because they're generalists with broadly applicable skills/disciplines and thus they can spread more effectively than specialists like the other six clans or the bloodlines who are really all about embracing from a very narrow selection of people and getting into niche markets (and tend to form sects of their own)

                        -The Ashirra have outlived their usefulness (putting the Baali/Assamites in check) and really now only serve to other the muslim world. That they still exist is the product of liberal whites fetishizing the east/minorities and/or corporate virtue signalling. I'd much rather just have the Cam be the global conspiracy it was originally intended to be.

                        questioning whether the Lasombra actually should belong to the sabbat when it's so against their values
                        I don't know how you can actually read the books and have this opinion. There's a lot of players who don't read the books and just trust that whatever is going on in their game is as-canon intended; that's the only circumstance where I can imagine a reasonable person would reason that Lasombra had been misplaced in their majority sect choice. They are perfect for the Sabbat. For the Camarilla, they're passable, but undesirable; natural brutes, authoritarians, power hungry control freaks and religious devotees are not what the camarilla is after.

                        Just looking at who-plays what clans without reading the books will make you terribly misinformed. You'd start to think the Tremere are clowns, that the Gangrel and Brujah could only ever be autuark murder-hobos and don't belong in the Camarilla at all, That Malkavians are a joke clan, that settites radiate sleaze and that all Ventrue are cartoonish bourgeoise assholes who don't know how to talk to common people but insist that you have to put up with their bullshit because they "Have 4's in Charisma/Manipulation/Appearance".
                        The setting should not be dictated by how it is commonly (mis)played, but by reason. That goes more than triple for LARP: You're all welcome to your own fun but the idea that well written art can take a dumpster dive because some adults were playing dress up and were using rock, paper scissors to decide if they could stay invisible and not get detected by the guy they were playing RPS with. Good movies have good writing and good casting and professional acting and plenty of retakes. I refuse to have concessions made for LARP impact tabletop rules, and I refuse to have LARP, one of the most limited mediums there is, influence metaplot for a game that primarily isn't LARP. IDGAFF that Lasombra are popular in LARP. If what I'm told is true and we go by LARP, the Sabbat should always win except for the last bit where the game master decides the Camarilla needs to win.
