                Their human counterparts basically did much such offers.... and then tossed the treaties in the trash after they stopped needing proxy fighters to settle things between each other. I think the WtA general portrays Garou history as a mirror in that regard: the first wave of European Garou made deals to stick to the east coast (esp. the lands without caretakers because of Middle Brother's sacrifice) and keep things that way, and then as colonialism spread west, the European Garou used the changing reality to claim old deals weren't valid any more and demand new ones, rinse, repeat.

                ----------

                Though to repeat myself from far too many other threads: Mage isn't about right vs. left. It's about statism vs. anti-statism (and it's many forms). The Union is an extreme take on statism: they are constantly pushing for the state to control more and more of personal life (even purging elements that supported non-statist cultural expressions like religion). The Union's enemies, from the organized Traditions to the various independent Crafts, have a whole spectrum of disagreements over why they don't like statism, but they all agree they need to fight it. By necessity and time, the Traditions represent as classically liberal anti-statist position where the state is only valid to protect individual liberties.

                        I thought the Disparate Alliance was one of the worst features of M20.

                        There's already a faction for anti-statist traditionalist mages who want to preserve the old ways and keep their brand of magic alive in modern times. It's called the Traditions.

                        The Disparates basically seem like inserts to give minority cultures their own faction. Even leaving aside the incongruity in the Dreamspeakers still belonging to the Traditions and the Knights Templar belonging to the Disparates (???), the four factions are meant to cover some really big ideas. The Traditions are the classic wizards keeping myth and wonder alive in a secular age. The Technocracy is the cold and sterile science relentlessly driving that secular age. The Nephandi are existence-hating nihilists who've given up on building a better world. The Marauders are mad wizards who've been shattered by hubris, trauma, and tragedy. Again, these factions cover big ideas that can encompass a wide range of belief systems and sociopolitical ideologies. The Traditions and the Technocracy can be left wing or right wing. They are bigger than that. They represent timeless ideas that were just as relevant in 1992 as they are in 2021.

                        And then we have the Disparates. They don't encompass a timeless idea that feels like it could've existed in 1992. They feel pulled from 2010s+ Twitter woke culture that insists on greater representation for groups that were represented just fine in the existing four factions. And, on top, there are some author lines that say they're the best at fighting the Nephandi and better/more hardcore than the Traditions in various ways. They feel like an obvious author favorite and clumsy insert to make the game more culturally sensitive.

                        I say this as someone who's pretty politically left-leaning.

                        Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.

                                        Originally posted by False Epiphany View Post
                                        I thought the Disparate Alliance was one of the worst features of M20.
                                        I partially disagree.

                                        The thing with the DA that makes complete sense is their place in the in-setting politics. The Council of Nine Traditions can potentially include each and every rl mystic practice, but in-game it is still a very specific political body, with specific structures and magical traditions that newcomers are required to observe. It isn't a happy union of cultures, it is an institution with its own history and chain of command. And with its own power structure that simply isn't that open. Even among the Traditions not everyone is happy with the Council, and inside each Tradition there is also a lot of conflicts and unhappy marriages among their constituent traditions.

                                        So I don't think that the Disparate Alliance is a bad idea. Maybe the Knights Templar being there, because of their own history, is. But for many Crafts the Council isn't their champion against the Technocracy, but just another bunch of tyrants wanting to impose their own ways all the same. And in that situation, the DA makes a lot of sense as communications develop among those previously dispersed Crafts.
