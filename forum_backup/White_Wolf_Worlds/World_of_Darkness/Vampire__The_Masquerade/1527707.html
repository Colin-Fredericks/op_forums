          The precise answer(s) to the question of why the Antediluvians, collectively, chose to disappear is one of those things you kind of need to settle for yourself to some extent. But the short answer is "the Jyhad."

          Accepting the traditional Noddist narrative, the Antediluvians are all too aware that, despite their incredible powers, they are not invincible. Otherwise they likely would not have been able to overthrow the Second Generation. You also have at least two of the truly ancient Antediluvians, [Ventrue] and [Brujah], who have at least possibly been sent to their Final Deaths, in some fashion or another by their peers.

          That doesn't include the cases we know of where the Antediluvians were thwarted by non-vampires. You've already mentioned what the Technocracy did to [Ravnos], and its worth mentioning that magic of the modern era is frequently regarded as having weaknesses or limits that did not exist in the past. [Set] was similarly defeated in his home territory by the various allies of the Osirian League, even though it came with a heavy price.

          All of this is to say that there are dangers in the world that even the Antediluvians cannot just dismiss (depending on the version of the story you go with), so like other vampires they hide themselves in the shadows as a survival tactic, just a lot deeper and more effectively due to their age and power.

          It's also worth noting that torpor is just something old vampires find it harder and harder to resist. And the Antediluvians are OLD. So if you're going to be forced into a turbo-nap, it's probably a good idea to make sure no one can bother you in the mean time.

                      The true nature of the antedeluvians and the eschatology of Gehenna has never been defined so you have to come up with your own reasons. But there are lots of things (some contradictory) scattered throughout the books that you can pick and choose.

                      One thing to remember is that the antedeluvians don't necessarily "hide" because they want to. Very old vampires "naturally" want to go into torpor and seem to metamorphosize into a different instar of their evolution. Methusalehs eventually become incapable of drinking animal or human blood and then can only feed on vampire vitae. That may not be the extent of the change.

                      It's entirely possible that it is a natural process for vampires to eventually go into torpor and change. We just don't know what that kind of change is or how long it takes to accomplish because there simply there hasn't been enough time yet. Maybe it is limited to the antedeluvians because they hail from the antedeluvian world which had different rules than the postdeluvian world. It's entirely possible Gehenna is just the future time period when such changes are completed, and any surviving antedeluvians will naturally awake to end this current world and begin the process of the next one that will replace it.

                      One Gehenna cult believes that the reduction of magic in this world puts more and more metaphysical burden on the godlike antedeluvians, and as long as the level of magic remains low the antedeluvians will continue to slumber. But should the level of magic increase, the antedeluvians will awaken and regain their full powers. In this explanation, it is not a function of vampire nature that explains antedeluvians slumbering, but a consequence of the status of the world around them. Even if this specific theory is not true, there could be other environmental reasons for why they slumber or awake. Maybe some kind of supernatural cycle.

                      Then there is the belief that the antedeluvians are choosing to hide in purpose - to avoid each other in the Jyhad, to avoid other incredibly powerful beings likewise considered to be players in the Jyhad (like the mummy Horus), or perhaps from other beings (like the Secret Masters of Ventrue lore). Revealing yourself too soon just makes you a target. Or perhaps they are active - but just on a different plane of existence that is unknowable to beings in the physical world of mortals, and they'll only return to their physical bodies for specific reasons.

                      There could be other factors or a combination of factors.

                                      One thing to consider is that it doesn't have to be the case that antedeluvians achieved any rank 10 or even 9 in a Discipline during the time they were active. Maybe they didn't have any. Maybe they only achieved it after thousands of years in torpor. Maybe they'll only achieve once they awake from torpor when Gehenna begins. The power levels of the antedeluvians that they have in the 21st century is not what they had during 15th century or in 4000 BC or 6000 BC. I imagine they were probably still powerful when they first awoke after the Flood, but maybe that was only at the level of powerful elder.

                                      The idea that the antdeluvians can be do whatever they want is mainly an artifact of the game developers not wanting to hear stupid stuff about players telling them at cons that they defeated an antedeluvian and gained their powers, in the same way Gary Gygax didn't want to hear about players telling him how they killed Thor or Zeus after Deities & Demigods was published. At any point and period in time, the antedeluvians only need to have as much power as you require them to serve the narrative need. If you think having certain powers at certain periods of time doesn't make sense given the lore and history, it's probably a good sign they didn't have them yet.

                                          I do sympathize with your situation. I also prefer the Antediluvians as "Vampires, but bigger and badder" rather than genuine gods with some special metaphysical function in reality. As I kind of mentioned, while I like treating level 10 Disciplines as limited only by the imagination and the power/concept of the Discipline itself, I am far less inclined to give them any special advantages in terms of the scale of their power. One of the hypothetical limiters on the Antediluvians getting out of control is Vitae. The rulebooks themselves never define the Blood Pool or spending limits of the Third Generation, but if you give their Plot Hole powers a noteworthy cost in Vitae, and put reasonable constraints on the Blood Pool available to them, then it helps to balance things out a fair bit. Even if they can do some downright insane shit if they're willing to go all-in, it may not be sustainable if they find themselves in a drawn-out conflict or facing multiple opponents with a comparable power level. Which incentivizes even these god-like monsters to walk *softly* as they carry their big sticks around.

                                          I mostly embrace the post-modern chaos of World of Darkness lore. I'd probably say that I'm happy to draw on content regardless of edition for IC lore, legends, etc. Regarding what's actually true from a Storyteller/OOC perspective, I generally try to stick as close to "canon" as possible, but there are inevitable contradictions, and I let my own agenda and preferences determine how I resolve the conflict. There are times where I wish for a more "solid" answer, especially when you get the grey areas that result from multiple ambiguities intersecting, and a lot of the issues that I haven't actually settled for myself come from stuff like that.

                                          Just one fun idea to share that I think was inspired by BJD is the idea that the diminishing power of Generation and (probably) the Clan Curses are something caused by the Antediluvians, not Caine. In particular, the idea that the rebellious assholes who rose up and murdered their sires would reasonably fear the same fate, and did *something* to ensure that the power of their descendants would diminish to prevent that danger.

                                          I'm also quite willing to take inspiration from other folks online. My preferred approach to the mess that is Saulot is somewhere between "strongly inspired" and "blatantly taken" from an idea that I got trawling around on the forums or Reddit; I didn't think to save the source at the time, and my minds wandered so much since then that I can't recall all the details. But then, even I'm still struggling on how I want to handle some of those details...

                                          It helps that there's almost nothing *really* locked into "canon" when it comes to the Antediluvians. Even Gehenna takes a pretty firm stand that you can take or leave whatever they have to say about the Antediluvians in that book, even the little blurbs at the beginning that they acknowledge as their ""canon"" answers regarding the fates of the Antes. If you like the idea of the Antediluvians as this menacing terror in the background, then you're welcome to have them be real. And if you're not really a fan, you can have them be far weaker than they are believed to be, or even already dead and gone.

                                          Finally, it's worth noting that my experience in actual play is barely existent. I've been chewing on answering the various mysteries of the World of Darkness in my personal canon for years now, but I mostly haven't had the opportunity to explore those ideas in depth with players. So there are limits to the value of my opinion lol.
