        Alright, I'm giving this a once-over. Apologies for not replying with quotes directly, I took all your feedback into consideration, which has been very helpful.

        Changes:
        - Dropped Meta-Path attempt, too complex and unwieldy. Not what I was trying to do anyway.
        - Removed True Will entirely from Sins and instead made it the aspiration of the Path
        - Removed elements that tried to put the spiritual aspect into rules
        - Added elements of P.o. Self-Focus, Metamorphosis, Entelechy, Eastern Paradox
        - Un-Crowleyfied description
        - Stronger emphasis on uncompromising nature
        - Removed redundant Sins and those logically facilitated by other Sins
        - Narrowed and rearranged Sins to more stringent progression
        - Adjusted phrasing to be in line with existing Paths
        - Reworked Rationales

        Path of Desire and True Will (V2)


        The origins of this Path can be traced back to the much older Road of Sin. There are key differences though: while it identifies desire as the driving force of both Beast and vampire, it tries to balance the egocentrism of the Road by integrating it with society.

        The followers of the Path seek True Will, a concept that is a combination of destiny, mindful action, and harmonic integration in the world all at once, while also placing utmost importance on the realization of the self. An adherent will strive to attain this state by refining themselves in a way not unlike a Metamorphisist would experiment with and refine their body, but will instead weigh their desires against themselves, their Beast, and society.

        Aspirants tend to combine introspection and self-reflection with living out their passions: in order to find their True Will, they have to engage with new ideas and conflicts both mundane and spiritual to temper themselves while staying true on their path to their destined place in the world. On the other hand, they need to have clear vision of where they see themselves, in the present and future, as well as a strong will to push for that vision.

        Much greater emphasis is put on control of the Beast than was usual for the Road of Sin. The Beast is a part of themselves adherents must find a common ground with, but set strict lines that cannot be crossed. There is no reason to deny the Beast pleasure in feeding, or indulgence in other desires – but there can be no quarter if the urges would imperil their progress on the Path. This stick-and-carrot approach eschews compromise, as with most else of the Path.

        The Path attempts to mediate realization of the self (Courage) with the Beast (Self-Control) and society (Conscience). In game terms, True Will closely corresponds to the character concept and the goals of the player on where to lead them. The player should have a clear idea of what those goals are, and what their character aspires to be.


        Virtues: Conscience, Self-Control

        Ethics:
        • Act mindful. All your actions will be reflected back onto you.
        • The Beast belongs to you, but you do not belong to it.
        • Share in your desires, but refuse unwanted ones. Never compromise.
        • Freedom, control, and will are your most valuable tools. Do not lose them.
        • There is no growth on toxic soil. Cultivate your environment as you would yourself.

        Hierarchy of Sins

        Path of Desire and True Will

        Rating Moral Guideline Rationale
        10 Feeling guilt for your actions There can be no guilt in mindful action
        9 Failing to explore a new desire Desire is kindling, do not let it run out
        8 Refusing indulgence that harms none Desire needs to burn to placate the Beast
        7 Refusing an opportunity to share in your passions Do not waste what is freely given to you
        6 Failure to treat others as you would be treated There can be no harmony in imbalance
        5 Acting against your own self interests Always evaluate, but never compromise
        4 Succumbing to Frenzy The Beast does not control, it obeys
        3 Becoming blood bound Your freedom is your greatest tool
        2 Losing Will or Virtue Your will is your strongest resource
        1 Apathy Losing desire is to embrace final death
        ​

                            I think you've done a much better job of putting the Hierarchy of Sins in tangible terms. But I can see where GreatHornedRatkin is coming from when he comments on it contradicting itself in places. Unless you are building a condition into the concept of "self-interest" that excludes exploiting others from being a viable option, then the Sins at Rank 5 and 6 are going to constantly contradict one another, where you'll constantly be forced to choose between committing the Sin of treating others different from how you would want to be treated or the Sin of acting against your own self-interest.

                            I also think that the Sin at Rank 6, basically "the Golden Rule," is doing a lot of heavy lifting. Depending on how you conceptualize it, that could be half or more of the Sins listed under Humanity, including some of the really nasty stuff like rape and murder. And you have it at Rank 6. Everything below it is largely the sort of stuff that a character is going to obviously want to avoid, like Frenzy and the Blood Bond. There were some things that I didn't include in my previous post because I was having trouble formulating my thoughts, but fortunately monteparnas kind of covered it. The Sins on a Path are meant to produce tension and drama in the form of moral conflict. You'll notice that most of the lower ranking Sins in Humanity's Hierarchy are extreme, but definitely not outside the bounds of what could be expected from PC murder-hoboery. I'm not enough of an expert on all of the Paths to be able to say whether they're all appropriately "balanced" in this way, but I can say that some of the ones I've looked at more, like Metamorphosis and Death and the Soul, introduce their own forms of drama by making it a serious Sin to avoid certain forms of antisocial or potentially self-destructive behaviour, like "showing compassion" or "preventing death" or "putting yourself at risk if necessary for SCIENCE." It's fundamentally different from a system that reflects more typical societal norms like Humanity does, but it still places demands on the role-player. Suddenly, a heart-wrenching description of an injured kitten becomes a moral drama for your character/player, to see whether or not they resist the urge to go "Aw" and try to rescue the poor animal, which would be a Sin.

                            I just want to quickly note that having "Apathy" be the ultimate Sin for this Path really doesn't work if you intend it to be available for PCs, who are by their nature highly driven and calculating in their actions. To the extent that Sinning against this kind of tenet is possible, it's the sort of thing that would likely be happening off-screen, not during roleplaying sessions with an engaged and attentive player. This is a Path that a PC cannot fail during play, effectively.

                            I do also want to address the points being made about how "True Will" conflicts with the efforts to make the Path more pro-social. This Path is supposed to be descended from the Road of Sin, but supposedly rejects the "egocentrism" of its predecessor. I think the problem with that is that Egocentrism is The Mission of the Road of Sin. That is part of how it staves off the Beast, along with most Paths of the Road adopting Instincts and giving the Beast more freedom. The way you frame the position just seems to take for granted that the Egocentrism of the Road of Sin is a Bug, when it's supposed to be a Feature, and I don't think you've provided an adequate philosophical justification for that rejection, or an explanation for how "True Will" functions as an alternative to Egocentrism as a way to stave off the Beast. It still feels too nebulous when the Egocentrism that supposedly influences it, and presumably serves as its guiding principle, has been hobbled by pro-social compromises, and has also rejected the compromise with the Beast that is implicit in the adoption of Instinct as a Virtue.

                            Of course, you've also mentioned the influence of the Path of the Devil, which is somewhat distinct. But there is still an important distinction between it and the Path you've created. To the extent that the Path of the Devil replaces the pure Egocentrism of the Road of Sin with a wider Liberationism, it still has a clear and identifiable Mission that it is aimed at, and the nature of that Mission informs the restrictions that it places upon the Vampire that follows it. And it thus informs the drama that it adds to that character's story.

                            And while I've kind of touched on this indirectly, I notice that this Path retains both of the Virtues normally associated with Humanity. Why is that? I know that some of the medieval Roads allowed Vampires to retain both of their original Virtues, but IIRC those were the Roads of Heaven and Humanity, which obviously most closely adhere to the sorts of standards we would associate with modern-day Humanity. This Path supposedly descends from the Road of Sin, but possesses neither of the Virtues associated with it. The Path does not seem to be particularly proud of its ancestry, as most of the discussion around it seems to involve criticism of it. It feels like you want to claim the exaltation of Desire associated with the Road of Sin while rejecting the challenges that following that principle to its logical conclusion would entail for a Vampire living in the modern world.

                            Does this really need to be its own Path of Enlightenment, or is it just a philosophy that a Vampire still on the "Path" of Humanity can adhere to in their nightly unlife?

                                        Roads occupy a distinct niche in the game. Overall I think they're a superior rule if you want to leave out the theme of Humanity as focus.

                                        In normal VtM Humanity is basic decency, it is restrictive in a sense, but allows for almost any character concept since you're not required to "follow" it, it's just there.

                                        Since the point of the game is to explore this, a Path can't be just an alternative, but a contrast, something that reinforces how far a character must go to compensate and be clear they're an extremist. In a sense, the Path is not protection against degeneration, but a less horrible way to degenerate, a psychological damage control.

                                        Humanity is by design the superior option, even if the character can't recognize it.

                                        But Dark Ages wanted to explore the contrast of values of medieval societies compared to the modern world. It's not about vampires who try to ditch their human identity, but the world before the Enlightenment and conflict between different social values.

                                        This had two effects. The first to attend to the need of a more flexible system since there was no expected standard to cover most character concepts. The second to make Roads as stand ins for the social conflicts in the game, as each Road also represents a culture, religion, caste and other social divisions clashing in the world.

                                        A chronicle devised to displace the notion of a basic natural morality from focus, remove the centrality of human identity from the game and instead work seriously with vampires following different worldviews either as holdover of their living days or response to their condition can do worse than using Roads instead of Paths.

                                            Since my previous post remains banished to the Shadow Realm for reasons I don't understand, I'm going to share some of the thoughts I've had since posting it, along with a bit of a summary of the key points behind those ideas, but there's a lot of details I just can't be bothered to repeat when I'm just waiting for the mods to approve them >_>

                                            OOC I think there's a problem with the Hierarchy of Sins you've constructed, collectively, where they remove or fail to reintroduce a lot of the drama and tension that playing a PoE is supposed to bring to the game, IMO. Most of the lower-ranking Sins are choices your character is already going to want to obviously avoid, so you haven't created tension in the form of a dilemma, but have instead just added additional weight and consequences to things that already tend to be Bad, like Frenzies and Blood Bonds. Again, the choices might make sense individually, but in the big picture they add up to is a Path that kind of looks like Easy-Mode to me.

                                            In particular, I think having "Treat others as you want to be treated" as your Rank 6 Sin basically front-loads most of the crimes that would trigger rolls on Humanity (theft, assault, murder, etc.) into one single slot on the Hierarchy, and its positioned within the "comfort zone" of Humanity/Path Ratings, that 5-7 area that's considered VERY typical to end up in during play. I also think that the "Golden Rule" does frequently come into conflict with your Rank 5 Sin, "acting against your own Self-Interest," because there will often be conflicts between the Self-Interest of a Vampire and the well-being and dignity of those around them. Unless you have a very particular definition of Self-Interest, or a very wild concept of "how I would like to be treated" that really needs some elaboration, these two things do seem somewhat contradictory to me.

                                            Just going to briefly mention that I have a completely opposite opinion of "Apathy" as a Sin. I think it belongs in the 8-10 range where most "thought-crime" Sins typically get put, and that Apathy specifically is a *terrible* choice for a Rank 1 Sin on a Path intended for players to use. See Missing Post for details.

                                            I also think there's an IC problem where the philosophy of "True Will" remains somewhat vague and non-committal from a philosophical or ideological point of view, and I think that lack of firm convictions would leave it too weak to resist the degrading influence of the Beast. I think you need to commit more to a particular direction, in one of a few possible ways:

                                            1) Stop trying to make a Path of Enlightenment. Instead, come up with a more general philosophy that a Vampire can follow in the Unlife while still ultimately being on the "Path" of Humanity. This isn't meant as critically as it possibly sounds: I found myself in a similar position a while ago, where I ended up deciding that my "family-friendly" Tzimisce character concept was better off as someone *influenced* by Metamorphosist thought while not being on the Path of Metamorphosis themself, instead retaining their Humanity.

                                            2) Commit to the Egocentrism. Get rid of the "treat others as they want to be treated" nonsense. "Do As Thou Wilt" *is* the sum of the Law. As you discussed above, there is ample discussion of how you can follow the Road of Sin and still be an "enlightened egoist" who understands the need to be nice to other people *when* it suits their own interests. As long as you have Self-Control to manage your Frenzies, and your Path tenets aren't actively pushing you into antisocial behaviour, your Vampire doesn't need to *actually* care about being fair to others.

                                            3) Abandon the Egocentrism. Stop trying to use the Road of Sin as your guide, because it just doesn't play well with a Path that wants to have serious moral obligations to other people. You might want to use the Path of Entelechy as a example of a Path that is more pro-Humanity and pro-Self-Control as a means of pursuing self-actualization.
