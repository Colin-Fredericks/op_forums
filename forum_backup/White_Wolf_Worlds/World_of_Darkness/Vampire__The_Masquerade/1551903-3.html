        Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
        The original Hunters Hunted sourcebook has several characters that can easily be used in a Free State chronicle. I'll start with one of No One of Consequence mentioned: Deborah Rose Keller. Keller was a suburban housewife of a physician when her son was kidnapped and murdered in an occult ceremony. In response she became a public advocate and began researching similar occult style murders and crimes across the country. She became known as a "rituals crime" expert and become frequently consulted by police departments across the USA. She's like a combination of John Walsh and Tipper Gore.

        Keller found enough evidence that she became convinced there was kind of occult conspiracy behind these crimes, and this being the World of Darkness, this could actually be true. Once she realized her theories about certain evidence was making police think she was a nut, she clammed up, toned down her public statements, and continued her investigations in private. At some point she encountered a real vampire and managed to escape alive, now knowing vampires are real. At the time of Hunters Hunted, she had not yet uncovered another vampire, but she's still well known enough that her name is recognized by many Kindred and Princes. She's also made some kind of connection with the Society of Leopold and presumably therefore has seen other evidence of vampire activity.

        Keller herself has some decent attributes on her character sheet. High Social and Mental skills, and three dots in Empathy, Leadership, Investigation, and Occult. She also has the Backgrounds of Allies, Contacts, and Resources, but I think that understates her influence. It's more of an indication of what she can bring directly to bear. She's fairly famous around the country and has a "huge following among other concerned housewives and citizen's groups." As a recognized expert on "ritual crimes", she also has some limited respect among law enforcement. She often tours the country giving seminars to police, citizens groups, and PTAs. Her ties to the Free States specifically is that it is mentioned she lives in California, and therefore I'm arbitrarily assigning her to Orange County specifically.

        By herself, she's not much of a threat. It's really her role as a public advocate against "occult murders" that can create problems for the Kindred and PCs. What she really needs is some kind of public group like the Parents Music Resource Center (the infamous committee created by four wives of prominent Washington DC politicos, including Tipper Gore that helped create the "Parental Advisory" warning label on music albums) and the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children. Like those, it should have some other members and have some big funders. It needs to act as an information clearinghouse and resource for parents, children, law enforcement agencies, schools, and communities to assist them in solving occult crimes and raise public awareness about ways to prevent those kinds of crimes. I imagine a not inconsiderable number of people involved have lost loved ones to vampires in some way - either as victims killed when feeding or "cult involvement" that lead to them disappearing as they became members of Herds or became ghouls or similar. Not to mention crimes committed by other supernaturals like infernalists, nephandi, Black Spiral Dancers, and other creatures.

        I envision most hunters in general likely target the vampires of the Sabbat instead of the Camarilla or independent clans. The Sabbat is just a lot more sloppy and leaves a lot more evidence. This group would not be different, plus the Sabbat's various ritae more closely resembles the kind of occult ritual crimes this group investigates. It's not that Camarilla Kindred do not engage in such activities, it's just that they are involved in things like the Sabbat Creation Rite, the Blood Bath, the Blood Feats, the Festival of the Dead, and the Palla Grande which involved a lot of ritual aspects and mortal deaths. I imagine these activities provide the basis for a lot of occult crime sites that are proof of a "occult conspiracy."

        Plus I'm sure Keller is documenting who among these local law enforcement seem to be real allies (police who have independently come to the conclusion of an "occult conspiracy" or vampires) and who may be secret enemies (which may number any number of clueless police or just those who don't want to be seen as crazy, but also has to be a significant number of police under vampire influence/domination/blood bond).

        I would probably never have Keller herself become involved in any plot. Instead, it would be local citizens groups, perhaps families of the victims or vampires, who use or assist Keller's organization. Keller herself simply runs the organization at a high level, builds networks, and is often on mass media. Make her and the group be seen as something potentially dangerous to the Kindred and lessen the idea that vampires control everything and don't have anyone pushing back against them. It's also a good group to use for any family members of the victims of the PCs to join or help fund. A parent whose daughter who mysteriously disappeared and whose body was later found drained of blood would be a good choice. Also, given the idea that such a group more likely than not exposes or identifies activities of the Sabbat more than the Camarilla, it could be a good way for other vampires to use in their struggle against the Sabbat. We can also utilize Keller - or more likely someone trained by her or her group in proper investigation techniques of occult crimes - to serve as a good outside consultant helping law enforcement. And possibly knowing a great deal about certain Sabbat rites, blood magic rituals, etc. in a lot more detail (but in a clinical way) than someone "should". In general, I think it's a good "low level" hunter threat in the sense that it can heighten concern for PCs to hear their name mentioned, but it isn't a threat that can be immediately solved with violence.

        The anarchs of the Free States, of course, are somewhere in the middle. They lack the obvious depravity and ritual of the Sabbat, but don't have the discipline or internal policing of the Camarilla. Many LA anarchs won't be exposed, but some of the more outrageous anarchs and their activities may be identified. Since I'm placing Keller somewhere in Orange County, I'll also base her organization in one of the cities there.

        We really a good name for this group. Names can really make or break concepts like this. Maybe something like the National( or American) Center/ (or Alliance, Foundation or similar) Against Occult Crimes. If people have any suggestions for the name of this group, I'd love to hear them!

                        The other hunter mentioned in Hunters Hunted that might show up in LA is Dennis Quincy, the Uncanny Danny. He's a stage magician/paranormal skeptic in the vein of James "The Amazing" Randi. He has vast media support and is a prolific author. Randi himself was a somewhat frequent guest on The Tonight Show with Johnny Carson and appeared on a variety of other TV shows. If Dennis is our analogue, he can likely show up in LA at any time convenient to the ST regardless of where he actually lives. He's only had one vampire encounter, but it was sufficient to make him realize that they do, in fact, exist.

                        How to use him though?

                        No One of Consequence mentioned a possible small group of hunters who were illusionists/mentalists. The only problem with that is such a group is likely to be skeptics so the only reason they would ever join such a group is if they had incontrovertible proof of vampire existence. It would be very hard for Dennis to recruit any fellow skeptics in that community. They just won't believe him, and Dennis can't exactly reproduce what happened to him. He knows he'll sound like a nut. So unless the ST just claims these other mentalists had similar experiences, it'll be hard to have a group like that IMO.

                        Of course Dennis could use his scientific and investigation skills to find other surviving victims, but they'll be of any number of beliefs about vampires. They won't necessarily share Dennis' own materialist philosophy.

                        Because despite Dennis encountering a vampire, I don't think it means Dennis thinks the supernatural is real. We're told that after the attack he came up with a scientific theory to explain vampirism. He won't look for a paranormal explanation. I think it is much more likely he'll attempt to explain them in terms of a "scientific" paradigm. Eventually that attempt might collapse under the weight of cumulative evidence that simply can't be explained by science, but that may take a long time. I think it's far more likely his initial theories might be some consideration that it is a result of some kind of parasite, parallel evolution, or something else. Maybe some combination of the vampires from The Strain, Necroscope, or Whitley Streiber's Keepers. It could go increasingly fringe science for a long time as he learns more, but he'll likely reject outright magical or supernatural explanations.

                        The one interesting thing about a "hunt" from Dennis is that it's basically done as a result of scientific curiosity and the advancement of knowledge. It won't primarily be about protecting people (though I think that would also be important in the long run). So a hunt won't be about destroying a vampire to protect or avenge one's loved one. It'll be about capturing a specimen for analysis, autopsy, investigation. That may mean Dennis' first evidence might be captured ghouls or thin bloods rather than actual full blooded Kindred simply because they're less powerful and easier to capture intact or kill and examine their corpse.

                        However, before he can do that, he'll still need to recruit people to help him, as well as think through the possible legal ramifications of his experiments. Kidnapping, corpse stealing, possible "murder", or unauthorized autopsy/dissection are crimes that could see him prosecuted. He would want to avoid that. That may also mean his first entry into a chronicle might not be as an "active" hunter, but someone who shows up at the morgue, crime scene, or other secured location that has evidence he wants to see, and gains access to it (and perhaps steals it).

                        I really like the idea of the character, but I do think it will take a lot of work to introduce him into a chronicle. I see this as a lot of steps slowly building up the idea that someone may be secretly investigating scenes of obvious Masquerade breaches (hopefully by NPCs, not the PCs) and some time before the PCs figure out it is this famous skeptic/magician.

                        I would sorely love to have a small team around him, perhaps the equivalent of Allies and Contacts rather than teammates or retainers (his character sheet in HH shows Contacts 4, Fame 2, Resources 4, and Reputation 1). Dennis is very much an intellectual challenge, not a physical one. Although not listed on his character sheet, I'd consider giving him a Merit like Iron Will or Unbondable, or simply making him one of the "Neutrals" often mentioned in early Vampire sourcebook lore as those who are simply immune to Dominate.

                        It's also worth mentioning that Dennis' one encounter with a vampire was someone who clearly intended to embrace him. She states she intended to give him a "gift" which he can enjoy throughout "eternity". So it is possible this vampire is still out there, stalking him, and she might be very protective of him and hostile to anyone who intends him harm. Nothing is said about this vampire other than she had one ghoul (whose job was to lure Dennis to his regnant), that she was beautiful (but that may be more a result of Presence rather than Appearance), and that she has glowing red eyes (indicating Protean 1). My gut instincts assigns this vampire to the Malkavian clan, but she could easily be most clans without a problem.

                                Another possible source for hunters is the FBI Special Affairs Division, first detailed in Hunters Hunted, but expanded in Project: Twilight. The first two corebooks mentioned an FBI agent in their Gary, IN chronicle adventure, but he was simply a lone FBI agent who had become convinced of supernatural activities.

                                HH shows a rather small SAD with only five field agents, three lab techs, a director (George Thomasson) and another admin (called a second in command), all presumably based in DC.

                                Project Twilight shows a much bigger SAD lead by Gerald Osbourne, but keeps it in continuity by saying Kindred manipulations got Thomasson fired, but that SAD expanded greatly due to the "Grubbholb Case" in 1993 when a US Senator made sure SAD was highly funded after his granddaughter disappeared after a pack of wolves ransacked the family's country estate. This would expand SAD's interest into werewolves (Project: Twilight was a WtA sourcebook after all). In this version, SAD is a far larger division with many agents, much more resources, and regional directors (including one Thomas Little as the West Coast Director, presumably based in LA). For any sourcebook detailing government hunters, you pretty much need to have a larger organization to justify the sourcebook.

                                I think how an ST proceeds really depends on personal tastes and the chronicle needs. Do you want a much larger SAD style organization (or even a Second Inquisition style one)? Or do you prefer the much smaller Mulder/Scully and X-Files approach of HH (although this precedes the X-Files)? Or even just keep it to local FBI agents (and other local law enforcement officials) like William Shepard and Detective Gregory Stephens in Chicago?

                                I'll probably keep it as small as possible. Despite my recent hunter posts here, I don't find hunter plots to actually work well in game. They tend to lead to dead ends, and players rarely act imaginatively. I would either need to destroy PCs during the day, or have hunters act stupid and attempt to fight them at night. I don't like either of those. The first angers players. The second makes me upset. So I now typically limit hunters as a background element that targets dumb NPCs in the hopes this scares (or reminds) the PCs to act smart. Their presence is a good justification to force other NPCs to take actions on the Masquerade. Only if I think the appearance of hunters will add something interesting to the game would I even try introducing them into actual play and interaction with the NPCs.

                                    Any discussion of hunters will likely inevitably bring up the Society of St Leopold, so here we are. In the LABN sourcebook itself, I don't believe there is any mention of the Society. Nor do I think southern California is mentioned in the Inquisition sourcebook, so we're entirely on our own. I am normally hesitant to establish any significant presence of the Society. They should be rare. Any established cenaculum likely has some powerful members and resources (at least compared to ordinary hunters). They should only be found in predominantly Catholic areas, and likely are founded only when there is significant evidence of vampire activity, which in my chronicles tend to be in areas not controlled by the Camarilla.

                                    However, southern California seems to qualify in all of those categories. Between a quarter and a third of southern California's population is nominally Catholic. The anarchs are much sloppier at the Masquerade than the Camarilla is, and roving Sabbat packs are common. A major cenaculum here could support Society activity not only in southern California, but the Southwest in general, including Mexico.

                                    The Inquisition sourcebook only has a brief high level description of the Society in the United States. There are five provinces dividing up the entire country (Mid-Atlantic, Southeast, Northeast, Midwest, and West). The text actually mentions "six" provinces, but then gives us only five regions although it states the Western province may soon split into two. This is probably just bad editing. We're told the oldest US cenacula are in Philadelphia, Baltimore, New Orleans, and Boston. The only provincial cenacula we're told is in the Brookland neighborhood of DC, so that is clearly in charge of the Mid-Atlantic Province, not withstanding the age of Philadelphia and Baltimore cenacula. Boston is most likely the home to the Northeast provincial, and New Orleans the Southeast provincial. The Midwest and West Provinces must cover a huge territory, but we're not given any hint of where their provincial cenaculum is located. For our purposes here, it's also important to note that the entirety of Mexico and Central America is one province, and that its provincial is located in Mexico City, home of the oldest cenaculum in the Western Hemisphere. They must be consumed with fighting the Sabbat.

                                    Because of the way Prince Maria of San Diego was destroyed (mentioned, surprisingly, in a post on the Sabbat thread), I have arbitrarily decided San Diego is the best location for a major cenaculum. My idea is basically that inquisitors destroyed Maria back in 1945, and that cell stayed to establish a cenaculum there. Since Tara provides a more "princely" leadership to her anarchs in the city, her coterie doesn't tend to be bothered by it. Other anarchs she tolerates in the city sometimes run afoul. It's mainly Sabbat who get gacked since they tend to leave a lot more evidence and are very active in next door Tijuana. Although technically part of the Central American province, I imagine most activities in Tijuana and surrounding territory is actually done by the inquisitors here with reports also going to Mexico City.

                                        There is also a cenaculum in Los Angeles simply given its sheer size, but it is basically just a safehouse with real support coming from San Diego instead. The Society is active there, but because Reverend Cutler is a more "important" hunter figure in LA, I don't want to take away that thunder.

LA's cenaculum is probably "Weak" or "Marginal". As the older major city, San Francisco probably has an "Average" of stronger cenaculum, and would probably be a better candidate for the location of the provincial (and may have been at one point), but for chronicle purposes and personal taste I'm placing it in San Diego.

                                        As a "Strong" cenaculum, the location itself has a True Faith rating of 2, each inquisitor gets a stipend of $1500 a month (1995 money), two full time and three part time retainers taking care of it, decent security including a security guard on location at all times, some technology (computers with a LAN, a CD-ROM reader, and even a fax machine!), and two dedicated cars.

                                        As for the "coterie" of hunters (technically a cenacle) based there, I imagine they all have True Faith ratings between 1-5. I might have one have more at 6+. There will be at least one member knowing the Via Necromantiae so they can do exorcisms, and someone perhaps may know one other Theurgy.

                                        One of the security guards will be one of the condotierri, and as the cenaculum grows and develops, eventually all security guards will be members. They just aren't there yet. The condotierre is the manager of security, and the other guards report to him.

                                        Besides this cenacle at the San Diego cenaculum itself, there is another one based on John Carpenter's Vampires that are usually on the road elsewhere in the American southwest that report to the San Diego cenaculum. There's a similar cenacle in northern Mexico comprised of Mexican citizens who are officially report to the Central American province, and may even have some weak cenaculum (maybe in Hermosillo, Sonora). However, they coordinate a lot with San Diego especially when they're active in northern Baja California.

                                        I'll keep the cenacle in LA itself fairly small, maybe only 1-2 members. They mostly collect information, investigate, and attempt to help victims find safety rather than truly "hunt" themselves. They act as local guides to the more experienced cenacles when they show up to do the actual hunting. There's no real reason to keep this cenacle so small except I don't want to take attention away from Reverend Cutler and the other hunter groups we've talked about here.
