    I fully admit to my Anarch bias and that it blinds me to some of V5's flaws. I should point out that my SECOND favorite sect is the Sabbat and I feel like there were a lot of massive mistakes with them so if you think I'm a defender of V5 and the Sabbat, let me state otherwise. I feel V:TM is an adult game and making any group "too evil" to play misses the point.

    Still, I do think there's an argument that V5 isn't so much actually making dramatic changes to the setting as they are undoing a lot of the changes that had preexistingly shit on the Anarchs. How a lot of people feel about the Sabbat (including myself) is how I feel about how the Anarchs were treated in Revised.

    * Destroying the Anarch Free States so the Kuei-Jin could get some cheap heat.

    * Having Salvador become a collaborator with the Kuei Jin.

    * Making the Sabbat the primary enemy of the Camarilla vs. the Anarchs.

    * Dramatically increasing the Sabbat's territory in North America while getting rid of the Anarchs control over California.

    * Not including the Anarchs in any relavent way in the Gehenna or Thin Bloods storylines.

    * No Anarch Signature characters. Not a one.

    * The Anarchs are barely mentioned in ANY of the Revised Clan Books.

    It wasn't until Bloodlines any remembered they existed.

    V20 earned a lot of respect for me with Anarchs Unbound.
    Blood Cults is kinda sect neutral as more of a spinoff. Outside of heceta I'm not entirely convinced it even matters on the larger scale aka states of grace in Revised.

    You are partially correct about revised anarchs being downplayed but overstate your case. They had sections dedicated to them in carmarilla and their own book as a playable option. Compare to how badly v5 sabbat players have been treated

    Your arguement works on the assumption that the 2/revised drift away from anarchs wasn't an organic occurance in reaction to how the game developed but some sort of imposed vision. As I've mentioned before the anarchs as written in 91 or v5 arnt very good protagonist over a extended timeline. They work very well in a medium like video games where the limited narrative and play time works in their favour as a protagonist faction. But as a large scale dissedent sect they just don't work very well as written in a table top rpg. We're already seeing this problem emerging in threads on this very forum including this one. V5's biggest lore gaffe was that they unable or unwilling to do anything with the anarchs resurgence and were caught at a point where their entire reason for existence is the other sects shat the bed.

    So while someone who likes anarchs might grumble that revised emphasised Sabbat vs carmarilla the truth is Sabbat vs carmarilla is a FAR more compelling dynamic because the anarchs don't have enough going on to carry an large scale decades long rpg conflict in that context.

    Going forward the writters are going to have to shit or get off the pot. With stable domains and praxis, if they fall back on bikers and malcontents angrily calling the prince a fascist because he barely tolerates a barony you're going to see a drift in players away from the sect as a player choice again. Which I'll admit is fine by me since I prefer the other two sects by a mile and even the ashirra seem more interesting.

        So on top with Anarch and Camarilla vampires:

        In the long run in Vampire, the oldest vampires with high level of Dominate and Presence and other such Disciplines will inevitably end up in charge. It's not much different from the United States, in that very wealthy people inevitably end up in charge. There may be a few exceptions here or there, but in the same way that people with wealth end up in charge politically, among vampires, elder vampires with certain Disciplines inevitably end up in charge.

        Anarchs can't really change that and any system they introduce will end up with the upper echelons being dominated by the oldest and most powerful vampires. At most they can dilute power or spread it around to keep it from being concentrated in a single individual like with Princes. But that can work. For example, in many Anarch territories in the Free States, they have a council of Barons. Each Baron has power over a certain era, like a part of a city, and then they work with other Barons to exert power overt the whole city. So you might have 3-7 Barons controlling a city which, if it were in the Camarilla, would see that city entirely controlled by a single Prince. Basically the vampires which, in a Camarilla city, would end up as the Primogen and working under the Prince, end up as fellow Barons in Anarch territory.

        That system seems like it could function, in the long term, as well as the Camarilla's system. But inevitably the same sort of vampires who would end up as Princes in the Camarilla would still end up as Barons in Anarch territory, it's just that they would have to share power with the kinds of vampires who would end up as Primogen.

                                                    Originally posted by The smiling gun-knight View Post
                                                    and we have to ask why is that.
                                                    I'm sorry to be negative but it's because that's the way they were written.

                                                    I feel like people have forgotten that all the various vampire sects/clans/tribes/etc are fictional and that none of them face any actual evolutionary or societal pressure to make them change, only popularity amongst the fandom. It's all too easy, given the amount of lore, to fall into the trap of actualising(?) the fiction.

                                                    One of the banes of speculative fiction is building a realistic 'world' that appears, to the reader, to be consistent and whole, while being made up of only a few words and hints, and the more words that are created towards building the world the more likely that there will be contradictions and unrealistic constructions within those words.

                                                    As such some of the aspects of the camarilla et al are going to be unrealistic and are there for the purpose of providing an entertaining game experience rather than a realistic socio-political simulation. Those unrealistic elements can be discussed and possibly justified but ultimately they cannot change because the books are written and published and cannot be recalled and replaced.
