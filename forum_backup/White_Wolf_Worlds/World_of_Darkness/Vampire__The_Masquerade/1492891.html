                  I include speculation and conspiracy mongering about the Inconnu (as well as other factions) in game by NPCs. But I have never introduced an actual Inconnu NPC meeting the PCs in game. We've not been given sufficient information for them to make them a really interesting faction in my opinion. Someone who "monitors" a city but mostly refrains from any interference doesn't add a lot to a chronicle.

                  The "obvious" thing to do with them is make them a potential source about Golconda, but there are other factions out there that can serve the same purpose but be more interesting.

                  If I need a mysterious group of elders mucking about, I am more prone to use existing, but seldom used, concepts like the various Gehenna cults mentioned in the Elysium sourcebook, groups like the Children of Osiris, certain coteries/factions name dropped in clanbooks, and the groups I've created myself for my own chronicles.

                  If the Inconnu had a more active plan and tactical bent, I'd be willing to use them more. But as written, I'm just not inspired to do anything with them.

                      I have used the Inconnu in a few different ways.

                      I had a one off where I ran the factions as political analogs. So the Camarilla & Sabbat were liberal and conservative (reader's choice which was which), the anarchs were millenials and younger generations, while the Inconnu were centrists/independents. For the one off I had condensed things down into an underground city design where it was more political fighting than factions owning cities or territories. The clans were changed to better fit the needs of a vampire city, thus the Nosferatu were a clan of engineers and architects, while the Ventrue were the procurers of unmissed mortals to add to the cities cattle pens, etc.

                      I ran a series of games where all vampires were essentially Inconnu. No factions, no Jyhad, just a society of immortals who made their way through unlife as quietly and unnoticed as possible. This was a cross over game so most of the drama came from the other game lines such as Werewolf and Wraith.

                      Lastly in the most unmodified version of their use, the Inconnu were used as a goal for the PCs. They wanted to get out of the eternal war and just exist peacefully, thus the mythical Inconnu seemed like a good thing to focus on. The Inconnu is more realistic than say having the entire coterie attain Golconda.

                                                  I use them with some frequency.

                                                  What the Inconnu offers over any other group is that their core is very, very simple. And importantly, it isn't just vague, but really simple. It can be a lot of things, but it only need to be a bunch of drop-outs from the Jyhad. You can make them into whatever you want, or just take at face value and you're ready to go.

                                                  This simplicity means you can have almost any concept in the form of an Inconnu without breaking anything nor having to delve into more complex explanations than you're going to actually use. A mastermind that works outside the sects, a dupe used by them, any age, clan, Generation or political view, any level of any Path, with whatever connections you want with other characters in the game. It is extremely versatile and without unneeded baggage for the ST to deal with.

                                                  Such versatility is really good to default a character you don't want to be directly involved with the main political conflicts of your game. Be it a mentor that can offer insight to the PCs, someone you like to have mostly as background, a villain to be faced at some point or an ally the characters must struggle to win over. It may be a "placeholder" for a new concept you have for the group or just an option to place and name a truly independent (on the surface) character. You may even have it undefined until later if that fits your needs.

                                                      Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                                                      We've not been given sufficient information for them to make them a really interesting faction in my opinion.
                                                      As I was rereading some sections of Children of the Inquisition for the other thread on Dracula's diablerie of Tabak, I noticed something that I thought was quite relevant to this thread which I remembered.

                                                      In chapter on Dracula (page 18), it provides this bit of information concerning the Inconnu that I cannot recall being mentioned anywhere else:

                                                      Originally posted by Children of the Inquisition
                                                      Eventually Dracula joined the independent sect of respected vampire elders called the Inconnu, who worked ceaselessly to cure vampires of their need for blood. In their quest for greater knowledge, these vampires allied themselves with demons. But then, Dracula always did make a career out of trading one evil for another.
                                                      Does anybody recall similar language elsewhere? Reading it, it changes how I think about the Inconnu.

                                                      Working to cure vampires of their need to blood doesn't exactly sound like Golconda. Although achieving Golconda can be one aspect of that search, it is definitely not the same thing as vampire never needing blood again. So that would greatly expand the Inconnu from a group of elders searching for the secrets of Golconda (which I and I think many other players merely see them as doing), and into a society of elders wanting to exploring the general nature of the Kindred existence and seeking to eliminate negative aspects of their curse. That includes reducing or eliminating their need to drink blood, but also maybe eliminating the danger from sunlight, preventing themselves falling into torpor, removing the need to drink vampire vitae later on as they age, and lots of other things as well. It could also mean information that allows them to return to being a mortal.

                                                      It doesn't mean all Inconnu members want them exact same thing, but it does mean all of them want to better understand their vampiric condition and looking at a way to improve it.

                                                      That transforms the sect from being mostly passive, into a more active one investigating a lot of mysteries, experimenting, contacting dangerous spirits in the search for answers, etc. That might also mean active investigation into the whereabouts of Caine and the Antedeluvians, who could know a lot more about the nature of vampirism, not to destroy them, but to learn from them. So they are doing lots of things as well. That could explain why they have Monitors in cities. Not only does that allow them to observe potential recruits, it is not a passive monitoring. They are looking for clues that could help them and their sect for the specific knowledge that helps them. And lots of things might potentially be interesting to them - Tremere experiments with blood, the activities of the Salubri, Noddist scholars uncovering new information, and lots more.

                                                      I think I will be revising my understanding of the Inconnu for my chronicles based on this. I might even begin including them in my chronicles in some capacity.
