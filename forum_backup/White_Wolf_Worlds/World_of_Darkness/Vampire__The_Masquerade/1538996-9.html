                        IDK how the thread went into crossover, but I have some ideas I wanted to share:

                        *The "Consensus shapes Reality" angle it's, of course, going to be contentious. However, I don't think it's as central to mages as one would think. Perhaps more than that, what Mage put's into question it's how "normal" it's normal people. The whole concept of Awakened lays on the ground of humans having the "potential" to Awaken. More than to reality being inherently subjective, Mage talks about how "humanity can change the world"

                        The Consensus it's, basically, the collective Will of sleeping humanity shaping the world. However, this doesn't mean that there was no world prior to humanity, that humanity can shape reality doesn't mean that there's no objective reality. And the extent and nature of this shaping it's debatable.

                        For instance, Bygones are chased off-world by Unbelief.

                        It could be that Humanity rejection acts as a sort of magical attack, some couldn't resist. But otherwise they could exist w/o our belief

                        Let's note that Enlightenment Era (overhyped) pradigm shift wasn't the only thing that hapened.
                        Reality itself was becoming more solid causing a diminishment of the amounts of Quintessence that was already felt by Tremere on the DA (making it harder to be a thaumnivore).
                        On crossover you could add stuff like Banality rising as Fae left for Arcadia ...

                        The world became more hostile in several ways, that include a change of belief systems, but arguably goes beyond that.

                        But in any case it's undeniable that belief it's powerful. In fact, on WoD Faith CAN heal. Even in Vampire, if I remember correctly, it's one of the things Faith can do. It's just that you need a lot of it

                        *Now, from the perspective of Vampire: Vampire never tells you that the curse it's the only source of magic. On the contrary, it's evident that humans have inherent potential for mystical power. Faith it's dangerous. Sorcerers exist. A normal person you killed could transform into a ghost and pursue you with their ghost magic. And whatever it's going on in the Ebony Kingdom ... The idea that humanity has inherent supernatural potential it's not something that should be too shocking to the Occult saavy vampire

                        Ultimately, the Vampire sees humans as victims. So it's natural they won't give a squat about some inherent power to influence the world humans may have, as long as it is too subtle, or too rare. Most just wouldn't care, even if they knew.

                        But it could be fun if some Elders, taking in count their long lives and subtle social influence, had chosen to cultivate the inherent occult potential of humanity as a long term project, discovered the Consensus (probably by another name, and with other implications) and started to manipulate it to their advantage.

                        For instance: perhaps some of those Elders that like to impersonate a God could be doing so in order to create a worship that benefits them supernaturaly (rather than just for the money and followers). Their abodes are Reality Zones where their word determines what's Vulgar and what's not. Take it up a notch and it could allow them to develop a form of Faith that doesn't hurt them, it could justify keeping some kind of Bygones w/o it being too taxing on blood to sustain, or justify some other forms of Static Magic miracles happening.

                        Moreover, while Mages tend to see themselves as shaping human belief to benefit them, if you see things in the really long term, the oppsite it's also true. Mages are shapped by human belief, on the grounds that nearly every Focus/Paradigm comes from human beliefs. Influence the formation of human belief systems, then see how people naturally Awaken to these. Create your own mages. Have your own Tradition even, True Black Hand style, and benefit from Dynamic Magick more directly. If DA mage it's right about the formation of Arete, one could even shape magick itself with belief, influencing the creation of new systems of magick.

                        Perhaps "belief" doesn't directly shape anything other than "magic itself", the magic of humanity - but then, as humanity applies said power, that could be enough to shape the world up to some extent.

                                I'd like to add, within WoD vampires, werewolves, fairies and demons don’t just exist, they do so in a manner that isn’t informed by human belief. Their culture, society, powers, strengths and weaknesses, while well known and expected by players, are in many ways counterintuitive to WoD’s humanity.

                                That’s also true for many supernatural entities and phenomena they know about. Spirits exist in many shapes and sizes, and no, they’re not all manifestations of human belief. In fact, most of them seem to not give a fuck to what humans believe, including godheads and other spirits that are known by humans in some form.

                                Chimeras are particularly interesting: even being born from human minds, they are birthed by imagination, not belief, and would find a creative scientist quite a good company, while a faithful zealot that actually believes in fairies could be too Banal to be around.

                                So the Consensus has factual, demonstrable limits on what it can create or destroy about the world. The history of Paradox also isn’t proof of anything: the very fact that Paradox only started to exist at one point in time, and the inconsistency in the forms it took, points more to questions than to answers. Why didn't it exist before? Why did it only affect botches early on? Why it doesn’t affect any other supernatural phenomena other than Awakened Magic? Why did a single, particular battle have so much effect on turning the Scourge into modern Paradox? And if it comes from the Consensus, why are its forms more informed by the beliefs of the very Mage causing it, frequently making things even weirder?

                                Even what if scenarios are poor evidence. Be they just thought experiments or umbral realms of their own, they’re another reality to begin with, and as such it is hard to prove their distinctions come from the Consensus being true, or simply from how the Umbra and its realms work. The What If realm may very well correspond to another “consensus”, but internally that be just due to its own natural laws. Or it may be defined by a Consensual Reality without that really being proof that “baseline” reality is the same.

                                One thing is certain, though: whatever reality exists behind the Consensus, it isn’t necessarily our science being true or anything like that. While it probably contains common sense rules (things fall unless uplift, things don’t change shape and form spontaneously without cause), the details can be as varied as the imagination of the ST.

                                                            The argument has several implications, because it opens to many different types of stories the group can tell. How the ST chooses to interpret Earthly Foundations and the Consensus tells what can and cannot affect reality and in which way, what are the possible results, and how far from success are the groups involved.

                                                            We’re literally talking about the core of the Ascension War, people ‘purely academicly’ kill each other because of this.

                                                            For practical uses, determining how much the Consensus is actually shaped by humanity, and how much it shapes reality, is relevant for any game that dares to explore the setting actually evolving from its starting point. It doesn’t even need to be the focus of the game, simply the background being that the status quo somehow changed.
