                To elaborate more on what the current approach towards WOD5E looks like, here’s an example Juhana Patterson (the current lead-developer of WOD5E) talked about: This was at Ropecon 2023, a gaming-convention in Finland and so he talked about pitching a book like “Helsinki by Night”.                     02-02-2026, 02:57 PM
                    Originally posted by Baaldam View Post

                    Knightingale was that bit about a “Helsinki by Night” simply a hypothetical for the sake of explaining Paradox' policy on the matter of WoD products or is there some "rejected pitch" for such a city book, on the developer or someone else's part, somewhere? Curious due to a recent interest relatred to my own table.
                    "Helsinki by Night" was the theoretical example. What he explained is that often this is how he finds out about these wishes from Paradox management. He pitches something, it gets rejected, he asks why and that's when he hears about this new policy for what kind of WOD5E-supplements are acceptable. He said that most of his pitches get rejected.

                    But you see that reflected in the output. Crimson Gutter has a sidebar called "Where?" on pg. 8 "This book is set in a floating domain not anchored to any specific city." or take W5's campaign book Scent of Decay that suggests the town it's set in is the fictional Milton somewhere in the midwestern US. But as a counter-example, in the V5 supplement "In Memoriam", any mention of Prague operates under the assumption that the game Bloodhunt is canon. So, as he explained, you can take stuff introduced in videogames and use that to describe a real-world location in these books but the Renegade-writers can't be like "For this section in this book, let me describe what Oslo in Norway is like in the WOD5E-setting.", for example.

                    Is it still like that? Who knows. Obviously, Paradox-management hasn't been consistent. Otherwise, Chicago by Night wouldn't have been greenlit.

                    Another illuminating bit from that presentation that says a lot is when Juhana Patterson talked about pitching "Bloodstained Love", the V5 romance book. He pitched it, it got the okay from management and so he said to them "Oh, did you okay that because of my past work, talking about romance in relation to the NordicLARP-scene?". But the response was more a blank stare and vague response with an undertone of "What the hell are you talking about?". Paradox-management liked the idea of the vampire-romance-book but they didn't really care about Juhana Patterson's background.

                    The way he describes it, the whole thing is less about Paradox-management making demands and just him fumbling his way through the pitching-process while trying to figure out what Paradox-management finds acceptable.

                    That's why my priority/wish for the WOD5E-line has always been to just get it away from Paradox. They just don't seem very competent. I mean, the way he describes it, it's entirely possible that whenever someone out there thought "You know what, the next V5, W5 or H5 supplement should be about this!", it's entirely possible people at Renegade also thought that but Paradox simply rejected their pitch. And as mentioned with the city-book-example, their reasoning for decisions like these isn't exactly stellar.

                            So a bit of a Necro bu Apprently White wolf just announced they working on a VtM related project to be shown at Gencon.



                            White Wolf is back, and we're building something new.

                            Today at Darkness Emergent LA, our team took the stage to share news that we've been sitting on for a while. A new Vampire: The Masquerade project is in active development, in-house, at White Wolf!

                            This panel was about letting fans get a glimpse of what we're working on, even though it's in a very early stage of development. Creative Director Jess Lanzillo, Creative Producer Martyna (Outstar) Zych, and Lead Game Designer Diogo Nogueira showed up in person to tell our fans that the people building this game are here, they know what it means, and they've been paying attention.

                            We’ve been reading your chronicles, your Discord, Reddit, and Tumblr posts, your correspondences, your house rules, and your feedback about what's been lost and what still matters. We’re making sure your voice matters to the future of White Wolf.



                            What we can tell you now:

                            The new project is our love letter to the 30+ years of vampires that have led us here. We’re celebrating the connection between lore and mechanics, rules and storytelling. The design is focused on player agency, collaboration, and making the experience feel personal. The art direction is built around illustration, with a strong commitment to human-made work.

                            We’re also looking at how we want to work with the metaplot in future books. The sense that history is moving and your chronicle exists inside something larger matters to people. It matters to us too.



                            What comes next:

                            Gen Con 2026 is where we open the door. Full presentation about the future of the World of Darkness (tickets here), multiple playtesting opportunities, and a live Actual Play (tickets here) using the playtest material and run by a very familiar face. If today was the introduction, Gen Con is the conversation; and we can't wait to have it with you.

                            Join the Discord, watch the channels, and stay close. Thank you for keeping this world alive.

                            The White Wolf Team

                                            Originally posted by MarkK View Post

                                            Late era V5, for lack of a better term, has gone pretty hard at veering wildly off from early-mid's mission statements and themes. They've increasingly been putting out lore heavier books as is.

                                            It does raise "who is this for" questions and all if they're really going to openly embrace (hah, terrible, unintentional pun) game approaches they put a lot of effort into trying to put to bed. People who were into what V5 was doing (they exist enough to have supported 8 years of this edition) aren't likely to be into anything that wholesale walks away from it.                                             It happens to a lot of franchises. Like with Star Wars, initially they said that they were planning to leave the old lore behind and take the franchise in a new direction to attract a newer audience. But then after the relatively poor reception to the new sequels and setting, Lucas Films has spent the last 9 years of books, comics, video games, TV shows, etc, going back over the same pre-sequel time period and introducing more and more Legends concepts back into Star Wars. They realized that the old fan base was what was driving sales and the new material wasn't attracting a new audience. So they've spent the last decade trying to go back and appeal to the the grognards.

                                            D&D also experienced something similar where 5th edition largely walked back nearly all of the changes that 4th edition introduced because WotC/Hasbro realized that their traditional fanbase was where the money was at, and newer fans (who 4th edition was aimed at) didn't purchase enough to make up for the loss of the more traditional fans who stopped purchasing products because they were unhappy with the direction the game went.

                                            Vampire is going through a similar readjustment right now where they're attempting to realign V5 to be closer to the traditional VtM and bringing in more lore, world building (and I expect to start seeing some meta plot) in order to try and appeal to some of the old fanbase.
