    Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
    I think a lot of that information should have been in the Player's Guides or ST's Guides instead of the additional clans, bloodlines, and other add-ons to the games.
    I think my ideal Players Guide template - with the idea that this would've been released early in the line - would have been:
    1. Mind Body & Soul, an over view of the psychology, physiology, and certain metaphysics of the game's characters. In the case of Vampire, this would be the place to answer all the questions about how vampire bodies work, trying to pass as human, how their emotions work, how the embrace impacts things like creativity, sensations, or emotions, general issues of alienation from mortals, some of the dehumanization vampires undergo and why/how that happens, can Necromancy effect destroyed vampires, etc. Also discussion of Humanity at its various levels, more about the Beast and how it works, things about Frenzy, Willpower as a reflection of personality, and anything else that might fall under the three general headings. All in all, aimed to give players a much better understanding of just what it is they are playing on a physical and mental level.
    2. Traits, an overview of Attributes, Abilities and Backgrounds, including what each of them means and represents, ways to interpret them, thinking about what each one means to the character and ties into their story/personality, discussion of specialties and what probably should or shouldn't count as one, and touching on what it means to have certain traits above five (I would probably save full rules for that for a future book about Elders, with this just giving basic guidelines), as well as probably optional character creation rules (doing Attributes and/or Abilities as just pools of 15/27 for example), and discussion of creating new Abilities when necessary (as opposed to a bunch of new ones that are mostly just hyper specialized versions of existing Abilities).
    3. Splats, in this case the Clans, giving an expansion on the two page spreads from the core book, probably four pages each, talking a little about their history, internal organization and factions (be it Malkavian mystery cults, Ravnos Jati, Tremere conspiracies, Toreador salons, Brujah political philosophies, etc.), certain Strains within each clan (I use Strain to differentiate between small-b bloodlines like Gangrel mariners vs big-B Bloodlines like Gargoyles or Salubri.), maybe a famous member or two, and whatever else might fit. I'd also like to see each one have a basic-but-interesting premade character template.
    4. Powers, in the case of Vampires, a look at the Disciplines, maybe with a few alternate powers for certain levels of a few of them, as well as the idea of Devotions/alloyed powers, where when a character has X level in two or more Disciplines, it's possible to learn new powers that build off of both effects (like say, something where if you have Auspex 2 or 3 and Obfuscate 3, it's possible to learn some sort of power where you appear as exactly who a person wants to or expects to see), and as with Traits, general guidelines for Disciplines above five dots, again just giving a basic idea rather than a bunch of new specific powers for each level. And, also some more stuff about Thaumaturgy and Necromancy, including the idea of types of Blood Magic outside of those two powers.
    5. Group Dynamics, which in Vampire's case is about the Coterie, with more about how and why they form, different types of them, what they do, how they function in Kindred society and its courts, handling inter-coterie conflict, thinking about the Coterie as a PC in its own right, and rules for Coterie members pooling their resources in order to create a joint Domain, including shared Backgrounds. This would also talk a little bit about characters thinking about their overall place in a city/domain, in a region, and even in the Camarilla (or other group) as a whole.
    6. Secondary Splats, ie Bloodlines. (I'm operating on the idea that the core book would've included all 13 clans.) I'd probably keep this kind of short. Just two page write ups for each, and rules for whatever their special Discipline is, although it might be worth it to have an extra page to spend time talking about how they fit in Kindred society and challenges of playing them. In my ideal world, this would be the two fallen, the Salubri and Cappadocians (with the idea that the Samedi are one of several surviving factions, including ones in Africa and the Middle East), two created, the Gargoyles and the Kiasyd (who'd be separate from the Sabbat), and two mysteries no one can explain the origins of, the Daughters of Cacophony (who may be harbingers of the end times) and the Tlacique (who are potentially a hint that there are Kindred lines in North American totally unconnected to the 13 Clans).
    7. Auxillery Character, meaning mortals and Ghouls, with character creation guidelines, and maybe a few paranormal abilities.
    8. Gear, a short appendix of character appropriate weapons, vehicles, clothing, services, and the like, including how much they cost or require in resources and how hard they may be to acquire under certain circumstances. I'd aim more for street level, civilian, and urban elite type stuff rather than military heavy weapons and the like. Maybe throw in a few arcane objects.
    A lot of this would eventually be expanded on in the Clanbooks, an Elders Players Guide, a book about blood magic, a Ghouls book, World of Darkness: Armory, and the like. I'd have probably have done the Bloodlines as two in one books, The Fallen, the Created, and the Mysteries, each one getting a chapter, and then the last part discussing the idea of additional Bloodlines based on said theme. The Fallen's example would be the True Brujah (with a better name; because no one calls the Salubri the True Tremere), the Created's might be the Blood Brothers, Arhimane or Nagaraga, and the Mysteries could be anything. Such is my ideas, anyway.

            Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand (part four)

            Our last two, and, interestingly, both are from the new bloodlines presented in the book.

            (I just want to note that I consider the True Brujah to have one of the worst names in the entire Vampire line. If they actually are what they claim to be, it's the equivalent of the Salubri being called the "True Tremere" or the Cappadocians being called the "True Giovanni". I think the only thing even comparable to this in terms of lack of effort was WoD:Gypsies having two of the "families of power" being called Ravnos and Lupine.)

            The Seller of Secrets (True Brujah)
            This one is somewhat interesting. He's a bit of a crossover character, being a former member of the Arcanum. Normally, I'd find that a little off putting, but in this case it works OK and seems to tie into his overall concept pretty well. (I'm also a little bit more forgiving of the idea of embraced witch-hunters than I am of sorcerers, kinfolk, and what have you.) He's a Southerner educated at Harvard, which makes him akin to Woodrow Willson. However, the character image makes me think of HG Wells. As both were of a technocratic bent (I mean that in the real world sense rather than the Mage one), it makes me think that such a take might've been a more interesting concept for a True Brujah character, effectively making it a total antithesis of the Brujah stereotype. But I digress. He joined the Arcanum and became something of an expert on vampires, including legends and lore about the Antediluvians. Eventually he traded his findings to a member of the Black Hand in exchange for the embrace. Given how much Noddist vampires sometimes struggle for and treasure the lore they collect, it feels a little odd that a mortal might actually collect such impressive and unique lore as to make elder vampires take notice. Maybe they were just impressed with the fact that he'd been able to learn anything at all. Now he continues on as he did in life, collecting knowledge and lore and trading it to other vampires in exchange for favors. He's Mental primary with a 4/5/4 split. Social is secondary, and he has both Manipulation and Appearance at four. Really, I'd expect a character like this to be kind of unassuming, rather than super good looking. Maybe it's the "bald is sexy" thing that Patrick Steward had going on for him. None of his abilities top three, including Investigate, Linguistics, Occult and Science. He has no Kindred Lore skill either, which feels lacking for this sort of concept. He does have Athletics, Streetwise, and Etiquette at three though. (I'm kind of imagining him being on Harvard's rowing team and still doing it as a hobby in the middle of the night on the Charles River, which amuses me.) Disciplines are threes in Auspex, Potence, and Presence, two in Temporis, and ones in Animalism, Dominate, and Obfuscate. Backgrounds include Age, Contacts, Generation, Resources, Retainers, Secret Identity, and Status, mostly at two. His Humanity and Willpower are both five. The Willpower kind of sticks out in this book, where most everyone else has it from seven to nine. Generally, this isn't a bad character. I think his major weakness is that he's very similar in concept to the Antiquarian at the start of the set, and of the two the Antiquarian is the more interesting. I also don't think he's the best representation of his Bloodline. He really could have been just about any clan, and it wouldn't have made much of a difference. So as a True Brujah, he's not that interesting. But as a generic elder template, he's OK.

            The Shadow Crusader (Old Clan Tzimisce)
            And in a total 180 from the previous character, this one's concept is entirely based on the bloodline's raison d'être. She was the daughter of a 15th century blacksmith and had a Tzimisce lord fall in love with her and take her for his bride. Then the Tremere and/or Sabbat came and killed him and his vampiric family. A servant saved her and took her to a creepy Tzimisce elder who embraced her after telling her everything was the fault of the Souleaters. (I'm a little amused that this write up leaves a little bit of the impression that said elder was a raving paranoid and this entire Souleater thing is some sort of shared delusion.) So now she hunts Souleaters and their pawns (especially the Sabbat). And if anyone questions this crusade, then maybe they're one of Them too. I actually kind of like the back story here. It has a nice fairy-tail that went off the rails quality to it. In both character image and equipment list, this is a total 90s edge lord. Black clothes, black trench coat, black sunglasses, etc. All she's missing is a shotgun with Dragon's Breath rounds. Physical is primary, with a 4/5/4 split, and Mental secondary with 4/3/3. I'm a little surprised it isn't 4/2/4. Melee is maxed out at five dots, with Brawl and Firearms at four. Dodge is only three, as are Alertness, Athletics, Leadership, Stealth, and Occult. Disciplines include Auspex at four, Celerity at three, and Animalism, Dominate, Fortitude, and Obfuscate at two each. Backgrounds include Age and Generation at three, Contacts at two and Allies at one. Given her background and the Leadership ability, I'd have thought this would've been a good candidate for the Military Force background. Humanity is six and Willpower is nine. By and large, this is another KillNinja. But at least it's a KillNinja with a nice backstory. I kind of wish it had kept a little more of the broken fairly tail aesthetic, and made her feel a little more "noble" in dress/look instead of the "clothing by Highlander" thing. So it's OK, but not the best.

            So, that's the Black Hand. As I said, I'm not really a huge fan of the book. There are a number of interesting ideas and stuff that could have been better developed into potentially cool stuff. And that includes this collection of templates. At least four, if not five, of the characters are KillNinja, essentially designed for combat with anything else being an afterthought. However, the Malkavian and the Tzimisce both have pretty good back stories, and the Nagaraja is at least amusing enough in his overall concept that I can kind of overlook it. The Nosferatu has enough of an overall not-KillNinja concept that I can largely forgive his traits being designed as one. The Assamite on the other hand, I really dislike on a number of levels, and I can very easily see how some people might take it as being actively racist. (And that's not something I tend to say lightly.)
            Of the other five, they in many ways feel like general elder characters as opposed to members of the Black Hand. On the one hand, that's great, as we didn't get any templates in the Elysium book to go with its new elder creation rules, so it's nice to see something like that. One the other hand, it does leave a little bit of the impression that the Black Hand are just elders, with out a lot that makes them stand out besides the constant "we're the secret heroes" theme. (I kind of wish there'd been at least one who was clearly a "hero" in the same way, say, Stalin was, proclaiming himself the great champion of the masses while brutally slaughtering them by the millions.) I really do like the Government Leader and Antiquarian though. The Corporate Conspirator and Seller of Secrets are OK, but a little cookie cutter, especially when compared to the other two. The Former Chatterling is the one of the few that seems to be based specifically on the Black Hand and its overall culture and structure. Even if I do have some quibbles about it overall, that's certainly a plus in its favor.
            I really would've liked to see one or more templates that dealt with the cult and its takes on blood magic, as well as their whole interaction with the Underworld angle. Also maybe one fixated on Antediluvian worship who isn't a terrible stereotype from a clan already totally based around that idea. Maybe one or two based on the new Revenant linages introduced in the book. And more examples from the Eastern Hand would've been nice.

            So, that's the end of this section. Next will be Clanbook: Assamite. However, as I started back to work this week, these entries are likely to start coming at longer intervals. However, I am determined to at least finish the original run of clanbooks. At that point, if there is still interest, I will probably start examining the early Dark Ages books. (And if I actually do have a copy of Alien Hunger, I'll try to do those as well.)

                                            Clanbook: Assamite (part one)

                                            So, the 2nd ed Assamite's had a number of ... issues. They were, essentially, the medieval Assassins cult turned into a vampire clan. The clanbook doubled down on this, basing their entire clan structure on said cult. In addition, they were all male, and all Middle Eastern or Indian. At least there were until, as this book claims, the 18th century when they started allowing women, and the 1900s when they started embracing white people. This book also introduces the Sorcerers and Scholars, but they're mostly just footnotes to the whole assassin thing. Every clan member follows the Path of Blood. And they don't interact with non-Assamites except on occasions when they pretend to be from another clan. This made it sometimes difficult to incorporate them into a chronicle that wasn't entirely Assamite focuses.
                                            (Ironically, the first edition Players Guide write up had them being about 50/50 gender wise.)
                                            This book was written by Graeme Davis, who I primarily recognize as a GURPS writer, especially on their books about the Middle Ages and Vikings, among other things. The interior art is, save for the chapter opening splash pages, entirely scans of real guns and melee weapons. The splash page and template art is by Robert Macneill, whose work is OK, but lacks some of the character and detail of Timbrook's. (This change in artwork from the Camarilla clanbooks has the probably unintentional effect of further "othering" the Assamites, which is unfortunate.)

                                            The Playboy
                                            This is one of those "pretends to be from another clan" characters I mentioned above. In this case, he was born in some third world slum, and worked his way up from petty thief to self educated wealthy gambler who was able to pass himself off as a man of class and breeding. A long rivalry with an older gentleman over, of all things, backgammon led to his being selected for the clan. Now he continues as before, just adding another layer or two to his personal masquerade. He's Mental primary and Social secondary, which I kind of would've expected to be the other way around, but it makes sense this way as well. Though his Socials are 3/2/3, and I'd have figured him for a higher Manipulation. His abilities also seem a little lacking. He's got a three in Alertness, and everything else is at one or two dots. This includes just two in Subterfuge, Stealth, and Investigation, as well as Gambling, and ones in Acting and Etiquette. (He's got a lot of secondary abilities, contributing to this skill creep problem. In addition to Gambling, he's got single dots in Bribery, Carousing, Masquerade, and Seduction. Once again, I think that if you're going to get a secondary ability, it should be something an existing one doesn't already cover and you should really commit to it. I think if you got rid of everything but Gambling and raised that to a 3 or 4, it'd make a stronger character.) His Disciplines are one dot of each, plus an additional one in Quietus. I'm not sure why this character needs that second dot of Quietus. I think he'd work just fine with one each or having a 2/1 or 1/2 with Celerity and Obfuscate. And those seven points could've been better used elsewhere. Backgrounds include four in Resources, three in Generation, and one each in Fame and Status. (I've no idea if this is clan status or Camarilla/city status.) His Path rating is six and his Willpower is four. (He has the lowest WP of any template in the book.) I actually like this character. He's got a pretty cool backstory and feels like he'd fit in easily with any regular Vampire chronicle. He totally plays against the clan stereotype while still being a form of predator. I could even see this character fitting in just fine with the revised version of the clan. I'd definitely change him over to Humanity though.

                                            The Holy Warrior
                                            So ... we've got an image of what looks like an extra from Lawrence of Arabia. The full press Saudi desert sheik with sword. And his backstory is basically the textbook 1980s Afghanistan/Kurd/etc. oppressed rebel. And at some point he was taken to the clan strong hold for more training and eventual embrace. And now he's back to doing the same as before. Roleplaying tips include mention that anyone who disagrees with his religious beliefs are just wrong and there's no point in discussion. As far as traits go, he's pretty much a KillNinja. Physicals primary with the four in Dexterity; Mental secondary with the two in Intelligence. Firearms at four dots;Melee, Brawl and Dodge at three (as is Survival). He's also got two dots in Demolition. Everything else is at a one, save Intimidation at two. Disciplines are again the one each with an extra dot, this time in Celerity. Backgrounds include three dots in Allies (Armed Group), one dot in Contacts (Black Market) and two dots in Generation. I will give credit for the added detail to the backgrounds. It's a nice touch that I wish more templates had. Path is five, Willpower is six. While I certainly don't have as strong a dislike for this character as I have had for, say, the Malkavian Freak, I don't really like it that much either. It is as if someone looked at the clan's ethnic stereotype and said, "make that a character". I think it would be possible to do more with this character if a little more thought had been put in to it and the idea had been advanced a little more. Does his mortal faith clash in anyway with his clan indoctrination and the Path of Blood? Or has the Path of Blood completely consumed his mortal beliefs and now he's similar to the Jyhad Fanatic from the Black Hand book, waging a "holy war" for only Haquim? I think those sort of things might've made for a somewhat more interesting character.

                                            The Psycho Killer
                                            OK then. This guy had a lifelong obsessive fascination with death, especially the moment of transition from living to dead. He quickly learned to keep this a secret - you know, as torturing and killing small animals is frowned upon in most places - and turned into the quiet, kind of creepy teenage loner, and once an adult became a serial killer. Eventually one of his would be victims turned out to be an Assamite who'd been watching him. So he got taken to the clan stronghold and embraced. And now he continues to kill people. He's Mental primary and Physical secondary. With the exception of three dots in Computer and Forensics, he has no skills above two in anything. He has two dots in First Aid, which is a bit redundant as he has two dots in Medicine. He also has two dots in Alertness, Stealth, Drive, and Science. Just ones in all the combat abilities, as well as Subterfuge, Streetwise, and Empathy. You'd think he might have a dot in Acting just to pass for normal. Disciplines are again the one each plus one, this time in Obfuscate. These mostly make sense, though I could see an argument for either cutting Celerity or moving that dot to Quietus or Obfuscate. Backgrounds are just three in Generation and two in Resources. Path and Willpower are both seven. This is not a terrible character. I'm just not sure it works very well as an Assamite. I think he'd actually make a pretty good Tzimisce or Giovanni (or Samedi). Maybe even a Ravnos. He's also work pretty well as a Sabbat, especially on the Path of Death and the Soul. (I'm not sure his obsessions fit that well with the Path of Blood's tenets.) His points need to be spent a little better, I think. But I'm not sure why he's in this clan other than the fact that he likes to kill people.

                                                I've always had a bit of mixed feelings on the OG Assamites despite the fact they're every bit as much of a rip off of popular fiction as the Tzimisce.                                                             Clanbook: Assamite (part two)

                                                            And away we go.

                                                            The Professional
                                                            A professional wet-worker, and judging from the art, one at least somewhat inspired by the 1990 French film La Femme Nikita (which was remade as Hong Kong's 1991 Black Cat and America's 1993 Point of No Return, and inspired two TV series). While the write up is gender neutral, the character image makes this our first female Assamite character of the book. Basically ex-military sniper recruited by the CIA only to get burned, fake their death and go into business for themselves. Then recruited by one of the Assamites. They approach killing as just a particular business they happen to have a talent for. Mental is primary, with Perception at four dots. Physical is secondary with threes in Dexterity and Stamina. Alertness and Firearms are at four dots each, and Stealth at three. Everything else is twos or ones. Two of those twos are in the secondary abilities of Gunsmithing and Scan, both of which are fairly redundant, as the character's four in Alertness and two in Investigation should cover anything scan-related, and raising the Repair skill from one dot to two in conjunction with the high Firearms skill would seem to cover actually caring for one's weapons. Besides Firearms and a single dot in Dodge, the character has no combat skills. Disciplines are, again, the one dot each with an extra dot, this time in Quietus. I'm not really sure why. I don't think the extra dot is really needed, but if it was going to be there, Obfuscate feels like a better pick for the character. Backgrounds are two dots in Contacts (Espionage), two dots in Generation, and one dot in Resources. Again, I appreciate the extra detail with the Contacts. Path rating is seven, Willpower is eight. This isn't a terrible character, but it's not that great either. It's one of those taciturn lone-wolf types that are popular in certain circles, and is a bit of a one-trick pony. The character can shoot really well and ... that's about it. A lot of the skills you'd expect for a former soldier or former CIA agent - hand to hand combat, survival skills, subterfuge, pretending to be someone you aren't - are missing. As is much of a personality. They have no long term goals or aspirations, and apparently never did. It's less of a person than a tool that walks around on its own and makes the occasional facial expression. Maybe that works as a member of a giant murder cult, but feels a little lacking as a Vampire protagonist.

                                                            The Sportsman
                                                            A long time Safari guide from Southern Africa. (It says he stowed away on a boat and came there at 15; I've no idea why as there've been plenty of white people being born in Southern Africa for well over a century or two at this point. And yes, the character image is of a white guy in the classic southern African bush ranger outfit.) He'd apparently been doing this for a fairly long time, and in the era of modern poaching decided to start hunting poachers. Which apparently drew the attention of an Assamite recruiter. Now he hunts even more dangerous game. Physicals are Primary, with Dexterity at four, and Mental at secondary. He's got threes in Alertness and Firearms. Everything else is ones or twos, including two in Survival, which feels too low for this character. He's also got no less than four secondary abilities, including a one in First Aid (why not just get a single dot of Medicine, which does the same thing?), and twos in Hunting and Tracking (which are redundant with Survival and in this case are actually taking away from points that could've raised that skill to four or even five), and that damn Scan ability again. Disciplines are, again, the one each plus one, again in Quietus. This doesn't bother me as much with this character. I can see a reason for him to want to learn all three abilities, and learning some of the more martial aspects of Quietus seems to fit. Backgrounds are three in Generation and two in Resources. Given his backstory, I'd have figured him for at least one dot of Fame. Probably some Contacts or Allies as well. Path and Willpower are both six. As a concept, I really like this character a lot. It takes the "Assamites as hunters" schtick in an interesting and offbeat direction fairly far from the ethnic stereotype. I just wish his stats were better, because I'm pretty sure that the Gangrel Great White Hunter would eat him for lunch.

                                                            The Mercenary
                                                            Basically a life long solider, (probably of the Silent or Babyboomer generation from the sound of things) who semi-retired into a life of training soldiers in Third World nations and taking on private military contracts in the days before that was really a well known thing. Eventually one contract led to an Assamite stronghold. And now apparently he's one of them. Basically the stereotypical loud and larger than life type up until the shooting starts and training takes over. Physical is primary (slightly surprisingly with Stamina getting the four dots instead of Dexterity) and Mental secondary (naturally Intelligence is two). Four dots in Alertness and Firearms, and three in Melee and Linguistics (which is nice to see recognized as a useful skill for this type of character, though I can't help but feel that it may be because the writer couldn't think of anywhere else to put those dots; not that a mercenary might need things like Medicine, Finance or Bureaucracy). He has one dot in that Scan skill again. Also one in Demolitions and two in Heavy Weapons. Strangely, no Military Science. Disciplines follow the same drill, this time with the extra dot in Obfuscate. Backgrounds are three in Contacts (no specifications this time) and one each in Generation and Resources. Path is six, Willpower is eight. I'm not sure how I feel about the character. As a concept for an Assamite, it sounds ok. But there's something missing here that I can't quite put my finger on. Maybe it just feels a little phoned in, like it was included just for the sake of having a soldier character. Which is a shame, as I think there's a lot you could do with the idea of an old soldier past his prime given a new lease as a vampire.

                                                            That's all for this entry. Next will be the final four.
