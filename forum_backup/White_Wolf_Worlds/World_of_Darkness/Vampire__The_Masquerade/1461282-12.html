                            I will say that what this book does positively can be summarized as bullet points:

                            1. The fact is that the Sabbat have been absent from the gameline for the better part of a few years and this has been a massive loss to the setting. They're an integral part of the setting like the Camarilla and Anarchs. Their return is an automatic win and I'm glad we've finally got them back to be used.

                            2. The fact that Paths are once more canonized is something that manages to at least fill the hole that certain players had a serious issue with. While Humanity in V5 is far more flexible, some players flat out have no ability to deal with it and the Touchstone rules preclude certain archetypes. Diablerists and mass Embraces for example.

                            3. I do like the write-ups for the Paths that we've gotten and the larger focus on them that the Sabbat has given them before. One of the failures of previous Editions is that it doesn't give nearly as much insight into how Paths interact as it does here.

                            4. The Sabbat now have a far more distinctive identity from the Anachs and the Camarilla with the "Dark Reflections" thing having always made it a bit questionable what the real difference was. It always was somewhere in the Middle just meaner and that made it seem like the sect wasn't as unique as it could be.

                            5. I really am intrigued by the "Clan is meaningless" matter that is a rejection of the Camarilla's super-focus on lineage and their Clan cultures. This was always a tendency and seeing that the Tzimisce and Lasombra did NOT destroy their Antediluvians, it's understandable why it would no longer be as important. Certainly Antitribu no longer is a joke for cannon fodder of the Tzimisce and Lasombra.

                            6. A lot of light retcons that fix what had been problems of the Edition so far like the Gehenna War going off to the Middle East and other matters. The Sabbat now look like they weren't all jumping down the gullets of the Ancients but inflicting serious damage.

                            7. I like the depiction of the Sabbat as simultaneously far more savage but far more united. This is not a Sabbat on the verge of Civil War but united under one cause and Blood Bound to one another properly.

                            8. All the implied defections to the Anarchs and Camarilla making the two sects more Sabbat-like now makes more sense with the mainstream's Sabbat's change. It means that there's a larger Sabbat story with the Church of Caine, Harbingers of Skulls, and other groups.

                            9. Nicely gross and evil occultism with the Disciplines and "mass Embrace of zombie Thin Bloods" stuff.

                            10. Vykos and Lucita are still there.
