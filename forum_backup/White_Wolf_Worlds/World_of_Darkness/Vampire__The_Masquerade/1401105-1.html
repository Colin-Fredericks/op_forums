            We've seen lots of By Night books that explore vast cities with demi-god rulers. I'd like to see a compilation book that explores cities which are smaller or more isolated than most domains.

            How do social and political dynamics change at smaller scales? How do vampires avoid lupines and hunters? How do they monitor media for masquerade breaches with limited resources? How does a city remain stable when the ruling vampires are lower-powered, possibly even book standard, vampires? What happens to clan and sect identity in such an incestuous city? What happens when it is not just a small city, but a small nation? Ordinarily, a local Prince might have influence over the mayor, but in Lichtenstein or Latvia the Prince might reasonably have influence at the UN or NATO.

            So, some cities I'd like to propose:
            • Azores by Night: An archipelago of islands, and a state of Portugal situated in the middle of the Atlantic. Despite a population of 250k, it serves as a vital stopover point for both air and sea traffic. A key location for kindred traveling between Europe and the Americas while avoiding daytime flights. Do local kindred fight to maintain strict neutrality or have the islands been seized by one of the sects?
            • Nur-Sultan by Night: A planned city which was made the capital of Kazkhstan in the late 90's, when it had only 300k people. It has experienced a population boom, growing to 1.2 million people. This growth led to vast building projects and a futuristic... if somewhat sterile... city-scape.
            • Murmansk by Night: This Russian city (the largest city north of the Arctic Circle) has a population of a half million. It's a port city expected to be on the rise as climate change opens year-round seaways. How do kindred survive the midnight sun of summer and noontime gloom of winter at such latitudes?
            • Western Sahara by Night: This land of conflict hangs on where the desert meets the sea. It has a land area larger than the UK, but a population equivalent to just Cornwall. The Moroccans, Mauritanians, and Spanish all have made claims to this nearly empty land.
            • Belize by Night: This former British colony in Central America is incredibly diverse despite having a population of only 400k. Despite its small size and less than vibrant economy, a kindred who influences its political leaders would have a hand in international affairs, including UN and Commonwealth membership.
