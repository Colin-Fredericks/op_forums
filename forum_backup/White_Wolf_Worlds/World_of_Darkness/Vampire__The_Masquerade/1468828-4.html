    Originally posted by nothing View Post
    We're talking right past each other, like I was afraid, but this - this is my original bone of contention (and the only point I'll be addressing). HOW do Thaumaturgical Paths require blood to work? Show me an example, because it's not mechanical - it's a Willpower roll. There's no Blood Point expenditure,
    I haven't checked second edition yet, but in both V20 and Revised VtM, and even in the original Vampire: the Dark Ages really, using a thaumaturgy path requires spending a blood point.

    V20, p213:

    Each time the character invokes one of the powers
    of a Thaumaturgical path, the thaumaturge’s player
    must spend a blood point and make a Willpower roll
    against a difficulty equal to the power’s level +3.
    VtDA, p164:

    Each time these individual powers are employed, a Blood Point
    must be spent and a Willpower roll must be made against a difficulty
    of the power’s level + 3.
    Same deal in revised, page 178, and at this point I'd pretty much just be copy and pasting essentially identical text from each book.

    So, it is mechanical unless I'm missing something there.

    There are certainly other supplements that go deep into the idea of how blood fuels thaumaturgy on a more mystical or philosophical or whatever level, but just to solely address the idea of the mechanical for the moment, Thaumaturgical Paths working off blood has been true since at least 1996.

    Are you referring to some 1e thing? Or maybe second edition?

    edit: Even in 5th ed, Blood Sorcery looks to generally require rouse checks to use, that edition's replacement for blood points.

                            Even Cruac, which is about as blood happy as you can make something, has plenty of powers that don't involve blood beyond activation. Because the mystic logic behind Cruac is that it uses blood as a conduit for the power of the Beast, and that's what you're really unleashing with its rituals (which is also part of the reason Cruac is especially damaging to Humanity compared to other VtR Disciplines). When a Cruac user uses their magic to let them deal lots of damage with just a touch of their hands, they don't need to start sweating blood, or cut their palm open, or something first. Their blood isn't what does the damage. Their blood is what drew out the wrath and rage of their Beast, and the ritual bound that condenses anger into their hands so powerfully it hurts other people with just contact.

                            For me at least, the "blood magic needs to be bloody," is just putting aesthetics over coherence.

                            If Thaumaturgy works by letting vampires use the magical power in vitae to empower debased forms of mortal hedge magics, then there's no need for extra "bloodiness" even if Thaumaturgy could use some more flavor on the "debased" side of things that can help visualize how vitae empowered magic is tainted by the curse in the blood.

                            Otherwise we're getting very quickly into less "blood sorcery should be about blood" and more into just having a blood manipulation Discipline that doesn't have anything to do with ritualized mystic powers.

                                What I personally see at the core of some complains about Disciplines, magic and bloodlines is a determination to have the game as strict as possible.

                                RPG needs variety. Players and Storytellers alike need tools to explore many concepts and ideas, executions serious and silly, to their hearts content. They have a right and need to explore the themes of the game in unexpected ways.

                                Every RPG works like that. A setting needs coherence, but also need options.

                                Vampire is, you know, about vampires. Every single PC is a vampire or at least vampire-related. The whole game is already single-note in this without a need to make its vampires being single-note themselves.

                                In a crossover game or any game where vampires aren't the single focus it's perfectly ok to have your vampires being all "normal" to the last one. Because you have other things to add variety. But a game about vampires does needs vampires that cover as broad a spectrum of possibilities as possible.

                                Crúac works because it's not the only magic in VtR.

                                And a lot of people are still adamant about "the true folklore of vampires". BS on flames! True folklore of vampires is at best a dead dude fucking his wife in her sleep, or a blackened corpse stumbling around in robes. Vampires are a pop-culture thing, period. Even Dhampirs actually have more legit folklore about them (the children of the dead dude after he fucked his wife).

                                So, please, less purism here. Sparkling vampires are not a blasphemy, nor is Vampire Harry Potter. Weird bloodlines are as ok as it gets. Signature powers are a staple of fiction and do not mean their users are "just their powers". And it wasn't removed from V5, all the signature powers are still there or being re-added, because they're signatures.
