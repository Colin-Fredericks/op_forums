                            Cappadocious was insane. He was so insane the amount of marauders and Malkavians decreased because he was hogging the otherwise freely available around of crazy flowing through the universe.

                            Joking side, Cappadocious was making irrational decisions by the time he planned for the embrace of the Giovanni, and this did not get better as time went on. I also suspect he was manipulated by outside forces, which exploited his weaknesses.

                            I have a personal theory about all of that as well. According to the lore, Cappadocious went into the underworld when he was embraced and lost part of himself in the process. He was always trying to recover this lost part of himself and the experience helps explain why he became obsessed with death, ghosts, and the underworld. My theory is what he lost was his Shadow, or an aspect of his darker self. All wraiths possess a shadow, which try to manipulate them into self destruction. I think Cappadocious lost shadow became Dis Pater, or the mysterious patron of the Giovanni.

                            Edit To Add: The Giovanni supposedly had a plan called "Endless Night." This was almost certainty something they inherited from the original Cappadocians and their founder in particular. This plan involved the destruction of the barrier between the worlds of the living and the worlds of the dead. Letting himself be (mostly) diablerized was a step in this master plan.

                                                    Not fond of Cappadocious on account of us knowing way too much about him. I do have an idea that Cappadocius and Augustus were the two most recent hosts of the soul of the Cappadocian/Giovanni/Hecata ante in clan history. Alternatively he was never the real founder, but a figure head and the actual Antediluvian is working offscreen. Perhaps one of its identities is "Dis Pater" the mysterious wraithly benefactor of the Giovanni. Or their founder really is just a pile of ashes, any of it works for me.

                                                    As for why... I don't imagine this was originally why, because V5's metaplot hadn't even been conceived back then as far as I know, but as of now I'd imagine it was done with the formation of the Hecata in mind. The rise of the Giovanni was important for that. Using successive and careful purges to divide a bunch of aimless, layabout nerds into three bloodlines (loyal to their founders and so conveniently guided by a mysterious ancient) and then converging those bloodlines together into an obscenely rich global apocalypse cult with mastery over life, death and undeath and a stranglehold on vampiric necromancy.

                                                    What step the Hecata is in the plan is unknown, but so far "poison the universe and watch it die" appears to be on the to do list. Perhaps Cappadocius' plan was to kill god, scrap the universe he built and then build his own universe and rule over that as god. Maybe he thinks by destroying the universe, he'll be able to expose god/the soul of the universe and diablerise it.

                                                        Originally posted by Sergeant Brother View Post
                                                        Personally, I much prefer an interpretation where everything goes according to Cappadocius’ plan rather than him being a crazy fool who screwed up.
                                                        The whole problem with his plan in this regard is if you accept his goal to Diablerize God, that is relatively core to his description and development. Cappadocius is one of the Antes that is best defined as character, and he is, indeed, an interesting character. So there's a trade-off to this, but it can surely be done with more or less sacrifice of this character.

                                                        Most people won't take this plan seriously. By myself I find plots at this scale ridiculous and boring, but I also prefer a more mundane destiny to all Antes in my games, so it's a matter of personal preference. If it works for your game, you can take everything at face value on that. If you scrap it as the real plan, then you must define, or at least have a rough idea, of what the real plan could be.

                                                        But even going by the route of Cappadocius being a "crazy fool", it doesn't mean that he ended up being truly Diablerized. He can have any destiny you want to give him, he was still immensely powerful and knowledgeable. Maybe what happened was according to plan regardless of the foolishness of his end goal because it wasn't the plan's finale yet. Maybe it failed, but the Ante survived and then just recouped his losses. Maybe he got Diablerized, but isn't the only one that ended up that way or worse.

                                                        It isn't even the worst blunder people ascribe to an Ante. While it is possible that Lasombra ascended, actually a lot of people think he probably just managed to snuff himself out of existence like an idiot, trying to gobble up something less abstract and more obviously dangerous than God. Or even that it was a no-win situation, the so-called "Ego-Death" where success would make him so diffused in his new state as to be effectively dead as a person, becoming indistinguishable from what the Abyss was before.
