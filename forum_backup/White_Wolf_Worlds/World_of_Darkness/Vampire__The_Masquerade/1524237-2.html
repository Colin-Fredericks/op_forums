    Originally posted by Asmodai View Post

    Allow me to reduce this to a level that may seem simpler to comprehend what we're telling you. Just because you have +10 to Persuasion and rolled a natural 20 to seduce the lovely barmaid means jack shit if she loves her husband and her children enough not to want to go to bed with you. She'll be kind to you and go out of the way to help you, but she is not going to ruin her life or her core relationships for you.

    This is the same thing.
    I think we actually think differently on how the game works. The game has plenty of traits and modifiers to represent people's feelings, convictions and attachments. They're a core element of the game. A barmaid in love with her husband and children may, by ST decision, get bonuses on her rolls to resist seduction. The intensity of the bonuses depends on what the ST feels is appropriate. She has her basic Wits + Selfcontrol (if being seduced is against her long term interests) or Conscience + Selfcontrol (if it is against her values) or whatever other combination of traits the ST feels is suitable to the interaction, plus a bonus for true love or whatever circumstantial bonus is at stake. The seducer has his/her rolls and whatever bonus/malus applies. In VDA20 seduction is done in 3 steps/rolls, so that's what I would use as the basis. If, after her being given quantified advantages based on her feelings and situation, the seducer still wins the rolls, that means to me, if I'm narrating, that he managed to seduce her, even if she's going to regret it later. We're humans and we have many strings that can be pulled, even when our hearts are genuinely into something. The spirit may be strong and the will clear, but maybe the flesh is weak and it just happened that she wasn't getting as much as she needed from her husband when the seducer passed by (low Self-control and maybe low temporary Willpower at the time), or maybe she loves her husband and children but is not too bright (low Wits) and the very attractive seducer was very skilled at confusing her and making her think that nobody would find out and that she would rarely get a chance at a night with such an attractive and charismatic guy, and it won't affect her in the long term anyway since none would know but the two of them. Or any other explanation, which I would prefer to craft on the moment based on how the different rolls go, so I can represent adequately the traits that rolled very well and those that rolled very poorly. I don't see any reason to give the barmaid a straight up immunity to seduction in a game with traits representing all the relevant factors.

    While I have abandoned the idea of the werewolves never converting due to their attachment and real knowledge of Gaia, I would also point out that they have a trait to represent this connection: Gnosis. Even on their own, they're susceptible to the Wyrm if their Rage score is above their Gnosis score and if they roll too many successes for frenzy. Vampires have Convictions and Road ratings, too. When a character has a bonus explicitly tied to rolls to convert people, I would not declare the attempt impossible. I would factor in the victim's traits that quantify their conviction - since they exist already in the character sheet -, give each person the bonuses and maluses and difficulty values and required number of successes that I think fit the situation, and I would let the game and the rolls decide how the Narrative goes. This gives more chances for drama and makes everyone feel the dots on their sheet are not just numbers, but actually represent something that will have dramatic effect and that quantify something true about the characters, including what is possible or impossible for them. I find no narrative value in giving essentially plot armor to characters who don't need it (and already have quantified game armor in such rolls) just because they love their husband or Gaia so much.

They might be stunned temporarily by the terror of how potent said vampire is (though they're get a reflexive roll to sense that it might be more terrifying than it should be for what it is), but there's nothing presented that's going to make them question what they believe. The Wyrm corrupting human forms into ridiculously powerful monsters is a core tenet of their cultural belief that they were shaped by Gaia to be her warriors to fight and slay such things.

                                    What your describing is more like a hunter moving to collect a deer, only to run into a grizzly bear that wants that kill for itself. A grizzly bear is a pants-shittingly horrifying thing to accidentally run into, even with a high powered hunting rifle on you, but hunters know bears exist and bears will try to take kills from humans, so it's not some radical shock to core and fundamental beliefs.

                                    Some Virtue rolls are replaced (in a turned around way) by Rage.
                                    The only place this is really the case is how the two games handle frenzies. Vampires roll to resist frenzy with their Virtues (or guide them for Instinct); with the implication being that once provoked, a frenzy will happen unless overcome. Garou roll to Frenzy, so provocations are not presumed to automatically turn into them without resistance; if a Garou wants to resist Frenzy rather they use Willpower for that.

                                    Garou don't "believe", except in terms of paradigm. They don't need faith in rituals because they see them working regularly and know the spirits that make them work on the other side
                                    Garou do believe as well. One of the truly scariest things about the Garou is that they have faith and facts that reinforce them. They have not only a religion with dogma taken on faith, but also divine beings affirming that they are right to do so. The Garou Nation is a culture of religious fanaticism; even more so in the past. The Black Spirals might put their faith in the Wyrm, but they're just as much a mix of faith and fact as well. In modern nights, there are more dissidents that don't fully believe, but that's still a relative rarity precisely because the few points where the Garou are clearly taking things on faith instead of fact (despite Gaia, Luna, and the Incarnae around) are rather academic compared to more immediate concerns.

                                    But İ still can't find it in myself to make a whole splat immune to a whole social interaction without this immunity being spelt out in their manual.
                                    Garou aren't immune to intimidation. Intimidation is one of the three traditional forms of Garou dominance challenges, and is frequently used to exert authority and hierarchy. Garou are not immune to demoralization, long term psychological tactics, and so on.

                                    What we're saying is that you're using the wrong approach, so it isn't going to have the desired effect. The "immunity," isn't in the rules, because they're not broadly immune. You're just opting for one of the worst possible approaches to all of this.

                                    It's like the Cuban Missile Crisis scene in X-Men: First Class, where Charles is trying to talk Erik out of tossing all the live munitions the US and Soviet warships just fired right back at them and killing all the sailors. Charlies picks pretty much the stupidest phrase possible to a very pissed off Holocaust survivor. "They're just following orders." It's not that Erik is immune to Charles's persuasive abilities (in fact the movie shows a few times that even without his powers Charles can convince Erik of things just fine), but in that case the persuasion completely backfired by being "successful." Erik wants to kill military people that are following orders to murder instead of rejecting those orders as illegal and immoral. Convincing him that he's facing such people is just increasing his motivation to use lethal force.

                                    The Garou aren't immune to intimidation, you're just coming up with things that would intimidate most Garou to do the exact opposite of what you're stated goals are.
