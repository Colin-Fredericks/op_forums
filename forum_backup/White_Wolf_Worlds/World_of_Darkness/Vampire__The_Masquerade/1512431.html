    Do you use magical items/artifacts in your games?

    05-23-2023, 08:37 AM
    Unlike other RPGs including other WoD games, Vampire, with the notable exclusion of bloodlines, isn't heavy on magic items. Hunters Hunted has relics to be used in the context of vampire, but the expected vampire exploitation of such relics hasn't been mentioned despite the great potential of vampires to accumulate such things. Thaumaturges have a handful of permanent items like the vessel of transference or Wards (a fantastic family of spells) Mirror Prisons, and can, if they're willing to invest four levels into a relatively obscure path, fetishes, although vampire fetishes are burdened with absolute bullshit like "Fetishes created by this power are often unreliable and fail at inopportune moments, as the spirits within are understandably displeased with their situation and will take any opportunity to escape or thwart their captors.", and werewolves will apparently frenzy at seeing a vampire fetish regardless of what kind of spirit you've trapped in there, at least in Rites of the Blood. I am very fond of malfean factories and weird stuff like banewhips, which aren't true fetishes, and I can imagine some trade going on in that direction.
    Probably, Vampires could use kinfolk fetishes as they don't require gnosis, and perhaps demon/ghost possessed items could have some power. Vamps probably wouldn't recognized something enchanted by changelings.

    Mages... in theory, there could be great parnerships between vampires and mages. Due to political realities, it's unlikely to blossom into anything positive, but there are some room for repurposements. Vampires look at mages and see cool toys, mages look at vampires and see... ingredients (to be fair, some vampires look at other vampires that way)

    whether it's actually useful items or unknowable McGuffins to sell to the Tremere, how do y'all see things?

      Usually with the Settite magical items made by Akhu ritual, like
      Dreams of Duat: The Setite needs parts of the victim's body. Sealing them in a wax figure, the lector-priest inscribes the victim's name in hieroglyphic script on it. Cursing the target, the lector-priest washes the figurine in warm water made bitter with natron. The victim will have nightmares about Duat in the following night.
      Scorpion Sending: The Lector-Priest sculpts a scorpion out of wax, incorporating small parts of the intended victim's body, like hair, nail clippings, and similar, into it. After writing Set's name on the back of the scorpion in green ink and reciting the tale of Horus' poisoning, the Lector-Priest has to carry the figurine to a place frequented by the victim. When the target comes near the figurine, it will transform into a real scorpion and sting him.
      Severing Sands: The Lector-Priest gathers authentic sand from the Nile bank, washed clean and dried in the sun. Sprinkling the sand with perfumes, natron, and his own vitae, he recites a prayer to the life-giving Nile. When confronted by a supernatural creature, the Setite may blow the sand against them with a prayer to the respective god (Seker for vampires, Anubis for wraiths, Set for demons etc.). Incorporeal creatures are banished back into their home realms, while other supernaturals lose their healing powers.
      Summon Sebau: The Lector-Priest constructs a clay model mixed with vitae of one of the Sebau to command it. If the Lector-Priest wants the Sebau to attack a target, he needs a piece of its body or its True Name.

      And of course, even to use the Path of Duat, the Magician must carry a talismanic gem of black onyx carved with the image of a mummified man - the form of Sokar, the god of Duat. A few minutes before using the path, the magician smears the talisman with a drop of her own blood as an offering to Sokar, and whispers a short prayer. The hypnotic aspects of the Path of Duat mean that all powers operate at a +1 difficulty if the lector-priest does not catch the victim's attention using the darkly glittering talisman on the turn when she initiates the magic.
      - So in a sense, the little amulet of Seker is a magic item

      Aside the Akhu, stuff made with Koldunic rituals also could be considered "magical items/artifacts" in my humble opinion

      In the Spear of Bethel thread we listed a tons of vampiric artifacts, and some of them seen a lot of usage in my games

          I wouldn't say common, but most of my longer running chronicles end up with a magical doodad or two being introduced at some point. Eventually some is going to show up with an enchanted cane or pocketwatch or knife, especially if the Tremere, Followers of Set or other magical focused clan play a big role. That doesn't mean the magical doodad will play a role in the campaign, or even that the players will necessarily know that NPC X has a magic knife, but it'll be there in the background if it becomes relevant.

          On the subject of Fetishes created via Spirit Manipulation, as a house rule I tend to rule that Fetishes created with banes or Wyrm spirits are generally okay with being controlled by a vampire, provided the vampire offers one point of vitae (it doesn't have to be his own) per week as a sort of payment for services. In such a case, the fetish won't break or malfunction, though of course the vampire (and his magical tool) will very much reek of the Wyrm. These tend to be relatively common among the Followers of Set.

              I don't really use them unless something specifically occult is happening or I want to throw the city into chaos by throwing in a powerful magic item and watching everyone kill each other over it. They're rare, and often require an understanding of what they are and how to use them.

              Ritual objects are the most commonly seen. Fetters, "devil sticks", blasphemy shrines, shabti, warding objects, vials powering the Domino of Life, etc. I'd have it that items (even mundane ones) that are used frequently in rituals begin to reduce the difficulty or add bonus die or have other strange side effects.

              I also have a personal favourite category of magic "items" that involves strange looking statues and idols and things that are actually perfectly aware, still powerful and still scheming vampires forced into fancy new shapes (like that Koldun from Rites of the Blood who likes turning his fellow kindred blood sorcerers into explanatory text books that teach you new paradigms). Sometimes they can still use their disciplines, make the right deal and they might use them on your behalf a few times while they're plotting to use you to free and avenge themselves of this humiliation.

              Then there's stuff like holy relics like Buddhist śarīras or the nails from the Crucifixion; usually the opposite of helpful to a vampire, since I'd probably give such objects True Faith ratings (unless we're going for a "cleanse me of the curse" type game), but if you desecrated them in some fashion they could probably pull of some interesting ritual effects.
              Or from vampiric faiths, so something or other used by Set, something from Caine's journeys at the dawn of time, a clipping from Lilith's garden... likely to have interesting benefits and side effects to the holder, and useful in rituals, but I'd probably use them more like plot hooks with visions and omens and such.


              For cross-splat objects, Quintessence, Gnosis, Glamour, Pathos, Qi and Vitae and anything else are exactly the same things in different format, it's simply a matter of knowing how to translate. So fetishes, chimera, wonders, relics/artifacts and so forth are technically usable to the kindred, but they need to have at least a basic understanding of the other splat and a "translation" ability - and possibly an ally or allies from the other side of the equation. You need to be a necromancer to make any use out of WtO's stuff, have a grasp of Chimestry and Ravnos lore for CtD's, etc. Those two in particular aren't of much interest to the other clans anyway, since there's nothing in the Underworld or the Dreaming that would interest the common vampire (as far as they know.)

              I actually made a magical item that allowed one of my necromancers to use some of the arcanoi by himself over on the other side of the Shroud. It involved a hell of a lot of risk, jumping through hoops to find and get the assistance of wraiths with the requisite abilities (some of which are illegal under Stygian law), and the expenditure of a ridiculous amount of time, vitae, willpower and pathos to make... The result only has a limited amount of uses before it needs "recharging," so it's not something used cheaply.
              But it did come in useful. Being able to spot when a wraith is under the control of its shadow, stepping across the shroud and teleporting to another location, time manipulation, divination, spatial distortion, essentially being able to use dominate while ignoring generation, being able to know the deepest desires of those around you (and then erase or edit them to your liking), making things decay, crafting illusions...​

              Prone to hyperbole

                          Magic items and Vampires are always a strange combination.

                          Thanks to an unlimited lifespan a vampire can become insanely wealthy, which means for a magical item to have value it would have to do something that can't be replicated by spending money or using a discipline that the cainite picked up along the way. But the only things a vampire would generally want that can't be replicated by money or disciplines is something that cancels vampiric weaknesses, but that breaks the game. Such as the rings from the Vampire Diaries that protect vampires from sunlight and allow day walking.

                          The Kindred of the East game line of V:tM has quite a few magical items as well as rituals for creating them, so they are not just artifacts from a long-lost civilization. But this tends to not break the game because the KotE line was designed to be a cross over native setting, rather than a stand-alone which can be crossed over. So, a Kuei-jin might be rich in the real world, but the instant they go into the umbra or one of the hells, they need other equipment or resources. Despite being one of the last game lines developed, KotE was more of a pick and mix of other systems. Specifically, the fetish crafting system from Werewolf was used as the basis.

                          So, you could get things like a player who binds a spirit with say 10 soak dice into an amulet, which they can activate for a scene to gain those soak dice, but since the character had rank five Iron Mountain for their Demon power, that means they are now soaking all non-agg damage with 15 soak dice at difficulty 2 and one auto success. Then add actual body armor and a max survivability build with Demon Shintai and yeah the ST will be hard pressed to do meaningful damage.

                          Vampire was originally designed to be street level with characters that wouldn't look out of place with the Lost Boys vampires. Magical items are usually plot items the PCs chase after but never get to keep. Personally I have no issue with using magical items in Vampire games but you have to consider how all the other vampires would behave if magical items were either widely available or if they were rare, that they could cover important weaknesses.

                          As for Mages, magical items, and Vampires... once again that gets tricky because there is a whole other area of ramifications that needs to be considered. Officially there is a specific combination of sphere ratings that would allow a mage to cure vampirism in a character and turn them back into a normal human. But if a Mage with the right sphere ratings can erase the curse of Caine, then theoretically they should be able to enhance it. Why build a magical item for a vampire when you can artificially induce Golconda which unlocks stat & discipline limits and locks down the beast. Even if you don't use the Golconda as a short hand, there are plenty of adjustments that can be made.

                          In the end magical items will always turn a game into a gear check, rather than a character build check or a role play check. Think of your favorite MMO and think of Vampires as max ranked characters but without any gear.

                                  I have a very liberal view on splat exclusivity regarding sorcery, so I would have absolutely no problem with converting the mortal Enchantment path to blood sorcery. Honestly, most of what runs under the blood sorcery label doesn't even make sense as magic that needs vitae to function. IIRC, one of the older books even mentions this as an option and I think there is a Thauma path that lets you create new paths in character.
                                  But even without dedicated path, I think it's not unreasonable to assume someone proficient in rituals could create a few minor items, even if it's just for narrative flavor. A wangateur with an enchanted skull is simply more fun than without one, as is the Tremere with Auspex 1 glasses.

                                  It may be different for Dark Ages games, but I think lesser magical gadgets are mostly absent from Vampire because they can easily create tonal dissonance with the setting. For Werewolves and Mages they are anchored in the culture of the respective splat, while for vampires they are tools for a minority. The utility of many lesser items can also be emulated with modern technology, and that is a lot more easily available if you have money.

                                  It didn't come up for me so far, but I would probably ignore most restrictions on Spirit Thauma fetishes. Vampires forcing spirits into service is too on brand than to spoil it with bad guy demerits.

                                  I think an interesting twist for vampire enchanting could be the use of cursed objects, since it's thematically fitting. Maybe something that resonates with Caine's curse and lets vampires bypass or redirect the effect of hexed objects.

                                          I've never actually used any magical items in the chronicles I have been the ST. I do not object to them in principal, but if I do ever introduce them I would want them to be few in number, appropriate for the genre, and actually drive some kind of plot. I don't want magic items just to be magic items, and would very much want to avoid any inclination or feel that this is something like a D&D scenario.

                                          So they could not be generic or mass produced. Each one would need its own kind of legendary history and lore behind it. They would not actually be all that powerful, though they would be useful in some kind of capacity. I would probably also want them to be legacies of an earlier age when magic was more present in the world, and there are less and less of them every year simply because of attrition (though some may also just stop working because the world is just becoming more inhospitable to them - at least until Gehenna begins). Each historical era has its own sweet spot in terms of how much magic should exist.

                                          There would also be some slight amount of crossover as there should be competition over control of these resources. Introduce a magical item, and it should not just be something that vampires fight over, but would attract the attention of Lupines, Bastet, Mages, Sorcerers, hunters, etc.
