            Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
            Fascism is fun when it's pure fantasy, that's where 90% of its appeal lies; fantasy.

            anyhow, I like my anarchs to be just as bad as the Cam. But V5 seems to go for team anarch.
            No faction in vampire is fascist. Every faction is a variation on feudalism. For most of history, the big conflict was between the version of feudalism where the the low nobles had more collective power than the king and high nobles, and the version of feudalism where the king and high nobles had all the power. For a very long time, "democracy" and "republic" didn't mean what it means today.

            Both were really just words that meant that the low nobles had more collective power than the high nobles and king. Ancient Greece, the Roman Republic, Magna Carta era Britain, early America, and the Confederacy, were all forms of government where the low nobles had more collective power than the high nobles and the top guy. None of them were great places to live for peasants and slaves. All of them were inline with what the "Anarchs" actually represent. Hell, the Anarchs even name their leaders "Barons" as an homage to the Magna Carta Barons.

            Imperial Rome, the various Absolute Monarchies that sprung up in the late Middle Ages, and indeed most Empires with a famous leader, were the form of feudalism where the King(or equivalent) and a few high nobles had all the power, and the low nobles were, depending on the era, poor farmers with the ability to tax a few even poorer farmers, or moderately wealthy people with no real political power. Not a great place to be a peasant or slave. And this is the Camarilla's inspiration.

            Regardless, neither group was "fascist", though the various low-nobles-have-more-collective-power-than-the-king versions has just as much claim to resembling fascism as the king-and-high-nobles-have-all-the-power versions.

                    I've been thinking about what they mean when they say that Paths have to be followed more fanatically to work, and I think they mean that Sabbat will not have any Touchstones at all, of any sort. If you violate one of the Chronicle Tenets, Tenets which will be mono-path-centric, you just eat the stains. Which makes a kind of sense, I'll admit, but is also rather a bland solution.

                    Like a Feral Heart chronicle Tenets might be: "Always be loyal to your Packmates. Do not engage in politics. Do not kill for reasons other than food or survival." and if you violate any of those, you just eat the full stains, since you can never have a human touchstone rooted conviction to mitigate it.

                    It makes sense for characters who avoid the beast purely via fanatical purity to a set of beliefs, but it's rather bland. The price for being on a Path will likely be a throwback to the old version, where you look like you have a Humanity of 3 even if you have a Path rating of 10.

                    It also explains WHY they insist on mono-path packs: because the Tenets are meant to be followed by all players, and the Tenets need to be tailored to a particular Path. The only way you could really create a multi-path tenet is by referencing older editions, like, "avoid all 10 sins of your chosen Path", which would require someone knowing all ten sins from older editions.

                    I actually do have several Paths memorized, and behave as if my characters had "avoid all 10 sins of your chosen Path" as one of my convictions even when it's officially not, but I can see why they would want to avoid making players memorize older editions. You have to REALLY love Paths to my degree to do this.

                    I get it, I really do, but I find it all rather bland. I think I'll keep using Long Road Out Of Hell if I'm right about this. I like my nonhuman touchstones, and I like my Beast Marks, and having it replaced with just "here are three Tenets rooted in one path, you have no touchstones of any sort, and you always look like you've got Humanity 3 even if you have Path rating 10", is sooooo much blander than that.

                                    Yeah. I get where they are likely coming from with the absence of touchstones/convictions. Convictions are supposed to represent past humanity and that's been stomped out of Sabbat crusaders. They've gained the ability to use Path tenets, tenets that are somewhat more forgiving than humanity tenets, but they've lost any ability to mitigate stains via Convictions. They need to hold to their Path Tenets like iron, because they eat stains full in the face when they don't.

                                    To go back to my earlier example about a Feral Heart pack. Let's say you had a pack whose tenets were "Always be loyal to your Packmates. Do not engage in politics. Do not kill for reasons other than food or survival."

                                    Members of that pack can suck humans dry so long as they have at least one point of hunger when they do so. Humans are food, you need to kill them to fully sate your hunger, so drink away. Sounds much easier than Humanity, right? On the other hand, let's say you have ambitions to rise to leadership in the Sabbat. So you engage in a bit of political scheming to do so. On Humanity, you might be able to have a Gorden Gekko type as your Touchstone for the conviction, "power is worth any price" and reduce the stains from engaging in politics. Not for Sabbat. You just eat full stains.

                                    Of course, all this is me doing an awful lot of reading between the lines of what they've been saying. But it does fit with their general design philosophy that rather than do something sophisticated, they do something extremely simple. And "they get Tenets that are a little more forgiving, but completely lose access to the Conviction mechanic" is as simple as it gets.

                                        What I don't get with all this is the whole premise that Sabbat is = evil.

                                        I mean, in Revised edition and V20, it was stated over and over that most Sabbat vampires are not on a Path of Enlightenment.

                                        As I player who began in Revised edition, I loved the Sabbat because it was a more interesting ideological and political structure than the Camarilla. It was like a bizarre fundamentalist warlord army about to turn on itself, a dysfunctional death cult with pseudo-democratic trappings run on a social darwinist platform, with an old testament:y jihadist doomsday sect hidden within. It was so insanely f*ing compelling. Even at pack level, the Sabbat had a much more interesting structure and internal culture than the Camarilla, with their vaulderies, ductuses and pack priests - the Sabbat packs had a clear reason for existing, whereas Camarilla coteries always felt kinda pointless and forced. The Sabbat was designed so that its core structure was a tight-knit yet conflict-ridden group of bizarre and motley player characters. Can we just dwell for a moment on how genius an idea that was?

                                        To just add to the giant smörgåsbord of awesomeness that was the Guide to the Sabbat, it was complemented by Mexico City by Night. The Sabbat's capital was what is basically the best city for Vampire chronicles in the entire contemporary world - Mexico City is both gargantuan, shock full of history and local culture, downtrodden yet vibrant, shabby yet sophisticated, corrupt, crime-ridden, extremely violent, a literally drug war going on in the streets...what an amazing place for Vampire play.

                                        I always imagined that Camarilla elders could be just as monstrous as Sabbat ones, and in a way, were even more monstrous, because they ran the Camarilla, a giant pyramid scheme meant to forever deceive its lower ranking members and rob them of all influence. The Sabbat at least had some elders who truly believed that what they were doing were for the good of all of Vampirekind.

                                        I really like a lot about V5. I love the rules, and several of the changes to the world were brave and clever. But the Revised edition Sabbat was a friggin' masterpiece of worldbuilding. The Sabbat was so dynamic, complex and compelling. I just can't understand why you would ruin something so great, but it increasingly seems to be what V5 has done.

                                            Originally posted by Natsymir View Post
                                            What I don't get with all this is the whole premise that Sabbat is = evil.

                                            I mean, in Revised edition and V20, it was stated over and over that most Sabbat vampires are not on a Path of Enlightenment.
                                            Most of what you talked about I have no answer for. But I can give you a pretty good guess as to this one: they are seeking to strip each sect down to its defining features.

                                            The Camarilla have always been the royal elites, but previously tried to be a big tent that included everyone. Now they are shameless about being a small, exclusive club of royals and those who seek royal favor.

                                            The Sabbat were always controlled by fanatical path-followers, but pretended to be a big tent where you could have humanity so long as you weren't preachy about it and followed orders no matter how brutal. Now they are packs of fanatics each following quasi-religious paths with with utter devotion, accept other packs only as loose allies, and don't accept humanity at all past a brief indoctrination period where you had best learn fast and get with the program or be destroyed.

                                            I think they also want the game to be about small scale survival horror, so big institutions in general were destroyed. You're not part of some big organization. You're a tiny creature with lots of weaknesses who struggles just to survive night to night. You're most likely an Anarch, which now seems to be the default, and you barely know what you are. You have to live extremely quietly, lest the smug, paranoid royals kill you because they deem you a threat to their only slightly less fragile existences, or vampire hunters discover you and kill you very, very casually.

                                            You're discouraged from being Camarilla, but if you insist on playing one, you are still tiny, tiny, tiny. The little bit of wealth and comfort you've managed to accumulate could be wiped out in a moment if the surveillance state peering over everyone's shoulder discovers who you are. You are constantly endangered not only by your own mistakes, but by the mistakes of anarchs who bring surveillance down on your territory with their clumsiness and ignorance. And if you beg them to be quiet and cautious, they act like you are the second coming of Hitler and attack you for it, forcing you to attack them in return.

                                            You're VERY discouraged from playing Sabbat, but if you insist on playing one, you are still tiny, tiny, tiny. You are not allowed to have any human friends, and must adopt a Path of Enlightenment quickly or be executed by your own Sect. The Paths are hard to follow, and since you aren't allowed human touchstones, you eat full stains for the slightest transgression, making the threat of becoming a wight constant and oppressive.

                                            The result is an extremely low-power survival horror game regardless of Sect.
