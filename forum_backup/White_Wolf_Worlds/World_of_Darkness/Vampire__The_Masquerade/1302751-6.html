                What I see on page 240 is part of a shotgunning of haphazard examples left over from days of the distant past - like, literally this is almost verbatim from 1e but tweaked to be about another werewolf (page 87 in case you were curious). Since 1e, W:tA has formalized a very different set of rules involving Intimidation, and while technically there's nothing that says that you have to use any given rule, the authors wrote them into W20, so I'm damn well going to be using them. Blatant displays of force are a bonus likely lesser in effect than using Manipulation + Intimidation or Rage (2e suggested only two dice for tearing a fomor apart, so smashing some stonework is probably not going to rate very highly). Loose bits of early system sketching do not a robust rules system make, and I see no reason to turn back the clock on it. Shit's old, and the ship has sailed; lots of people since have done systems work, and I don't think that just tearing them up for an incredibly self-indulgent attempt to browbeat Garou with Potence is a good reason to continue to coddle those rules.

                Further, what you do not seem to grasp about the warrior culture of the Garou is that, given the existence of Rage, they have elaborate ritual systems put in place to avoid more outbreaks of berserk killing than are already a risk just from having Rage. Mediating these disputes is the life's calling of an entire auspice (the Philodox), and places them in between wild murder machines on a regular basis. Strength is secondary to this task; they need Willpower and social graces to get through some damnably impenetrable hides and skulls so that they can actually put a stop to this shit without escalating it. To that end, Garou society really does not seem to put much value in your ability to do the violence. This ability is already incredibly high in every werewolf; if you want it to mean something to others, you either need to be very good at talking or else have had the spirits and Garou society vouch for you in the form of Renown.
