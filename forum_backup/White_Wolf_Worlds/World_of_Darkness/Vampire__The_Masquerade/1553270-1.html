                                We got a little bit of a preview last year. The tl;dr is that after spending years being a very ambitious publisher that wanted to tackle all sorts of genres, Paradox wants to scale things back and focus on games "they're good at". Here's an excerpt (The Lilja mentioned here is the deputy CEO of Paradox):

                                "It is not in our strategic direction to make this kind of game," Lilja says. "So if Bloodlines 2, God willing, is successful, Bloodlines 3 [will be] done by someone else, on the licence from us. I would say it's the sort of strategic way this would work. So it's still an outlier from what we're supposed to do, we don't know that stuff, so we should probably let other people do it."

                                Paradox has its own small-scale experimental label, Paradox Arc, where it can try different things, but a big RPG doesn't remotely fit that. It would have to be something "very different", says Lilja, like a CRPG, but even that's a "big investment", and nowadays expectations are higher than ever thanks to Baldur's Gate 3. So "regardless of outcome," he says, Bloodlines is a "dead end".
                                I'm not sure that this will really solve all the problems of Paradox because this doesn't really explain why over the course of the last couple of years Paradox-published games have been failures more often than not. Them being highly ambitious probably was one aspect but there's more to it than that, I'm sure. And so if there's not enough self-reflection on why their publishing-ambitions crashed and burned, I'm not sure a smaller budget alone will fix all the issues.

                                Then, of course, you had the resurrection of White Wolf recently and the goal to move everything in-house regarding the WOD (at some point). But we're only at the starting-point of that process, so who knows how this will go...

                                I just hope that considering what Paradox was willing to say about Bloodlines 2 last year, they're not gonna end up blaming the Chinese Room if the game "underperforms" or "disappoints" or whatever phrase they want to use.

                                                        With the Day 1 DLC, I think it's an unwinnable scenario for Paradox/Chinese Room.

                                                        I see two options:

                                                        Option one: It's not a big deal at all. Once the game comes out, we find out that Clan actually doesn't have that big of an impact on the story. Sure, Phyre will say "I'm from Clan Tremere" or "I'm from Clan Brujah". You get the occasional remark from other characters but that's about it. The Trainers are there in the hub-world where you can go and buy abilities with XP but outside of that, those characters never turn up in the mainstory, so the absence of the Toreador and Lasombra Trainer NPCs isn't that big of a deal. And when it comes to the costumes, sure you get some extra ones but most of the time you're in first person anyway, so it isn't that big of a deal as well. The only thing you might miss are the abilities.

                                                        But if that's how it is, 30 extra bucks for this seems a bit overpriced.

                                                        Option two: It actually does make a meaningful difference what Clan you're playing. I'm just inventing an example here: There's a scene where a Toreador holds someone hostage and you need to save the hostage. You have some sort of dialogue-tree-puzzle with a countdown-clock where you have to pick the right thing to say to save the hostage. This always plays out the same - except when you've picked Clan Toreador at the beginning. Then you can immediately resolve the situation and it's only in this scenario where you don't end up killing the Toreador to save the hostage. And so you get extra information from that character as well. And the Trainer characters are integrated into the main-story and so the presence of the Lasombra and the Toreador Trainer characters does make a difference and they give you extra insight/context, for example, on what's going on in the story.

                                                            There's sorta of a middle ground option (but it's the worst of both) where the impact is highly subjective. We've been told that things like outfits actually do have mechanical impact on the game... but we don't know how much.

                                                            Like, if feeding has a 10% chance of dropping XP (I forgot the V5 term and I don't remember the video game previews even using it when you feed off of the right blood resonance), and the right outfit can bump that up to 15%.

                                                            Can I save scum and reroll feedings as many times as I want? Do combat feedings (which seem very common) count or just hub world feedings? Esp. if it's just hub world feedings... how many of those can I do? How these are played out in practice could make the 5% nothing, something, or a huge deal. And that's going to be subjective to different play styles. People that like to just play the game and see how it goes are going to react differently from people that like to be completionists and explore everything, which will be different from people that like to minmax (esp. if in-game resources can be sparse).

                                                            This is another side of day 1 DLC... the consumers are basically blind on not just the theoretical game-play value of the content, but the personal subjective value it has.
