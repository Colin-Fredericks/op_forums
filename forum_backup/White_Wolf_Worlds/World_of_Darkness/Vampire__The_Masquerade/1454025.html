    Which clan is the most dangerous in the modern world?

    07-01-2021, 09:07 PM
    Considering everything: ability to influence mortals, ability to detect ambushes, ability to conduct ambushes, utility, single combat ability, least crippling bane, etc.

    Note that this is NOT the kind of thread where you say things like "this can has the most influence, this other clan has the best combat ability". It's assumed that not all abilities are of equal value in the modern nights, and that some discipline sets synergize internally better than others.

    Rather, which clan is the most complete package to survive and thrive in the modern nights?

    I'd nominate the Toreador: great influence discipline, great combat discipline, great discipline for detecting ambushes and learning secrets. Great synergy between Auspex and other two Disciplines, in that detecting ambushes and learning secrets and having a get-out-of-dodge power is perfect for when you've detected that bomb or 50 caliber sniper rifle. Presence is perfect for exploiting those political secrets you've learned. Bane is more a nuisance than crippling. Lacking in stealth and brawling ability, but that's more a nuisance than crippling in the age of sniper rifles and explosives. In many ways these creatures are just made for the modern world.

    Your nomination?

      So, yadda yadda yadda, different tables, different results, varies by edition, blah.

      Vysha's Flawlessly Perfect Strategy for Surviving the Modern Nights states the following, in order of importance (in V20 at least, less familiar with V5):
      1) It is always better to avoid a fight than to win a fight.
      2) It is always better to defuse a fight than to win a fight.
      3) It is always better to escape a fight than to win a fight.
      4) It is always better to survive a fight than to win a fight.

      For surviving and thriving the modern nights, no clan can beat the Toreador, because their discipline spread allows them to fulfill 1-3 within their discipline spread. Auspex allows for a greater chance of noting danger well before it reaches the kindred, allowing them to just simply go somewhere else. Presence hits the sweet spot for #2, allowing them to make an enemy a friend, or at least scare them away. Both Celerity and Presence give great escape avenues, through Dread Gaze penalties to an enemy and Celerity to GTFO faster.

      But that's all baseline, using disciplines as vanilla as can be. Other clans have discipline spreads that can be applied in cunning and unusual ways for equal or greater effect.

      Surgeon General's Warning: Vysha's Flawlessly Perfect Strategy for Surviving the Modern Nights is only a treatment, not a cure. See your Storyteller if VFPSfStMN is right for you. Side effects may include Willpower and Blood Point drain, cowardice, itchy scalp and ghoul meat shield armies.

                              I think that's where the "modern nights" factor helps narrow things down. My own suggestion of Malkavians dropped considerably down the list if we change ears. An era where there's a much harsher stigma against mental illness means their drawback hits a lot harder around the kine. In an era where public violence is more "acceptable" their lack of affinity for physical Disciplines limits them. Vampires are, by their nature, all dangerous. But the kinds of dangerous they are can ebb and flow regarding the current state of affairs and how adaptable they are.

                              Venture are dangerous, but their very lopsided towards social power. Fortitude is nice, but as it tends to go, not being hit is better than being able to take a hit. In an era where information is even more powerful than ever, lacking the more covert intelligence gathering abilities you can find in Auspex, Animalism, some forms of Necrmancy/Oblivion, or the stealth of Obfuscate of Protean to boost mundane surveillance, is a pretty big drawback.

                              To use a less modern example, consider the strategic issues the Allies faced when the Enigma Machine was cracked. On one hand, they suddenly had a massive upper hand because they had access to all that thought-to-be-secure information, on the other hand they didn't want the Germans to realize the machine was cracked so it would keep being used.

                              More specialist/focused Clans like the Venture and Brujah don't really have a lot of flexibility. Without the edge in the cat and mouse game of using enough information to defeat your enemies without them realizing how you're doing it, they're force to lean in hard on attacking directly and trying to finish it before their enemies can retreat and reassess. They basically have to announce that they broken the Enigma Machine, and hope that they can do enough damage before it's replaced that it was worth it. The Clans that offer a more flexible package in information warfare can play a longer game.

                              That's the sort of consideration I think lets us create reasonable arguments for Clans to have an edge in a specific context.
