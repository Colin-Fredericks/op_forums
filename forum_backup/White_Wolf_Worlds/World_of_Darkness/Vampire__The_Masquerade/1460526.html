    Declaring Praxis over a city

    09-20-2021, 10:24 AM
    So, I've been looking a few places and I'm having a difficult time finding references here.

    A little backstory. My game takes place in Minneapolis, and the Prince of Minneapolis is looking to declare Praxis over St. Paul (since St. Paul hasn't had a Prince in a few years (unknown to my players, he was beckoned)). She has already made her declaration, so what is next? Right now, St. Paul has a loose amalgam of several self-proclaimed Anarch barons who will likely protest this move, but none of them are strong enough to resist.

    tldr: When a Camarilla Prince declares Praxis, what steps do they usually follow to attain Princedom of that city?

      Generally, the thing with declaring Praxis is it boils down two things, IMHO:

      1. You get people to respect your declarations.
      2. You can and do punish anyone who doesn't obey your declarations and no one stops you.
      Basically, if you declare Praxis and show up at Elysium the answer of whether you're Prince is answered by anyone challenging you.

      And then you ripping their heart out or threatening to and no one stopping you.

          It's important to note choosing violence isn't something the character declaring Praxis -- or rivals -- necessarily needs, or desires, to do. Realistically, the key is to build a coalition of influential vampires who can impose their will on the city by carrot or stick, by which the character with Praxis is the front-facing element of that bloc. Most commonly that's a Primogen council, or the ruling pack of Ducti beneath a Bishop. Note even within the Sabbat, this is one sphere in which Monomacy is generally considered a resort of last choice for conflict resolution, due to the vaulderie and need to preserve nominal unity within a city's packs; it's far more advantageous to resolve conflicts behind closed doors, or have the packs duke it out themselves, than it is to display dissention in the higher ranks.

          The Anarchs are, paradoxically, the outliers and rear-looking element among Kindred. They want to have their cake and eat it too, in the form of decentralized leadership but also to have leaders consolidated enough to mediate conflicts, enforce the Traditions, and ensure the smooth operation of a city's domains. In other words, they set themselves up for failure because, as was pointed out in the OP, the question then becomes "what happens when Barons come into conflict?". The city probably implodes, and fewer, more powerful and consolidated, Barons come out the other end, that's what; and at that point, the city's leadership structure begins to resemble a Dark Ages princedom more than anything even the most autocratic Camarilla domains have to offer.

          It's important to note, influential and powerful vampires rarely want to be the front-facing element of a power bloc. A vampire that claims Praxis is simply painting a target on their back on behalf of the ruling bloc, whether from threats external or internal to that bloc. This is precisely why Praxis generally falls to vampires of two age categories: ancillae eager to make their mark who don't understand the full implication of Praxis, and extremely old elders and methuselahs who are powerful enough to not care about the full implication of Praxis. The run-of-the-mill elder who can't foresee a plot bubbling against them based on whispers and premonitions, soak an anti-materiel incendiary round to the face, remain conscious and active in torpor, or call upon a global web of influence to exert their will, finds their most-advantageous position to be able to exert influence without being the most-visible element of a ruling bloc.

          In other words, if you're 250-350 years old, it's better be "just" a Harpy to whom the Prince is secretly blood bound, than it is to be the Prince. Even if you're the oldest vampire in the city, with the most backgrounds, and a discipline layout no other vampire can hope to compete with; you may be "entitled" to Praxis, or "supposed" to be the one with it, but it's a fool's game if you can wield the power of a Prince without being one.

          Sure, a vampire can simply beat others into submission, or rely on any number of brute-force tactics to become that front-facing element; but horse-trading is a far more desirable choice, given it tends far less to result in public humiliation, long-term grudges, or Final Death.

          For example, when I'm time-strapped and can't run my game, or we decide to shift gears, one player in my group ST's a modern nights game. The last we played, my Nosferatu PC just declared Praxis. Heinrich didn't declare Praxis because he's a 150-year-old vampire who spent the last century in torpor, and is nominally the oldest vampire left in the city after the massive wave of bloodletting that cleared it of ancillae and elders. He didn't declare Praxis because he stepped up after being awoken, and coordinated a blitzkrieg attack against a coterie of Giovanni that had taken over the city in the wake of its past Prince's (and most of the Primogen's) Final Death. And, he didn't declare Praxis because he figured out the previous Nosferatu Primogen had been murdered and replaced by a Tzimisce infiltrator.

          He declared Praxis because he was the first to start making deals, and made the best deals.

          In this particular case the Tremere Archon who'd been tasked with overseeing and correcting the current situation, because Heinrich figured out what the Tremere were after, and the Archon fucked up and fed my PC some key blackmail material. The way Heinrich's appearance, merits, and flaws play out, he can be mistaken for a Zulo form Tzimisce if one doesn't get a good, close, look at him; the Archon thought my PC was the Tzimisce infiltrator because he violated the Masquerade immediately after awakening and showed up on some traffic cams. So, the Archon sent some ghouls after him in an exploratory attack, packing various thaumaturgical goodies; the attack didn't work out too well for them being my character can vanish from the mind's eye, and yeet dumpsters, and my character ended up with some fairly damning evidence against that Archon in his possession.

          He forced the Archon to the bargaining table, and worked out a deal. The Archon would cover up Heinrich's...fairly substantial...Masquerade breaches, most of which the Archon caused due to his stupid-ass ghoul assault, in exchange for the artifacts back. And, the Tremere would support his Praxis claim and remain in an advisory role within the city, in exchange for exclusive access to the research site in the city they wanted. It means the city is nominally Camarilla while functionally Anarch, with all the bells, whistles, and obligations that carries, but for the moment it's the best-available position for all involved parties given the strategic resources currently in play.

          The deal sounds bad, but Heinrich realized everyone wants the site -- it's what the Giovanni were after, and it's what the Sabbat are after, being the vampires in the city are already aware the Sabbat is preparing for a siege. The Tremere are the stablest, most reliable, party that could control the site, and Heinrich realized if the Tremere want to hold the site they need a strong presence in the city with a leadership they can work with, and that means helping defend the city. Not to mention, whatever's at that research site is something best kept under lock and key, and close scrutiny. The Tremere are better-equipped to ensure that happens, opposed to random dumbfuck Anarchs screwing around and waking something up by accident.

          Ergo, once the Giovanni were out, the city experienced a bloodless transition of power -- something fairly necessary, after years of unrest following the demise of the city's past leadership.

              If there's any doubt in any Kindred's minds, it's smart to back up proclamations with some kind of event or spectacle to show they have teeth. Beheading a few uppity Anarchs in an open court for violations of the Traditions is a pretty surefire way to do it, as right of destruction is a prince's right alone.

              On the other hand, if the prince wants to make a "softer" declaration of power, they could just travel to St. Paul, throw a party at a site they've declared Elysium, and invite Kindred from both cities to attend. They can show off they have the resources to arrange a lavish spectacle and effectively do it in these Anarchs' living room. The prince could issue grants of domain to various supporters (best of all would be Anarchs who choose to bend the knee) and name various Kindred to offices such as sheriff, seneschal, etc. If the Anarchs don't do something about the prince's little fete, that immediately establishes they didn't have the strength to.

              Said party also be combined with the above mentioned beheadings in an open court. That's probably the best way to do it. Show both cities' Kindred a fun time, hand out rewards to supporters,
              make an example of what happens to Kindred who resist the new regime.


              Public spectacle aside, though, the prince needs some kind of actual power in the city for their claim to mean anything. So what does this look like?

              Do they have useful pawns in mortal institutions? If an Anarch baron is a long-time "advisor" to the city's mayor or police chief, that needs to be dealt with. Similarly, since one of the foremost jobs of the prince is protection of the Masquerade, they need some kind of influence (personally or through loyal Kindred) over emergency response services. The Camarilla needs some kind of mechanism in place to deal with Masquerade violations.

              Do Kindred in the city actually owe fealty to the prince? This could involve getting enough Anarchs to bend the knee, sending loyal Kindred from Minneapolis to St. Paul, or both. Granting permission to Embrace can be very useful here, both as a means of gaining more supporters (after the initial time invested to train the childe) and as a carrot to convince more Kindred to relocate to St. Paul. Most Kindred want to Embrace while a prince without actual supporters won't get very far as prince.

              Does the prince actually spend much time in the city? If you don't, the claim is pretty nominal. This is the main reason princes usually don't claim to be princes of distant cities. Minneapolis' prince should either have havens in the city, host Elysium events there, and make their presence felt, or else appoint a "viceroy" to run the city for them.


              All of this may also be unnecessary if everyone already knows the prince of Minneapolis calls the shots in St. Paul. A simple proclamation may be all it takes.

                  Declaring praxis is what one does AFTER they have already assumed effective power in a domain. It's not what you do beforehand because any relevant contestation of your claim makes you look weak.

                  Now protests by Anarchs can more or less be ignored. By definition, they of course are going to complain so who cares. But that assumes the Anarchs don't really have much power. However, the presence of Anarch barons indicates they hold at least some kind of real power in the city. But since you said they have no power to resist, it seems they really aren't "barons". Just some leaders of a coterie of anarchs. So I'm not sure what the real situation is.

                  The most important thing for a Prince is 1) to "control" the relevant mortal institutions most important to vampires (police, media) to preserve the Masquerade and not allow someone else to utilize them to undermine the Prince, 2) to be acknowledged by the most important vampires in the city that she is in charge, and 3) to begin dividing up the spoils and reward her close allies and cement loyalty.

                  I am assuming any pro-Camarilla vampires in St Paul already support the move. If not, that's her first priority to win their support.

                  So in terms of practicality, if she hasn't already she needs to gain Influence in St Paul over anything necessary for her control (but can be her directly or one of her trusted minions), she needs vampires actually living in St Paul to publicly admit she is in charge (either natives or people she's sent over there from Minneapolis), and begin dividing up certain territories into personal domains, grant permission to sire, and so on to her loyalists that will help enforce her rule in St Paul.

                  I would also say that she should try to cultivate some of the Anarchs to her side by agreeing to acknowledge them in the Domain, pardon for any possible previous crimes, and so on. This last bit is dependent on the specifics of the Anarchs. If one of the more powerful anarch leaders defects and is able to bring his crew along, maybe reward that person with some minor office or put him in charge of enforcing laws there. That divides your enemies and establishes you are in control. Probably the Prince herself should not begin the conversation, but send someone who obviously speaks on behalf of the Prince who can make the initial contact and sound them out. Multiple Barons indicate there is rivalry for power among them, and it's possible at least one would be willing to cut a deal in order to get an edge on rivals and move up in the process.

                  A Prince normally does not need any validation from a higher authority. But it is always possible that at some point a Conclave may be held, and the precise legal technicalities of Camarilla law be resolved. But I doubt very much any Justicar or Archon is going to complain about how the Prince goes about establishing domain unless there are some good reasons to do so. Moving into a vacant city with Anarch gangs doesn't seem to present any problems for me. It's even technically the same urban area despite being two separate cities.
