    Promise of 1528, event and fun ideas?

    07-01-2022, 07:10 PM
    So, how you people think occurred the Promise between the Camarilla and Giovanni Clan? And what repercussions did it had immediately?

    I'm running a Giovanni Chronicles game, and at the same time a Transylvania Chronicles one parallel to it (with the same players, different PCs). And I'm currently at the Convention of Thorns with both coteries and games intertwined. But, while Transylvania goes with smaller jumps to the next chapters, Giovanni Chron. goes hooping from 1444 to 1666, which is way too much, so the players would have to make way too much choices for characters that played a couple of fast chapters and now the Convention of Thorns. So I wanted to have some sessions, no more than 3, around the Promise of 1528, and was wondering what kind of game could there be.

    The final meeting or signing of the Promise may have happened in Rome, Florence, Milan, Venice or some place else maybe in the Holy Roman Empire. Probably some Justicar and archons were present (if not most Justicars, like they were in Transylvania to stop the Conspiracy of Isaac bur failed), and I imagine Augustus, Claudius, Ambrogino and maybe even some other traitor Cappadocian turned-Giovanni by baptism of the blood of their kin, or simply other Giovannis. Besides staying out of each others business - like a mutual pact of non-aggression and non-interference, baring Masquerade breaches I suppose - what else went immediately into effect?


    What kind of stories could be made about it? Prior, during or right after the signing of the Promise? I don't think there is much space for interference of the PCs into the big points of the Promise but there could be some exceptions, some minor rulings, especially in exchange for something.
    In this particular game, the Children of Isaac are even if shadily and loosely associated with the Camarilla, and definitely aren't friends of the Giovanni, who they ruined the ritual-diablerie and cut Claudius arm off right after his diablerie of Japheth (although it wasn't for any love of the Cappadocians). But one of the characters is actually an Archon now, so he is very likely to be there in the Promise locale, some way or another.

    General ideas about the Promise's event, possible laws and rules listed in it, and the effects of it, would be a very nice for the topic as well.

    Strange... When coincidence seems too convenient, I prefer to call it fate.

      My take on the Promise is that it is based around the assumption that they're dealing with a Antediluvian. The Inner Council knows their claims of the Antediluvians not existing is bullshit and they believe that Augustus Giovanni devoured Cappadocius. So, they're dealing with something that could become a new Tremere Clan.

      The Promise seems like it was probably a non-interference pact with the Giovanni as well as their business.

      * The Giovanni will not attempt to seize cities or praxis.

      * Giovanni might be welcome at Elysium and in Camarilla cities but can't take any positions in court (this might be more flexible)

      * The Giovanni will not join or support the Sabbat or Anarchs that haven't folded into the Camarilla.

      * The Giovanni will not attempt to join the Camarilla, which is probably because the Camarilla is afraid that they will have too much power with a recently alive Antediluvian.

      * Giovanni who announce themselves to a Prince will be subject to the Traditions and its protections like any other vampire.

      * The Giovanni will be allowed ONE city where they rule (Venice) and can operate from.

      * The Giovanni are allowed to gather financial power and political power among mortals to their hearts content.

      * Possibly the Giovanni are restricted in their numbers of Embraces per year (which works out for the Giovanni since their numbers of deaths are far less staying out of the Jyhad)

      * The Cappadocians will receive no aid from the Camarilla and can be slaughtered by the Giovanni at will. This means that Giovanni won't be persecuted for their diablerie or murder of the Cappadocians in any domain they do it.

      * The Camarilla may report any Cappadocians they find to the Giovanni (they won't protect them or actively help in the hunt, though). Being the Camarilla, I expect they'd make it clear that such information would have to be paid for and the "default" state is they'll just turn a blind eye to the vampirecide.

      You might even retcon the weirdest element of the Giovanni as being part of the Promise.

      "We will only Embrace members of our family" with the Giovanni getting around that by keeping an extensive mortal breeding stock and marrying other families.

                                                  The original clan description of the Giovanni in the 1e and 2e Players Guide mentioned the Giovanni would only embrace 3 people once a generation (which I interpreted as about once every decade). There wasn't any reason given for this, but I always associated it with the deal made between the Giovanni and the Camarilla in the Promise of 1528.

                                                  From the Camarilla's perspective, the Promise of 1528 was to bring the Giovanni into the "Camarilla system" so they were part of the new order that the Inner Circle was establishing. They were a part of the Camarilla's "great society" of the undead, just not an equal part. It put strong limits on the Giovanni, but kept them part of the system instead of them becoming bitter-ender Anarchs/Sabbat. It allowed the Giovanni internal autonomy that would not be subject to Justicar supervision (who got to be embraced, internal punishment/destruction, diablerie within the clan, etc.), but kept a firm thumb on them so they couldn't run wild.

                                                  I always designated the "Devil Kindred" to be about in the same condition as Jews were in Medieval Europe - a "protected", but legally inferior, class by the Camarilla's ruling authorities. They were not to be molested, but their activities were limited and proscribed. But just like Jewish moneylenders were able to provide a useful service that Christians could not do, the Devil Kindred sometimes got to take advantage of similar things. They could be useful to Camarilla Kindred by doing things they couldn't do themselves without bringing scandal or punishment down on them.

                                                  Unfortunately, about the only time the Promise of 1528 was mentioned was in the context of the Giovanni themselves, so it was usually mocked as nothing special, and sometimes even implied it was done to protect the Camarilla from them. I know a lot of the players of Giovanni tended to be very arrogant about it and not think it really restricted the Giovanni in any way. But I never held that view and took things differently in my own chronicles.

                                                  They were subject to certain restrictions like they could never enter a domain without explicit permission from a Camarilla Prince. Although once they were there, residency couldn't be easily revoked without cause. The aforementioned restriction on how many embraces they could do. That they could not hold Status or office in a Camarilla domain. That Giovanni in a domain would need to provide certain services for the Prince if requested without need of a boon. And similar things.

                                                  That's how I handled it. If you there is anything here you can make use out of for your own chronicle, go ahead.

                                                          The original clan description of the Giovanni in the 1e and 2e Players Guide mentioned the Giovanni would only embrace 3 people once a generation (which I interpreted as about once every decade). There wasn't any reason given for this, but I always associated it with the deal made between the Giovanni and the Camarilla in the Promise of 1528.

                                                          From the Camarilla's perspective, the Promise of 1528 was to bring the Giovanni into the "Camarilla system" so they were part of the new order that the Inner Circle was establishing. They were a part of the Camarilla's "great society" of the undead, just not an equal part. It put strong limits on the Giovanni, but kept them part of the system instead of them becoming bitter-ender Anarchs/Sabbat. It allowed the Giovanni internal autonomy that would not be subject to Justicar supervision (who got to be embraced, internal punishment/destruction, diablerie within the clan, etc.), but kept a firm thumb on them so they couldn't run wild.

                                                          I always designated the "Devil Kindred" to be about in the same condition as Jews were in Medieval Europe - a "protected", but legally inferior, class by the Camarilla's ruling authorities. They were not to be molested, but their activities were limited and proscribed. But just like Jewish moneylenders were able to provide a useful service that Christians could not do, the Devil Kindred sometimes got to take advantage of similar things. They could be useful to Camarilla Kindred by doing things they couldn't do themselves without bringing scandal or punishment down on them.

                                                          Unfortunately, about the only time the Promise of 1528 was mentioned was in the context of the Giovanni themselves, so it was usually mocked as nothing special, and sometimes even implied it was done to protect the Camarilla from them. I know a lot of the players of Giovanni tended to be very arrogant about it and not think it really restricted the Giovanni in any way. But I never held that view and took things differently in my own chronicles.

                                                          They were subject to certain restrictions like they could never enter a domain without explicit permission from a Camarilla Prince. Although once they were there, residency couldn't be easily revoked without cause. The aforementioned restriction on how many embraces they could do. That they could not hold Status or office in a Camarilla domain. That Giovanni in a domain would need to provide certain services for the Prince if requested without need of a boon. And similar things.

                                                          That's how I handled it. If you there is anything here you can make use out of for your own chronicle, go ahead.

                                                          Yeah, I have a tendency more for this side of things.



                                                          And sometimes it is that old case of Vampire not filling in these matters, and "leaving to the Storyteller" a little too much. Which isn't bad, but sometimes it is a shame. Not only the Promise in this case.
