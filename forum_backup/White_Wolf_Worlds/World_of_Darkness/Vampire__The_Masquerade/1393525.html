    V20 how to simulate incendiary weapons?

    06-01-2020, 06:00 PM
    The rules here are kinda light.

    For flamethrowers and 'dragonsbreath rounds*' I'd say you roll to hit, 1 success gives you 1 level of damage a turn, 3 successes gives you two, five successes gives you 3 levels of damage a turn. I'm unsure of how many rounds this stuff should burn for, or if you can actively try to put such fires out without them getting worse, but it seems a good plan.

    *From my understanding, Dragonsbreath rounds shoot small, light, burning projectiles that don't really have much of an impact to them. It'd be most appropriate to ignore the shotgun's normal damage.

    Now, for other guns... I really don't know what to do here.

    -Deal normal damage, then deal fire damage?
    -Deal damage as aggravated?
    -Deal damage as stamina-soakable aggravated?
    -Deal damage as lethal?
    -Convert a number of bashing damage into aggravated damage based on the number of successes to hit?

    I guess this also applies to burning melee weapons. Torches or red hot pokers...

    Best solutions?

          I like that idea for a lowered damage rate than just 8 dice but agg like a normal shotgun. It makes more sense to do lower damage. Dragon's breath also can't be used in an auto or semiauto weapon because it doesn't produce enough recoil to cycle the next round. You also would need to wait anywhere from 3-6 seconds for the round to burn out, since ejecting a round that's still spitting fire would be a bad idea. So it'd only be barrel loaded or pump action weapons that could use dragon's breath and it'd be fair to keep it to a single shot per round, if that. You could also increase soak difficulty by how engulfed the person is, so maybe a cap of 3/turn like you have but additional successes on the attack roll past 3 make the soak difficulty higher. If fits with the rules for soaking fire damage based on intensity and how much exposure they have to the flame.

          Most of the smaller stuff I'd probably limit to only one or 2 agg, but not engulfing like dragon's breath, so probably not worth making the soak difficulty higher. Maybe even have lower soak difficulty for some of the really small stuff.

              Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
              For flamethrowers and 'dragonsbreath rounds*' I'd say you roll to hit, 1 success gives you 1 level of damage a turn, 3 successes gives you two, five successes gives you 3 levels of damage a turn. I'm unsure of how many rounds this stuff should burn for, or if you can actively try to put such fires out without them getting worse, but it seems a good plan.
              Generally, I use successes to govern igniting things, but cap successes based on the device and target (it's a lot easier to set someone ablaze with a flamethrower than with incendiary rifle rounds). More successes means more of the target's clothes/hair/environment starts burning to deal damage in future rounds. I let this build over multiple turns/multiple attackers. Trying to put yourself out is generally possible outside of some high end materials (napalm isn't going to go out because you pat your arm or roll around),

              Remember that the goal of most incendiary devices is to set things on fire, rather than just "stick" fire to them. Outside of things like napalm which is designed to stick for increased damage to the target, the fuel for these devices burns up fairly quickly. Hence my preference for using successes to dictate how well you set the target on fire as the most important factor.

              From my understanding, Dragonsbreath rounds shoot small, light, burning projectiles that don't really have much of an impact to them. It'd be most appropriate to ignore the shotgun's normal damage.
              While there are more advanced options where you can combine incendiary compounds and a shotgun slug for more damage, yeah if the dragonbreath doesn't burn, there isn't really enough force to worry about more damage.

              Now, for other guns... I really don't know what to do here.
              Honestly? They're not really worth worrying about outside of a few specialized cases.

              Most incendiary rounds aren't really effective on a human-like target. They're designed for firing on vehicles or buildings. They're moving fast enough, with enough force, that against normal flesh the incendiary part doesn't have time to actually do anything (remember that bullets are already pretty hot).

              One of the best examples of this was actually a Mythbusters episode where they were trying to recreate the "bullets make cars explode" thing from action movies. Shooting at a gas tank with incendiary rounds took multiple hits before the gas started burning mildly.

              So, for the most part, incendiary bullets are just going to do normal damage to a vampire. If you really want to action movie it up a bit, maybe make it lethal, or the first damage is fire agg, or something.

              Now, if you know what you're doing, you can engineer rounds that would actually be very nasty if you wanted to mix the impact of a firearm with fire. Even so, you'd generally end up with normal firearms damage with some aggravated damage and/or lighting them on fire for damage over multiple turns.

              I guess this also applies to burning melee weapons. Torches or red hot pokers...
              This gets into a metaphysical question. Red hot pokers aren't burning, even though they're hot enough to burn things. Are actual flames necessary? If so, you can just ignore very hot melee weapons. If you want to try to work in hot but not flaming weapons... that's more complicated.

              For something like a torch, I'd give the attacker two options. If they're just beating the target with the torch like a club? The odds of the fire really mattering in terms of damage is minimal. It might be good for provoking a Rotshreck check, but only on something like 5+ successes to hit would the flames really come into play, and then only to set something on fire in the process. If they're using the torch to try to catch things on fire? Then I just go back to my answer for flamethrowers and the like.

                          There is also the fact that fire weapons tend to be contextual in use when discussing results. Basically there are three aspects to fire weapons that need to be accounted for.

                          1. Psychological
                          Even if it doesn't do damage, being able to force vampiric fire freak outs on use of a weapon does make for odd scenarios. Like if a vampire gets hit with a dragons breath round, their clothes might be charred and have a few little flames and the vampire took no physical damage from the impact, but they failed their roll and vent off the deep end for a little while.

                          2. Starting Damage vs. Reuse
                          A red hot poker getting stabbed into a vampire is going to do a fair amount of up front damage, but it will loose its heat damage rather quickly since all that energy just got absorbed by the mass of the vampire. So its not like you are able to pull the fire poker out and immediately stab again for the same amount of heat damage. You would need a dozen fire pokers in the fire and swap out after every hit.

                          3. Damage Over Time
                          A dragons breath round causes fire only for a few seconds, and even when used on dry clothing it doesn't produce much more than a cigarette lighters worth of flame per pellet strike, most of which flare out quickly rather than catching properly. On the other hand if you toss a Molotov at the vampires feet, they are instantly standing in the middle of a bonfire and most of their body is in direct flame contact. This will do a decent amount of damage before they dive or run out of the fire, but that is a lot like flash searing a steak. If you want serious damage you have to keep the vampire in the flames, which means controlling the environment as much as the weapon. Toss a vampire into a closet with a Molotov and they have no where to go, this is why using a flame thrower to clear bunkers and trenches is so functional while using them in open field battles is much less so.

                          Of course all of this makes "fire weapons" very hard to model in game terms. Then you have the fact that fire, once started doesn't distinguish friend from foe, so as you are swinging your fire poker about you could be setting the drapes and furniture on fire, which means you are now in danger.

                                          Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                                          Dragonsbreath rounds, if you ignore the burning, is about as effective as a BB shotgun. I really feel it should be treated as a semi-auto flamethrower rather than as a 'shotgun'
                                          Pretty sure most of us agree with that?

                                          Are you sure about this?
                                          As confident as I can be without the ability to throw down anything more than being a very long time arm-chair observer of military technology.

                                          Pistol ammunition just doesn't have the room for a bullet and an incendiary charge behind it.

                                          A lot of lower powered rounds are really tracers, not incendiaries (frequently confused because tracers use similar compounds to generate the flames that make their path visible).

                                          The smallest incendiary round that's seen significant combat was based on a rifle cartridge that's now NATO standard for sniper rifles and machine guns (as in dedicated automatic weapons, not assault rifles, though the AK series's larger chamber can handle the same size).

                                          But assuming you're correct, what if the bullet's velocity is low enough that it remains in the target (Or the vampire's stacked with fortitude so it can't really exit all that well)
                                          This is one of the reasons shotguns get brought up a bunch. A modified squash head is basically perfect for this sort of tactic.

                                          The big problem? Even if it's a really hot flame, it's still really small, it doesn't last very long, and flesh doesn't ignite easily. While the books aren't super technical in the language here, vampires are vulnerable to fire based damage, but they're not particularly flammable. That is, they're not like a piece of paper where holding a candle flame to them is enough to set off a chain reaction.

                                          Consider trying to build a campfire. You don't just hold a match to a big log. It doesn't work. The log will singe a bit and you'll run out of matches long before you start a real fire. What you do is set up a series of fires. Traditionally you start with tinder, which combusts easily but doesn't burn too fast. The tinder then combusts the kindling, and the kindling then combusts the log (or for a bonfire smaller logs, which then get the bigger logs). If you've got some modern supplies you can speed things up with lighter fluid and metal shavings, but the process stays mostly the same.

                                          So a small-arms incendiary round designed to 'stick' into a human-like target can do a lot of damage, but it's going to be very localized unless, just like every other incendiary, it sets things like clothing on fire to act as kindling. At that size, even things like napalm and thermite are going to leave a nasty charred divot in the target before falling out once the force of impact isn't pressing them against the target anymore; like a match being held up to a log.

                                          Of course, to a human, a base-ball sized chunk of flesh being burnt off of the body in a matter of seconds is an immensely dangerous wound even without catching fire (shock, infection, and permanent scaring are all major risks). But beyond the "fire bad" reaction vampires have...how threatening to their unlife is that really going to be?

                                          In the end, doing more than 1 agg + bullet damage, barring ignition of something around the vampire, is just way more than these weapons should be doing.
