    Ghosts in mortal armour: one of the most durable objects to possess (assuming animating a statue is not an option) and a horror classic, the implacable empty suit, infused with the spirit of its former owner, or a sacrifice or an evil spirit etc, killing to avenge/protect/because it's fun.     Not really. Even if the armour is animated by the ghost, any attack to the armour has no bearing on the ghost. The ghost, by WoD definitions, is incorporeal, meaning mortal armour is irrelevant.

    Someone thinking of getting into a serious conflict should be thinking of armour with the same focus they apply to automatic weapons, incendaries and explosives, it works, it makes the mission more possible, and more survivable.
    A vampire looking to get into serious conflict would probably need to look beyond mortal means of defense.

                            It is not useless when acting against non-projectile weapons that cause trauma either - a baseball bat or brick used effectively could be classified as something that causes Aggravated damage against a mortal, because they can be lethal, which can be reduced with Riot armour accordingly.

                                        Since it seems to come up routinely, I'd like to point something out: vampires are not zombies. Zombies function purely via indifference to damage. With vampires there is some utterly ambiguous combination of indifference to damage and magical properties of their blood. It's unclear how much they are like zombies and how much they are like T-1000's, but earlier editions implied at least a degree of T-1000ness to their ability to soak. Shoot a zombie in the elbow, and the arm is reduced in functionality. Shoot a vampire in the elbow, and maybe the arm is reduced in functionality, and maybe the blood holds it together and keeps it animated normally.
