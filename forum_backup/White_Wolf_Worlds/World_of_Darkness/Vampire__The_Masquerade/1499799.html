    EXP cost for Humanity

    12-15-2022, 08:05 PM
    So, some of my players often tell me that they think Humanity should be sometimes awarded to players without Exp cost. What do you guys think about it? Do you agree that some actions could possibly give the PC a free/reduced cost to raise their Humanity, or do you think it should always be bought with the normal cost for raising it?

      As I've said in other threads, I really don't like the "waive XP" approach to things. I'll try to avoid going into that too much yet again.

      I think part of the problem for VtM, is that Humanity really doesn't work well with the way XP costs are calculated. While the general idea of "clawing your way back from the depths of depravity to normal is extremely grueling," where getting back to 7 from 3 or 4 is a lot of XP, the cost of 7 to 8 is pretty steep. It almost feels like it should be reversed so getting to high Humanity from average Humanity should be cheap (it's' easy enough to lose as it is), but trying to come back from 2-3 should be a huge deal that feels like you might never do it.

      I also do think you could look to the CofD 2e books for a few alternate setups where equivalent traits (not Humanity because it doesn't do this) are not changed by XP at all, and going up or down is its own system. Something about those though, is that there's a lot more consequences to smaller changes that how Humanity works. A werewolf in Forsaken 2e that moves 2 points of Harmony in either direction is going to be dealing with some major changes to their interactions with things like shapeshifting and Rage. The no-XP options in the CofD 2e tend to be less about "high good, low bad" and more about what rating suits the way a character acts (which might not be what they want, but then they either need to change, or wrestle with that internal conflict).

              The thing is, Humanity is partially designed to fall and then stabilise at a lower rating, and to tempt the players into letting this happen. The scaling costs reinforce this, it's hard to keep your Humanity at a high level for relatively minor benefits, but if you let it fall just a couple of points it'll ping you less, and you'll have more XP to spend on other stuff...

              Now I don't particularly like the way Humanity is set up in Masquerade, it's all too much about being a moral person. I wouldn't bring in the Requiem 2e version though, I'd rework the concept of Roads/Paths. Not sure if Humanity would remain as 'be a good person' in that setup, I might switch it to the 'Patb of Humility' and have it be about remaining beneath noticed, I'd have to be likely to run Masquerade before I put in serious effort.

              I would not have good acts raise the Humanity meter, like many video game morality systems. Being a good person takes more than just the occasional good act, you've got to be consistent about it. Lots of soul-searching and soup kitchens for the penitent vampire, and if you're already quite moral it takes a lot of searching to find things to atone for.

              That said there are systems in CofD 2e that rely on in-game actions to raise them, but as Heavy Arms says they're structured very different to Masquerade's Humanity and meant to be potentially in more flux. Imagine if having your Humanity too high influenced your ability to use vampiric powers, it's an interesting idea but probably not what the 'free Humanity' crowd wants.

                              A real simple no XP system if you don't want to make Humanity too much about XP, and want to get away from some of the strict morality aspects of it:

                              Get rid of Humanity completely.

                              The mechanical aspects of Humanity that deal with things like interacting with humans, or how well you can withstand weaknesses/banes is based on how full your vampire is. A completely full vampire has the appearance of a normal Humanity 10 vampire. The Beast is sated, and doesn't subconsciously freak out humans, and not hungering for blood makes it easier to focus on behaving like a human.

                              How long things like Torpor lasts and similar mechanics that require a more stable number key off your highest Virtue instead

                              Any time a vampire does something the violates their personal creed (make it completely individualized, or keep the Ethics of Humanity/the Paths), they risk losing a Virtue dot instead of Humanity (since Virtues only go to 5, they never get that expensive, and with three of them you can spread the damage around a bit to soften that more). The difficulty of the roll is based on how egregious the violation is rather than using the sin charts.
