                    My review on DriveThruRPG goes along these same lines, but know that I believe this is FAR from being a perfect book…

                    First of all, the various chapters do not build upon one another in any linear way. Rather, they interlock in ways that don’t make sense until you’ve read it all – and even then it’s sometimes hard to find the proper connexions. That is, mentions in Chapter One of groups or ideas don’t come up ‘in the spotlight’ until Chapter Four, and things show up in Chapter Three that demand going back to read Chapter Two. This is, I feel, less a problem than a FEATURE of the subject matter. The Black Hand is, by its very nature, a tangled web of knots, and I’m going to be coming back to this book over and over to figure out how to unravel the Hand’s motives and activities.

                    Second, there ARE holes in the text. Drakaina, revenants, blood magics, and the place of Enoch’s waterfront district on the Sea of Shadows, these all are dead ends in the book. The thing is, what would a book about the Tal’Mahe’Ra BE without those? Sure, Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand had more blocked streets than thoroughfares (or so we all seem to remember), but THIS book’s dangling bits more often come in the flavour of evocative rather than malicious omissions. Of course, I WOULD like to have the promised Appendix with – if possible – ghoul families and a modern take on the Danava, but that’s not a deal breaker for me.

                    Third, it does contradict itself in subtle and gross ways, and THAT, my friends, is what the Manus Nigrum has ever been all about!

                    Finally, I gotta say that yeah, I kinda love the book! It’s lit fires in my mind that hadn’t been there before, and I call that a win.

                                                        Originally posted by Günther View Post
                                                        Hi!Can somebody tell me how Vicissitude and the Soul Eater thing was solved in this book? I contemplate buying it, but I worry of getting another book of self-censored that DS 1 was. I consider the first Dirty Secrets a warning to all writers why you should not drink more than a glas of wine before writing stories.
                                                        The Soul Eater thing was replaced with Asakku. It is explicitly called out that Asakku's reality is optional, and that it may be self-delusion on the part of its Kindred investigators that perhaps others have taken advantage of.

                                                        So what is Asakku? A small group in the Black Hand has noticed that when some vampires study Vicissitude, something makes contact with them, offering them power in return for the ability to manifest itself. If they accept, they lose their identities to it, becoming its vessels.

                                                        The two most prominent theories the group has are that either it's a form of madness, the vampire coming to identify with the Discipline to the extent their original consciousness is obliterated, or... it's a manifestation of the Watchers, the Second Generation, seeking to return to the physical world. Misapprehension about the first time the group discovered Asakku resulted in the Soul Eater idea popping up - that is, the belief Vicissitude was contracted from a spirit, etc. - but this is explicitly not the case, as it's established the Tzimisce Ante had Vicissitude from the earliest times.

                                                        (If you know your WoD cosmology, reading about the first case of Asakku might get you wondering about connections to the Neverborn Malfeans instead...)
