Supposedly Shaitan — or Baal or Set, depending upon whom you ask — unleashed the 10,000 Djinni as a plague upon the Earth. The sheer magnitude of the problem led to an alliance among various Hebrew, Egyptian and Babylonian priests and demon hunters. However, this group was only an alliance of convenience and, once the threat had passed, the various sects went their separate ways. No group formed solely on the basis of a common enemy has any hope of holding together in the long run. Take away the threat and they fall apart.

                              Originally posted by Rock113 View Post
                              Yeah Mage improvement is broken with Vampire standard >_< a mortal-age one can have elder-level sheer power, a elder-age one can almost destroy Death itself, a ante-age one can really f**k the whole Creation.
                              It probably helps that Mages 1) have a more freeform and spontaneous way of creating needed Effects (b/c of Spheres) that a vampire would need to spend lots of time/XP learning piecemeal, 2) have a time pressure to develop as quickly as possible due to their mortal lifespan, and 3) can benefit from written texts and mentorship that most (non-sorcerous) vampires cannot.

                              So Mages are very suited to burning fast and bright, at least by vampire standards. Vampires, meanwhile, must develop their power slowly. Very few Mages reach the tipping point where they're both growing fast and living forever.

                              There are advantages Vampires have that Mages don't, though. Setting aside Paradox - very generous indeed - Vampires can produce Vampire underlings far faster than Mages can train new Mages or even just Sorcerers. Mages have the bottleneck of natural (or, more rarely, Grimoire-assisted) Awakening to check the rate of them coming into existence. Moreover, they need to then be found, if a given existing Mage is to make use of them. Whereas Vampires can create more Vampires very easily, and have them on hand throughout the whole process. Sorcerers are more plentiful because they don't need to be Awakened, but require years of training to be an equal to a newbie Mage. Whereas Vampires can produce Ghouls as quickly as they can obtain blood.

                              None of this is to say that it's wise for a Vampire to produce loads and loads of Childer and Ghouls at once. It just means they could if they wanted to. And that they will naturally have more minions at their disposal under "normal" circumstances than a Mage will. (On the matter of cultists or mind-controlled minions, this is a wash. Many of the same tricks available to one group are available to the other, so neither pulls ahead in this regard. We're talking about minions who, themselves, have supernatural power.) Combine this with a Vampire's long unlife, and they have more opportunities to build strong power bases, at least until the Mage develops life extension.

                                  Or maybe it could be that it is an innate quality of being a Mage that they can reach the age of 150-300 years without problems? They grow as old as they feel like, some staying young as if in their 20s or 30s, while others stop at an older physical appearance.

                                      Or maybe it could be that it is an innate quality of being a Mage that they can reach the age of 150-300 years without problems? They grow as old as they feel like, some staying young as if in their 20s or 30s, while others stop as an older appearance.
                                      First, the Technocracy is a united organization. Pooling their resources is part of the advantage they have over the frequently fractious Traditions. It's not always possible for Tradition mages to spend time, effort, and resources keeping every single member of their alliance alive.

                                      Second, as an extension of the above, even the Technocracy is not liable to permit these life extension procedures/drugs to just any member of the Union. The fact that they're a big organization means they have far greater control over where resources get allocated. They'd be selective about who gets to have their life extended. If any random Technocrat wants to live longer, they need to make themselves useful enough to the Union that they're worth the time and effort. (That, or the agent is expected to start learning Life procedures themselves, which can distract from other projects or studies that may be more important to them.)

                                      Third, going back to the Traditions, because they're less united, both between and within the nine Traditions, the distribution of Mages who have sufficient ranks in Life to allow for life extension (much less that can perform it on others) is going to be uneven. It's entirely reasonable to assume the Verbana have plentiful access to life extension. Not as much for other Traditions, and in particular factions within those Traditions that focus hard on non-Life Spheres. If you're a Virtual Adept that works mostly with Correspondence and Forces focused Adepts, you'll have to go further afield if you want some quality anti-aging.

                                      Fourth, even where access to life extension exists within a network, that stuff isn't necessarily going to come cheap. Tradition Mages are not only frequently strapped for resources (money, Tass, knowledge, etc), they also have their own work to do. Even Time masters only have so many weeks in a day to attend to someone else's business. Which is not to say you don't see Mages giving each other helping hands, especially for matters of life and death. You're just more likely to see them trading favors or resources. Youth potions are Charms that cost Tass to produce (or else requires the Mage themselves to be on hand to administer, which is even more imposing on their time). That kind of thing isn't cheap. There's inevitably going to be a bottleneck somewhere.

                                      (Plus, not every Mage likes each other. The most readily available Life mage one has may be one's greatest rival.)


                                      None of this is to say that trading life extension doesn't happen. It's just that it's not as easy as it would appear on paper, just looking at the Spheres involved. Whereas with Vampires, so long as the blood keeps rolling in and they aren't killed, Immortality is the default. Being able to live forever isn't a separate problem on top of immediate or long-term survival. They do not have the same bottleneck as Mages, either in knowledge or resources or connections.

                                                      Life extension is one of those things that can vary depending on how its achieved (linear magics might be able to get around the limits True mages have such as through alchemy) and we know of merits that give long life or immortality even without true magic extensions so I don't see it as a hard and fast rule. Heck, 1st edition WOD had Talaq the 'human' assamite (who still had vampire powers) achieve that through his deal with the kabbalist mage (who also got immortality in exchange as I recall). I'm also pretty sure the middle east is a different prospect entirely when it comes to Consensus than the Western world is which is also going to be a factor without the chronological factor (the Consensus related troubles didn't occur until 1000 BCE IIRC, and we didn't see serious problems - the ones that caused the Tremere to investigate vampirism - until ~1000 CE. And that again was just in the western world.) It strikes me there's plenty of wiggle room to explain/justify such things without much problem depending on the sources you're willing to engage (Especially if it involves crossovers of sources.)

                                                      In that same vein, the Vampire vs Mage dispute is also crossover context dependent - especially if you factor in the opinions of 'crossover' comments in various sources (including mage vs Vampire books like Blood Treachery or Red Sign) you face the fundamental differences between the two systems narratively before you get into the mechanics (such as Technocracy being incapable of taking down even one antediluvian collectively despite.. being mages. I feel that alone could argue there's significant differences between the Technocratic union as envisioned by VtM and the Union in Mage proper.)
