    The Conclave of Prague

    This entry, by Dalia Nakache, opens with section that thematically connects to the last section, Specifically, about vampires who may want to just hunt and do their thing and generally ignore larger sect politics. Basically she says no one – Anarchs included – can just be hunters anymore because of the Camarilla Inner Circle.

    In short, as the Second Inquisition emerged as a threat, the Camarilla began feeding it specific information on the Anarchs and the Sabbat.

    When the SWAT team comes to knock on your door, they're there because a bunch of aged assholes half the world away decided you were expendable.
    -Nakache
    This is probably more hyperbole than literal fact. Consider, this assumes the vampires in question have something as specific as a recognized address, which the Camarilla leadership knows and can turn over to the SI. I don’t think I’ve ever played a vampire who had a haven in the form of an apartment or anything so mundane. The last one I played dwelled in the ruins of abandoned high school. Also, why would a vampire tell others about their havens – or at least why would they not lie about their havens?

    Even so, the rhetoric stands. Worldwide some surprisingly well trained, well informed, and well equipped hunters began killing vampires. And a SWAT team raid during the middle of the day could easily wreck some neonates or even ancilla foolish enough to just sleep in the closet of an apartment or duplex. Nakache argues this is why Bell killed Hardestadt at the Conclave of Prague in 2012, which led to the larger Brujah defection from the Camarilla and to the Anarch. Bell has never specified why he did it, which just leaves it all open for speculation – and lets Storytellers fill in the details.

    Nakache is also critical of viewing elders as masters of the jyhad and manipulation, creatures capable of foreseeing and controlling everything. She argues rightly that the blowback from the elders response to the SI specifically triggered the murder of Hardestadt, the Brujah defection, strengthening the Anarchs, and so on. These are probably not things the elders wanted, at least not all those events.

    Nakache says that the Camarilla no longer has “no claim to universality,” but they Anarch do. This is debatable. The Camarilla never really claimed universality, or dominion over the Giovanni, Settites, Ravnos, and Banu Haqim. I don’t think they now try to claim Dominion over the Brujah and Gangrel. So this is a bit of rhetorical bluster, which sounds more better to an Anarch than to a Camarilla vampire.

    However, she does make a solid point, which has game possibilities, about Camarilla withdrawing and abandoning territory.

    So what does this mean for you? In many cities, the Camarilla has had to retreat, only controlling part of the territory, leaving the rest to Anarchs. Many of their holdings in the mortal world have been abandoned, and if you're smart enough you can find them and take them over. After all, the banker addicted to vampire Blood doesn't really have the option of saying no to you if you're her only hope of avoiding blood withdrawal.
    -Nakache
    Finding such weaknesses, and claiming them and exploiting them, and fighting to hold them, could easily be the subject of an entire campaign for an Anarch pack.

            Untitled Section

            This bit has no title, and doesn’t appear in the table of contents – which I think has to be an error.

            Anyway, Hesha Ruhadze writes about how the Ministry (formally the Followers of Set) almost joined the Camarilla. However, he also writes an observation about how the retreat of the Camarilla has opened up new areas of war between the elders and the youth. This thematically connects to the last entry, which more or less said the same thing.

            The elders are as deadly as ever, but the war between the old and the young acts as a convenient distraction if you want to pursue other goals altogether.
            -Ruhadze
            Ruhadze does not get into what he thinks of as the weakness of the Camarilla, though he suggests it is cowardice (probably among other flaws). He also does not specify what those “other goals” might be. He does appear to be resigned to his (temporary) fate as an Anarch, following the Ministry joining the movement. Ruhadze, 400 year old survivor and corruptor, is also savvy enough to know the Ministry is stronger among the Anarchs than it would be on its own when it comes to dealing with the SI.

            Ruhadze argues that for many elders, personal relationships will trump sect allegiance. So vampires (Ventrue, Tremere) who are BFF with Brujah will probably remain BFF even after the events of the Prague Conclave. This is debatable I think, given how betrayal is second nature to vampires of all clans and ages.

            However, he also says…

            This has always been the rule in high level sect politics: The rules apply to the young, not to the old.
            -Ruhadze
            Which is to say, the rules do not apply to people with power.

            He circles back to an earlier point about the burgeoning war between Anarchs and the Camarilla (and the SI by implication) will provide a lot of chaos he can exploit. But he does not specify what he wants.

            He probably remains a Theophidian, which is to say someone who follows the doctrine of Set. This doctrine is something of a cross between Gnosticism and Sith philosophy. Pursuing these goals, and teaching the philosophy to the Anarch will define his goals (and probably the goals of other members of the Ministry) in general terms, but not specific ones. What this means specifically is up to the Storyteller.

            As an aside, Ruhadze is a Georgian (as in the nation) surname. But Hesha himself supposedly comes from Nubia, or in southern Egypt and northern Sudan (as modern nations). Which is to say it is a long ways from the nation on the Black Sea.

            Edit: I wonder what the Path of Typhon would look like in the V5 moral system.
