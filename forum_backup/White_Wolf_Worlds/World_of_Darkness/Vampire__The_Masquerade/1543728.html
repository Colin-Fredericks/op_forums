    Is Koldunic Sorcery OP?

    10-14-2024, 12:37 AM
    In a lot of the games I have come across online everyone always seems to think/say that koldunic sorcery is really powerful. What is the consensus here? Do you think it’s over powered? And how have you dealt with it at your table as either a player or storyteller?

      Koldunic Sorcery is elemental magic based on spirits
      You don't learn Koldunic Sorcery by sitting in your dusty library studying scrolls and tomes like with Blood Magic, you have to go out and work and learn "on the field"
      Literally
      You slowly become acquainted with the spirits of the land, you learn what makes these entities tick, what they like and dislike, what piques their interest and how they can be called for aid or manipulated
      You make deals
      Unlike other sorceries that make use of Power of Will and formula or a blasphemy Shrine to a supposedly Godly Patron or systematic violation of taboos kaldunism makes use of environment and Lore - Tales and stories, floklore based superstitions passed from mother to child
      There are some rules variations here with some books insisting that Koldunism is a separate knowledge others treating it as an occult specialty and others treating it as a regular discipline requiring an attribute plus an occult roll
      But the theme reamins - it is a knowledge you have to work for
      Depending on the type of spirit being called to aid the practice itself is based on lore on the practitioner's familiarity and connection with spirits and ability to manipulate them
      Koldunism is the closest in Vampire how the Garou operate
      To learn their Gifts and Rites a Garou basically have to do the same what a Koldun have to do to learn - became familiar with the Spirits, explore the Land, respect and honor the land, the traditions, the history and make bargains and deals, earn the trust and fear of the Spirits and pay their price

      Overpowered? Maybe
      In the right hand
      But to be a Koldun, you have to know the proper "protocol" when you deal with Spirits.
      The Storyteller and players can get really creative with that protocol since it isn't described in detail in any of the books.
      It can consist of gestures or sigils or sounds verbal commands in dead languages maybe instruments that resonate with the spirit being called forth but the procedure will always differ from element to element
      One does not call out to living flame in the same way they call out to the spirits of the wind!
      Understanding the proper way and the safe way to interact with the Spirits and enlist their aid without rousing their wrath can take decades and depends a lot on the region
      The spirits themselves - though they might be of the same element the Koldun is already familiar with - differ from land to land
      The water spirits of the Black Sea are not the same as the water spirits of the Mediterranean Sea or those of a river like the Volga

      Among the Tzimisce who are tied to their Land the Koldun have the deepest connection and on a strange, unfamilar land far from their home they have tremendous difficulities to use their magic - sometimes it is impossible to properly use their ways - untill they became familiar with the local spirits and bargained and made deals to get on their good side

      So Koldunism could be considered overpowered yes
      But it is well balanced by how you should use it, learn it and basically build your unlife around it
      It is not like Thaumaturgy or Necromancy (tho' necromancy is the closest since you have to do the rituals and the legwork, gather information about the Wraiths and Specters like how a Koldun works with Spirits and sometimes Banes but the difference is the approach: a Koldun and the Spitits are working together, a Necromancer and her ghost are a slave and master relationship based on hatred and threats) where you pack up your books and just move... when a Koldun is forced to abbandon her domain that is going back to square one basically and start everything from scratch.

      (Not to mention if your ST is in to it a Koldun should attract the attention to the local Garou/Lupine community (and more often than not a Koldun could do a better job than the Claws of Gaia witch could lead to very funny situations where the Spirits themselves smack the nose of the wolfs to "hands off of my Partner" and could lead to rivalries or strange bedfellow teamups against serious dark spirits) )​

                  It also really depends on which form of "Koldunic Sorcery" you mean. There are the Old ways with Kraina and what are essentially spirits gifts/rites instead of rituals, and then there are the New ways which are much closer to thaumaturgy with a more elemental focus.

                  New Koldunism, I have to say no for the same reason I think thaumaturgy is under-powered despite the bad rep, it's a massive XP sink to do anything useful and is often weaker than equivalent XP spent in disciplines.

                  Old Koldunism is actually a solid "Maybe", but it's not a given thing. Old rites can be really strong and cost Blood+WP to learn rather than XP, but they also come with their own internal balancing in the form of bans. Either way I like this style better, having to gain weaknesses to gain more powers is actually a really cool idea for vampire powers.

                                  Originally posted by Shadeprowler View Post
                                  You slowly become acquainted with the spirits of the land, you learn what makes these entities tick, what they like and dislike, what piques their interest and how they can be called for aid or manipulated
                                  Like all powers that require a much more active ST to adjudicate things on the fly, it can be very easy for the ST to not portray it right and just give too much discretion to the PC so that other players start complaining about that character being overpowered.

                                  Even in Werewolf, the spirit world can put a lot of extra burden onto the ST. The ST needs to create another world of spirits out there, figure out spirit broods, character reputations, determine what they want and so on. I've seen a lot of Werewolf games where the ST just handwaves this - despite it supposedly being a critical element of the Werewolf setting - because it's too much work, they don't feel adequately prepared to roleplay it, and players may not demand it or worse, heavily disagree with the ST's approach. So a lot of the supposed complexity and balance of the game just gets thrown out.

                                  So to put such a thing in vampire, where that aspect of the setting doesn't come into play and many STs have no experience in running, seems like it is asking for trouble.

                                  It's very easy to say in an online forum, "oh, this is balanced by the ST making additional roleplaying requirements" than actually running the game that way.

                                  I've seen the same thing apply to Necromancy and ghosts in the underworld. STs often just default to "yes, you can do that" because they are already busy just with trying to master the Vampire setting, and don't have the same time or interest to put in the effort to understanding the underworld (whether it's the official Wraith version or a homebrew one). Or if other players complain too much, then all of a sudden the PC can't seem to do anything with the power. I think Astral Projection is also similar with the Astral Plane being handwaved because the setting doesn't provide much guidance to the ST.

                                  When I first played Vampire games, I had zero interest in learning about the other games or involving their elements into my Vampire chronicles. There was already so much lore about Vampire itself that I could barely keep track of that. It took a long time before I got to the point where I wanted to begin including select elements in my vampire games like having a haunted house, willing to deal with spirits for various blood magic, perhaps a dangerous area of town patrolled by Lupines, etc. My first attempts were not very successful in getting the right balance of doing it right and having the game be fun. I had to put in a lot more thinking of how to handle things.

                                  I do agree with your statements, but I also wanted to point out that this is not as easy in practice as it may sound.
