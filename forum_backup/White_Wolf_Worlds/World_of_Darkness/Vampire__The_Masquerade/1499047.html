    Embracing a sorcerer

    12-07-2022, 06:12 AM
    I'm not sure how to do this but now I've got a game coming up where it's an issue, so I'm going to need a rule on it. Do they keep their paths as a separate ability paid for with out of clan costs? Do I translate it to equivalent thaumaturgical paths? Do they just lose sorcery altogether? I think they just loose it, as Sorcerers and Mages who became clans generally used their knowledge to build new blood sorcery disciplines, like Thaumaturgy and Necromancy, so should that translate to not having your mortal sorcery but gaining an affinity for an equivalent vampiric sorcery/path?

      There's never been an official answer on how exactly to handle this.

      Technically, if you Embrace a sorcerer, they lose their Paths and sucks to be them. However, in the past the books have recognized that this is not fun for most games where the sorcerer is either a PC or a central NPC. The usual solution is to convert the sorcerer's Paths into XP (or freebies) they can then use on Disciplines and supernatural Merits that help replicate some of their previous mystic skills and affinities.

      From a dramatic/narrative standpoint, it usually a good idea to avoid blood sorcery and necromancy during this conversion to help keep to the idea that the Embrace is a massive shock to the system and the now fledgling is going to have to relearn their former skills over time via training in blood magic. Focusing on common Disciplines and Merits is better for the idea that the Embrace shaped some of that magical affinity into a vampiric form. A sorcerer skilled in scrying having Auspex, or mental magic gaining dots in Dominate and Presence for example.

                          My take is that if vampires can perform normal sorcery then it invalidates the idea of Blood Magic because it is a needless duplicate. Why invent it at all if Tremere (or any other mortal magician) could keep using their old Sorcery Paths anyway?

                          Originally the entire idea behind the Tremere was that they knew magic was going to die, and wanted to preserve their powers in a more hostile environment to magic. That was kind of achieved when Mage established the idea of Paradox. But the proliferation of Sorcery Paths (especially Paths that have effects that would clearly be impacted by Paradox if it was Sphere magic) really invalidated that. Now saying that Sorcery Paths can just be used by vampires really make the entire Tremere backstory incredibly stupid - not just that they became vampires to preserve their magical abilities, but also the idea that they had to relearn everything and recreate new ways to perform those tasks.

                          Having said that, most Blood Sorcery Paths and Sorcery Paths are either the same mechanically or practically the same. It should not be that hard for the new vampire to "relearn" how to do the same magic, but now using vitae as its source of magical power. It will just take a certain amount of study and effort, and if you have access to an existing body of knowledge (like the Tremere do), that learning curve will not be long.

                          For NPCs, I'd strip them of their Sorcery Paths and force them to relearn what they want to do as Thaumaturgy. If they are among the Tremere or some other organization that can assist with that, they should be able to adapt very quickly. If they are on their own, it may take them years, decades, or longer to relearn everything. That is mainly just flavor and setting detail.

                          But what about for PCs? PCs don't want to lose the XP they invested, start from scratch, and so on. It won't seem fair to them. In their case, I'd translate any Sorcery Path they knew to their Blood Magic equivalent. If there is not an equivalent, then they lose that Path, but I'd refund them the XP they lost by that. While I would want to provide an IC explanation for them "relearning" their Sorcery to Thaumaturgy, I would not want to waste time in the game to do so, and would want to keep them happy as a player. So I'd talk it over with the player to see what we could come up. Ideally there would be certain amount of time in terms of preludes before he starts playing the game, downtime activity, or perhaps a schedule where after so much time IC certain powers could come back online if the player is willing. But if the player does not want to roleplay that and simply have access to his powers immediately, I'd bite the bullet and make him happy.


                          (As an aside, in my own chronicles I rule that Sorcery Paths whose effects would be something that would invalidated by Paradox if it was Sphere magic always requires the use of an actual magical resource (Tass) that is consumed. So it is a declining resource. Sorcerers may know Paths that actually do not work for them because they do not have any Tass available to make the magic work. Certain Paths still continue to work fine without the need for Tass because there is no Paradox issues with it. But if you want to use Hellfire, Flight, or some other power that would make people go "that's magic!" if they saw it, then that Sorcerer better have a source of something that provides Tass for them. The benefit of being a vampire who knows Thaumaturgy is that vitae is Tass and a vampire always has it, so it avoids that problem of a declining resource.)

                                          This is a question that is actually answered in the original Sorcerer supplement from 1997 (p.54-56).

                                          If a Sorcerer becomes a Mage or Vampire, they can use their dots in Paths and Rituals to either buy Spheres or Blood Magic Paths on a 1-to-1 ratio. Or else cash them in for Freebie Points. The book even includes a chart showing what Sorcerer Paths could turn into. (It also suggests Arcanoi for Wraiths, in a narrative sense, but is very clear that all dots or XP are lost upon death, requiring a start from square one).

                                          This transition is not instantaneous. Rather requiring a period of adjustment, as the former Sorcerer comes to terms with their new state of being and adapts their old methods to the new supernatural framework that governs them. The book gives the timeframe as "six months or more".

                                          There's also a list of suggestions for how the more specialist splats might or might not adapt Sorcery. But it needs to be taken with a grain of salt, as it includes characters of World of Darkness: [Ethnic Slur] among the choices. I'm honestly surprised White Wolf still hadn't disowned that book by the time they were developing Sorcerer.

                                                  Originally posted by Muad'Dib View Post
                                                  This is an interesting change in the basic / normal rules. Are there any other noteworthy differences, challanges, benefits, or advantages for Sorcerors in your chronicles?
                                                  I've only used Sorcerers as NPCs. They can make great antagonists, mentors, allies, contacts, rivals, etc. The players have always had standard PCs for whatever game. While I keep Mage metaphysics, I personally prefer to use Sorcerers as NPCs as they better fit what I need those NPCs to do. The changes I made was just to preserve the general idea in early WoD that "magic was dying", keep the Sorcerer rules intact, but not allow them to be better in way than Mages by avoiding Paradox. If they could do that with static magic, I wanted it to cost them and not always work. So limiting the Paths that always work to things that could be ignored or discounted by Sleepers was good. I still wanted the option to use the more blatant magical Paths, but keep it consistent with what I thought better fit the setting, so making them dependent on some form of Tass to create these effects seemed obvious to me.

                                                  It also provides a source of conflict if needed. If vampire vitae allows vampires to use Thaumaturgy, then it can also be used by Sorcerers as Tass to do the same for example. It isn't the only source of Tass out there, but some Sorcerers might want to exploit one or more vampires to gain access to that. Likewise, being able to harvest a tooth or fang from a Garou, or steal a real Fetter from a Wraith can provide real benefits to a Sorcerer.

                                                  I also think it flavors the game a lot to show certain Sorcerers dependent on "components". Someone who knows Shapechanging can be dependent on only one form by that old wolfskin magically prepared centuries again and require him to create a salve using wolfsbane, henbane, etc. Steal grave soil if you actually want to be able to cross over into the Shadowlands or force a ghost to actually become visible in the Skinlands, and so on. Know when harvesting certain plants in the right conditions and times actually produce usable Tass rather than just having normal plants. I try to depict magic more like how the Call of Cthulhu encourages you to depict spells rather than D&D style, "I cast it and the effect happens". It does require more work as an ST, but this is an aspect of the game that I really like and have access to a lot of great books like the old Time-Life series The Enchanted World.

                                                  Having said that, I don't think many players would like it if they wanted to play Sorcerers as PCs because it's a handicap and players tend to not like that. It's more important for them to get stuff done and accomplish things in game, rather than concentrating on creating neat flavor text. I certainly would not allow a Sorcerer PC alongside a standard vampire coterie or werewolf pack of PCs. But if I ever ran a mortals only game, I think it could work, and would be a good way to balance out a Sorcerer PC versus those PCs who might only have a single Numina or just be your standard detective, antiquarian, etc. with no powers at all.

                                                          Sorcerer Revised had the Blood Magic flaw (erroneously labeled a Merit). Where the character needed to use their blood (in the form of unsoakable Bashing damage) in order to cast spells.

                                                          One of the potential explanations for the flaw was having learned magic from a vampire, or having plundered their books. Meaning, at least in regards to linear practitioners, if you were taught sorcery from a vampire, physical and/or metaphysical requirements peculiar to the vampiric condition color the Sorcerer's arts. A Mage could have the flexibility to invent a Rote that circumvents the more grisly or bloody aspects of vampiric magic, but a Sorcerer may not have that option. At least, not without buying off the flaw later.

                                                          The flaw also states that, owing to the sanguine nature of the character's magic, their spells have a dark or "martyred" nature. This, more than anything, would probably carry over even into a Mage's castings, depending on how closely they're replicating the vampire's methods and/or the nature of the desired Effect.
