    V20 - Potence + Vicissitude combo in a way I hadn't noticed!

    06-09-2021, 08:07 PM
    Spend 1 blood to activate Potence for the turn. Strength checks now get automatic successes equal to your Potence.

    Bonecraft an enemy. Strength + Medicine check.

    Assuming you are Generation 9 or lower, you can activate both powers and greatly increase your odds of scoring 5 or more successes. If that works the target loses half of their blood pool instantly as their ribs pierce their heart.

    Against a typical enemy this is a waste of time since with good Potence chances are you could just take them out with some heavy hits. But against a "boss" enemy like a recently risen Methuselah, cutting their blood down by half could greatly weaken them.

    Just a combo I'd never considered before, somewhat relevant to Tzimisce with Potence and Blood Brothers with Vicissitude for instance.

                                                      Just a suggestion here, but I house rule combat applications of Vicissitude thus:

                                                      Starting at level 2, characters with Vicissitude can make attacks with it that deal lethal damage (even at level 2 you can do something like rip a muscle or organ out, or punch a hole through their torso). It resolves as an unarmed attack, but the character substitutes their Body Crafts rating for weapon damage. These attacks can be healed with blood expenditure regardless of generation limits unlike other, more deliberate, usage of Vicissitude as a balancing factor.

                                                      At Vicissitude 3, the "blood pool halved" effect is just a bonus effect for having dealt five lethal post-soak.

                                                      Is this nasty with Potence? Well...yeah. So is everything else in the realm of melee combat (and thrown weapons). That's kind of Potence's thing, it's just part of the game.

                                                      Bit of a post-script on retrospection: Vicissitude is an open-ended, broad, and inconsistent enough discipline ST's really have to get their hands dirty at times to get what they want out of it, thematically and mechanically. And, a lot of its power derives from how much freedom ST's grant PC's and NPC's to get the most out of it. In the big scheme of things, it's really not that far off in power and application from other disciplines or combinations thereof (ask me how my favorite discipline combo in the game is Obtenebration and Melpominee), but it takes a lot of finesse on a ST's part to get it from its baseline to where they really want it to be.

                                                      Contrary to a lot of opinions, that's not necessarily a bad thing.

                                                          Vicissitude is one of those "Mother May I?" powers that really gets obnoxious quick as you try to keep up with player creativity. When used by NPC's, it can have as much power as you want it to have ("Oh well you see, he's reinforced his bones with metal and has two layers of graphite underneath his skin"). And that open-endedness is all upside. But when a player gets it, you have to suddenly figure out hard limits that the power doesn't really give. You could state that it does what it says it does, nothing more, but there are a host of NPC Tzimisce who have done all kinds of wild things in the setting, so that breaks immersion if you can't give yourself extra arms or create super ghouls. Sometimes this can be explained away by Devotions or "Elder Powerz" but not always. So in a way, it's kind of like having a Sphere of Magic in Mage, and you have to work out all the guidelines yourself. A big book of Vicissitude Do's and Don'ts would have been nice at some point. Allowing it to be vicious is fine, most Disciplines can be, in the right circumstances (sorry, Fortitude, you're still bad). But as always, Potence and Celerity become an issue when they become a force multiplier for a given Discipline, much how I'm sure many first-time Storytellers freak out when Potence is combined with Protean for the first time "What do you mean, you just did 8 levels of aggravated to the Sheriff?!". Blood Sorcery, Dominate, Presence, Dementation, and the like don't (usually) get that kind of benefit, forcing you to be extra careful. I've run Vicissitude with fairly few limits on what it can do, and the results have been fairly amazing, quickly turning the Chronicle into a superhero game. Which was fine at the time, but in my current Chronicle, I've massively weakened the Tzimisce (and those pesky Lasombra) to keep things more 'street level'. So yes, by the rules, Potence (and Celerity) can make Vicissitude more dangerous. Whether or not you're alright with that is a decision the Storyteller has to make.
