    What is the genre of your vampire games?

    05-15-2021, 12:48 PM
    For me, they always come in these flavors:

    Gothic Punk is when you're a penniless Brujah and his runaway Goth girl Tremere girlfriend of 12th and 13th generation respectively living in an abandoned hotel because the Ventrue Prince murdered your mortal families after you revealed you were vampires to them. You now plot to get your revenge but need, first, to be hired by the Primogen to go sabotage the art gallery of stuffed human corpses (faked of course ) displayed by their rivak.

    Gothic Horror is when you are a Toreador Embraced by a mad old Elder as their concubine who has successfully staked them, leaving them to burn, only to discover that you actually are not the person you remember them to be. No, your memory of your past was erased and you must use your wits and charm to pretend to be the monster you were made to be while trying to discover if you had a past at all.

    Urban Fantasy is when your characters enter a strange and fascinating world of vampires, demons, werewolves, and magic living under the surface of the mortal world. The point is not horror but to appreciate the beauty, terror, and weirdness of the surreal. The World of Darkness as a whole tends to be invoked here. Marco the Malkavian is an occult investigator who follows clues only he can see to find missing girls stolen by Boogeymen, solve the passions of wraiths, and investigate the secret of Nod's mysteries.

    Gothic Punk Urban Fantasy Horror is when you're named Bloodrage Stonecold, 7th generation Caitiff with Celerity, Daimonion, and Thaumaturgy armed with two silver plated pistols that shoot explosive rounds while wearing a bandolier of incendiary grenades. Plus, you have a katana that is also silver plated. You exist to hunt werewolves, Sabbat, and giant flesh monsters. You follow the Path of Badassitude that has the central tenets of "Fuck this shit."

    I admit, I pretend to do the first two primarily but always end up with Stonecold.

    Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

                      My games are a highly specific sub-genre called: Vampire the Masquerade.

                      Larger genres and their influence are never entirely constant enough. They shift in and out of focus as the flow of the story reaches various beats.

                      I found out, quite some time ago, to stop trying to force myself, my games, and the games I play in, into hitting some genre. Genres are vague for a reason, and generally only applicable after the fact.

                              My V20 Chronicle? EXTREMELY Urban Fantasy. Admittedly, this chronicle was also conceived as a mostly episodic chain of adventures/plot-arcs, which would cause the tone to depend heavily on the scenario. First Episode ("Blood in the Gutters") was meant to be low-key and gritty---the Coterie was ordered to resolve a string of murders which had the police scrutinizing a popular rack/hunting ground too closely. Although even that ended with the players briefly communicating with a Wyrm Spirit. Second Episode ("A Dream of Red") saw the Coterie stranded inside a sorcerer's astral Demesne---exploring a seemingly endless temple-spire; matching wits with djinn; freeing a Mummy's ba; and coming away with a magic greatsword for their trouble.

                              I admit my V20 game is very, very weird (although I think "A Dream of Red" will be the high water-mark for a long time to come).

                                  We almost exclusively play with the DAV20 Rules in various historic settings.

                                  Urban Fantasy might be the correct genre by your definition OP, although direct crossovers a far and few between in my chronicles but in the constraints of the Vampire Lore no holds are barred. Of course I take some creative freedom to make it fit a bit more seemlesly

                                  For example I did a Chronicle with the Bath Domain from DAV20 Companion and made some vague crossover with the arthurian legend. A ventrue elder named Lucius Artorius Castus (Arthur) rises from torpor and rallies his faithful cainite "Knights" and the local Lhiannan under the leadership of an Elder named Myrddin (basically a version of Merlin). They are branded heretics by the mythraic Cult and a threat to the domain. The players are thrown into the conflict. Maybe choosing sides. Maybe choosing no side. Their decision.

                                  My players tend to play very scolarly characters who like to explore every nook and cranny of the setting.

                                                      My setting is definitely Gothic Punk-- although Gothic massively predominates and the "punk" elements tend to be a little more gang-driven than traditional punk. My stories are very heavily political in terms of Camarilla politics. Even the parts of the stories that aren't political take place within the milieu of vampire politics, and players can never lose sight of the political ramifications of their actions.

                                                      I suppose that would make my game something like 65% political intrigue thriller (with explosions of Bourne-like violence) and 35% novel of manners.

                                                      In Phipps' terms, it's definitely more Gothic Horror with just a touch of Urban Fantasy.

                                                          As far away from convention, genre, and trope as possible. I stick to one too reliably, my players start being able to predict my story beats, and if they become able to predict my story beats they can plan in advance and/or eventually get bored.

                                                          If I have to turn a stomach-churning fomori encounter or a meeting with a methuselah into a Monty Python-esque non sequitur-laden comedy of errors, by god I will if it keeps my players on their toes.

                                                          One of my more recent TC stories in The Before Times involved the PC's cooking up this scheme to infiltrate Mediasch with a Tzimisce fleshcrafted to look like Nova Arpad, to kill and replace her...along with a handful of other Anarch packs who had the exact same idea and nobody thought to coordinate their efforts. Meaning there were like six Nova Arpads running around Mediasch all trying to kill each other, none of which was the actual Nova Arpad.

                                                          EDIT: Well...I shouldn't say "nobody" thought to coordinate their efforts. None of them did. Nova Arpad, on the other hand, was absolutely happy to take up the job for them.
