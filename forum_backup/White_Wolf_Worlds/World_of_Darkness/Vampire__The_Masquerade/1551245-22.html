                    Let's try this again...
                    Originally posted by MrornX View Post

                    Honestly I half suspect that white wolf would take some inspiration from the latest COD game approach where they terminate the unpopular plot lines with extreme prejudice.

                    Cult of Frenris, couped by moderates they apparently didn't purged and are reduced to a bunch of rouge packs, Pentex leadership was like wise coupled by hard core wyrm cultists and all the followers of the fly are dead or in hiding.                     Honestly, heavy-handed retcons were a big part of why the V5 changes turned a lot of people off. Same with Revised, though on a different scale—most of the big changes didn't come in the CRB (at least for VTM), so they could manage expectations a bit better.

                    Revised primed us for the Final Nights with the blurb. The CRB snuck the 14th Generation in the back and laid little clues throughout (such as the Scourge), helping along by the general buzz of the impending millennium in popular culture. The default setting, however, was intact with only a few minor changes. It wasn't until The Time of Thin-Blood that things started getting blown up. And that's when most of the gripes about that edition began.

                    V5 not only packed a surprising lot of metaplot into the CRB to "update" the setting, it also packed in a lot of explosive changes (sometimes quite literally). It attempted to move beyond the weight of the metaplot not by ignoring it, but by setting it on fire. That irked a lot of people.

                    W5 course-corrected by ignoring the prior metaplot, but it also removed the default setting of prior editions and initially didn't follow it up with any clear communication of metaplot or setting afterwards, either. So I don't envy them the task of making a W6 that follows on from W20 and W5. That's probably why they're not tackling it straight away.

                    But since W5 was intentionally its own thing, it doesn't have to "count" towards any kind of canon. That's a pretty attractive get-out-of-jail-free card. It remains the easiest option. It's a bit harder to do that with V6, since a) VTM has always been the most popular gameline so any edition is bound to have some fans, and b) V5 did (sometimes) consciously tie into V20, so there's already some sense of continuity.

                                            GreatHornedRatkin those plots don't have the same weight and development in a form we can lump them in a same trend:

                                            Salubri - limited to seven, and mostly kept at that. When new Salubri groups were added they just extended the same concept to new regions. There are seven in the Ashirra, an undefined but low number in Africa, and another undefined but low in East Asia. This only changed in V5. They were never deemed destroyed.

                                            Cappadocians - kept down for a long time, they only got back in Revised for two reasons: their complete wipe out doesn't make sense and the Dark Ages line really gave them a lot of fans even among players that don't otherwise touch Dark Ages.

                                            Tremere Antitribu - only got wiped out in Revised and it was always a contentious plot. Only V5 would reverse it, though, 20 years later.

                                            Ravnos - mostly whipped in Time of Thin Blood and the immediate backlash made them soft- retcon a little each time. Even then, never said to be completely wiped out, and the original event was never retconned, just the extent of the killings was revised.

                                            Camazots - the possibility that some survived deep in the Umbra was there since the beginning. It's not a change of mind, it's their design as setting element.

                                            Gurahl and Mokolé - never wiped out, both are presented from start as low in numbers, able to appear out of nowhere and holding an ancient grudge as if it was yesterday. Again, no change of mind, their mechanics for this are there from inception and consistent.

                                            Grondr - never recovered. As setting elements the Skull Pigs are actually older, and the notion that they are descendants of the Grondr in some way is a wild theory with little water or game attention, it isn't likely or important.
