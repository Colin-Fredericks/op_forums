      While they certainly feed into each other, I think they are two concretely different concepts. Finance just represents the ability to operate within the financial world. Resources represent the actual liquid and capital resources at your disposal. I would let my players build their Resources using Finance-based projects, but if they had finance without resources, they would just be Wall Street wizards without the ability to play the game.

              TL;DR: Yes, But...


              The answer depends somewhat on three questions/factors, IMO:

              1) How much money/Resources do you want?

              2) How quickly do you want to acquire them?

              3) How much risk are you willing to accept?


              One of my coworkers recently brought up the concept of "three pillars" when it comes to projects (in this case, he was talking about cosplay): Quality, Time, and Cost. If you want something good, and you want it fast, then it will be expensive; if you want something good, and you want it cheap, then it will take more time; and if you want something cheap, and you want it fast, then it's going to be lower quality.

              I think you can take an analogous approach to this question.

              You can make money quickly, without too much risk, but you probably won't be making a lot of it. Someone with Finance 4 can probably land a job that will get them Resources 2 or 3 fairly quickly, but that's not really 'big money'.

              You can make a lot of money without taking too many risks, but it's probably going to take you a long time. From the perspective of a character in a game, this means that you might spend an entire chronicle slowly building up your wealth, but you may not have the Resources you need when you need them.

              If you're making a lot of money quickly, there's probably going to be some kind of risk involved. 'Risk' is probably the most nebulous concept here, and it can be approached from a few different angles. You might be putting a lot of your money into high-risk-high-reward investments, and you might end up losing some of the Resources you already have if those investments go poorly. Or you might make enemies, whether they be people who lost money to you, or even just business rivals who see you as a potential threat. And if you're making a lot of money really fast, there's a good chance that the authorities are going to be paying attention; even if you aren't breaking any laws, that level of scrutiny could be a threat to the Masquerade for a Kindred. Said scrutiny could also be a problem if 'risk' takes the form of bending or breaking the law to make your millions.

              Separate from the points made above, there's also the consideration that time your character is spending on improving their Resources is time that isn't being spent on the rest of the story. A character that just chooses to start with Resources 4 doesn't need to split their attention between their investments and the actual plot of the chronicle.

                  Based on the answers so far and my own previous thoughts on this, I think it would be very possible to devise a system to grow Resources through Finance (or Influence through Politics, or Fame through a suitable skill like Performance, etc.). And I think it would make the game more interesting: these are already things that characters try to do and are part of vampiric unlife. I don't get excited when it's all handled just by negotiation with the ST and purely narratively, so I'd find the game more interesting with systems like these in place.

                  Obviously the systems need to account for what is needed in the real world to achieve these goals: knowledge is not enough, you also need to find the right people, the right resources, etc., but knowledge makes you aware of this and of where and how you're more likely to get these things, so it's always the basis.

                  Aside from the skill dots, the system would factor in background dots that can be of help and maybe give the chance to take risks by accepting flaws for greater opportunities, ex.: if I accept to make an Enemy worth X dots in the process, the rolls will be made easier in a proportionate way. Botches may produce unintended flaws while yielding no benefit.

                  Valuable assets are likely to be targeted by others: if my high Resources are due to a coveted job, there are probably others out there who want my position; if they are due to something else, it can be threatened too. In a Dark Ages game, my castle and lands may be attacked. Analogous things happen today. This offers great opportunities for story hooks and to check players who are feeling a bit too secure, and it gives a realistic sense of why young vampires are unlikely to hold onto high backgrounds unless they're under someone's protection.

                  Finally, being able to increase background dots through rolls + roleplay means being more accepting of when they get lost through the same system, and STs can decide how much play time to give to scenes related to these efforts from the characters and how much to keep in the background to preserve the flow of the main story.

                      To begin with, you don't, yet you do if you want to have it.

                      I'll break my reasoning down in sections:

                      ------------------------------------------
                      HOW IT WORKS IRL
                      ------------------------------------------

                      Just to be clear: I work at a bank and have a few finance-related certifications myself. I also, of course, know a lot of people with varying degrees of financial knowledge. I'm not saying anyone should take what I say at face value because of that, but just to be clear on from where I'm coming with this.

                      You need money to make money.

                      Our common depiction of a financial wiz who builds a fortune, or even just live off of it, is based on very few and extremely circumstantial cases. I'm not talking people that are quite good with it, as this is certainly something the characters are entitled to be if they have the dots, but really people who had a lot of non-obvious advantages that others with the same or superior skills didn't.

                      So people that would have 3 to 5 dots in this Ability in game terms, yet can't make ends meet, are common, as are those at just the next rungs of the social ladder. It isn't really much more likely for people with such skill to get better pay than people with any other Ability at a similar level.

                      The idea that skill is all that you need to get a good job is nonsense, especially in this area. It certainly helps, but the job offer isn't really on par with the demand, and selection isn't just an imperfect process, it's full on full of bullshit that makes a lot of such process just as reliable as a coin toss or even less (there are employers that consistently chose terrible candidates).

                      Which, by the way, brings us to the problem that this is one of the jobs least affected by skill, although we can handwave this a little in the fiction of the game. But we shouldn't handwave the extreme difficulty at evaluating skill in this area, even through past performance, as I'll explore in another section.

                      In the end you either have a job or a lot of money to begin with, and skill alone won't give you either. Actually a lot of people in better positions and more recognized than you by their skills may be fairly below your level, yet they're successful where you're not.

                      Finally, for whatever it's worth, in this area you can reasonably expect a well managedinvestment, whatever form it takes, to have a return of about 10% a year above inflation. It's variable, of course, but overall this is a valid rule-of-thumb benchmark all across the world, and while extraordinary gains happen from time to time, anything above this by a large margin shouldn't be taken as more than either a seasonal opportunity or dumb luck. And that basically means you'll hardly earn enough to change your lifestyle from financial skill alone.

                      ------------------------------------------
                      LITERARY FRAMEWORK
                      ------------------------------------------

                      This is the World of Darkness, our worsened mirror. Things shouldn't be fair.

                      Corporate environments where nepotism, bad faith and systemic incompetence define the totem pole, abuse and hopelessness dominate the processes, and gains come from hidden crime and immorality instead of actual, honest work, aren't just a very real thing but also a staple of literature, especially this type of gritty genre.

                      However you think about your real chances at getting a better job just by being more skilled, in the WoD people shouldn't expect to get anything out of their effort, talent and skill alone. Either the system unfairly helps you, or you'll have to break it down. This is punk 101.

                      On top of that, overall a starting character sheet is also a statement about expectations for the character, at least for the time being. If a player makes a skilled character that's down on the dumps, then that's as much a defining trait of the character as any Attribute, Ability or power. You're not just building a stat block, you're building a fictional concept to add to the story, so this concept should be coherent. If you want a rich character, make a rich character.

                      ------------------------------------------
                      RULES AS WRITTEN
                      ------------------------------------------

                      The idea of building up Resources with Finances is a very common assumption, but the actual rules never said that, not even when talking about changing Backgrounds over time. And overall the rules are quite consistent that no trait imply any other trait, which is what keeps the process flexible. Nothing says you have to justify your Resources through some other trait, just a nothing says building up Resources is an automatic process.

                      Even because Resources defines regular, reliable "income". Making money out of a roll or power use has never been addressed as a change in Resources, it's a fix for the moment (even if a big one) that won't come up again without another roll, unless events, not rolls, in the story change your Background. Regardless of whatever role dice rolls may play on this process, it does indicates that Background changes are meant to be story beats, the result of dramatic actions, and not of having a given skill.

                      Just imagine if having 4 dots at Medicine should guarantee you get a job as a surgeon after play begins and without any connection to the events within. If this isn't the case with any other Ability, it shouldn't be the case with Finances.

                      ------------------------------------------
                      GAME FAIRNESS
                      ------------------------------------------

                      Increasing (or decreasing) character traits is always a complicated thing to deal with.

                      Every player got to build their character under the same rules. We all know how people feel about others gaming their way towards getting more dots even when it happens completely within the rules. We can't even completely prevent players from getting frustrated from time to time due to poor choices that are either objectively or subjectively worse than other equal-cost options at the time. Understanding a given trait as capable of guaranteeing the raise of another through a predictable process simply isn't fair.

                      On how to solve this... I personally think Mage did the right thing and CofD improved it: let Backgrounds have XP costs like everything else and apply a Sanctity rule. It doesn't means you can't get nice things during play through your decisions and roleplay, it just means you're not entitled to them and they'll likely be short lived if you don't also invest your actual game resources on getting the relevant trait.

                      A rule like Hermeticus proposes wouldn't just be a daunt task to create, without making sure every Ability is either capable of a similar feat or balanced in some other sense, would just compound the problem, not solve it. If it does go the full mileage, though, I would at least accept to test it.

                      We have also to consider the type of dot being used here. This isn't just about doing something other Abilities can't, there's also a potential problem in interfering with areas that should be the purview of supernatural advantages, as they're the game's staple on significantly changing a character's life suddenly.

                      ------------------------------------------
                      TIME CONSTRAINT
                      ------------------------------------------
                      ​
                      Simply put, even if we ignored all of the above and had it work just like that, it isn't instantaneous. Making money takes time, however you do it. It also frequently takes effort, professional investors don't simply make on the fly decisions and that's it, if you want to improve returns you have to dedicate a lot of time to market analysis and other stuff, it's as much a full time job as any other.

                      A character that's trying to advance Resources with Finances will possibly have to dedicate time to related activities just as anyone trying to keep a job would, and it would take in-game time for it to happen, a time that may be symbolic or the whole chronicle depending on the game.

                      Resources let you be rich at the very beginning. That's not an irrelevant advantage.​

                          I don't think Finances 4-5 gets you any more money than you would otherwise get from any other highly ranked Ability in a well compensated profession. Medicine 4-5 or Legal 4-5 should put you in a top earners in those professions. Computer 4-5 probably does the same. Even a Drive or Crafts 4-5 indicates a highly trained auto mechanic and can earn a good living. (Good mechanics are hard to find and always in demand!) So would a lot of the other abilities. In other words, there's nothing that special about having a Finance 4-5 other than you are in the top of your profession/career.

                          Furthermore, what do these abilities actually represent? Does Finance 4-5 make you a Warren Buffet, Kevin O'Leary, George Soros, or Carl Icahn? Or does it just make you a really good accountant, floor trader, commercial realtor, or financial advisor? I would argue probably the latter. In terms of becoming a business tycoon, corporate icon, or legendary company founder, that's probably something outside your character sheet. To get there, it requires a lot of things - opportunity, good timing, being at the right place, entrepreneurship, solid networking, and a little bit of magic/good fortune. For game purposes, that's a combination of ST fiat and great roleplay by a PC.

                          If a character has Finance 5, but zero Resources, my first question as an ST is how is this possible? It is very unlikely a newly minted MBA would be Finance 5 or even 4 unless that person already has lots of real world experience. Most newly minted young MBAs probably start at Finance 3 and begin working their way up. And if the person has such experience, their Resources would not be at zero. There needs to be some kind of story there. Did they have some kind of mental breakdown and walked away from it all? Drug or alcohol addiction that caused them to crash and burn before hitting bottom and getting back up? Such a person can certainly begin to rebuild their fortune, but it's not going to be overnight.
