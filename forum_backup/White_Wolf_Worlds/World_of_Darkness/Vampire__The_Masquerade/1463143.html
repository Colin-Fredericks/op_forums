    How many pigs to feed a vampire without killing any of them?

    10-08-2021, 04:33 PM
    An high humanity vampire keeps a pig farm and regularly takes a bit of blood from several of them in order to keep himself feed without killing the animals.
    More or less how many pigs it would take to do that, taking in account the time needed to recover fully from a light blood loss?

          I hope you were told there would be math. Because that's what I am doing.

          We know adult humans have around 10 pints of blood and a blood pool of 10. We also know that animal blood is less nutritious for vampires. Both Revised and V20 corebook say a domestic pig's blood pool is 3. Pigs are supposed to have around 7 pints of blood. To make the math easier we could say that every 2 pints of pig blood produces 1 point of blood pool. (Note the game is inconsistent on things. Draft horses have around 14 gallons of blood (about 112 pints) yet only provide 4 blood points. That's almost 30 pints of horse blood per blood point! If you want to explain that, maybe because the horse is a herbivore, it's blood is worth even less than that of a pig who is an omnivore that eats meat So not all animal blood is worth the same.)

          In real life, donating a pint of blood takes several months to recover. But the game says mortals will regain one blood point per day. Big difference. But it's the game's rule so we'll go with that. Now for pigs, I'd say because you have to drain more blood to get 1 blood point, it should take at least 2 days maybe 3 to recover.

          However, we're also told that vampires can't take more than 20% of a vessel's blood without harming the vessel. 20% of a pig's 3 blood pool is only 0.6 blood pool. So to get one blood point out of a pig, you risk killing that pig. But I'd rule that feeding equally on two pigs per night get's you that one point you need, and both pigs are just before the point where they'd be harmed.

          Now per the rules, if you feed on humans you only need 2 humans to avoid draining that person of blood. On one night you drain 1 point of blood from one human. The next night you drain another point of blood on the second human, and the first human's blood pool replenishes. The third night you can go back to the first human who has a replenished blood pool. So technically you just need two. I think that's cutting it too close as too many things can go wrong, so you'd likely really need 4 so you have some slack in case one of them gets sick or you need more than one point of blood for the night. (And this assumes the person doesn't resist with you taking what you need).

          For pigs, we know you need to feed on two per night. And the pigs would need at least 2 days to recover. So you need four more pigs to cover two more days before the first batch could be ready. That's six pigs. But just like with the humans, I think that's cutting it too close. You should have double the amount for emergencies and take into consideration something happening to the pigs.

          So I would say a vampire needs a minimum of 12 adult pigs to safely recover from low blood loss (1 per night). If you plan on burning more blood, you need more pigs.

          ​That's my logic anyway.

              Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
              In real life, donating a pint of blood takes several months to recover. But the game says mortals will regain one blood point per day. Big difference. But it's the game's rule so we'll go with that.
              As someone who donates to the Red Cross regularly, I can explain this one.

              Lost blood volume will indeed recover in about 24 hours, for the first time or two. The mineral reserves in our marrow that are used to build those blood cells, however, take longer to recover and ongoing depletion will result in anemia and similar symptoms.

              In game terms this means a human can physically recover from the effects of being fed on once in a day or two, but would suffer illness if they were drained repeatedly without their body having a chance to restock the minerals and proteins needed to keep constructing new blood cells and that’s the part that can take a month or two.

              The Red Cross doing 8 weeks between donations is more a “better safe than sorry” standard. Most people will recover the lost minerals in 4-5 weeks, but could be quicker or longer depending on diet. 8 weeks is essentially the outside timeframe where even someone on a mediocre diet (but still healthy enough to donate) will have fully recovered with time to spare.

              As such, a dedicated herd member on a high iron and protein diet could be safely fed from at a rate of about a point per month with little risk (barring underlying health issues).

                  Black Fox's math is solid.

                  Though if you want to add a bit on the realism front: give your pigs a lot of supplemental iron (though not too much because iron poisoning is also a thing).

                  The biggest bottlenecks in mammalian blood regeneration are water intake and iron intake. Water is something your pigs should have constant access too anyway. Mammals are capable of regenerating all of the core blood components in a day or two... but don't store significant amounts of iron to fuel a ramp up in red blood cell production. The iron stores are also what takes the longest time to replenish in the blood regeneration process (we go through a lot of iron daily and siphon very little of it for storage). The biggest risk of repeated low level blood loss is iron-deficiency induced anemia.

                  It doesn't matter to the magical process of taking vitae, but since that requires draining blood and all, if you're going to try to feed on a minimal herd, it's worth keeping in mind if you want to keep an eye on such details.

                              Amassing all that and assuming the best interpretation of the rules (since our goal here is for this to work, but within the rules).

                              Minimum Safe Herd = 2 pigs for 1 BP/month. So you need 60 pigs for basic necessities, plus 2 pigs for your expectations in excess Blood intake.

                              For ideal safety, 120 pigs would let you give each pig 2 months to recover, so their health is better cared for. Consider that not only you may need more blood, but also that you herd may have problems of their own, so at the very least twice that would be better, a whopping 240 pigs. Ideally I would keep my herd at 600 or more whenever possible.

                              Now, a typical US pig farm actually houses about 8k pigs. They're factory farms, but that nonetheless means that having and handling that many pigs isn't completely unreasonable. You may have to deal with it being a production farm, not just your yard or something, as that many pigs will have several costs and aren't really cheap or subtle to keep, but they are a viable option.

                              You only have to keep one last thing in mind while feeding on pigs: they are omnivores capable and whiling on eating humans, both freshly killed or corpses, so it isn't impossible for a careless vampire to end up on their menu.

                                                  There's nothing that actually says you can't store animal blood for use, but with the idea that stored blood is even less nourishing, the sheer volume of it you'd have to consume starts to get a bit ridiculous.

                                                  While I know pulling from VtR isn't always the best or what people are looking for, the VtR 2e rule for stored blood has a nice thematic twist to it: To get 1 vitae out of cold blood, you need to consume your Blood Potency x 2 the equivalent amount of warm blood. This isn't hard to translate to pre-V5 by just using your dots in the Generation background instead (though I'd put that at a min. of 1 unless you take the Thin Blood or 14th/15th Gen Flaws).

                                                  It actually puts a bit of counter pressure on dumping all your Background dots in Generation (starting at Generation 8 would mean you need to consume an entire adult human's worth of cold blood for 1 bp, so stored blood goes out the window as an effective strategy), and adds a bit of a practical and grounded psychological side to the worries about the every diluting blood in higher Generations. Older and lower Generation vampires simply can't practically sup on stored pigs blood purchased from a butcher, and have to feed on warm blood, increasing their alienation from humanity, and making Humanity more difficult for them, while a 14th Gen or a Thin-blood can basically get buy on a couple pints of cold pigs blood a night. It might not be pleasing to the palate, but it allows them to act more human.
