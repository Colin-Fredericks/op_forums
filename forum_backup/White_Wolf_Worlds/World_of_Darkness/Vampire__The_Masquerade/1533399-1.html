        Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
        t my first response to the question of ‘What does the average Kindred know about the Clan Giovanni?’ was “Vampire Mafia”
        This is an issue I've gotten more cross about over the years. While I initially liked the first Giovanni clanbook (and it does have many useful things in it), I've enjoyed it less over the years mainly (but not only) because of this issue. As originally written, the Giovanni had nothing to do with gangsters. Northern Italy in general, and certainly the old Venetian Republic, had no connection with the criminal gangs of southern Italy. And the Giovanni were meant to be merchant princes of the Venetian Republic. A decadent, corrupt, and degenerate family of merchant princes, but merchant princes nonetheless. Classy, stylish, and hanging out with the cream of society with lots of sick, degenerate secrets lurking in the shadows.

        But because the first Giovanni clanbook was written from the perspective of some goomba Joisey-shore mafioso, the clan seemed to suddenly become a family of low class (if rich) gangsters.

        Now I have no problem with a sub-family like the Putanesca being a mafia family and representing clan interests in the criminal underworld. That is perfectly fine. But southern Italian organized crime gangsters should 1) have no obvious connection to the other mortal families of the Giovanni, and 2) outside those specific domains where the Putanesca family is represented, there should be little or no association with the Giovanni clan with organized crime. Northern Italian merchant princes and aristocracy, and their non-Italian allies who are likewise important merchants and financiers simply don't associate with low class thugs. Of course, there can be secret connections, and vampires in general can utilize the criminal element when needed. But it shouldn't be used to identify the Giovanni clan anymore than it does any other clan. That's not the archetype.

        As someone on this forum once said, "Modern day Borgias" should be the main concept associated with the Giovanni clan, not "mafia vampires".

        In my chronicles, I have the "Putanesca crime family" be one of the (mafia) Five Families of New York with interests in other cities, and many Kindred are aware of their connection to the Giovanni clan, but the Putanesca are treated as an embarrassment by the rest of the Giovanni clan. Their red headed stepchild and black sheep of the family. Useful and necessary to many clan needs, but only tolerated, kept out of clan leadership positions, and not allowed to be the face of the clan among Kindred society. That's to my taste.

            IMO, if you want to consolidate Clans/Lore, kinda do what Requiem did.

            Giovanni would be a bloodline within the Venture. It would fit the Familial Cult, banking, privateer, and merchant aspects of the clan.

            I think most of the latter 13 could be consolidated into the original 7 +/-2(Lasombra/Tzimisce).

            Giovanni seemed like Ventrue Lite to me. Necromance SEEMED powerful until a table-mate pointed out that it's a lot of extra experience spent on what Dominate/Presence to for cheaper.

            And for people thinking Necromancy is big bad scary evil, it only seems so when STs and Players get numb to the fact that Dominate and Presence remove individual agency and essentially turn the victim into a slave while the user generally gets a free pass on repeatedly using it. Dominate is essentially mind rape to a human.

            Plus the Giovanni are less numerous and have to juggle two worlds. That's twice the enemies, folks. It's just always seemed to make sense the Ventrue would beat them at everything but necromancy...and if they needed necromancers they would 'cut a deal'(read Dominate/Presence/Blood Bond) some Giovanni that were desperate/weak.

            So, make the Giovanni a bloodline of the Venture, remove Potence, add Fortitude, remove Presence, add Necromancy. Keep the painful feeding instead of the Ventrue weakness.

            As far as the Cappodocians, they rebrand into the Hecata because(handwave) the Feast only killed the Euro vamps. The Cappodocians in the Americas weren't effected. Augustus wasn't a vamp that usurped blood but rather a big time merchant mage that cut a deal with the Venture in exchange for undeath.

                Originally posted by InspectorG View Post
                IMO, if you want to consolidate Clans/Lore, kinda do what Requiem did.

                While an interesting take, this has nothing to do with my original post. The Cappadocians/Giovanni have nothing to do with the Ventrue or any other clan.

                I understand someone wanting to take the basic idea of a family of merchant princes and thinking, "This would be a good concept for a Ventrue character." The Ventrue are known to embrace within mortal family lineages, and it's an entirely valid concept to say, "what if one of those lineages were particularly degenerate, corrupt, etc." So yes, there can be overlap. In fact, in my chronicles I do like to have Ventrue lineages of specific mortal families whose power and influence equal that of the Giovanni, and have all the intra-family intrigues the Giovanni are known for. Totally valid and lots of fun.

                But the Cappdocians/Giovanni/Hecata are much more than that. Concepts of a morbid obsession with death, death like visages, and other tropes particular to the CGH distinguish them from the Ventrue or any other clan. There's no reason why vampires that look more like corpses and keep to those kinds of primal tropes shouldn't be in the game. It's a good vampire archetype IMO, and the CGH is the best clan to do that. I'd like to keep those rather than just abandon the clan simply because of some (minor) overlap with the Ventrue of one aspect for it.

                                                Originally posted by gamiem View Post
                                                I've never really understood the idea clans must map onto vampire archetypes, or even that the clan system is the best way of covering all the existent archetypes; it invariably seems to lead to archetypes being arbitrarily cut and trimmed to avoid thematic overlap between different clans.
                                                It's just a gimmick that allows players to quickly decide what kind of character they want to play. Certain clans lend themselves to certain kind of characters. You can always create a character that goes against archetype, but that too is an advantage because it leads to certain kind of interactions with other members of the clan.

                                                There's no problem with different lineages of vampires essentially being the same, but that doesn't really provide any hook for players (or the ST) to latch on to. It would make clan choice irrelevant and make a lot more work for the ST to distinguish NPCs from one another. It's just an artifact of being an RPG. And I think it's a major reason why VtM proved so successful. Around the time Vampire came out, there were several RPGs that allowed people to play monstrous characters like Nightlife and Nightbane, but the World of Darkness line won out over them for a reason.

                                                Originally posted by gamiem View Post
                                                I wouldn't advocate merging clans in order to have them better fit established archetypes—the Ventrue, the Lasombra and the Giovanni are individually more interesting to me than a singular Bela Lugosi clan would be—but I think it'd be wise to avoid the excessive proliferation of clans.
                                                I would not disagree with you. The original seven Camarilla clans are pretty much all that one needs. Yes, the game always intended to have thirteen clans. But the four independent clans were obviously intended as limited antagonists that served the setting in particular ways as opposed to broad based archetypes. The Assamites were simply intended to be a group of vampire assassins that anyone could hire or be targetted by. The Setites were worms that would eat the apple from within. The Giovanni were untrustworthy merchants you could deal with, but had their own agenda. Ravnos were meant to provide a rival to the Gangrel in the wilderness. The Lasombra and Tzimisce existed simply to provide the Sabbat unique clans to it. Little thought was really given to distinguishing them other than their intended role in the setting combined with a unique Discipline.

                                                Revised and later editions attempted to broaden these clans to varying success. Some good things were done to make them more interesting and believable, but by V5 the main result has been to make certain clans unrecognizable from what they originally were.

                                                I certainly found the many bloodlines to be excessive, and their unique Disciplines seemed to be making vampires more like a superpowers game than a game about vampires.

                                                Originally posted by gamiem View Post
                                                Given that looking and behaving like a living corpse is a trait shared by more than one kind of vampire, I'd rather have the 'Giovanni look' be achieved through a mechanic than a designated clan—is becoming corpselike not already stated to be a consequence of low Humanity?
                                                I would argue the clan weaknesses are not meant to be unique takes for each individual line. They are usually just more pronounced takes on general vampire weaknesses. All vampires have to make Self-Control checks, but the Brujah make theirs with a penalty. Any vampire can have a Derangement, but the Malkavians all have a permanent Derangement that cannot be removed. All vampires intake blood whose different types provide different value of "nutrition" and often have a particular style of hunting, but the Ventrue have stricter requirements. All vampires take damage from sunlight, but the Setites take even more damage. The Cappadocian weakness is just a stronger version of the normal rules for vampire appearance. Some clans do have a distinctive weakness (like the Nosferatu and Lasombra) that do not really affect other vampires, but these are usually represented by Flaws that can be given to any vampire.
