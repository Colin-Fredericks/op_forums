                                            Someone, and I wish I could remember who (I want to say Ethan Skemp or Bruce Baugh, but Geoff Grabowski is another likely culprit), explained their take as that while all off these games nominally share the same setting, each has its own distinct cosmology, and which one is true depends on which game you are actually playing. And for the most part, if you are playing, say, Vampire, then Werewolf's non-humancentric animism or Mage's gnostic consensual reality don't really matter when it comes to what Kindred are doing in their everynight unlives. Similarly, Vampire's dark Christian¹ cosmology doesn't really matter when playing those other games (and in some cases may not even matter when playing Vampire if you aren't doing anything involving the Final Nights or Noddism).
                                            It only really becomes an issue when doing crossovers. Werewolf and Mage, for example, are harder to do because they have almost totally opposite positions on the importance of humanity and their place in the cosmology. Vampire and Demon, on the other hand, is probably easier. (In hindsight, I really wish they'd done a dedicated crossover guide for each pair of game lines - Vampire + Werewolf, Vampire + Mage, etc. - that went over all the reasons the two groups might interact and what issues the Storyteller would have to work out beforehand.)

                                            ¹ "Dark Christian" was a phrase Bruce Baugh came up with IIRC. The general idea is that God exists and He is a very harsh and judgmental God, and while the potential for redemption/salvation exists, it is a long and difficult road to travel. Also, demons are real, and if you ever actually met an angel, it would be incredibly terrifying. In many ways, it is influenced by what you see in certain Gothic fiction, some 80s and 90s movies involving angels and demons, and Jack Chick tracts.
