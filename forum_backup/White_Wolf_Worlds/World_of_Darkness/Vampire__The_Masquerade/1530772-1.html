                        This entire discussion is why I never liked the idea of "sub clans" or the different set of in-clan Disciplines between the Giovanni and Cappadocians. It's also why while I am not interested in V5, I do like how they combined all the various sub clans of the Cappadocians back together.

                        To get around this debate and establish continuity, I established in my chronicles a long time ago that all the Cappadocians and their offshoots had the same Discipline set, and had the same clan weakness. It's just that certain lineages also had an additional weakness in the form of a mandatory Flaw (which any PCs would get build points for). All Giovanni also had Gripped of the Damned (which prior Giovanni embraces developed as well because the Curse affected all of Augustus's lineage). The Samedi had the Monstrous flaw. Lamia had whatever the diseased blood Flaw was called. In my mind, it's easier and quicker to explain how these additional Flaws came about than trying to resolve all the retcons and edition changes.

                        There are semantic discussions and esoteric continuity debates I do find fun to have, but this isn't one of them.

                                    Good point, although I'm wary of older translations of Sumerian sources, because many adopted the abrahamic bias in word choice that came from later sources like the Talmud. What is commonly translated as 'demon' from Sumerian or Akkadian culture is actually closer to what would be filed under Spirit in the WoD. Some did bring disease, some protected against it. Some did both at the same time. Lilu and Lilitu were a subclass of night spirits associated with Inanna/Ishtar that varied between incubi/succubi and other kinds of fertility-related things, including health and disease. It's complicated.
                                    All ancient Mesopotamian religions also made a clear distinction between these spirits and ghosts, btw.

                                    But since WoD lore around her mainly draws from abrahamic mythology your point stands.
