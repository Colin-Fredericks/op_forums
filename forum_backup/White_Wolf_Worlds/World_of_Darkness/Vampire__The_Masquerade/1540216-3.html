    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post

    It's a bit of a mixed bag.

    V5 Discipline activation pools generally use an Attribute + Discipline rating rather than a Skill. While Disciplines aren't cheap in V5 (char gen or with XP), you have less overall traits you have to invest in to get as good as you can with them.
    Could be cheaper in real terms if the overall skills were fewer to the point that 1XP 'buys more' but in V20, there are a relative few skills that are too advantageous to pass up and the rest are flavor or char concept.

    What dice pool you "need" depends entirely on what you define as "regularly be proficient," and how liberal your ST is with taking half
    Gonna vary slightly table to table but in V20 1 success is a 'win' and 3 a 'strong win'. At difficulty 6. Rough math: 2 dice tends to = 1 success(less so if 1s subtract but im just illustrating the point). That means 6 dice in the pool to be 'above average good' at something at normal circumstances. Diff 7 3 dice += about 1 success, if 8 = 4. So on.

    So IMO, if someone is really good at a task, they should be able to regularly display average or above average competence in un-ideal circumstances. And in V20 with the Dice Pool Splitting mechanic, if your split dice pools synergize(they all have equal values in dots so none are restrictive to the potential pool), then with 8 dice in a pool, a char should be able to output 3+ successes in most situation barring any huge mitigating factors.

    V5 i think(unless it has changed) you can spend a WP for 3 extra dice(about one success?) or "win at a cost" which will really depend upon the ST. im not sure how 'Taking Half' works or could be exploited.


    No, thankfully, as Animal Succulence would be completely worthless if you had to make Rouse checks to feed off of animals for a bit longer than normal as your Blood Potency increases. That said, V5 Animalism is rather Rouse heavy with only two core powers that don't require checks. Though the three checks to use Bond Famulus last until the animal dies, and negates the Rouse checks of two of the higher level powers you're going to want to use on them a lot; so it's a large net savings for your one close animal companion compared to ordering around random creatures.
    V5 Feral Whispers has a cost of 1 Rouse per animal type, per scene. That, could, get expensive over a night. V20 i dont remember lvls 1-5 having a cost, though for 4/5 you may likely want to spend a WP for an auto success.

    Not really? As far as I can tell the Bond Famulus power is the only one that seems inspired by Requiem directly instead of being a power VtM and VtR have both used at some point previously. Though I think most people would prefer the VtR 2e version of that power over the V5 version given the respective contexts (esp. given the point of the thread VtR 2e's Raise the Familiar explicitly makes that animal as intelligent as an Intelligence 1 human when it comes to understanding orders given, cutting through a lot of the "what can it do exactly?" issues discussed in the thread even if VtR 2e Animalism in general doesn't fully get rid of that problem either).
    I dont remember but im pretty sure ive only played VtR 1e.

    As far as animal intelligence, that would be a difficult one to ever properly define given the subject matter. I view it in one of 2 ways:

    A.The blood is the intelligent force
    B. The animal is the intelligent force

    A is easier but can be ham-fisted with a bad ST. B is far more subject to interpretation and ST/PCs would need some study reference. Example: studies on Crows/Ravens show them to be far more intelligent than previously thought. Perhaps closer to a 7 year old child in some problem solving aspects. Plus, they recognize individual humans and can pass that info along.

        In almost all editions/variations of Vampire, B is the stated way Animalism works. My point about Raise the Familiar is that one of its major perks is that it swaps to A. Your animal blood familiar sacrifices some of its instincts (but not natural capabilities) in exchange for supernatural intelligence that makes it much more valuable as a reliable servant. It's not impressive intelligence, but an order like, "Keep the haven safe while I'm out," will be understood in both the wording and spirit of your intentions because the blood is taking over instead of the animal's brain.
