            Disagree with the D&D 5e comparison. With D&D 5e I can open the Players Handbook and play any core concept from the previous editions of Dungeons and Dragons.             “I think there’s a tricky balance when you’re doing a sequel, between making it fresh, surprising the audience, but not surprising them with such a swerve that they feel that it’s not honoring the first film. You’ve got to play to expectations, you’ve got to play against expectations. And I always think that it’s about answering a question that you didn’t know to ask. But when they see it, it seems obvious.”

                I think the closest example I can think of is the fact that the Sabbat is a bit like the Galactic Empire in the Star Wars Expanded Universe. A lot of writers felt the need to start humanizing the Galactic Empire with characters like Grand Admiral Thrawn, Admiral Pellaeon, and other "heroic" Imperials. They even had the Galactic Empire make a peace treaty with the New Republic and become the co-stars of the Legacy comics that were the Grand Finale of the Old Expanded Universe.

                This was something I feel was a serious misstep and missed the point of the characterization. I had a similar feeling about the Technocracy Revised books, which continually and repeatedly tried to paint the Technocracy in the best light possible. Something I was uncomfortable with then and now realize was because, "Wait, these guys are genocidal fascists. WHY are we trying to put a smiley face on them?" I had a similar moment in Warhammer 40K fandom when I realized some people were sincere Emperor fanboys.

                The Sabbat has continually lost a huge amount of its edge as more of its details have been exposed to the public. They also have been reduced from being a terrifying and mysterious force to just stealing from the Anarch's stick (FREEEEEDOM) and stealing from the Camarilla (manipulative alien elders). Seeing them get broken up into new sects I think is a good thing for people who want to have deeper concepts.

                We know from CULTS OF THE BLOOD GODS interview that the spiritual side of the Sabbat survives in the Church of Caine and they're going to have all of the Sabbat's ritae, secret rituals, and bizarre activities. We know the Anarchs are now going to have all of the violent freedom and pack-like war on the Camarilla. The Lasombra are now available as a Camarilla sect. We're also getting rules for the Tzimisce. I have no doubt a smaller version of the Sword of Caine will survive as well. But the Sabbat as they were envisioned, the Boogeymen of Kindred SocietyTM are coming back and that's not a bad thing.

                The Old Sabbat has broken up and the individual pieces are stronger for it as well as regaining some edge. No more failed wannabe Anarchs.
