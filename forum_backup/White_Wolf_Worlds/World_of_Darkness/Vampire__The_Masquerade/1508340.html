    Some questions about Vitae

    04-14-2023, 05:15 AM
    1- So, if a Ghoul feeds every night his Blood Pool can increase to the point of replacing all his normal blood with Vitae...
    but since he has still a mortal biology, isn't that a nonsense? Vitae is dead blood. Even if it isn't, it may come from uncompatible blood donors or even be animal blood. It can't replace normal blood's biological functions.

    2- If a Ghoul dies with a lot of Vitae in his system, it's possible that he revives as a Vampire?

    3- Can a Vampire be created by taking the Vitae from another vampire and pouring it into the mouth of a dying mortal? May it be done even with preserved Vitae, mabye from an already - destroyed Vampire?

    4- and if point 3 is possible, may a vampire ( or a group of vampires ) use the Vitae of a captured / staked / slain elder to Embrace some common mortals then diablerize those newly created vampires to increase their generation?

    ( example: a 16th generation Caitiff has, somehow, captured and staked a 5th generation elder. Instead of diablerizing him and becoming 14th generation, he kidnaps 10 mortals, use the blood of the elder to Embrace them - creating ten 6th generation new vampires - then diabolize them one by one, increasing his generation to 6th ) ( and then he diablerize the elder too and get to 5th )

    - - -

    Another one: does anybody remember the various serums developed by vampire Pasteur in Alien Hunger? Serum Home ( pg 30 ) allows to gain 1 generation...
    that would open up ways to use and abuse. What's the lowest Generation a vampire could reach?

      Interesting questions, interesting thread.

      At the beginning of the V5 corebook, there's a medically inclined Kindred saying that the vampiric body is no different than the human one - no denser bones, cellular changes or mutations that can be observed by microscope, so this seems to imply a purely spiritual or supernatural phenomena taking place. If that's the case, then something like the Embrace is less a biological transformation and more a paranormal one. After all, this former doctor Kindred claims Vitae is not scientifically different from human blood. Of course, that's just one account and one that I personally reject. I can't at all get on board that vampires don't go through broad physiological changes, along with paranormal ones. Personally, I view the condition as both biological and spiritual. So:

      1: Not sure.

      2: I don't think so, but who knows? It is called the "Embrace" for a reason and it's possible that not just the blood that is necessary, but also part of the soul of the sire vampire. We don't really hear anything about ghouls reanimating, despite a presumably high death rate from the Jyhad, so there might be a mystical aspect to vampirism necessary for the change.

      3/4: See above, but I also see this one as feasible through some obscure Blood Sorcery ritual or something to that affect.

      The WoD is a crazy place and I presume crazy things happen, so everything you suggest might be highly improbable, but not necessarily impossible.

          V5 may have tweaked a few things (I don't have my book to check) or introduced some conflicting information, but the biology of vampires is quite clearly described in the opening fiction of V1 and V2, and in the Storytellers Handbook for VRev. Ghouls: Fatal Addiction has info on ghoul physiology and psychology, and Time of Thin Blood (and later Beckett's Jyhad Diary) covers Thin-bloods and Dhampirs. Ghouls and Revenants also touches on ghouls and revenant biology, but in less detail.

          Technically, vampires do change subtly after the Embrace. Some of these changes are supported in rules, and some only get mentioned in the fluff.

          Fluff-wise, vampires are said to become more beautiful (except Nosferatu) and/or their physical imperfections are evened out a little. They also apparently become more predatory, including having altered senses that can appreciate new tastes, textures and details.

          However, the rules have never supported these parts of the fluff, beyond vampires having more Attribute dots than mere mortals; and the existence of certain Flaws that confer physical drawbacks suggests even if it is supposed to be canon, it's not universal or uniform.

          What we also know (and this is covered by rules to varying degrees) is the following:
          • Most vampires can't have sex without vitae, and they're just going through the motions, even then. There are mechanics for this.
          • Vampire organs are vestigial and eventually shrivel up inside a vampire. This happens with age, so that thin blooded Kindred probably look relatively normal, but if you cut a elder open, he might only have some shrivelled husks where his organs were. (I think the heart retains its shape, but the body transfers vitae via osmosis rather than a circulatory system.) This is reflected in bashing damage from bullets in VRev and V20.
          • Vitae infuses everything in the vampire's body, including their bodily fluids, which means they cry blood (and technically ejaculate blood), although their eyes don't turn red and other fluid-rich body parts don't change colour either. Put it down to magic.
          • The outside shell of the vampire stays pretty much the same for eternity. Some changes are wrought by the blood (Nosferatu ugliness, Assamite darkening in earlier editions), but otherwise they're static and their hair grows back, tattoos push their way out, and piercings heal when they sleep during the day. There are rituals that get around this.
          • Vitae is magical and sustains the vampire. It can be used to heal any mundane injury and can even be used to reattach or even regenerate lost limbs.
          • In humans, vitae causes a kind of hypermetabolic reaction, in which healing occurs very quickly and cells regenerate without any of the copying errors that normally happen. This results in ghouls not ageing while ghouled, having some of the same strengths as vampires, and yet, still being "alive".
          • In earlier editions, ghouls can carry children, but the pregnancy is "paused" while vitae remains in their systems, and they have to wean themselves off it to allow the child to be born properly. (Revenants are an exception.) V20 was more vague and suggested ghouling the mother will probably just kill the baby.
          • Ghouls can regenerate limbs, but it's harder. Their mortal diseases are also held in abeyance while ghouled -- cancer doesn't affect them, asthma wouldn't make a difference. Once the vitae is spent, it comes back, just as it was before they were ghouled (and their ageing resumes too).
          • Ghouls feel very "alive" and intoxicated when they drink vitae. It rouses their darker natures, and they even develop a milder version of the Beast.
          • There's some evidence that some elements of ghoul physiology operate on a natural cycle of approximately a month, which is the length of time they take to burn through a point of vitae. IIRC, things like stubble, growing fingernails and periods can still occur over a month, although this may be the effect of the vitae's power wearing off towards the end of a cycle. (Contrast this with the vampire's cycle, which resets every night.)
          • Revenants (and dhampirs) have a quasi-vitae that sustains them. They create it on their own at a slow rate but cannot use it to ghoul or blood bond others.
          • Revenant vitae doesn't arrest ageing completely, but slows it down to about 25% of human ageing.
          • Revenants can carry children, but it takes a looooong time (which is extra creepy and may have been walked back in V20; I'm away from my books to check). Delayed puberty may also have been retconned for similar reasons (i.e., slowed ageing kicks in at puberty or just afterwards).
          • Dhampirs are a special sort of revenant, essentially, which may also indicate how the Tzimisce and others bred ghoul families despite the problem of arrested ageing.
          So, to answer your questions:

          1. No, because magic. But also, for the reasons I've detailed above, the vitae acts to preserve the body in its own way (or rather, it acts to "quicken" the body in such a way that it repairs itself almost perfectly, which looks like preservation).

          Ghouls who aren't fed are described as looking sickly and gaunt, for example, but I believe they can occasionally (/usually?) subsist on vitae alone if needed. The vitae lends the body the life force it needs (and most other functions), instead of the body having to make it itself through the usual means.

          There are some exceptions (I think ghouls still need to breathe, for example, but can't check the books right now), but this is a general guideline that works well when understanding ghoul physiology.

          2. I think the official answer is no, because the Embrace requires a very specific sequence of events: the target must be drained of all their blood and then at least a little vitae from the sire must be given back to them.

          If a ghoul died with vitae in their body, they wouldn't have been drained. If they were drained, they wouldn't have vitae in their bodies.

          Requiem fixed the confusion, as this question used to get asked a lot, by requiring sires to spend a point of Willpower as well as vitae to enact the Embrace -- making it an intentional act (with a few horrific exceptions).

          In the fluff, there are stories of dying people saved by the Embrace. But I think they still have to be drained; dying alone isn't usually enough. But you could always use Merits and Flaws to simulate freak accidents and the like, if your ST allows it.

          One theory that used to circulate on the old email lists and message boards was that the Embrace is a sort of blood magic ritual that Caine learned from demons/Lilith/the Crone. I.e., he wasn't supposed to be able to pass the curse on at all, but he found some sort of magical workaround.

          3. If the mortal is drained and the blood properly stored/preserved/handled, yes. Vitae has to be used pretty quickly for the Embrace (and blood bonds), so that's why various clans have their own ways of preserving it.

          The vampire doesn't need to be alive for the Embrace to be performed in this way, but I suspect you're more likely to end up with a Caitiff/Pander if you engage in this sort of "indirect" Embrace.

          4. Yes, with the same caveats as above, plus the caveat that you need to subdue this Methuselah in the first place.

          Bear in mind, too, that elder Disciplines can subvert the usual limitations of staking and torpor, and most Kindred who think they've taken advantage of a Methuselah (or Antediluvian) and/or who have tried to diablerise one, later turn out to have been manipulated by said elder, or else wind up being possessed by them.

          Your ST is under no obligation to play nice if you want to cheese the rules, either. They probably will make it hard for you -- though if everyone's having fun, I always say, go nuts! Diablerise Baba Yaga if you want to.
          ​​​
          5. There are a few dodgy rituals and treasures like that in early VTM. Most were never mentioned again, and others were retconned or nerfed later on. I'd personally come up with some more interesting, lower powered effects, but really, it's between you and your ST.

          In theory, and canonically, there is only one serum that lowers your Generation and you won't find any more. The creator is also dead, so he can't teach you how to make it again. That means you would still only be able to drop one Generation -- and I'd put a hard cap on falling no lower than First Generation (if you're Enoch, for example) just because logic.

          ​​​​​​​Though there may not be any real difference between the first three Generations anyway, except via age -- one theory mentioned in canon, IIRC, is that the weakening of the Blood that comes with higher Generation was a curse levied on the Antediluvians and their childer by Caine, along with the clan curses.

              In terms of how a ghoul can possess blood points (meaning vitae, not actual blood) by drinking vitae - hey, it's a kind of magic.

              Originally posted by Conradine View Post
              2- If a Ghoul dies with a lot of Vitae in his system, it's possible that he revives as a Vampire?
              No. Simply having vitae in your system is not sufficient. Ghouls don't turn into vampires upon death, and neither do Revenants. So what's the difference between a ghoul dying with vitae still in their system, and a vampire slaying a mortal and then feeding the dying mortal their blood? Not sure. The game never addresses this in detail.

              But my imagination and house rules say that there is something the vampire must do in order to create a new vampire. And if you don't know this (whether it is an issue of timing, application, or something else), a vampire can't actually create another vampire. You just end up killing the mortal. Now this isn't a "secret" among vampires. It is very well known. But it does prevent freshly made vampires from being able to create new vampires so you don't have the classic vampire plague of the movies. It is something only demonstrated by their sires (or Sabbat pack) to their childe once the fledgling has demonstrated they are ready for the responsibilities of "adulthood".
