    So, a couple things about Paradox:

    1- They've repeatedly shown that they don't understand a thing about the things they've been trying to do. The very fact that they think (and repeatedly asserted) that a game like Bloodlines has a "short tail" is a statement to how little they understand even the very video-game market beyond their niche. This is their biggest weakness by far, regardless if others can do it, they simply can't. It has been failure after failure as they tackle every possible genre and product in the same way they do their niche, as someone who's both ignorant of things and unwilling to learn. At this point and given their proven inability to learn new tricks, focusing on doing the only things they actually know how to is the only smart decision;

    2- What do you call a failure? The thing is, while the players' perception is that their games are consistently losing quality over the years, they're actually selling well. Paradox is making good money and keeping up the pace expected by the market. Their shares are at a price as stable as it gets, and they pay their dividends as expected. They seen to have mastered their trade, getting the buck from their investment without either spending too much or failing to do the job;

    At the end of the day, Paradox was over-ambitious and paid the price. It's riddled with problems that it completely failed to address. Their highest dreams lay shattered and, while the problems didn't killed it or even made it poor, they need time to patch things up. They're bad at PR. They're bad at a lot of things. So yeah, they're licking their wounds from all those lost battles and will stay in their safe zone for a while. They are a relatively successful company after all, they became big, so it's not as if keeping their current margins is a bad thing for them. And catering to the previous expectations of gamers simply isn't a winning strategy by now.

    Unfortunately this decoupling between consumer expectation/interest and corporate profit isn't even uncommon in the art and entertainment industry.

    Now that's all said and done, all we have to do is wait for the game to actually launch and see what we got. And overall wait for someone else to want to do something, because WoD remains a pet project that only made them loose patience and opportunities for frugal gains, one they don't want to invest so deeply into ever again, as they finally recognize they simply can't make a real profit out from it (even if they still don't recognize it's their own fault).

The malignance of Day 1 DLC has increasingly become a fact in the gaming industry (at least the mainstream AAA sphere of it), something I despise but have to accept as a reality; locking the Lasombra in this Day 1 DLC scheme would make sense at least, since they are a unique clan that used to be Sabbat (a V5 idiocratic development, but that is a rant for another time and one I have done already in the past) and they have unique powers that are definitely "DLC-coded" from how apart in power and utility their signature discipline (Obtenebration) match against the more classic Clans...

                                                        ...but the Toreador? The Toreador?

                                                        Nothing about this game will be good. Nothing about it "captures the atmosphere of V:tM". That atmosphere has not been a thing since the release of V5. This game is an exemplar of that killed atmosphere continued. It's just a corny, trash cash grab with the Bloodlines label on the box, which has been a common x5 edition tactic of Parawolf adapted to the video game format as can be plainly seen here. An 80-year-old man at a nursing home who has only been brushed up with near basics of the Storyteller system could ST a game with better "V:tM atmosphere" than what they are doing here.

                                                        No ability to play as the Gangrel. No ability to play as Malkavian (both of which will probably be rendered as future DLC, if this game even broaches far from being a complete financial disaster). No real character customization. Just generally a milquetoast pre-determined protagonist that, like I've mentioned above before, does not seem interesting. At all. A pre-determined protagonist at least has to have something that draws the player into finding them cool or palpable to play as if there is an exclusion to making your own character.

                                                        Who is this game actually for? What is Paradox, and by extension The Chinese Room after taking up the mantle to finish this game, trying to reach out to? Another "mainstream" audience like they tried to achieve with V5? How can that be achieved, when an outsider looking outside at Bloodlines 2, finds nothing about it appealing at all? All Paradox has is shills-upon-shills and backchannels to many of the "game media" journalist companies, but that can go only so far; especially with the negative reception from the Day 1 DLC nonsense.

                                                        This game is going to be a disaster.

                                                        At least I'll gain some entertainment from the +1 hourlong critic YouTube reviews that will be absolutely scorching it on release.
