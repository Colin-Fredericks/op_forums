                                            This thread took a bit of a jog after I went to bed last night, so I'm going to try and just quickly respond to the things that stand out to me:

                                            I disagree with the argument that Vicissitude cannot be Protean because Protean is only temporary. That's an extremely subtle distinction that can be pretty easily retconned in order to combine a pair of Disciplines that really don't have a huge difference between them otherwise, aside from Vicissitude being far more specialized. It's especially noticeable when you start comparing Elder-level Vicissitude with Elder-level Protean and see how the differences mostly seem to come down to flavour; being able to turn into a *fire-breathing dragon* seems more like Protean to me than the work of a vampiric mad biologist.

                                            And nothing bugs me more than the argument that the Tzimisce cannot be interesting without Vicissitude. I love the Path of Metamorphosis, and think that Vicissitude (whatever version of it you want to use) fits with that really well. But trying to argue that the Tzimisce or the Path of Metamorphosis must focus and rely on Vicissitude, with everything else being secondary or irrelevant, cheapens both of them.

                                            Again, I don't know much about how V5 handled things, but my understanding is they mostly made it into Amalgams with Dominate. I dislike that and disagree with it; I think they should have become an Amalgam with Blood Sorcery, because it makes sense for the idea of them being mystics (and eventually mad scientists/technomancers), and because most of the more infamous Vicissitude users were Blood Sorcerers already (Vykos, Yorak, Velya, etc.). I can kind of see what they were going for with Dominate, but it just doesn't seem right to me, and it seems like Blood Sorcery would be a smoother transition while also leaving more room for Tzimisce that don't use Vicissitude to be their own thing. It also heads off the argument "Thaumaturgy/Blood Sorcery on its own simply isn't capable of such significant changes"; well, combine Shapeshifting to cover the big changes with Blood Magic that makes the changes permanent, and voila.

                                            That being said, I do *not* think that Blood Sorcery should have been one of their Clan Disciplines. I rather appreciated the traditional conception of the Koldun, that while this was a big part of Clan tradition it was something separate from the main line, and required an extra amount of effort to dedicate yourself to it (the extra cost of an out-of-Clan Discipline). I think their spread should have been Animalism, Auspex, and Protean. I disagree with the idea that Tzimisce simply having Auspex makes them too close to the Tremere as a concept; that'd be the case if they had Dominate, but having Animalism and Protean puts some significant distance between the two IMO. Which doesn't even mention that having some similarity between the two makes a lot of sense from a lore perspective since Tzimisce blood was used in the ritual that created the original Tremere. I think Animalism/Auspex/Protean sets them up to be a more intellectual version of the Gangrel, while you can still have the "Old Clan" that replaces Protean with Dominate to represent the more lordship-focused Tzimisce (in fact, I tend to head-canon the idea that the Tzimisce used for the ritual was Old Clan since that easily explains 2/3 of the Tremere's Clan Disciplines). (PS I wouldn't really mind if Auspex was replaced with Dominate either, for those who really want those Dracula vibes)

                                            Of course, I don't especially care which of those spreads is the "original" vs. "new" bloodline. Again, I think flexibility and change are probably the most interesting aspect of the Tzimisce, rather than lordship or scholarship, which coincidentally leaves room for both if you allow for bloodlines that have "evolved" in different directions.

                                            To head off any arguments about the above Discipline spread being too close to the Gangrel, I'd point out that similar Discipline spreads don't automatically create redundancy once you include different weaknesses and Clan cultures; you could argue that either the Brujah or the Toreador invalidate the other because the only difference in Disciplines between them is Auspex vs. Potence. That one difference, combined with a distinct weakness and Clan culture, can result in something significantly different. Gangrel are (semi-)nomadic survivors, without necessarily having a specific attachment to a particular territory, whereas Tzimisce being strongly tied to a particular territory while also lacking the raw endurance provided by Fortitude creates the need to be a more strategic master of your domain, including fortification, which gives us our master-of-the-border-castle Dracula-style vampire lord.

                                            (I am mostly ignoring the new V5 Clan weakness because I know little about it, and am unsure how I feel about it based on what I know of it so far. But again, plenty of pull away from the Gangrel.)

                                            Regarding the ethnic stereotypes... *stares VERY HARD at the Banu Haqim* You have an example there of a Clan that has a traditional culture very strongly tied to a particular region and ethnicity that nonetheless leaves room for members of the Clan from outside that context to still have a place and help define it as something broader. There's no reason you can't do the same with the Tzimisce.

                                            In fact, there's more reason, because (again) I am of the opinion that change and "evolution" is the most interesting defining feature of the Tzimisce.

                                            But, to put my influences out in the open for a bit, I really got into Masquerade after spending a while with Requiem and the Ordo Dracul, which is why I dig the Path of Metamorphosis so much: the idea of vampires consciously working to overcome the weaknesses and limitations of their condition in order to become something MORE. I also found it the most interesting aspect of the backstory of the Eldest, the idea of the Antediluvian that rejected/challenged the notion of the Curse of Caine as static and dead.

                                                Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                                                Clan cultures are natural, but for Tzmisce the lines between clan culture and mortal culture has been aggressively blurred. The Ventrue, Tremere or Toreador can adapt to any culture, the FoS and Giovanni can reinvent themselves for any culture, but the Tzmisce are explicitly archaic eastern europeans with slavic terminology and a demonic attachment to the carpathians, a perfectly fine and scenic place; as far as vampires go the Tzmisce are particularly backwards, which aligns with the 'murican perspective that eastern europe is backwards.
                                                That has to do with laziness on the part of writers. They are looking for inspiration and therefore go to things that already exist, but them overemphasize them. The clan's two main inspirations - Dracula and Necroscope - rely heavily on the same tropes. (Plus for some strange reason, White Wolf seemed to think that Transylvania - a land populated by Romanians, Hungarians, and Germans - is Slavic when in fact none of those peoples are Slavic at all. The real life Vlad Tepes was Romanian, and the fictional Dracula describes himself as a Szekely.) So there's a lot of emphasis on those elements. Other clans too were stereotyped in certain cultures, but at least the Camarilla Clans had a more variety of examples.

                                                We knew that the Ventrue were heavily associated with Rome, but the game didn't use Latin and Roman structures to describe their modern organization of boards and directorates. And we had early examples of ancient Roman vampires that weren't Ventrue (like the Gangrel Lucian) and Ventrue vampires that were outside Rome (like Hrothgar and Siegfried).

                                                Because the Sabbat and Tzimisce were described much later, the clanbook ever later than that, and only had one Sabbat By Night for a long time, we didn't get anywhere near the variety of examples. It didn't help that because of the clan's role in the Anarch Revolt, almost all the backstory centers around that, and that takes place in the Balkans. So we've seen indiscriminate use of some Slavic terms in an effort to give them more depth and authenticity (cheap use of foreign words was a problem elsewhere too, just see any number of Werewolf tribebooks). But it actually doesn't do that because it's just superficial. There's no need to call the clan's "warlords" by the term "voivode" or their hulking monsters "vozhd". We know that Carthage was a big thing for the Brujah, but writers never felt to use Phoenician terms for Brujah leaders or organization.

                                                So while I agree that the clan's presentation has to often been too strongly associated with Eastern Europe, it's not too hard to deemphasize those things. In terms of actual Tzimisce PCs and NPCs I've seen in chronicles, most have no connection to Eastern Europe at all. It's just an artifact of the official writers overusing characters with Slavic names especially for elders.

                                                        And now, to respond a bit more specifically to the OP:

                                                        "I wish the Tzimisce were more like amoral Progenitors": I think I need a bit of an explanation regarding how you think the Tzimisce *aren't* like amoral self-serving Progenitors, unless your argument is essentially "not all of them are," in which case I'd be critical of making a clan entirely into a one-dimensional stereotype. Regardless of the Clan, trying to put the exact same Hat on every single member is boring. Or "they don't care enough about evolving humans with them," to which I would respond "why would you want to make your cattle more powerful and dangerous?" That being said, I find the idea of a Tzimisce Metamorphosist who is 100% onboard with the segments of the Obertus Revenants that want to replace traditional humanity with themselves to be quite a neat idea personally. But I think the broad argument that most of the Clan view vampires as superior and aren't interested in turning their food supply into rivals makes a fair amount of sense.

                                                        Baali as the example of an Interesting Clan: No disagreement, the Baali are pretty neat. But if Interesting = "dark and twisted history" and "interesting philosophies," I'm again wondering how the Tzimisce fail to qualify, needs some clarification. If you want "dark and twisted," why doesn't the Cathedral of Flesh qualify? "Interesting philosophies?" Setting aside Metamorphosis, you have the Christian mysticism of the Dracon and their followers in Constantinople, and the Tzimisce more generally being involved in the development of many of the Sabbat's modern Paths of Enlightenment. I'm not saying you can't make distinctions on those points, but I think you need to be more explicit about the "why" since there are a lot of superficial similarities.

                                                        (I do note that the Clan's relationship with the demonic Kupala is one of the things you acknowledge as still being interesting regardless of your other critiques)

                                                        "I wish Vicissitude was more like Lore of the Flesh": This one just seems a bit nit-picky to me? It seems like you don't have any problem with the concept, just the particular mechanics in practice, and I don't see how that makes the Tzimisce as a whole uninteresting. But I am also not really familiar with the Lore of the Flesh, beyond the earlier topic that covered this point more specifically.

                                                        "Tzimisce are just more sadistic Ventrue": This is again a consequence of focusing on the Tzimisce culture as "lordship" rather than "adaptation." It also ignores that Ventrue are fundamentally lords of *people* while Tzimisce are lords of the *land*, which is why they have Animalism and a prominent tradition of pagan Blood Sorcery. The Tzimisce have also always been depicted as more scholarly and mystic than the Ventrue; Ventrue scholars and sorcerers definitely exist, but they have never had a prominent place within the culture of the clan the way that Koldun and Metamorphosist philosophers have among the Tzimisce, to the best of my knowledge. Which is also why Auspex has historically been a Clan Discipline for Tzimisce, while Ventrue with Auspex in-Clan have always been a very minor bloodline or the consequence of ritual alteration.

                                                        On separate notes:

                                                        "The Ventrue and the Brujah need to be Archetypal due to their Clan Weaknesses": Nope. The argument arguably holds better for the Ventrue, since their particular needs will kind of force them to adopt particular strategies to meet those needs, but even within that there is room for them to be battle-lords versus scheming aristocrats/businessmen. See the Antitribu. For the Brujah it's even more incorrect, because there are two fundamental approaches you can take to a weakness like theirs: lean into it, or strive to overcome it. Ancient Brujah developed an entire Path of Enlightenment that focuses on overcoming their destructive impulses, the Path of Entelechy. It's another Path I'm rather fond of, and it serves to demonstrate that the Brujah are capable of being more ordered and constructive than you imagine them. It's just more of a struggle for them. But to paraphrase old Paarthurnax, is it (morally) better to be born with a good temper, or to overcome a bad temper through great effort?

                                                        "Ventrue as creative vs. Brujah as destructive": Again, Nope. Setting aside the above point about Brujah and Entelechy, and setting aside the reasonable argument that all vampires are more parasitic than creative, it would probably be better to imagine a triad that includes the Toreador as Creative vs Ventrue as Static and Brujah as Destructive. And also to comment on the fact that Creation and Destruction are not as separate as they are often framed; the creation of something new almost always involves the destruction of something old (or at least radically changing something old). You destroy the natural stone of a quarry to make the bricks needed to create a city. Creating a new order almost always requires the overthrow of the old order. Which is what the Brujah ideal is all about, the creation of a Vampire/Human utopia. Many Brujah traditionally were part of a group known as the Prometheans who were all about the recreation of Carthage, which in their history was such a utopia (and in the rest of Kindred history was a Baali-infested hellhole, history is fun that way). I would argue that the difference between the Brujah and Ventrue is less about Creation vs. Destruction, and more about Order/Hierarchy vs. Chaos/Anarchy, which are very different things.

                                                            "I wish the Tzimisce were more like amoral Progenitors": I think I need a bit of an explanation regarding how you think the Tzimisce *aren't* like amoral self-serving Progenitors, unless your argument is essentially "not all of them are," in which case I'd be critical of making a clan entirely into a one-dimensional stereotype. Regardless of the Clan, trying to put the exact same Hat on every single member is boring. Or "they don't care enough about evolving humans with them," to which I would respond "why would you want to make your cattle more powerful and dangerous?" That being said, I find the idea of a Tzimisce Metamorphosist who is 100% onboard with the segments of the Obertus Revenants that want to replace traditional humanity with themselves to be quite a neat idea personally. But I think the broad argument that most of the Clan view vampires as superior and aren't interested in turning their food supply into rivals makes a fair amount of sense.

                                                            Baali as the example of an Interesting Clan: No disagreement, the Baali are pretty neat. But if Interesting = "dark and twisted history" and "interesting philosophies," I'm again wondering how the Tzimisce fail to qualify, needs some clarification. If you want "dark and twisted," why doesn't the Cathedral of Flesh qualify? "Interesting philosophies?" Setting aside Metamorphosis, you have the Christian mysticism of the Dracon and their followers in Constantinople, and the Tzimisce more generally being involved in the development of many of the Sabbat's modern Paths of Enlightenment. I'm not saying you can't make distinctions on those points, but I think you need to be more explicit about the "why" since there are a lot of superficial similarities.

                                                            (I do note that the Clan's relationship with the demonic Kupala is one of the things you acknowledge as still being interesting regardless of your other critiques)

                                                            "I wish Vicissitude was more like Lore of the Flesh": This one just seems a bit nit-picky to me? It seems like you don't have any problem with the concept, just the particular mechanics in practice, and I don't see how that makes the Tzimisce as a whole uninteresting. But I am also not really familiar with the Lore of the Flesh, beyond the earlier topic that covered this point more specifically.

                                                            "Tzimisce are just more sadistic Ventrue": This is again a consequence of focusing on the Tzimisce culture as "lordship" rather than "adaptation." It also ignores that Ventrue are fundamentally lords of *people* while Tzimisce are lords of the *land*, which is why they have Animalism and a prominent tradition of pagan Blood Sorcery. The Tzimisce have also always been depicted as more scholarly and mystic than the Ventrue; Ventrue scholars and sorcerers definitely exist, but they have never had a prominent place within the culture of the clan the way that Koldun and Metamorphosist philosophers have among the Tzimisce, to the best of my knowledge. Which is also why Auspex has historically been a Clan Discipline for Tzimisce, while Ventrue with Auspex in-Clan have always been a very minor bloodline or the consequence of ritual alteration.
