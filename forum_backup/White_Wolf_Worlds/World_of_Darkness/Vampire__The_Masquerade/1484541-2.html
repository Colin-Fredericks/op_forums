If they were a piece of shit, that piece of shit has been taken into you and you are now a little bit more a piece of shit. Either way you slice it, Diablerie should have a humanity drop IMO.

                        Originally posted by Vilenecromancer View Post
                        First, you're eating their soul, which is worse than killing as it guarantees that if there is an afterlife in WoD, other than the shadowlands, then they won't see it. Not only that, but it also opens you to being possessed by said monster.
                        First, you're supposedly eating their soul.

                        What if the one doing it simply does not believe that to be the case?

                        You also supposedly run the risk of possession, but considering that a) the text makes it very clear that this is an extremely unlikely outcome even when devouring the power of an extremely ancient vampire (being partially affected by their memories/personality is far more common and already rare), and b) it also says directly that most characters are unaware of such risk, this is both too low and too unlikely to have moral weight in the action.

                        The discussion is not about the effects of Diablerie, but about its impact on Humanity and what it says about Humanity. Do you mean by this that there's an objective, non-personal morality, this morality will get to you even if you're unaware of the "correct" interpretation of your actions, even with this interpretation not necessarily being correct as a phenomenon, just as moral grounds for losing your sense of self?

                        And what if Diablerie is the only way to be sure that your opponent will be completely destroyed? So many vampires have so many means to fake their deaths and flee. Diablerie can give a sense of security in the deed that may actually be absolutely necessary for the task at hand.

                        If we run with the "Classic" Humanity/Roads system, we have the advantage that no moral code need to be "true" to work, as long as it is sufficiently ingrained (which is the reason why merely believing you're in the right does not make you "feel" right, and feeling is paramount to the process), but then Diablerie being inescapable doesn't really make sense. If we run with Requiem 2nd Humanity we have the advantage that it isn't about morals to begin with, and then we can examine the effects through another lens and have the same result with completely distinct meanings for the fiction, but that also means dropping the moral side of the equation completely.

                        Humanity 0 being permanent is its own can of worms. Things get more confusing when you consider that even the authors (or some of them) agree with Herr Meister at least to an extent: Kemintiri is an example of a character that supposedly came back from there with the help of Bardo, and this story has some consistency. That would make at least one known way back from Wassail. But I think that for now it is simply too hard to even get there to worry so much with the come back process, like too hard even if you try, and you hardly have any reason to try.

                        Just stationing at Humanity 1 or 2 is too easy even for the most depraved vampire, unless Diablerie Rules fuck them, and as long as we have this little problem, being able to bounce back becomes an afterthought. For now there are only two ways to go that low that are likely to affect a PC, or to get even an NPC unaware: failing at changing Paths or getting addicted to Diablerie. Even then both are hard to end that badly in practice. Without other motivations to get there, there are few stories to be told about the downfall to Wassail, and no need to come back from something you won't get into.

                        Requiem solved this partially with two additional Rank 1 Breaking Points, but keeping in mind that Humanity in Requiem, again, is more about sense of self and ability to function among the sheep than being a moral person. You risk the last dot if you go 10 years without any human contact or if you kill your Touchstone yourself, even by accident (Touchstones in Requiem have less downsides, more and better benefits and are far easier to replace, even expected to be so no matter your age). This makes the risk of losing it far more palpable, something you can succumb to by accident, intervention of your enemies, or even trying to do good, yet still so hard that you won't find yourself at the end of the line just by dumb luck, but you certainly have to keep on your toes about where your Humanity is going to. It has also far smarter Draugr, the Humanity 0 vampires that still follow the concept CajunKhan gave, but can even fool the other vampires in believing they're still functional if not pressed hard (hunting Draugr is a far more pressing matter in Requiem).

                            One way to better define why you lose humanity is to understand what is the beast and what is his influence.
                            As described the beast is an instinct of pure destruction, the animality of the vampire that devour him with time.

                            So why are you losing humanity? When tou awake the beast or accept his influence you let it take over, so you become more a monster. But it also feed on you when you let your guard down, when you're feeling shame, sadness or whatever emotional combination that weakens your mind and allow the beast to force his grip on the character.

                            So the beast grow stronger when you let it or when you're mind is too weaken to resist
                            What can weaken your mind? Well this is where it get difficult, because it depend on the person: a soldier would better accept killing when it's self defense or for a very good reason but a doctor will never accept that.
                            Humanity as described in the game is a generic system for the character, but the same way there's path of enlightment there's different morality, and therefore humanity depending on the person
                            So basicaly if you want to make humanity working the way you want in your game you must define a clear moral code for each of the players:
                            A soldier would accept killing (no roll or at lower difficulty) while a doctor would not accept it unless getting really low in his humanity
                            In addition it bring mixity in the different way of playing, the players can complete the other when one can do something the others cannot

                                        Well, there is a third, or maybe a 2.5 option to that list: Vampires that lose their last dot of Humanity because a Path vampire is trying to indoctrinate them and pushes them too far.

                                        Losing that last few dots of Humanity pre-V5 depends a lot on how the ST treats the Beast and Frenzies. As you drop to low Humanity, you lose dice to resist Frenzy, making them more common. If you have to roll degeneration checks for actions taken during such Frenzies, and the Beast pushes your PC into the horrific acts necessary for low Humanity loss, the possibility does grow significantly compared to either or both of those interpretations being treated differently.

                                        It's not a particularly fun thing, because it's basically waiting for the ST to screw you and take your character away, but it is a possibility in how one might play the game.

                                        Again, I think VtR 2e does this better. There are Humanity checks that you run the risk of over time even at Humanity 1. You can never be truly safe there, even without the ST needing to hijack your PC during a Frenzy to make you do horrific things. Diablerie addiction in VtR 2e is probably one of the surest ways to eventually drop to Humanity zero because while VtR 2e don't make it automatic Humanity loss, it's always a check, and a lot of the ways to help get bonuses to detachment breaking points are reduced or lost at low Humanity.
