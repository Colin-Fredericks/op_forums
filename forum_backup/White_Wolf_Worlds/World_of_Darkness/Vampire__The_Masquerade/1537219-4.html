    Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
    This is a bit us WoD5 critics kind of clocked to at the start: all these setting changes were not made to add more stories, but to SUBTRACT existing ones. They were written to sweep away NPCs and factions from the old continuity, so V5's writers would never need to deal with them ever again.

    They were never meant to be mysteries. They were excuses. Excuses for why the setting is suddenly a huge blank slate.

    At least V20's "Metaplot Agnostic" approach didn't invalidate the old fixtures. It just made them optional. And it still took time to make its own plot as the edition went on, like with the Montmartre Pact or BJD. They knew the metaplot had value, even if it shouldn't be a shackle around the wrists of Storytellers.

    V5's "mysteries" were nothing of the sort, because they were never meant to be. All they existed to do was wipe the slate clean. And then V5's writers proceeded to replace it with basically nothing.
    Eh, the metaplot agnostic approach of V20 effectively "reset the clock" with all of the events in the setting from the Clan Novels to the Kuei Jin invasion to other events in the books. Which invalidated a very large chunk of supplements. This isn't always a bad thing and plenty of people liked it but a lot of things like the Assamites, Ravnos, Salubri Antitribu, and thin Bloods were heavily tied to the advancing metaplot.

    I can't dispute the idea that Paradox Interactive didn't actually have a plan for the things they created, though. The Beckoning and gehenna War seem to have been rewritten mulitple times behind the scenes and the Sabbat were transformed multiple times. They also weirdly had an aversion to being clear about some things.

    Like, why NOT just say, "The Kuei Jin are non-canon now and we're never going to address them." It would have saved some trouble, I think and I generally approve of that decision.

    The Second Inquisition is a continuation of my point above. V5's writers plainly did not conceptualize hunter orgs like this as potential protagonists of their own stories. (Something made depressingly obvious by H5's open, textual, seething contempt for hunter orgs). The SI was intended exclusively as a Vampire-facing fixture. An antagonist faction that existed to hammer Kindred down when they got too uppity, and to enforce greater levels of secrecy and insularity. Especially on the part of the Camarilla, which V5's designers didn't want players acting within anymore. (It's the same reason why the Sabbat got blown up; the Gehenna Wars are not playable, because V5 did not want people playing Sabbat characters).

    In earlier editions, SAD and other groups got detailed write-ups, internal factions, and NPCs, because they were meant to be playable. Now the SI are baddies thrown at VtM players if they deviate from the street-level play V5's was meant for.
    I come from a very different school of VTM thought that you don't NEED for a faction to be playable to want them to be as detailed as player characters. Chicago by Night wrote up the antagonists the same way they would player characters with all the personality, motivations, and goals needed because that was inherently more interesting and effective. Lodin may have been a piece of shit no one was expected to like or work with but the write-up for him but damn if I didn't know what made him tick.

    Which made him a much better villain when I did use him.

    Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post

    Yeah, when V5 first released, even though I was fairly tepid towards it overall, I was looking forward to some of the metaplot mysteries that the game had put forward. What is the Beckoning? What happened at the Family Reunion? What exactly is the nature of the Ministry/Followers of Set split? I've seen a lot of different interpretations on that, from the Ministry just being a rebranding, to the Ministry tossing Set out and replacing the idea of Set with worshipping numerous different figures.
    ​
    I get the impression that it fell upon (a) certain author(s) with OPP to fill in that gap and others. Not going to say it outright that I don't think Paradox cared beyond, "oh yeah, they're Anarchs now."

    I think the OPP idea of an ideological split along "god of darkness" ecumenist polytheism and monotheistic orthodox Typhonism was the best one of them by far and unsurprisingly what they were hinting at in BJD.

    CULTS OF THE BLOOD GODS really felt like trying to save as much as possible while being lore friendly to new players.
    Last edited by CTPhipps; 06-20-2024, 09:48 PM.

    Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

        The Followers were always one of my favorite clans in theory, but I always felt disappointed that the clan of religious fanatics didn't have schisms, fights over interpretation, inquisitions and the like. Rather than one large monolithic clan, I always wished that the game had presented the Followers as a large group of smaller competing sects, which would work together, but were often as much enemy as ally. In theory you did have this sort of stuff back in the clan's past (especially if you dig into the Typhon-Set concepts) and the concept of different types (warrior/priest/witch). By the end of V20 you finally started to see that with different smaller cults (the Children of Judas, the Church of Black Magdalene, the Hall of Jorgmunder, the House of Eclipse, etc) and I was really digging the direction that the Followers of Set were finally going..

        Then we get V5 with the Ministry/Followers split, and the concept was initially great because I felt that the Clan had finally gone in the direction I had always wanted to see them go (with the Ministry basically being a gathering for numerous different cults whose primary similarity was in the fact that they rejected Set as the primary god-figure), but it just ended up being kind of a mess as to what the split actual entails, how it actually looks and how you can use that as a major focus for a chronicle.

                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                        I know no one will care and it's hardly a thing I should care about but I am leaving V5 behind and returning permanently to V20 with the only exception being incorporating the lore from the Onyx Path publishing books for V5. I've long been a cheerleader for V5 and was a defender of this when they were otherwise very unpopular but I just can't continue onward and I'm not sure I'm even going to buy any more V5 material other than the Visual Novels and Bloodlines.

                        Why?

                        It's the handling of Legacy.

                        The reason I came back to V:TM was because of the announcement it was going to be the Old World of Darkness revived. I liked what OPP to save the license with V20 but never really jived with the "metaplot agnostic" approach and it was only when they adjusted that I changed my view on things. BECKETT'S JYHAD DIARY remained the absolute greatest book ever written for V:TM and celebrated everything I loved about the setting.

                        I also came back for CBN and its spin off titles, spending way too much money (money well spent) on the Kickstarter. Same for Cults of the Blood Gods.

                        But the decisions for WoD5 are now exactly the opposite of what I loved.

                        Less and less metaplot, less and less NPCs, less and less lore.

                        W5 should have been the death blow to my idealism with the hints that W:TA 1-20 were now non-canon and irreconcilable but I stubbornly denied what was in front of my face. I should have known you can only trust people with a genuine love of the material. I don't get that feeling outside of the livestream games.

                        So from now on, it'll just be cherishing what OPP has already made and Storyteller's Vault material.

                        You guys were right.
                        You lasted longer than me mate, I wish it wasn't so, but here we are.

                                                I think Paradox as a company is a bad one. It's only thanks to a few of their games which are these juggernauts on the market that they stay profitable. Aside from those, namely Hearts of Iron IV, Crusader Kings 3 and Stellaris, Paradox as a company would be in a world of trouble. If there's anything that has been a constant in the last few years, it's them fucking up as a publisher.

                                                Let's go over some of the highlights over the last few years:
                                                • Lamplighter's Leauge: Paradox had high hopes for the game and Harebrained Games are a good developer (they made those isometric Shadowrun games, for example). But months before the launch, thanks to a lack of support from Paradox 80% of the staff at the studio were let go. Then the game got released to middling reviews (mostly because of the bugs and lack of content) and then Paradox had to announce that it considered the game a failure. And it doesn't seem like there's any more content planned for it.
                                                • City Skylines 2: That game launched in a disastrous state. Just google it. A complete failure. The developers are still busy trying to crawl out of that hole and win back the goodwill of the players...
                                                • Star Trek Infinite: One of the more popular mods for their Sci-Fi-grand strategy game Stellaris revolves around Star Trek. And so Paradox thought: Hey, let's have someone develop an official version. And that's Star Trek Infinite except... the fan-mods are simply better. The game launches, disappoints (middling reviews, lack of interest, sales...) and six months after launch it's announced that Paradox will stop any support for the game. In other words, Paradox isn't paying the devs to patch the game or make DLC for it.
                                                • Life By You: It was pitched as a new Sims-type life-building game. They even created a studio for it called Tectone. Well, a month ago or so, Paradox announced it had decided to cancel the game. This game had been in development since 2019. Five years of development, a whole studio of developers and 19 million dollars in investment... all flushed down the toilet. Ah, yeah, they closed the studio Tectone as well on top of that.

                                                Bloodlines 2 turning out to be a failure wouldn't be that surprising.
