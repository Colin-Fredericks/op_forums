    It depends on how you answer the question, "How many people does your average vampire kill?"

    Which varies from chronicle to chronicle and ST to ST.

    My take on the subject is that every vampire has probably killed one or two person they didn't mean to kill during their unlives, even as Neonates. However, my take on the subject is very few vampires are FLIPPANT about this (which is a small change but presentation is all). Most vampires in my games go out of their way not to kill people and their failures are things that haunt them. Anarchs and Sabbat both have people who gleefully indulge in murder as do Elders. However, those vampires who don't care are rapidly lost to wassail. An Anarch who casually kills people in his preferred feeding type is going to be someone that other Anarchs avoid or think of as sick.

    I don't think that these Anarchs are very Son-like but seem much more like Gordon Keaton. Which is to say he doesn't care about killing vampires but lacks the sadism. "Oh, well, drank too much. Shame. Now I have to dispose of the body. That's going to be a pain in the ass." Even so, that kind of vampire is going to be on the margins of Anarch society or part of a very nasty gang.

                    Originally posted by Konradleijon View Post
                    Yeah but by not talking about it. It makes it look like the Anarchs hate the Camarilla for no reason, and they are just a bunch of whiney teenagers,
                    Everyone is going to bring their own biases, inspirations, and ideas to the Anarchs. Since they're basically defined as a negative (originally not the Camarilla, and now not the Camarilla or Sabbat) they're a blank slate the ST will project on. So the devs may be trying not to step on people's toes by "defining" things. Still, I agree a lack of some local groups and their ideas would be a good thing as long as it is clear that such a list is neither definitive nor comprehensive.

                    My own view is that the Anarchs can be divided into several groups.

                    1) Misfits, outcasts, and oddballs who aren't political at all, but rub the people in power the wrong way so they get labelled as Anarchs. On one hand, everyone knows they're quite harmless so they usually get left alone. On the other hand, they make convenient scapegoats when one is needed. Other Anarchs really don't consider them to "anarchs", but they're not seen as enemies either. Rubes to be ignored or exploited. Probably less than 10% of the Anarch population.

                    2) Loners who got hassled and fought back. I'll call this the John Rambo scenario when an abusive sheriff needlessly hassles a guy who just wants to move through and all hell breaks lose. This is like the first group, but the powers that be judge the situation wrong and it backfires on them. Probably happens more in low populated domains with a stable Kindred population than bigger domains when the Prince and his cronies have better things to do. These guys may not consider themselves Anarchs and would probably be willing to live in a Camarilla city provided their reputation does not precede them, but sometimes it does and they're stuck with the label. Again less than 10% of the population.

                    3) Adolescent vampires who don't like to be told what to do and like abusing their power. This is the typical poseur vampire and made up a lot of the original Chicago By Night anarchs. These characters would likely "grow out of it" provided they live long enough. I suspect many Camarilla vampires know this, and this is why the sect had many anarch sympathizers - they know these people will eventually join the system. They will mostly bitch and complain a lot, but will keep their heads down and keep their crimes minimal. Not really anarchs, but they make up a large recruitment ground for the "revolution". Probably around 15%.

                    4) Outright criminals. These people aren't revolutionaries, they're just criminals of vampire society. But because they're hardened and willing to deal in physical violence, they make up a lot of the muscle of the "revolution". However, because they're criminals, while they are useful, they will always be a problem for the "movement". Both in terms of the movement's reputation and what to do with them should they succeed in creating a new "free state". Some of these people would be perfectly willing to sell out to the Camarilla in return for amnesty and position. Much of Juggler's crew from the original Chicago By Night fits here. Smiling Jack too. Probably another 15%.

                    5) Vampires with a just (or at least reasonable) complaint against the local Prince. This is more about complaints against the despotic actions of the local guy in charge than any reasoned alternative to the Camarilla system. But it's probably the bulk of the anarch population. These people may not be happy with the Camarilla, but could tolerate it if the local guy wasn't such a jackass. Their ideals don't go beyond "we need to replace this guy with another." Some of these people could very well be recruited for the greater Anarch cause, but just as many would be willing to cooperate with the Camarilla afterwards. Again, many of the Camarilla's anarch sympathizers are due to knowing people in this category. Large number of Anarchs here. Maldavis is probably a good example here, although I know some will put her in a more idealistic group. I'll say 25%.

                    6) Recruited into the cause. These are vampires that exist solely to be cannon fodder for the revolution. As such, they'd normally be a combination of #3, #4 and #5, but the distinction being is that they were embraced to give the Anarch numbers and their ignorance is exploited by their sires (all for the right reasons, of course). A lot probably get killed quickly. Like the criminals, they're here to provide muscle - disposable muscle. Should they survive, they'll either be angry at being exploited or become dedicated Anarchs. Anita Wainwright is probably an example early on. Maybe 10% - mostly in domains being contested to some degree.

                    7) Actual dissidents from the Camarilla. These people aren't just upset that the local Prince is a jerk. They have major problems with the entire system of the Camarilla - the Inner Circle, Justicars, Archons, Conclaves, and Princes. Whether out of idealism, a desire to end tyranny of the elders, or megalomania, these vampires have given serious thought as to the system of governance which will replace the Camarilla. The big probably is that there are various factions with their own system of government. So they're like the Spanish Republicans of the Spanish Civil War - constitutional democrats, socialists, anarchists, and communist all fighting against a common enemy, but hating much of their own side, and some factions are actively killing their own side as well. Without them, there really is not an Anarch movement, but they're also the reason the Anarchs will never win or unite. Hind's Socialists in CbN (including Blackjack), and Jeremy MacNeil's group are the examples here. This is also where the game has quite frankly disappointed the fans. We really don't get an idea of what the future is that they're fighting for. We never learn how exactly Hinds and Blackjack intend to apply socialist principles to a vampire domain. We do learn about Jeremy MacNeil's strange libertarian ideas, but LA By Night never presents us with other visions. This is probably the block that needs the most development in the game. Current Anita Wainwright is probably here. Probably another 25%, but they make up a disproportionate number of Anarch leaders and most of its philosophers.

                    8) Collaborators and Movement Exploiters. These are people not really sympathetic to the Anarchs, but jump to them either because they're on the winning side (I would say David Geduld in LABN) or because they're the best option to gain power (Gordon Keaton in CbN). They hitch their ride because its an opportunity to gain power. The game has plenty of other examples. Relatively small number, but because these are people seeking power, they have a tendency to become vital to the movement as movers and shakers especially in the middle tiers before they get big enough to run their own crew. Of course, most of them would not hesitate to defect to the Camarilla is they can. Probably less than 10% of the Anarch population.

                        Night Circus



                        This is one of my favorite sections in the book – the Night Circus. A circus with vampires getting away with shit because they are vampires. It’s a fun read with a lot of story possibilities.

                        The Anarchs of the Night Circus use disciplines to draw crowds – which provide them with money and feeding victims. This is done under the guise of a grab bag of circus shows to (barely) keep it all from being one colossal masquerade breach. For example, the person compelling the animals to do tricks is using Animalism, the hypnotist getting people to do the chicken dance is using Dominate, and the trapeze artists is simply using the fact she can get up and run off after an 50-foot fall.

                        This section carries bit of Something Wicked This Way Comes, Freaks, The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari, and presumably Vampire Circus (which I’ve not seen but the title is right). The Night Circus has been around for centuries, so it might be something the PCs encountered as mortal children only to run into it again. This aspect, and the relative appeal of a circus (“run away and join the circus,” as the saying goes) means could the show could be a starting point for mortals who become vampires.

                        On the other hand, that same seductive appeal of a circus – and the call of the road – might well persuade Camarilla PCs to joining the Night Circus on its travels. It does sound like a lot of fun with few responsibility or obligations to bow to Princes. Even if they don’t join it, they PCs might easily get roped into dealing with the Night Circus.

                        In 1996 (24 years? God damn it) White Wolf published Midnight Circus. That supplement was intended to hit similar notes, but it mostly suffered from being over stuffed. All the splats at the time (Vampire, Werewolf, Mage, Wraith, and Changeling) found representation in the book, in addition to other worldly entities and an evil plot from the dawn of time. Night Circus is leaner, but might still be home to any number of strange vampires.

                        This section does a nice job of depicting Anarchs who are generally apolitical, but still deadly and wicked.

                            Delusions of Humanity

                            This is an interesting read and another that feels like the back story of a PC.

                            A former au pair for a British couple talks about how the husband of the couple was - and is - secretly a vampire. And he turned her into a vampire, even as he led a highly closeted life. The vampiric au pair had the wife of the couple turned into a vampire out of pity as much as anything else. Eventually the wife and the vampiric au pair fled the scene, leaving the children to the closeted vampire husband. The vampiric au pair is an Anarch probably more by Camarilla definition than by her own political stance.

                            The section reads more like bathos than outright comedy, even dark comedy. There is some interesting, if bleak, insight into human nature with the husband in denial of his own nature and the wife drowning in her own misery and finding pitiable relief in a fantasy life. But aside from being a possible background for a PC, the section offers little for a VtM game, Anarch or not.

                                    I'm not certain i would call it healthier...

                                    Is it OK to feed vitae to a baby?

                                    This is my favorite single part of the book because it is utterly made of wrong.

                                    In this short section an Anarch with a mortal child posts to Anarch Reddit, asking if it is alright to feed vitae to the human baby. You know, to settle them down if they are fussy or something and you are out of the whiskey you normally use. A bunch of other Anarchs jump in to say yes, or no, and even discuss moderate feeding from their own human children. Assuming that the respondents are not utterly full of crap, then a striking number of Anarchs have mortal children. It is worth considering if those children are actually their own (as in ones they had in the normal way while still human), or ones they picked up somewhere along the way. The Anarchs arguably are taking the children of others - possibly their murder victims - and then raising and grooming the children to be ghouls. At which point the Anarchs have children in the same way humans have cats and dogs. In any case, one or more of the children's actual parents may be out and about looking for them, putting posters up on telephone poles and the like.

                                    Vampires are not good people. Vampires cannot be good people. The Camarilla is simply Control, and the Anarchs are simply Chaos.

                                    This section was also sadly cut when the book was reworked, following the issues with the Camarilla book. It is disappointing this section was removed as it clearly depicts the Anarchs as monsters in a way almost nothing can.
