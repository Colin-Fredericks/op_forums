                    So.. to still be topical and to try to put things another way since it's coming up, right, so, the revised Sabbat and opinions on that, both in contrast to V5 and otherwise. Honestly there were some good moves there in my view.

                    Take otherwise for example something pre revised like Montreal by Night (I do not mean to harsh anyone who enjoyed Montreal By Night, but on the other hand, to me personally, woof). Anything else you could say about it besides, it certainly does have more of the Sabbat portrayal that seems to influence where CTPhipps' understanding of the sect came from (on the other hand it does also have some npcs who clearly understand 'we can't just be an overt dumb freeforall' so, it's a mix).

                    One of the things that aggravated me about this book was that the entire whole story and plotline of it boils down to infernalism shenanigans. Which just for me completely sucks all the air out of doing things with it, if I wanted to have a focus that isn't infernalism shenanigans and get use out of this book. Cripes there's an outright Decani under the city, and most of the plot boils down to the rivalry between it and another Decani. That's so huge it is almost unignorable. It touches almost every npc in some way or other. It's not even a hidden methuselah type rivalry. It's demons playing shenanigans with vampires. And this is basically kind of the signature Sabbat city as far as the underlying plot being that.

                    Whereas in revised, the central plot around Montreal instead shifted to Cardinal Strathcona attempting to slowly and carefully encourage a Camarilla siege of the city that while not succeeding (or so he hopes), does succeed *enough* to spark a renewed spirit of unity and dedication in the sect (or so he hopes) by threatening its religious heart, because he feels the Sabbat are falling short of their ideals in the modern nights (and the idea that, hey, maybe he's not wrong, is something that can be thereby explored/debated). There's politics in that, potential direct conflict, philosophical debate, a bunch of stuff.

                    That to me was pretty neat, and left a lot more room to play around with a lot more things. Certainly it had more depth as a concept than, y'know, demons warring for influence over each other with vampires as pawns (I actually don't hate Baali/Infernalism plots, they have their place and use and can be done very interesting, have a place in horror/corruption tales, and hey, sometimes as a break it's good to just fight something explicitly the worst. But holy eff making the entire city an infernalist plotline is kind of a lot. A looooooooooooot.).

                    So I would say revised was certainly capable of taking the Sabbat and exploring interesting ideas with them, at least in some ways. Perhaps not all ways of course. There were for instance some npcs in the pre revised Sabbat that you didn't really have the caliber of in the post revised one, as an example. But I think saying that revised was 100% bereft of interesting handles on the Sabbat, or even a couple of improvements, is a bit much.

                            As much as I think infernalism is inherently interesting and having it as a part of the Sabbat is a great thing*, I generally prefer Revised as my favorite version of the Sabbat. V5's take on the Sabbat is useless to me as a Sabbat Storyteller and player. It seems like the END of the Sabbat as an organization and while that isn't automatically a bad plotline and has room for ex-Sabbat stories or building a new Sabbat, it definitely requires a lot of that to be done. This seems like a coda for the Sabbat as a sect: The Sabbat have degenerated and are on the verge of losing everything that made them great.

                            The Revised verison of the Sabbat struck a good balance with the fact the Sabbat were overtly monstrous and despicable villains but also people who had a decent number of people actually seeking higher posthuman enlightenment. There was a tension between the two sides of the sect that made for an interesting twist. You had the PATHS OF ENLIGHTENMENT vampires who were inhuman, alien, and spiritual versus the Low Humanity...assholes for lack of a better description. Part of what I think is a problem is they seemed to have combined these mutually exclusive ideas for V5's Sabbat.

                            A Path of Enlightenment follower shouldn't be a mindless ravening beast. That isn't what Paths are about. A Path follower is weird and alien in their morality. The Low Humanity vampires are the ones who act like soccer hooligans.

                            At least how I always played them.

                            * I think they tried to get rid of all the infernalism in the Sabbat to make them more playable and I feel this eliminated an interesting setting element.

                                The problem with the infernalism as a player facing thing in the Sabbat is that the writers never seemed to have a handle on using it without it basically dominating the plot where it came up. The idea of the Sabbat was to have a lot going on with them ideologically, politically, and otherwise, as much as any other playable group, that was an outright almost verbatim mission statement with the group once it became playable. The problem of infernalism, or certainly the way it was written, was that it basically took up a lot of space in which other things could fit in a playable Sabbat.

                                Maybe if it had been written differently? But as it stood it was a thing that when invoked took up a lot of conceptual real estate that other very playable notions of the sabbat had to fight for space with. In the case of a full out city book this verged on the literal.

                                I mean it was still there to a degree in revised. Certainly something like Archons and Templars devoted space to talking about how Inquisitors did their thing and if you wanted the players to be them and etc. etc. and I thought that had some interesting handling.

                                I did kind of like the idea as far as the Inquisition that the sect who best understood what a terrible thing the infernal was and that it had to be combatted was, of all people, the Sabbat. It was both funny and interesting as an ironic thing as one of the dark little commentaries Vampire could make.

                                    I think the thing about running a Sabbat game is exploring what good and evil means to a society that considers vampire life sacred, and homo sapien life to be irrelevant beyond purely practical concerns. How do you build such a society? Infernalism certainly makes for an easy adversary in such a society, since it involves giving power over to creatures who want to enslave vampires.

                                    However, it's rather unsubtle. More complex issues come up when considering the balance of freedom and the survival of the vampire species. Do you want to become purely military? That helps the Sabbat defeat their enemies, but destroys the freedom that makes unlife worth unliving.

                                    Should you devote yourself purely to advancing on a Path of Enlightenment? That helps tremendously with your personal peace of mind and freedom from the beast, but takes away time from you working to preserve the survival of all Sabbat.

                                    How much recklessness should be tolerated with regards to treatment of homo sapiens. While there is no evil that can be done to them, since they are not vampires, they are dangerous in rather the way that uranium is dangerous. That is to say, they are a valuable source of power that can destroy everyone if treated recklessly. How much recklessness do you tolerate before considering the balance between freedom and security to have overbalanced in the direction of freedom?

                                                My thoughts on the Infernal is the idea the Sabbat are the best sect to do themes of occultism and dark mysteries. As much as I love the Tremere, I feel like they come across as a bit mechanalistic in terms of exploring dark secrets and supernatural power. If you want to do things like robed torch-lit ceremonies in the middle of graveyards, Cthulhu-esque idols, and "things man was not meant to knoiw" then the Sabbat are the best people to explore.

                                                The infernal was, admittedly, shown to be the "designated bad guys" of the Sabbat but Montreal by Night always struck me as something that really did a great job establishing the Sabbat as thoroughly infiltrated by demonic forces but having people willing to fight and die to purify the Sabbat of their influence. Yet it had the interesting extra layer that these people were STILL damned and evil so what were they fighting for?

                                                BLOOD CULTS did a good job of moving what I loved best of the Sabbat into mainstream vampire society but I still felt very few people ever did the "Anti-Catholicism" nearly as well. Maybe my infernalist ridden verison of the Sabbat was a little too CHILLING ADVENTURES OF SABRINA but it was always a lot of fun.
