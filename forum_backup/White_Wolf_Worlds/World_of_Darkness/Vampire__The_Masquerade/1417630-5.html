                                Originally posted by SuperSabbatST View Post
                                I actually always assumed their number was in the low hundreds, like most bloodlines.
                                I never assigned them any numbers, but I had a general idea in my head that the Lasombra AT were "confined" to a handful of cities or regions within the general Camarilla domain. That was mainly several cities in Spain/Portugal, Italy, the Southern Cone and Brazil in South America, the Maghreb, and a few other random cities that would be considered "non-traditional" for Lasombra. In those cities, they were essentially the eighth clan of the Camarilla locally. But they were absent elsewhere for a variety of reasons. One was that Lasombra AT that left the nest to be by themselves without other Lasombra AT around them were easily prone to being slain by roving Sabbat (or have other mysterious deaths/disappearances). Another was that few Camarilla Princes outside those domains wanted a Lasombra AT in their domain, and they were heavily discouraged from visiting or staying. That was either out of a desire for less competition from another clan; old grudges against the clan; or not wanting to attract Sabbat attacks. The British Isles in particular were known to be free of them.

                                So there easily could have been one or two hundred. (In contrast, I think most bloodlines have far fewer numbers than you. I imagined most bloodlines to be in the dozens, not hundreds.)

                                I held that Sabbat statements about having destroyed almost all the Lasombra AT were mainly propaganda and hyperbole combined with ignorance as to the true state of many Camarilla domains.

                                Like all things, depending on one's assumptions and tastes, you can have a wide variety of answers and fit within canon lore.

                                One reason I wanted a noticeable, albeit still rare, presence in the Camarilla is that I thought much of the clan's description in the clanbooks made sense for a Camarilla clan, but a lot less for the Sabbat. A major problem I had with the Sabbat was the clanbooks acting like the two Sabbat clans were similar to the Camarilla clans instead of being heavily influenced by the Anarch Revolt and the institutions and culture of the Sabbat. I briefly discussed that here and here. So in my own chronicles I altered the Sabbat versions of the clan to better reflect that. As a result, some of the interesting aspects of the Lasombra were excised from the Sabbat, so I made them a prominent part of the culture of the anti-tribu in the Camarilla.

                                                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                            No, because Sabbat agents are smarter than Camarilla ones. There's entire books about Sabbat infiltrators and they will fool Camarilla Primogens and Princes 9/10. The "open door policy" of the Camarilla almost always ends in the Sabbat's favor.

                                                            Sabbat infiltrators almost ALWAYS accomplish their mission.
                                                            This is something I think every ST needs to determine for themselves because I always found this idea to be stupid. Princes just don't acknowledge whoever shows up in their domain. They have the power to refuse admission. Nor does acknowledgment simply require a vampire to give their name and clan, and the Prince accepts it at face value like a chump. It's more like an interview collecting lots of personal data that can be verified later. Who is their sire? How long have they been a vampire? What were there past domains? Who can vouch for their character? And there's likely a probationary period where a new vampire is allowed to stay, but not truly acknowledged while things get checked out. And during this time they are watched by ghouls, mortal servants, and the Prince's henchmen and allies.

                                                            I think this gets overlooked by a lot of STs and PCs because people want to move on with the game, but that's actually what would happen. Different Princes and different domains will vary in terms of their strictness and leniency, but you can bet when it appears any threat rises, Princes become more strict. The Sabbat Crusades of the 1960s would have put everyone on alert for the next few decades.

                                                            While all sects have their geniuses and fools, in general a vampire does not get to be Prince unless they're smart and cunning. So this idea the Sabbat can easily infiltrate is just dumb. No system is fool proof, so infiltration can and does happen. But I don't find the idea credible that Sabbat infiltrators always accomplish their mission. There's a lot of risk. Some fail. And to make a mission succeed, the Sabbat needs to spend time and resources to enable it to happen so even success has its cost. And if you burn a long time deep cover agent to accomplish something, there's extensive opportunity costs.

                                                            In general, the game has often relied on the idea that NPCs are stupid in order to make the metaplot work. This was very true in Revised. But it makes for bad movies, and it makes for a bad RPG setting.
