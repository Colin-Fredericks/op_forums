            That's a far, far cry from the current "darkly heroic" framing of Anarchs by certain parts of the community. "Fighting the Man" has always been part of the setting and theme, sure, but that doesn't mean it's an heroic or even antiheroic conflict by default against designated villains.

            People need to remember this is a game about nocturnal sociopaths with a compulsion to hunt and kill humans that extends beyond the need to simply survive, with a fight-or-flight instinct that turns them into Doom Guy at the drop of a hat. There's no heroism or antiheroism there, just self-delusion.

            Bad World of Darkness Plot:

            The player characters are hired by the Prince to kidnap someone. They find out it's a little girl. They turn over the little girl to the Prince and she's eaten. They collect their fee.

            The player characters are hired by the Prince to kidnap someone. They find out it's a little girl. They raise hell on Earth to try to protect her. They might succeed or might fail.
            No. Just, no.

            Good World of Darkness plot:
            The player characters are hired by the Prince to kidnap someone. And they have to figure out the best possible way to navigate the clusterfuck in which they find themselves and keep their necks intact, to whatever end that may be.

            Bad World of Darkness plot:
            The player characters are hired by the Prince to kidnap someone. They find out it's a little girl. There's no nuance, moral quandry, or deeper levels to this, it's just purely superficial with one "right" way to resolve the conflict towards which players are shepherded regardless.

                                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                Because if there's no sense the PCs actually want to do the right thing then there's no fucking point in Humanity.
                                In my experience, most players do not find it fun to roleplay the whole "Beast I am lest a beast I become" that is supposedly the heart of the game. Some players do indeed like that, but let's face it, a lot of fun with RPG is a power fantasy element. Too much concern over Humanity, or too many scenes that put Humanity in jeopardy can suck the fun out of the game. They want that to be an occasional spice used in the game, not the main thing done.

                                Usually players decide there is a certain level of Humanity they are comfortable for their PC (and the "right" level of Humanity can change from PC to PC for many players). Anything above that, they don't care that much for morality checks. They only start caring when it involves things that drop them past the level they like for their style of play.

                                Some players do want their PC to be fairly high Humanity, say 7 or above. Usually the PCs that slot there are vampires not overly concerned about the "typical" plots and activities of most Vampire games. They aren't concerned about their PCs becoming powerful politically, fighting any combat, sect wars, etc. They're interested in other aspects of the setting. They'll typically make the "right" moral choice because they'll avoid most moral dilemmas entirely. They won't turn over a kidnapped child to the Prince to be eaten because they have no reason to do so in the first place. The Prince simply cannot offer them anything that is meaningful for them. There may be moments when such PCs do face a moral dilemma that tests them, but such moments are rare and typically have meaning. And if they are not rare, but common, they tend to get pissed off at the ST for constantly targeting them to lose Humanity when all they want to do is have fun (their way) roleplaying a fairly moral vampire.

                                Other players don't want to be moral monsters, but are perfectly happy to settle at a noticeably lower level of Humanity that allows them to accomplish most of their goals. They are happy to settle on Humanity 5. They don't intend for their PCs to be murderers, but they want to build power, increase their status, and defend themselves when necessary. These PCs often start with much higher Humanity. When I ST, I typically ask my players what level of Humanity they would be comfortable at for most of the game. If they start at 7, but know their playing style is going to drop that to 5, I ask them how often do they want to do roll a Conscience check that is above their "target" of 5. I get all sorts of answers. Many players do not want to roleplay out any kind of remorse or moral drama. They are find having their Humanity drop 7 to 6 and then to 5 automatically. Only once they reach the level they feel is "appropriate" for their character, do they want real Conscience checks. Other players tell me they want to decide so to give them an option to do so when I think a check is required, and they'll tell me if that situation would be fun for them. Some players always want it. It just depends on how much this aspect of the game is fun for them.

                                More rarely, the PC intends to be a real thorough going bastard. They know their character is going to fall to 3-4, maybe even lower. I give them the same options of determining whether they roll above that level of Humanity. Typically such players don't find it fun to roleplay through Humanity checks. They know their character is a ruthless bastard and find roleplaying fake conscience checks to just be in the way of having fun.

                                Typically the only time I see players really enjoy playing through preserving their humanity is when their PC is at a middle group. They have a comfortable level of Humanity, but really don't want to go below 5, and legitimate scenes that force them to make a decision at that point of Humanity (but not before) is something they can enjoy. As long as they truly make the decision in a scene they think is appropriate for their character. But like the first group, they get upset if they think the ST is purposefully creating plot to force them to make rolls, rather than something they find as organic.

                                But in no case do I see players wanting "to do the right thing" regardless for their PC. That's not why they play Vampire. If they want to have PCs where they always make the morally good choice, they typically will play some other RPG setting.

                                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                They're not loyal in the fucking slightest.
                                Whether or not the Anarchs are any kind of "controlled" or "loyal" opposition really depends on what kind of Anarchs you are talking about, and the expectations of the individual player.

                                I would argue most Anarchs in the game, and most PC Anarchs, just tend to want to get rid of a "bad" Prince (who makes fun of them and does not treat them as favorites) and put in a "good' Prince instead (who makes fun of other characters, and treats them as favorites). Such characters could indeed be seen as "controlled opposition" by certain people (characters and players) because they aren't going against "the system". Yeah, you can replace Lodin with Maldavis, but "the system" hasn't changed at all. You still have a Prince. You still have a Primogen. You still have the salon culture and Elysium and all of that.

                                I think very few Anarchs actually want to abolish "the system" because neither the characters or players have any idea of what would replace it. If they do, it tends to be a fairly weak city domain democracy and some kind of Bill of Rights. But most of the "Camarilla structure" is left intact. There may be some actions a "Prince" or whatever you call the executive cannot do. There may be certain things that the masses can veto. Certain positions might be elected subject to recall or time limits. But it's all fairly tepid. There's still Justicars, still Conclaves, still an Inner Circle, still the Traditions, it just has some guard rails put on. Once again, there will be other characters or players who see this as not abolishing "the system". Those Anarchs aren't "real' Anarchs because they are not radical enough.

                                To those characters and players, what they want to see are Anarchs who want to blow up the entire setting blown up and replace it by something else. They want to see Anarchs abolishing the Traditions, setting up pirate republics, organizing soviets or anarcho-syndicates, establish a theocracy, killing the Inner Council, legalizing diablerie against the elders, ignoring the clans and incorporating Caitiff into the power structure, or whatever. Because that is the only way "the system" can be overthrown. Unfortunately for them, the game has never really supported that. We get pitifully few ideas on what kind of specific agendas some Anarchs support as a replacement, and usually if players really want a game that is completely a non-Camarilla structure they just play Sabbat instead. So without some radical reimagining of the setting, the Anarchs simply cannot be anything other than a "controlled" opposition that simply swaps one Prince for another with no real changes.

                                To other players, yeah, killing Lodin and putting Maldavis in place is hardcore Anarch, and they don't understand why these other characters (or players) don't get it. There's just little common ground between them.

                                And I think that's fine. There's no reason why the Anarchs cannot have "radicals" like Salvador Garcia or Smiling Jack, "moderates" like Jeremy MacNeil or Hinds, "reformers" like Maldavis or Modius, and "sell outs" who comprise Camarilla loyalists who are sympathetic to the Anarchs. This kind of infighting and accusations of betrayal between factions is just normal.

                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                                    No, just no.

                                                    Because if there's no sense the PCs actually want to do the right thing then there's no fucking point in Humanity. The choice is endangering themselves, their loved ones, and their power in the city.

                                                    The "moral quandry" implies that vampires are somehow equally moral in their decisions versus bad decisions vs. worse decisions.
                                                    Okay, so what happens if or when the "little girl" being kidnapped is a revenant? A Giovanni? A mortal descendent of the prince in bad circumstances, and the prince has zero interest just admitting a potential vulnerability to a group of neonates and instead wants to leverage it for a show of ruthlessness? Being raised as a sacrificial lamb to some infernalist cult? Hell, in V5 mechanics that little girl could be a touchstone and the prince's interest is in protecting her.

                                                    The last one's by far the most interesting scenario. Do player characters oblige the prince, trusting they won't damage or destroy their own touchstone (once they discover the relevance of this kid to the prince), do it themselves as a strike against the prince's humanity, or act to protect the prince (and ultimately city) from themselves? Does the kid's current circumstances actually warrant intervention, and if so, might there be a preferable alternative to resolving the conflict?

                                                    Of course, V5 is also the edition with the only solid mechanical justification for why an elder prince would just want to up and eat a little girl (ignoring the potential for Ventrue feeding preference to be children, but you didn't specify clan so I'm sticking to generalities).

                                                    Those are the scenarios I could name off the top of my head in about thirty seconds, in which the act of kidnapping a little girl may not actually be the "evil" thing to do after all. It just appears to be as glimpsed through the fog of war, because player characters won't (and shouldn't) be privilege to that information on the outset -- it must be discovered through the course of the story.

                                                    How about if the kid's possessed, a medium, has latent magical talent, or something else along those lines and poses a threat to the Masquerade -- and those around her -- for it? In this case, an occult-minded prince may have no interest in harming this child at all, or if they do it's in the form of raising them in preparation for the embrace when she reaches maturity. Either way, the end result is removing a potential threat to mortal and vampiric society and placing that kid in an environment where her abilities can be excised, suppressed, controlled, or developed -- or at least, keep them away from worse factions.

                                                    This is grey all around, because the player characters must now openly speculate whether the prince is correct to have this child segregated, whether other parties might handle the situation better, or if the prince isn't just acting hastily (or on incomplete information) and the situation will course-correct on its own.

                                                    And last but not least, what if we're dealing with a Damien Thorn/Michael Langdon/'baby Hitler" situation where the kid's just straight fucking evil (superpowers not necessary), and the prince or one of the primogen with Auspex out the ears has foretold this kid has to be taken out of the picture for self-preservation. Doesn't even have to be an "Antichrist" type situation, maybe the kid just grows up to be the hunter that wipes out Elysium with a firebomb in ten years' time. Now we're not even talking about grey-on-black morality, we're talking about black-on-black for all the "baby Hitler" moral arguments to either side, in addition to relying on the accuracy of an elder's prophecy and the potential for this to become a self-fulfilling prophecy when or if the players' own actions are what ultimately cause the firebombing in ten years' time.

                                                    Here's the thing. You presented a scenario in which the prince uses PC's as procurers for their midnight snack. That's just a shitty, superficial, scenario with not nearly enough narrative meat to drive a plot or tie into a chronicle's narrative on its own -- and it doesn't hold up to scrutiny in the context of WoD's internal logic. After all, why the fuck would a prince send neonates to perform a rote task their own retinue and/or retainers could do on their own with far less chance of matters going awry, and far greater operational security?

                                                    Simply put, a prince who would pull shit like this is a shitty prince -- and more on that later.

                                                    I'd argue that "seizing power for themselves" isn't hypocrisy for a lot of low level Anarchs. The Anarchs include a bunch of idealists but plenty of them make no pretenses that their reason for hating the Camarilla is the fact they want to do whatever the fuck they want.
                                                    Including their turn on the tyranny bicycle.

                                                    There's a lot of street gang influence in the Anarchs in addition to communist, socialist, and justice.
                                                    Indeed, and it's all pretense and self-delusion.

                                                    Was this a product of Revised? Because the Anarchs murdered and overthrew Princes in Chicago by Night, Los Angeles by Night, and The Anarchs Cookbook.
                                                    And of those, who were competent princes that enjoyed alliance of their primogen, tolerance of neighboring princes, support of archons and justicars, and the approval of the inner council? Don't mistake the sect throwing a shitty prince who already had one foot out the door under the bus, for power or a lack of loyalty on Anarchs' part. It just means the Anarchs were the Camarilla's catspaw, witting or not, to take out a figurehead who was going to go sooner or later anyways.

                                                    It's one thing to say Camarilla vampires are backbiting, arrogant, power hungry monsters. Nobody debates that. But, people who talk up Anarchs obsessively insist on one imagined aspect to the Camarilla lore by and large doesn't support -- they're incompetent. Anarchs don't have appeal unless the Camarilla is inept, and that's why players who like Anarchs must fixate on the Don Sebastians, Lodins, and LaCroixs of the setting -- as opposed to the Marcus Vitels (while he was still pretending to be Ventrue), Kevin Jacksons, and Mark Deckers of the setting. Because while those princes may not be nice people and certainly keep Anarchs on as tight a leash as possible, they're competent.

                                                    And by all means must avoid at all costs mention of the Benison Hodges of the setting...as in those cases it's the Inner Council and Justicars themselves coming down on a prince hostile to Anarchs, specifically for their hostility to Anarchs.

                                                    This is the core problem with Anarch "normalization": by way of comparison, every other sect must be presented as a less-appealing option. And in this case, it mandates the 600-year-old vampiric society run by the apex predators' apex predators -- who for the most part are acutely aware of their own vulnerabilities -- who have had centuries to perfect statecraft, somehow abrogate their own skills, experience, and indeed common sense itself, to run that society in the least competent and sustainable way possible.

                                                    And we know for fact that's how V5 canonized the post-Second Inquisition Camarilla, where instead of the likes of Lodin being the idiots who slip through the cracks of competency, it's the likes of Kevin Jackson being the competent administrators who slip through the cracks of idiocy.
