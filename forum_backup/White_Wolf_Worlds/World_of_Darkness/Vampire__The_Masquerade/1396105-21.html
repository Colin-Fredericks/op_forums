    I'm sorry, I'm very confused. No, the point is to create the ability for player characters to play as well as interact with various types of folklore monsters as members of the same race in order to interact. To create a wide variety of fascinating and twisted monsters to exist in the setting that have a common origin as well as society. In-universe, the idea that vampires come in a variety of sizes and powers while inspiring the Folklore that is going to be at least largely inaccurate about them.     It makes the Kindred world all the more diverse and fascinating while avoiding adding like, "There's hundreds of different kinds of supernaturals that are completely unrelated" flaw. Which some people don't see as a flaw but stretches credulity and isolates the kinds of monsters from each other, IMHO.

    In my campaigns I have the viral model vampire, along with the vampire that spontaneously rises, along with the vampire that rises because the human it was made specific preparations before death, the vampire that offended the gods or broke societal mores leaving it unable to rest.....and the vampire that incubates you in its water filled stomach over a series of days until you become what it is. If its a little extra mechanical work on my part its worth it.
    The rises from the dead because of a curse or sanctified ground thing being a good example as its a rumor in-universe about how Kindred REALLY procreate. Which is totally unrelated to folklore. I think part of this being that I don't think of Kindred as "viral model" because that's not a real thing in folklore and Kindred in the WOD are dead, they're dead diseased. It's a magical transferance based on Anne Rice.

    It's a good thing to establish one single creation method so that its all under one big genus.

    Im glad you've put all the work you've done into yours but I guess I'm a "Common Origins" guy. Sort of like I'd prefer Mutants to be the only source of superpowers or magic or whatnot if I do a comic book setting.

                            I mean, everything is artificially lumped together under the “vampire” umbrella. There is no original folkloric vampire, strictly speaking. It’s a whole bunch of stuff that modern media have hammered into that shape, which doesn’t faithfully represent any of them. But at the same time, that’s kind of how folklore works in the first place, as those legends were themselves distilled from other stuff. It’s just that pop culture is kind of the folklore of today.

                            So while I get it when people say X type of vampire-like creature doesn’t really fit the Kindred mold, doesn’t seem like it should be Embraced, etc., the thing is, that’s no less true across the board. I find it weird that people only focus on, say, Asian vampire creatures but don’t object that European vampire creatures have experienced the same treatment. Is it because deep down they still want the Asian critters to be exotic? Is it because they think the Kindred actually represent European vampire lore? Is it because they (incorrectly, in my view) believe Kindred lore is all worked out with no room for mystery?

                            The fact is that vampire media across the globe is becoming increasingly assimilated to this artificial shared mythos. A Korean movie about vampire is going to have them working the same as they do in American movies. It seems I only ever hear white Americans/Europeans insisting things need to be segregated, and that’s not great. I’m not saying every vampire-like thing has to be the same species in the game, but I think we have to be careful about how and where we insist that they shouldn’t be.
