    What is ACTUALLY stronger: Abilities and Attributes, or Disciplines?

    10-29-2023, 08:36 PM
    Hello everyone.

    I was talking to some friends and I noticed that we seem to understand very differently some of the rules of the VtM20 and VDA20. I'd be interested to hear people's views.

    Some might have seen some other post about intimidation and werewolves, so that is what sparked the discussion between us. We got into discussing how intimidation works in general (not just with lupines) and social rolls at large, though it could be applied to all abilities.

    When I read, say, VDA20, I see that social rolls have quite well defined mechanics and results. Also non-social rolls, like the Stealth rolls to sneak. Many are contrasted rolls with a given difficulty that can be adjusted to suit the circumstances. The winner in the contrasted rolls does what they wanted to do or foil the other's attempt. Spending Willpower can add one success to your roll and prevent botches.
    By contrast, a disicpline like Presence is both stronger and weaker: it is stronger because it can do something that's very hard to do with contrasted rolls with a single non-contrasted roll at a given difficulty; it is weaker because it is enough to spend 1 willpower point and roll Willpower at diff.8 to be protected from the emotions stirred by the Discipline. Dominate allows you to utter commands subtly and implant them with single rolls whereas normal intimidation gets others to do what you want (they become cowed, i.e. they submit to your wish, as per the Oxford Languages definition of the term) but after a contrasted roll and in a rather plainly threatening way, likely to draw attention and enmity and a bad reputation. But you can resist Dominate if you're good at not looking in the eyes. Animalism 3 also makes people cowed in a special sense, likely more intense than the one for simple intimidation. It grants an easier roll at a fixed difficulty with which you may cow even the strongest mind. Obfuscate and the Stealth ability are similar: you can do a lot of things unnoticed with a good Stealth rating, but Obfuscate allows you to do the same without needing to roll,but might be shattered by things that you could avoid by activele using Stealth. .
    My understanding therefore is that disciplines do not replace or compete with standard interaction, but occupy a different space to it. And standard interaction has a truer nature than the false emotions produced by Presence, hence why people can resist the latter by spending Willpower, but not so much the former. After intimidation, opponents are "effectively cowed", as the book says, and it is regardless of the supernatural nature of the victims: they already have higher Willpower and Strength and Intimiidate ratings as supernaturals, so why should they get an extra vague bonus that is not in the books if one interacts with them without using disciplines?
    My friends seem convinced that normal abilities do nothing except against some human, and that anything supernatural needs to go through disciplines to be defined and effective, and that, since Presence can be dismissed with 1 point of Willpower, social interactions must be weaker than that - though this is not written anywhere in the book and I think it missed the point of Presence altogether.

    A look at the description of what is commonly done with each level of each ability in the manual would also support the idea that abilities are already capable of geting done many things without powers.

    Any thoughts?

          To do my best, I guess, to give a functional answer: they're generally tied (though specific exceptions exist).

          Abilities are the most efficient of the three, but have the lowest "cap" in how much they can accomplish; even if that can be in breadth instead of depth.

          Attributes are in the middle. They cost more than Abilities, and do more than Abilities.

          Disciplines aren't efficient in the sense of having high individual costs, and requiring decent investments in other traits to get solid use out of them. But they can expand character capabilities beyond what Abilities and Attributes can do on their own.

          People frequently treat the last part of how Disciplines expand your PC's potential, as what makes them stronger, but you can look at plenty of theory craft threads about different "builds" and see that this is simply because high Abilities and Attributes (or things like Generation for high vitae cost ones, and Willpower for Thaumaturgy) are just assumed to have been invested in first.

                                      Dramatic Actions taken with Attribute + Ability are powerful but - tipically - mundane. They won't allow you to do the literally impossible, nor are they mandated to work if what you're asking for it's absurd.

                                      The way I understand it: With Dramatic Rules, after you declare what you want to do and how, the ST it's the one in charge of deciding what Atribute + Skill you're going to use, at what Diff, and how many successes ... or if the roll it's possible at all.

                                      To give some examples:

                                      *If you were surrounded by a group of enemies, you can't climb the tree next to you and become "functionally hidden in plain sight", the way Obfuscate 4 does, with just rolling Stealth and a trillon successes "at Diff 10. Because it's absurd".

                                      I don't think that's how the system it's intended to work. You would have to sell me your "misdirection" in a way that it's at least somewhat credible, then I would determine things like Diff, and so, and so.

                                      In that sense, Obfuscate it's more "powerful" than Stealth. Which doesn't mean it's more important for a Stealth build, considering all it's weaknesses and that you will have to have Stealth anyway

                                      *I would say you can't "cow" a sept of werewolves into worshipping Set by whiping your tail against the ground and yelling very hard "convert or die!".

                                      This is not about "Werewolves being immune to Intimidation because they're too cool for that shit", it's about an action that doesn't seem it would realisticlly be followed by that conclussion, to a point it goes beyond what Diff 10. it's supposed to represent in the way how I see it.

                                      If you had a Discipline that does just that, barring other powers countering it, it would work. Because "it's magic". In *that* sense, it could be considered "more pòwerful"

                                      Maybe the difference I'm trying to express could be summarized as that you don't get to explain things as "a wizard did it" if you're using mundane skills, so the result depends much more on the criteria of the ST about how the world works.

                                              First of all, thank you. You actually gave me an idea on how to set difficulties for rolls using as reference the descriptions of what each Attribute and Ability can do on each dot. It would need adjustment due to many factors, but I might try something like 4 + the amount of dots fitting the description of what you're trying to do (similar to some forms of Blood Magic). For example, in VDA20 the description of Stealth for dots 4 and 5 is:
                                              4: Expert: You could tweak your target’s noses if you wanted, and disappear again before they blinked.
                                              5: Master: You were never there. No one ever saw you. You’ve infiltrated fortified keeps and sat in on the most secret of meetings. Your name is spoken in awe by highwaymen and in consternation by lawkeepers.

                                              So if a player said they wanted to tweak a target's nose and disappear before they blinked (for example by swiftly going behind their back and staying out of their line of sight right behind them until they manage to leave unseen, movie like but not actually magical), I would give them difficulty 8. If they wanted to infiltrate a fortified keep and sit in the most secret of meetings, it would be difficulty 9. Obviously it would be a contrasted roll against the opponents' relevant traits, thus rolling a single success would not typically cut it.
                                              A more complex way of doing this would be to look at the Attribute dot that does a comparable thing, and the Ability dot, and add them together. Ex.: if what you're trying to do seems to fit the description for Dexterity 3 combined with that of Stealth 4, your difficulty is 7. Just an idea. Still need to mull it over. And needs adjustment, as I said.

                                              Second, I see what you mean with the examples and the wizard thing.

                                              Third, as specified in the other thread, the intention was not to convert. It was your standard medieval fantasy "surrender to my god or die" supported by a display of strength (12 + 5 activated Potence, which is way more than 5 dots in Strength) superior to most things they deal with (which cap at Strength 10, regardless of their other powers) plus 2 powers amplifying my intimidation and a Road bearing relevant to conversions and appearing divine (houserules with the ST because the Road had no specified bearing in the VDA20 manual). The reason why genuine conversion was discussed in the thread was because I wasn't sure how to interpret scoring 7 successes more than the opponents on a roll like this, because it's supposed to be well above and beyond what I intended or hoped to achieve. I just hoped for a faked submission, small lowering of the head and ceasing hostility as long as I vacated the premises swiftly, but 7 successes (mathematically averaged against a Garou maxed in Strength and Intimidation, not some lucky roll) should have some dramatic weight and also indicate that the character's level of perceived menace (even though in reality he's not that mighty) is way above the normal levels of interactions between werewolf and vampire stats.
                                              If you have a Road bearing specific to conversions and appearing divine, both of which are almost miraculous things generally, and that bearing is -3, taking every difficulty 10 task to a merely challenging difficulty 7, I would be cool with ruling that it can do, with a mundane roll, pretty over the top things.
                                              I would say the same if a characted had the VDA20 Road of Bones at 10 and the merit Powerful Aura, granting him -3 difficulty on any Stealth roll. His limits are definitely not those of others when it comes to Stealth. I count Road bearings as a supernatural, mystical benefit, and they do expand the scope of what is normally possible for a character.
