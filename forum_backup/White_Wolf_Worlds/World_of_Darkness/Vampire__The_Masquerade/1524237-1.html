    While in theory you could convert a werewolf to replace their faith in Gaia for Set, it would take an extended series of rolls over a fairly long amount of time and likely require a significant amount of physical and mental torture. This is often what happens when Black Spirals try to force Gaian Garou to begin following the Wyrm. It's something that happens, but it's by no means easy at all. A single success might cow them for a few moments. Maybe they could say, "Sure, yeah Set's the guy," but then they'd tear you apart a few moments later when you let your guard down.

    Unlike humans, werewolves are very familiar with the supernatural, many of them would prefer to die before turning their back on Gaia, and they all know about Set. In fact if there's any Silent Striders among the werewolves, they well might frenzy and start attacking you.

    So it might be a useful short term strategy to throw them off guard momentarily, but I'd suggest getting the heck out of there, and don't expect that you'll have a loyal pack of werewolves following you around from now on. Unless that's the direction your ST wants to take things.

                            There's also a simple problem with most iterations of the ST system: what 6+ success means in most rolls is completely in ST fiat territory. We're given a spectrum of success from 1 (minimal) to 5 (complete and then some). Outside of combat and similar subsystems where there's a specific role for each success, there's just a superfluousness to trying t adjudicate that many successes in most situations. If a character is picking a lock, what does 7 successes mean that 5 successes doesn't already include?

                            Beyond that, I think two things need to be addressed First, is that mundane (even if modified by supernatural power) rolls aren't mind control. Individuals are going to react differently to the same thing. This is where a lot of the "Garou shouldn't care" sentiment is probably coming from. The Garou train to fight huge powerful monsters, so a threat display by a huge powerful monster isn't going to provoke the same reaction from them as it would a bunch of normal humans. The Garou also don't know your character's sheet, they don't know your Stamina, or that you have a power to soak high amounts of Agg damage. What they do, it dependent on my second issue:

                            So, secondly, what is your characters intent for their roll? Is it reasonable for 3-5 seconds worth of time (convert to worshiping Set is not IMO), since smashing shit to show how dangerous you re is provoking an action time scale interaction? Is it reasonable for your methods (using Strength for intimidation attempts for that juicy Potence bonus comes with an inherent difference in what kind of threats you can make)?

                            A big part of adjudicating what successes mean in the more vague areas of the rules, starts with what the intent of the roll is in the first place, and making sure the player and ST are on the same page. The ST has the right to say things like, "that intent isn't appropriate for this context, please refine/rephrase it," If you want results that you'd get out of using Manipulation, but you insist on using Strength, it's completely within the ST's bailiwick to request you change things.
