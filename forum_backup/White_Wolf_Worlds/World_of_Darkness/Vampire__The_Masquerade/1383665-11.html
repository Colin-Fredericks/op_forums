    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    Speaking as a fan:

    My opinion on body armor is that it should be something that is more useful to mortals than it is for vampires but it should be able to add just a little in the way of protection. Vampires don't have functional organs for the most part and don't bleed so bullets don't do the same sort of damage they do to normal people.

    So, maybe a kevlar vest would add +1 or +2 but not nearly as much benefit to a mortal, which would allow a different kind of damage.
    Let me nerd out for a bit: From someone interested in the subject, it'd actually be more useful. Physically, not just because of the masquerade benefit. Especially considering "dark ages" armour.

    A vampire doesn't need their organs and can't bleed to death form a wound, but they need to avoid getting ripped apart because a massive amount of damage will still lead to their final death. We could say Vampires are pretty good against penetrating attacks (stabs, rifles) and resistant against blunt force trauma; but they're weak to relatively wide area attacks like chops and shotguns turning them into spaghetti. Armour, coincidentally is really great at dealing with those spread out attacks like slashes and shotgun pelets; Even if kevlar is almost useless for stabs and with high velocity rifles, it's still really tough against slashing attacks and buckshot. If you go back in time to full-plate, a vampire is almost invincible. You need absolutely massive force to get through full plate armour, you absolutely can't slash at it unless you're using a poleaxe or something excessive. Your best bet at fighting someone with full-plate, if you don't have a powerful gun (which, again, vampires are pretty resistant to those deep and narrow wounds) is to stab through the gaps of the armour; But then you arive at the problem: you're dealing with a vampire. A common way with dealing with medieval knights was to somehow get them on the ground and then put a slim dagger through their eye slit or in a gap at their neck, or their armpit; What's that going to do to a vampire? You can put your dagger through the eye and the brain and they'd still be trying to kill you. You can't just decapitate the vampire because there's too much armour at the neck, you can't just cut off a limb because those are protected well. You can do small damages, but the vampire can heal those during combat. Your only choice with a vampire wearing full armour would be to have teammates and smash the vampire with a hammer repeatedly, praying they don't have fortitude. Maybe if you dent the armour in enough it'll stop the leech from heailing. Vampires also don't need to breath, which is really beneficial for wearing a lot of armour and for wearing modern concealable armour. In modern times, when you get shot in armour, there's often some deformation at the back face of the armour and you still get a big punch from this even if the bullet is stopped: I don't think this would actually be a problem with a vampire because you don't need to breath and don't need all your ribs, and all this is before considering Fortitude. Having armour limits the kinds of attacks that can be made against you and limits where the attacks can go.

    Considering what we know of vampire physiology, Armour would give vampires an even bigger advantage than it would mortals. Of course, mortals benefit more since armour would save their life whilst for vampires it would just multiply their resiliency, but the advantages for a vampire can't be understated. The only problem with vampires wearing armour is a social one; "Just what do you think is going to happen to this elysium?" "what you doing with all that gear, son?" "Going to school, are we?"

        Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
        If it's not explicitly magical, it's mundane, and armour works just fine as mundane. It just seems like you've disembowled a chicken to look at it's guts and now know the words of the gods. Why doesn't armour work? What's the thematic benefit of not having armour work? What's the satiric benefit of armour not working? Have the masters of the word descended from the heavens to tell us that armour doesn't work with the holy 'gothic punk' ? What's thy drug of choice? What have thy got? What is this premise, which eludes common sense, that says the laws of physics should be suspended so that armour shouldn't work?
        I believe the setting conceits behind it are:
        - Bullets don't affect vampires very much, because bullets generally kill people via bleeding or organ damage, neither of which matters to a vampire.
        - Blunt impacts affect vampires about as much as they affect humans.

        This is why, in all the editions I can think of (maybe even back to 1e? I don't have the 1e book with me to check), shooting a vampire does bashing, shooting a mortal does lethal, punching a vampire does bashing, punching a mortal does bashing. (Vampires are also more resilient to punches than mortals are, sure, but that comes from rapid healing and Fortitude, not intrinsic downgrading.)

        The devs then said, well, if the main benefit of a bulletproof vest is that it turns a bullet wound into a blunt impact, and bullets and blunt impacts affect vampires about equally (they're both bashing damage), then it would make sense in-universe that armor doesn't really help vampires at all. The main benefit is preventing the bleeding and organ damage from a bullet wound, and vampires are already immune to those things.

        It's not the armor magically becomes permeable when a vampire wears it. It's that armor turns one type of damage into another, but those two types of damage are pretty much equivalent to a vampire anyway, so there's not much benefit to having a broken rib instead of a bullet in your abdomen. Flavorfully they look different, but mechanically they're both bashing damage on the sheet. Because bashing versus lethal versus aggravated (or superficial versus aggravated) is just an abstraction, representing how much of a problem the damage is and how hard it'll be to heal.

        All that said, I do think it would make sense to go into more detail on what protects from what. Different types of armor should protect you from different things, and some of those things would be useful to a vampire, even if a bulletproof vest wouldn't. And it would also make sense to have rules for armor absorbing the damage rather than just converting it, like ceramic trauma plates shattering. I agree completely on that front. But the hyperbole about "this premise, which eludes common sense, that says the laws of physics should be suspended so that armour shouldn't work" is getting tiring. The reasoning behind this has been part of the game ever since long before V5. Yes, maybe the existence of undead vampires defies biology. I don't particularly care. "Vampires exist and work like this" is one of the premises of the setting, and "vampires take bashing from bullets because they don't need their organs" is too.

                                        When armour gets shot, it's not like every KJ of force still travels into the occupant. A good amount is absorbed and dispersed by the vest. If you're wearing something very protective and get hit by something relatively small, there's good odds that you won't even notice you're hit, whilst if we're talking about a bullet that slightly exceeds the limitations of what a vest is designed to recieve without penetration, you might actually be looking at something which, in VTM terms would be... 1-3 lethal damage. Because that bullet might as well be a warhammer and the internal injuries will kill somebody. But unless players are going around with anti-material rifles or bear-hunting cartridges, you're not really in that territory. If you shoot someone in military body armour with a big-but-expected 7.62 Nato round, they might get knocked to the floor, they might be shocked, they might be bruised, but it's not 'damage' territory insofar as wound penalties should start applying. If you shot at a cop with .22LR whilst they wore a duty vest, they might not even notice they've been shot, but they'd probably notice 9mm, and if you shot the soldier with 9mm, they'd maybe not notice this (Obviously, I haven't tested this stuff myself and I'm ball-parking, but I should be close to accurate if not accurate) And yet, in V5, If I'm wearing military body armour, I'd still recieve a 22LR shot as if I was wearing light police gear or if i was a vampire, as if I was wearing nothing.
