    Personally, I'm pretty mixed on the Anarchs fully diverging into their own sect (it was one of the V5 updates I chose to take on, but ended up diffusing it in my chronicle's city, where the local Anarchs and Cam are keeping a truce). On the one hand, I tend to go with the characterization of the Camarilla as basically a MLM scheme--to see any benefit, you need at least one rung of membership below you. For the "rank and file" Neonates, pretty much the only thing keeping them in the Ivory Tower is their Elders' insistence. All Kindred are considered de jure to be part of the Camarilla, whether they personally agree or not. To me however, replacing the tension of Anarchs being a restive element inside the Tower with pure emnity makes things a bit too simple. I like Anarchs being able to enter Elysium and trade barbs with the Harpies, developing tangled relationships across party lines. Having them be their own sect with their own territory does lose that.

            This is why it's nice to have a spectrum of "Anarch"

            Let's say you have Alice, who's rather close to the prince and benefits immensely from her loyalty.
            Then you have Ben, who would like changes to the status quo, but is Camarilla enough to get along with Alice.
            Then you have Chad, who sits the fence. Alice would describe Chad as an Anarch, but Ben would describe him as Camarilla, chad might describe himself as either or neither, it doesn't matter.
            Then you have Danny, who's a self-admited Anarch. Alice won't sit in the same room as her, Ben will keep the relationship quiet, and Chad is happy to deal with her.
            Then there's Ernest. Ernest is hardcore. Alice wants him dead. Ben won't touch him. Chad will keep the dealings secret. Danny is both in awe and repulsed by him.
            Lastly there's Frank. Everyone hates Frank, and Frank hates everyone, at least on the surface. Somebody probably has uses for Frank.

            And of course, in each category, there are people who are equally for or against the prince but also against people within their category. This gets more extreme as you move out; Alan merely wants to supplant alice as the prince's favourite, but Ernest, Ed, Erin and Eric absolutely fucking despise eachother, all being radical and yet having very different ideas of what to do when they get power. Fortunately there's more Alices, Bens and Chads than Ernests and Franks.


            V5 is like.. You're either red or blue, there is no purple, there is no yellow. The Anarchs fight the Cam. That's not realistic. That doesn't reflect reality in any way, That's just pulpy bullshit

                In earlier discussions, I have argued strongly in favor of anarchs as a tolerated faction within the Camarilla rather than a separate sect that has been kicked out of the Camarilla. It's no surprise that my tastes have a lot in common with the views expressed by Ventrue Busboy and MyWifeIsScary above.

                In fact, although I don't always agree with the manner in which he presents his arguments, it was MyWifeIsScary who wrote something in the recent Sabbat thread that I have never been able to articulate as clearly. The conflict between anarchs and the Camarilla isn't disenfranchised punks versus The Man, it's low grade millionaires versus high grade millionaires. Even the lowliest anarchs are members of an elite class of superhuman people, literally 1 in 100,000 compared to us poor mortals. The real downtrodden masses of Vampire: The Masquerade are the humans. Even if the Camarilla were destroyed tomorrow, the anarchs would be perfectly happy to continue to exploit, abuse, violate and cultivate human beings like the resource they are. All vampires, regardless of whether they are Camarilla, anarch, independent or Sabbat, belong to a bloodsucking aristocracy. I would go so far as to argue that this concept is built into the very premise of the vampire as a fictional creature.

                What this means in practice is that, for me, the anarchs have to have a certain degree of "just slashing paintings" about them. The alternative, that I'm supposed to sympathize with and be invested in their revolutionary struggle -- even though anarch rule is likely to be just as abusive toward human beings as the great Camarilla conspiracy -- is too preposterous to consider.

                    Yeah, the Anarchs are just John Wilkes Booth unironically shouting "Sic semper tyrannis" which is to say, "thus always to tyrants!" while assassinating Lincoln, because he genuinely believed that destroying his right to own slaves was destroying freedom.

                    The Anarchs vs the Camarilla is just the low nobles vs the high nobles, and that conflict has been around forever without it helping the slaves one bit. The ancient Greeks, the Roman Republic, and the Confederacy were all places where the low nobles had the collective power. The various empires and absolute monarchies were all places where the high nobles had all the power. They were all awful places for the peasants and slaves.

                    The Confederacy is remembered as especially heinous because there the low nobles thought they were rebelling against the high nobles, but they were actually rebelling against a government that, while it had elements of high noble government in that people who could trace their ancestry to the Mayflower had disproportionate power, and were seeking a more centralized government, was also well on its way to transitioning into a genuine democracy.

                    But even if that were not true and Lincoln and company really had just been the latest iteration of the High Nobles seeking to centralize power and stick it to the Low Nobles, that would not have made the Confederacy the good guys. It would just have made it the latest iteration of Evil Low Noble vs Evil High Noble.

                    The latest edition seems to be trying to brush that aside and push people into playing failed superheroes. You're always supposed to be an Anarch who is trying very hard to be a conventionally heroic, but who gets dragged down by the hunger and frenzy and the wearying complexity of vampire politics. You're supposed to try to be a superhero with fangs, every time, and fail, every time. That's it. That's all. Every game is supposed to be that one story with trivial changes like different powers and different backstories, but it's always supposed to be the story of a failed superhero, every single time.

                        Poverty.
                        Survival.

                                I'm of multiple minds about this and my somewhat mealy-mouthed judgement is the WoD is big enough for both the "Fangrunner" interpretation and the more level political landscape--dependent on many factors: the number and strength of resident Elders, of local Anarch gangs, and the leadership style of the resident Prince. I try to avoid dropping Elders who can't be outfought or outmaneuvered into my games, as I don't like boxing my players into or out of a given path ("you better do this guy's quest cuz he's REALLY STRONG" is.... not good STing, to me). On the other hand, I don't want a WoD devoid of entities greater than the coterie of PCs (the Beckoning was one idea from V5 I didn't take onboard). Neonates who are simply at the mercy of their Elders isn't interesting, but "stand together or fall divided" is a useful impetus to bring dispirate PCs together and make politics happen at all levels of the Jyhad. The Camarilla in my chronicle's city can't simply murder luckless Neonates at will or throw their weight around through sheer terror. I've also found, gothpunk noir notwithstanding, my players tend to feel rudderless without some Big Good they can take a cue from; a Nines Rodriguez or a Rebecca to say "it's not all horrible; see, this guy made it." Not that my Barons are angels or my Elders not ruthless and manipulative, but the gameworld needs carrots as well as sticks. L.A. and Chicago's canon environs provide useful goalposts for opposite ends of this spectrum---a wild west of baronies and gangs versus a monolithic power structure that holds everything in its orbit. Somewhere between these polarities is the city of my chronicle (literally split down the middle between Anarchs and the Tower, with peace and safety always hanging by a veritable thread).

                                                        To expand the thought; this is nothing against the developers of V5. I doubt anyone could make a better attempt.

                                                        Rather its just the limitations of the medium. Some books are extremely difficult to adapt into movies due to them being mostly internal journeys with little overt action. Similarly, large spectacles like massive battles or dog fights can be clearly depicted in the span of minutes on screen that would take pages upon pages of a book to convey.

                                                        I'm reminded of a discussion I had on another forum about the limits of genre emulation for superhero RPGs and how many classic comic plots; heroes being captured, heroes being mind controlled, splitting the party, etc. just aren't very fun when used in the context of a multi-player tabletop RPG. I think the same applies to the personal horror genre... multi-player tabletop rpgs just aren't that effective at conveying that genre in the way a television series, film, book or a single player computer (or tabletop) rpg experience could.
