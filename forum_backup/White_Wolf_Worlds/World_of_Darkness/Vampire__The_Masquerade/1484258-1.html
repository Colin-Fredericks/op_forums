    Ghouls with military backgrounds and a decent amount of silver > Garou
    One on one, the Garou is a likely victor. Hell there's a few old monsters that can cut through a thousand ghouls in a row before they fall

    However, a single vampire can replace as many ghouls as they want within 3 days. Garou replacements are measured in years. And those old monsters? Doomed to die of old age while their potential replacements are killed off.
    You can't replace your ghouls if you're killed while solo punching hobos for food in an alleyway, or a while solo seducing someone in a nightclub to go off somewhere private with you, or while solo breaking into someone's house to feed on them while they sleep. It's just not practical to go around surrounded by ghouls because vampires are solo predators who depend heavily on stealth, even the ones who hunt via seduction depend on stealth in the sense of being unobtrusive.

    Werewolves are extremely well-equiped to cut through stealth like it was air, and their pack nature means they are much better designed to actually group up to kill a target. Effectively, it's a wolfpack versus a solo animal like a cat.

    And, of course, they have their own backup: spirits, some of which can manifest in the material world and kick ass all on their own.

    Even without spirit backup, and even with the ghouls armed with silver, I'm betting on the werewolves. They have ways of countering silver, and they are insanely fast and strong.

    In fact, it's telling that if you have a werewolf NPC in a vampire game, the werewolf pretty much has to fight stupid for the players to win. Whereas if you have vampire NPCs in a werewolf game, the vampire NPCs not only have to fight smart, they have to be unnaturally on guard for solo predators. They have to be constantly surrounded by a degree of security that a solo stealth predator just wouldn't be most of the time.

        As the relative variety of answers show, this is a complex question.

        My first point so is to bring up that the setting isn't a Battle Royal, it is an ecosystem. The splats occupy their own niches and have limited ways of perceiving and interacting with one another. When it happens, it is always in a context. So here's one of the things MyWifeIsScary said about the advantage of attackers: whenever you see a scene of conflict, by definition it isn't those splats in their usual circumstances, but already the culmination of a series of events. You can reasonably expect that whomever is attacking is going to win just because they managed to do the attack to begin with, unless they're idiots.

        Further examining the Big 3 (because things are far more nuanced outside them), this impression heavily comes from their themes and the situations where they interact. Werewolves are, as a splat, warriors. So even if not everyone of them is indeed a warrior, they have more combat-oriented innate abilities, more combat-oriented individuals and usually interact for combat. So any werewolf that you find is likely to have a more combat-oriented build simply because they're more common and otherwise they wouldn't be there.

        Werewolves also live in other places and have other enemies, they seldom have any reason to interact with Vampires at all. Even the Glass Walkers aren't likely to just wander the streets at night looking for vampires, they have lots of better things to do with their time, and so do the Bone Gnawers. And if any encounter happens, it is likely that both splats will just avoid each other afterwards to not risk confrontation under non-optimal circumstances. They want to live more than to kill. Finally, they're simply not numerous. The number of Garou in any single country will hardly be greater than 1 to 300 thousand humans and probably less than this, and they don't simply replenish easily. So all together, it is too hard to have chance encounters between the splats, more probable for any confrontation to be the Garou attacking someone who failed at hiding their tracks.

        The Mages are even fewer, and their stronger trope is that a prepared Mage can do virtually anything, including beating up anyone. This is not just with Vampires, but the downside to it is the "prepared" bit. In this regard, you don't get to see Vampires beating the hell out of a Mage so frequently just because putting a werewolf there to do that is funnier, vampires are usually making a fool of the unprepared Mage. They could easily switch, though, Mages can really be caught off-guard and be screwed royally by anyone. But the caveat to the advantage of the attacker is that a Chantry will usually be heavily protected, playing in their preparedness, so getting the better of a Mage in their own turf isn't so easy. Finally their preparedness and curiosity also means that you'll also have particular Mages messing with all sorts of things, like experimenting with Vampires and Werewolves.

        Vampires are a paranoid bunch. Any inter-splat interaction already means things probably aren't going as the vampire planned, since the typical vampire will plan to get information without ever giving up any, much less exposing themselves to risk. Your successful Kindred may be capable of ripping a werewolf without effort, but is also unlikely to try. They're nocturnal, secretive and city-bound, so they have less opportunities for chance encounters and less engagement with the strange. Their numbers are compensated by this tendency to stay out of sight from everything and everyone at all costs. And most of their activities are equally subtle, keeping a low profile even for the standards of the supernatural. So when a vampire goes physical with other splats, it is likely that they already failed anyway, probably are the weak ones, possibly set-up by their enemies and going against tough attackers.

        Being hated is another tricky bit. Mostly it was a strong 1st edition theme of everyone being at each others throats, that was slowly dropped as a general rule and kept to specific circumstances. As a society, werewolves are purist zealots that have a sworn enmity to anything Wyrm-Tainted, and simple lack of contact and knowledge does the rest, so many of them hate vampires on a matter of principle. Most won't ever find one, though, and in practice individuals are individuals, unease truces and even friendships between them, albeit rare, are a trope on itself. Outside the Euthanatoi Mages mostly don't have many fixed opinions on vampires. Other splats don't give a damn, Changelings and Mummies party with vampires with some frequency, Demons are curious about them, Hunters Hunt, but some even Hunt under the patronage of a vampire.

        On the matter of political influence, it is important to understand that it may be a lot of different things and accomplished in a lot of ways. The reason why Vampires do have the upper hand is, simply put, because this is their main game. Others also know how to play influence, but it is a secondary matter for them, while for vampires it is the core of the job. Here methods, numbers and reaching are a big difference. Typically a Mage has very broad influence, but little dept. They can pull strings all over the world, but their goals are to influence thought and ideology more than to micromanage things, and they pull things by control of a few key pieces. Kinfolk are good, but they're not nearly as numerous as humans themselves, so they're a big help, but still limited in many ways, and their attention is focused to few interests. Vampires do collectively micromanage the entire system in comparison, a tighter, more robust and widespread web of manipulations kept by a horde of dedicated and stealth spiders.

        Each method has its ups and downs, but usually in the context of a possible direct confrontation the Kindred method will be the best, simply because it means more warning for immediate threats and more "direct" means to deal with it. Which is what their web is meant to do, instead of influencing the thoughts of the masses about technology or funding eco-terrorism.

                                            Vampires are pretty subtle about their feeding because they don’t actually have any innate ability to warp the perceptions of witnesses the way the Delirium does. As such, your average werewolf isn’t going to notice them any more than anyone else would unless they have and are actively using gifts to detect them.

                                            Vampires also take steps to secure their feeding grounds. They don’t just hunt at nightclubs, they OWN the nightclubs and have ghouled bouncers and private rooms they can invite favored patrons to. They worm their way into hospitals, rehab and hospice facilities where a pint here and a pint there from those already ill (and whom they can offer a respite from their suffering through the Kiss) is unlikely to be noticed. Some even open blood donation centers or arrange blood drives (why do the Masons in town hold so many? Because the Tremere backing them need a lot for their Thaumaturgy). Some own apartment complexes where they show up at residents’ doors after dark to do maintenance and are invited in with a tone of relief. Hell, hookup culture turns dating apps into delivery menus for the enterprising vampire (“do I feel like Mexican or Chinese tonight?”).

                                            And it’s not like there’s much evidence after the fact unless the vampire was already starving; an intense wave of pleasure as they bite down, a lick to close the wound after and the victim is left a little lightheaded (maybe… I don’t even notice a difference after I donate at the Red Cross) and replaces that lost pint of blood volume in about 24 hours (what takes longer to recharge are the mineral stores the marrow uses to replace that volume).

                                            In short, unless you’re actively looking for them via generally supernatural or hi-tech means or the vampire has already screwed up (which letting yourself get to “starving” most certainly is), your typical werewolf, mummy, mage or hunter just isn’t going to notice that a vampire is even present.

                                            By the same token it’s not like vampires don’t have their own means of supernatural sensing; Auspex is not a rare discipline and one rank is enough to generally let you know you’re being watched while two ranks just puts a flashing neon sign over any supernaturals in their line of sight while it’s active (and both are generally useful for hunting as locating an isolated target healthy enough to survive feeding and thus not leave any evidence makes your unlife much easier.

                                            The nocturnal vs. diurnal schedule also makes a big difference. Sure there’s some overlap on the edges, but the human society with which most mages and homid werewolves directly interact is the diurnal world where only a vampire’s proxies are present.

                                            In short, I just don’t think many splats would encounter each other directly unless they went out of their way to do so. Their proxies? Sure… probably all the time without even realizing it. The file clerk the mage private investigator has as a contact has no idea her precinct captain is the ghoul of a local vampire or that one of the precinct beat cops is a Bone Gnawer kinfolk or that a barely remembered cold case box is the fetter of a Wraith who died while this close to cracking it.

                                            But basically each splat has their own particular concerns that only tangentially come close to each other.
