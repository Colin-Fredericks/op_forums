    Vicissitude Adding Limbs

    05-28-2022, 08:53 AM
    Dark Ages Vampire had a specific Vicissitude 6 power to do this but it’s not found anywhere else. As neither V20 or VDA20 repeats this, I’m wondering if it’s an air breathing mermaid, that introduced the idea you CAN’T just use Vicissitude 2 & 3 to do so in order to give you a power to buy. As war ghouls were a thing, adding working pieces of different bodies together is definitely “a thing” that doesn’t seem to require that specific power.

    What I’m leaning toward at the moment is with Vicissitude 3 you can add working pieces of different bodies together with an extended Body Craft roll and a lot of careful work, but Adding Life to Life let’s you rip someone’s arms off and just stick them on your sides and have them work. Thoughts?

      I'm not sure Vicissitude 3 would be enough
      Usually I hate dragging "science" in to debates, but in this case adding a limb is a bit more complicated than just rip off an arm and stick it on someone's side; we have a skeletal structure like this for a reason, muscles and nerves and all works for a reason they works like that and while most of the changes could be explained with the Body Craft, Medicine and Science skills as background, a whole new limb for a mortal/ghoul is something I think you need a serious power to modify the brain so it could process the whole thing and actually be able to use it.

      More so "war ghoul" is a bit broad term
      A Vozhd is a creation of the Tzimisce, a mishmash of living beings melded together into an elephant-sized, multi-limbed monster. About a dozen lobotomized ghouls are merged through Koldunic sorcery and Vicissitude, resulting in a 15-foot high walking biological weapon.
      A Szlachta are a type of ghoul used as soldiers, bodyguards and sentries. As szlachta are considered superior to other ghouls. They are created from either human or animals, and fleshcrafted in order to increase their combat prowess. While this process makes them more powerful, they are also hideously deformed. Each szlachta emerges from its creator’s laboratory unlike any other, since the process requires handwork. Some features are fairly standard despite the variations. A typical szlachta has spikes, armored plates or both. The Tzimisce creating the ghoul removes all unnecessary fat, or transfers it to areas where extra padding might come in handy, such as over vital organs. Most Tzimisce replace the ghoul’s entire skin with something tougher while some add on extra layers to create whole-body calluses or otherwise increase the ghoul’s capacity to resist wounds. Almost all szlachta possess armored sensory organs with increased sensitivity, so as to make better observers - but this frequently results in the szlachta going insane or being permanently crippled

      Contrary to popular belief the "War ghouls" - usually Szlachta - never were famous of their multiple limbs or tentacles, they were strong, monstrous, shocktroops, bone blades, spikes, armours, but multi limbed, tentacled monstrosities were actually rare to see, those were usually the Vhozd-category or belonged to an Elder with Vicissitude 6 at least
      In the modern nights living siege engines and Vozhds are rare and the "war ghouls" are usually used in a Siege - and made by veteran Tzimsices of the Sabbat for the occasion, but I doubt they are multy limbed - , aand Pack not really have a few lying around. Szlachta in Tzimsice heavens? Sure.

      In the modern night when the scientific becground is a bit more available than in the dark ages I might allow the creation of a multi limbed creature with vicissitude 3 tho' but it would require months if not years of work (with heavy nerve surgery rolls), not to mention the recovery of the ghoul and the time he actually learn how to use its new limb
      Vicissitude 6 power (or coldunic sorcery) bypass this by "magically" rewire the brain and body so to speak
      Well in my humble oppinion anyway

                          I think this is an interesting topic to consider. I've mostly just taken it at face value up to now that adding biomass from other subjects simply required the use of Vicissitude 6+, but that's always been a bit unsatisfying because it restricts so much of the behaviour that is depicted as common among "mad scientist" Vicissitude users to the lower Generations. Which isn't necessarily a bad thing, but it could definitely be inconvenient if that's a character type you want to play without necessarily having to engage in diablerie or get special permission from the Storyteller just to make it feasible. Even with regular Szlachta, you've got to wonder where all that extra bone plating and the sharp bits are coming from unless you imagine every one of those type of Szlachta is being made by someone of Seventh Generation or lower. And it seems like it is more common than it should be unless you imagine nearly every Vicissitude user taking the same 6-dot Vicissitude power.

                          I think I have two possible answers to the conundrum:

                          1) This would ironically be a case where I think the regular Medicine skill might be more useful than the Body Crafts employed by the Tzimisce. Without high-level Vicissitude to solve all the problems by "magic" essentially, you have to deal with issues like blood-types being compatible, transplant rejection, etc.; not problems for Cainites, but possibly for Ghouls and definitely for regular mortal subjects/victims. I feel like the Medicine skill might actually be better equipped to deal with those problems than the more anatomy-focused Body Crafts.

                          2) If you can't "magically" solve the problems listed above with lower-level Vicissitude, then combining it with some other kind of "magic" in the form of Blood Sorcery seems like the go-to option. Perhaps have the Storyteller create rituals or Combination Disciplines that allow lower-level Vicissitude to transplant biomass between different subjects.
