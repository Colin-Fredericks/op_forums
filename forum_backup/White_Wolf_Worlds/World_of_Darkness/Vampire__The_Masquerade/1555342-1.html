    Originally posted by Gryffon15 View Post
    Chicago of course is the gold standard really. While I’m someone who isn’t a huge fan of all the 2nd edition killings, both are still all-timers in my opinion
    I think it has two problems, one compounding the other.

    First, why did it had both a Prince and a Primigen? Domains aren't modern states with a president and a senate, those institutions are meant to be interchangeable, not simultaneous.

    Second, it was taken even by the devs as gospel for how Camarilla cities structure themselves. The problem isn't the city's structure itself, but how much it solidified as the to-go way for making a Domain. Even today we have lots of STs that struggle with their settings because they feel obliged to make every city have the same institutions and stuff as Chicago, even when they're very different places.

    The core book painted a picture where cities vary a lot, and many configurations can exist within the Camarilla, but Chicago became the only right way to do so.

    Bloodline's Santa Monica is a far more interesting starting point to describe a "standard" Camarilla Domain in my opinion than Chicago, because it actually works as a bare-bones structure you can build upon instead of a massive system you have to figure out how to disassemble.

    Originally posted by Gryffon15 View Post
    outside of the secret Lasombra
    My major gripe with D.C. is that it was a missed opportunity to showcase how you shouldn't be too attached to expectations. Vitel's shouldn't have been a secret at all. Why?

    Sure it plays to the stereotypes about clan affiliations, but it also plays those stereotypes as absolutes while ignores the actual information in the books.

    There's no rule against a Lasombra becoming Prince. There's no rule about any clan or bloodline assuming any role within the Camarilla, except as a Justicar (and even then this isn't an unbreakable rule). Members of every clan can diverge. And the Prince is simply the strongest monster around that wants the title. Why should Vitel have to hide his clan at all? I'm talking it from the conceptual level, not his background as it came to be.

    Originally posted by Gryffon15 View Post
    Plus the timeline gives me a headache when I try to square it with Demon, KotE, & Bloodlines (which tbh I largely ignore in practice). Still some great work has been done on these very forums to flesh out the setting and that deserves a mention.
    Also Mage, LA is important to Mage. It's possibly the single most used city for metaplot events and headquarters for shenanigans in the game. If you run every line as canon in your game there's no way to not have a crossover in LA.

    I think the problem with LA was mostly a problem with the devs not having really thought well what a rebellion could look like after success. LA is just the absence of a Prince, anarchy less as a system or policy and simply as the state of lawlessness and how it was perceived in the 90s in big cities within stable countries. It was a big "so what, now?"

    ​
    Originally posted by Gryffon15 View Post
    I think the Tzimisce Antedeluviam being under NYC thing, while pretty funny and cool in theory, does more harm than good to the setting in theory long run.
    It was another spin on a problem the setting had with many other things, especially Noddism: legends were established, but then deepening them seemed easier than making conflicting alternatives that could shake things up, sow doubt and give STs options.

    Strictly speaking that thing isn't anything in particular, it may be. It may be almost anything you can think. [Tzimisce] is simply the easiest conclusion for people used to Vicissitude's flesh-crafting. Even in the book Gehenna only one scenario assumes the NYC's Big Bad Blob to be something relevant, and that book is adamant in saying it isn't canon and I take that to heart. But in the end they never managed to write about it without focusing on this specific legend, and that's an error.

    VtR in this sense goes to great lengths to make sure you have conflicting narratives, which not only deepens the sense of unknown and mystery, but also feeds the ST with ideas to use, discard and tweak, and less pressure to make any particular version the real one.

    ​
    Originally posted by Gryffon15 View Post
    The whole Midnight Siege for NYC metaplot thing is a cool and all but I honestly think that NYC before the Camarilla takeover would have been a more interesting setting: the Camarilla stronghold on Manhattan Island, the quarreling Sabbat bishoprics in the Outer Bureaus, the Anarch & Independent enclaves strewn throughout - I think it would have had more sauce, but maybe that’s just me.
    It isn't just you, I think so, too. A dynamic setting where various factions fight (and make truces) is far more compelling than just a recent recovery, even because that would make easier for STs to put the city in whatever hands they want just by deciding the conflict resolved how they want.

                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                I'm confused by this idea because if a city has enough vampires to have a prince they will absolutely have enough vampires to have a Primogen. The Primogen is everyone who either doesn't want to be Prince but is powerful enough to dictate policy in the city.
                They have the same role. It's literally like having both a President and a King with the same powers.

                It has nothing to do with the size of the city, it has to do with someone being powerful enough to claim Domain or having to share power with others.

                Even if each Primogen only cares for their clan, it's still a heavy interference in the power of the Prince if the city has one. No autocrat would accept such interference, such parallel power. If they have no choice, then it's the Primogen that don't need a Prince to interfere in their affairs.

                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                Vampires are hierarchical creatures.
                No, they're not, that's the big problem with Chicago.

                It created a notion of how vampires structure formal power that isn't really a thing in their nature nor was meant to be a thing in the setting outside specific situations.

                Vampires are egotistical and greedy. Creating titles and whatnot isn't a matter of hierarchy, but of appeasing their vanity. Even then most don't care that much and are happy with feeding grounds and access to the powers that be. What titles actually exist are well beneath those who really have power, because those just take what they want.

                Most of the hierarchy in Chicago, that is copied elsewhere, is interesting for players not because they say something about Vampires, but because they make the setting easier to digest when we have such a lack of other examples of how they could organize.

                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                Honestly, I think the irony is that no one can really think of a better way to run V:TM games that creates drama. We've had many other non-Prince/Primogen/Elders/Anarchs settings but they really don't generate the kind of intrigue that players love. It's even infected the Anarchs and why they have Barons.
                I don't think you understood what I said, because I never said a city could have nothing at all to vie for.

                What I said was that DeLacroix's LA, as far as the Camarilla part goes, worked as the basic arrangement. It has only the Prince and Sheriff as formal roles, and it doesn't really need more. There are other powerful kindred he has to negotiate with, even something that resembles a Primogen, but no formal titles are needed, and there's the very point I'm making that they were as much potential allies as enemies to DeLacroix because he was a particularly weak Prince, although even then he wasn't someone they were willing to just ignore, he was still personally dangerous.

                From there you can build a lot of other things. You may have minimalist structures for small Domains (or like LA, ones with a small Camarilla presence). You may have whichever titles you need in your city and ignore the rest. You can have other vampires that can stand to the Prince in some way assume their own self-made titles (which is mostly what the Barons are). You may have a Primogen-that-isn't, a group that exists as a challenge to the power of the Prince, not as a part of the same structure.

                You can have lots of things, even Chicago. You just don't have to be another Chicago. And Chicago didn't need a Prince at all.

                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                Is it? Because I thought it was Therese's Barony and everyone answered to her. There's just not enough vampires to be a Primogen.
                That's besides the point, even because in this sense it was disputed territory.

                DeLacroix claimed the entirety of LA as Domain, and as powerful as the Barons were, none of them directly disputed this to his face.

                His actual power varied as the Barons and other relevant Kindred opposed him more or less, and Therese was more his ally than of any other Baron, so they kind of shared Santa Monica.

                So much so that the Fledgling remained there, and while you get to do a lot of things for a lot of people in the game, you're above all else DeLacroix's thug until further notice.

                My point isn't if the city had size for one thing or the other, but that its Camarilla part worked with just two titles.

                ​
                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                I thought it wasn't because Vitel was a Lasombra but the fact he was someone that was nearly universally reviled by both the Sabbat and Camarilla both in his true identity.
                That's the Watsonian perspective, you have to keep in mind that he wasn't a secret Lasombra because he was reviled, he was made reviled because the devs wanted him to be a secret Lasombra.

                And the whole "secret identity" shenanigan didn't really depend on him taking on the role of a Ventrue. He could have pretended to be a Lasombra Antitribu all along.

                ​
                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                As you mentioned above, the Chicago system is so inherent to vampire even the Anarchs copied it with Barons initially supposed to be "ironic" but getting actually accepted as the defacto system for Anarchs.
                Which doesn't change what I said about them not knowing what to do with an Anarch Domain.

                Even then the Barons aren't so much Princes as they are gang bosses.

                They control small-ish territories and have to go to turf wars if they want something more, or to negotiate if they want access to resources and areas without such wars.

                None of them has the power or cohesion needed to actually manage the city as a whole, which is what a Prince or a Primogen does.

                ​
                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                In Gehenna, the PCs slay the giant flesh monster and permanently break the masquerade. It was like, "There's no real other way to make this thing relevant, so why not do something immensely stupid?"
                That's the price of never exploring any possibility besides the same legend again and again. When push comes to shove you don't have much else to do, any creative use would need a build up you don't have the time for anymore.

                ​
                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                I have my issues as I love Noddism but Requiem does have a good sense of mystery. Sadly, the attempt to give Chronicles' sense of mystery to Werewolf was a massive misfire.
                Noddism isn't the problem, it has never been.

                It was not exploring anything else because Noddism was the low hanging fruit that was the problem.

                Through the whole of the three original editions WoD had this problem where once a myth is given the devs will explore it as much as possible because it was easy drama.

                While this gave players a sense of deepening and coveted answers, it also stripped things of mystery and made it hard to convince that other legends could be just as valid. Which was a problem every single time they tried, when they finally noticed they needed other legends to tell some stories.

                Requiem 2nd Edition gives you 4 or 5 legends from the get go with each clan, and a bunch more in other places. The book about VII solves the problem of exploring them without answering the mystery by being four contradictory books in one, four complete and meaningful answers to whatever the group is, so the ST has the inspiration and the tools to easily build their own version.

                And as I said, myths are more important because of what they inspire people to do than because of any reality behind them. If all the energy spent deepening Noddism had been spent in making factions based on interpretations of it and other things, and their influence and activities, we could have plenty of Noddism and other things without ever having to get actual evidence for anything.

                But W5 isn't that, either, it is... I think it is just stupidity.

                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                I like what they did with New York by Night but that's because they more or less got out of the transition period depicted in the books. However, I don't think the Kindred wars as depicted in so many places are that interesting a concept.
                I think it is, at least for this one city. It didn't have to be an actual war, but a city divided between factions trying to coexist without mutual annihilation.

                    I’m not sure if this is the most apt place to put this, but one of the MASQUERADE cities I think about most is Victorian London during the period when Valerius was obviously working towards a transformation from Regent into a Camarilla Prince (Between the late 1840s and the early 1880s), with something of a ‘Gold Rush’ as newcomers attempt to stake their claims in what was arguably the single most powerful & important metropolis on Earth, presumably whilst old hands manoeuvre for or against the transition from Regency to Princedom, as well as the new openness.

                    All whilst Mithras remains the proverbial Elephant In The Room without actually leaving anything but his long, ice cold shadow over London during the course of a lengthy absence: there’s no guarantee that he’ll come back, but if he does return and doesn’t approve of goings on, well …


                    I’m also curious about Rome as a Kindred city: my impression is that it was a place to visit rather than a place to put down roots for much of the Christian era (Being something of a minefield of supernatural relics & oddities even without considering the World Religion in prolonged residence) and very clearly a city that the vampires have no exclusive title to.

                    On the other hand it’s too juicy a major city (and has too many layers of history to mine), with significant ties to more than one Clan, so it’s impossible to imagine there not being a steadily growing Vampire presence in such a population centre - especially after government of the city was taken into purely secular hands - however precarious it’s position.
