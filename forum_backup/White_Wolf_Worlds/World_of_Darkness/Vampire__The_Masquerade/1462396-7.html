                                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                        Yeah, I get the Good Guy Technocracy has fans but I've always loved them being the bad guys.
                                        The point is that this is a change in how most fans want the Technocracy to be.

                                        Original Techy works on the assumption that Fascism = Modernity, and is a symbol of Luddite ideals the fandom never bought as much as the authors and shied from it even more with time. So much so that this movement started already in 2nd ed.

                                        Like it or not, most people don't see very well the idea of the Technocracy as representing such things all the while it defends proper and safe science and collective development (because they do, while the Traditions focus on individual development), so changing focus to the Nefandi in this regard is also an old strategy already (implied, for example, in the Syndicate R&D shenanigans).

                                        Personally I think they should develop more the role of Threat Null, that is already a mix of parody and reference to Old Techy, but in a way that makes more sense than just proper academy and formal institutions being universally tools of evil and our future depending on our timely salvation by mediums and deluded charlatans.

                                        I think we could even have a very interesting game if the focus changed to both Trads and Techy fending off a renewed War between Threat Null and a similar Ultra-Luddite Traditions-equivalent. In this sense I do agree that focusing on the Nefandi is kind of dull and too much on the steps of Werewolf.

                                        Originally posted by Manfr View Post
                                        As much as one cannot agree with their artistic vision, I wouldn't say that the IP is managed by people who don't know business (especially now, that is directly in the hands of the former WW Vampire-in-Chief under scrutiny from a world-class videogame producer) or who treat the franchise as a pet fan project.
                                        While I do agree that just slandering their preparedness isn't a good or accurate take on the matter, praising them as if it meant they're not making mistakes isn't either.

                                        AFAIK Achilli is very knowledgeable about Vampire, but also very biased towards personal preferences. Less now than in the past, and in no extreme terms, but still. The upper management of Paradox demonstrated a very defined vision about the game at least since the actual core publication, which is both good and bad.

                                        It certainly isn't managed as just a pet project. Quite the contrary, it is the most ambitious and business-oriented management team WoD ever had, and this isn't bad per se. But it doesn't mean they won't commit mistakes or that their vision don't have problems, not just their artistic vision, but their business strategy.

                                        The whole Sabbat thing in the end boils down to it. They don't like the Sabbat, they don't want it in their vision of the setting as anything remotely playable. Why it is so is irrelevant, either personal preference or business strategy boil down to the same. I fully disagree that it has anything to do with Chechnya, because this stance was actually very clear since the core. The only change was they even deciding to make a Sabbat book in the first place, and even this I think had more to do with distancing the Gehenna War from their initial lore that it happened in an undefined Middle East where, surprise, there are also WoD players.

                                        Now, from what I get from their vision and stance on the matter and how they conduct their business, I do think they're making a series of mistakes, not because I think fans should have everything they want, but because from a franchise perspective I think they're cutting off avenues for exploration without really obtaining the benefits they seek with a better defined and coherent setting. And sometimes they're putting aside complexities that are responsible for giving the setting its coherence in the first place.

                                        Originally posted by SetiteFriend View Post
                                        Indicates what? That the book not made for "Sabbat players" didn't satisfy "Sabbat players"? You can't really fault them for something that wasn't their goal at all. You can disagree with the decision, but not criticize them for not failing at something that wasn't their goal.
                                        You can't defend their decision to not make a product because fans making it is somehow a better move like you said earlier.

                                        The Vault isn't official, canonical lore. Despite they getting a cut, licensed product with little oversight is not the same as corporate product. It isn't a good decision, it is a non decision. They didn't promoted the community work to bring the Sabbat to the game, they decided that the game has no playable Sabbat, period. And they believing this is a smart decision does not mean it is.
