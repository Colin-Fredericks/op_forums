            Originally posted by Vilenecromancer View Post
            . The main reason Oblivion likely gives a stain is the popular depiction of Necromancy.

            ​Necromancy is almost always seen as evil in popular media. This comes from years of several different cultures and beliefs dictating it as such..
            As much as the WoD has always been more pop-culture than folklore, it's actually done pretty well in terms of adapting real world belief systems concerning magical practices: Hermetic Tremere, Dur-An-Ki, Ahku, awesome supplement books like the thaumaturgy trilogy.... As much as Anarchs like to joke about it, magic in WoD was really set apart from DnD magic. Alchemy took inspiration from the real world: a science-mystic practice geared around changing one material to another or creating the perfect medicine and perfecting the mind and body beyond ordinary means. It very much avoided the so-commonly adopted DnD simplism of alchemy: making potions for temporary effects. And yet, that's where we are now with TBA. So as much evil necromancy fits the trend, that doesn't mean it fits the series.

                Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post
                There's an implication behind all this: the implication that your humanity is actually your soul, and not just a representation of your psychological resistance to berzerker hunger and rage. The force behind Oblivion literally eats your soul bit at a time. The idea of a soul eating discipline is...familiar...to those of us who played earlier editions, and I didn't like it the last time.

                I'm not sure I'm comfortable with the idea that someone with Humanity 8 literally has a bigger soul than someone with Humanity 7. I've always treated that number as representing something psychological, a unique form of insanity that gradually overcomes vampires because their human brains are not designed to deal with berzerker urges. But things like this strongly imply that, nah, it's not a form of insanity: your soul literally shrinks over time.
                Possibly. But V5 is no clearer on what Humanity is than earlier editions. It sort of wants to be VTR2e's Humanity (a type of Integrity), but then scrubs all the rules that establish that, ties it into morals again (Convictions, Tenets), and makes it about remorse and Stains rather than detachment and breaking points.

                This is one such muddled element in the mix. The idea behind it could be that Oblivion causes Stains because it has a deadening/detaching quality that makes you less human rather than less humane. And I think that's what it's going for, rather than that it eats your soul.

                So, yeah, it's all a bit muddled.

                                                Originally posted by SetiteFriend View Post
                                                Remorse checks makes sense if thought of as rationalization as well, besides just remorse. Things like "But they deserved it" or "I did what I had to do", while losing Humanity would be accepting it, "So what?".

                                                Though now that I think of it, rationalizing the shitty stuff you did, means you feel remorseful for them, otherwise you wouldn't bother.
                                                Well, no. Rationalisation is what you don't want to do. It's the unhealthy response to what you've done, as opposed to remorse.

                                                When you rationalise things, you're trying to stop the cognitive dissonance of, 'I'm a good person so how come I did a bad thing?' So you start making excuses for the things you do, making it easier to keep doing them.

                                                You change the above statement to, 'I'm a good person and I was right because...' or 'I'm a good person and what I did doesn't matter because...'

                                                Essentially, you're deluding yourself that the line isn't here (killing is bad), it's over there (killing is only bad if it's a good person).

                                                You keep doing that until the line makes no sense (killing is okay if I wear my purple hat while I do it) or doesn't exist (killing is okay).

                                                And that's when you become so far removed from humanity that you're a monster and either don't realise it or you don't care.

                                                Remorse is thinking, 'I'm [trying to be] a good person but I killed someone. Therefore, I won't kill anyone ever again and I'll make amends, so I can stay [trying to be] a good person.'

                                                It's supposed to stop you from rationalising the behaviour as acceptable. It reasserts the line (killing is always bad, regardless) rather than moving it.

                                                    Actually I think SetiteFriend is right here.

                                                    Humanity in VtM isn't about believing in a certain code or having a healthy response (it isn't mental health exactly). It is about feelings. How do you deal with your feelings, or lack thereof, is another matter (albeit still a pretty relevant one for the character). You can have a character that pretty much doesn't think killing is ever justifiable, yet are below the Humanity rating (for V20, for example) to feel about murder. If they commit a murder they may feel a little bad for going against their belief, but it is more abstract, not a response to the fact they actually ended a life. Likewise, you can have a character that whose beliefs completely justify murder in a lot of circumstances, yet it doesn't mean actually doing it became emotionally easy.

                                                    In V5 it is about the feelings become gradually more muted instead of things slipping in or out of the list, which may be a better or worse take for it depending on the character and the Chronicle Tenets, but the baseline is still the same. Convictions may help with this notion as they may signify situations where your belief in the justification is strong enough to help you cope emotionally. But otherwise it is about emotions, not beliefs, and as such rationalization is an indirect factor.

                                                    Of course, if you rationalize it may also mean you're more likely to do it again.
