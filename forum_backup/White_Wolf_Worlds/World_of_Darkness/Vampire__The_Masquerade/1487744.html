          For Harmony I disagree because of Sins 2, 4 and 7. 2 - Feeling guilty about killing when necessary, has the problem of not really being something under your control. It the Path is not helping you to not feel guilt, but instead demanding that you don't, then it is a really hard command to follow, and at level 2 nonetheless.

          Sins 4 and 7 have the problem of being too open to interpretation, and that isn't necessarily your own interpretation. What is pointless, how much is too much? Those are questions that an ethical path should answer, not ask and then punish you based on which answer you find elsewhere. Depending on the ST it may really be a piece of cake, but just having distinct interpretation from your ST may be grounds to having a really hard time with the Path (I don't worry so much about those above 7 because then you're talking Paragons).

          And Feral Heart is easier, but still has similar problems on interpretation with Sins 2 to 4, which again is a problem because of how low they are. While it is less likely to be an issue, it is still too open to interpretation for too low, which means basic, tenets. When does need counts for a follower of the Path? How do you define survival? What counts as an instinct? While those are less dubious, they're still too open, specially under STs that are either too inexperienced or just kinda jerks. And if things get too complicated, then even level 1 may become too much of a burden as the player can't say when a death is necessary and when it isn't (or just end up with the ST calling Degeneration anyways).

          Now, if we take the overall idea and ditch the current rules, just comparing their ethics and assuming a corpus of philosophers that really help with understanding their nuance, then I would completely side with Harmony being the winner by far (and Feral Heart loses position do to the principles on it that are not codified on the rules.

          But within the current classic RAW (as V5 have no official RAW on Paths), I have to say that sorry, but the most practical and easier to follow is, hands down, Humanity. It is the easiest to understand in the modern world even if you don't particularly follow it or its ethical principles, and the level of active effort necessary to really fall too low is huge. It shouldn't be like that and I think this is a design flaw, but really it is the most practical.

                                                      Originally posted by Hello View Post
                                                      I may be the odd one out, but to me it seems like something that should not require much thought from the practitioners of these paths. Now if the ST in question is having trouble with it, well, I guess just stick with what they are most comfortable with and roll with that.
                                                      Please, you don't need to explain those Paths to me, I don't think you understood my point.

                                                      1 - You don't control your feelings. Stating the Path's rationale behind making a feeling into a sin doesn't change that you don't control your feelings. And if the Path states a feeling as a sin, then the Path already assumes that you're not achieving immunity to your own emotions just because you're following the Path and assuming its rationale. A Path sin is, by definition et necessity, something a person following it is still prone to commit.

                                                      How do you define when the character feels something? Usually this is completely up to the player, but not always. Not only powers can directly mess with your feelings (forcing you into a level 2 sin, nonetheless), sometimes character feelings are dictated by the ST or by a dice roll for whatever story purpose at the moment. Those aren't usually relevant because they have no direct weight in the mechanics, but a Path that makes a feeling into a sin basically forces this to become a relevant question.

                                                      Is there a need for some hard rule on feelings or for the ST to press on the matter? Unless it becomes a problem, like if Degeneration is being a strong theme and the other players are feeling at a big disadvantage because you got a cheat code Path, I don't think so. And I don't think many chronicles would end up like that.

                                                      But what I would do or what you would do doesn't matter as much as the fact that the rules of the Path leave this in the air, and so any ST can answer the question however they want and it will be valid within the game. The whole idea of discussing the matter only makes sense if we take into account that the game is open for anyone to play, and so the material must be evaluated with other interpretations being accounted for.

                                                      Not only that, Paths are meant to put ideologies into game terms, and that means that sins exist in terms of behavior/decisions that can be observed in the game. A subjective feeling is an extremely poor fit as a sin, no matter how much it is relevant to the ideas that inform the sin.

                                                      2 - What the practitioners will understand or not isn't relevant, they're figments of our imagination. What we will understand or not, and if we're able to understand the same thing, is what matters. And making examples with really easy to differentiate situations does not really help when things get complicated and open to interpretation.

                                                      Those aren't abstract questions during the game, they're things that affect real decisions by the characters. Your interpretation of your Path informs your strategies and decisions to deal with the things that happen at the game, but the ST's interpretation define when your character is going to roll for Degeneration. A disconnect between the two means that you can't take informed decisions by yourself, since you can't know their results by yourself.

                                                      Simple on paper, messy in practice. If the Prince orders you to kill someone that doesn't threaten you in any other way, what is the right thing to do under the Path? Does the order of the Prince itself consists of enough threat to justify the kill? What if you don't know what the consequences will be, you can just imagine? There are many situations in the game when killing is a smart move, but asking if it is a necessary move under the purview of an ethical code is a whole different matter. And unless you're happy at level 2, Feral Heart leaves you in those situations with a very tricky question, as you have to guess what the ST will deem the right answer.

                                                      Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                                      Part of the problem is that Harmony (and plenty of other Paths) maintain the false idea that humans are not animals with instincts; and thus lots of things humans do for survival are listed as not proper.
                                                      That's true, but not quite a problem.

                                                      All in all, the point of the Paths is to give the characters a protecting philosophy for their ego, not to be right or sound under scrutiny. And while that would probably be better, I don't thing it is a necessity in the setting given how many are actively used as social and as political tools, and that in the end their grasp on your Beast is more important than their intellectual honesty.
