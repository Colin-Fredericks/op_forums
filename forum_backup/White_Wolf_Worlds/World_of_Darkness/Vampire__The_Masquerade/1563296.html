    Musings About an Actual Chronicle

    08-21-2026, 01:34 AM
    My wife is running for us a Chronicle of V5, my first long experience with the edition. I won't say this will be an Actual Play because I'm not sure what I'll make of this thread, but whatever I put here, you're all welcome to comment, use, criticize, all in all do whatever pleases ya.

    Our story is taking place in the US East Coast, a version with a strong coat of the New England found in the writings of H. P. Lovecraft. Anyone familiar with the place, be aware that for us it is an exotic, distant location we only hear about and wonder about the culture of the natives, so please be patient with any errors.

    Carcosa, a fictional metropolis of a few million inhabitants, has never been a normal place. No few visitants joke (or talk very seriously) of the city's inverted Masquerade: the world must not know what this place is like. In the impossibly clear sky the full moon is always visible, in the nearby lake the water reflects a second, alien sun, people hear stars sing and feel the dark places of the earth caress them with tendrils of shadows. Most Carcosans never saw normalcy in their lives.

    But it is facing an unprecedented disaster. An accident with a cargo ship 2 years ago turned the sea into a horribly toxic hazard. Fortunately the last decades of economic diversification prevented a deeper social crisis, but cleaning is nowhere near completion if possible at all, and there's risk that something escape to the world at large with this contamination, bringing some unwanted outside intervention.

    The mortals share theories about what spilled from the cargo. Oil is a common assumption, but all kinds of compounds are guessed, as the authorities seem evasive on what's it and the known evidence is inconclusive. The creatures that hide in the darkness know better. A thing died there, that shouldn't have been alive for so much time as it did. An undead thousands of years old and riddled with eons of accumulated experiments, blood magic and corruption.

    Now the tainted waters mutate its residents. At first the vampire regency of the city worried if it would affect blood quality and resonance, and moved to stop fishing altogether. Today those fears seem quaint in face of the things that are found with increasing frequency. A few days ago the things started to appear inland, and the PCs were called as a desperate task force to investigate anyone and anything trying to profit from making things worse.

      The PCs

      We were all Embraced in 1996, though we did not share this information with each other yet in game. That's 30 years before the start of the chronicle, Neonates with some experience. All vampires are of 11th Generation, although one was originally 12th.

      Pretel Sullivan is a mortal. He's a diplomat of sorts, accepted by local factions as liaison between different creatures. This role has been filled by mortals for several generations and he's the current one, receiving several benefits in exchange and a mansion whose total extent and mystical resources are a mystery.

      It has been a huge source of pressure, though, driving Sullivan to all sorts of coping habits, but mainly drugs and lack of self preservation. The constant exposure, some innate trait of his and access to supernatural means to restore health made Sullivan able to function while under the combined effects of all recreational substance known to modern society. The price is just to look like something between a zombie and a walking inflammation, and recently to suffer of memory loss although it may be psychological.

      For the time being Sullivan is off-limits to any vampire as either Ghoul or food. It's uncertain if that's for his services or for his blood being a biological hazard. Either way, he's the youngest PC, being in his mid 30s.

      Sam is a Brazilian small child that came to the US some time after the embrace. He's a boy, but pretends to be a girl with a Gothic-Lolita style, the other PCs don't know yet that he's actually a boy. He's a vlogger and influencer with some local fame.

      Having been Embraced by a show runner that were secretly both a vampire and the head of a child trafficking and prostitution ring, today Sam tries to find dirt on every adult that works with children's shows, assuming they're part of a major global conspiracy of child trafficking that is the power behind institutions like the Camarilla. Sam is 40 years old in total.

      Drew Blood was Embraced into the Brujah with 28, being now 58. He was then a circumstantial victim that got Embraced out of morality crisis, an old story, and isn't really engaged in politics or almost anything, anyway. The closest he has of usual Brujah rebellion is that he still holds a grudge against his last employer after decades and keeps tabs on children and grandchildren of the old bastard.

      Our group currently meets at the Morrigan's Den, Blood's vintage arcade that still somehow survives with a semblance of profit, partially thanks to patronage of other Brujah, the city's Sheriff and resident tall tale teller, Camael. The Den also sells occult stuff, a compromise after years of new age customers asking if it was an occult store because of the name and possibly another explanation for it making enough to stay afloat. On an unrelated note, besides the owner the night shift is run by a legit Get of Fenris Cliath. We found out she is a werewolf infiltrated recently, but Drew doesn't care and just gave her a promotion to cover her security service to the store.

      At 60 years old, Victor Shadowthorn is from the Lasombra and, still unknown to the group and the city at large, a Diablerist, originally from the 12th Generation and the only in the group now at Blood Potency 2. He's a businessman and a game developer struggling every night to code something under his Bane.

      Victor got the attention of his sire after building a successful company out of just a single indie game still back in the early 90s. VoidByte Soft is now a powerhouse and Shadowthorn is the main shareholder and CEO. His single crime to local vampiric law was against a Ventrue who dared try to destroy the company, and his end is thought to be work of the Second Inquisition (which, to be fair, it would be anyway).

      Given Carcosa being now it is, Obtenebration's powers have a skewed psychological effect on natives, mortal or vampires. Fear/discomfort effects from exposure to its shadows are done in reverse to them, they find it soothing, warm, cuddly and nostalgic, as the city has a natural Obtenebration-like effect that everyone is used to feeling from time to time (children encounter it on a weekly basis). Since Shadowthorn is a local he's used to this and uses his signature power to put people at ease. Since his sire is not a local, he finds this reaction creepy as hell and rarely uses Obtenebration directly on people.

      My own character is 62 years old, despite the appearance of 20. Hoshino Yūbi arrived just a few days ago fleeing Japan. Like Shadowthorn she's a very successful game dev since the 80s as part of the Dōjin scene in Tōkyō. Contrary to Shadowthorn, being a woman with low social skills and too many opinions made her a victim rather than star. The most profitable franchise in Japanese videogame history was mostly her doing, but before the final release her name got erased from records and her murder arranged.

      But someone among the traitors went above and beyond and got the sweetest deal of their dreams. The hired assassin asked a heft price for an over the top service. First her body was extensively modified to make her a pretty doll, then she was killed, turned undead and sold to them back as a slave, all the tricks needed to keep her under control given to them. Yūbi has no idea yet what kind of dealing went on, only now she's slowly comprehending how bizarrely out of place was such a deal, and she has few memories of her absent Tzimisce sire. Neither does she know much about her clan outside a few recent pointers.

      Japan went through a commotion less than a week ago in the game, as a terrorist attack left Yasukuni Shrine in ruins. What Yūbi can say of the event is that she noticed the guards of her prison/office leaving, and a woman came and took her out of it, a woman she found out to be a Kitsune (general sense, not necessarily the Fera splat). She found herself less than an hour later in a private jet, flying to the US in the august company of the younger brother of the Emperor. She is understandably more curious about her release than her Embrace. She was brought to Carcosa and given a pretty decent Haven, an old but safe Cold War bunker still in working conditions.

      Lastly, Margarida is from Acapulco, Mexico. At 86 years old at the time of Embrace, she's seen as an Elder by some, especially in conversation when she's not around. She has a successful business franchise in the city selling plants and flowers, goes to the church twice a week, work with Yūbi as volunteer at a Soup Kitchen, goes around town sharing gossip like a pro, attend to club reunions where she teaches shibari and dishes nightly brutal punishment to those who mistreat animals.

      No one knows why her sire, born when she was 30, chose her for the Embrace. Most accepted is the theory that her fierce protection of wild and domesticated life alike was a main factor, but many find her ties to the BDSM community at least suggestive, and the most actually told thesis is that she prove her right to Gangrel resilience by outliving her great grandnephew, Rameses III.

              Three days ago we heard of a weird shark with glowing mouth that was spitting a bizarre goo over the beach. Our Lasombra also rescued an employee of his that had been locked in the bathroom for three days to hide from a giant eel that managed to wander on land like a snake.               And things are only getting worse. Now we're in a full crisis. Some of the sea mutants are forming an ecosystem in land. Many of the mutant fish are intelligent and understand English. We're trying to control at least two colonies of giant anthropophagous crabs with human mouths that laugh like Kefka and have the powers of the Blood Brothers. There's also at least two psychic flying crabs around that at least don't eat mammals and, since they're telepaths, can be communicated with, so we're trying to use them for intel.

              I helped the local Garou pack with the autopsy of one of the spitting sharks (they multiplied a little) and the fucking thing shouldn't be possible, it's body is composed of soft heavy metals, chlorine based organic compounds and absolutely no water. One of the Garou is also the District Attorney and we tipped him on a nightclub that was capturing more mutant fish to use as attractions in the dance floor.                                   For horror I like books more than comics/manga, but that is mostly because I like the pacing of good horror text, for me it clicks well. For other genres depends.
                                  The original mirror bane was too much of a binary: it's irrelevant or outright incapacitating depending on how much the ST puts reflective surfaces everywhere. It's classic, but bad game design.

                                  I think the technology thing was a valid attempt but not really a successful one, although the big problem in V5 is the Difficulty being too high. Maybe a cost rather than a roll would be a better compromise.
                                  Character bleed is cathartic indeed. I think the important thing is to be mindful of it and try not to treat it as inherently positive or negative. It's good when it generates interesting experiences and bad when it puts you in a bad place, and all players must just be ready to respect the limits.
                                  CoD has a bigger dice pool difference, but it also gives a lot of Merits and Equipment bonus a mortal can use to do stuff. They're weak, but still very capable. V5 has almost nothing for mortals besides skills.

                                  Legacy had Hunters Hunted, so even vanilla mortals had access to an interesting list of options. Numina won't make you as powerful as a full splat, but gives you options they can't easily copy, either.

                                  Both in the end give you something so if you play a mortal in a Vampire game, you don't feel useless. You may not be as powerful, but you have your own nice toys and everyone has things to do.

                                  V5 really is limited. For now it's working, but we are really worried that Sullivan may end up with too little space to grow without house rules.
                                  From what I know of the US, there's less overall variation when it comes to cars specifically. A lot of places really go hard on the city simply not being made to be navigated by foot even at short distances.

                                  It isn't a personal experience, but it's consistent among the experiences I've heard, and I try to have some diversity of sources. Either way the US seems to be quite extreme in this regard by the words of people who lived there and in other countries, it isn't just bad public transportation but an effectively hostile city design for people without a car.

                                          I’ve done something similar in my own setting for the Ume house vampire mythological explanation, taking inspiration from actual Shinto myth.
                                          Mythological Origin
                                          The story of Tsukuyomi’s murder of Ukemochi takes a darker turn than the version recorded in the Kojiki.

                                          Upon her death, Ukemochi cursed Tsukuyomi so that he would never again be able to enjoy any form of food. When evening came, Tsukuyomi found himself starving, slowly dying of hunger. He was then visited by Okage-no-Fukurô, the Owl of Great Shadow, who suggested that he drink Ukemochi’s blood. Blood, he explained, was not truly food, yet still possessed enough nourishment to sustain him.
                                          Tsukuyomi followed his advice and thanked Okage-no-Fukurô.

                                          When Tsukuyomi returned to the heavens, Amaterasu was furious and disgusted by his actions. She refused to ever again set eyes upon her brother and forced him to move to another part of the heavens.

                                          Once separated from Amaterasu, Tsukuyomi was forced to survive by feeding upon humans, descending to the mortal world after nightfall to drink their blood. He survived this way for some time, but eventually grew lonely and once again sought the counsel of Okage-no-Fukurô.

                                          The Owl of Great Shadow taught him a method through which mortals could be helped to achieve ascension. They would first have to be purged of their mortal blood and then infused with the blood of a Kami.

                                          Thus were the vampires born.

                                          Vampires are the children of Tsukuyomi and, therefore, his divine descendants. They are known as the Tsuki-no-ko, the Children of the Moon.

                                          The Beast and Vampiric Nature
                                          Although they are half-Kami, all Tsuki-no-ko were once human.

                                          Their transformation is a violent passage through death, and because of this, every Tsuki-no-ko carries within them a dark energy originating from Yomi, the land of the dead. This energy is infused with Izanami’s fury toward the living and possesses the power to transform a Tsuki-no-ko into anOnryō, a wrathful spirit.

                                          Every vampire is therefore inhabited by their own inner Onryō.

                                          The Role of the Tsuki-no-ko
                                          As beings touched by the Kami, the Tsuki-no-ko possess the potential to attain enlightenment, cast out their inner Onryō, and eventually achieve the status of a true Kami.

                                          To draw closer to the Kami, a Tsuki-no-ko must learn to control their Onryō and seek the state of veneration offered to them by mortals.

                                          Within mortal society, the Tsuki-no-ko have a responsibility to inspire veneration while also behaving as Kami themselves. This means granting good fortune to those deserving of it, but also punishing transgressions when necessary.

                                          The Tsuki-no-ko also have a responsibility to remain part of the living world and to avoid polluting it with the death, pestilence, and misfortune of Yomi that they carry within themselves. They must strive to live in harmony with nature, in the hope that one day they may earn Amaterasu’s forgiveness.
                                          Because of their tainted nature, the Tsuki-no-ko are expected to purify themselves upon awakening each evening and again before returning to their slumber before sunrise.

                                          Kigan
                                          Kigan is the ritualization of the power of the Kami.

                                          As half-Kami, the Tsuki-no-ko cannot simply command divine forces as they please. Instead, they must petition other Kami and the forces of nature, asking them to lend their power by offering their divine blood as tribute.

                                          Through Kigan, a Tsuki-no-ko invokes these divine and natural forces and temporarily draws upon their power.
