    Beware False Religions

    Type: Fiction

    Synopsis: Someone calls out the general insanity of blood cults.

    Analysis: This fiction opens with the infamous L. Ron Hubbard quote about how to get rich by starting a religion. Scientology being one of those easy targets to take pot shots at but also, honestly, deserving every single one of them. The unnamed author highlights that religions that demand material wealth from their followers are almost certainly scams and this is not a particularly bad observation with the caveat, "enlightenment is a gift from the universe to you."

    I also like how the unnamed writer used RL mythology to point out how Mithras the Ventrue is a horrifying bastardization of the Roman/Persian god. This is a bit fourth wall breaking but works in-universe as well. What is less likely to be a Sun God than a vampire? Yet, I could totally believe a vampire takes the name of a deity associated with things utterly unlike himself and get away with it because the miracles of Presence.

    Yet, the idea that Mithras IS the avatar of a god embraced by Ventrue isn't IMPOSSIBLE in the setting.*

    Just very-very unlikely.

    Another thing this memo highlights is the fact that blood cults will make perfect sense to their believers but will almost certainly be PROFOUNDLY STRANGE to anyone outside of them. In a Church of Caine temple, you being an angel makes sense and everyone will nod along. However, explaining Gnosticism and how being an undead horror is a BLESSING is going to strike everyone else as nuts. Which is something to remember with the blood cults. They are not normal and need to be led into slowly.

    Sort of like how the ancient Romans wondered why the Christians were cannibals eating the flesh and blood of their god.

    * I know The Gentleman Gamer loves teasing Mithras' sire but I just go with him being the childe of the Antediluvian. What can I say.

        The Lord of the Red Desert

        Type: Fiction

        Synopsis: Two old-school Followers of Set discuss the nature of their religion.

        Analysis: If I were to list my top five favorite clans in order it would be Malkavians, Tzimisce, Lasombra, Toreador, and Followers of Set with Brujah just being barely edged out because they're so often portrayed as idiots.

        The old school Followers of Set have a lot of charm to them and I think part of that is that they pass the Bester Test. The Bester Test is a rule that I made for writing my genre fiction based on the titular character from Babylon Five. The rule is: "Can the antagonist explain his motives in two or three sentences in a coherent manner?" The Followers of Set were created from the John Milinus Conan movie and yet the Clan Books have managed to take a ridiculous and insane concept: a vampire cult of darkness devoted to corruption as well as evil then somehow make it a coherent ideology. See my above comments on the Cult of Shalim.

        This isn't really a discussion of Setite ideology but does give a pretty nice in-universe perspective of two vampires discussing the Catholic Church's scandals (which drove me from that church), the apocalyptic mood of the times, and incorporation of the "Real" Set's mythology. Mind you, one of the most famous stories about Set and Horus is how the former tried to sodomize the latter but ended up eating semen soaked lettuce but the Egyptians were like that. No one admitted the obelisk IS a penis until, like, 2010.

        *Waring RL mythology and all its dirtiness*: https://wincestuouslove.livejournal.com/34336.html

        Either way, I like that OPP brought back the classic Egyptian themed wizard Setites. I'll have a lot more commentary on them when we get to the Church of Set.

        Finally - onto the REAL meat of the book!

                Now onto the actual cults!

                The Ashfinders part 1

                Type: Cult

                Synopsis: A Thin Blood cult of rich fame-hungry millennials and Generation Z drug-addicts.

                Analysis: The Ashfinders are an interesting work to begin COTBG with because they're an unconventional example for Kindred. They're absolutely a cult and very similar to many RL ones. I'm particularly reminded of NXIVM a.k.a "The multi-level marketing sex cult that Allison Mack belonged to." This an ultra-modern drug cult that markets enlightenment through memes, jargon, motivational speaking, and other bullshit instead of resorting to the apotheosis of the past. It is also a cult that is by and for Thin Bloods a.k.a The Duskborn that has all the sinister edge it could ever need.

                The Thin Bloods are generally considered to be the Diet Coke of evil in Vampire: The Masquerade and are especially the only vampires that aren't going to be, on some level, evil parasites. They're alive and free(ish) from the worst ravages of the Beast as well as Hunger. That doesn't mean they can't be evil, though. The Cinder Institute shows exactly the sort of rich evil scumbags that would see Thin Bloodedness as a nice compromise with the immortality of the undead but keeping (most) of life's pleasures.

                The short-lived BLADE: THE SERIES was arguably even more influenced by Vampire: The Masquerade than the trilogy. I heartily recommend picking up the thirteen episode season for some sexy vampires, politics, and melodrama that made a better Kindred the Embraced than it did Blade series. However, one of the things that was interesting about that series was the street drug "Ash" that was made of, you guessed it, vampire ash. In place of drinking vampire blood, junkies could snort this stuff and temporarily gain the powers of the undead. I have to wonder if the creator of the Ashfinders was familiar with this piece of vampire media.

                The Ashfinders are pretty much the embodiment of every Elder's fears about the young because they are going to start murdering "real" vampires to provide their fixes as soon as their supply of Gehenna War ash runs out. I assume they'll also start employing other vampires to Embrace to keep a consistent "product' as well, even if its not going to be the Good StuffTM.

                I like these guys because they're both believable and horrifying enough that their appearance inside a domain could very easily fuck with player perceptions. If they're sympathetic to Thin Bloods, the Ashfinders might be an interesting wake up call. People they like or want to protect starting to hunt them or their vampire friends for a "fix." The same wealth and power the Camarilla uses now being employed to hunt undead in the name of cannibalism.

                Good stuff.

                Fun Fact: The Operator a.k.a Oscar Vasquez from "Children of the Blood" was conceived as a psychopath who found new life in the Blood Bond. He'd never felt love and devotion before, so the Blood Bond was a gift. He was reimagined later as a member of the Ashfinders because the feelings he got from snorting Elder ash were still something more than he'd ever experienced in "normal" life.

                        The Ashfinders part 2

                        Type: Convictions

                        Synopsis: The morality of a drug cult

                        Analysis: One of the things that Cults of the Blood Gods promised and delivered was the fact that it would present alternative moralities to the traditional humanity system. V5 talked a big game about broadening the Humanity system so it didn't find itself limited to the original Hierarchy of Sins but, for the most part, players were treating it similar to the old one. This struck me as grossly limiting and I wanted the Convictions to be a viable substitute for the Paths.

                        That didn't happen.

                        However, COTBG comes closest to making it happen as it does provide the cults all with their own alternative moral system that may not be equal to Paths (diablerie will still fuck you up) but can certainly make you be a High Humanity evil bastard. There's no corrections to the Touchstone system either but at least a game effort was made to try to patch things up. Indeed, OPP patches are part of what saved the edition for me.

                        • Always sample new experiences on offer

                        The Ashfinders are libertines and new experiences are a part of the package that comes with taking Ashe.

                        • Never withhold Ashe from others who wish to sample it.

                        Experience and sensation are to be shared. Solitary euphoria is a hollow high.

                        • Never allow someone to silence you

                        The Cinder Institute requires the spread of its word to draw new adherents and new experiences. Si*lence is a method of the Camarilla.

                        • Always listen to those of greater experience.

                        Wisdom can be gained from those of greater age and experience. One doesn’t have to believe or emulate every word and action, but listening is mandatory.

                        • Always know where your next fix is coming from

                        Ashe isn’t to be sampled once and forgotten: it’s a way of life. Only a fool takes the drug and fails to take account of their forthcoming supply.

                        • Never allow the kine to realize your undead nature
                        These convictions provide our Ashfinder characters with the morality of a drugged-up psychopath who is primarily motivated by their next fix as well as a glorious sense of entitlement. It is also a morality molded by people who want to spread the cult via word of mouth.

                                                Ashfinders part 3

                                                Type: Base

                                                Synopsis: The Cinder Institute in Ibiza.

                                                Analysis:

                                                "I took some ash in Ibiza,
                                                to show Beckett I was cool.
                                                When I finally left torpor
                                                Felt a hundred years older
                                                But suck it, it was something to do."
                                                - Cover of "I took a pill in Ibiza" by Damien, Baby Chrous

                                                Ibiza is apparently the headquarters of the Ashfinders and is the perfect location for them to do some recruiting as well as a place to spread their miracle drug. Personally, I find it difficult to believe some Ancient Kindred isn't sleeping there as building a tourist resort over your island haven is exactly the sort of thing someone like the Sybil or Helena might do. On the other hand, maybe they were beckoned and the island was suddenly free for newcomers.

                                                Personally, I don't think the Ashfinders probably have much difficulty recruiting Thin Bloods to their cause. While Jenna Cross was building her militant Thin Blood Liberation Front in Revised, there's going to be other people attempting to organize the Duskborn. The Ashfinders offer not only drugs, answers about the Kindred condition, and protection but also potentially money as well as jobs. Security is what most Thin Bloods are going to be looking for and if that means joining an MLM scam as well as dealing drugs then I doubt they have any difficulty recruiting. What do they have to lose really? Their souls? Please.

                                                I'm reminded of one of the (minor) controversies of Bloodlines 2 when it was stated you would start as a Thin Blood then become one of the major Clans later in the game. For some players, it was a complete shock because the idea of players committing sanctioned diablerie was simply not done at their table. Diableire was such a horrifying taboo that the idea you could do it (even though that's what the Blood Hunt MEANS) and not be forever branded a pariah in Kindred society simply didn't register.

                                                But it also established that for most Thin Bloods, ceasing to be one was a goal. Presumably preventing you from continuing the game as one. The Ashfinders WANT to be Thinbloods. Food may not taste any good anymore but it's not something you violently throw up. Animal blood is unappetizing but it's not like drinking raw sewage. You can stay up during the day even if you're not going to want to open the curtains. Plus, you never have to worry about ripping the throat out of your spouse and child if you get into an argument. I mean, if you're starving, yes, you'll eat them but that isn't a night-to-night danger.
