    I'm not sure if the "so it does 10 levels of aggravated damages in a radius of 140 meters" and the "plus 10 dice of aggravated damages in the range of 350 meters" are meant to stack, though.
    I've never made vampires suffer damage from radiation, personally. Acute or chronic radiation sickness is based upon one thing: the target having a metabolism in the first place. If it's a massive enough dose to start damaging (non-living) matter, honestly, the source of that dosage is going to be the problem, not the dosage itself. In other words, dump a vampire into the core of a purely hypothetical, active, nuclear reactor, and they'll die to "fire/heat" damage long before the radiation has its chance.

    That said, I've always played Fortitude 10 to be "The vampire adds their Stamina + Fortitude as additional health levels, and all health levels except for Incapacitated convert to "bruised" (i.e. they never suffer wound penalties). The vampire's soak pool against any form of damage is Stamina + Fortitude, including fire or sunlight, and the vampire soaks at maximum difficulty 6. Spend a Willpower point to ignore any single source of damage which would incapacitate or kill the vampire, after soak. Spend a blood point to ignore any other attack which deals damage after soak."

            Originally posted by KRB View Post
            Antediluvians are quasi-mythical plot devices/bogeymen...the game has devolved into a parody of itself.
            I can't speak for anyone else, but I do it for a number of reasons.

            One, Antediluvians can and have shown up in my games. Hell, I ran an MtA story about the rise (and defeat) of [Tzimisce]. That story also included Arikel, and for like a half-second, Tremere. It does, in fact, help to be able to provide some quantifiable limit and expectation of level 10 disciplines, both to plan the story and shape it as it develops (how Character A, that being an Antediluvian in this context, would cause or react to Scenario B within the confines of their power and character). Because players -- or at least, my players -- have a strong tendency to call bullshit on anything which strains suspension of disbelief on grounds of depth, scale, or character-breaking.

            Two and Three, but placed together because they're very similar in intent, it makes an excellent thought experiment. I find it helps to learn game mechanics better, and what can and cannot be done under normal circumstances within the confines of the game's rules. Believe it or not, I've actually worked out pretty good Master- and Archmaster-level magickal Effects based upon ideas I've had for level 8, 9, and even 10 discipline use, and vice versa. But, that's my perspective as an almost exclusively Mage and Vampire player; I'm sure Werewolf players might find fertile imaginative ground for Incarna and Celestines, as well as more flamboyant Legend-level Gifts, as an example.

            Plus, for those of us who like playing with Elder-level powers (either on a purely creative level, or in the course of actual gameplay) and creating their own, imagining how a level 10 power might look thematically and mechanically provides an upper boundary by comparison for level 9 powers, and so on down the line. That sense of perspective is important when planning, testing, and balancing homebrew Elder-level powers.
