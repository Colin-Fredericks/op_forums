    Los Nueves

    For a good time the Acapulco Anarch’s shoot each other in the face.

    Adeyemi reports on another subculture – but this one probably doesn’t have a mortal counterpart. But it probably has a Sabbat origin. While vampires can survive getting shot most anywhere – chest, groin, face, etc. – that is not the same thing as saying it is enjoyable. The level of violence of this sport, the abasement of the participants, the way it sets the participants apart from the human, and it being in a formally Sabbat city… all goes to suggest the Anarchs adapted it from the former Big Bag Sect of the WoD.

    This section does most to a potential plot for a game session; the Pcs go and hang around with NPcs in some forlorn place and shot the hell out of each other.

    Adeyemi also possess a strong character voice.

                                Roll Call of the Revolution

                                Rudi returns to reiterate a statement from Garcia back in No Prince, No Caine. Basically, there a many types of Anarchs and a lot of them do not care about the jyhad.

                                Anarchs is a catch all term used by the Camarilla for all the vampires than are not Camarilla (I wonder if they use it for the Hecata?). By and large only the really politically active Anarchs bother to call themselves Anarchs – the rest of them are preoccupied anything other than the endless revolution and politics are simply the Unbound. What the Unbound and the Anarchs have in common is a desire for a better existence than the one possible under the rule of the Ivory Tower.

                                Interestingly, Rudi writes that…

                                …the worst enemy of Anarch cohesion is simple geography. We’re all connected to the mortal communities that spawned us, much more than the Camarilla is.
                                I would have thought the reverse to be the case. A nomadic existence is far more common among the Anarchs than it is among the Camarilla. And the Camarilla seems temperamentally the type to encourage staying in a home city for decades, or even centuries at a time. Further, the “conservatism” (big air quotes) of the Camarilla would suggest they are the ones to nurse hate boners from their living days, more so than the dynamic Anarchs.

                                Still, this section is repetitious in specifics and themes – something that has dogged the last few sections.

                                    I disagree because I think the implications are that Camarilla communities SUBSTITUTE for human interactions.

                                    One of the things that came up in my 90s V:TM games was the fact player characters almost ignored the existence of humans entirely. All the NPCs they dealt with were vampires and even ghouls were more like furniture. In a Camarilla city, you can easily never deal with humans save as slaves and have your entire social circle be undead. In TRUE BLOOD, they said this was one of the ways that vampires could become monstrous. If you were part of a "nest" then all your worst attributes would be magnified by each other normalizing them.

                                    Anarchs, by contrast, must deal directly with humans on a regular basis due to their lack of a formalized society and that will have result in them having more difficulty dismissing them as just food.

                                    Re: The Morality of the Anarchs in V5

                                    I think one of the things that V5 suffers from a bit is the fact that it is trying a bit too hard to be dark and edgy. It wants to capture that 90s era zeitgeist of being grimdark before we had a word for it. This is a post-Game of Thrones and HBO's Watchmen world, though. What passes for dark and edgy, especially among geek media, is a great deal different.

                                    Still, I think this could have definitely used some nicer vampires as putting everyone in a moral event horizon doesn't actually benefit the story. The Lannisters are shocking because their behavior contrasts against the Starks. Part of what made V:TM interesting is that it made vampires into creatures with consciences (or at least popularized them among gamers) instead of making them Always Chaotic Evil creatures.

                                    So this is actually a step back from the more nuanced portrayals of previous editions. After all, there's nothing "new" about a vampire preying on a bunch of innocent hikers or metal fans. That's the most classic vampire depiction there is. The monster who portrays itself as a nice person and then murders people that consider it a friend.

                                                Lasciami

                                                The book finally provides something a bit bloodier than vampires hanging around night clubs in Iceland or climbing mountains.

                                                It gives us the Anarch Mafia. Who probably operate a lot like this…


                                                Lasciami is Italian for “leave me alone,” or “get off of me,” more or less. Such as might be the final words of an Italian speaker being attacked by a vampire.

                                                The section presents as the transcript of a recording between people speaking Italian about murders are public relations statements, while in the city of Naples. The recording was probably made by Italian law enforcement pursuing organized crime, and it probably eventually ended up in the hands of the SI.

                                                Classically in the World of Darkness, vampires and crime in Northern Italy were dominated to a large extent by the Giovanni and the Mala del Brenta, with crime and vampires in Southern Italy dominated by the La Cosa Nostra and the Brujah. Given its position, I would guess Naples fell under the dominion of the La Cosa Maybestra.

                                                Yes, I know it would actually be the Camorra, but La Cosa Maybestra is funnier.

                                                In any case, some assassins with apparently little respect for subtly, law or good taste are scaring the mortals and performing notable murders in Naples. A pair of hoodlums are talking about their fear of these assassins – who they do not realize are vampires – but the two are willing to do business with the killers anyway.


                                                The hoods ask the assassins why they want blood as payment, and why they drain blood from victims. The assassins flatly state they are vampires – taking refuge in audacity. The hoods are not very bright but are pretty determined. They are making a deal with the Devil, one they will not be able to control. The entire sections reads like an early scene in a horror story involving the mafia and vampires, and it is great.

                                                The section does pose a lot of questions, but ones which should provide a lot of fodder for Storytellers and PCs. Such as, how does the local Prince respond to the theatrical and Masquerade breaking murders committed by the Anarch mafia? How would any vampires involved with the Camorra respond to Anarchs poaching on their territory?

                                                The section presents vampires who are again probably not worried about the jyhad, but are more engaging that pretty much any of the others in the last six sections.

                                                            Soy Cuba

                                                            I enjoy research and chasing down references. I mention this because I usually try to catch and check out the references in these WIR, in this Anarch book, in the Camarilla book and in the Beckett’s Jyhad Diary book.

                                                            Soy Cuba, as a term, led down an interesting rabbit hole.

                                                            “Soy Cuba” is Spanish for “I am Cuba.” Soy Cuba is also the name of a movie. According to Wikipedia;

                                                            I Am Cuba (Spanish: Soy Cuba; Russian: Я Куба, Ya Kuba) is a 1964 film directed by Mikhail Kalatozov at Mosfilm. An international co-production between the Soviet Union and Cuba, it was not received well by either the Russian or Cuban public and was almost completely forgotten until it was re-discovered by filmmakers in the United States thirty years later. The acrobatic tracking shots and idiosyncratic mise en scene prompted Hollywood directors like Martin Scorsese to begin a campaign to restore the film in the early 1990s.

                                                            The film is shot in black and white, sometimes using infrared film obtained from the Soviet military to exaggerate contrast (making trees and sugar cane almost white, and skies very dark but still obviously sunny). Most shots are in extreme wide-angle and the camera passes very close to its subjects, whilst still largely avoiding having those subjects ever look directly at the camera.
                                                            Mise en scene is the arrangement of scenery and stage properties in a play, including the costumes, the selection of actors, sets, props and so forth. Mise en scene can also be understood as a single scene, or even a single image, that arguable serves as a symbol of the entire play or movie. Jack and the ghostly bartender in Kubrick’s version of the Shining is a representative mise en scene for that movie, for example.

                                                            Soy Cuba the movie is noted for its odd mise en scene, or odd staging, costumes and so forth. It presented a deeply distorted view of Cuba, which did not accurately represent the Cubans. The Cubans had little use for it.

                                                            Soy Cuba the section in this book might serve as a representative mise en scene for all the problems and failures of the entire Anarch movement.

                                                            Soy Cuba the section tells the story of a Cuba vampire reaching out to the Camarilla, asking for their intervention to depose a Russian survivor of the Brujah Council who has lately set himself up in Havanna as a strong man. This particular Cuban vampire resents the decisions of this strong man, how he is inviting Anarchs from across the world to come and set up shop in Cuba.

                                                            History doesn’t repeat, but it does rhyme.

                                                            There are some humorous bits in the section as well, such as when the Cuban vampires says parties thrown by Russian blowhards suck, and when he worries about CIA involvement in Cuba.

                                                            In any case, this is a solid section and one of the best in the book.
