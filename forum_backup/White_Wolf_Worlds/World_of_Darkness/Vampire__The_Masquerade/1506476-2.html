So off hand, it seems that Avellone's lawyers likely had some sort of evidence (maybe text messages or emails or recordings) demonstrating that one or both of them were lying, such that Barrows and Bristol's lawyers urged them to give up now rather than allow the case to trial where they would likely end up losing worse and even more money.

        It's possible that they were telling the truth before and are lying now, but it's more plausible to me that their initial statements weren't entirely truthful and, had the case gone to court, that would have come out and there was a high likelihood that they would have ended up having to pay even more in damages.

                                                Truth is an affirmative defense to defamation in most states, not an element that the plaintiff has to prove. If you sue someone in defamation, all you have to prove is that the defendant made the statement and that the statement damaged your reputation.
