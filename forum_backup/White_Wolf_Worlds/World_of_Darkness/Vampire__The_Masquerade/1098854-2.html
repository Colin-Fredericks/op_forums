                                            Since the thread has reappeared, I'm going to drop out of stealth mode.

                                            I think Aya Tari's numbers are acceptable for kindred overall, though probably quite high for the number of fifteenth generation vampires... probably by an order of magnitude. Aya's numbers for human fertility pretty much match data for fertility among heterosexual, married couples who don't use birth control. One pregnancy per year for such couples is about right, historically. All this assuming they are having reproductive sex, of course. (ie Gay 15th gens aren't having accidental pregnancies.)

                                            I could quibble about the percent of kindred who feed using sex. Given V5 lists a dozen predator types, it might be about a tenth of vampire feedings overall.

                                            The fact remains that Aya Tari's numbers can be quibbled with, but there are some dhampir in the world. More than a small handful.

                                            Let's be very, very conservative with the numbers. Let's say:
                                            • There are a thousand 15th generation vampires. About as many as a clan.
                                            • Only a tenth of them (100 individuals) use the Siren method regularly.
                                            • A third of those (33 individuals) regularly complete the act of reproductive sex, without birth control.
                                            • Those who do this have a similar reproductive rate to mundane humans, one birth per year.
                                            • Except, according to ToTB, half of all such pregnancies end unsuccessfully. (So, about 16 per year.)
                                            • This has been going on since 1999. (So, 300 dhampir exist.)

                                            Further, let's say, 2/3 of the children (200 dhampir) are being raised inside the Masquerade. By that I mean they are being reared in an environment which intentionally hides their existence from the public eye. This could be as simple as a mother who knows her ex-boyfriend is a vampire and knows to hide her child. This could also be a dhampir who has grown up in a Tremere chantry undergoing experiments. Point is, they are not available for public view.

                                            Let's say the other 1/3 are available for public view. This gives us a conservative estimate of 100 dhampir being raised by casual former sexual partners of a thin-blood, being raised by a family member of such a partner, or having passed into the adoption/foster care system.

                                            Okay, so a (very conservative) total of 100 dhampir roaming unsupervised through human society.

                                            Now, onto the actual question at hand, as posted by Aya Tari.

                                            My questions are how noticeable would the birth of that many Dhampir be to mortal authorities and what effect would that many 15th generation Kindred have on Kindred society (especially since they would likely have numerous Dhampir children under their guidance)?
                                            The most important fact about dhampir for this discussion isn't their precise population numbers. It's that they are, mechanically, similar to Revenants. They have a dot in Potence, and a dot in another common discipline. They can heal wounds, including sometimes regrowing amputated limbs.

                                            Most important, they mature/age at 1/4 the normal rate, while remaining within age-appropriate parameters for their reduced age.

                                            So, there are 100 children who went to the pediatrician for a well-baby check-up at one week, and presented as a three day old baby. Not unusual. The practitioner might suspect the due date was incorrect, and the baby was slightly premature. They might double-check the infant is feeding well, and prescribe supplements or breast feeding support. Overall, the medical system would watch and wait.

                                            As such check-ups continue an alarming pattern would emerge. At the age of one year, the child would have the cognitive and emotional development of a three-month-old. Not too unheard of; developmental disabilities are a known thing. Okay. Refer to specialists and start testing, testing, testing.

                                            But, there are 100 children who went at age four (around the time of pre-school) and had the physical, emotional, and cognitive development of an entirely healthy one-year-old.

                                            At eight, they will appear two years old. And so forth, until the oldest among the dhampir are now twenty years old and appear to be perfectly normal five-year-olds. Except, by now, they have shown super-strength, healing abilities, and at least one other discipline power. It will become clearer and clearer as time goes on that something previously undescribed is going on.

                                            In the unlikely event it hasn't happened yet, there is no way I can imagine these children not being written up in a medical or educational journal, probably as an unexplained, rare syndrome. If there happen to be two unrelated dhampir identified, the floodgates will open as practitioners around the world put two-and-two together for their own dhampir.

                                            If anyone is inclined to quibble with the exact number of dhampir, fine, go ahead.

                                            But, somebody, please, also explain to me how they do not become public knowledge.

                                                    The main issue I have with the original numbers is that it seems to assume that all *potential* pregnancies have a very high rate of success rather than a low. This seems unlikely to me, given that ghouls (as part of creating Revenants) often miscarry and it takes some very clever management (and no small amount of sorcery) to create enough ghouled-from-birth servants to breed said Revenants. Dhampir are functionally revenants and their parent is going to be under a similar stasis so I'd imagine they'd have it at least as bad as breeding ghouls (probably worse.)

                                                    Indeed page 31 of ToTB mentions that there's a 20% chance of success (at best, or 'if all other factors are optimizied' as the book puts it) to impregnate (or for a vampire to get pregnant) IF they spend the requisite willpower and BP to 'reactivate' the needed anatomy.

                                                    Even if conception occurs, there's about a 50% chance of carrying it to term rather than miscarrying (and the rules apply equally to a 15th generation mother or a mortal one) And its likely that the 50% chance is ALSO ideal circumstances since they ad the caveat 'assuming the conception, pregnancy, and birth take place unhindered' to the whole process.

                                                    So at BEST you have a 1 in 10 chance of a 15th generation vampire producing a Dhampir under optimized circumstances. And if they're fucking around randomly as part of their feeding process, I kinda doubt any of that would qualify as 'optimized'.

                                                    That said, this all also occurs without magic being involved, so I suspect if magic becomes a factor (like it does with Revenant creation) those chances could be modified (but use of said magic probably keeps them relatively rare the same it would for revenants.)

Dhampirs age at normal human rates until a post-puberty stressor causes their vampiric blood to kick in (which can be really annoying if it kicks in while being bullied at 13) and then slows to a crawl.

                                                            So that’s, using half those previous numbers (because 15th gens aren’t always fertile), 50 kids who mostly grow up like ordinary kids (they’re said to be unusually quiet as infants, but extremely temperamental toddlers) until something bad happens... then they spontaneously develop super-strength and some other common discipline (so one of the less obvious ones), recover quick from injuries and all but stop aging (which, depending on the age, might not be all that noticeable... a thirty year old might look twenty if their vampiric nature kicked in at sixteen... it’d be their late 40s to 50s before it starts getting obvious and some people just age well).

                                                            Presuming half those kids are in smaller towns because that’s where 15th gens are statistically safest... 25 of them live their whole lives as just the small town trouble-maker (who eventually graduates to a serious of dead-end jobs, failed relationships and trouble with the law because of their temper and inordinate strength) and not even be noticed for what they are (suicide, death by cop or similar other violent incident that goes the wrong way probably takes out quite a few before the slowed aging is notable. Some might not even have a trigger event their whole lives.

                                                            25 of them grow up in the city where a vampire MIGHT notice them... but again, not that noticeable given the disciplines they’re likely to spontaneously develop (by the rules; auspex, celerity, dominate, fortitude, obfuscate or presence + potence) and more opportunities for death by unrelated violence due to their tempers.

                                                            But even if all of them survive the mundane dangers of life that’s still maybe two-dozen Dhampirs spread over the entire United States and a span of two decades who might wind up in a situation where they’d be recognized by vampires as supernatural.

                                                            And even then, the most likely means of doing so (Auspex) is probably going to identify them as a ghoul/revenant and further investigation would reveal a lack of an obvious master.

                                                            Frankly, it’s a minor miracle that the Camarilla is even aware Dhampirs exist (due to Dr. Netchurch’s penchant for investigation instead of just disposing of a masterless ghoul without further investigation which I imagine would be the norm for Masquerade preservation).

                                                            Outside of the 15-16th Gen parents, the average Cainite probably doesn’t even know Dhampirs are possible (and the vamp parent still might not believe it). The average city has probably only ever had one Dhampir turn up and the odds of that one actually being recognized for what it actually is are rather slim; most probably got taken out without ever even knowing what they actually were.

                                                            I think that’s probably why Requiem tied in the notion that Dhampirs have some degree of supernatural luck simply to explain how enough of them exist to even BE a player character.

                                                            So far, every Dhampir I’ve ever made has had some type of “good” fortune in their background that has allowed them to learn both what they are and a means to avoid being insta-ganked as a walking Masquerade violation.
