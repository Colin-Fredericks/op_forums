    Non lethal punishment. Maybe the big bad has a soft spot and thinks the players will come round to his way of thinking... after a few rounds of torture. Hell the big bad could even play the roll of the saviour.
    This is more like him. The thing is the case of the antagonist in my game is very sui generis as my players quite like him in a way, also because he's not a "black and white" kind of "bad guy", but more like someone who thinks the ends justify the means. He had more than once the chance to destroy the characters, but he "acts in mysterious ways" (his demeanour is "enigma", btw).They don't quite grasp his real goal.

    So, all in all I'm more interested in hearing about other stories you people have to share about situations where "everything went wrong" for the group and how you manage to make it "fun" for everyone, even when the antagonist wins or gets the upper hand, etc.

                        This "Xanatos gambit" or "Xanatos roulette" pattern, attitude, method, or tactic could also very well be described and defined as the villian or the antagonist ( or a team of them ) as winning and succeeding despite setbacks and failures - and also no matter the chances and circumstances - in practically every encounter, situation, and also in regard to almost each event.
                        It's a good point. At the moment the group is in a situation (caused by their own actions and choices) that there's just isn't much they can do.

                        I feel if you're really going to try Xanatos gambit, you aught to write down a flow chart of what could happen, just to keep you grounded and away from the unreasonable and you can actually take a step back when players do something your villain can't account for. (plus like you can use it to appease your players if they think you're BS'ing them too hard)
                        You mentioned something that I always make. As an aspiring writer (I'm yet to publish a book), I like to write this kind of things, like what the NPCs in general know and what are they up to and how their plans could be foiled and sometimes even their actions "behind the scenes", because I don't really like to take "too arbitrary decisions", one of the things I find more fascinating about RPG is that the story has this many ramifications of possibilities, it's always open and I try to keep it this way. By the way this is one of the reasons I often post these kinds of situations in a forum, to hear other thoughts, to explore the many possible ramifications the story can take as another way to let the "RNG" factor influence my writing, the direction the story goes, etc.

                        PS: I'm also a great fan of Shakespeare and I love MacBeth, but I think perhaps Hamlet is my favorite play. I think Hamlet is one of (if not the) the best characters ever made.
