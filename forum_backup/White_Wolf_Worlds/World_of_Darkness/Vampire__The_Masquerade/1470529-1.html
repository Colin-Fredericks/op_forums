    Originally posted by Thoth View Post

    While redundancy is a good strategy, the scope of it is what I am disagreeing with.

    Why have a larger herd, which must be maintained by the way, when you can own hospitals and blood banks.
    As CTPhipps said, having control over a hospital or blood bank IS having dots Herd and/or Domain. If I were the Storyteller in charge, that is absolutely how I would rule it.

    It is consistent with the intent behind those backgrounds: consistent sources of blood the vampire doesn't have to work for, and a location under the vampire's control that can be legally denied to other vampires, respectively.


    Unless you are running a compound where the Herd never leave, each additional herd member is a potential security hazard. Just having more of them doesn't solve a problem or prevent one, it just adds to security hassles.

    Even if you do go the hole private herd in my basement route, have you considered the logistics of housing, feeding, and upkeep on that many people? The average american home only has about 3 days supplies in it. Without a lot of planning and investment there is no way to survive a siege with a large herd for any meaningful length of time.
    That's not actually a strike against the concept, though. The game is not obligated to make Herd a free background with no upkeep or downsides. Having one's Herd be vulnerable to being disrupted is just the cost of doing business in the vampire world.

    Again, that's the Jyhad.

    Moreover, my suggestion was in response to the assumption that Herd 2 was alright, but any more was not worth it. Well, ANY amount of Herd is going to vulnerable to something. Redundancy is the way to deal with that.

    If anything, the fact that Herd is a security issue is all the more reason to expand and diversify. If any given vessel or source of blood (like a blood bank) can be targeted, having more of them insulates the vampire from attacks against it.

    It is not unreasonable for an Elder to think as you do, and say that having more Herd is more trouble than it's worth. That's a perfectly valid motivation for some Elders. But it's also perfectly valid for an Elder to think as I do, by diversifying "revenue" sources. On the assumption that it's impossible to protect everything, all the time, so it's easier to just cast a wider net.

    Is this necessarily the smartest thing? Maybe, maybe not. But, as I said in my previous post, vampires are not perfectly rational actors. They are "allowed" to make bad decisions.

    Especially if they creates conflict in the game.


    Domain is the same scenario, why claim a domain that you have to defend. That basically is putting up a huge neon sign saying "find this elder here". Since most Elders tend to have lower Generations than most of the locals, that is just endless conflict waiting to happen for nothing more than bragging rights to an eating area.
    Again, that's the Jyhad for you. Endless conflict is sort of the name of the game.

    Moreover, while I stated in the previous post that Elders need no other reason to hold Domain other than the animalistic urge to have it - and I stand by this - there are plenty of practical reasons to hold Domain.
    • Having a hunting ground that other vampires aren't legally allowed to enter. In turn, giving you the right to murder any vampire trespassing in that Domain.
    • Having a part of the community or essential infrastructure that another vampire cannot legally interfere with. If your Domain is the police, no one else is allowed to manipulate the police without your permission.
    • Speaking of permission, Domain is not merely valuable for its ability to be denied to other vampires, but because access can be offered at the Domain holder's discretion. Whether as hunting ground or in its own right, access to the Domain is a bargaining chip the vampire can leverage to their own advantage. Elders carving up a city between them isn't just for their own edification, it's to allow them to strong-arm younger licks into doing what the Elders want or risk starvation.


    Of course just become you are an Elder and claim Domain, it doesn't mean a younger upstart Prince won't just declare immanent domain and seize it or declare it a public hunting area.
    That depends on how powerful the Prince is. Remember, the power structures of Kindred politics are built on a Kindred's ability to enforce their will. An upstart Prince can declare eminent domain over anything they please. What matters is whether they're strong enough to make that claim stick.

    In some cities, Princes are strong enough that other vampires must acquiesce to their demands. In other cities, it's the other way around. Lodin, the archetypal Prince in VtM, was in reality subordinate to the Primogen who actually controlled Chicago.

    If a Prince is strong enough to claim an Elder's Domain by fiat, they better be able to make that claim stick. Otherwise, they're looking at a reprisal that may be bloody. Especially if the Elders, collectively, decide that the upstart Prince's ability to take one Elder's Domain means they might do it to all of them, so it's in their collective best interest to gang up on him. Eliminate the upstart, before they can consolidate power.

    This is by no means guaranteed. The Jyhad is complicated, and depends greatly on the local balance of power and the interests of the Kindred involved. But this is A way the situation can go. Kindred politics has a lot of give and take. A lot of jockeying for position. Vampires only gain resources by taking them, often from other, more established licks. Power plays are made. Sometimes, these plays don't work. Often, there is retribution.

    Perhaps it is just my way of thinking, but Elders should never be "the Lost Boys" but with bigger bank accounts and older clothing fashion. That focus on street level things is left behind after a certain age bracket. Does this mean that most game storylines actually don't work with Elders, unfortunately yes. There are still plenty of cool games to be played with them, but they won't interact in the same way with the world as younger vampires.
    I would argue that vampires, being creatures of Stasis, leave behind old ways less readily than one would imagine. Moreover, Elder vampires are very much like the mafia. A veneer of respectability, stretched over a truth of violence, exploitation, and power politics. Indeed, while an Elder would rankle at being compared to gangs scrambling over city streets as "turf", that's more or less exactly what they are.

    If anything, playing into that is Vampire: The Masquerade at its most thematically consistent. For all the posturing of old vampires, they are little better than their younger rivals. They are violent predators that seek to claim stretches of territory, even if it means protecting it with brutal violence. It doesn't matter that the Elder will send minions to break legs for them. Their Domains are still, ultimately, violently enforced. Even if they spend most of their unlives in courts or art galleries, or building stock portfolios, they will still have an upstart murdered for touching their stuff.

    That's not a contradiction in terms. That's pathos. That's Punk.

    We need to ask ourselves not why the classic VtM dynamic doesn't work. We need to ask ourselves how Elders need to think in order to MAKE it work. The world-building of VtM exists to serve the needs of the roleplaying game, not the other way around.

    Luckily, we don't need to stretch to make street level games involving Elders work. Elders are flawed individuals, whose actions are not always perfectly rational. They're just older, more powerful, more entrenched. More entitled. They may even delude themselves into thinking they're better than the Neonates scrambling for sustenance in the slums. But they're just another parasite.

    Making them all completely absent from the night-to-night operations of vampire unlife is not only unlikely - no Elder can maintain their operations without having a power base, especially when Diablerie is a factor - it's also contrary to the needs of the game. Why even have them in the setting if they aren't going to be a source of risk or opportunity for vampire PCs?

        Also I think we are coming at the story points from opposite ends of the Background spectrum. Lets break it down a bit to show the differences.

        Blood Bank/Hospital when accessed by the Resources background means that you own it, the same way you own your Haven or vehicle. Thus you can access the blood packs either completely legally or with only minor paperwork manipulation. As long as you own it, there isn't much in the way of hostile take overs or board membership changes that can threaten your access to what you need. Also it is worth noting that ownership in this case also means you have access to things beyond just the blood.

        Blood Bank/Hospital when accessed by the Domain background means that someone else owns and operates the institution and you just feed there. If you are breaking in after hours then you have to contend with security, if you are just trying to get blood via falsified paperwork then you have to hope the office drones aren't paying close attention.

        Blood Banks/Hospital when accessed by the Contact background is where you get the archetypal nurse or doctor selling blood packs under the table. Once again the location is owned by a third party.

        In short the Backgrounds system is based in abstraction rather than simulation, this is great for book keeping, but troublesome for figuring out what applies to it. Resources is a good example of how poorly it is abstracted, Resources 5 as a stat can describe a mortal with a few million in property ownership and less than 500k in the bank, it can describe people like Elon Musk, Jeff Bezos, or Bill Gates who are in the Multi-Billionaire category, or it can describe the obscenely rich kindred that the fluff always seems to reference as being many times richer than the richest mortals on earth so multi-trillionaire then?

        I wonder if certain things like Blood Banks/Hospitals should require a combination of backgrounds to get. Like you must have Resources, Domain, and Contacts to be able to lay claim to such a thing.

                        An Elder is by definition just a vampire who has survived a long time. Now this can be accomplished by excessive amounts of topor which results in a vampire who has the age, but none of the experience of having existed for so long. This is simple enough to understand, but being an active Elder, there is the implication that the vampire has matured psychologically, gained patience, learned how not to overextend themselves, etc.

                        In the Kindred of the East game line they had a term for younger vampires called a "Running Monkey" which described a Kuei-jin that was under one century old. They were expected to revel in their power, settle scores, and tidy up business from their mortal days. But after that century was up, it was essentially considered an embarrassment to act like that. As ritualized and organized as V:tM society is, the fluff never really addressed the notion of maturity as a social construct other than regarding paths of enlightenment. Though to be honest this could simply be a side of affect to V:tM being earlier in the game development cycle than KotE was.

                        Getting back to defining an Elder, assuming they didn't snooze their way into the age bracket, that means that they would have had to survive that amount of time being active and interacting with the rest of the setting. I think we can all agree with that as a baseline, but where things break down is the ramifications of that statement and how some people hand wave it away.

                        First off the vampire regardless of Path of Enlightenment will have demonstrated the psychological endurance for existing in the WoD for centuries or millennia. They are not going to have an existential crisis this late in the game, if they had a weakness to that they would either died/been killed off/resolved it within the first century or so of unlife.

                        Second if a vampire Elder has a particular preference for how to do things, ie being a warrior, businessman, influencer, etc., then they will have the stats to support that in a meta kind of way. A vampire won't exist long enough to become an elder if they want to live as a warrior but then never learn combat disciplines or boost the related ability stats.

                        Thirdly after having existed for so long, after a certain point the rewards of certain things just don't matter to you. As a child in kindergarten, we can easily be motivated by a gold star sticker, as a teenager we want validation from our peers, as adults.... well unless it has a paycheck, we don't care. There are certain aspects of vampiric existence that function the same way. Trying to have an Elder involved with certain storylines is really like suggesting that you can pay an adult in gold star stickers. Admittedly Malkavians throw that arguement out the window...

                        In the end by the unforgiving nature of the WoD setting, an Elder has to be more of a Dark Souls player using banana controllers to do a speed run than a casual gamer playing a match three game on their break.

                                    My general take on ALL the various things here in this thread:

                                    A shit ton of random V:TM thoughts about Domain

                                    1. Generally, Elders are different from Methusalehs. An Elder tends to be someone who will probably avoid combat but does have a lot of exceptional powers. A Methuselah is effectively invincible from most vampires' standings.

                                    2. Elders don't deal with mortals save as slaves so Elders CREATED the whole Elysium system so they can interact with vampires. The Toreador especially but all vampires really have their equivalents because vampires who aren't wights are social creatures.

                                    3. Elders like having Domain because Domain is basically something that makes vampiredom easier and Elders are set in their ways. Every vampire wants to have an established hunting ground with no competition unless they're Gangrel or Ravnos.

                                    4. Vampires will always pick "from the tap" over blood bags because blood bags are cold and nasty but will slake the Hunger if you're comically busy.

                                    5. Herds can represent blood banks and hospitals too as well as Domain but generally represent mortals you have easy access to that won't put up a fight.

                                    6. Not all vampires keep herds, not even all Elders, but it certainly is CONVEINANT. Like having wireless internet connection vs. going to a public wifi route.

Public Domains with "free hunting" are always in existence in a successful city but these places often find themselves with unusually high amounts of deaths, crime, and missing persons.                                         A few further points to avoid confusion over some issues - good job by CTPhipps for the clarifications:

                                        - Most elders are by definition embraced from before the 1800s, and most ancillae date to before the 1900s. The social framework of Kindred society as envisioned by the Camarilla doesn't account for the huge changes to cities and to transportation, specifically. It dates to times when there were no blood banks, apartment complexes that provide super-easy feeding, phone and internet to run things from afar, or taxis, subways and airplanes to zip around. Domain and Herd were much more difficult backgrounds to acquire back in the day, what with the smaller number of people, the prevalence of pestilence, the lack of nightlife, and the much lower population density. Assets were concentrated mostly in landed property rather than cash, and even the running of things like merchant companies was done face-to-face on physical location, rather than over the phone or such. Thus, Domain was related in an absolute way to the Resources and Influence backgrounds as well. The result was that the Camarilla elders parcelled out both the cities and the countryside, so that in practice there was no room for new Kindred, many of whom traveled to new lands like the Americas to establish themselves. In the present day, the old concept of Domain falls under the valid criticisms listed here, which is why so many neonates are tempted to go Anarch or join the Sabbat. These two sects suffer from turf wars and from overpopulation, but have much more freedom in operating on a nightly basis than the Camarilla with its feudal domain system.

                                        - Some elders follow a successful strategy of remaining hidden, and either withdrawing from the Jyhad or operating from behind the scenes. There are several issues here, and also reasons for operating openly instead. Firstly, it is taken as common wisdom in the setting that vampires need to work towards overarching goals and interact with others in order to stave off the Beast and avoid madness. The Embrace introduces monstrous urges, but does not take away the human craving for companionship or new stimuli. Isolated vampires don't end up sitting at home watching TV, they end up like Bulscu, like the Tzimisce's Metamorphosist weirdos, or like the feral Gangrel on the variants of the Road of the Beast. One could make the valid point that vampires who focus on knowledge and scholarship can remain sequestered for long periods of time, but eventually they also need other vampires to fetch further research material.

                                        - Unless they have strong childer in place, like the Camarilla's Inner Circle do, vampires who conceal themselves become isolated and weak. The elders have their own elders, who have long-term agendas and are not squeamish about discarding useless tools. A vampiric elder knows too much and has too much personal strength to be left alone; in Sins of the Blood, on the chapter on autarkis, it's made clear that one of the Camarilla's goals is to account for all elders and embroil them in a society of mutual pretestation debts. An independent elder like Vlad Tepes is a threat to the leadership of the sects, and is marked for elimination if possible. In all clans, even in the Camarilla, reclusive and 'useless' elders are seen as a waste of blood and are potential diablerie targets for ambitious and more active childer. The Lasombra do this sort of thing openly, but to some extent this happens across the board, as long as the other elders are willing to turn a blind eye. Vampires who tire of playing the games of the sects choose to go into torpor in undisclosed locations, rather than be the target of such plots.

                                        - It hasn't been pointed out that having a big Domain background, much more than is necessary for a single vampire. is a key stepping stone to establish a brood. When a new vampire is Embraced, the sire typically parcels out a piece of their domain to their progeny, unless that vampire is taking over a vacant domain opened up by the deaths of a lot of vampires after a conflict. Having a lot of Domain means being able to support a lot of childer, which is hugely important in the long term. Vampires who don't want this sort of headache will find themselves outnumbered 10 to 1 by vampires who do establish broods, and all sorts of bad things can happen to them.

                                            As for the rule of thumb of 1 vampire to 100,000 mortals, there are a few things to keep in mind.

                                            - This is a guideline for the number of vampires who can be supported without them coming into conflict or causing any noticeable ripples under modern day conditions. With this few vampires, the occasional murderous frenzies or corrupt activities are not statistically significant. Cities do end up supporting more vampires than this, but the result is squabbles over turf, and a lot of troubleshooting to take care of the problems that crop up.

                                            - Tiny princedoms of 4-5 vampires don't really happen, because outside of the major cities, a system of feudal overlordship exists. Many small domains of a few thousand or ten thousand people can fall within the territory of a single vampire, who is subject to a Prince who is based in a relatively larger town. Vampires can have an Elysium in this town but are otherwise spread out over a wide area, unlike the clustered conditions in major cities. Even if the vampire in charge of a minor town has the presumption of calling themselves Prince, they will be outranked by much more formidable vampires and fall under their purview.

                                            - 1 vampire to 100,000 mortals, even in the ideal case, refers to the number of permanent, established vampires that are present in a city. In practice, there will be many more vampires, with the known but typically unstated fact that most vampires don't make it. Vampires meet the Final Death or are exiled for all sorts of reasons; the Tremere, for example, have a lot of apprentices who fall because they mess up a Thaumaturgy path or ritual, or who are asked to get some difficult ritual ingredients like werewolf fangs or something and understandably fail. Thus, there will be many neonate coteries contrary to the established statistic, but with the unspoken understanding that they will ultimately not be permanent residents of the domain.
