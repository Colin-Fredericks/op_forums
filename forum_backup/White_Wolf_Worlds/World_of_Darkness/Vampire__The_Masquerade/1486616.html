    Vampiric Nature, and why the Anarchs will always fail on a long enough timeline

    07-16-2022, 10:20 AM
    We begin this groaning and long post (that I'm sure my girlfriend would roll her eyes at, were she to see me hacking away at my laptop right now about a fictional game, instead of watching America's Got Talent with her) with a quick foray into comparing human and vampiric nature.

    At its core, the Vampire must feed on blood to sustain itself. This is the crux of the vampiric condition and matters more than all the political and scheming machinations the World of Darkness has to offer. It is the one burning need that must be satisfied regularly. We as humans can relate this only to the need for food. In its purest form, this presents itself as the nightly hunter who seeks prey, similar to that of a Hunter Gatherer. It has no guarantee of success, and relies solely on its skill, wily nature, and its understanding of its prey. Of the thirteen Clans, the Gangrel have traditionally kept to this idea the closest. Indeed, a Gangrel maxim is that if one is confused, they should subsist solely on animal blood for a month to gain perspective. That is, one should strip away all that is not the core of the vampiric condition and exist solely as a predator, to understand one's nature once again. One will notice this continual "back to roots" lifestyle of the Gangrel has kept them out of political forays that do not directly affect them, in the way that, say, the Toreador or Ventrue have been affected.

    This moves, like humans did, to a local cultivation of a food source. In human terms, this became farming crops and livestock. In vampires, it is analogous to maintaining a Herd. This is obviously beneficial in that the question of one's source of vitae becomes much more reliable. Given some cultivation, one can easily create an overabundance of vitae, as human farming created an overabundance of food. We see this parallel through the formation of the first cities, and in vampiric history in the formation of various blood cults.

    It is a crucial point to make though, that with this move from hunter gatherer to farmer, one shifts their focus from reliance on one's hunting skill, to one of reliance on their ability to keep and maintain their crops/Herd. With this shift of mindset, one begins the thought of not just cultivation, but protection. It is no secret that farming led inevitably to the creation of the first human cities, banded together for mutual protection and trade, for the loss of one's crops and livestock meant death by starvation. We see analogous behavior among vampires, who, faced with an overabundance of Vitae, would choose to band together for mutual protection and pooled resources, rather than risk losing their Herd. Indeed, I suspect many Embraces throughout the history of the World of Darkness were ultimately driven by a desire to create an underling to inherit a portion of this Herd, so long as they protected the greater whole (this will become important later).

    With an overabundance of Vitae and a more complex social structure forming, vampiric "civilization" developed a hierarchy, as humans did. Humans developed five castes: Outcasts, Farmers, Craftsmen, Military Rulers, and the Spiritual or Philosophical Caste. In some cultures, Caste was fluid, as in the case in Greece, where Epictetus was born a slave and became a philosopher. In other cases it was more rigid, as in India. It should be noted, that if one views the Indian caste system from the perspective of one's bloodline rather than one as an individual, it was indeed possible to climb castes, it just took a much longer period. In vampiric culture, one sees a similar trend, though the lines are a bit more blurred. Certainly, some Clans were more pigeon holed into some castes, as we see in the division of High and Low Clans, but one could also ascend or descend in hierarchy. With that said, generally speaking, one was embraced into the general caste of one's sire.

    Now, this works perfectly fine as a Feudal type caste system, as long as there are enough resources to go around, and there is an equitable distribution of power. This doesn't mean everything could, should, or must be equal, but merely that there is a mutual respect between all parties, who seek cultivation for the greater whole, rather than an accumulation of individual power. When the former becomes the latter, those on the bottom inevitably rebel, as they should, for the social contract has been violated. We can see this very concept in the American and French Revolutions, as well as beginning to boil up in the United States, where the lower castes (mostly outcasts, farmers and craftsmen) are being abused by ruling castes. (I'll not speak further on this, as further exploration of that idea will I'm sure turn this thread into a current events shitshow, and pretty much everywhere is a current events shitshow these days, so I beg you, please, let's not.)

    Now, back to Vampire.

    This Feudal system works relatively fine if the population remains somewhat stable. An established sire embraces here, an Elder goes into Torpor or an Ancilla dies there, and for the most part, everyone knows their place. What we've seen in the last century is an explosion of Neonates, with relatively little established structure in place to "puzzle piece" them in. This has led to Elders scrambling to maintain their vast swaths of power, while Neonates fight for scraps of next to nothing, creating a system where the higher abuse the lower. This has led to rebellion, and the creation of the Anarch sect.

    Now, we have seen this before, in the Anarch Revolt, which led to the formation of the Sabbat. The same events were taking place (abusive elders lording over large numbers of younger vampires), and the result was relatively the same (the formation of a "new" sect). However, as they say, Revolutionaries are Idealists, and Rulers are Pragmatists, and we can see that the result has been to create a sect which, from a 1000 foot view, looks remarkably similar to their Camarilla rivals. Elders maintain their grip on power, and large swaths of Neonates fight for scraps. Over time, this has been allowed to bubble up and cause multiple civil wars, which has allowed the distribution of power to be more equitable. We can see this taking place, in that several Neonates have taken the position of Priscus or Bishop and ascended relatively quickly, and the formation of the Panders "clan," which helped to provide a path "upwards" for the Clanless.

    The Camarilla's pressure valve is not one of civil war, but that of the Anarchs. It has been amusingly discussed as such in multiple splatbooks (and indeed, hinted at in V:tM Bloodlines) that joining the Anarchs is little more than teenage rebellion, as after a few decades of raging against the machine as an Anarch, one falls back into the Camarilla fold, older, wiser, and more willing to play ball. This worked well enough, until enough Anarchs had congregated to form a "revolution" of sorts to take hold of power (I will use the Anarch Free States as a specific example), which coincided perfectly with the explosion of the population of vampires.

    And, with the Idealist Revolutionary winning the battle, the Pragmatist begins ruling. Here, we find a division of ideals.

    On one hand, one collection of Anarchs would see to it that a more equitable distribution of power (effectively, domains and access to blood) remains in effect. On the other, we see the formation of what has been amusingly dubbed Camarilla-Lite. In adopting the latter, even by the smallest of degree (for the smallest deviation from one's ideals will inevitably lead to their inversion), one simply proves the point of the Camarilla, as Tara Kearney, Prince (and former Baron) of San Diego has shown.

    In adopting the former, one must by necessity adopt the Hunter Gatherer mindset mentioned at the beginning of this groaning tome. While this is indeed a viable long term hunting strategy, what it does not provide is a pragmatic reason for vampires to collaborate, only an idealistic reason. This will almost universally lead to an atomization of power and thus be ripe for steamrolling by organized groups (the Camarilla, Sabbat, Kuei Jin, etc).

    While the Hunter Gatherer may work well for the Gangrel, it should be noted that the Gangrel are a Clan, and not a Sect. They may identify with Anarchs, Camarilla, Sabbat, or simply disappear into the woods without reason for a century or two, simply because they want to. They are not attempting to hold territory, power or Herd, as this is a strictly "farming" mindset. A Clan is a family, and is inherent to the Blood. A Sect requires collaboration, which requires as its foundation the very thing the Anarchs attempt to undermine (Herds, and thus a cultivation of power to protect said Herd).

    This is an important fact to ruminate on. It all comes down to Blood and access to it. The moment you establish a Herd (night clubs, blood cults, etc), you must then defend that Herd, for your own survival. The moment you defend that Herd for your own survival, you establish a Domain. The moment you establish a Domain, you establish power bases and collaborate for mutual protection, and by that point, you've already proven your rivals correct, you're just at a different point in the continuum.

                                              Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post

                                              Okay, so explain it to me. Enlightenment me. Give me the cliff notes on these Zapatistas and Rojava.
                                              This gets into some serious weeds regarding how hierarchy is defined in a political organizational sense.

                                              But the basics for both groups (as they operate on similar principles) is that all authority is granted by direct democracy Anyone with the power to "give orders" was granted that power by the subjects of those orders, who can never lose the power to revoke that authority. So, while in battle, their armed forces have a standard military chain of command (aka there are people giving orders) it cannot be considered a true hierarchy because all of the soldiers have the power to vote out their leaders for different leaders if they don't like how the military is being run. A general is never superior to their troops, as their troops are part of the directly democratic body that sets the rules for how military commanders can legally act. The general is immediately authorized to give orders, but is ultimately socially and politically subordinate to their soldiers.

                                              The problem with the whole long post is that the goals of the Anarchs are never defined, so "inevitable failure" is impossible to pass judgement on. Since the Anarchs do not, as a generality, advocate for the complete dismantling of having vampire societies they aren't proven wrong by creating vampire societies once they gain control over a territory.

                                              The Anarchs aren't talking about becoming your grossly oversimplified take on the Gangrel, because they're ultimately not separated from the Camarilla and Sabbat by what to do about local organizational matters. Their focus is on how strong centralized authority should be. The Camarilla has always been an organization with strong centralized authority, and the Sabbat became that before V5 nuked them as a functional organization. The Anarchs seek less centralization if not outright decentralization on an international level. The Anarchs do not believe that the Camarilla's Inner Circle, or the Sabbat's Regency are good methods for setting laws that apply to all members of their organizations, or setting policies to handle internal disputes, due to skewed priorities of such centralized power being threats to stable blood supply (as the Inner Circle and Regent are far removed from such concerns in any given city), and want a system with greater authority given to local vampires in how large scale vampire society is run.
