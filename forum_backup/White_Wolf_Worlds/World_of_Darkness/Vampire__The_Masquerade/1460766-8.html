If things heat up, I'd say they will need to move quickly to have a chance.

                            Ashfinders part 7

                            Type: Rituals

                            Synopsis: What the Ashfinders have come up with.

                            Analysis: The final part of the Ashfinders is a short list of abilities that Ashe can provide and what sort of abilities you need with Thin Blood Alchemy to create Ashe. The cult doesn't really break the mold with these abilities and I'm pretty much of the mind that its abilities are not that different from what Thin Blooded Alchemy should be able to come up with normally.

                            This is going to be a somewhat controversial opinion but I thin Thin Blooded Alchemy was a poor decision on the part of the writers to try to make Thin Bloods more viable gameplay wise. Personally, I think it'd have been better if they'd stuck with the idea that Thin Bloods can either get up to 3 in Disciplines and no higher or 1 dot in any Discipline with the possibility of having their own personal Discipline. If you're going to play Thin Bloods then you're wanting to avoid power gaming in the first place.

                            Maybe also make them able to take Numina like True Faith or Hedge Magic. As such, this tells you how they can make Ashe and also replicate other Kindred's disciplines that they have literally smoked.

                                Ashfinders part 8

                                Type: Conclusion

                                Synopsis: These guys are fucked.

                                Analysis: Overall, the Ashfinders are an excellent cult and a group that can definitely add an element of horror to the Thin Bloods. They're adapting to become the same kind of vicious evil monsters as other Kindred and the cannibalism element plays into the very same stereotypes that the Elders have had about the Duskborn.

                                I don't think the Ashfinders are long for the world, though. This is an organization that once its particular drug of choice dries out is going to have a very nasty crash. Unlike Elder Blood, Elder ashe is a non-renewable resource and the Gehenna War is eventually going to peter out in terms of supply. They might be able to Embrace younger Kindred for a "weak" version of Ashe but that's not going to be too great of a supply and will cause their Humanity to plummet like a stone.

                                I predict the Ashfinders are eventually going to receive a meeting from a Justicar in Ibiza and they are going to exterminate the cult root to stem. I also wouldn't be surprised if Mortius did end up on the Red List for his role in creating such an organization. As mentioned, the Ashfiners pretty much play into a trifecta of Elder fears: diablerie, Thin Bloods, and modern technology that endangers the Masquerade. It will probably spill over and get Naomi Stewart destroyed as well unless she's the person who decides to destroy the Thin Bloods (and lose Humanity in the process). I suspect Ashe the formula will survive the destruction of the Ashfinders but something used much-much more judiciously as a weapon by Thin Bloods rather than a party drug.

                                That's also the BEST outcome for these people since I could easily also see the Ashfinders suffering a more supernatural PSA ending where their rampant use of smoking Elders results in them becoming the first Thin Bloods to become wights as the Beast Shards destroy them and leave them a bunch of drugged out murderous monsters possessed by the fragments of the Damned. You could even go full Poltergeist and have the Cindy Incident sucked into the Underworld.

                                        1 - As an ST I like a sense of balance because it actually makes my work easier when exploring different chronicle styles. With mechanical balance I can have a mixed group without players feeling cheated, and I can have a "low level" chronicle without robbing myself of the option of high challenges for the players at a later moment;

                                        Corollary: Balance doesn't mean perfect math or anything like that. What it means is that players feel like all the options are valid and interesting, and that any drawback is worth the advantages for the player. A character don't have to feel the same, but the old saying that choosing a weaker option just for the challenge makes you a better player is BS.

                                        2 - More importantly, I like the concept for both Caitiff and Thin Blood that those vampires have stranger powers, regardless of strength. That those weird vampires play by other rules no one is sure about.

                                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                        Mostly agree, but I can see their storyline going some other ways.

                                        As much as their supply is doomed to diminish greatly, it doesn't mean they will be without something to offer, especially with Mortius still studying Ashe and other things. They may learn how to sacrifice quality for quantity, to get more from a lesser supply. They may develop a way to extract it from living Elders as Grumpy RPG Reviews describes. They may even learn how to extract Ashe from previous Ashe users, encouraging addiction to make the future supply.

                                        They may also learn the secrets of other drugs and stuff that fit their theme. So one day they stop churning out Ashe, but start to deliver other things.

                                        I totally agree, though, that they'll be on a Justicar's to do list ASAP if they just stay as they are. But their first real clashes with Kindred power may also make them learn to adapt and find a niche. They don't need to be influential in the Camarilla, just to have enough clout to stay in peace at their little corner of the world.

They certainly encourage the destruction of elders for production of Ashe, but they're also less murderers and more receptors. Bigger Jyhad players may start to use those chains and use the cult as a dump for their own purposes, and keep them under protection.

It may make human, or even animal, blood work and taste as elder vitae, turning their product into a highly sought after asset.

                                        Finally, they may end up getting SI protection instead, making Ibiza into the definitive Elder Hell: a seat of power and money in the hands of Thin Blood and protected by cutting edge technology in the hands of humans that already know too much.

                                            With regards to the Thin-bloods, I've long had a mixed opinion on them, stretching back to the Rev Ed days. I don't hate Thin-blood Alchemy and in theory, like it as a concept (I haven't played V5 much less as a Thin-blood or with one so I can't attest to how it plays) but I do find it questionable as the sole Discipline of the Thin-bloods. An opinion? Sure. But the sole Discipline? That I struggle with.

                                            But honestly that's kind of the problem with Thin-bloods. They're supposed to be a watered down version of Kindred but if you make them too weak, why play them? And to be frank, I've never been in game with a Thin-blood PC. None of the people I've gamed with ever expressed an interest in playing them. And I can kind of get why. They're looked down upon by many in-universe and quiet weak. There's really not much of reason to play them, especially when we have long had Caitiff and Ghouls.

                                            So I can get why they would give them Thin-blood Alchemy as something new and cool to get people interested in playing them. But limiting them to what is basically Blood Sorcery: Gutter Magick Edition was a wrong move. Blood Sorcery has to be taught from another or created through experimentation which wouldn't be a viable thing for every Thin-blood character but now that's all they have. And in the case of the latter, it would still require the Thin-blood to think of experimenting with blood, theirs and that of mortals. So yeah, if the Thin-blood doesn't have connections with other Thin-bloods or a curiosity towards their new condition, where exactly would they pick up the knowledge of Alchemy?

                                            Thin-blood Alchemy is an interesting concept but with a flawed execution.

                                            As for CTPhipps' final thoughts on the Ashfinders, I pretty much agree. Long term, the odds are not good for them surviving. Not completely impossible but not good either. The real question will be how effective the Cam will be once they eventually hit them. I could see them being less than successful in destroying the Ashfinders, getting most or all the people at the top but the cult as a whole fragmenting, leading to several successor cults that spread the ideas of the cult and Ashe further across the world.

                                                            I don't personally think it fair outside an All Thin Bloods game. As much as some players do like to make such choices I was always irked by this argument when you have a character concept that could work in a mixed group, but is avoided because it is an objectively worse option without needing to be.

                                                            Of course, my experience also includes a lot of pedantic players that like to defend that such differences make for a superior RP challenge and players that complain are just "inferior roleplayers that should grow up or go back to their D&D and video games".                                                             But mechanical balance don't have to mess with the notion of being weaker or less potent from a Watsonian perspective.                                                             Thin Bloods already have several weaknesses outside messing with the Disciplines. I like 'em weirder to compensate, not just overall weaker.
