        The previous versions had you lose Humanity at 1 if you killed anyone, even in self-defense.
        This is false. No edition prior to V5 allowed for this. Here, trimmed for space:

        V1, page 130:

        "6 -Unintentional killing
        ...
        2 -Murder
        1 -The most heinous and demented acts"

        V2, page 125:

        "6 Negligent killing
        ...

        "6 Accidental violation (drinking a vessel dry out of starvation)
        ...

                            One thing I'll say about V5 is that it's given me a deeper appreciation for Vampire: The Requiem, ironically. V5 uses a lot of concepts from there, but VTR generally does them better. I'd always been put off VTR because of the setting, but now I'm open to just stealing mechanics I like because V5 has kind of thrown the rulebook out the window (as it were).

                            I have said this before, I think, but the Hunger mechanic works better in a one-off cinematic game like Alien. Alien has Stress Dice that work pretty much the same, except they add to your dice pools rather than replacing dice. For a while, a bit of Stress is okay, but it soon spirals out of control. For games where everyone freaks out and then gets killed, it works, but even there, we got exhausted with it after a few sessions (so longer adventures of even three sessions don't hold up as well as one-shots).

                            In VTM, there's no benefit to Hunger Dice, and every Messy Crit or Bestial Failure brings problems, whereas rolling 1s on Stress Dice in Alien only presents a risk of something going wrong (you roll on another table to see what happens and anything under a certain threshold is fine).

                            But there's something I want to like about Hunger Dice. I think Shadow Dice and Nightmare Dice present better examples of how the system could work. They present a better feeling of risk, and you get a tangible benefit from engaging with that part of the system. In V5, they're all stick and no carrot. I want some carrot. The CoD has some good models for this (Satiety in Beast, for example, and the Lost Boys in Hurt Locker, who gain power as they run low on juice before hitting a point of no return).

                            I also think the simplicity of blood points can't be overlooked. I never once thought, 'Gee, I wish we didn't have to track these mana points.' I find the various tables of rouse checks and rerolls more complex than just saying 'this costs x BP, and you can only use y BP per turn'.

                            I love the Paths in V20 (and V20DA). Those hit the sweet spot for me. I think, as others have said, we started out with Anarchs v elders, playing Camarilla games, for a few years. Then we felt that was done and wanted to explore more things.

                            The benefit of something like the Sabbat is how alien those vampires can be. And that, for me, was always the biggest pull after those early games. I came to the table because of vampires, angst and Gothic Punk. I hung around for decades because of the kitchen sink setting, the ability to explore wildly different mindsets or belief systems, all the lore we could engage with without ever running out, and for the chance to actually be the bad guys.

                            When so many settings are still--even now--about killing evil monsters, even in games with lots of shades of grey, it's still incredibly refreshing to play Vampire. Sometimes you just want to be evil, and enjoy that.

                            That's why, after playing a Sister of Sigmar in Warhammer 4th Edition for months, for example, I'll feel such relief when we switch to Vampire for a while. Of all the popular games we regularly play, VTM is the one that really lets us scratch that itch. Of course there are other games that do that, but there seem to be some that are more popular than others and which we therefore get to play a lot more.

                            Going back to what Heavy Arms said, VTM is best when it lets you do lots of things. It has always been a kitchen sink setting, despite what the marketing said. In this case, I don't think four iterations of the game were wrong--I think the marketing was. Yes, it can be 'A Beast I Am Lest a Beast I Become', but it can also be so much more than that. That was a strength, not a weakness.

                            Now, onto something more positive, I do like the Bane/Compulsion system. The Compulsions add something nice. I just don't think they need to be as common as they are in play. I'm also thinking that, as suggested in some of the Requiem books, it would be nice for each Clan to also get a 'boon' like in CTD. So Lasombra get bonus dice for being in pitch black, for example. That balances all the negatives out a bit, IMO.

                                    The same goes for Hunger dice. 10% chance of triggering (since 10’s only matter for success and 1’s only on failures) means you’ve got a 50/50 shot at the Hunger die triggering a bestial success/failure by your 5th roll of the night and if you have 15 tests in a session it’ll trigger at least once in 80% of your sessions.
