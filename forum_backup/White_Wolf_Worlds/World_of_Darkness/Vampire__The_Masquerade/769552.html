    Dating the Oldest Stories - Enoch and the Second City

    11-19-2015, 05:31 PM
    Where do you put, in your personal opinion, the dates of Enoch and the Second City? Given that these topics are radically unclear and all supporting evidence is wildly contradictory of itself from the various sources?

    My thoughts, and I seek your own (and comments on mine)
    Enoch: Assuming that it existed and was destroyed by a flood, and that it was the source of (most) of the 3rd Generation - It seems to me that Enoch falls in either 14,000 BCE (Glboal Meltwater Pulse 1A) or 9,300 (Global Meltwater Pulse 1B). Im also assuming that 1.) No 4th Gens survive the flood, 2.) Embrace dates when possible are to be believed. Note this puts Set embraced long after the fall of Enoch. Assumption 1 is a bit of a mess, given Niktuku, etc... but I can live with it.

    [The Second City] We know that Japheth is the second or third child of Cappadocius, and is embraced in 7225. I doubt Cappadocius waited THOUSANDS OF YEARS after his siblings to start popping out childer, so again, our 4th gens are coming in the era of the second city. This puts the Second City founded around 7300 BCE at the latest. My best theory on its destruction is around 5600 BCE - later global meltwater pulse, possible [though not necessarily supported in archeology] sill-collapse flood event on the Black Sea and Persian Gulf. I like having the [Second City] be destroyed by a BLack Sea Flood, and spread out.. even as the Persian Gulf Flood drives the people who will be sumerians into the fertile crescent. Co-mingling of scattering 3rd and 4th gens gives us the confused mess that is early Cainite society after the second city.

    Thoughts?

                                  Taking all the general factors into consideration (most of the in-book sources, meshed loosely with Real World(tm) events -- but, as always, with the idea that "if the supernatural is *real*, then much of history is most emphatically *NOT* what it appears to be...), I/most of those I've played with over the past 25 years date the "First City" as being before the meso-Atlantis era, or before 12,000BCE, back into a truly (M)ythic Era of history. When the Age of Leo came and went (approx. 10,000BCE, give or take) the "Flood" had already occurred, and over the millennia other "cities" developed. The Second City which was the "last to fall" (that is, be the point when shit hit the fan for the "last time" among the 3rd Gens) was circa 3,000BCE, which goes well in-hand with most book references.

                                  The real key to grasping how to (re)interpret all of the sources is to keep in mind that -- in all probability -- *most* of the stories/dates are conceptually true. That is, different events occurred at different times across many millennia, and just as in Real Life(tm) where various legends/myths arose and blended, melding into a "history" wherein something which had been passed on by word-of-mouth might thought to have occurred a few centuries ago actually has its roots stemming from *millennia* ago (and, occasionally, vice-versa), many of the seemingly "sequential" events in Cainite history are anything-but. The "Caine" and "Lilith" of "proper" Jewish lore (dating back approx. 6,000 years) are not, in all likelihood, the "Caine" and "Lilith" who are the progenitors of the vampires (and, in Lilith's case, a bunch of other critters, too). It's very easy for us, as readers/players, to too-quickly gloss-over certain aspects while analyzing this fictional setting, such as in this case in the assumption that the 3rd generation would move/act with a speed that mirrors our modern day sensibilities. Given the context of the narratives (and meta-narratives) involved, it's entirely plausible -- and, in fact, highly probable -- that the post-First City vampires could have easily spent millennia traveling, sleeping in torpor, plotting agendas that would take many centuries just to see begin to take shape, or spend an equal amount of time merely indulging in their desires/pursuits -- for god-like immortals, the passing of many centuries or even several millennia can be as how with mortals the passing of years or even decades can go in a blur, conceptually/cognitively.

                                  The time tables between the "First" and "Second" cities could very well have spanned a considerable number of millennia, and in all probability did. (There were, of course, many other cities/domains in the interim -- more, certainly, than had existed in the Real World(tm), given the existence of supernatural beings/powers, over that same time period -- so the title/attribution of "Second City", even when kept solely in reference to the Ante's workings and ignoring humans/others, is simply that: a title/naming. Taking *any* of the concepts presented as stand-alone is, both in-narratively and more so meta-narratively, playing right into the hands of those blood-gods for whom a more "simplified"/occluded history works best for their manipulating/deceiving both their lineages as well as the broader world at large....)

                                                  Here's what canon gives us:

                                                  - Time of Thin Blood names the Antediluvians as more than ten thousand years old.
                                                  - Japheth is Embraced in 7225 BCE. Clanbook Cappadocian says that Cappadocius never sired during the era of the Second City.
                                                  - Rage Across Russia has Baba Yaga Embraced in 5000 BCE. By this point, the Second City is clearly over. Absimiliard disappears from history after his childe escapes his clutches, and finds purpose in hunting down his descendants to atone to Caine.
                                                  - Nights of Prophecy says Vasilia is more than twice (!) Baba Yaga's age, putting her own Embrace at 12,000 BCE.
                                                  - The Book of Nod states that the Fourth Generation did not exist until the Second City.
                                                  - Clanbook Nosferatu says the Fourth Generation was around during the First City. So was the Matriarch to escape Absimiliard's clutches, who may or may not be Baba Yaga.
                                                  - Clanbook Tzimisce puts the Embrace of numerous Fourth Generation Tzimisce before the Great Flood.
                                                  - Clanbook Assamite gives a very extensive time frame for events in the Second City. Haqim settled there 100 years after its founding. 200 years later, the Baali appear. 100 years later, the Assamites/Salubri wipe them out. 500 years later, they return and ur-Shulgi is Embraced. 100 years later, they're beaten again, and the Second City starts to fall apart. That comes out to 1,000 years for its lifespan.

                                                  As the OP says, canon is also a mess. Pick whatever dates you like, whether for the Second City's existence, the Embrace years of the oldest Methuselahs (Vasilia being twice Baba Yaga's age is one I'm happy to throw out), or simply the general order of events.

                                                  So far as dates I'm a fan of, 10,000 BCE for the settlement of the First City, 6000 BCE for the Great Flood and foundation of the Second City, and 5000 BCE for the fall of the Second City. The end of the Ice Age nicely gels with Caine deciding it's time to end his self-imposed exile and rejoin civilization, and the Great Flood/Second City's founding (roughly) coincides with the 8.2 kiloyear event and start of agriculture in the Nile valley. This also fits with Gehenna placing the Second City in present-day Egypt: the Antediluvian god-kings needed food produced in outlying villages to support their city's mortal population. The Second City's fall 1,000 years occurs around the same time as the invention of the wheel and spread of symbols as proto-writing. Mortal survivors flee across the Middle East and bring remnant's of the city's (then for the time) advanced technology with them.

                                                  In terms of age, that puts the (true) Antediluvians at 12,000 years old, ur-Shulgi and his Second City contemporaries (Yorak, the Dracon, Kartarirya) at 8,000-7,000 years old. It makes Japheth "only" seven thousand instead of nine thousand years old, but tough luck there, J.

                                                  Conceptually, I see the era before the Great Flood as a time of unrestrained myth and wonder, when many of the rules that govern the contemporary World of Darkness aren't yet in place. There is no Gauntlet, no such thing as Paradox, the Shroud is all but nonexistent, Kindred clans effectively don't exist (each Antediluvian being a unique Kindred without childer), etc. The supernatural doesn't simply exist in harmony with the natural world, it governs its laws. By the time of the Second City, the Gauntlet and Shroud are up, Paradox is a minor problem for mages, and the Kindred are divided into clans. The supernatural still exists openly, but is no longer a seamless part of the world.

                                                  It's also worth noting that between the Second City's fall in 5000 BCE and the appearance of writing in 3500 BCE, we have 2500 years of prehistory. That's a long, long time. The Antediluvians would've been as ancient during the fall of the Second City (5,000 years) as Baba Yaga was during the modern era. By the time writing is a thing, they have her beat at 7,500. I figure those 2500 between-years were a savage, barbaric age where the Third Generation freely sired, warred with, and diablerized one another and their progeny. A nightmarish era of non-stop Gehenna where the Fourth and Fifth Generations were used as cannon fodder by their infinitesimally older progenitors. There may well have been a number of now-extinct clans active during that period which didn't survive into the modern era; Gehenna's third scenario names a lot more than 13 Antediluvians as residents of the Second City. Some of the Third Generation may never have sired, but just as many (and the abortive clans they founded) may well have been wiped out during the Jyhad's brutal first battles.

                                                  Also, I love Black Fox's explanation for the Great Flood and geological evidence.
