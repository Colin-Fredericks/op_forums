    I'm not saying that the ritual doesn't have an intense drawback, wiping out a population center should. We aren't dealing with a human being any longer but rather a low generation elder (since its a Level 9 Ritual that requires a Level 9 Discipline before you could purchase it). 2 miles an hour is significantly slower than the Lasombra could move according to DAV20
    Characters may move at up to half maximum running speed, then attack or perform another action.
    and running speed is described as
    If all-out running, a character moves at (20 + [3 x Dexterity]) yards/meters per turn.
    . So lets say our angry Lasombra elder blood buffs his Dex to 10 for the scene "painting the line of pitch" each turn he could move 25 yards per turn (half of 20 + 30 [3 x 10 Dex]) and still take an action (paint the line). It is 1760 yards in a mile so it would take 71 turns to travel a mile and the metric of time granted for a turn is
    Normally, a turn takes whatever time it needs to take. In a combat scene, turns are about three seconds.
    so 213 seconds. Circling Bolingbroke would take 3774.36 seconds, or roughly 63 minutes. There is no description of how thick this line of pitch needs to be it would be possible to "paint" a much thinner line (why not a tenth inch wide) which would require significantly less pitch and a pissed elder with Potence as an in clan Discipline and shadow tentacles could carry a lot of pitch easily. I'm not saying it isn't hard, but its easier than that makes it seem when you consider its an elder not some neonate performing the ritual.

        I like to think that a potential student doesn't need to go through the Amer Noctis to learn Abyss Mysticism. Indeed, they might not need a teacher at all. After all, you learn about the Abyss by studying it. If you stare into the Abyss long enough, you're bound to learn something.

        Obviously, learning the magic on your own is significantly harder and takes much longer than if you had a teacher. But the way I picture it (and I have no book evidence for this; it's just my imagination) is that Abyss Mysticism - even instructed one - involves a great deal of solitary study and contemplation. Of the initiate into its mysteries sitting alone in an unlit room, contemplating the dark. Or, more importantly, creating a little ball of Abyss stuff using Obtenebration, and gazing into it like a pitch black, amorphous crystal ball. Looking into the darkness, and listening to the chittering of alien shadow beings.

        And I think "create a ball of pure darkness" should be well within a Lasombra's ability, as early as when they learn how to create Abyssal Arms. I'm of the opinion that Obtenebration users should just be able to shape their "arms" into whatever form they fancy, so long as it doesn't change its offensive capabilities. In practical terms, there's nothing overpowered about a Lasombra's ability to summon a stool out of darkness to sit on. Especially since it costs blood to do so. Considering all that, making a ball in the vampire's hands out of dark would, logically, only be useful to someone trying to study the Abyss. And I suspect that many Lasombra explicitly tell their childer not to do this for that very reason.

        Even a vampire who has a teacher for Abyss Mysticism must look into the Abyss. The secrets of the magic cannot be conveyed in mere words. Lessons given by a more experienced user only serve to provide hints or interpretations, or procedures for specific Rituals. Actually understanding Abyss Mysticism itself requires an intimate, personal understanding of the Abyss itself. There can be no substitution. To paraphrase Morpheus, no one can be told what the Abyss is. You have to see it for yourself.

        Which is why I think, while most Abyss Mystics were taught by older practitioners, there's a sizable minority that learned it through no source but their own experiments and contemplation. Secrets stolen from the Dark individually, and Rituals derived from ruminations on that realm's alien, primordial nature.

        Of course, many of those self-initiated mystics end up dying or going irretrievably mad, because they had no teachers to guide them away from lethal errors. But such is the risk when tangling with the Abyss.

        Magestuck: The Ascension
