    A couple of points:

    1. In the World of Darkness, it is very likely the term "Anarch" among vampires isn't a reference to anarchism as a political philosophy. The Anarchist political philosophy was a product of the 18th century but vampire Anarchs referred to as Anarchs have existed since the 12th century (600 years earlier). Vampire Anarchs is thus likely to be a reference to "The Anarchy" of England in all likelihood (1135 to 1153) during the Civil War of King Stephen and Empress Matilda.

    The Anarchy - Wikipedia
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Anarchy


    The term "Anarch" is thus a pejorative of vampires referring to the fact that individual vampires were revolting against the natural order of their Elders and a breakdown of formal vampire society. It is something that said revoltees would take as a badge of honor symbolizing their defiance against the Law of Caine.

    2. Anarchs don't have to be a traditional nation state to win against the Camarilla. They also don't have to have much in the way of organization or even shared ideals to work together. The relatively small numbers of vampires and their secrecy means that really they just need to share a limited amount of resources across global lines (which is a lot easier in the Modern Era) to work against the Camarilla.

    As we've seen with Marcus Vitel, Emperor of Washington D.C., you might even have an incredibly despotic Lasombra Methuselah as a "Anarch" as long as he's paying for the revolution elsewhere. Certainly, the Soviet Union sponsored a lot of revolutions elsewhere and Anarchs Unbound retconned the Brujah Council to be financing Anarchs far and abroad.

        You have to find another vampire in order to feud with them. I think an excellent example of this is actually a social group we’re all quite familiar with given the forum; tabletop role players.

        I know dozens of them in my area (again a midwestern city with about the population to support one vampire af the idealized level); which is only a fraction of the total; and if not for the couple of FLGS and a Meetup group, I’d never have been aware of even them.

        If I started my own group from newbies (i.e. sired some fledglings) and played only with them, the larger gamer community wouldn’t even know they existed.

        Now, gamers are more insular than vampires who need to procure a stable blood supply without arousing suspicion, but the ratio is also much bigger too... on the order of 1:1000 or more and we’d still completely miss each other if we weren’t actively seeking each other out.

        Similarly, even in this city of 100k, there are easily a dozen plus night clubs and bars that singles hit to hook up almost nightly.* A vampire can only be in one place at a time so that’s a dozen or more small but separate hunting grounds just in a population of 100k people using only the most stereotypical method. If you had just two vampires in the entire community (twice the “safe” ratio) there’s zero pressure to fight... you see the other vampire already at that nightclub and go to one of the other eleven. The first vampire would literally need to be wanting to pick a fight for their to be a conflict.

        And even if they DO want to pick a fight, predators rarely fight to the death unless there is a desperate shortage of resources, it’s just not a good survival strategy; you don’t provoke a fight with a fellow predator unless you have to. Instead one proves their superiority and the other avoids them and goes elsewhere if they turn up... and again, there’s no shortage of hunting grounds at even 2:100k.

        Basically, you need a 12:100k ratio just to get 1 vampire per prime feeding spot and a 13:100k ratio before someone’s really going to want to compete for a prime spot. And that’s just the prime spots... throw in the men’s and women’s homeless shelters, the Red Cross center, hospitals, outside of 24/7 shopping locations, etc. and you could easily double that... and this in a small city where just about everything shuts down at 9pm vs. the all night bustle of a larger city.

        Which is why I say you really need ratios in the 1:10k or higher range before enough of the easy hunting grounds are taken for there to be a reason to start problems over access to them. That or a bunch of really careless vampires who overfeed and start leaving bodies on a regular basis... which is also unlikely to happen if there’s enough food to go around since it’s mostly the hungry vampires who lose control and have a drained body on their hands.**

        Sure, the Camarilla/Mean Girls’ Society doesn’t need an excuse to plot against each other and would do so regardless the ratio, but that’s not the same thing as having an Anarch movement. For that you need enough have “nots” to make the dictates of the “haves” feel like an unreasonable burden instead of something you can just ignore.

        That’s also where you’d expect the “haves” to start getting exclusive rather than inclusive and start persecuting various outgroups to protect what they see as theirs. Until there’s actually the potential for a shortage, there’s little need to rigorously enforce access to the resource and probably not enough manpower to actually police access to something as fluid as human activity.

        Going back to the two vampires and a dozen nightclubs; the dominant vampire would need at least 11 assistants informed of who specifically to look for to cover the other locations before they could reasonably stop the lesser vampire from getting a meal in one of the others... and more to keep the lesser out of the hospitals, the homeless shelters, the jail, the parks, the truck stops, etc. The logistics just aren’t there for policing access to such a massive population relative to a lone vampire.

        1:100k is the ticket to a quiet life of easy hunting for everyone involved; but this is the World of Darkness so the idea that undead existence should be that idyllic should be thrown out on its ear in the name of having a setting with actual dramatic tension and conflicts.

        My current campaign is using 1:10k and even it feels a bit on the idyllic side; there’s just too many places to hunt even in clusters of 10k humans for there to be much to fight over. The Anarchs are less Anarchs as they are the suburban bar vampires; ones who’s available hunting grounds are basically eating at McDonald’s compared to the elite’s 4-5 star dining options. You could gripe about the quality, but that’s not usually enough to get into fights to the death over and not enough for the Prince to feel much pressure to persecute anyone for stepping outside their lanes. You’d need a serious spark to set off any sort of powder keg of conflict there (my PCs are thankfully cooperating there as one of them is looking to overthrow the Prince as revenge for a slight the Prince may not have even been involved in... the sheer number of contenders if they were to succeed would probably lead to the city exploding into something like last summer’s riots as various players activate their assets against each other... and even that would only peripherally involve actual Anarchs; mostly as foot soldiers made promises by whichever factions they throw in with).

        * one thing that made the population issue readily apparent was when my group moved to Discord due to Covid and someone started putting together a Google Earth project marking key territories and sites for the city... and we started seeing all the local bars and nightclubs and other prime hunting grounds dotting the city. It really makes the 1:100k number feel like it was pulled out of the ether by a bunch of people who’d never lived in a big city (even a sleepy midwestern one like mine) and explored it’s nightlife and got all their ideas from reading about city life in the 90’s from books printed in the 80’s.

        ** and since guilt over draining someone is one of the bigger reasons for an impulsive unsanctioned Embrace, it’s a problem that will actually escalate the overpopulation crisis.
        I actually think those numbers don't work at all but I'm someone who has put an inordinant amount of thought into my silly vampire game as well as the idea that vampires are people who have a lot of preferences. Feel free to disagree and I welcome your insights into this matter but this is what I have thought about vampire competition for resources.

        1. Assuming 1 Blood Point equals a pint of blood, drinking from the same person every night will kill them. So you must drink from multiple vessels without them ever being drank from regularly.

        2. 1:10000 sounds like its perfectly idyllic except for the fact that vampires are not going to be feeding evenly.

        3. Vampires are going to want to avoid feeding from the sick and dying or geriatric unless they are on a Path and able to stomach regularly killing their victims (which is why the Giovanni never have a problem feeding in my games).

        4. Vampires as a general rule do not feed off of children, which eliminates another huge chunk of the populace.

        5. Vampires generally prefer to feed from easy targets which means that clubs, bars, homeless, and prostitutes are an obvious group.

        6. Vampires generally prefer to feed on the healthy within the 20s to 40s range.

        7. Vampires by nature are social animals and do want to associate with their own kind because they require it as a sense of normalcy.

        8. Vampires who use their Disciplines in any shape or form is going to require far more than to feed on 30 separate people per month. Which is only if they are
        doing their best not to "double dip" on the same vessels and do permanent lasting harm.

        9. Barring the use of Mental Disciplines, actually getting these 30 separate people into positions to be fed on is a lot harder than it sounds.

        10. Vampires prefer to feed on the physically attractive too because it's that sort of game.

        11. Vampires do not only compete for blood within their hunting domains. They also compete for resources such as wealth, status, social position, and power.

        12. There are other supernaturals to worry about in a World of Darkness if you make a large enough stink such as werewolves and hunters.

        13. Most vampires congregate in Big Cities since rarely does a vampire want to be the only vampire in Nowheresville, IL even if it gives him access to, say

                I have a long post, so to start with, I'll just say that my approach is largely a 1st edition, early 2nd edition, V20 combination. That is the short answer, the long answer follows regarding the politics of the Anarch movement.

                First let's talk about how vampires have wealth:

                Imagine a hungry man is walking across the country side. He sees a bunch of sheep grazing on the grass. They don't even run away, he can walk right up to them and kill them without any struggle. He sees edible plants growing in the field, arranged in neat little rows so he can just go and eat to his heart's content. It seems like this man, with little to no work, can have all of the food he wants. But, the truth is that he can't because all of this food belongs to other people who have spent years cultivating their land and building up their flocks. If he tries to take their stuff, he's in for some trouble.

                So the world of the rich and powerful must seem to a new vampire with the ability to blood bind people and a few points of Dominate and/or Presence. The world seems to be your oyster - go to the mayor and make him your servant, make the local billionaire businessman your servant too. Make the chief of police your ghoul so that you can get away with crimes. As with the hungry man above, just walk through the garden and eat to your heart's content.

                BUT - other vampires exist too and they have already plucked or claimed the low hanging fruit. The elders are invested in these humans with wealth and power, they are invested in the businesses and institutions that get things done in a city. You can't very well go along and claim them as your own any more than you can walk into a grocery store and start eating food off the shelves.

                Vampires are social and economic parasites as much as they are literal blood drinkers. The humans can do the day to day chores of running businesses and they can learn all about economic models and marketing or how to day trade - but which ever mortals end up making money are going to be under the direct or indirect sway of some elder vampire somewhere. Becoming a rich vampire has less to do with being a businessman or even a domitor yourself and more to do with playing the political game against other vampires to get your own piece of a pie that has been baked and sliced long before you started playing the game.

                So this is the world that a young vampire finds itself in. A world of seemingly unlimited riches, but its all tightly controlled and to get a cut you must serve creatures that could potentially live forever. Of course, most older vampires are going to let neonates, especially their own childer, get a big enough piece of the pie to stay loyal and contented, a reward for good service.

                That opens up an extremely important question: why does a vampire embrace a childe? To be a companion, a slave, a servant, a lover, a warrior, a research assistant, a bodyguard, an heir, a extra vote in the political arena, for fun? All of the above. Embraces are tightly controlled in the Camarilla and usually the vampire doing so had to the earn that right, do a favor for the prince, or maybe just has a fortunate opportunity. In any case, most vampires of moderate or greater age are going to want something from their childer and wont take kindly to them running off and saying that they don't like the man. So childer are going to selected for obedience and/or alliance, so if that childe opposes the sire, they will be punished severely or perhaps killed. A childe opposing the sire is a disaster, because it was a big investment that didn't pay off and that wise sires are going to try to prevent. So then where do Anarchs come from?

                The simplest explanation is the the sire is an Anarch and so the childe is an Anarch too. Ironically the childe becomes an Anarch in obedience to the sire. In the scenario, the Anarchs are acting like a political more than a movement of the youth against the old. We have Anarch elders served by Anarch ancilla, served by Anarch neonates - opposed to Camarilla establishment elders, establishment ancilla, and establishment neonates. The Anarch faction may have rhetoric about rights and freedoms for the young, but the political struggle is more about which faction gets the bigger pieces of the above pie - both factions have elders at the top and neonates at the bottom.

                Another source of Anarchs might be vampires that aren't that young, but who are ancilla. Brand new vampires are probably too much in awe of their new powers and too terrified of mysterious elders to want to rock the boat. Give the vampires a bit of time though and having mind controlled human slaves or super strength starts to get boring, they can see the pie in its totality and realize that they want a bigger piece of the pie. Vampires of this age have more independence from their sires and can't just be killed like an unreleased childe. They also have enough power to not unlive in terror of elders or Camarilla authorities. In many of these cases, maybe the vampire's sire or other authorities have died, gone into torpor, left the city, or otherwise are no longer in the picture. Once a vampire of intermediate age gets actual independence, then they will realize that they may be independent but don't have much individual power. So they team up with other young to moderate aged independent vampires to fight for their own power against more established elders.

                This time is a vampire's life, when they start to actually gain some measure of independence and power, is probably regarded as dangerous to the elders. Many vampires of this age probably don't become Anarchs because their drive for power is probably redirected, not at the elders but somewhere else. Maybe such an ancilla identifies with their Clan and gains power within the framework of their Clan and at the expense of rival Clans. As many socialists might say, the rich elites divide the working class based on ethnicity so that they fight each other. Clan divisions are kind of like that, though whether or not vampires should unite based on age or based on Clan is a bit subjective. Alternatively, the ancilla might ally with elders and become valuable to them, increasing power by being useful to other vampires of power. Alternatively, the vampire might strike off to a new place where there are more opportunities, so could become an elder, or perhaps an Anarch, in some distant city.

                In very tightly controlled cities, there would likely be no Anarchs or the Anarchs there would just be one faction ruled by its own elders, as I mentioned above. In such a city, embraces are tightly controlled and young vampires are made to realize that disobedience could cute short their immortality. In more chaotic regions, embraces might happen far more frequently and childer may become independent far sooner, maybe even immediately. In such a city, a true Anarch movement can quickly grow to power, though likely still under the control of older vampires. This kind of situation is probably what made California possible.

                I see the Anarch movement as something that happens within the Camarilla, and very seldom an independent political. In many cases Anarchs are labeled such not by themselves but by the elders who derisively label young vampires as Anarchs when they organize to advocate for more rights or powers for young vampires. Like in 1st edition V:tM, I see the Elder vs Anarch conflict as the central political conflict in the setting, not Camarilla vs Sabbat, vampires vs werewolves, or anything like that. Well, Clan versus Clan might sometimes compete with that, or humanity versus beast. A high percentage of cities are going to have Anarchs or something like them, though very often its less a movement that actually advances the rights of young vampires, and more an opposition party that just empowers a different set of elders. These Anarch elders might be worse that the establishment ones in many ways, since they are demagogues who gain power by telling younger vampires that they deserve just as much power as vampires who have spent centuries gaining wisdom and earning power, and they wont have that power anyway because they will just be serving a different set of elders.

                Anarchs very seldom drive the Camarilla from a city, usually they don't even want that. They want varying degrees of greater rights and powers for young vampires or merely their own faction, and very often they succeed at this by getting the prince to change certain policies or even having a prince who is affiliated with the Anarchs - in which case we might ironically have a dissident faction within the city that seeks more traditional elder lead politics. In either case, it is still a Camarilla city and the Anarchs succeed within the context of Camarilla law rather than completely rejecting it: which would seriously increase the dangers since they have then declared war on the Inner Circle and Justicars and will probably be crushed.

                Depending on the local prince and dominant elders, even those Anarch elders might actually represent an increase in freedom, resources, or some other desirable policy.

                Brujah elders tend to attract the support of ancilla (and maybe neonates) by promising them a bigger piece of the pie at the expense of the elders. The Ventrue elders tend to attract the support of ancilla (and maybe neonates) by telling them the benefits of cooperating with the elders and the virtues of patience. Other Clans fall somewhere along that spectrum, or go in a perpendicular direction of advocating Clan loyalty above age loyalty - like Tremere or Nosferatu.

                Well, that is enough rambling for now.

Bite down during hookup sex and the target has an orgasmic experience and leaves a bit light-headed, none the wiser and probably more than willing to swing by again if you called them up.

                                Honestly, an easier way to calculate a vampire population than some ratio is just count the number of night clubs and bars in the city and double that to account for alternate feeding methods and venues large enough to support multiple vampires.

                                The only way 1:100k makes sense is if vampires are regularly killing their victims a dozen or more times a year (the current annual death rate in the US is 897 per 100,000... so an extra dozen would be well within the range of sampling errors), but unless you’re playing V5 where the only way to fully satiate your hunger is murder that’s just one of “loser” methods of hunting (because it leaves evidence behind) vs. slurping down a couple pints every other night or so with no one the wiser.

                                Which is the real irony for me since V5 heavily pushes for lower ratios like the 1:10k or 1:3k I mentioned in its core book when the need to actually murder to fully satiate makes 1:100k more sensible... while V1-V20 uses 1:100k even though vampires could be fully satiated without ever leaving a body in those editions.

                                And just to be clear, my population numbers and their plausibility are based off V20 rules since I don’t play V5.

                                    A couple of points:

Vampire Anarchs is thus likely to be a reference to "The Anarchy" of England in all likelihood (1135 to 1153) during the Civil War of King Stephen and Empress Matilda.

                                    The Anarchy - Wikipedia
                                    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Anarchy


                                    The term "Anarch" is thus a pejorative of vampires referring to the fact that individual vampires were revolting against the natural order of their Elders and a breakdown of formal vampire society. It is something that said revoltees would take as a badge of honor symbolizing their defiance against the Law of Caine.

                                    2. Anarchs don't have to be a traditional nation state to win against the Camarilla. They also don't have to have much in the way of organization or even shared ideals to work together. The relatively small numbers of vampires and their secrecy means that really they just need to share a limited amount of resources across global lines (which is a lot easier in the Modern Era) to work against the Camarilla.

                                    1- OoC I'm pretty sure the authors meant the Anarchist political philosophy when they created the Anarchs. That isn't a fact in the setting, but helps to contextualize it.

                                                Yeah, modern Anarchs have all the ups and downs of a decentralized political movement.

                                                            The Anarch philosophy is simple. Young vampires shouldn't be controlled/enslaved by older vampires.

                                                            Anything more than that, and Anarchs are going to disagree with each other. Of course you do need more than that to organize any kind of society, so Anarchs are going to quarrel over what kind of new society should replace the current one. And many vampires who initially are anarchs when they are wrong are going to think differently once they've become older and more powerful.

                                                            Human political movements and philosophy have very little application to that struggle. The vast differential in powers between elder and younger vampires aren't present in human society. And that fact that everyone dies and opens up positions of leadership for the next generation has no counterpart among immortal vampires. Though of course certain ideas can always translate.

                                                            Incidentally, the original Greek word "anarchos" simply means "without an archon". Archons (meaning "ruler") were the (sometimes elected) magistrates of Athens and other Greek city states. It simply means without rulers. One of its uses was to describe those years when no magistrate was elected to rule Athens. I wonder if the original writers knew that and selected the term archon as a helper to the Justicars for a reason.
