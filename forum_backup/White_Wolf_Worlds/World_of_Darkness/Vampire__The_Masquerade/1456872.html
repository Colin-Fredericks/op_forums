    unlearning accepted rules that I learned incorrectly

    08-15-2021, 10:56 AM
    It's kind of ironic. When I first started playing VTM I had read through the book and found the group I was playing with had been ignoring rules like guns do only bashing damage to vampires unless you target the head. My first character was a shootist at a traveling carnival, and I treated the rule as written. While the storyteller instead insisted that all guns did lethal damage even to vampire. Over the years I helped correct many of the things (good rules lawyers style?) in an effort to avoid the hours of arguing between the storytellers and others. Personally I was always welling to go with the what a storyteller would call during a scene, and bring up after the fact what the book would say. Now I find myself in doing the story telling having to re-exam some of the things that I "learned" and understand that perhaps I need to reevaluate some of the rules that I had accepted as doctrine.

    For example: last night a player decided to use the flanking maneuver on their own. They failed their ambush attempt, but because they were in level 2 obtenebration they mortals the character was facing was aware they were being attacked, but still blinded and unsure where to face to avoid getting flanked. I think since my ideal of flanking most likely came from D&D I just assumed you could only do it if multiple attackers were involved, but in this case I allowed it because of the power being used. A conversation happened outside the game about what had happened, and about being able to attempt the maneuver outside of the enemy being blinded. Such as just winning initiative, going first during your action to move behind the enemy and making your attack without the enemy being able to act yet. Even if the enemy does a defensive action it still happens after the PC rolls to attack with flanking bonus.

    Another rule that i encountered was about movement.

    (from V20) Movement: Characters may choose to walk, jog, or run. If walking, a character moves at seven yards/ meters per turn. If jogging, a character moves at (12 + Dexterity) yards/meters per turn. If all-out running, a character moves at (20 + [3 x Dexterity]) yards/meters per turn. Characters may move at up to half maximum running speed, then subsequently attack or perform another action; see p. 274 for particulars. Characters may also wish to move while taking another action. This is possible, but each yard or meter moved subtracts one from the other action’s dice pool. Note that injured characters (p. 282) cannot move at maximum speed.

    When I had read that part about moving, then acting up to half speed I think I subconsciouslyalways added the word "an" to the statement. Like you can move and perform an action, meaning one and not multiple actions. I tended to think that since the book went through the effort to list that walking was 7 meters that that was the only amount of movement you could make and preform multiple actions. Being forced to reread the rules after being confronted by a players about my thinking helped me to realize my error.

    This can all be a very humbling experience which was harder for me to process that I thought it would be.

    Where there any situations that others found themselves in that they corrected without just making a houserule?

      Let's see. I've also been "that guy" when it comes to mechanics, and I'm not sure why; White Wolf systems have always been kind of shoddily written, and the intent is to promote flexibility; these are more suggestions than actual hard rules in many cases. Something that always trips me up is the system for how and when vampires enter torpor due to damage. The rules seem to be able to be read two ways, and different groups handle it differently. Under Incapacitated, it states "further damage suffered by an incapacitated vampire sends her into torpor, or, if the damage is aggravated, inflicts Final Death on her". Some groups say "well see, if you take one damage beyond Crippled, you're Incapacitated, and if you take two beyond you're in Torpor". Others say "no no, if you take 50 damage beyond Crippled you're Incapacitated. And THEN, once Incapacitated, if you take any more damage, then you're in Torpor". One of these is more permissive, and I tend to use it, but naturally, I get told I'm wrong a lot.

      Related to this is Wound Penalties. Under Health it tells you that the penalty you get for Wounds is applied to all die pools except reflexive actions (such as soak). But under Initiative, it points out that Wound Penalties reduce Initiative as well. I tend to not reroll Initiative in combats each turn, and so when someone says "hey, that guy took damage, he should be going slower now" I tend to go "what? Don't be absurd, it doesn't say that under Health at all". Cue much searching of the books. Also, in a completely different Health section (on page 121), it helpfully points out that while Strength based damage die pools are reduced by Wounds, Firearms are not.

      Or here's one that happened a few nights ago. I couldn't find the rules for readying weapons (the player wanted to craft a custom weapons harness, and he rolled 5 successes, so I decided to give him a bonus). I recalled something about a die roll to fast draw a weapon from Revised, but I couldn't find it in V20. The Index said it was on page 260. It is not, it is on page 258. Another one I tend to forget is ranged weapons being diff 4 at point-blank range, but diff 8 at maximum range. That last one can be brutal combined with attacking from the rear during an ambush.

      Another often forgotten weapon rule is Weapon Length, where it states that when dealing with someone using a longer melee weapon, you need to close in one yard/meter before striking, losing a die from their attack roll.

      Also, the rules for moving while performing an action (something I also always forget about), "Characters may also wish to move WHILE taking another action. Each yard or meter moved subtracts one die from the other action's dice pool".

      And finally, Healing in combat. In Revised, to heal in combat, you had to make a reflexive Stamina + Survival diff 7 to heal; failure meant the blood was wasted, and a botch meant you took an additional level of lethal damage. In V20, it states "characters must be resting and relatively inactive for this healing to take place". Yikes.

          Rules in games tend to lean towards either "simulation" or "extrapolation". The more you simulate how things work the more accurate it becomes but it becomes mind numbingly dense and boring. The more you extrapolate the faster and easier the game play is but it easily can become quite nonsensical because the rules are too far detached from how things work. WoD games as a rule are written and played almost exclusively in the extrapolation side of the spectrum.

          But then there is another issue, namely how an ST is supposed to handle a non-mechanical answer to a problem that most people only think about in mechanical terms. An example of this comes from of V:tM own books. Specifically a group of combat build Sabbat rolled into town and started having fun. Rather than take group head on one of the Camarilla vampires found out the sabbat group was using a vacant lot to earth meld during the day. So the vampire purchased the empty lot and had it paved over in a single day, turning it into a parking lot. The sabbat vampires are now trapped until the parking lot is destroyed. This example with the parking lot vampires shows that you can use Background dots to win a fight against combat mechanics.

          There are tons of rules written about how to engage in combat, most of which have glaring errors or loopholes. But very little is written about how to fight in other ways. This is where the ST has to decide if the combat rules even come into play, rather than worrying about if they are interpreting the rules correctly. So don't be too hard on yourself about RAW adherence because chances are those rules could be bypassed legitimately by using a completely unrelated set of rules & mechanics.

          Beyond that, as long as everyone has fun, there really isn't a problem.
