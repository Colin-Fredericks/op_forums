                      Basic rule of improv: "yes, and", not "no". If the player wants to play a plant mage, who will it hurt? Even if it unbalances the game, who will it hurt? Even if you have trouble finding opponents, who will it hurt?

                      It can be scary STing with powers you aren't familiar with, but it's really no big deal. Just tell your player you are a little scared of where the power might lead. Sit down with them and do a quick read-through (aloud!) of the power's text. Make sure you're both on the same page. As long as the two of you agree on what it can do, and no other player has an issue with it, you're good to go.

                      Oh, and when in doubt about a story element, always err on the side of making it more awesome, not less.

                                              Like so many Thaumaturgy paths, the power comes from the personal knowledge of the character & player rather than explicitly from the mechanics. The ability to produce trees by itself isn't a direct combat ability by itself, but if you are setting up an area for combat to happen later, it can become incredibly powerful.

                                              If you have a group of mortals or ghouls coming after you in the foreseeable future then creating a grove of Manchineel trees in a contained area can be rather deadly, especially if you can then use the path powers to animate the limbs or whole trees. Taking a page from the batman baddy Poison Ivy only takes a little googling to pull off with the Green Path.

                                              On the other hand you can make the argument that a few toxic trees won't do much to kindred. In direct combat this is correct, unless the enemy took a flaw about garlic, wolfsbane or wild roses. Additionally if your opponent kindred has their hand in the drug trade, what happens to their business model if you use your powers to make huge crops of drug plants spring up ready to harvested practically overnight? Imagine what happens to a Ventrue when they just put in an order for millions of dollars worth of drugs, only to have some one crash the local market by selling for a quarter of the price or just giving it away for free.

                                              Need to deal with local lupines? Then start growing toxic plants near their kinfolk. Depending on the plant it can contaminate the soil for years or decades with toxins. I mean garlic, onions and chocolate are often toxic to dogs, so they wouldn't automatically know to avoid them any more than the human kinfolk would know to avoid oleander. Yes this approach can take years or decades, but so what, as a vampire you have eternity.

                                              The Green Path, the Path of Conjuration, and the Path of Technomancy basically are the scariest paths when mixed with real world knowledge. I have seen STs with a decade plus experience under their belt start to really feel the pressure when their players were IRL botanists, hackers, or engineers.
