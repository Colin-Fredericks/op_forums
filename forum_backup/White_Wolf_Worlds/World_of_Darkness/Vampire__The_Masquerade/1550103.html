          One way to use Vicissitude is as a way to justify your vampire being able to acquire Physical Merits that they did not initially possess. That opens up a lot os possibilities without having to stray too far from pre-existing crunch.

          Overall, Vicissitude is one of those tricky Disciplines where a lot of its potential is going to depend on what your Storyteller is willing to permit. The options listed in the actual rules are not nearly as broad as what it is capable of in the lore.

          One particular limit to consider is that you cannot add new biomass without an Elder-level power, Graft Life to Life. There might be a bit of wiggle room there, but you're mostly stuck working with the meat and bone that's already present.

          So, to use the examples you provided, crafting wings would require you to "hollow out" other parts of your body for the necessary flesh and bone, which may leave the recipient more frail than they had been previously.

          A "bone gun" would be quite limited because your Tzimisce can't infinitely respawn their bones (assuming you're talking about shooting bones). They might regenerate during your daysleep due to vampiric stasis, but you would still be pretty limited in ammo, and I'd probably require it to cost extra Vitae like healing if I was the Storyteller.

              As a rule of thumb, it's good to stick to preexisting powers and cosmetics when it comes to self alterations, although Horrid Form isn't a bad combat transformation.

              Most of what you'll get out of Vicissitude in most tables is about changing others, not yourself. You can play around with your ghouls, craft objects out of human tissue and so on.

              Turning your arm into a blade is certainly within reach, but you can also directly attack with Vicissitude, impaling the target with their own bones, and that's maybe better than a sword.

                  Vicissitude is one of my favourite disciplines because of how open it is to player creativity, and I tend to enjoy exploring bodily augmentation and transhumanism within games. However, it's chief weakness is also that it is so broad and poorly defined, what your character will be capable of will vary wildly between GMs. The easy limits to remember is that it covers the alteration of the human body, flesh, sinew, fat. bone and all that good stuff. You are still limited by the subjects available tissues without learning elder powers, I usually rule the blood expenditure is a mixture of meysphor8cslly wetting the clay and transforming existing flesh or blood between states so bigger projects will either requite external sources for grafts or large amounts of blood. I'd also expect players to pay for attributes or advantages gained, perhaps with a discount. There was a good third party system for it on the Storytellers vault. Another rhing to condider is think of how things would mechanically have to work, mouth hands might need whole new arrangements of flesh and bone to incorporate connections to your gullet and muscles to make them work. Transformed ghouls and people still need their organs to live, still need feeding etc.
                  finally, a good rule of thumb another poster came up with here some time ago was the "duct tape" rule: can you accomplish the task by taping a piece of mundane equipment to yourself? And thus run these augmentations as if they were freaky mundane equipment, within reason (no vampire fangs don't magically continue to do agg when you move or disconnect them, a common player belief in my circles.).

                      The problem with using Vicissitude in combat is that it takes a combat system designed to be abstract and tries to make it concrete.
                      Also what Kale said is very true, it is up to a GM how they treat Vicissitude

It become disfigured and easly ends the fight when the opponent's face become a Picasso painting from his later years after a bottle of wine and some mind altering substance

                      (no vampire fangs don't magically continue to do agg when you move or disconnect them, a common player belief in my circles.).
                      I think you are right
                      Vicissitude does not inherently have any mystical properties, and the bones of vampires do not inherently have any such properties, so any weapons made with it cause only lethal damage, without something like Thaumaturgy or Koldunic Sorcery to supplement it.
                      You can use Vicissitude to create weapons out of your own bones and such but don’t expect them to be any good. A spike made out of bone is… well durable like a bone.

                      However, you CAN make bone denser, more durable and a Szalchta with serrated arm blades tough as steel is very dangerous, but still not aggravated. It is more like the shock vallue and of course the terrible wound; gutted by such thing even would make a Vampire's unlife very uncomfortable

                      The fangs and claws are a different metter on the other hand. A vampire's bite is agravated like protean claws.
                      What will happen if you tear them out with Vicissitude or, well, simply by force?
                      I would say unless you somehow preserve them they
                      A, dust away or age quickly likie a Cainite's body would do uppon Final Death
                      B, quickly return to normal; both the Claws and Fangs require effort to "summon", unless you have a flaw your fangs are always out (just don't bite your tounge... agaravated, ouch) your normal bite is quite a blount one. But the editions and systems never really were clear in this matter.
                      Vampires can cause aggravated damage naturally only if they use their teeth...somehow. I'm not sure there is really a satisfactory description for how that really works but if you bite down using normal teeth, that's blunt damage (often not even causing damage), if you use the vampiric fangs as an attack, it's aggravated.
                      So I tend to treat this as the Garou transforming back to its breed from, like their bodyparts if something tear it off; a big bad Crinos arm become a human arm or a wolf paw (unless it was a metis) like how you will not have a Crinois trophy-head any time soon (again unless it was a Metis). A dead Garou will be a dead human body or a dead wolf body.
                      Following this, unless you soemhow with a ritual preserve it, you will have a normal tooth or nails if you tear them out from a vampire. At least untill they dust/rot away. There are of course Vampire artefact like the Femur of Toompler, but they are exeptions not rules. Similar with a Gangrel's fangs in wolf-form, yank them out they will return to normal
                      At least in my interpretation... other GM? Who know?

                      Of course you can make weapons out of your body but that needs a combination discipline, Body Armory. In V20 you can find it in Lore of the Clans page 241.

These weapons do terrible damage to their targets. Many Tzimisce make sword-arms, knife-bladed knuckles, and the like, but more dramatic implementations are possible.

                      System: This power costs two blood points per weapon (though larger weapons like two-handed swords and great axes cost four), and the player must roll Dexterity + Medicine (difficulty 7). Each weapon takes two turns to form (larger weapons take three turns). Weapons created in this manner cause aggravated damage."

                          I don't thing vampire fangs would revert to normal upon death. Garou are shapeshifters, that's a whole another thing, they're about metaphysics and spirituality. Vampires are about physicality and stasis. Also, WtA is strongly about the idea of being cut-off from humanity in a way that humans won't ever bear witness to your fights, for good or ill. VtM is strongly about going out of your way to clean the evidence of your existence.

                          And the game does say vampire fangs cause Agg because supernatural. A removed fang simply ceases to be supernatural, even if it doesn't crumble. For me, it would retain the form and size it acquired upon the Embrace, clearly inhuman. And it should remain mostly intact.

                          Teeth are the body part that's most resilient to the passage of time, and the crumbling into dust in this game is about the body undergoing decay until it reaches its true age. Young vampires shouldn't become actual dust, vestiges of bones should be in most "dusted" vampires, and teeth remaining should be a common thing, specially if removed with Vicissitude instead of pulled by force alone or collected from a killed vampire.

                          Clean after yourself. Don't count on "dusting" being an automatic elimination of evidence.

                                  Precisely because there's no mentions of those details in the rules, it's a matter of preference.

                                  I don't personally think that reverting the canines upon death combines with the themes and tropes of the game. Same way as I think only the canines are mystic weapons, but if suddenly comes a situation where you think every teeth being Agg dealers makes for a better story, go for it.

                                  But as far as corpses go: leaving corpses behind is generally a bad idea, specially if they're vampire corpses. Even the "fresh" corpses of shovelheads or other fledglings will be decayed by hours or days more than they should, possibly showing marks of death causes that shouldn't exist, and while WoD's police isn't very effective, taking chances doesn't make for a long life. Cleaning up the bodies should be a regular, expected work when violence escalates enough for vampires to suffer Final Death. In such a circumstance, cleaning teeth is more of a curiosity than a game changing work.
