    It's interesting to see the different rationales, there are good points for whether more or less knowledge is sensible. One thing I notice is there is little mention of the role of the Sire in tutoring the Childe. Most embraces aren't impulsive bite and run affairs; in most cases the Sire has made the conscious decision to embrace their Childe for a reason, and would do their best to not lose it to ignorance. If you want to be callous, their Childe is an investment and it is in their interest to protect that investment, especially when the actions of the fledgling can backfire on them.

    I can vividly remember the IC prep sessions I had with the player of my Sire before my first LARP evening in the Elysium. I knew nothing of the WOD, and was about to be introduced as a Neonate of Clan Malkavian into a medium sized Cam Domain. So by the end of a few hours I knew about:

    - the Traditions
    - Cam Clans, their Modus Operandi, their Disciplines. Basically what you get from the overview pages in the Core rule book, but of course biased.
    - Sects, and why the Cam is good and Sabbat bad. Indies were just mentioned as a side note.
    - Offices of the Cam and what they do
    - Etiquette towards said offices and elders. General etiquette around vamps: why you don't look them directly in the eyes and such.
    - the Traditions, again
    - Essentials about vitae; generations, blood bonds, ghouling, Embraces. Diablerie was not mentioned.
    - Current political standings in the Domain
    - Elysium, hunting grounds, local stuff
    - A lot of what NOT to do with specific Clans or people or in general
    - the seventh Tradition: Don't get caught.

    Granted, the character of my Sire was rather Cam-conservative, but that's about the baseline I expect Neonates to know, too.

        Yeah,

        I feel like the Mentor background is really underdone. So few player characters take it, even though it is incredibly useful. I suppose my rationale is that preserving the Mystery and sense of uncertainty about the setting is an end in itself. The players may know a lot more of it but the sense of dread their characters experience can be experienced vicariously. I also think the Joker's words in THE DARK KNIGHT apply.

        ​

        The big advantage of keeping your knowledge to yourself is that it can be bargained for in the Rat Race of Kindred society. It doesn't have to be a BIG boon but virtually every little bit of information can be bargained for in a way that can be useful in the long term. It's a minor favor, potentially, to say, "Could you tell me about the Thirteen Clans" or "What, exactly, is a fucking Hecata and why are eveyrone acting like they're Sauron meets the Godfather?"

        A lot of us really enjoy talking in-character about lore and having your character comment on everything everyone already knows OOC is a cheap session but a session it is.

        "So, the Salubri are what now, exactly?"

        "Vegetarian humane healer vampires."

        "Bullshit."

                                                What my Malkavian knows about the other clans in general.

                                                Banu Haqim: A clan of warriors and low key blood mages and power brokers that make the rest of Kindred society wary as they have strange ideas regarding amarath as a good thing.

                                                Gangrel: Tough survivalists that can travel the world with far greater ease then the rest of the undead and have a keen understanding of the beast.

                                                Hecate: Giovanni whom decided to change there name for some reason and reached out to other necromantic bloodlines to join them.

                                                Lasombra: Ruthless manipulators whom can summon shadows to aid them.

                                                Ministry: A vibrant clan that others overlook because of their worship of a long gone god and not understand how well connected they are to all facets of society where the other high clans would overlook.

                                                Nosferatu: Don't let the fact of being deformed and living underground fool you. They have sharp ears and hear and record all sorts of useful knowledge. If you really want to know what is happening in your domain, they will share it with you for price.

                                                Ravnos: A nearly extinct clan of outcasts that do dirty jobs that no one will else do.

                                                Torador: High society snobs in love with there own voices and beauty, but don't get caught in there games as you might not be able to get out of.

                                                Tremere: Upstart brats that think there blood magic makes them superior to the rest and overlook the fact that they are dealing with kindred whom been playing these games way longer then all of them combined.

                                                Tzmisce: A clan of flesh crafters of some sort.

                                                Ventrue: The so called lords and ladies of the undead with the power to back it up and try not to get on there bad side to much.

                                                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                                            That's not what I'm saying. I'm saying Harpies don't track trivial boons, because they're, you know, trivial. What is what the level of information we're discussing is worth. Not all information is of equal value.


                                                            Which is to say general information costs a lot more when you're a Neonate.
                                                            Information also follows the law of supply and demand. If it's general knowledge, it's minimal value. You're talking about trading the vampire equivalent to pub trivia for minor boons or more -- and up-charging on shit like that is going to give the creditor a reputation for usury, and potentially harm their status as consequence.
