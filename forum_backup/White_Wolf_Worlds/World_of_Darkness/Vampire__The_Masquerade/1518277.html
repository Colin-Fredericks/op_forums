    The hatred the other splats have for Vampires is weird

    07-18-2023, 08:19 PM
    Vampires are pretty hated by everything and everyone. Mages, Fera, Demons, Changelings, Spirits, etc.

    Other than the fact that any criticism thrown at vampires can easily be reflected back to the other splats, who at times have done way worse. It's just kind of weird. Why have VTM a part of the same universe? Especially when it's quite clear that every other splat seems to just want to exterminate them, and could easily do so if they set aside their differences, which doesn't seem that unlikely to happen due to their immense loathing for them. Also, why allow any access to the middle umbra when its denizens for some incoherent reason also hate vampires?

    IDK, feels kind of weird.​

      It's not really the doing of the other games. V:tM set down the setting element of "lupines kill vampires who try to go out into the wilderness" back in the first green marble book, and everything just followed from there. The setting details of, say, W:tA only flesh out the Vampire-specified setting element (that being that Kindred act like BSDs, which no one wants to be around due with ill spiritual shit that follows them around a compounded issue, so Garou and Fera avoid them most times and end them when possible).

      As to why have them be in the same setting? I ask that a lot myself, but for different reasons. Mostly, I think that the foundational work just wanted vampires to be full of angst all the time and this was just another way to do it. Also, later W:tA specifies that vampires cannot enter the Middle Umbra; if a power from V:tM says that it allows a trip to the Umbra, the Werewolf Storyteller's Handbook reroutes them to the Dark Umbra, maybe the Astral, never the Penumbra familiar to W:tA. Even if you don't op[t for this, however, it's worth noting that crossover into W:tA is fucking around until you find out. Nothing is resilient enough to deal with the splatter horror setting of W:tA but characters from W:tA, where a form that can soak agg is standard and Rage or something like it usually is, as well. This is not a fault or W:tA, or something wrong with it, unlike what the development directives of W5 apparently thought; it's a fundamentally different game, and this setup can be taken as just another reason why Garou and Fera generally view vampires as lower priority targets than ferectoi and other fomori, BSDs, or banes and cults dedicated to banes, if you have to keep them in the same world.

          Should other splats be welcoming of the ambulatory corpses that steal the blood of innocents just to survive? Whose powers seem custom built to predate upon, manipulate, and exploit normal humans (including the humans you like)? Who seem to only catch the attention of other supernaturals when they're doing decided monstrous things? (To the point an entire sect of them - the least caring of secrecy, mind - openly reject all matters of ethics or humanity, and choose to commit atrocities because they think it's fun).

          While less-evil vampires exist, from an outsider's perspective, it certainly looks like vampires are all diabolical parasites. Creatures whose very existence is a literal drain on the lives of normal people, and seem disinterested in seeking alternatives (if, indeed, any alternatives to that unlifestyle are even possible, for as far as outsiders know).

          All of this before you get into specifics. Like how vampires generally "read" as Wyrm-tainted to Garou (those who don't tend to be overlooked). Or how Mages tend to have very well developed and firmly held belief systems, and thus have very strong opinions about blood-drinking cadavers that spurn the light and corrupt Sleepers for their own myriad ends. Or how Wraiths might have a natural antipathy towards vampires, since the latter so often MAKE the former through murder (and also have just enough necromancers, that actively enslave Wraiths, to be a big red flag). Or how the entire point of monster hunters (capital-H and lowercase h) is to protect mortals from the supernatural predators stalking them, or to get revenge for loved ones lost thereof. Or how Mummies might see vampires as their only "real" competition in the game of eons.

          Not to mention, vampires engage in a lot of "social engineering" (read: conspiratorial manipulation/mind control), that ends up causing wide-spread negative effects. Not because they're fighting (or serving) the Wyrm, or because they seek to affect the Consensus in a way that makes their vision of Reality come to fruition. But seemingly for no other reason than personal power and gratification, and to spite a rival. A Syndicate member of the Technocracy will push policies that leave thousands homeless and municipal programs underfunded because he genuinely believes in Capitalism (the idiot). A vampire will do those same things because homeless people with no access to shelters or even functioning street lights are easier to feed on.

          In that light, it would be weird if everyone else didn't hate vampires. Splats dislike/distrust other splats (and rival factions within their splat) at the best of times. But vampires are arguably on a whole other level, just at their base layer.

On my recent read-through of the original 1992 Werewolf: The Apocalypse core book, it amused me that the very first item in the appendix is a list of ten ways to do vampire-werewolf crossover chronicles -- including even a Romeo and Juliet type scenario where a Gangrel and a Garou fall in love!

                                                          Earlier in the book, not buried in an appendix but right there in the main storytelling section of the game, there is this astonishing paragraph:

                                                          Originally posted by Werewolf: The Apocalypse
                                                          [B]ecause of the tremendous power the Kindred do have in mortal society, some Garou feel that Vampires are among the best hope for a quick solution to the current emergencies. In this type of Chronicle, you can essentially tell stories similar to the ones explained in Vampire: The Masquerade and the supplements for that game. Garou can work to end the intrigue and power games between certain Kindred in order to create an opportunity to gain the trust and assistance of important and powerful Vampires.
                                                          Honestly, in its early stages, the World of Darkness seemed to be quite amenable to crossovers, but that element was downplayed heavily after around 1994-1995, when all of the original writers and developers had either left the company or were on their way out.​
