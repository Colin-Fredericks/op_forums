    Haired Children of Osiris!?

    06-28-2022, 11:21 PM
    So, I've never allowed anyone to play a Children of Osiris in my games. I'm far from an expert on the bloodline for this very reason (also, there's not much material about them to read in any case...). So, my question is, while Children of Osiris are always described to shave their heads, does it ALWAYS HAVE TO BE the case? I mean, I have a girl from one of my tables and she wants to make a Children of Osiris as her next character (because the current is probably going to be destroyed), but she said she really couldn't stand to play a "bald girl" and asked me if she can make a converted child of Osiris with normal hair. So, what would you do in my position? Do you think it's ok to allow it? This bloodline has always struck me as "weird", but I want to experience something new...

      I don't remember any statement being made that the Children of Osiris shaved their heads. But I could easily have overlooked that. Do you have the source?

      Now I've seen at least one picture showing one of the Children with a shaved head, but I think that was to illustrate someone who had been an actual ancient Egyptian, and the ancient male Egyptians shaved their heads (the women didn't).

      I also don't think it makes much sense. Although there is probably a core of ancient Egyptian Methusalehs among the Children of Osiris, I would imagine most of their membership now were of vampires recruited afterwards. They would have whatever hair they had at the time of the embrace. And if they shave it afterwards, it would keep coming back. Not much purpose in shaving it day after day - that has nothing to do with the real reason the Children of Osiris exists.

      So I think it's totally fine for them to have hair.

      Also, the Children of Osiris aren't actually a bloodline. They are a sect with access to a unique Discipline, and its members don't embrace. Sometimes the sourcebooks forget this.

      BTW, you may be interested in this earlier forum discussion on the Children of Osiris, and their comparison to the Salubri.

                  The Children of Osiris are essentially a monastic order, and I can understand them subjecting themselves to some kind of Rule. But the idea that they must shave their heads seems to me some kind of affectation, and if the V20 quote from Thoth is its origin, then it is likely just some flavor text a late author decided to add. It is not something mentioned in the description of the Children of Osiris in 1992's The Hunters Hunted. If you like it, keep it. If you don't, ignore it.

                  Even if you want to keep that as some kind of monastic discipline when the member of the Children is at temple, it's a rule likely to be relaxed when that person is sent outside the temple to do whatever work the local temple wants them to do. You don't want your undercover operatives to be noticeable when they are on a mission.

                                      But with the trope in place, and considering Kemintiri's situations anyways, I think she's probably bald, using either wigs or Obfuscate to make up for it. She was a priestess, and that makes for a higher probability of hair either shaved or at least wore short in Egypt (many sources depict shorter hair in religious contexts and many indicate that priests probably shaved, at least men, but maybe women), and if that wasn't the case, then Sutekh would taken care of this just to piss her off (she was a priestess of Isis, Embraced by him out of spite and vengeance because she didn't revealed the Spell of Life).

                                      I decided to confirm things in their original write-up in Hunters Hunted and it is actually there: page 51, title Methods, second paragraph.
                                      Originally posted by Hunters Hunted pp 51
                                      Appearance: Their physical traits vary, as they recruit
                                      from various other clans, including Nosferatu. They are
                                      usually adorned in pure white robes, sometimes trimmed
                                      with mystical symbols and mandalas. They never wear items
                                      that would distract them from their all-important discipline,
                                      like the secular distractions of T-shirts or expensive clothing.
                                      They are all shaved bald, including females.
                                      Personally, as others pointed, I don't think either the extreme keeping of the description is strictly needed or that they would demand such from members in a mission outside the temple.

                                      Strictly speaking, they're monks. To be precise, they're Nepalese/Tibetan monks despite the Egyptian background. From there you can read the Children in a number of ways depending how you want to depict the order. If you want them more open and trying to interact with Kindred society at large, then it isn't unfeasible to have "lay followers" that do not live in temples, do not follow the monastic rules, and it is on you if they would teach Bardo to such people or not, but it is actually not unlikely. Kemintiri herself never caused any trouble for them from knowing it, by their description it is less a secret and more something hard to keep and use without rigid discipline and their monastic life.

                                      Just talk to the player to see what she knows and what she expects from the character, not only for the bald thing, but for their whole outlook. Even if a Children Embraces, the childer won't be members until they get into it by themselves. Their standard depiction isn't in HH for nothing, they're monks that leave the monasteries for only three reasons: maintenance needs of the temple and its inhabitants, hunting FoS and inviting new members from vampires that seem to keep consistently at Humanity 8. But since the sect has so little written about it since 1992, you can stretch things a lot to accommodate your needs.

                                                          Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                          I mean, having the Children of Osiris shave every day just to make themselves even MORE likely to be targeted by the Followers of Set and easily identifiable is incredibly stupid.
                                                          Not necessarily.

                                                          First, they look like Buddhist monks. What do you expect a vampire that was a Buddhist monk in life to look like? Or what do you expect from a vampire that converted after death and became a monk? The Children do not hide that they're monks, they hide the nature of their order if that much.

                                                          Second, as far as we know the FoS have no clue that the Children still exists. The order is small, tightly united and lives in secluded areas far away from anywhere the FoS have any presence. It's easy to see the signs when you already knows the answer, but what is really the most probable cause of a random vampire being bald? Even being a FoS from another cell is far more likely than being a CoO even for those who know about them, imagine for someone who never heard about them (it isn't likely that the FoS tell this every new Embrace just 'cause) or fully expect them to have been wiped out thousands of years ago.

                                                          Third, and compounding to the above, the FoS think they wiped the Children before the CoO went to north India, so current depiction has zero to do with the CoO as the FoS knew them.

                                                          And fourth, depending on your depiction of the Children, it simply doesn't matter. They seldom appear on the world and then they go quickly and directly through their businesses before going back to their temples. They have no greater stakes, much less participation, on local politics except on matters that relate to the temple. They're not on the Jyhad, their's is not a hidden war. If you go through this route, while it is certainly risky to not make your presence a secret, it is just another approach to your conflict. One very similar to how other Hunters behave.

                                                          Remember, the Children of Osiris are first and foremost defined in the game as Hunters, as a Hunter trope. That changes their role and their uses in the setting drastically. While it doesn't mean they can't work in a game with other vampires, even with Followers of Set, as they're not necessarily zealots and even have FoS among their numbers, it does mean they approach the whole thing from a place very different from other vampires and more closely related to some human Hunters with just as much high Humanity.

                                                          Of course, those things do not add up well without making more stuff about them, but that comes with the territory of working with a group that was only ever described briefly in a single supplement where they were barely more than a side note. They're not a finished product in comparison with almost everything else in the setting, in fact many of the other groups in that same book got more space in later products than them. But they definitely can be fitted to work just fine as in-the-open opposition that denounces the FoS at every opportunity and sometimes come to blows with them without hiding their identities.
