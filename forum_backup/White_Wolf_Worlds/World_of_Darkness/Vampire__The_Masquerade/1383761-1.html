                                        Here's how I see it: in Anne Rice's novel, vampires were made sexless so many things could be explored utterly via metaphor. All the relationships in Interview are abusive in a way that making it about sexless vampires renders abstract and allows the audience to have a greater comfort level reading about. It's a lot like Star Trek in that way.

                                        Giving vampires the ability to have sex eliminates that abstraction. In novels, this is generally for one of two reasons. One is, as in the Necroscope books, the writer isn't interested in subtle metaphor, and wants to tell a story about monsters who are amplified conqueror aristocrats who rape and exploit and enslave and see ordinary people as nothing but resources to be used as food, clothing, furniture, fuel, soap, labor, and sex. It's less metaphor and more satire, parody, or caricature. Vampires as Orcs. Rather than metaphor, orc stories are about taking something that is subtle in real life and making a blatant, unsubtle caricature of it in fantasy form.

                                        The second reason some writers give vampires sex is they really just want to write an old fashion bodice-ripper about a sexy, powerful, brooding man that the audience-stand-in female is ravished by and who tames him, making him her devoted mate after she saves him from himself. Fifty Shades of Bloodsucker stuff.

                                        So the question is, do you want to deal with abusive things via subtle metaphor, satirize real life horrors via fantasy caricature, or play bodice: the rippering?

                                        If you want the first, then sexless vampires work best. You can explore extremely dark themes in a way that is no more uncomfortable than the four lights Star Trek episode.

                                        If you want to do satire, then vampires should not only be sexual, they should be superhumanly sexual, monstrous caricatures of every evil aristocrats and imperialists routinely committed, which includes a superhuman ability to commit rape.

                                        If you want to do a bodice-ripper, then they obviously need to be sexual, though not necessarily superhumanly so, just very high-end fantasy lover sexual.

                                        I think the White Wolf guys have always been more comfortable with the first. They want to deal with dark themes in a way that is, like the darker episodes of Star Trek, comfortable and safe because it is done using utterly abstract metaphors.

                                        Meanwhile you've got a lot of players who want to play Bodice: The Rippering or blatant satire games.
