    in-setting vampire texts and the general public

    10-27-2021, 05:02 PM
    What vampire texts should be known to the public in the setting of VtM? By that I mean not only do vampires know about it, but ordinary mortals in the setting also know about it. It might be popularly known, known only to the academic community (scholars, museums, and such), or among specialized audience (like the occult community, Egyptologists, archaeologists, conspiracy theorists, horror literature aficionados, etc.). For our purposes, this means more than just the Arcanum or Inquisition and the like. It's out there for people who may not know or believe vampires exist, but still encounter the text.

    For example, both the Book of Nod and Revelations of the Dark Mother (a version of the Lilith Cycle) are supposed to have been released to the general population. If I remember correctly, the English translation vampire author of the Book of Nod intended his book to only be for vampires, but somehow entered general circulation; and the Revelations was intentionally meant as a Masquerade breaching non-fiction book. Of course, the epistolary novel Dracula was meant in the VtM setting to also be a non-fiction expose of vampires, but was mistakenly thought to be fiction.

    There's been a lot of fictional texts over the years in the game, but it's not always clear whether it should be limited to just vampires knowing about them, or if any are known to mortals.

      I think unless there is a successful effort in tracking down and securing every single copy of a book, you can bet there's a huge chance a given text went public. And there are others, as the opening letters from 1st and 2nd cores, very recent and very thorough expositions on the Kindred.

      Dracula puts a complex twist to it. It exposes a lot about the Kindred, but also hides a lot, as contrary to common belief a lot of the modern image of vampires that informed VtM is a posterior development, and Dracula itself isn't really the first novel to do it, being preceded (and inspired) by The Vampyre, by Polidori.

      Going for the literary expositions, The Vampyre reveals the existence of the Kindred and a few core characteristics. Dracula reveals many more traits and common Disciplines, but also mix them with several falsehoods. Nosferatu reveals the weakness to the sun, kept a secret until then. And Anne Rice's Vampire Chronicles reveal a ton of information, either directly or by straight opposition, almost every detail not yet covered, including Humanity, Wassail, Disciplines and so much more.

      Of interest is also what could be inferred of their creation with we cross the RL origins of those stories with VtM's implications. The Vampyre is based on Lord Byron to a great extend, so maybe Byron was a Revenant that found it amusing to tell that little bit just to see where it goes. The mystery is how Byron got to be nicknamed after Lambach. Dracula, contrary to popular belief, was not based on the rl Vlad "Dracula" Tepes III, but since in VtM they're one and the same, it could be based on Stoker's hearing about him either from himself or Lambach. Anne Rice especially must be protected by someone really powerful, as she kept releasing secrets about the Kindred for many years, actually still does to this day.

      But all said and done, it is hard to tell what the effects are in the public. Without records of any original fragments to give it academic credentials, The Book of Nod is left weightless, the same goes for the Revelations. Saying that your weird book is non-fiction don't really get you much farther than the usual nutjobs that would believe it even if you said it was fiction. The market is choke-full of books about vampires, each with its own version of them, and without knowledge of actual Kindred it is impossible to sort things out among so much information.

      At this point I'd say that absolutely everything about the kindred, with every detail, is accessible to the general public, just buried in the sea of weirdness and pop-culture and mixed with a shitton of false information.

          My personal take is that leaking the basics to the public under the guise of fiction (including rpgs) actually reinforces the Masquerade because it makes anyone who does try to go public look like someone who can’t tell real life from some popular work of fiction.

          Basically, someone claiming vampires are real in such a situation looks like some nut who unironically lists their religion as “Jedi.”

          In my own setting “Kindred the Embraced” was an effort by some neonates who were executed for what was seen as an egregious Masquerade breach (which is why it lasted only 8 episodes) and their claims of doing it for pretty much the reasons above only recognized in hindsight. “Bloodlines” was a more deliberate effort launched by a group of Anarchs to basically make unusual activity in the Free States be blown off as nutters who can’t tell video games from real life (though it only became a cult classic).

          This goes basically in line with my Order of Hermes once using an Ars Magica LARP as cover for one of their conclaves. There’s just something to be said for taking refuge in audacity and being a vampire LARPing as a vampire at a vampire-themed nightclub that also sells chincy jewelry based on the “clan logos.”

                                                          I think a major difference between vampire texts and historical works is that all of the historical works were meant to be published and disseminated. So it's entirely possible lots of vampire texts have never been released to the general public. For example, it is entirely conceivable that Etrius' Veneficorum Artus Sanguis is only found in Tremere chantries (and possibly a few other places like a copy possessed by an Arcanum chapterhouse, one held by the Uktena who stole it, a third in the archives of the Society of St Leopold, etc.) and isn't in circulation.

                                                          I've had it as part of my chronicle setting that there are in fact Archons whose main responsibility is to find books that reveal too much about vampires and remove them from circulation (or at least remove the specific parts of them that they care about). They were most active in the first two centuries of the Camarilla pillaging abandoned monasteries, private libraries, and various archives, but the work still goes on today. That sole copy of a chronicle that is the only source of history regarding the Merovingian Dynasty between 604 and 650 is actually a copy whose mentions of vampire involvement was excised by some Toreador archon before they let a human scholar discover the work.

                                                          I do think that various bits of the Book of Nod, Lilith Cycle, etc. probably has been part of the academic scholarship for a while. But they may not be organized as such, and interpreted it as various things. Maybe this piece might be believed to be part of biblical apocrypha like the Book of Enoch. This other piece considered to be a Gnostic text. This other thing a medieval heretic text. This other one a forgery. Cainite scholars may recognize them for what they are, but to mortal academia they might be odd text fragment found in the ruins of a monastery in Turkey and stored in the archives of the Vatican Library.

                                                          But what I think is fun is deciding what things are actually just known to the public, even if is limited to specialists or fringe groups. It might be cool if Egyptologists know the Book of Going Forth by Night just as well as they do the Book of the Dead (aka Book of Going Forth by Day) and other funerary texts. Or that the Codex of the Damned is just one of the many Aztec codices that the Spanish created after the Conquest of Mexico and is just as well known as the Codex Borgia or Florentine Codex. Or that a near intact text of the Cycle of Lilith was discovered by some fictional equivalent to Margaret Murray or Charles Godfrey Leland, and chunks of it is now part of neopaganism because it isn't clear it is talking about vampirism, just Bahari religion on Lilith. This kind of stuff can often lead to hooks by the ST.
