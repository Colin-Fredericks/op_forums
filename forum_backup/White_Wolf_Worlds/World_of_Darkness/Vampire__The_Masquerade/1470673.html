    [V5] Alternative Ravnos Bane

    12-16-2021, 08:41 PM
    Okay I'm gonna put this out there, the Ravnos are a good clan. Sadly, this was often overshadowed by a history of stereotyping and bad writing on part of White Wolf. Now with all that said, it is no wonder that V5 felt like it had to change the weakness for V5. That said, it wasn't the best. Seriously being forced to move around or burn sucks! Not only that, but it limits playing Ravnos in V5 to only modern timeline, which is also my issue with Tremere, Salubri, and Banu Haquim. With all that said, here are the two possible alternative weaknesses for Clan Ravnos:


    Option 1
    The Ravnos are slaves to the call of the blood. Whenever a Ravnos suffers from a Compulsion, the dice penalty they suffer is increased by an amount equal to their Bane Severity. Even worse is that the stigma the Ravnos suffer from their reputation is on par with the clanless and thin-blooded, imposing a two-dice penalty to all Social tests against fellow Kindred who know of their infamous lineage.
    The above weakness is based off of their original weakness found in previous editions. Honestly, I thought of this in the shower and wanted to share.


    Option 2

    The words of Caine were clear. Those of the Ravnos clan were to be outcasts and trusted by no one. Anywhere the Ravnos settle quickly develop rumors about the travelers and their supposed misdeeds. After the first few nights in a city, the rumors about the Kindred quickly spread, sowing the seeds of mistrust and fear into both the Kindred and mortal populations alike. The Ravnos suffer a dice penalty to all Social test dice pools against anyone, mortal or otherwise, who have heard the rumors about them. This dice penalty is equal to their Bane Severity. Creating an alternative identity may alleviate this weakness for a night or two, but it too will eventually develop a similar string of rumors. Ravnos can not buy Influence or Status at character creation.
    This one is based off a quote from the Erciyes Fragments:
    He who took no action, but abandoned others to their fate,
    Shall be himself outcast, and trusted by no one.
    Thoughts? Opinions?

                          I don’t see why a Ravnos’ weakness needs to be any more external than a Brujah’s rage, a Toreador’s fugues in the face of beauty, a Malkavian’s mental illness, or a Ventrue’s refusal to feed on anyone but a specific type.

                          The Brujah’s reputation for being hot-headed and violence-prone doesn’t come from some external force making them look bad. It’s because the Beast inside them rages.

                          Similarly, there’s no reason for the Ravnos’ reputation to be some supernatural smear campaign against an innocent bystander… they have the reputation they do because their Beast urges them towards self-destructive actions and no one likes to get caught in someone else’s hot mess.

                          The desire to basically absolve the Ravnos of personal sins feels a bit like trying to make up for the past Gypsy stereotypes by making them the opposite instead… when the real answer is that, post-Week of Nightmares, the majority of Ravnos are just people of numerous backgrounds and ethnicities chosen by the survivors for the Embrace and the Ravnos’ clans personal Beast is one that urges self-destructive behaviors in people.

                                          There's a lot of ways you can deal with the Ravnos weakness along interesting lines, making the curse either tragic or vicious. The Ravnos weakness is disturbing to the Ravnos themselves, as the urges are both inconvenient and involuntary: unless they are on the Path of Paradox, they have to resist it to some degree, as otherwise they will create a huge disturbance or move around a lot to spread out their predation. They need some sort of ethical framework and build a life around it, similar to how the Brujah deal with their own clan curse. The Ravnos Beast is insidious; it taps into hidden desires and is sometimes similar to a Kuei-jin's Shadow compared to bloodthirstiness of other vampires.

                                          Consider a Ravnos whose weakness is physical confrontation and assault. Based on the vampire's personality, this could enter his or her consciousness in several ways. The vampire could be angry and vicious and be pressured to vent; it could be a traumatized or insecure upbringing's way of compensationg; or it could be an urge to punish the unrighteous and stupid behavior typically encountered in everyday life. A gangster who corners and beats people for fun is very different than a courier who pursues an example of stupid driving that caused a fender bender and kicks the drunk driver's teeth in to make a point. A high-Humanity Ravnos with this weakness could adopt a Batman-sort of vigilantism, positioning himself to thwart the most dangerous targets, and try to not kill as an outcome of the fighting. A Ravnos could seek out the biggest, toughest bruisers or multiple opponents for such fights, or he could revel in knocking around the weak and helpless. The Ravnos may outright attack other vampires and seek out a scourge-like role in the domain, or he may do his utmost to resist the urge around Kindred, spending willpower, then going off on mortal targets. The Ravnos may use such behavior to disguise his feeding, or not. His ghouls may be or may not be targets for his weakness. The Ravnos with this weakness might have Fortitude as his primary Discipline, and be distinguished by this specialty instead of the stereotypical Chimerstry user. He could be a two-bit punk, or an honorable warrior with high levels of Brawl and Melee. He could be liked and respected in the domain if he compensates right, and could be a hated outcast if he does not comport himself well. He might choose to cover up his attacks, or not. He could see other Ravnos as fellow criminals, or have no sympathy for vices such as compulsive lying or pointless theft. He might have a strong clan identity, or he could be the sort whose clan is indistinguishable at first glance, belonging to a mixed coterie and not having his behavior stand out.

                                          The Ravnos weakness is one of the best clan curses for crafting good characters; the lack of official good writing in the setting just means that the players have to do some original thinking, rather than having the concepts be served on a silver platter like with the other clans.

                                                      Ravnos is the only clan I've never saw done well by someone without both a lot of knowledge of the game and experience at creating interesting characters, at least when exploring their weakness, because for every clan you always have the option of ignoring the weakness altogether when creating the character and then just slapping it in as an afterthought. You'll have an interesting character, but it will be despite the weakness, not because of it.
