        Then we have different players.

        I had once a high humanity Tremere. She never used her powers offensively. In fact she pretty much was useless in combat, and that was on purpose. In everything else, she was a beast. (no pun)
        With Path of Blood, Technomancy, Auspex, Dominate (yes Domination was fair game), and high mental stats she was one hell of an investigator. And she was a more than adequate face with Dominate, Presence (yes she had that too) and good social scores.
        And of course High Humanity came handy for the social aspects.

        The rest of the group (One Brujah, One Nosferatu, One Gangrel) were more focused on the offensive but nobody thought that the Tremere was deadweight despite not participating in battle. She was just as usefull as the rest of the group. Perhaps the most important member of thre group even.

        I also once seen a Salubri in a Dark Age Sabbat games. Pretty fun. The Salubri was not very comfortable with the rest of the group, but survival make some strange bedfellows.

        Having players in different paths is not really an issue when they understand what they can take from each others. And yes it can lead to some interesting rp moments that V5 is sorely missing.

        Inner conflicts is also part of the personal horror afterall.

                                            Originally posted by Vilenecromancer View Post
                                            What page from the V20 book is that? Cause I can't find it.
                                            Actually, I erred slightly that it's a penalty on the defender not a bonus to the one trying to be convincing. Page 265.

                                            Anyway, I really am not interesting in a citation debate on this. I wanted to do something I felt was a bit more illustrative than just quick one-line responses to illustrate one of the mechanical areas where V5 puts up a roadblock (and not even a vampire specific one) that didn't exist in the previous takes on the game. I could detail the general V20 rules where Difficulty and how to modify it is kept separate from different kinds of rolls, and even points out that Resisted roll offs can have different difficulties for each character rather than equal rolls where whoever gets the most successes wins.

                                            But that's really not that point, and this was something I was trying to avoid with my three point initial response. The V20 rules for social conflicts are hardly perfect, but I can actually use them to blackmail someone if in a bit of a clunky fashion. And blackmail and similar backroom manipulations are a staple of the political drama VtM traditionally has as a supported playstyle. V5 doesn't support it. At best the ST can make it work by stretching a rule to something that borders up on the Golden Rule (which is a good use of it for sure, but as noted something we're trying to avoid).
