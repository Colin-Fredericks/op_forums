As such, the Brujah (and possibly others) who joined him would have been whatever motley crew impressed him in the decades since. Most of them would likely be fairly recent neonates, but a few like Crispus might be Kindred he had befriended during his travels westward before he arrived in LA.

        As I’ve noted elsewhere, my sources are quite limited - at the moment mostly dependent on the White Wolf Wiki and an online review of the sourcebook LA BY NIGHT.

        All mistakes are, therefore, quite plainly my fault.


        Regarding your own points -

        (1) I agree with the notion that the MacNeil Boys almost certainly mustered in only after the Second Anarch Revolt, but it wouldn’t surprise me if MacNeil (Whose career seems to have been adventurous for quite a long while) hadn’t run with a coterie or three even prior to that point (If only during the inter-Kindred conflicts surrounding the Wars of the Three Kingdoms and the American Revolution), with the survivors of those previous posses presumably serving as the core of the much larger network now wearing MacNeil’s name.

        N.B. One assumes that this would have occurred only after the Second Anarch Revolt made MacNeil not only storied but famous - with old acquaintances presumably rocking up to LA looking to take advantage of past association and old friends deciding to lend MacNeil their help.


        (2) One interesting question having over the exact membership of the MacNeil Boys is whether Jeremy MacNeil’s past alignment with Clan Toreador - specifically during the English Civil War and the Jacobite Risings - balances out his singularly unhappy experience with Don Sebastian.

        It’s not hard to imagine him forging friendships with the Franco-Scottish Toreador, but also easy to imagine these friendships in becoming rather conflicted after the Second Anarch Revolt (Incidentally, it’s intriguing to wonder if it was MacNeil’s sometime association with the Toreador that pointed him in the direction of Los Angeles in the first place - as opposed to San Francisco, for example.


        (3) I had not realised that there was, canonically, a Setite convert actually in MacNeil’s coterie - my notion of Nicholas as a non-Setite member of the Cult of Set was grounded more in the basic facts that Nicholas had kept something of the Cult
        of Set going between the First & Second Temples, that his leadership dispute with Dawn was not really explained and that he was brought down after MacNeil became involved in this dispute.

        With that in mind, it struck me as interesting to wonder whether the origin of this dispute could have lain in the divide between a Setite of the Clan proper and a ‘mere’ convert.


        (4) Incidentally, I definitely think it makes more sense to keep the Setite member of MacNeil’s coterie a going concern - it’s a
        logical plot hook, especially for a mole hunt of the TINKER, TAILOR, SOLDIER, SPY sort (As well as a desperately needed asset for the Setites, very much of the sort required to keep them viable in LA after two failures within fifty years).

        For my money the most interesting interpretation of this Brujah’s conversion might be the desire to see the Anarch Revolution not only replace the previous political order, but supplant the Road of Humanity with something more … vampire (But less blatantly dysfunctional than an Anarch’s understanding of the Sabbat).

        So this person would be a convert very much of the sort sought out by Hesha Rubadze - someone who converted not merely with appetite for decadence, but with an interest in transgressing and surpassing conventional Kindred morality (An aspiration distinct from the classic Brujah approach, but not so very alien to their combination of rebellious spirit and occasional investment in higher philosophies).


        (4) For the record, my understanding is that the ‘Second Temple’ period occurred during the early-to-mid 1960s, possibly in parallel to the Sabbat infiltrations: if nothing else, this would definitely keep storylines in LA swinging (Like a heavyweight with a grudge, in fact).


        (5) Finally, it’s interesting to note that while Dawn is a remarkable survivor, there’s a case to be made that she’s grown a bit gun shy after her repeated failures in LA - my impression is that she’s an extremely capable subversive, but not very good at growing her organisation (Possibly because all her training and early experience involved bringing down elements within the European colonial powers, an aristocratic culture and political situation rather different to the one prevailing in the United States of America, at least in Los Angeles: perhaps to some degree she still thinks in ‘Top Down’ rather than ‘Grass roots’).

                Black Fox, since they’re not all Setite related I’ll reply to your points above in this thread, then move all Snake-related discussions to the appropriate thread.


                (1) At least back in the days of the Ancien Regime, I’d bet that keeping allies amongst the Scottish Toreador meant having more than one friend in France - it amuses me to imagine that a key point of personal tension between MacNeil and Germaine would be the former’s record of cooperation with the sort of Kindred who would have liked to see Germaine torn apart by wild horses (Though the fact these cooperations were directed against the ‘English’ might slightly mitigate this).


                (2) It’s not hard to imagine Los Angeles Toreador - whether of Don Sebastian’s bloodline or not - being famously discrete (At least by Toreador standards) or famously open-handed when it comes to bribing the local Anarchs with ‘Bread & Circuses’.

                Incidentally, I tend to believe that Isaac Abrams - of BLOODLINES - would most likely be a grandchilde of Don Sebastian* (Mostly because the Prince of LA seems to have placed an emphasis on ‘Spanish’ blood, which suggests that he would limit his personal embraces to those who possessed it, but might well have been shrewd enough to command his own childe to embrace a Yankee or two to act as a ‘face’ and/or mouthpiece in an increasingly Anglo city.

                In other words, one assumes that Isaac Abrams is - or ought to be - of the Eighth Generation (Presumably embraced about twenty years before the Second Anarch revolt, possibly discretely).


                *Much to my amusement, I’ve only just realised that the first and (In one version of events) second Prince of LA have exactly the same Christian name - amusing mental image of Smiling Jack claiming that nobody else noticed this ‘cause Don Sebastian’s first name was ‘Don’ obviously - which can hardly have been a good omen for the success of Prince Sebastian LaCroix.

                Honestly, somebody on the Camarilla must have wanted him dead ‘Aut Caesar aut nihil’.


                (3) I agree that it’s unlikely that the Clan Setite proper would let mere converts hold the reins of power in the Cult of Set, which is why it struck me that depicting the Free States as somewhere the situation was fluid and unconventional enough for exactly that sort of situation to arise would be a useful opportunity to explore a probably-unprecedented scenario.

                If not through Nicholas then through the Brujah mole in MacNeil’s coterie.

                Incidentally, I’m pleased to learn that this would, in fact, appear to be a situation without precedent in the literature - if you can’t find the unprecedented in the Anarch Free States from time to time, then where?


                (4) Incidentally, given that Gloria appears to have been resident in San Francisco between the first and second Temple periods (There’s an interesting tale or two to be told there, I suspect - this is the Peak Film Noir era and San Francisco has significant associations with the genre - given that Prince Vannevar Thomas runs a tight ship and Setites love to shake things loose), it’s possible that Nicholas was the one who kept the Followers of Set working in LA, suggesting that it could well have been HIM who put the Word of Set into our Brujah convert’s ear.


                (5) I’d bet that the subject of ‘New Carthage’ Utopianism has been discussed ad nauseum in private gatherings, though one suspects it’s the sort of thing brought up at local Rabts only to receive a decidedly-mixed (and possibly confused) reception.

                Mental image of Smiling Jack only just barely refraining from shooting the notion down with extreme prejudice - all the better to hear Johnny Brujah and Billy Anarch ask sterling questions like “Where’s a Carthage and why should we ****ing care?”


                (6) That shoots my vague mental image of the timeline all to pieces, but it does suggest that there could have been a connection between the Sabbat Incursion and the second Temple - presumably the Setites slithered in through the gaps in Anarch ranks left by the shovelheads et al.

                                    I'm considering whether to include the Gangrel Valkyries as cast members of the chronicle. LABN does not include any Gangrel at all, and there's only a few Gangrel ever mentioned in regard to the Free States. However, in Clanbook Gangrel Revised it is mentioned that there are now four Gangrel Valkyrie on the West Coast. It's part of the Revised metaplot where the Free States no longer exist, the Cathayan vampires for some reason have conquered Los Angeles, and now four powerful elder Gangrel are there to fight them. There's probably a Death Star, Agent Smith, and John Wick involved as well for all I know.

                                    It's anachronistic to include any Valkyries at the time of the original sourcebook, but I'm only using canon as inspiration here. Using some of my other ideas about the All High and his followers (located in the Werewolf forum here), there's no reason why a group of four Valkyrie wouldn't be sent to North America. And given their hostility to the Toreador and Ventrue (and the Camarilla), it would make sense for them to use the Free States as a safe haven as they travel North America on secret assignment for whatever macguffins they're given.

                                    Unfortunately, there's no details given whatsoever on these four Valkyries except that they're all blonde and big on teaching people Viking ways. So I'll becoming up with characters from scratch.

                                    I'm more interested on what I can do with them in the chronicle. First, they aren't actually anarchs even if they have common enemies in the Camarilla. As explicit followers of the All High who are part of an army preparing for Gehenna, they don't have anything in common with MacNeil or the Anarch Movement. The Valkyries just find the anarchs to be convenient allies. Still, I can have fun with the idea of the PCs and other NPCs learning this. They're just another group with their own agenda exploiting the Free States while they can. Second, they are another possible source of magic among the Anarchs, and this is something I'd like to explore. The All High knows blood magic, and so do some of his followers. Having one Valkyrie know some old school magic (like Weather Control or Neptune's Might) that could potentially be taught to some worthy other (who wants to help Odin in Ragnarok!) could be an intriguing option. Third, they can be potential allies or enemies to the PCs as they do whatever it is they're doing (for Odin IC, for whatever reason I want them to do OOC) and the PCs come across them. They could become a convenient quest giver especially for more esoteric or unusual scenarios.

                                    I think a group of four Nordic vampire beauties (all speaking English with a pronounced Norse accent and making common language mistakes as speakers of English as a foreign language) are probably going to be hits among PCs. While World of Darkness puts Brunnhilde herself as riding a Harley-Davidson, I'm more inclined to give these four a European cruiser motorcycle. (I think they need cruisers if they intend to travel throughout much of North America). The Moto Gussi California was not only in production at the time, but used by the LAPD and CHP. Of course, if people who know motorcycles suggest a better option, I'll take it.

                                    I'll not have them be in LA when the chronicle begins, but may introduce them as arriving after the PCs have established themselves.

It's a bit uneven but does have a lot of interesting 80s LA scenery that goes well with LA by Night, and the gang make good visual inspiration for a general sort of Anarch coterie.
