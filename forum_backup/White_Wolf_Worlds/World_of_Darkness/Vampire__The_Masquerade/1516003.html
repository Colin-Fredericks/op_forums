    How static is the appearance of vampires for you?

    06-24-2023, 08:22 AM
    Vampire books
    Vampire appearances don't change
    Also vampire books
    He was low on humanity, and looked monsterous.
    Vampire mechanics
    My character was embraced with an appearance of 1, he was ugly, but given a hundred years and 24xp, he now gets regular offers from model scouts.
    So on and so forth

    So, short of magical intervention like thaumaturgy, how do you all run things? I've heard ideas like 'never mind the colour and length, the style reverts exactly to whatever it was when you were embraced' and 'appearance improvements are purely magic and you actually look the same' but I've also heard 'You just become really like... photogenic, your best side is always shown' and 'All that XP you just spent is just your character learning to dress well and do makeup, that's the only way!' (never mind that vampires have a lot of resources and easy access to people very capable of giving toads makeovers, and an XP cost would just be strange there, also nevermind that some characters just wouldn't be into that)

    For me, permanent hair dye is very permanent, less permanent solutions won't stick with the embrace (but bleaching your hair will, so there are a fair few white-haired neonates). Styling, however? no. That's a bit on the cartoonish side, something of a problem in a setting where the masquerade is important.
    Supposedly, vampires are supposed to lose fat when embraced, the Beast is into optimizing the hunter. Vampires with very low humanity have gaunt faces and deathly low amounts of bodyfat. Vampires with higher humanity are a still lean by human standards but look healthy. The water content of muscles can also change.

    Vampires who improve their appearance do have subtle changes to their shape: They no longer get bags under their eyes, their skin is clearer, fat and muscle tissue swells or shrinks to more ideal proportions. They smell better. Many changes concern how the vampire carries themselves themselves with their expressions subconsciously. Their disciplines small effects on beauty that can't be justified this way: Obfuscate users find people ignoring their flaws, presence users have an indescribable aura to them, Animalism and Dominate users subconsciously have people increase their appraisal of them once eye contact is made, Auspex users have people feel like there's a connection between them,

    A lot of changes (typically unwelcome ones, like damage) made shortly before the embrace will be reverted or fixed, but such things are inconsistent.

    How well fed a vampire is has a lot of changes for it's appearance in folklore, with vampires being quite healthy looking or even slightly bloated if they've fed, or even turning purple if they've overfed. Should this be a feature? I don't know. A vampire can sacrifice their own body if their blood stores are out, becoming mummy-like in appearance, but that's an extreme scenario. Broadly speaking I imagine Vitae is either dense or ethereal and doesn't really impact the way a cainite looks

    How does everyone else do it?

              The way I view it, vampires are beasts first, the walking dead a distant second. The beast wants its shell to be as efficient a predator as possible, which means it tries to fix physical defects and even improve the body, though it is rather stupid and goes about it clumsily and inconsistently. That stupid clumsiness and inconsistency is why you have some vampires who barely change at all over the centuries, and others who change so much as to be almost different people.

              Of course, as a mechanical effect, it's all about whether players want to spend experience on appearance or appearance-related merits like Stunning. But in-universe, the reason is that the Beast wants to inhabit a perfect predator, but goes about achieving this in a stupid, clumsy manner.

              Truly making vampires static doesn't work in a gameline. You could never justify spending experience on so many things, nor could you justify NPCs being formidable for reasons other than raw disciplines. Vampires as static might work in a one-shot novel where the protagonists are very ordinary humans facing a supernatural creature they don't even believe in at first. But in a gameline, this idea causes all sorts of problems.

                  The monstrous appearance of low humanity is a side effect of the subsequent difficulty correctly/fully implementing the blush and the psychological deterioration that sets off the human subconscious' panic button. They don't physically change; all vampires unsettle humans if the blush isn't active.
                  Appearance also covers other aspects of presentation as well as how conventionally attractive you are; knowing how to dress appropriately for situations, and how to convey meaning through fashion and body language and blah.
                  Although considering protean, vicissitude, the gangrel and nossie weaknesses and the fact that the embrace physically edits you to have fangs... yeah, I see no reason that vampirism couldn't edit you overtime to be more attractive.

                  Whether the embrace has the power to shape you into a perfect predator and remove physical disabilities depends on the strength of their blood. 4th gen embraces a disemboweled amputee who's had two hip disarticulations? Their intestines magically put themselves back into the body and they can walk. 12th gen does it and the childe remains legless and gets either the permanent wound flaw, the open wound flaw, or both. Embracing somebody who's taken lethal levels of physical trauma has increasing chances of failing as generation climbs higher, as diluting blood lacks the strength to repair the damage and perform the embrace at the same time. The embrace has a time limit to work within.

                  As for the reset on waking; considering that vampires' clothes don't magically transform into what they were wearing during their embrace, I think it's safe to say that something placed on the vampire doesn't change. Makeup and certain forms of hair dye will remain. I'd also allow for tattoos as they don't so much alter the body itself as just stick things underneath the skin; the wound heals, the ink remains. Not sure where I stand on implants which stretch the tissues around them to make room. I'm leaning towards allowing them, but I dunno. Unlike humans, vampire tats won't fade or stretch with time and if you go too long without using the blush and produce all those oils (plus the fact that vampire cells don't deteriorate so no dead skin flakes or hair damage) makeup and hair dye can take a lot longer to rub off.

                  Bleaching your hair changes the actual hair and the lost pigmentation will be reset to embrace levels. Artificial tans are off the table. Obviously no plastic surgery to rearrange your facial features. Piercings get shoved out as the entire wound resets itself. etc.

                  The strength of the "stasis" weakens over time. Taking inspiration from V5's Nuit Mode merit, and the fact that I count all generations below 10th as thinblood; generations 11th-16th don't revert to the night they died automatically.

                  11 - alterations (cut hair, piercings, etc) reverse slowly over the course of the week instead of overnight. Wounds from aggravated can also "scar", which are treated as alterations.
                  12- alterations reverse very slowly over the course of the month
                  13- Alterations do not reverse on their own. Can "heal" alterations (cut hair, for example) as if it were bashing
                  14 - "heals" alterations as lethal
                  15- "heals" as aggravated
                  16- can't reverse alterations at all and also struggles with mending wounds. Frankly, at this high a generation it's a miracle your blood managed to animate you at all.

                  While stuff cut off/out resets, it doesn't grow. You can shorten a shoulder length bob into a pixie cut, but if you were embraced with a pixie cut it's never going to grow longer without the assistance of a hairdressing Tremere/Tzimisce.

                  Also I'm just plain stealing the Nuit Mode merit (sans blood potency nonsense), so some vamps can go as crazy with body modification as they like due to those random quirks of the embrace.

                  Prone to hyperbole

                                              In my chronicles, the basic condition of the corpse at the time of embrace is static (given the usual caveats of Clan curses, Flaws, use of Disciplines, etc.). However, appearance of the physical body is variable to the extent that Humanity/Path ratings, spending Blood to appear alive, and other special status conditions work in game. In other words, the appearance of the corpse can fluctuate to looking how it did before death, to looking far worse (although perhaps not in the sense of decay).

                                              The XP system always complicates this, as a person can always have an Appearance of 1 at character creation and spend enough XP to make it be 5. It's just the artifact of an RPG. And it isn't any better or worse than having it down for Intelligence or some other attribute we don't normally associate with such changes. I normally don't see this happen though. It's generally rare for a PC to improve even one Attribute as they work on Abilities and Powers first. And when they do, it's generally only one or two key Attributes by one dot. So I've never actually had a condition at table top where this matters (LARP and Online games are different because XP relative to dots means characters can improve much faster, and all stats are essentially power states, not any meaningful roleplay limit).

                                              If I ever ran a chronicle long enough that things like this should matter, I'd probably discuss with my gaming group how we should approach things in terms of "realism" and "fun". In practical terms, increasing Appearance should be something limited to developing one's sense of style, demeanor, etc. for most characters, but I would also accept that there can be an "ugly duckling" that can go through such sweeping changes to greatly increase their appearance.

                                              In terms of non-invasive temporary cosmetic changes like hair dye or makeup, it'll eventually wear off. But this is so trivial as to not really matter in game. In terms of invasive changes like tattooing (or tattoo removal), the corpse looks like how it did the next day. Vampire slumber "resets" things.

                                              If needed (a Player wanted this), I would probably allow some kind of major "natural" change to happen (something normal if they were alive). Like a female vampire's hair grows out to allow for fashion changes, or for a male vampire who lived in age of long hair and beards for men to shorn it if they live in the age of beardless crew cuts. Or perhaps an older vampire to look more like their youth. It's relatively minor and supported by classic literature like Dracula. As long as they were still recognizable, I'm not against it. Not sure what would be the price - probably something like more Blood Expenditure rather than XP.

                                                  I am almost the polar opposite of Hi. I have a socialite Brujah whose first composure roll after quickening most nights is fixing the stubble and bad hair day from when his domitor unexpectedly embraced him. So each time he awakens that blood point also puts his face and hair back to how it was.

                                                  Conversely the Prince is well known to impose bodily mutilations (strike off the hand that offends thee) as a punishment for minor infractions with a restriction on when the kindred in question is permitted to heal it.

                                                  So like minor or cosmetic changes are so easily reversed that there is no conscious way to prevent the curse from doing so, but larger more drastic changes can be prevented from healing through act of will on the part of the vampire (roll willpower to not start regenerating the eye that your prince put out, you fail? you better damage it again before anyone notices)

                                                  Gaining appearance is normally done in the kismetrose way where it mostly represents a better body language or apparent confidence, but also represents subtle changes over time such as becoming incrementally more symmetrical or rugged or whatever. I do like the idea of vampires, especially low humanity vampires, becoming more inhuman looking over time out to the limit of sort of being kiasyd-looking (in scale of change, not that all kindred eventually look like kiasyd). Also I had a player talk me into their gangrel being transitioned as an effect of their body more reflecting their spirit on embrace and I dig it. So for gangrel and nosferatu in particular I'm more into the idea of some vampires coming to reflect their inner selves over the course of years or centuries.

                                                  But to get a tattoo or a scar you need thaumaturgy. To not have to trim your hair to a different hairstyle or have dye or henna stick to your skin, you need thaumaturgy. Making a change to the for your quickening tries to restore you to requires changing the shape of your soul.
