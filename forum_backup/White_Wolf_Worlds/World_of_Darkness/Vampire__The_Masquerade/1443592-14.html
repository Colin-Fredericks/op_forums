                    On the whole calling out alt-righters thing:

                    I think this thread has lost a lot of context. There was not one misstep by nWW, but a series of them.

                    People are not getting upset at the idea of neo-Nazi Brujah, but the phrasing in the book equating their status as outsiders and rebels (despite a rise in political power by the far-right in both the US and Europe) to non-politically charged groups.

                    Then there's things like the accidental use of numbers in a mechanics example that are used in alt-right iconography. And Martin's incredibly poorly worded statement on FaceBook about how there's nothing they can do but accept the reality that with so many Swedes voting for a far-right political party with Nazi sympathies means they have lots of Nazi customers (which... might make Jason's statement a bit more sensible when you look at his specific phrasing as a way to refute Martin's).

                                                04-22-2021, 04:25 AM
                                                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                Honestly, I think the biggest benefit of V5 is the fact that it dramatically broadens the scope of underutilized genres in the vampire game. Not only is combat and mechanics more simplified but it really does give a lot of incentive for doing street level gaming that didn't exist there before. I used to be very hostile to MAGE REVISED due to the extensiveness of use that I made of the Umbra in my games, almost to the point of being Nobilis, but when I actually tried out a street mage game, it proved to be incredibly fun.
                                                I can't see how this is true. It's not broadening if it's only in one area. 'Honing in' may be more accurate. V20 definitely allows for a broader style of play. I think traditional roleplaying games are better for that sort of play. Modern indie game design attempts to express theme and genre through mechanics, which is fine for games that offer a very specific experience, but I don't think a game such as VTM has ever been that narrow. It has never focused on just one experience. By making it narrow, it does gut a lot of the opportunities from the world.

                                                To be honest, I play VTM for that world. I like rules that support a range of themes and don't get in the way. Bells and whistles can be good, but I prefer them as something in my toolbox I can pull out if I need them for a specific chronicle with a specific tone.

                                                I'm extremely comfortable creating theme, tone and mood through narrative and roleplaying. I don't need systems to do that. I get that others do, though. So the best solution is one that caters to me as well as those other players. You give a basic system and then provide a bunch of dials for those who need to tweak things or need extra support building a cohesive ambience.

                                                When I think of the games I return to all the time, they're games like Warhammer, Fading Suns, Shadowrun and anything World of Darkness. Even D&D and Exalted are not as narrow as V5. I don't play hyper specialised games that always play the same because that's not my bag. I like games to surprise me and go in new directions, not always play the same.

                                                In this case, I don't think the combat rules are actually better either. Simpler is not necessarily better. The new rules put way more on the ST to adjudicate, due to basic things like not having a definite initiative system. V20DA provided a much faster, deadlier and more versatile combat system that's usually done in fewer than three rounds anyway, but with enough dials that you can have more intricate options or you can keep it simple.

                                                CTD2e also had one-roll combat, which I still think was better than V5's, and that was two decades earlier.

                                                Honestly, I think perception is the cause of the problem here because if you play the rules as is, the V20 vampires are the ones that should be frenzying all the time. I believe that a lot of Storytellers got out of the habit of calling for frenzy checks when in danger, when angry, when there was any fire larger than a cigarette, when injured, or the scent of blood nearby. The rules make it very clear that this is meant to be an extremely common thing. I note that I actually think that the ability to manage the Beast goes up with the fact that the mind is "quieted" when you kill someone in the game. Plus, Compulsions mean that you are LESS likely to go on a killing spree but just have your behavior altered.
                                                Heavy Arms dealt with this, but this is your misperception, I think. V20 and Revised are both clear that these rolls are for exceptional circumstances. It's there in black and white.

                                                But there are ample ways of managing the Beast in-game, not the least bit keeping that Hunger at 1 and only having to deal with the threat if confronted with great provocation. Let's not pretend that V20's rules don't make it clear that if you spend that last Blood Point, you won't carve up your own coterie and the nearest baby. When you don't, that was because the ST made a decision to not do so because it wouldn't be fun. V5 has solidified that lessened tendency to go on a murder spree into the rules.
                                                I mean, you explicitly don't need to kill anyone to avoid getting hungry in V20 or earlier. You can sip little but often and be fine. It's really not the same at all. Even one Hunger Die can cause havoc in play.

                                                Even the Bestial Failures have been designed to move AWAY from random murder.

                                                RANDOM COMPULSIONS

                                                1-3: Hunger
                                                4-5: Dominance
                                                6-7: Harm
                                                8-9: Paranoia
                                                0: Clan Compulsion*
                                                Murder is already baked into Hunger, so the fact that Bestial Failures don't mandate murder doesn't reduce the murder requirement. It just stops it from being even higher.

                                                But overall, Messy Criticals and Bestial Failures do increase the risk of bad things happening significantly, and you're rarely free of them. And I actually like the idea of them; I just think, as is, they're a bit of a narrative straitjacket. They ensure a certain style of play at all times.

                                                I don't think that in the entirety of the genre of vampires has ever made this an attitude of a protagonist. Vampires may be cursed in the literal sense with an uncontrollable lust for blood but when they do something horrible under duress they still carry that traumatizing memory. I once got a 1-star review by a poor woman who was HORRIFIED that there was a scene in my STRAIGHT OUTTA FANGTON book where the protagonist was locked up in a basement with a little girl to be starved until he fed on her.
                                                That's where content warnings can come in handy. Personally, I'd find that gratuitous, too. I'm not into that sort of story.

                                                That said, it's clear we have different tastes as to what we want to play. I like low levels of squick, even when I'm playing Sabbat vampires. Vampires can be dicks to each other and adults without involving minors, pets, vulnerable people, etc.

                                                I'm like, "Yes, it's meant to be horrifying."

                                                It obviously traumatized the protagonist despite having no control.
                                                Honestly, this anecdote makes me uncomfortable. What's the effect of such traumatic incidents on the reader or, in a game, on the player? It sounds to me like the person who complained in her review didn't feel there was adequate warning about what was in store, and that's a legitimate critique.

                                                To bring that back on track, there are different levels of horror. I'm hugely into classic horror movies, but I've never been into things like Hostel or Saw because I think they're gratuitous. Horror isn't just one thing. VTM doesn't need to be gratuitous either, just because horror sometimes is.

                                                Political horror is something that I think has been modified primarily for this game based around the decades of lessons of LARP and material. The loss of Elders and focus on jockeying for power has taken front stage so we do have people like Kevin Jackson actually able to move into the big seat. However, it's because the power vacuum that currently exists low-wise is open to them. It's not like there's more frenzy opportunities than Elysium than there were before either and when you frenzy, you might NOT rip out the throat of Annabelle as you would before but instead just be a real asshole.
                                                Most of us aren't LARPers, which is again why this feels like the game wasn't written for us. I get the intent, but it makes more sense that the things LARP needs happen to the LARP version of the game (and V20 MET does have a separate metaplot and rules for that precise system). Merging the two disadvantages both groups, really.

                                                I'm not entirely down with that but let's not pretend the Compulsion system doesn't downgrade the dangers of frenzy.
                                                It absolutely does not. Compulsions do not replace Frenzy. They are in addition to it. How is that not *adding* to the dangers? 3+1=4, not 2.

                                                Now, if you could opt to take a Compulsion instead of Frenzying in certain low-threshold situations, that would reduce the dangers of the Frenzy system, IMO. And actually, that might be a neat way for me to port Compulsions into V20.
