            Originally posted by Aahz View Post

            Herd is one of those Backgrounds that can be very powerful or useless depending on which aspects you focus on and how you play your character. Using V20 for the purposes of discussion.
            Herd: * is 3 Vessels, which is enough to ensure basic survival from night to night.
            Herd: ** is 7 Vessels, which lets you spend a few blood points per night on top of basic feeding.
            Herd: *** is 15 Vessels, which pretty much covers a high BP use character build.
            Herd: **** is 30 Vessels, which covers a whole coterie of efficient users.
            Herd: ***** is 60 Vessels, which covers a whole coterie of high BP uses characters.

            Then you can pool the background for larger groups or higher use situations.

            A Tremere who specializes in Auspex and being a Seer who has a Gargoyle bodyguard can easily get away with a one or two dot Herd easily. But a Tremere who is constantly casting Thaumaturgy and doing Rituals by themselves should have a three or four dot Herd on hand. Given the proclivity for Tremere Chantries to be besieged, it really is a wierd plot hole for them not to maintain live in Herds of considerable size.

                                                    Originally posted by Psychotobe View Post
                                                    To be fair that's every rpg. If you ever put legitimate thought and planning into the situation it's usually fairly easy to nullify whatever conflict the setting relies on. Presumably the explanation in wod is they've already all been taken by reclusive vampires as soon as their abandoned and up for sale. Entirely to just wait out the apocalypse
                                                    Personally, I have always been a fan of exploiting the aspect of gaming where a little bit of planning or thought nullifies aspects of the game.

                                                    If you play a fighter, there are mechanics to represent how good a fighter you are. If you want to play a magic caster, then there is a list of spells and rituals to learn and use. If you play a "thinking" character on the other hand, there are rarely mechanics to support it.

                                                    It's kind of like trying to play a genius inventor like Edison or Tesla, who are defined by their ability to redefine the technology of the setting but are inherently not allowed to change the setting or its mechanics. So you instead just role play a person with whacky ideas that never really do anything in the long run.

                                                    We get a vicarious thrill from the thought of being able to be the action hero and do ridiculous amounts of violence in games, mostly due to the mechanics being designed to allow us to experience it. But how do mechanically support the thrill of being the smartest person in the room? Few games get within a mile of mechanically supporting a "Xanatos Gambit" style character that isn't an NPC.
