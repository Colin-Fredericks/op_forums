    It just occurred to me that for ghosts at least, there are some obvious defenses.

    Aura Perception does detect the auras of wraiths. Someone untrained with it might not realize it is a wraith, but seeing any aura that is not associated with an obvious body would create suspicion. It's not a typical Nosferatu power as it is an out of clan Discipline, but many warrens could have someone who knows that power. Many long established warrens could have learned the benefit of this from visitors of other broods. It could easily be something of a checklist of standard Nosferatu knowledge of warren defense.

    Forbiddance and Warding does provide some protection. That's always mentioned as something available to living humans so most likely undead vampires should not have access to it. However, ghouls are still living. So Nosferatu may have one or more servants who have enough knowledge to provide it and be trusted enough to allow inside the warren. How common is this done? Probably depends on the domain, but if a local brood decides it's warranted, they probably have enough connection with other broods to find one where it is done and quickly learn what they need to do, or request someone to arrive to perform it while they wait to develop a local resource. It's certainly easier than trying to find a vampire with the appropriate blood magic rituals or necromancy.

    I also believe animals can also perfectly see and hear wraiths as well. I am pretty sure that is established lore, but I can't find a reference after a very quick look through my books. (If anyone knows, please let post it!) Now ordinary humans may not understand why their dog is suddenly barking at nothing, but anyone with Animalism can quickly learn the reason. This is probably the most obvious "defense" and would long be known to the Nosferatu. Probably every warren (or Hosting outside the warren) would have a bright dog or other animal who could provide such warning. Such ghouled pets probably already exist for other obvious reasons so this should be a default.

    Now what the brood can do about such things after detection is a different matter. It could just be a normal ghost without connection to the Giovanni, other vampires, or other enemies. Maybe even an entity they could befriend. There may not be any obvious suspects of who in the domain sent the ghost to spy on them. But if this is something they detect on an ongoing basis, at least they could begin investigating how to deal with the perceived threat.

    I don't think there are any similar measures against an astral projector whether the be vampire, mage, or human with numina or sorcery. But there are some things you can't help.

            You could have an Ethereal Filcher down there as a Bygone of sorts.

            It's a D&D creature that wanders in the Ethereal Plane, but is actually just a beast slightly more intelligent than a common animal. It uses the Ethereal as a hiding spot while hunting for food and baubles (it is a magpie of sorts), but its actual nest is material.

            The overall idea is simple and versatile. The monster can be as tough as you need, and its ability to wander through the Umbra or similar makes for a hard investigation. Being a trinket snatcher it can either get something from the Nos, or have something the Nos want. Yet while it may be extremely disturbing to have around, it isn't really a lethal threat if left alone.

            For traps, you need three types: gas/spore clouds that can put humans to sleep or worse without losing precious blood, alarms that detect the presence of undead intruders preferably hours before they arrive, and anything that makes a fight against ghouled animals more difficult. That way those traps mean nothing for members of the clan, and can help a lot even if they aren't deadly by themselves.

You can also think about the anatomy of the animals near a given trap, as that may make something hard for a normal humanoid body to pass by, but some animals go through no problem. For example something simple like a tangle of ropes from waist size and above, forcing intruders to crawl. Also you can reduce visibility with mist or darkness where you have animals that don't rely a lot on sight, like bats or specially trained dogs.

                                                The problem with traps that do actual harm is that you should always consider the inhabitants and possible accidents. Be it someone coming back drunk from laced blood or just having to move around during the day, even just a forgetful moment or a misstep, relying on people just not triggering the trap isn't a good policy, you need to have a contingency for allies falling to the trap. If the trap just hamper movement or sight, counting on animals to do the actual harming, then it is just a small inconvenience for any resident going through it.

These are relatively safe, but more hidden than others. There may be no obstacles here, or obstacles like you described that hinder or delay movement, but do no damage.

                                                    Then there are paths meant for non-Nosferatu. The Nosferatu do not enter those except for those paths they "safely" escort a non-Nosferatu to the antechamber. All the other paths are dangerous as they're just meant to stop intruders trying to invade. These would lead to harmful traps, dead ends, obstacles, spawning pool animals, and the like. The only time the Nosferatu enter these would be to repair or reset traps, and there would be some kind of safety protocol involved. Other STs can run it differently, but that will probably be my default.

                                                    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                                    I think with the current "bloat" of Blood Magic, it wouldn't be too out there for the Nos to have their own Path of Warding. A single Path bought as Out of Clan and focused on making wards for the warren, with a small list of warding rituals.                                                     It is certainly an option. I would probably select the actual Path of Warding described in the Dark Ages Companion. It's something I'd argue that Zelios might be able to discover on his own, and then tie in several warding rituals and the like with it. They are the secrets of the Craft of Zelios or whatever you want to call it.

                                                    If I went this route, I'd make any Nosferatu who knew it to be part of some secret craftmason society within the Nosferatu exclusively made up of stonemasons/engineers interested in sacred geometry. It would be very similar to Freemasonry, but it's own thing and very much inline with the legendry associated with the more spiritual or occult aspects of cathedral construction that go back to the Temple of Solomon. As such, it's not simply meant to be a practical teaching, but something designed to spiritually elevate its members. Therefore, the number of Nosferatu who know it are very rare. They essentially need to join a guild, learn its rules, work on its degrees, take vows not to reveal its secrets, etc. I would prefer it if only a rare few warrens would have such defenses, and like other wards they don't always work or cover only some places or times.

                                                    Keeping it limited also provides an ST with the option of using it as a story for their PCs. If there's not simply an NPC already there doing it at every warren, then it can become a plot point where a PC decides to visit to another brood and request help, or joining the order and becoming an apprentice, dealing with politics within the masonic organization, finding out ways to provide boons so such a mason would show up and begin placing some wards in your warren, etc. It creates more plots and stories.

                                                    I actually don't want Nosferatu warrens to be invulnerable anymore than I want Tremere chantries to be invulnerable or any other lair/haven/place (whether individual or communal) to be invulnerable. I think it's perfectly fine to have warrens be vulnerable to someone with Auspex 5. It's an advanced power, and players who work their way to it should be able to get some use out of it. I don't like simply saying, "No, the warrens are supposed to be invulnerable and the Nosferatu meetings secret, so there..." I just like thinking it through before games begin so I can determine that this is a vulnerability you just have to accept. Or if it turns out to be something like ghosts where we figured out there may be some easy things to do to mitigate it, even if it cannot be eliminated.

                                                    Rather than letting characters use astral projection without consequences, or arbitrarily screwing them over so it becomes useless, I'd rather have some idea of complications, threats, obstacles, diversions, or other opportunities arise so it is not simply an auto-win. But that's a larger question in regards to the use of that power and what exactly the astral plane in Vampire should be, not something limited to its use against the warrens. I do have some ideas on that, so I'll just need to review my notes and ponder what other things might happen to astral travelers while they are on their way to the warrens or any other place.

                                                    However, every ST and gaming group has different tastes, and if this is the route they want to go down, more power to them.

                                                    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

                                                    I'm of two minds on that. I do like that there's an acknowledgment of other blood magic traditions outside the Tremere. Their exclusive monopoly of it created problems IC and OOC. I think it's better that there are more traditions out there while still keeping the setting role of the Tremere possessing far more occult power than any other rival groups. However, there's the potential problem that it makes it seem as if everyone knows blood magic, and the game stops being about vampires and becomes about vampire sorcerers. And that's not the same game. I still want blood magic to be relatively rare, and the magic itself to be secrets that are hard to learn.
