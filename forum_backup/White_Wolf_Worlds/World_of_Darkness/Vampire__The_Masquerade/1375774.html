    Alternate Tremere Clan Flaw - NO in clan disciplines - V20

    03-20-2020, 05:03 AM
    This alternate clan flaw is something that I've been working on for a bit. It came to me all at once in a moment of inspiration and it comes with a fair bit of fluff that some of you may like or may wish to ignore.

    The jist is this. The Tremere, having been brought to the curse of Cain by unnatural means are not true vampires in the purest sense, they have no direct link to the 13 antediluvians that inherited the curses given to them by Cain but they are of Cains blood regardless, as such, these vampires are neither clanless nor are they a true clan in the traditional sense (although they have many distinctive traits that utterly separate them from the caitiff).

    While the Tremere possess no unique clan flaw that distinguish them from other vampire clans, they also have no unique in clan disciplines that are their own either and must learn them from other vampires who possess them, save one: thaumaturgy, the discipline of many paths.

    The art of magic runs in the blood of the Tremere and all who have that blood feel called to it, but unlike the other vampires who rule the night, this discipline requires study to be learned, unless the vampire is of the rarest sort. Tremere may still learn disciplines out of clan, which as per optional rules, requires them to drink the blood of another vampire, though they suffer blood bonding no worse than anyone else, and still practice the binding to the will of the clan as well as the taking of blood from every newly embraced so that it can be kept by the council of seven.

    keep in mind, thaumaturgy takes practice or a true calling to magic to be learned and also is blood intensive more so than any other discipline with a few exceptions for higher level powers (it always requires blood to be spent).

    This means that Tremere are even more likely to embrace with care, as unlike in other clans, a Tremere has no chance to learn their native disciplines intuitively (with exceptions), thus building on the insular and also very carefully built nature of the clan.

    lastly, as a caveat, though not a hard rule, the Tremere should be called to the path of blood first, as it is the magic over this substance that brought them, as a clan, to vampirism; it is the power which flows within their blood with the greatest primacy. The first step down the path of blood is the divinatory revelation of the true nature of vitae, and for most, it is used to divine the true nature of other vampires; but for a fledgling Tremere, it is almost always first used after a rigorous instruction in the magical arts and entails the magical discovery of the very blood that runs within them. During this first revelation, the vampire learns about their own blood and what its properties are, what vitae truly is, and what it makes them.

    This mystical self discovery is the first step to being able to master not only the magic of the blood, but also, the condition of vampirism itself, and also leads to a significant degree of self discovery as well. Some vampires may even learn from it, in some small subliminal way, the fashion in which the Tremere created themselves as well, and gain intuitive insights in how their clan came to be.

    "As you came into my curse through magic, so too shall the only powers of my blood that you inherit be that magic." Thus spoke Cain in his judgement upon Tremere. Either in a moment of awakening at his blood's transmutation and the ensuing alteration to the the curse itself, or in the times of Gehenna... or perhaps both.

              From a point-value perspective, it could potentially count as one of (if not THE) most devastating clan flaw. In other words, if you were to reduce this to a point value so that other characters could take it at character creation, how much do you think this would be? There is no flaw "One Less Clan Discipline", but the Merit "Additional Clan Discipline" is 5 points; accordingly, I think this would make this probably a 10+ point flaw. There's also the additional question of whether it would be intellectually honest to allow them to even start the game with the 3 basic dots of Disciplines that other vampires get at character creation. Theoretically, every newly-minted vampire just has those three-dots-worth of Disciplines as soon as they're embraced--but they also have in-clans disciplines.

              I've never understood people trying to power balance Tremere. Don't get me wrong, I understand the desire and inclination, but never understood why someone would look at the wild power disparities present across basically every single edition of old school V:tM, written over the course of 20+ years, and not realize that that indifference to imbalance is a feature of the game. In other words, this has never been a game that cared about balancing the clans or Disciplines at all. Potence is indisputably worlds better than its direct counterpart, Fortitude; Celerity is arguably (though I think generally accepted to be) better than both; every level of Auspex is like 3-4 powers in one; most Dominate, Presence, and Auspex powers are free; most Obfuscate powers are not only free, but don't require a roll. Then there are Disciplines that are unapologetically terribly--Valeren comes to mind (or, in Dark Ages lines, Warrior Valeren); the Level 3 and Level 5 are pretty good, but 1, 2, and 4 are hot garbage when scaled in comparison to the average 1st, 2nd, and 4th levels of Disciplines. And it's not like the Salubri Antitribu are a super esoteric clan originally found in the Storyteller's Handbook and aren't really apt to be played by PCs; on the contrary, they're right in the Sabbat book.

              It's not fair at all--but that's sort of the point.

                      Otter, you are both wrong and right.

                      Clan specific disciplines are meant to be very powerful but very niche. All the common disciplines are broadly applicable to undead life, but being able to summon shadows? That's not a great party trick with the masquerade in mind. Obtenebration is more powerful than, say, potence because it's got way more downsides to it. But Auspex struggling against obfuscate or Potence beating out fortitude isn't really an intended feature. Thaumaturgy is in a very weird spot because of this, because it's both very broad and highly specific, and it's the only discipline that isn't strictly linear. Storytellers worry about the potential of it, rather than the reality of it. It doesn't help that Tremere players, like malkavians, are the best and worst of the table, and are more likely to powergame as the wizard. Nosferatu are also great for powergaming, but they're less of a problem because they don't really attract powergamers so much as -all the spells-.

                      I really think it's an out-of-game problem with players.
                      (Honestly, the fact that Tremere is so attractive to powergamers, both in that they've got the spells and the clan culture for it, is probably a canon reason why everyone hates the Tremere)
