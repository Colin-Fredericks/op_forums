                Ahh, I’m sad to hear it. I’ve always been more of a lurker here only posting once every few months, but I always enjoyed your enthusiasm contrasting with the omnipresent dislike of v5 over here.

                It’s been a while since I ran v5, since I’ve been busy building my own RPG system and I have way too many unread v5 book, but it does seem like the good parts of WOD 5 are behind us.

                To me v5 felt like a great pitch meeting. I felt like it had a bunch of interesting new ideas that could have been developed and refined.

                I loved the initial v5 lore and the mysteries they introduced.

                I fell in love with the hunger dice system which I thought was a stroke of genius. It suddenly incorporated vampiric hunger as a mechanic you could feel and visualize instead of relying on some magic points with a very controllable limit.However, some subpart of the system I didn’t quite like, I preferred damage and hit points in Requiem 2nd. I originally liked the discipline powers of v5, but after a while I felt like they were closer to magical spells, or MMO abilities than discipline.In the end I ended up creating my own mix of Requiem and v5 using the parts I loved from both.

                I guess i’ll stay tuned and keep updated on how it all evolves in the future.

                                    Originally posted by Lysander View Post
                                    Sounds like Kindred of the East wasn't to well received by many of the posters here. I take that it had lots of bad stereotypes and stuff that didn't appeal to anyone?
                                    I've always been a big fan of the concept, there's some issues with particular word choices​ and a few ideas that haven't aged very well, but I love the transhumanism and inhuman enlightenment. I also really like the concept of the Yama Kings and how they make White Wolf's Asian setting more crossover friendly, giving a big bad guy that you can use to more easily run mixed Chronicles. Really, Kindred of the East was my favorite White Wolf game, but it was a product of the 90's and the early 2000's, and I can I see why White Wolf wouldn't want to go back to it.

                                    Originally posted by MrornX View Post
                                    Well that and the core book outright said that Asians have different souls to the point where western vampires can't actually embrace them. Or at least something similar. Which is yeah... You can see the unfortunate implications...
                                    I don't know where you got that idea, but it's not true at all. There have always been lots of Asian Kindred, even whole Bloodlines like the Anda. What's different about Asia is that the Yama Kings (powerful spirits) betrayed the Jade Emperor (basically God) tore up huge sections of the Umbra and the Underworld and created kingdoms and then began stealing and torturing the souls of the dead to get power. Sometimes souls can escape from the 1000 Hells and when they do, they emerge as Eastern Kindred. The Jade Emperor created this process so that some sinners can have a second chance at redemption, and the Yama Kings have to deal with their soul-theft coming back to bite them in the ass by creating their most dedicated enemies.

                                    The Eastern Kindred do have a Higher (Hun) and Lower Soul (Po), but so does every human - in VtM the Kindred's lower soul is more active than in normal humans and is the Beast. In Wraith: the Oblivion it gains intelligence and becomes the Shadow. Eastern Kindred's Po operates more like the Shadow and is sentient, but ultimately their soul isn't much different than normal, other than the fact that they went to Hell and crawled back out.

                                    Ultimately though it's a game mechanic. Like with Mummy - they have 7 parts to their soul. But it's not like the game is saying Egyptians have unique souls from everyone else in the world and can't be Embraced or anything like that. Just that the magical system that was used to create Mummies split their soul into 7 different parts, it's not supposed to be saying anything about actual living people.
