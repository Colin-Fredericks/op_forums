    Most of the NPC vampires in my campaign city hover around Humanity 4-5 unless they’re under a century old (those are generally 5-6) or an aberration that proves the rule (there’s a Nos with Humanity 8 who feeds only from animals and keeps no ghouls… he was once a monster, but got a wakeup call,
    found religion; the Cult of Osiris specifically; and has rebuilt his humanity over the last century and now seeks to “balance the scales” before he goes and takes a sunbath).

    The PCs have higher Humanity scores listed than what I consider them to have (and the ones involved both have the blush of health and early riser merits too which basically makes degeneration irrelevant until they drop below a 5 where bearing and virtue caps can start to ding them so it’s not worth the table fight that would result), owing mostly to these players really not giving any deep thought to their action. The short version is they started a blood donation center where volunteers can donate blood to help at the local children’s hospital; except they’re essentially embezzling a good chunk of the donations (to the tune of over a hundred pints a month for multiple vampires and their ghouls) for their own personal use, not just to sustain themselves, one of them is trying to become a pop diva using lots of presence at every performance and justifies burning off the extra blood that takes with the notion that part of the proceeds go to various charitable causes she supports. Having recently turned some of their long time ghouls into vampires they’re also looking to blood bind a brand new batch of mortals in order to advance their interests (which are for the “greater good” obviously).

    I dunno about anyone else, but I’d consider stealing people’s charitable donations for personal use, brainwashing people into buying tickets to your performances using said charitable donations and also planning to enslave people through addiction also using those stolen charitable donations to be rather monstrous even as they tell themselves they’re being humane and even heroic.

    It’s downright hilarious to me because they’re all about what good people they are as they actively shun the few high humanity good NPCs (who make them uncomfortable when they try to get them to be better people) and sidling right up to some of the lowest Humanity ones (who praise their actions even as every last one is planning to backstab them as soon as they’ve gotten what they wanted out of them). It’s all going to blow up in their faces eventually.

                    B) One issue was when to even have them make a Degeneration check and how often should it be repeated if they pull off the first one. When they set up the LLC? The first embezzled blood bag they guzzled? Just one check (if you can rationalize it the first time with the intent of repeating should the original rationalization hold)? Every blood bag? Every ten? Only the vampires who actually came up with the scheme or the ones who got blood bags as a gift without questioning the source?

                    Likewise, if I get too sticky on this I’d realistically have to require a degeneration check every time a vampire takes a blood point by deceit in a nightclub… because I can see the argument that the blood bank embezzling is no worse than stealing blood during a make out session in a dark alcove could argue this is no different and also eliminates any accidental overfeeding risk so is arguably more moral.

                    C) Related to when to do degeneration checks; they haven’t actually started ghouling yet because they’re still looking for candidates. The Embracing was, in one case, of a ghoul well past his mortal lifespan and had accumulated flaws like light sensitivity and their domitor’s clan flaw. The other was spur of the moment as they had been poisoned by an enemy of the Sire-to-be and was moments from death when they drained him and fed him in an effort to “save” him. I DO plan on making them check for degeneration on that unless they are utterly upfront about the consequences of the third drink with whichever candidates are picked.

                    D) The consequences that will bite them has nothing directly to do with degeneration; it’s the company they chose to keep… the ones who appeared charming and seemed like friends who would do favors without any obvious benefit to themselves by doing so that they started trusting without question. These are vampires and when one of these guys came to them with a warning of another vampire plotting against the PC and advised the PCs to strike first; they didn’t even question it… they just started making plans as if it were true.

                    That’s not something them getting degeneration checks for the blood bank is going to alert them to; but it’s more a case of their being unable to recognize that charm, wealth, power and glamour have nothing to do with whether you’re a monster or not.

                    I mean, I can only do so much if they ignore obvious signs. I literally had a thin-blooded seer break into a prophetic trance where they screamed out “Beware the Bronze Man; he brings ruin for us all!” in front of them and then, at Elysium, an extremely attractive Mediterranean vampire with bronzy skin presented himself to the Prince as a goodwill ambassador from the Assamites as the schismatics negotiated for entry.

                    All the warnings went right out the window at the description of his smoldering brown eyes, square jaw and muscles and he became a trusted confidant (the one who gave them the “warning” of the vampire plotting against them actually). Why? Because he was “hot” and the de facto leader of the PC coiterie was envisioning her Anita Blake novels.

                    I’m not deliberately out to get them, but they have somehow systematically picked the worst of the worst among the NPCs as the “good people” they can trust (invariably the elites of vampire society) while distrusting every last NPC who’s tried to warn them of how scheming and evil their associates are. At some point the bill comes due.

Part of this really comes down to the fact that WW never did proper rules for what a mortal hacker could do, so the thaumaturgy path is a bit weird.                             So in the example of a self sustained vampire hive, you would use Path of Conjuring to fabricate the supplies or parts to build the hive and maintain it. Then you use the Path of Technomancy to hack blood banks to send fresh deliveries on a regular basis. After that the Path of Technomancy becomes your long range weapon of choice to manipulate stock markets, change property records, fabricate email leaks, generate endless money through bank hacking, etc.

                            It might not be exciting, but in truth a Coterie could just purchase a decommissioned missile silo and have it converted into a private vampire fortress/luxury apartments/vampire hive in the middle of no where and hack most of the country. Normally this would leave them open to tracing or back hacking, except Path of Technomancy allows you to connect to the internet without using hardware so no IP address and no signal trace.

                                        I think vampires using their powers to "help" people is one of those things that is going to be an essential part of their storytelling at least as part of what V:TM does. Now the game doesn't bother to explain this or lecture you but it's fairly clear that killing all those people has messed with this guys head. You may be able to wipe out 100+ people in the game and not go insane from it (or maybe you're already insane as the kind of person who has killed 100+ people) but the experience has left him in a bad mental space.

                                        If he keeps doing this, he will be lost to the Beast because it's only made him angrier and nastier.

                                        You as a vampire must encourage him to drop his crusade to be a vigilante and try to do something else with his unlife. I always liked that because it added a bit of dirt and muck on what might have otherwise been a more justified story in another game.
