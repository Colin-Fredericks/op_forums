    Oh, I forgot the Thin-Bloods! I have to say, I massively preferred them in V20 and Revised. Those versions of the Thin-Blooded actually made me want to play one--a combination of the Insight Background, the ability to Incept new Disciplines (or spontaneously learn old ones), and the specific balance of weaknesses and strengths.

    I wasn't sold on Thin-Blood Flaws offering a pick and mix approach to the highest Generations, and I just don't like Thin-Blood Alchemy or free Disciplines from drinking blood. This may be tied to how Blood Sorcery is generally not my bag in V5 anyway, however, and the way Resonance turns humans from walking vitae batteries into walking Discipline batteries instead.

    Of course, it's all highly subjective. Maybe my excitement for the 14th-16th Generations came from seeing them for the first time and the cool lore around them. Perhaps if V5 was my intro to the game, I'd be as excited by this new iteration. (Though, the more I think about it, the more I think it was precisely the lore that drew me in, rather than the rules. The lore is different now, and that may explain it.)

            Personally, I actually find the Thin Bloods to be the best addition to the game and I really like Thin Blood Alchemy as an alternative set of powers. The potential for an all-Thin Blood Chronicle is high, although they can be problematic to include in a full Vampire chronicle for various reasons.

            I quite like what they have done with The Ministry also, to a degree, as their inclusion mainly as Anarchs gives a new spin on that sect. The Tzmisce and Ravnos are OK, although I mainly see their uses as NPCs to be honest, along with the Salubri. I haven’t seen any player clambering to play Banu Haqim either, which is another other ‘add-on’ Clan from the core, although at least one player opted for Lasombra in the Chronicle I am currently running and I personally like the Hecata Clan, if I could get to include one moving on.

            Of the ones I find a bit more difficult in their development, I’d say it is the Tremere. Their weakness to make Blood Bonds (and susceptibility) isn’t as much of a disadvantage as being already Blood Bonded to Elders in the Clan I feel and, thematically, it feels strange that they aren’t ensconced in their own ultra-organized pyramid power structure anymore. I think they have become too easy to play.

                I would also like to state that I think the Salubri are the second most improved group of the Clans.
                Why?

                Because I actually think it's possible now to use the Salubri in a standard V:TM horror game and not have them utterly wreck the mood. I've used the Salubri less than virtually every other Clan in the setting and part of that is they simply don't fit the Gothic Punk "personal horror" game of the setting. Being able to heal the injuries of the living and having a flaw of being unable to feed on the unwilling is like a Friendly Neighborhood Vampire Jackpot. You could still kill people during frenzy but they mostly seemed like a boon to those around them and that's terrible if you want to play vampires as a plague to humanity.

                I'm not 100% behind the fact the Salubri are just magically tasty as a weakness now (albeit, what a shitty time for any Salubri who shares blood with an Assamite--NO SEX FOR THESE TWO!). However, the fact that they don't have anything that prevents them from feeding on regular people and their Third Eye bleeds during the use of their Disciplines makes it a pretty decent flaw alternative.

                Its been centuries since the Tremere wiped away most records of the Salubri so the majority of vampires have no idea who the hell they are or what they did. If the Salubri are among Anarch territory, they might as well be claiming they're from Clan Pokemon. That means that they're actually somewhat useful in a long term game if you're in a city that has weak Camarilla presence or is controlled by a cult too.

                I also am going to probably get some flack for the fact that I think that nerfing Obeah was the correct decision. Making it so that it is a power of MENTAL healing is something that is a lot less grandiose than other healing and has a severe consequence by screwing with your soul.

                            Without getting into the other clans, I like what 5e has done with thin-bloods a lot.

                            First, streamlining what they are. In Revised, you had 14th generation vampires with or without the Thin-Blooded Flaw, who may or may not have been Caitiff, giving you four different potential "types" of thin-bloods. 15th-generation vampires who were all Caitiff with the Thin-Blooded Flaw, plus more severe weaknesses, brought that up to five. OPP introducing 16th-generation vampires brought that up to six. While this was interesting from a conceptual standpoint, and gave a sense of evolution (or devolution) to the youngest generations, the distinctions were only likely to be meaningful in games with lots of thin-bloods. It's better to just give each thin-blood a buffet of Merits and Flaws to pick from.

                            The "extra emphasis on Merits and Flaws" approach is more streamlined and gives each thin-blood a lot of customization options. I also like the flavor that past the 13th generation, the rules go out the window, and you never know what powers and weaknesses a given thin-blood is going to manifest. I find that more interesting than each generation growing progressively more feeble in ways predictable enough to have a chart for. Thin-bloods are supposed to be big unknowns and that's harder to maintain when they've been established parts of the setting for 20+ years. (PCs in my current game have met a daywalker thin-blood and they now assume, falsely, this is something all thin-bloods can do.)

                            Daywalker thin-bloods are cool. Revised just had 15th-generation ones take a little longer to crisp, which doesn't have nearly the same conceptual oomph. Losing access to Disciplines, Dracula-style, was a good trade-off to pair that with.

                            Removing frenzy is cool too, and a big way to drive home that these aren't your ordinary vampires. I've seen frenzy come up vastly more often in games than I have sunlight vulnerability. I might have tweaked it so that thin-bloods still frenzy when they are hungry or starving, but as a whole, good direction.

                            Being able to diablerize their way into a clan or become human again is cool. Losing thin-bloodedness via diablerie was already a thing in Revised. I don't see this kind of reward being offered to thin-bloods as often as the setting makes out, though. Maybe among the Anarchs, independents, or Sabbat, but the Camarilla has every reason to discourage diablerie.

                            I like the new "you are what you eat" Discipline system for thin-bloods. Personally, I never saw them walking around with more than a handful of Disciplines; they aren't meant to be supernatural powerhouses. The way V5 does it is more interesting than simply capping how many Disciplines they can have, and also lets a clever thin-blood leverage their weakness into a strength if they know in advance there's a particular Discipline they're going to need. Swapping out Disciplines isn't something ordinary vampires get to do. If someone really wants a given thin-blood to have more Disciplines, make "Permanent Discipline Dot or Two" a Merit they can take.

                            I also like how it preserves (in spirit) their previous inceptor angle. Thin-bloods inventing new Disciplines in Revised was interesting, but Revised also suffered from significant Discipline bloat. There wasn't really a place for thin-bloods to author entirely new ones under the new rules anymore. (I can understand this being a con to people who preferred the old Discipline system, but I loved V5 trimming things down to a core list and instead opening up multiple powers at each level.)

                            I don't see most thin-bloods having Thin-Blood Alchemy, but I dig the gutter magic-slash-"Walter White meth lab" feel to it. We already had chaos magic vampires in the game. Gutter magic is new.

                            What I mainly miss is Insight. That should have been a thin-blood-exclusive Discipline alongside Thin-Blood Alchemy. What happened to it?

                            Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.
