      If they're open to the public? Because it's the safe option. The Sabbat and Anarchs are more open to concepts that are much harder to mesh into a cohesive player group even when everyone is acting in good faith, and if someone's trying to make a character with intentions that they know are going to be unfun for certain people (either specific people or groups of people) it's much easier to "sneak" them in as justifiable concepts that would be draw into the other main factions.

      It's also a lot easier for sandbox style STs because it's the most "defined" faction in how day-to-day (un)life is supposed to function within a Cam domain. A lot of set-in-the-past settings are still pretty Cam-like in their set up which helps players skip doing a few days of homework looking up specifics about a given time and area.

                              Also, it's not so much that anarchs are part of the Camarilla, it is they are part of the Camarilla's milieu. If you play an anarch, you want to fight against the establishment. The establishment is the Camarilla. An anarch is someone in a domain ruled by a prince defying the elders. I say "defying" because it's not true it's always about "fighting" them. It's not 100% violence and combat. Almost by definition, if you play an anarch you'll be playing in a city ruled by the Camarilla. So the Camarilla's structure still applies.

                              Sure, you can run an anarch game set in an anarch domain, but that eliminates something like 90% of why players who want to play anarchs play anarchs. The one published setting of an anarch free state (LABN) does not do a good job making that city interesting to play in. Instead of princes, you have barons. It's Camarilla lite but without the more interesting things about it. And the writers seem to assume that every anarch domain must be exactly like that (even though the whole baron thing was supposed to have grown out of local conditions). No difference in governing philosophies. No variety. There's just an appalling lack of creativity there. If you ever wondering, "Well, what happens after the anarchs win?" That sourcebook seem to say, "The same thing as what happened before."

                              I saw the same thing in LARPs based in anarch domains. Players got bored quickly because the only "plot" that seemed to exist was fighting the Camarilla and the prince. Once that was accomplished, what should they do? Nobody seemed to have any idea. So the STs just went back to creating a "new" plot when every so often some new Camarilla plot/agent would appear so all the players could go against that. Repetitive and boring.

                              It's why I've been creating lots of threads recently on running a chronicle set in the Free States. I'm trying to brainstorm things out so I can have a compelling, interesting chronicle dealing with issues that should be addressed in an anarch domain. Have real distinctions between the types of domain governance each group wants. I have a player who loves playing anarchs, but has almost always played in Camarilla games (because that is what the other players wanted even if his character was an anarch type) and been disappointed in the few anarch games he's been in. So I finally want to give him the experience I think he's been waiting for. Hopefully I will.

                                          Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                          A somewhat more cynical take is that if you want to play a vampire, you want to play either undead mobsters or sexy people in ball gowns not Lost Boys.
                                          A Lost Boys story where the Lost Boys are protagonists could work. It's just that such a movie would need to center some kind of antagonist (if it's not just a Personal Horror story about a gang that spirals into madness and internal conflict due to their individual foibles and vampiric natures). And if there's an antagonist in such a movie, they would need to be either 1) human authorities/hunters, 2) older vampires, 3) a rival gang of Lost Boys, or 4) Something Else.

                                          And that Something Else is generally the place where Anarch games fumble. The first three at least create plots organically. When Anarchs are left to their own devices, figuring out stuff for them to do requires...significantly more creativity and effort. Creativity and effort neither White Wolf nor Paradox seemed able or willing to muster.


                                          It's also the case that when the Anarchs are left to their own devices, the idiosyncrasies of their locale, and the structure (or lack thereof) they adopt, become much more important as the engine of conflict. And that's a lot harder to standardize, without simply recreating the same dynamics of Haves and Have Nots from the Camarilla, but with Barons instead of Princes.

                                          If your Anarch domain runs on a pure democratic council, with every Lick having their own co-equal vote, how do you actually run that? There are ways, I'm sure. How do you design that model of governance as a framework groups can drop into their own games? Pretty hard. The power blocs at play are much more fluid and person-specific than Camarilla titles or even just Baronies would be. So it's hard to generalize.

                                          And if you - the hypothetical designer of a City by Night book where this model is in play - manage to pull off an interesting and playable dynamic, you still run the risk of the particulars of that domain just being copy-pasted to all similar domains. The Chicago By Night problem all over again, where the most popular classic depiction of a Camarilla domain becomes the template everyone replicates.

                                          (Not that this would necessarily be BAD, since you'd have at least one functional model for an Anarch domain, that isn't just Camarilla-lite. It would get repetitive, though.)

                                          Magestuck: The Ascension

                                              The best thing that could happen to Anarch domains as a concept would be a book devoted to explore a variety of options. Something similar to a By Night, but with four to six unrelated cities instead of just one.
