    Originally posted by Microcuchon View Post
    I am 100% with the OP. I my games Vicissitude has also disappeared as a Discipline and Tzimisce run with Koldunic Sorcery or Protean instead (using a caste like system). Vicissitude has remained as a Koldunic Sorcery Path, only affecting the caster's own body, removing it more grotesque uses. So now the Tzimisce is able to change their appearance, mimic some other person or even become a combat monster if they wish so, but vozdhs, human shaped screaming chairs and the like are out of the picture
    So, at that point, why bother calling it a Tzimisce? What you have there sounds closer to a Tremere, or maybe a Setite, with some appearance altering rituals (some of which are already in game). I'm not sure I understand the appeal of choosing a clan you don't like and twisting it into something you do like by changing it's nature completely...though I appreciate the delicious allegory, considering the clan in question. Are you more interested in the soil-dependency weakness, or the in-clan cost of Protean? Or maybe just making a Tremere without the pyramid?

    Just seems like there are easier ways to meet that concept with the tools already presented in game rather than taking away from a clan's established lore and flavor. It's perfectly okay to not like a clan, but changing it to match more closely with another clan seems like a waste of effort when those clans already exist. Just my opinion, of course.

                It has occurred to me that the four original revenant families more or less embody the clan's four major archetypes.
                • Brutal rural barbarians who view the world entirely in a predator vs prey dynamic with themselves at the top of the food chain and enjoy keeping/creating monstrous "pets".
                • Decadent hedonistic aristocrats who treat other people as their playthings and engage in bizarre fetish behavior including excessive body modification.
                • Reclusive creepy mystics who keep delving into forbidden lore, ancient witchcraft and things man was not meant to know.
                • Old World nobility who keep with antiquated codes of honor and behavior while caught up in a dangerous tangled web of alliances and obligations.

                (And in hindsight, I rather wish the Grimaldi had had Animalism, Auspex, and Dominate as their family discipline affinities. Not only would it have made them stand out a little less, anyone who liked Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand could've made the obvious parallel with the Old Clan and speculated if they were secretly their agents or something).

                    Originally posted by Garygeneric View Post
                    I think assigning Dracula's clan as Tzimisce did a disservice to Dracula, Tzimisce and the game in general. Quite a few clans embodied at least as many Dracula aspects as does Tzimisce; Gangrel, Lasombra, Nosferatu, Tremere, Ventrue...hell, even Brujah and Ravnos have distinct Dracula aspects. They should have left his clan an item of contention, with multiple clans claiming and/or blaming, as the case fit. Trying to make the Tzimisce clan fit the man or vice versa leaves something less than the sum of its parts.

                    But it's worth noting that in the books, Dracula is not a typical Tzimisce, and is disliked/scoffed at by most of the clan.
                    Frankly, Dracula never should have been made a canon character. He feels far more of an amalgam of several clans, and that's what he should have been in-universe: someone's grand Masquerade breach that called out a bunch of different clans' abilities to spread knowledge far and wide.

                    But of all the clans he could have been, Tzimisce is the weirdest, considering body horror flesh-shaping is one of the powers he very clearly does not have.

                                    Originally posted by Dogstar View Post

                                    I've got to disagree with you on this, that certain powers are innately evil and that using them makes you evil. It's an overly simplistic approach to morality (see examples here: Bad Powers, Bad People - TV Tropes), and prevents any interesting nuance from becoming a story.

                                    As an example compare the one ring in Lord of the Rings with the Dresden files cursed coins. Everyone in LotR treats the one ring like toxic waste and anyone who uses it inevitably pays for it. Whereas in the Dresden files the cursed coins are treated by everyone as toxic waste, but when Harry picks one up we have a several book 'struggle against temptation' story that ends really well.

                                    Enforcing that certain powers inevitably corrupt their user takes away player agency, and to me, a good story comes from giving players the agency to struggle with the moral nuances of powers that could corrupt them.

                                    See alos: Evil Makes You Ugly - TV Tropes

                                    I agree with many things you said here, as I said in a post somewhere around here once, I think even vampires who got demonic investments and/or Daimoinon/Dark Thaumaturgy could "redeem" themselves and/or use the "evil powers" to fight "evil", whatever it might mean. The idea of the One Ring though, is that, to simplify things, it kind of gives "absolute power" to certain powerful beings, so as Gandalf said, he would turn into a terrible tyrant if he had it even though his intentions were good. In any case, that happens because the idea of "absolute power" inevitably corrupts the user, but then again it becomes kind of a philosophical discussion. The thing is, in my opinion, to have certain powers should not inevitably corrupt the user, otherwise, as you pointed out, it would take away player AND ST agency.
