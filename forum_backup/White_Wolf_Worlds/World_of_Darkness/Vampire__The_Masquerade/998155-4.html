                                I wasn't sure where this accusation of sexism came from either, but also not entirely sure why weapons charts are that big a deal.

                                Anyway, I'm not really sure what I think about the book, so far. It kind of feels like how I felt about the Dark Ages 20th book in some ways. I really loved the style, the art, the visuals, but wasn't as sold on the writing. In this case, and this is completely subjective, I didn't really care for the vast majority of the magical sections dealing with Thaumaturgy styles. I don't know, I can't place my finger(s) on it, but it seemed like a large break from prior concepts and material for the sake of trying to push something new, but also comes off, at least on early reading as largely unplayable and unusable.

                                Borderline like trying to get someone's own home game into published canon. But I'm not sure that's right, or to what extent, and there is certainly some degree of not liking change, especially as contradictions to, (or not knowing of), prior material. Some of it, like with the Koldun, I felt was a backwards step (and like I mentioned, gets very close to making it unusable), and I literally thought as I was reading it, "someone is trying to make Sith Vampires".

                                I think my biggest gripes are that I think this book really just wasn't for me. It seemed to overly focus on things I didn't really care much about and barely (if at all) touch on things I would love to see ten times more of, (Knightly Orders and Holy things come to mind).

                                Despite my original reservations, I actually really liked the rules and thought put into the Knightly Fighting style/maneuvers, and I'd like to see more of this sort of thing all around, (doesn't need to be combat orientated). Little things that can help personalize characters.

                                At the same time, considering that I got it for free, in a sense, I can't complain too much. I don't care for it much, or the direction that the Vampire 20th line seems to be heading, but, perhaps once I read it again and more that might change?

                                    Originally posted by The Gentleman Gamer View Post
                                    I appreciate historical accuracy, to a degree, but I also want to play Vampire. I think it's fine to accept that some elements of reality will need to take a trip out the window.
                                    And hasn't that always been stated about WoD, in all the game lines? That WoD is more of a dark dismal echo of our own reality.

                                    In the end it's all about suspension of disbelief though. To some an anachronistic weapontable destroys that, for others it is errors in descriptions of social structures in a certain culture or era. It's hard to get every detail right.

                                    Historians make a difference between Cultural Heritage and Actual History. Most of the history we know collectively is actually cultural heritage, not actual history. So I do think that even though we should expect the writers to be fairly correct in historical assumptions, they deserve some leeway. It is very easy to mix up history you thought was actual history with cultural heritage. Because you will make subconscious assumptions where it is blank.

                                    I assume that's what happened here. Sadly. But it's really no big deal.

                                    It would have been nice to see a more accurate weapon table, or even chapter. More like in the Revised ST companion but for DAV. But Tome of Secrets don't feel like it would have been the right place for that.

                                    But... Do you know what this means? There's still room for OP to make even more books for the game! There's still content we need

                                    As for ToS itself I've only skimmed the pdf. I'm a traditionalist and prefer to lean back with my hardcover copy and take it all in. But some few words I can share is...

                                    1. I dislike that it keeps following the tradition of the streamlined rules for Thaumaturgy found in DAV20 and Rites of Blood, because it ignores and/or simplifies a lot of great content without obvious purpose.

                                    2. I lean towards really liking the Abyss and Koldun parts though. The Abyss stuff feels like a great tool for storytelling, so very useful for me as an ST even though PC's won't find much use for it most likely. But that's just as it should be as I see it. This is the Tome of Secrets, not the Tome of General Knowledge. And I don't mind the Sith-iness of Koldunism, since I find it works greatly with how I've used Tzimisce/Old Clan and Kolduns in my games. And it fits greatly with other V20 material on the subject imho. Also loved the Harbinger connection.

                                    3. The knight and war stuff seems to work great, at least from my superficial understanding. Previous systems have been lacking to say the least so this could be very helpful.

                                    4. More faith stuff? I am ambivalent here. Is it necessary? Maybe not. Does it help? Probably, considering how important religion was back then. So yeah, why not?

                                            Originally posted by Beckett View Post
                                            Anyway, I'm not really sure what I think about the book, so far. It kind of feels like how I felt about the Dark Ages 20th book in some ways. I really loved the style, the art, the visuals, but wasn't as sold on the writing. In this case, and this is completely subjective, I didn't really care for the vast majority of the magical sections dealing with Thaumaturgy styles. I don't know, I can't place my finger(s) on it, but it seemed like a large break from prior concepts and material for the sake of trying to push something new, but also comes off, at least on early reading as largely unplayable and unusable.
                                            This is very much how I feel about the Tome of Secrets. It is very well presented, and feels like it should be a useful book, but I have yet to find a single section which I feel actually enriches the game. One problem is that the sections on magic, which take up a large part of the book, present their contents in a fairly generic manner. Take Abyss Mysticism, for instance; the section discussing this starts with four paragraphs on "The Horrors of Abyss Mysticism". Whilst this is not a bad notion in itself, said paragraphs amount to no more than telling us that the Abyss is a thing of horror. As the section tells us of the Lasombra:
                                            Their arts of shadow manipulation and drawing forth the primordial darkness rightly infect their foes with fear.

                                            What is missing is any material to let us know what is so terrible about the Abyss and its darkness. There is nothing here which is sufficiently substantial to let the reader know why the enemies of the Lasombra should fear the darkness they are conjuring forth, rightly or otherwise. Not only is the writer not showing us, the readers, but the writer is barely even telling us, with a description that doesn't even touch the contextual background of the period. This, I think, is one reason why some readers are not connecting with the sections on magic; whilst a large proportion of older material for Vampire: the Dark Ages was background-led, with rules designed to actively bring the background into the game, these sections are very much rules-led, with a small amount of background to explain their inclusion in the book. The result is text which feels flat an insufficiently connected with the game it is designed to support.

                                                Originally posted by Beckett View Post
                                                Borderline like trying to get someone's own home game into published canon. But I'm not sure that's right, or to what extent, and there is certainly some degree of not liking change, especially as contradictions to, (or not knowing of), prior material. Some of it, like with the Koldun, I felt was a backwards step (and like I mentioned, gets very close to making it unusable), and I literally thought as I was reading it, "someone is trying to make Sith Vampires".
                                                I'll confess - as the writer of the Koldunic Sorcery section - to not actually liking or being terribly familiar with Star Wars. Never got on with the series. I found it horribly bland. Any similarities between Koldun and Sith is purely coincidental. My intention with that section (where Koldunic Sorcerers jealously guard their power) stems back to Tzimisce territoriality, the idea of the crotchety old mage in his tower, and to act as a reason for Tzimisce disunity when opposing the more organised and cohesive Tremere. I feel it stands up.

                                                By dint of my creating a lot of the material in this book from scratch, it is indeed effectively homebrew, but not material lifted from a home game. Everything I write attempts to expand the setting or elaborate on existing material - whether in major (we've got seeds of Harbinger of Skulls and Setite history in this book) or minor (Abyss Mysticism is likely only useable by Lasombra) ways. I attempt to be incredibly reverent to old material, mind you, and am well-versed in it.

                                                I'm more than comfortable with some readers being unhappy with my content, of course. I also never mind being questioned on what I've written I write for players and Storytellers like me, and the members of my groups.
