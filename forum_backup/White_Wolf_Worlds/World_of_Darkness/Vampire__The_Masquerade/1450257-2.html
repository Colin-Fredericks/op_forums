    I'm going to hop in on the side of "Bardo isn't just a cheat-code for Humanity that invites abuse." I think partly it has to do with the fact that we are reflecting on a game mechanic, and it is easy to look at it as abusable from that perspective, like many game mechanics. But as people have noted, getting a Conscience re-roll, In Character, ought to represent authentic moral reflection. If the game mechanic is being abused, the Storyteller ought to intervene. But I don't think that the Discipline, In Character, invites the kind of abuse you describe, or represents an especially pernicious way for people to do bad things and avoid the psychological/spiritual consequences. This is a game where Elders creating childer and grandchilder to do dirty work for them in order to avoid Humanity loss is considered a feature of the setting; Bardo is just a more personal and mystical way of achieving the same result. Except it's also an occult secret held by a group of religious ascetics who make it their business to destroy other vampires that fail to live up to the standards of their cult. I also don't think that it being "artificial" is especially problematic. The vampires in question might struggle with Humanity, but they clearly care enough to try and maintain it, and I don't think seeking out some external support invalidates the effort. I think an analogy might be made to mental illness; some people need a supportive community to make it work, some people might need medication to handle their worst symptoms. Bardo is a spiritual prescription against the Beast, from that perspective.

            Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
            So when examining the Children of Osiris and their potential role in the game, we shouldn't ask how we can change them to fit into pre-existing molds. Instead, we should ask ourselves "What can the Children of Osiris do that other groups cannot or would not? And how do we leverage that into unique plot hooks?"
            I agree that is a really good angle to use as an ST. And you brought up a lot of good points (as did Elphilim) on the most salient aspects of the Children. In short, they are active participants in the Jyhad and taking out their defined enemies (the Setites mostly, but probably some of the most inhuman Sabbat on certain Paths of Enlightenment, and possibly some individuals of the Camarilla) but from the other side of the Humanity trait than what we're used to. I like the idea that their effectively a wild card in vampire society because so much of what they do ignores what are supposed to be the defining marks of vampires.

            An issue for many STs is that after some of the excesses of 1e and early 2e, they are keen not to have "vampire superheroes" who effectively ignore the damnation of being undead, or used to pull the PCs bacon out of the fire. So that has to be avoided. But an easy mistake to then make is to make them stereotypical tortured souls, which is another trope that is easily overused, or a mere plot device to rescue the PCs from their enemies. So getting the portrayal right is a minefield.

            A good example might be like monks in the Dark Ages desperately preserving the tattered remnants of lost knowledge in a more barbarous age. In this case though, the knowledge is the teachings of Osiris. And not just preserving them, but setting an example for others. They probably have some knowledge of Golconda and meet a lot of prospects in Golconda seekers, but would present it as a practical issue ("We don't know if Golconda is true, but we do know that Bardo - and the teachings of Osiris - work".)

            I think one of the keys in their portrayal is that they are an extremely tiny sect with few members. Therefore while they are a danger to their enemies, they can be easily crushed and therefore need to minimize their exposure and maximize their secrecy. I would portray them as a hard target, but so few and so secret, their enemies are more likely to forget about them and move on. They are also helped in that they would be one of the few vampire sects with allies among other supernaturals.

            Also, they reason they "hunt" their enemies is not so much a proactive measure (i.e. we really don't like evil vampires and eliminate them because we're good), but a reactive one (i.e. there are a lot of other vampires that don't like what we represent and are trying to kill us, so we selectively kill them so we don't get eliminated ourselves.) They kill threats because they are in a war with dangerous enemies, not generic bad guys who don't know or care about them. That means if they ever show up in a chronicle, it should be for a sufficiently compelling reason for them. They shouldn't be there just to be there.

            So the question now becomes why are their Temples and Bardo a threat, and who seeks to kill them? Obviously the Setites are an enemy for historical reasons. This a blood feud. The Baali would be another likely enemy (the Baali are pretty much at war with everyone).

            Another enemy are those Paths of Enlightenment most opposed to what the Children of Osiris and their philosophy represent. Path of Evil Revelations is certainly among them. I would also consider adherents of the Path of Caine, Path of Blood, and Path of Night to see the Children of Osiris as infidels to be destroyed. Possibly the Path of Lilith, but I think that really depends on the kind of Lilith her followers envision. Some may not think of the Children as an enemy, merely deluded and ignore them so the Children would do the same. It really comes down to the intolerance level of the Path for vampires to choose to be something very different from them, and how dangerous they think the philosophy of Osiris represent to their teachings.

            The Giovanni or a faction within them might be another, if we consider Osiris as a wraith king in one of the Far Shores and/or Dark Kingdom. There is probably a Heretic group of Wraiths loyal to Osiris, and this group could run afoul of some plans of the Giovanni. Also the possibility that the Children might host some of the surviving Cappadocians who joined the cult earlier.

                Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                A good example might be like monks in the Dark Ages desperately preserving the tattered remnants of lost knowledge in a more barbarous age. In this case though, the knowledge is the teachings of Osiris. And not just preserving them, but setting an example for others. They probably have some knowledge of Golconda and meet a lot of prospects in Golconda seekers, but would present it as a practical issue ("We don't know if Golconda is true, but we do know that Bardo - and the teachings of Osiris - work".)
                Agreed. Individual members of the Children of Osiris may have different views about Bardo. Not surprising, since they have a lot of time to meditate upon it, and contemplate its meaning.

                For some, Bardo is an end unto itself. This is why I disagree with CTPhipps about Osirans "throwing themselves off the path of Golconda". The notion presupposes that Golconda is the only goal worth seeking. Rather, Bardo is a different path altogether, based on a different set of value judgements. If you merely desire to keep the Beast at bay without losing your Humanity, Bardo answers this need. Some vampires just want to continue being immortal, while doing as little harm as possible.

                For others, Bardo represents the ultimate stopgap measure. They may think that Golconda is possible, but that the possibility of achieving it does little good for them in the immediate sense. They're still vulnerable to degeneration, and they still must take blood from humans in order to survive. Unless or until Golconda is achieved - and possibly not even then - they must still contend with the Beast. Bardo insulates them from this threat, allowing them to maintain a minimal harm unlifestyle for the foreseeable future. Moreover, unlike Golconda - a state the books have always kept vague, mysterious, and mythical - we KNOW Bardo works. We have game mechanics for it. It's no big stretch to assume that the Children of Osiris are very aware of its efficacy.

                While the search for Golconda is ongoing, a Child of Osiris can use Bardo to buy time.

                There is, of course, the very real possibility that Bardo and Golconda are mutually exclusive mystical states. That you cannot follow one path without abandoning the other. This very well may be true. If it is, some Osirans may doggedly persist in believing they can have their cake and eat it too. Characters are allowed to be mistaken. Others, by contrast, may be aware of this possibility, and either accept that they won't be able to achieve Golconda, or work to follow it by proxy.

                There are two possibilities in the latter case. One, the Osiran works almost as a vampiric Bodhisattva, not pursuing their own transcendence in order to help other vampires seek Golconda for its own sake. Two, the Osiran uses other characters as a test case, following their progress with the hopes that they can follow the path themselves later, once they have a better idea of how it is done. The former is hardly sinister at all, while the latter is only potentially manipulative, since the Osiran is throwing someone else into the spiritual lion's den in hopes of riding their coattails. A given character may be motivated by both.

                The above scenario may give reason as to why an Osiran NPC might avoid teaching a PC Bardo. They need the character to be solidly OFF the path of Osiris, if they're to follow Golconda unimpeded. Whether the NPC is open about this, or is not forthcoming about it, depends on their motivations and the role the ST wants for them. If they're intended to be an honest force, they may give the PC the choice of learning Bardo, with the understanding they may never achieve Golconda if they do.

                                    Children of Osiris Revised

                                    Nickname: Cultists (vulgar), Osirians (formal)

                                    Disciplines: Animalism, Blood Sorcery (Bardo), Presence

                                    Description: The Children of Osiris are a group that are known about among Kindred and usually derided or existing on the fringes of vampire society. They take in Caitiff, Thin Bloods, and those on the run from the Camarilla or Anarchs.

                                    Those who join the Children of Osiris are very rarely ever seen again and this contributes to people choosing not to seek them out save as a last resort. Most Kindred believe, correctly, the Osirians are a religious cult that will not let you leave if you join them. That is, if you are allowed to survive and join their ranks in the first place. The Osirians are, in simple terms, a Humanities cult.

                                    They revere the "concept" of Humanity and despise being a vampire like many other Blood Cults but they take the concept in a bizarre direction. Its members practice monasticism, self-denial, flagellation, and drinking from one another as well as animals at the higher generations to avoid ever harming humans. They also drink from their fellow Kindred that they hunt.

                                    The Children of Osiris have exacting standards on what they consider to be proper etiquette among the undead. Only those willing to completely empty their minds and agree to become members of the cult according to their beliefs are allowed to become full members. The rest are staked then fed until they can exact enough blood to feed the group for some time until they leave them for the sun as sacrifices.

                                    As such, the Camarilla considers the Children of Osiris to be a hostile degenerate cult of cannibals even though they don't officially practice diablerie. Surprisingly, they have avoided complete destruction by the Camarilla due to the fact that many Princes consider what they do to be a service that leads away the weak minded of their domains. The Children of Osiris are never stupid enough to actually attempt to overthrow the power structures of vampirekind but exist as an "alternative" on the fringes of more popular domains. The Anarchs and Sabbat are not so forgiving with many leading overt attacks on their group havens, often with great casualties ensuing. The Children of Osiris are strong from the blood they drink, freely share disciplines, and often possess fanatical loyalty to each other.

                                    The Osirians practice their own Egyptian-flavored form of Blood Magic called Bardo that provides them rituals to regain lost Humanity as well as make animal blood more palatable. Those who observe these rituals wonder if the Osirians are actually brainwashing their followers while mentally binding them to specific behaviors. Others believe that their magic is a boon to Kindred society and wish it was more freely available.

                                    There is some suggestion that the Children of Osiris are a creation of the Red List member, Kemintiri. The ancient may have created the cult and their magic as a way to undermine the rest of Kindred society. If so, it has not yet been proven and she may have abandoned her weapon long ago.
