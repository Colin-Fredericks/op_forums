    Vampire vs. Mortals: Relative Power Levels

    04-25-2021, 10:37 PM
    I'm fine-tuning the latest (and final) version of my homebrew rules, and I was wondering what all the other GMs out there thought about relative power levels between vampires (particularly but not exclusively PCs) and mortals.

    My general view is that even fairly inexperienced PC vampires should be able to curbstomp your average beat cop, detective, or private security (at least at night). The challenge of such encounters shouldn't be in the combat, but in the context. Indeed, I generally think that level 4 or 5 disciplines with "instant kill" on mortals are calibrated just about right.

    But I'm open to the idea -- and would love to hear more about -- games in which vampires are more "powered down" so that a run-in with a bunch of random street toughs is a moderate challenge. I'm particularly curious in what sorts of games (horror vs. politics vs. drama vs. high violence, etc.) various power levels manifest themselves.

          Well, in my games mortals can beat you, if you are not a physical character. Yes, you can blood surge, of course, and you can use your powers. But, pure fighting, it's not that clear-cut. If the PC was an old librarian and he is fighting a member of a SWAT team, they can be sure it is going to end badly for them if they won't use the powers of the blood to neutralize the target somehow.

          In my current game, the PCs are a tattoo artist, a psychology professor, and a zoologist. With vampiric powers, they can surely remove mortal obstacles. But a one-on-one fight? I am not so sure about it, it keeps challenges interesting and tasking on the PCs' resources and forces them to think smart.

          An important note here though. This concept vastly depends on your campaign and the theme that you are looking to express. We usually play street level, neonate, campaigns, where everything is new and frightening with a lot of personal issues the characters need to deal with in their former lives. So making mortals a considerable threat enforces the overall impact on them in my games. But, in other games that focus more on vampiric politics and wars maybe mortals can be made to become more of a resource or an afterthought to boost the theme of immortal predators, and the triviality of mortal in the grand scheme of vampiric society.

              What edition are you playing and how simulationist do you want your combat? are we looking for the spirit of the rules or the actual rules? Do people have the time, resources and information to prepare or are we dropping them naked into the andes for a deathmatch?

              Because Cheese is going to happen.
              A Lasombra in Tenebrous form is going to wipe out an army, with tanks and helicopters, unless they work out they need to use incendiary weapons or somehow have a source of Faith or Agravated damage with them.
              If we don't look at rules and just take 'the spirit of potence' and a simulationist approach to armour and weapons, A vampire with potence could conceivably cover himself with enough steel that he remains impervious to small-arms, and move around like he isn't weighted down by more than his actual bodyweight.

              Excusing cheese; imagine a vampire gets shot several times. Do we account for the shock value that he can get up again after taking two bullets through the head? Normal soldiers are going to panic and run seeing that, and even special forces might not take that so well.

              A vampire is going to be more agressive in a gunfight, just because he knows for certain he can take a few hits. Potence/Fortitude also means he could conceivably run around with a more devastating weapon, like a medium machinegun. Celerity means he's going to shoot first, or perhaps the vampire can replicate full-auto fire with a semi-auto weapon. His auspex also means he can probably see better at range without the inconvenience of a scope, and Eyes of the Beast is just decisively better than modern night vision.

              Vampires just have an overwhelming advantage in gunfights and melee/brawl. Melee/Brawl is especially devastating because they just have too much power in a relatively small body. In an unarmed fight, two VS one is an extreme advantage, but if the vampire is durable enough to completely ignore a weaker opponent, or strong enough that being held down isn't that much of an impedement, shit will fly.

              Edit: similarly, with melee weapons, humans are at a massive disadvantage. If you set up a sabre duel between vampire and mortal, the vampire is going to win unless the difference between their skills is extreme. A realistic sword fight is going to be over before the third move, but with a vampire, they can act as if they're fully armoured, because it doesn't matter if they get their belly sliced open or their tendons cut, they can still fight. So a lot of the time they'll see an attack or a feint, realize 'ha that won't do much to me', and go all in on attack. Better yet there's the chance of the human going "Ah I skewered him, fight over" before the vampire counters. Unless the sword is burning with holy fury or something, the vampire can simply drink from the corpse made and recover fully from any wounds incurred.
              This is all, well, mostly it doesn't need saying because it's already said concerning guns, but the part that makes things real lopsided is if you start armouring each side with chain or plate armour. When you fight someone in such armour, you either need to bludgeon the enemy with a lot of force or stab them, trying to cut through armour doesn't work. What's the least effective kind of damage on vampires? Stabbing, because the surface area of the damage is small, which is why guns do bashing to vampires. What's the next least effective way to hurt vampires? Concussive force, because they're tough and... half bashing damage. Furthermore they're not going to bleed to death and aren't going to worry about exhaustion.. and this is before we get to disciplines. A Vampire Knight is almost invincible among mortals, assuming they don't shoot him with artillery. or point-blank his head with an arquebus (the latter depends on fortitude levels, and both are extremely difficult to pull off)

              Of course, if a vampire is caught with his pants down, his enemy outnumbers him (as humans often do) and they have sufficient equipment and decent strategy, the humans are very likely to win. It's not often that vampires run around in lots of steel (although carrying a few concealable plates is wise. Vampires, not needing to breath and having access to powers like potence, aren't as worried about comfort as humans, it's mostly about concealment)

                      My expectations are in terms of the genre. At the same time, most vampire fiction visualizes one lone vampire and a group of mortals opposing him. As a group RPG, the PCs are generally in a group which makes running any kind of hunter plot doomed to fail. After many failed attempts to do a hunter plot right, I decided it's best to only use against NPCs as a background setting element. (UNLESS something in the PC's background or personal actions leads to building a personal plot just for that PC which is less about killing the PC, than building a prolonged story with those elements with the mortals usually only succeeding in thwarting some of the PC's plans rather than killing him, but also making it hard for the PC to destroy the hunters. If the PC tries to involve other PCs with their problem, I have the mortal hunters disappear. Needless to say, you need the right kind of player for this work.)

                      In general I would want the following results for each scenario assuming one vampire:

                      1) Unknowing mortal at night that interacts with vampires get defeated
                      2) Knowing mortal at night has a chance based on specifics, but will most likely be defeated
                      3) Unknowing mortal during day that stumbles across the vampire may get defeated or may escape
                      4) Knowing mortal during day destroys the vampire

                      That's very general and the specifics might change things. For example, the more mortals there are, the more likely they may escape rather than be defeated because they can help one another out.

                      I think lone mortals hunting vampires are very risky. They risk too much unless they are some special character like Blade who is more like a superhero.

                      Most successful hunts occur when there is a group of mortals. And said group of mortals do not need to have access to anything ridiculous like Sorcery or Numina. Ordinary people with perhaps minimal True Faith should be able to destroy a vampire that they know about. It's determining that vampires exist and X character is a vampire that is hard. The group that destroyed Dracula in the book consisted of a psychiatrist, a petty noble, a cowboy, a lawyer, a medical doctor with some occult knowledge, and one of Dracula's victims. That kind of group should be able to kill most active vampires (as opposed to Methusalehs slumbering in their hidden lairs).

                      Organized hunter groups like the Society of Leopold should almost always be successful against lone vampires. They're the only ones who could conceivably take on a group of vampires and destroy them all. They also make the most arrangements to minimize risk to themselves.

                      Any mortal hunting vampires should only do so during the day. At night, they should only observe, preferably at a distance.

                                  The "vampires are predators so they need to be more physically powerful than their prey" thing strikes me as a weird metric. Most predators would get their assess handed to them by their prey in a standup fair fight. That's why predators generally prefer ambush or shock based strategies that avoid as much direct conflict as possible. That's why predators tend to end up eating the young, the old, and the infirm: they're easier than an average healthy adult.

                                  To me, the metric should be far more in the narrative of vampires as monsters. Vampires need to be able to be physical powerhouses compared to humans, because they're monsters and humans need to justifiably shit themselves once they realize what they're really up against (even if non-combat focused vampires might be bluffing a bit and can't actually keep it up all that long).

                                                      It is possible (just possible) that a capable mortal could dispatch a vampire, or at least drive them into torpor, if the vampire does not have inherently impressive physical attributes, can only blood buff one point per round, and the mortal is able to avoid their fangs. The vampires' advantages come in the inherent hardiness of their bodies (automatically halving bludgeoning/firearm damage, soaking lethal weapons), and that the Beast does some of the work when making a bite attack (difficulty for this maneuver is lower delivering than a simple strike). Older vampires are almost always stronger, fastier and hardier than living humans but Fledgelings and young Neonates are not necessarily so different from the mortal they once were (then again, it's not been unknown for once-ordinary men or women to awaken into the night with strength raised to 4 or 5.... per the openess of character creation, it can be said there are many permutations of the Embrace. And then sometimes vampires remain physically unimpressive despite decades of unlife). There are too many possible variables for a less generalized answer. An unarmed human is scarcely a threat to a vampire, but one with a stake or a broadsword who knows how to wield it, or a shotgun loaded with dragon's breath rounds, better be taken seriously.

                                                      More broadly, I run my WoD a little pulpier than some. Champion martial artists or special forces soldiers may be capable of quite amazing things, despite having no "supernatural" edge. A cocky fledgeling might want to think twice before going toe-to-toe with such an exceptional mortal. But these are rare and if they're interacting with Kindred at all, are probably already some vampire's ghoul or prized asset. More common threats such as gangbangers, beat cops or bar-room rowdies are closer to normal and can generally be dispatched with little trouble even by a fledgeling with a few dots in Brawl.

                                                      Of course this is discounting Disciplines which can make fighting out of the question. Mortals have virtually no chance of resisting Presence or Dominate, and vampires with these powers scarcely need to condescend to blades or fisticuffs.

                                                      e: FWIW - in V20, Vampires "always" start with more dots to distribute in Attributes than mere Humans. But I see this as reflecting the game's presumption that starting characters will be Neonates, some years or even over a decade out from their Embrace. For a just-created Fledgeling Vampire, I would consider using the "normal" human spread (6/4/3, 11/7/5).
