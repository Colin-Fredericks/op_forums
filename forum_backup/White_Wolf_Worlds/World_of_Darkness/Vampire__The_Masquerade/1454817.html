    [2nd Edition oWOD] How to create a horrifying grappler/biter?

    07-13-2021, 08:54 AM
    Hi,

    My idea for new character is a vampire who is absolutely terrifying when it comes to close quarter combat. Once he gets his hands on you - you are done. I don't want to abuse it, but I want others to fear my character reputation as absolute savage when it comes to fight and with that - my character has respect is harsh underground of gang-wars city.

    We play 2nd edition so no cost auto-success Potence is in play and 1 BP for full Celerity extra actions too.

    So I have few ideas:

    1. Classic Brujah, start with 3 Potence, 1 Celerity + Maw merit. High DEX + WITs for Initiative. Action: Hold, Celerity 1 for Bite STR+2 Agg dmg, min.5 agg. For extra discipline at start I thought Fortitude
    2. Lamia, Potence 3, Path of the Four Humours/Deimos 2 (Kiss of The Dark Mother). Maw Merit. High Dex + WITs. Action: Hold, next turn bite with Dark Mother Kiss for min of... 10 agg damage! That's enough to probably one-shot almost everything. For extra discipline at start I thought of Celerity, though it makes this character glass cannon...
    3. I also though about Brujah but with added Serpentis 2 for Tongue + Celerity attacks and Serpentis 3 can be great replacement of Fortitude investment, however I really think Hold has huge merit instead of just attacking because it prevents enemy from doing anything else but trying to free himself. But I might be wrong and it's better than Hold->Bite combo.

    For merits I know Maw is the must for +1 extra damage from bite, Ability Apptitude (Brawl) is also great for that Hold and Bite attacks.

                          There was one for big hands or long fingers, another for extra nasty bite, and another that lets you slip out of grapples easier (slimy? Lizard limbs?) which might be really good when you find you've grappled more than you can chew.
                          While Auspex is defo one of the -must have- disciplines that everyone should pick up eventually, usually you'll have more Obfuscate than they have Auspex, at least if it's an in-clan discipline, and I'm not sure about 2e rules specifically but the rules are pretty lopsided in favour of the stealthy character than the person looking for them in later editions.

                          Animalism is severely underrated. Presence is powerful, but 1 is poorly worded in most editions and your ST may make it godly or shit. If you're using 2, you're not grappling, and using 3 is messy. 4 is nice and five is particularly interesting from the point of view of "I'm grappling this guy, stay out of it".but at this point you're spending lots of XP on stuff that isn't grappling, and presence is both blood expensive and unreliable in comparison to Dominate and Animalism. Animalism 1/2 in contrast, are godly assists for the grappler. Grappling excells 1 vs 1, or 3 vs 2, but it's terrible when you're even slightly outnumbered, because you're really leaving yourself exposed while you've got two arms on a guy. Dogs are the most normal choice for fighting with animalism. If it's just you and a single dog, it can hamstring your single victim while you're grappling them, greatly increasing your odds. If it's you+dog vs two human opponents, the dog can take on one foe while you take care of another, or they can deter the second foe from getting involved in your 1v1 fight, or the dog can scare the shit out of both opponents and you simply need to run them down. If they've got a gun, the more-threatening dog can draw fire while you close the distance with your foe like a lunatic to grapple them. When you have animalism 2, you can call a horde of dogs. Animalism can do more at low levels and is more reliable than the high levels; it's generally not a good idea to Entrance a lot of people, because they get out of that and grow to hate you, but you can Feral whispers every dog in the neighbourhood to defend you whenever you call them with animalism 2. Other than dogs, there are more exotic/fun options for a grappler, like keeping a snake on your person to bite your foes when you've got them grappled, or you can hold people in place for the rat hordes to devour them

                                              Nosferatu, take ECD Presence. If you absolutely, positively must go with the maw merit, it then serves double duty as it provides extra dice to Intimidation rolls. Otherwise, forget it (an extra damage die isn't that great) and take spawning pool instead. That way you get a virtually limitless supply of vermin ghouls and bonus dice to Animalism rolls, with all the non-combat benefits those entail (spies, messengers, et cetera) and can focus on a couple good attack dogs.

                                              ECD Presence seems an odd coupling with Nosferatu, but trust me, it works fantastically. As you don't have an Appearance rating, you don't have to spend valuable dots on it in character generation and can pump Charisma and Manipulation instead. Charisma being the key there, as it's the linked attribute for levels 2, 4, and 5.

                                              And don't forget, when using mask your effective Appearance becomes two which means you still get workable dice pools for Awe and Entrancement...this is precisely why that got nerfed in newer editions, and the vampire has to spend blood to get Appearance dots out of mask. Not that those are the valuable Presence levels for a Nosferatu. Dread Gaze, Summoning, and Majesty are the 900-pound gorillas in a Nosferatu's discipline arsenal.
