    What would you change about Blood Magic in VTM?

    02-05-2023, 10:26 PM
    I would make it to where practitioners of Blood Magic would have to be on a Path of Enlightenment that correlates with the Blood Magic they practice. For example, an Anarch thaumaturge that practices New Age sorcery will be on a Path of Enlightenment that correlates with New Age sorcery. I feel that it would really hit home the fact that the ability to practice Blood Magic alters the practitioner on both a mental and spiritual level.

      I would grab the VtR rules and adapt them; specifically there is a VtR supplement at the end of 1e that's actually the basis for 2e VtR blood magic too.

      What this does:

      1) Ritual only magic. There's no need for blood magic Paths, or learning lots and lots of different sub-Disciplines. Combat blood magic is either pre-prepared rituals or rituals you can do in a few seconds. This cuts down a lot on the "power gamer" aspects of blood magic. Mind control blood magic will never be as good as Dominate, because you have to finish a ritual before anything happens, not because it's artificially worse in effect.

      2) Functional rules for designing rituals. This can be purely OOC to help homebrew them, IC rules for magically researcher type, or even allow some level of mage-like improvised magic to come up with rituals on the fly.

      3) Standardized rules for how different blood magic works. The differences are more who you can learn it from, and what costs you pay to make your magic work (which is also more fun).

          -Thaumaturgy and mortal hedge magic are the same thing, because thaumaturgy is something that comes from belief/willpower and thus comes from the human side of the vampire, which is why the Beast can't use it in frenzy. Mortals don't have something as convenient as Vitae, but they can get around that by sacrificing other things/ using objects charged with magic/faith.

          -Thaumaturges need a knowledge related to their craft. In-clan thaumaturgy needs 1 dot, caitiff, ghouls and mortals need at least 2, OoC practitioners need 3.

          -"Dark Thaumaturgy" is the art of channelling a spirit to do magic you can't do yourself. A level of Dark thaumaturgy adds to normal thaumaturgy rating. for example if you have 4 Thaumaturgy and 2 DT= you have a thaumaturgy level of 6. This works to the ninth level. DT is always learnt at in-clan costs. Using DT always comes with temporary side effects but in-of-itself can't do permanent damage to a vampiric user. Dealing with spirits and "demons" itself can be difficult, and usually means additional burdens on the user. Even in cases where the contract is favourable or the spirit is tightly controlled by the user, which is a difficult thing to achieve, DT is highly stigmatized as Vampires hate those who can quickly gain powers and upset the social order.

          -There's a lot of extremely minor/novel powers that practitioners can learn without xp. These have very little mechanical power but serve to make the world more interesting. If it exists, there's magic for it.


          -Rituals are learnt during downtime. Using downtime for rituals means you're not gaining other backgrounds which could also be gained during downtime.

              The change for blood magic that needs to happen is the XP sink nature of the systems. Every White Wolf game line seems to have one mechanic section that requires an absurd amount of XP to level up. For VtM it is all the magic paths, in exalted it was martial arts and crafting, in werewolf it was so many charms, etc.

              You can make a fully functional combat character build for around 75 xp post character creation and have plenty of options. Playing a "mage" means you can easily burn through 250+ xp post character creation and still not be all that well rounded as a caster character. In short, the gaming group is more likely to break up or start a different game before you get to actually play your character.

                                  What would I be cutting in an all ritual system based on late VtR 1e/VtR 2e's methods? Again, those come with a more much exhaustive guide to making your own rituals. As I noted on three different levels of appeal to play, you can design any ritual you want. You can adjust the thematic tone of the rituals to help ensure they fit your group's vision of what vampire blood magic should look like. You also don't have to worry about what should be a Path and what should be a ritual. Why did studying if a vampire can control fire turn into Lure of the Flames instead of a few rituals that allow various levels of pyromancy? Why does Levin Bolt just get better and better lightning bolts to shoot at people, while Path of Blood has five distinct powers?

                                  Going all ritual makes it all easier to do because it all follows a more coherent set of design principles. It's more accessible as a rule, which tends to be more fun. Since you can remake any power as a ritual on some level, no power or ability is actually being cut from the game.
