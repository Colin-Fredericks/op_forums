                        Yeah, the comic uses a number that is basically the minimum required to have gangs and wars and in which vampires are men of action, official ratio be damned.

                        The official ratio kind of assumes almost everything will be settled politically, with ghouls doing most of what little fighting actually occurs. Which makes more sense in a story about immortals who actually live for centuries, and don't have the death-rate of gang-bangers and goodfelllas. But which makes for a rather boring comicbook.

                        Not much of a comic if someone tried to snuff Cecily by having her haven bulldozed in the middle of the day, and her ghouls called their contacts in the unions and tried to have that bulldozing cancelled. And then come sunset, she either heard about how she had been successful in accumulating sufficient worker union influence over the years, or she woke up buried in concrete and slowly starved into torpor. That kind of slow accumulation of political power by a tiny number of widely spread out vampires is what the official canon moves of the Jyhad are about, but, well, canon is really boring. Bureaucrat the Accounting.

                        Originally posted by Frontline989 View Post

                        Second am I wrong or did a Justicar diablerize a Kindred in the middle of Elysium? It looked fucking cool but just curious. Also Ian willingly becoming partially blood bound to Cesily.
                        Yeah, I noticed that the way they do blood magic in general is very different from the comics. It seems like in the comic, blood magic is basically blood Obtenebration. Vampires use it for quasi-telekinesis for the most part. There is no suggestion that hunger mechanics exist, since they use it with a casualness that the hunger mechanic precludes. Presumably vampires in the comicverse become hungrier at more or less the same rate as humans, with routine discipline not really speeding this up, at least not more than routine excersize does for a human.

                        It's also unclear if blood-bonding exists in this universe.
