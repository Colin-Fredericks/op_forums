    Feedback on a homebrew Path of Enlightenment [V20]

    08-25-2026, 04:59 PM
    Hello,

    I'm looking for some feedback on a homebrew Path of Enlightenment I'm working on.

    My motivation is to fill a gap in the existing Paths that I see for vampires who recognize that they are no longer human, but aim for co-existence as much as personal fulfillment. Paths with Instinct as a Virtue always have the problem that they create an incompatibility with societal structures, often emphasized by using Conviction as well.

    The few Paths with Conscience and Self-Control only cover a very narrow spectrum of characters. I wanted to try something that can be broader in application, or at least fill in between Path of Redemption and Honorable Accord. I'm looking for feedback on whether the Path would be interesting enough for a character, whether the concept and descriptions are clear enough to understand, and of course whether you could see it working or not.

    Just in case, but important: while the Path clearly borrows from Thelema, it is not Thelema, nor is it meant as a commentary on it. The core actually comes from the Via Voluptarius and Via Adversarius in DAV; I only noticed the Thelemic elements later and decided to go with it.




    Path of Desire and True Will

    The origins of this Path can be traced back to the much older Road of Sin. There are key differences though: while it identifies desire as the driving force of both Beast and vampire, it tries to balance the ego-centrism of the Road by integrating it with society.

    It stipulates that while personal development in a vacuum may be possible, the benefits of a society to the individual are by far too useful to dismiss. Instead of restricting the follower of the Path to a specific social dogma, it relies on the understanding of how social integration will emerge from their True Will as a natural consequence.

    True Will can be described as a purpose or path through life. It is refined through reflection on desires and shaped into a more holistic concept: True Will means mindfulness of the path as it progresses. Disruptive desires will inevitably create conflict, which by itself will hinder further progression – by getting what you want, you may find yourself barred from what you want. So is this truly what you want?

    The Path takes this idea of refinement of desire into True Will, and applies it to the nature of vampires: desires are the root that nurture the Will and stem from the vampire or the Beast, not from a divine mandate. Motivations will change over time, and evolution is an important part of traveling the Path. The single most important aspect for a follower is to be always conscious of whether the consequences of their actions will allow them to continue their journey; True Will is the goal as much as the path itself.

    Much greater emphasis is put on control of the Beast than was usual for the Road of Sin. The Beast is a part of the vampire they must find a common ground with, but set strict lines that cannot be crossed. There is no reason to deny the Beast pleasure in feeding, or indulgence in other desires – but there can be no quarter if the urges would imperil the Will of the vampire. This stick-and-carrot approach eschews compromise. Either a desire can be transformed or adapted to satisfy the Beast, or it is denied.

    The Path seeks to mediate realization of the Self (Courage) with the Beast (Self-Control) and society (Conscience). Therefore, a sin for each is given in the hierarchy; however, these are ultimately the same concept expressed through the three different lenses and should be understood as such.


    Virtues: Conscience, Self-Control

    Ethics:
    • Act mindful. All your actions will be reflected back onto you.
    • The Beast belongs to you, but you do not belong to it.
    • Share in your desires, but refuse unwanted ones. Do not compromise.
    • Freedom, control, and will are your most valuable tools. Do not lose them.
    • There is no growth on toxic soil. Cultivate your environment as you would yourself.

      The problem I see is that it's hard to use in practice.

      A Hierarchy of Sins has to be clear and objective. Deciding if a sin occurred or not must be as unambiguous as possible, the best case being an objective behavior. That's the greatest challenge with Paths built around open ended goals like "do what you will".

      Reading the list, I don't know how I'm supposed to judge a real case. Looking at each level, in a inverted order:
      1. I can't know if the character want something for themselves. I can see if what they desire is the desire of the Beast of another, but not if no other motivation exist.
      2. How can I say if a character understand their own desire of not? How can I distinguish the mistake between instinct/desire or conformity/will and legitimate overlap, especially in a Path that teaches precisely the need for indulgence of the Beast's desires and following of social norms?
      3. How can I access the character's will to say when it's being stifled vs followed?
      4. Those work well when the Path gives clear parameters for what you should seek, but when the point is one's own will I can't say if it was lost or not, if submitting the Beast is necessary or if appeasement of another is submission or desire.
      5. Unless you define this one as any dice roll failure, the problem again is that all of those rely on knowing beforehand what the character will or desire. If I didn't act, who can say I willed otherwise? Who can say I didn't will my Beast to do what it did? If my desire is to harm those of others, then what?
      6. Those are antithetical to your stipulations: I can't evolve my desires if I can't be tainted by the desires if others. I can't accept and indulge the Beast without letting it influence me. I can't associate with people without a risk of having my desires regularly fouled. This is nonsense.
      7. Demand to expand, indulge and engage with society, without recognizing they clan clash. It's one thing for a Path to demand two conflicting goals for you to find a balance, it's another for it to be unaware of the conflict to begin with.
      8. The description doesn't follow prescription. I can apply if I interpret it as "harming anyone by accident", but the description indicates this is nota The intention. Again, it relies on knowing beforehand a will from the character that can be anything.
      9. How can I say an action occurred without will? How do I measure self reflection?
      10. This is the only bone close to be actionable and still requires to assess feelings that may be masked for any reason.

      Now, my impression of the whole project is that you're trying to fill a perceived gap without asking what the narrative goal of the Path is.

      This is basically an awkward Path of What I'd Do Anyway, and one that's literally that, it exists for the character to act freely, without ideology or pressure beyond wanting to do whatever you did. The reason this gap exists isn't negligence, but that Paths were made precisely to not do that.

      If a character just wants to do what they want, but without disengaging society and following Conscience and Self-Control... That's Humanity all along. It was never defined by not knowing you're a monster, or not accepting it. It's just about following basic social norms.

      On another thread I explained how Humanity in Vampire is a naturalistic morality, not a moral code. It isn't being good in accordance to what a given society says, but in accordance to what life in a society demands to be understood as basic decency. Being imperfect is a part of it, so you shouldn't, in any Path, be required to live as a paragon to be recognized as a follower, less of all Humanity.

                                          Thank you all for the feedback, this is already very helpful.

                                          I will try to address and clarify the points you brought up.

                                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                          This is basically an awkward Path of What I'd Do Anyway, and one that's literally that, it exists for the character to act freely, without ideology or pressure beyond wanting to do whatever you did. The reason this gap exists isn't negligence, but that Paths were made precisely to not do that.
                                          Yes and no. I think I failed to convey an essential part of what True Will encompasses, which is a pretty dramatic failure, since the whole thing is built on it and subsequent rules make no sense without that context.

                                          I will try to explain by relating to existing Paths. Many of them have a specific role baked into them: ruler, martyr, scholar, monster and so on. Instead of making that role specific, True Will is meant to function as a role that results from the personal desires. It's what people may say is 'their calling', their destined route. Crowley called it the divinely ordained place in the universe, but I want to keep concrete spirituality out as much as possible.
                                          If the desire of a character is for power and they see their calling as a ruler, the Path of Power and the Inner Voice becomes their Path of What I'd Do Anyway. In that sense True Will is a generic placeholder for that. It's very much a long term perspective in stark contrast to short term Wants, although the Path acknowledges that it may change over time.

                                          The attempt here is to build a framework that supports the restrictions that must emerge from a chosen role. Instead of having "Failing to respect your superiors" as a concrete sin, the sins are meant to facilitate those concrete restrictions when confronted with the Self, the Beast, and Others. You called it naturalistic morality, which is what I'm going for, albeit with a different nature than Humanity. Taking "Do what you will" literal as a license for wanton action is pretty much the opposite of what the Path tries to achieve.
                                          This makes formulating concrete sins difficult, though. There is certainly some struggle with language on my side to convey the right ideas.

                                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                          Now, my impression of the whole project is that you're trying to fill a perceived gap without asking what the narrative goal of the Path is.
                                          Narratively, the idea is to allow for characters a more flexible role than the existing Paths provide, if they choose to follow a Path.

                                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                          A Hierarchy of Sins has to be clear and objective. Deciding if a sin occurred or not must be as unambiguous as possible, the best case being an objective behavior. That's the greatest challenge with Paths built around open ended goals like "do what you will".
                                          Yes, absolutely. As you can see, I'm struggling with it. Since you are asking from a player's perspective, I likely can only offer 'roleplay' as an unsatisfying answer to most of the questions that concern the psyche of the character except a select few that have a corresponding mechanic: I will go through the list point by point and explain from the 'perspective' of the Path, that can hopefully help me to condense the hierarchy better. I won't necessarily agree with the truth or validity of those explanations myself, the goal is to make the internal logic coherent.

                                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                          1. I can't know if the character want something for themselves. I can see if what they desire is the desire of the Beast of another, but not if no other motivation exist.
                                          Motivation of a desire is, at this level, not a restriction: Inevitably, all desire is created through interaction with the world and others, but a desire created in you is distinct from a desire in another. Even a shared desire with another is distinct because it resides in a different place (yourself). A desire awoken by the Beast that is not the Beast's own, or a desire inspired by another are raw material. Making it truly your own (or not) is covered by the second step.
                                          The cardinal sin is apathy, having no motivation besides base necessities and serving the desires of others.

                                          Originally posted by GreatHornedRatkin View Post
                                          The path is about pursuing your own True Will, correct? So what happens if the vampire reaches a point where they are content with their lot in life?
                                          Fulfillment does not mean being devoid of desire, it means you are able to satisfy your current desires. You will probably want to add sustaining this fulfillment as a new desire, which starts a recursion (that is wanted).

                                          Mechanism: The character's desires, motivations, and reason for existence have been thoroughly corrupted, abandoned, or suppressed to the point where they merely exist as a shell. This also triggers as result of a Discipline or other manipulation of the mind that causes this state temporarily.

                                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                          2. How can I say if a character understand their own desire of not? How can I distinguish the mistake between instinct/desire or conformity/will and legitimate overlap, especially in a Path that teaches precisely the need for indulgence of the Beast's desires and following of social norms?
                                          Introspection. A new desire must be examined to decide if it is something you truly want, or whether it needs to be adapted or abandoned. Where does it come from? Why does it exist? What are the consequences if you follow this desire? This would be a truly daunting task if it would happen in a vacuum, but it does not: it is a recursive process in which the new desire is contrasted with the existing True Will, and a decision must be made to either integrate or abandon it. Integration means that the True Will changes, or rather, evolves: the decision is carefully made with consideration whether it is detrimental or beneficial to the long term realization of the Will. Overlap is natural and actually helps with that process - if a new desire caused by conformity harmonizes with the True Will, all the better.
                                          This does not cover the experience itself. Spontaneous reaction to a new desire is hard to control and covered later on the Path.
                                          The sin is sustaining a new desire without the process of reflection and integration.

                                          Mechanism: The character, after experiencing a new desire, keeps following that desire without stopping to reflect on it.

                                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                          3. How can I access the character's will to say when it's being stifled vs followed?
                                          This one builds on the previous step: if your True Will is the core of yourself, acting against it is detrimental by definition. Conversely, you know something to be detrimental or stifling if it is against your Will. Since acting in accordance with your True Will comes naturally, this happens mostly by force - by your Beast or from the outside. If you cannot justify acting against your Will momentarily by being able to better act with it in the long run, you are betraying yourself. When you find yourself in moral agreement with a rule that runs clearly against the codified rules of your Will, then you are in crisis and need to re-evaluate the rules that make up your Will.

                                          Mechanism: The character is forced to act against themselves, or worse, they deliberately sabotage themselves.

                                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                          4. Those work well when the Path gives clear parameters for what you should seek, but when the point is one's own will I can't say if it was lost or not, if submitting the Beast is necessary or if appeasement of another is submission or desire.
                                          This one is perhaps the most literal one and it can be tracked with stats. Hopefully the previous explanations have been enough to establish that the clear parameters are defined by the Will itself, this sin is concerned with maintenance.

                                          Mechanism: Losing a permanent point of Willpower, running out of temporary Willpower. Losing a Virtue. Not spending Willpower when resisting the Beast. Becoming blood-bound, or not trying to resist Domination.

                                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                          5. Unless you define this one as any dice roll failure, the problem again is that all of those rely on knowing beforehand what the character will or desire. If I didn't act, who can say I willed otherwise? Who can say I didn't will my Beast to do what it did? If my desire is to harm those of others, then what?
                                          As mentioned before, Will is not made up on the spot. It is a gradual development and as tangible as the paradigm of any other Path, and as a consequence, becomes a more predictable role. Who can say whether a decision was caused by opportunity or by Will? The player, hopefully.
                                          The "Denying others their desires unless they harm yours" needs to go, though. I'm going to replace that with "refusal to share in your desires, if asked". The sin is forfeiting opportunities to strengthen and nurture the desires that are the basis of your Will.

                                          Mechanism: The character refuses an opportunity to engage in their chosen role, or misses the opportunity to let the Beast act in that role.

                                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                          6. Those are antithetical to your stipulations: I can't evolve my desires if I can't be tainted by the desires if others.                                           The rules are not against change per-se, the quality of that change matters. A change that is integratable into or harmonizes with the paradigm is welcome, a change that would weaken the desires it stands on is not. Taint and befoulment are not value-neutral words; evolving the Will is going forward, not backwards. You should be able to judge when the influence of another person makes your psyche less healthy - yes, this is sometimes too subtle to notice, but that is exactly what the rule is trying to prevent. The sin is letting others weaken your Will, or allowing it to degrade rather than improve it.

                                          Mechanism: The character is put in serious doubt about the soundness and validity of the desires or rules that build their Will.

                                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                          7. Demand to expand, indulge and engage with society, without recognizing they can clash. It's one thing for a Path to demand two conflicting goals for you to find a balance, it's another for it to be unaware of the conflict to begin with.

This rule just means you must examine it (as per the explanation of the second sin), and are not allowed to simply ignore it - because it may turn out to be a valuable addition to your construct of Will. You can not know until you have considered it. Likewise, categorically dismissing any desires of the Beast will not only waste finding possibly compatible building blocks, it also dismisses opportunities to soothe it by satisfying shared desires. Finally, refusing to engage with society excludes you from encountering new desires, as well as of robbing yourself of the opportunity to share in them. Conflict is kind of the point, as it requires re-examination and re-validation of your Will.
                                          The sin is categorically refusing to confront your Will with new desires to temper it.

                                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

Harm is meant as literal as it is metaphorical, and to put it bluntly, just means to not shit in the bed you made.                                           The sin is to provoke conflict with your Will. And no, if your desire is provoking conflict as part of your True Will to become the Best Evil Vizier, it's not that conflict.

                                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

If the motivation for an action was 'because I felt like it', and not a deliberate choice as in 'it was in line with my Will' we are back to the literal 'Do what you want' and not the 'Do what you are'. Every action causes an equal and opposite reaction, and this reaction is socially expressed through your associates. It will reveal how implementing your Will is received from the outside, and how society perceives you. It gives you an outside evaluation of yourself, and in turn should affect your decision on how to evolve your Will further.

                                                  Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
                                                  I will try to explain by relating to existing Paths. Many of them have a specific role baked into them: ruler, martyr, scholar, monster and so on. Instead of making that role specific, True Will is meant to function as a role that results from the personal desires. It's what people may say is 'their calling', their destined route. Crowley called it the divinely ordained place in the universe, but I want to keep concrete spirituality out as much as possible.

                                                  If the desire of a character is for power and they see their calling as a ruler, the Path of Power and the Inner Voice becomes their Path of What I'd Do Anyway. In that sense True Will is a generic placeholder for that. It's very much a long term perspective in stark contrast to short term Wants, although the Path acknowledges that it may change over time.

                                                  The attempt here is to build a framework that supports the restrictions that must emerge from a chosen role. Instead of having "Failing to respect your superiors" as a concrete sin, the sins are meant to facilitate those concrete restrictions when confronted with the Self, the Beast, and Others. You called it naturalistic morality, which is what I'm going for, albeit with a different nature than Humanity. Taking "Do what you will" literal as a license for wanton action is pretty much the opposite of what the Path tries to achieve.
                                                  This makes formulating concrete sins difficult, though. There is certainly some struggle with language on my side to convey the right ideas.
                                                  Bolding mine, to highlight what I think is an important part of the problem here.

                                                  You've noted how many of the pre-existing Paths are aimed towards a particular kind of role, but I think a better way to put it might be to say that they are all directed at some kind of ideal, principle, or Mission. Whatever the new role or value that the Path espouses, its purpose is to provide something as an alternative to Humanity that keeps the Beast from running too wild. Even the Road/Path of the Beast places certain specific ideological constraints upon the Vampire that follows it, despite giving the Beast some freedom to run wild being a central part of how the Path is supposed to operate.

                                                  Where your Path runs into trouble is that it has no Mission. The Path doesn't lead anywhere; you haven't made a Path as much as potential framework for a Path. And I think that's part of why your explanations for what qualifies as a Sin for each level of the Hierarchy ends up getting so convoluted: you don't have a clear Mission or Principle to point at, and are instead trying to describe a complicated framework that will permit you to do the equivalent of a Find+Replace by just inserting whatever values you decide to give the character that can fit into that framework.

                                                  I also think that lack of a clear defining Mission is also a failure of the Path from a Watsonian perspective. This Path is so flexible and conditional that I don't think it would actually work to help keep the Beast under control. I think a functional Path needs one or two things from its Mission: a guiding principle that the Vampire can aim at by following the Path, or boundaries that the Vampire must stay within to follow the Path. Without some combination of those two features, the Path won't be able to functionally replace the norms of Humanity by giving the Vampire a standard to adhere to beyond the impulses of the Beast.

                                                  I personally think you should focus less on trying to create one Uber-Path that can cover countless roles, and instead be more willing to make multiple specific Paths suited for the specific roles you feel that the current Paths aren't covering.

                                                          Originally posted by GreatHornedRatkin View Post
                                                          Unless you're playing in a mixed group, there's not supposed to be any moral conflicts behind a PoE.
                                                          That's wrong in several ways.

                                                          A Path is meant to change moral conflicts, not to eliminate them. That's why it has a Hierarchy of Sins. The whole point as design is to allow for characters that don't work under Humanity, but still give them moral conflicts to engage with. A Path follower is also a moral play by contrast, which is engaging with morality.

                                                          When the character has to chose between their wishes, safety or convenience and following an ideology as represented by a Path, when they ponder about how the Path changed them or how it puts them in a role that compliments the roles of mortals in the grand scheme, when they feel doubt (which is something they face, the whole point of the rules is that), all those are but a few examples of how a character can follow an alternative moral framework and still that be a way for the player to engage with a moral conflict.

                                                          In the books where they're presented, the goal of using them as tools for moral examination without making the character follow a regular morality, and above all the intention that Paths will still challenge the characters morally even if through their own perspectives, are blatantly clear and directly stated more than once.

                                                          Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
                                                          I understand that this is a lot more complex than existing Paths, but at the same time, I don't think it is that complex for a personal philosophy. I still may have to consider dumbing it down a bit, simply for functionality.
                                                          You will have to change it somewhat, yes. And thanks for the clarification, and I can get the Crowleyan vibe you warned about.

The player is not a follower, nor is the ST, and likely aren't philosophers either. Nor is a game trait the place to give a proper philosophy lesson, even if it is solidly built over one. You need a set of guidelines that anyone can use and say if that character followed suit or not.
