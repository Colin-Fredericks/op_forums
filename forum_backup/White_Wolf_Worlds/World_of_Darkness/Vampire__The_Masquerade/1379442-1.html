    An idea I came up with as a headcanon.

    The Great Leap Outward Betrayal

    By. Beckett

    There's no actual conflict between the Camarilla and the Quincunx. Quite the contrary, they have been at peace for the better part of a few centuries with diplomats sent by the Inner Council almost immediately after the Convention of Thorns that were greeted with welcoming arms. This is almost in complete contradiction to the assumption that all Kindred who journey East will be killed. In truth, such is only likely to happen if you don't bring any gifts or pay the proper respects. The Camarilla and Quincunx have a long-standing "strong fences make good neighbors" policy that primarily exists to keep younger Kindred in line.

    So why the ignominiously named "Great Leap Outward"? Well, the truth of that matter is that it was entirely the work of Justicars Cock Robin and Lucinde working with a number of highly ranked Devil Tigers. In simple terms, the Great Leap Outward was a cruel and vicious backroom deal designed to eradicate the Anarch Free States.

    The Wan Kuei were paid extremely large sums of money and there's some question that Queen Anne Bowesley made sure the Chinese takeover of Hong Kong went without a hitch for their part in the story: they were to beat the drum of war and talk about the White Devils that needed to be purged from California before sending an army of young soldiers to slaughter the Anarch-dominated territory. It was an immensely cynical ploy that was designed to take advantage of the fact that the number of younger Wan Kuei had exploded, the lack of spiritual balance following WW2 and communist China [atheism not affecting the number of undead but affecting their loyalty to ancient Chinese warlords], and needed to be pruned.

    The Wan Kuei invaders were never meant to hold the territory they acquired and it was, in simple terms, as nasty a frontline assassination as Uriah the Hittite. It was, initially, a smashing success as the number of Anarchs in California were reduced by half and some suggested even more. Certainly, the Anarch Free State seemed a thing of the past. Unfortunately, the Camarilla miscalculated by sending the venal Sebastian La Croix to "claim" Los Angeles even as the leader of the Wan Kuei, Ming Xiao, figured out that she had been sent to lead a crusade never meant to reach Jerusalem. The two ended up planning to betray their patrons, assassinated, and the Anarchs rallying around a new victory.

    Still, if the Anarchs endured and were pruned of dead weight, the Camarilla and Quincunx largely achieved its aims. They inflicted immense casualties on their enemies while barely lifting a finger.

    Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

        For me, the Great Leap Outward is most easily explained by ancient elders who don't like the modern world and hold "the West" as responsible for the Turning of the Wheel so for them, this is "revenge" (even if the place they're invading has nothing to do with what happened). Further, there are a lot more Wan Kuei than there used to be (due to basic population trends but also the fact that a greater percentage of souls end up in Yomi after death) and, of course, you have various hidden Akuma (some of whom might be those very ancient elders) trying to convince everyone that sending a bunch of young vampires off to die in America is a great idea. Because when you look at it, the only winner in the Great Leap Outward are the Yama Kings (lots of Wan Kuei off somewhere else means it's easier for them to amass power, plus with a lot of younger Wan Kuei seeing themselves getting thrown away like that, more opportunities to tempt them into becoming servants of Yomi).

        Ultimately the Great Leap Outward is viewed by many Wan Kuei as a terrible idea, but it's got enough support to go through and there are a lot of younger vampires who are on board with it because it gives them the chance to go off and be heroes and make big names for themselves, which would be a lot harder if they stayed at home.

        Even those who don't necessarily like or agree with it find themselves forced to sort of help out a bit or risk being branded as traitors or even akuma.
