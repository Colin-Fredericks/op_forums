            This has been an interesting thread to read, it has helped me a lot with considering how I might want to handle the Wan Kuei in my own stories. There are a couple of points that have been brought up which have raised a question I have regarded the lore developed for the 'Middle Kingdom' and its relation to the wider mythology of the WoD. There has been some back and forth on the extent to which the portrayal of Asian culture and mythology in KotE and related content has been accurate or respectful. People have also debated the extent to which the mythologies of VtM and DtF depend on Abrahamic religion being true to some degree, and whether or not that subordinates other cultural and religious perspectives.

            Now, a great deal of my knowledge of the World of Darkness comes from wiki-browsing, but much of what I have read on this forum suggests that the wiki shouldn't be treated as an ironclad source. But, on the wiki, "August Personage of Jade" immediately redirects to the article for (the Judeo-Christian) God, while "Emerald Mother" sends you to WtA's "Gaia." Obviously, Storytellers can do what they wish with their own game, but it seems to me like this relationship is as close to canon as you generally get from White Wolf. My question is, to those in the thread more familiar with various Asian cultures and mythologies than myself, how accurate or respectful is that particular bit of canon-welding to the source material? It seems a lot easier to reduce the mythic/historic perspectives of VtM/DtF to "just one point of view" when something capable of fulfilling the role of God in their myths doesn't relegate all other myths to falsehoods and distortions.

                                                        I’m not forgetting anything. For people who actually understand these languages, and there’s a good number of us, screwing them up to that degree isn’t charming; it’s immensely distracting at best, often just plain tiresome, and it’s lazy work on the part of the authors.

                                                        Also, “kuei-jin” just doesn’t work. Even in California, Sydney, etc., for reasons that are hard to explain in brief, this is not at all how Chinese and Japanese get mixed together, which in the real world they do all the time. In fact “Jin” is a Japanese pronunciation of a Chinese word to begin with. Chinese-speakers would just say it in Chinese if they came across it, and Japanese speakers would use a Japanese pronunciation for the first character.

                                                        As an analogy, we have a lot of French loanwords in English, and there are conventions for these things. But there’s no way to write a standard word so that people pronounce the first half in a French way and the second half in an English way. That’s about the level of absurdity we’re talking about.
