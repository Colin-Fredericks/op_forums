                                Ultimately there's a reason why Requiem changed the Camarilla into the Invictus, and the Anarchs into the Carthians.

                                The Invictus are always "The Man." Even if they're not in charge of a city, they're gunning for power, and they're going to hold on to the power they have. In power or out of power, they offer continuity and stability at the price of obedience. Playing and Invictus in an Invictus city actually means living up to your end of the bargain while vying with your fellow Covenant members for power. Invictus Oaths are seriously business (magically so), but also help push their more classic conservative political views on how vampires should conduct themselves. If the Invictus aren't in power, those Oaths and the ability to enforce them are still hella useful to whoever is, letting the Invictus to back up their offers of maintaining peace and order on behalf of the current crown even if everyone knows they're still trying to take it for themselves.

                                The Carthians are always "The Rebels." They map much more cleanly onto collectivist anarchist ideology because their goal is not rebellion, but fluidity. They want vampire governments that can adapt with the times instead of calcifying and becoming weak; and only local vampires making local agreements on how their local stuff should be handled can do that. Out of power, that's easy because nobody else agrees with them for the most part and run things their normal ways. When they're in power, they have to justify what they do to their fellow Carthians and other vampires. They need to prove their ideas actually work, and are actually good or even better... or internal revolt is as big of a threat as external opposition. Carthian Law makes sure the "rabble" are never voiceless, even if their own leaders are the ones with the power that need a talking too. Since static laws are weak laws, there's always a tension in Carthian cities between those that think the way things are is the best, and those that think they have a better idea, and that's never going to be settled permanently on purpose.

                                While I don't want VtM to copy that setup, it's the sort of depth of design V5 could have really used more thought put into. The Sects needed work, and one thing that needed work was more thought about what members of that Sect look like in all the possible situations they can find themselves in so that you never have anyone saying, "what's the point of playing an Anarch in an Anarch city?" If that's a valid question for players to be asking of the game.... the game should have an answer somewhere already.

                                    I personally like the Anarchs, but also agree that this is in the context of a Camarilla city. The Anarchs as portrayed in VtM aren't a sect, they're the collective of kindred that on some level disagree with the powers that be. And their appeal is exactly the variety of people you can have in this situation, they're not an organized group but simply the common mass of the people. In a way, they ARE the check and balance of a "healthy" Camarilla-like city.

                                    And that's not a bad proposal in itself. Wherever you have a power structure you should describe the people on the bottom and, among them, the ones trying to call on the bullshit and have some change, for one reason or another. You need to depict the niche of the characters that don't want to accept the system as is. You have to do this both for NPCs and for PCs that feel like it, that may not exist on some tables, but do at others.

                                    What's lacking in the Anarchs is the post anarchy, and here the problem isn't not giving them enough depiction as a sect, but exactly insisting in making them a sect. Instead of discussing how new local systems can evolve when the rebellion start to win (and it doesn't have to mean overthrowing the system), the game stubbornly kept to the notion of them as a sect of, well, Anarchs. And that's stupid.

                                    Because on a ideological level they're not Anarchs. They're a multitude of ideas in varying levels of development, from the ones that have a clear picture of an Utopia to the ones that just want better feeding grounds without politicking. Anarchism is a very specific political ideology that depends on a very specific mindset to work even in theory, a theory few people ascribe to.

                                                My take has been for a while that too make the Anarchs make sense you have to spend as much time thinking about the other sects as you do them and look at why they would rebel.

                                                It’s easy to come into a kind of quasi-‘capitalist realism’ with the Camarilla where it becomes the only system that works or could work and you don’t analyze it and how oppressive it would be to dwell within. If a domain is simply a prince and sheriff who uphold the law and ignore everything else then the only real reason to be an Anarch is to want to replace the prince. You can already do what you like, so if you want to collectivize, you can.

                                                The necessity of the Movement comes about more when you demonstrate that the Camarilla is an organization run like a neo-manorial mafia. The prince and the sheriff rule through monopoly of violence, the elders hold the young in indentured servitude via prestation and quasi-feudalism through the allotment of feeding grounds as controlled by the elite of elysium. Society is maintained by a rigid and archaic social contract that does not allow for freedom of speech, thought, or expression. Life is held to be cheap behind a veil of human facades.

                                                This lenses allows one to understand why Anarchs would organize. To have justice be delivered not through the whims of a tyrant serving the interest of the elites but by a council jury of citizen peers and an elected judge. To have power over the many come from the many rather then an oligarchy of gerontocrats. To have the right to feed be distributed according to need so that all have freedom to do so without entering into a debtors contract with any landlord. To allow for systems where vampires may construct domains which are secure and comfortable without becoming chained by prestation.

                                                All of these are ideals which would be incredibly difficult to achieve and would suffer from the corruption of vampirism and humanity alike, though that doesn’t make them not worth striving towards. They would come with their own complications and nuances if a group of anarchs do claim praxis in a domain.

                                                Thinking about how organizations like the Camarilla and Sabbat run and function are critical to understanding the dialectic of elder against youth which forms naturally out of it and what resolutions either side seeks to create.
