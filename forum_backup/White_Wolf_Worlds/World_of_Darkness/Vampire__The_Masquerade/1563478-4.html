    Originally posted by Tjalei View Post
    I'm gonna go with the monstrous transhumanist body horror stuff cause that's what's fun about the path and what's in the book description.
    Go with whatever you want, but saying the Path is X because you like X is objectively an error, not a matter of taste.

    Metamorphosis is designed to be broader and does not follow your view of "clearly defined role". Period. It giving you an easy stereotype to lean on if you don't want to engage with its open nature doesn't change that it is not what you claimed it to be.

    It's also not exactly what's in the description, as the description makes a point to say this is not the only approach, in fact not even common among older practitioners. You're making a selective reading, which is more about you and what you like in the Path than about the Path.

    And that's what's fun for you. The Path isn't an open framework just for the sake of being, it is also because plenty of people have different opinions on what's a fun approach to its key concepts and what is boring. I personally find the body horror stupidly boring, yet I do like the Transhumanist idea and several of the other things that can be done with it.

                    Current revision.

                    I added some fluff - hopefully not too purple - to use as some help with reconstruct the Path development and the reasoning behind it at the same time. Making True Will the goal and method at the same time was a priority. I tried to use the discussions in this thread as a guide and incorporate it as a means to answer potential questions, although being that far involved makes a neutral point of view very difficult. I added a bit of practice to make things easier to follow.

                    Also while editing, I noticed some trace amounts of Stirner and Rand that I had to tweak, but I think they add some spice to it. The ethics were expanded to include some of the more practical elements on how to resolve apparent conflicts between them.

                    If you think parts of the description don't fit or are missing, let me know. Mostly I'm interested in whether the concept is understandable now, though.




                    Path of Desire and True Will (V3)


                    As I travel through the night, the sun shines only on the road behind me;
                    but I know these steps by heart, and my will lights the way.

                    A hungry beast stalks me, to scavenge on my spoils or corpse;
                    but shared meals and victories have tamed it, and now my will guides its strikes.

                    When I meet you on the path we shall journey together:
                    for gods above and below are silent when they find themselves in obsolescence.

                    - Three Books Without Name, Book I, Chapter I


                    Travelers on this Path need to find their True Will, a harmonious integration of their desires and self-realization with their Beast and society. Because True Will also includes the journey to find itself, it is an ideal that is aspired to but never attained.

                    The main source for the philosophy of the Path is the first of the Three Books Without Name. The oldest extant copy (as Sine Nomine Libris Tres) is safely locked away in the collection of a Sabbat scholar, but modern translated versions keep finding their way into the hands of young seekers. There are no known extant copies of the remaining two volumes, if they ever existed.

                    Very few details can be glimpsed about the anonymous author as they write from the point of view of an abstract persona, but the scholars who have bothered to examine this curiosity in detail speculate its origin lies somewhere in the Maghrib of the Renaissance.
                    A single reference to a third eye invites speculation they could be a Childe of Saulot, but this is pure conjecture on what is more likely just a simple metaphor.

                    At some point, the author had access to On Hunger and its Satisfaction by the Brujah philosopher Tanitbaal-Sahar as evidenced by verbatim quotes. They likely have traveled on the Road of Sin, but must have fallen from it at some point.
                    Isolated and in danger of Wassail, they undertook a spiritual journey and rebuilt themselves from their lowest point: as the book tells it, the Path of Desire and True Will was developed while the author traveled on it, in the same process of refinement that the Path itself describes as one of its most important epiphanies.

                    Reduced to a shell, a Wight is a dead body with a Beast. The spark that differentiates Cainite from Wight is desire: the desire to be more than a Beast, the desire to be and become. The author postulates that since the desire to exist in a void would be pointless, one must understand the world, their place in it and what reality one wants to live in.

                    These realizations are not trivial - each carries consequences in how they influence ones actions, and these actions in turn have consequences that change the world one tries to place themselves into.
                    The result is a causal loop that requires steady re-examination of whether that understanding still holds true, or in a word, to find self-knowledge. On this basis, one is then able to build the second central concept of the Path: finding True Will.

                    This concept is again ostensibly shared with the Road of Sin, but despite the name it is almost the complete opposite. Will based solely on the self or base desires is too limited and ignorant, it is only self-deception. Neither is True Will a measure of direct satisfaction, it is commitment to a goal built while being mindful of the consequences, because an unwanted consequence is a direct failure of self-knowledge.

                    A goal that ignores the world - or more immediate, community - is not serving True Will because it would ignore the desire to care by ones own nature and is a part of it as a fact of reality. True Will must fit the world, and the world must fit True Will: if ones actions make society reject them, or degrade it into a society they would not want to live in, they were not actions of Will, but of ignorance.

                    The Path is highly skeptical of ritualized actions, as these tend to mindlessly repeat instructions without verifying whether the underlying reason for them still holds true. The only ritual found in the Books Without Name is consequently called the Breaking Ritual. Its purpose is to verify whether the practitioner's True Will is, indeed, still their True Will and to prevent it from becoming itself ritual.
                    As everything about the Path is highly individualistic, there is no formal way of performing it. Some use divination as an aid, others meditation. Some talk to their reflection in a mirror, very few discuss it with confidants.

                    The goal is however the same: stripping down what they are to the core, and rebuild themselves following each step they took on their way to the True Will, examining every part for its validity. Encountering a mismatch means they will have to mend that damage, and adjust it to fit with their current True Will.
                    Because it is a very taxing and potentially dangerous process to their Self, the Breaking Ritual is usually only performed when a traveler on the Path encounters a roadblock they can not circumnavigate by other means.

                    In practice, a new initiate on the Path will perform their first Breaking Ritual under guidance, either by a fellow traveler or the detailed instructions in the book. Formulating the first iteration of their True Will, they need a clear vision of what their future should and will look like, and see a path towards their goal.

                    Once their True Will has been established, it becomes the only authority they will follow, and any other authority must match or at least not contradict its paradigm. Storytellers and players should use this opportunity to discuss the character's concept, how their True Will would dictate their actions, and what conflicts with it.


                    Virtues: Conscience, Self-Control


                    Ethics:
                    • Act mindful. All your actions will be reflected back onto you.
                    • The Beast belongs to you, but you do not belong to it.
                    • Harmony is not conformity. Two voices with different tones can be harmonic.
                    • Do not compromise, but share in mutual desires.
                    • Freedom, control, and will are your most valuable tools. Do not lose them.
                    • Beware hidden authority. It lurks behind all rituals and concepts.
                    • Destiny is never dictated, but found through self-knowledge.


                    Hierarchy of Sins Path of Desire and True Will

                    Rating Moral Guideline Rationale
                    10 Feeling guilt for your actions There can be no guilt in mindful action
                    9 Failing to explore a new desire Desire is kindling, do not let it run out
                    8 Refusing indulgence that harms none Desire needs to burn to placate the Beast
                    7 Forfeiting long-term for short-term progress There are no shortcuts to your destiny
                    6 Putting desires of Beast or man before yours No-one but yourself must control you
                    5 Failure to treat others as you would be treated There can be no harmony in imbalance
                    4 Acting against your own self interests Always evaluate, but never compromise
                    3 Forfeiting a personal freedom Your freedom is your greatest tool
                    2 Losing Will or Virtue Your will is your strongest resource
                    1 Failure to seek self-knowledge Self-deception obscures the Path ahead
