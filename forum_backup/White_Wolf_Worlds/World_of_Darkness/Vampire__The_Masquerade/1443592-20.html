    Originally posted by SavageGM View Post
    For me the here and now sucks. Hard. I’m sure many people are still setting their games in the 90s and have been for 20 years. I’d say playing a game of VtM that’s always in modern times is far down the list of must haves in people’s games, but maybe I’m wrong.
    Well, put it this way: Vampire: The Masquerade is listed as one of the 100 Best Hobby Games (published by Green Ronin) - along with a bunch of other awards - in a ranking chosen by other industry professionals and is pretty much a gold standard nomination.

    It was nominated by George Vasilakos, noted as the driving force behind games like Eden Studios’ All Flesh Must Be Eaten and Buffy The Vampire Slayer, among a lot of other credits. In his essay to explain what he found compelling about the game - one of things he clearly cites is the fact that it is set in modern nights. Now, I am not saying you are wrong, but is a clearly identifying aspect of the game and, to be sure, all of the VTM editions have defaulted to be set in modern nights - even V20. You can get supplemental games, like Dark Ages, for different eras and you can always take the option of using historical settings, but the core default game is deliberately set in the here and now.

    Now, to flip this criticism on its head, if fans are arguing that V20 is preferable to them because it feels like it is set in the past (in the 1990s), then I’d say this is a flaw of the design. That is, it’s intent is to be set in modern nights, but apparently it doesn’t feel that way. It isn’t a major problem for me, in as much as my general take on V20 is that it is a ‘Greatest Hits compilation’ edition that celebrates past editions and supplements. V5 however, is an attempt to recapture the spirit of the original game in tone and style - but updated to the modern nights of the 21st century. By contrast, it is a ‘Remastered’ edition.

                        Originally posted by Trippy View Post
                        To me, the look and feel of V20 was the same as that of Vampire Revised, from the very late 1990s moving into the early 2000s. It felt like an expanded version of Vampire Revised really, especially without the original Tim Bradsheet art or the original art to illustrate the clans (not Leif Jones). In actuality, the original two editions of Vampire actually evoke the tone of the 1980s in many ways, when goth-punk was a genuine alternative counter-culture in music and style. In the early 1990s, basically, the alternative became mainstream - so the general public started listening to Nirvana, watching The Crow - and playing Vampire: The Masquerade - in greater numbers.
                        Given that the first edition of Vampire: the Masquerade was published in 1991, I think that it's natural that it reflects the 1980s more than the 1990s; it was a book about how people felt at the end of the 1980s, looking back on what was, in essence, the very recent past. This is, of course, a feature of any book, that it will never properly reflect the present, in that the formative experiences of the author will have occurred in the past. I would also argue that Vampire: the Masquerade is a better book about the 1980s that New Wave Requiem, as the former is a very immediate reflection, whilst the latter is a pastiche of the period, which would appear to be written by people too young to have properly experienced the decade...

                                    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post

                                    I'm sure you don't intend it, but this is probably one of the biggest sources of negative feelings from the V20 camp towards the V5 camp: the attitude that those of us that have playing VtM and other games with vampires in them that aren't close to what V5 does, haven't been playing &quot;real&quot; vampire games. This whole way of phrasing the conversation tends to raise hackles because you, again giving you the benefit of the doubt that you don't mean to come off this way, are basically saying that in my near 25 years of playing vampires in RPGs, I don't appreciate what a &quot;vampire game&quot; should actually be because I don't like the specific way V5 does vampires.

                                    I hope you can see how off-putting that can be when seen from the other side of it.

                                    Vampires are fictional creatures. Of thousands of years they've seen countless variations in different mythologies and folklore. Even in pop-culture there is a huge range of what vampires are, what they do, what dominates their concerns, and what narrative point their creators are trying to make. Blade or Morbius are two very different vampire stories (as much as one can sum up comic books as stories like that), but they're both vampire stories. There is no platonic ideal of vampires to appeal to when saying &quot;this is good because you're playing a vampire,&quot; or, &quot;this makes the game more vampire-like.&quot;



                                    As someone that's been rather involved in the discussions around V5 here since they started, I think there's a somewhat rosy picture of history.

                                    When V5 was announced, and first few playtests were out, things felt rather the opposite. V5 was all the hype, expressing negativity about it put you firmly in the minority in most threads; though the flames were plenty heated and I'm sure lots of people just avoided those threads on both sides. Plenty of us felt like it was turning from the VtM forum into the V5 forum. Then the core dropped. Then the Cam and Anarch books dropped. That's when the mood of this forum started swinging towards where it is now. There were some very serious V5 supporters that jumped back to V20 during that period here as a sign of, at least on this forum, the reaction wasn't so positive once the product got in people's hands.

                                    Lots of V5 posters here spent a lot of time actively posting about how much better V5 is; not just &quot;being happy with what they got.&quot; It's just that after the hype settled, these forums settled more on the V20 side than the V5 side, and that's colored discussions since.
                                    This is pretty much exactly how I feel.

                                                V20 is not a 90's game. It can be a 90's game. But it is not a 90's game. V20 isn't quite timeless; It assumes we've got computers, but it's pretty close. There's a healthy level of seperation between Immortal, resistant-to-change vampire society and mortal events. It's not entirely static; Rites of the blood gives us a nice runover of evolving anarch sorcery, but it's certainly not so stupid as to claim SYRIA IS GEHENNA. V20 works for 1995, 2005, 2015, and assuming nothing apocalyptic it'll work for 2025, 2035, 2045 and 2125. V20, Ignoring east Asia if you're adverse to a sensible retcon, v20 works anywhere. It feels like it was written for everyone. V5, in contrast, is a 2018 product haunted by the USA's 2016.

                                                RE LARP.
                                                LARP is fine, you do you, but I'm gonna be honest; I deeply resent the Idea of LARP impacting tabletop. Everyone is welcome to their own little word, but I refuse to have the power of Imagination shackled down by a medium highly limited by a bunch of actors. Unless you all work out how to throw cars at eachother or accept the cruelties of Dominate, you shouldn't impose your problems on the tabletop majority. Keep your canon seperate. V5 takes too many cues from LARP, and it suffers from it.

                                                RE How Vampires are supposed to be.
                                                Maybe it's because its the first 'vampire' thing I really clicked with, being a "youth" and all, but nothing seemed to do Vampires better than VTM, so I don't see the need to immitate other vampires with compulsions, especially so because doing so goes against two of the most important themes vampies have going for them in VTM:

                                                1-The Masquerade. How can we keep this thing up if the beast is constantly making clowns of us through messy criticals, beastial failures, compulsions on top of the rare frenzy? What V5 does is interesting for a few games or so, but it really stretches suspension of disbelief that such clowns could ever maintain a conspiracy for a few months, nevermind five hundred years, especially since blood potency makes you even more clownishly incompetent and thus elders are a comedic disaster only mitigated by having more backgrounds.

                                                Second, VtM is all about control;" a Beast I am lest a Beast I become"; you do mean things of your own voalition so you don't lose control and do worse. V5 has us do mean things outside of our control regardless of how well we approach things. Why should I take responsibility for something the red dice made me do? In V20, I would be to blame for putting myself in a possition where i am likely to frenzy. In V5 the Beast will rear its head any time I try and do something; my only recourse is to stay at home and have bloodbags delivered to me, or suicide; that's no fun is it? Anytime I screw up in V5 it's probably because 'the beast made me do it". Compulsions might work great for an alternative Malkavian or Brujah weakness but it doesn't work at all for the vampires who're trying to build businesses around themselves.

                                                    I had a longer post written up before but gave up on it as pointless. Since you bring this up, and I agree, I'll put some of those thoughts here.

                                                    But then V5 adds in things that were never there before: Resonances, You Are What You Eat, trait caps at 5, restrictive Feeding Styles, etc. One or two of these things might be okay, but all of them together make it feel more like a reboot or reimagining.                                                     Obviously, if you wanted a different game with V5, that's good. If you didn't, then it makes sense you wouldn't like what's there.

                                                    TBH, I think if V5 had been presented as an alternate timeline setting, that would have avoided lots of the aggro. But I can see why, from a business perspective, they didn't.
