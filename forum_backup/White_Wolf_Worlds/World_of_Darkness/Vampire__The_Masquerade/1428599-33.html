    The Brujah were one of the High Clans for a long time. Even now, they hold tremendous influence over groups prone to violence. Really, the main reason the Brujah fell from High Clan status is not due to Presence being weak, but rather because their rage weakness is much more of a liability in modern times. Back in the day, if you slaughtered a council of Lords in a rage, you could make up some story about an ambush by the barbarian or the Saracen and how only you survived, and your presence would sell that explanation.

    These days, you can't really get away with such things in a corporate boardroom. As a Brujah, you need to build your influence among violent revolutionaires who are well known to eat their own over minor political disagreements, and who gather in secret backrooms. In their own way, the Brujah need to hide almost as much as the Nosferatu. Hiding just means something different for them than it does for the Rats.

                    SO, about them Tzimisce!

                    Since I was invited to put my thoughts out there, and the VS thread seems pretty busy with some more meta topics, I figured I would throw my two cents in on the V5 interpretation of one of my favourite clans in here. As I've mentioned once or twice before, I am an outsider to V5, especially in terms of the crunch. But since the overwhelming majority of my engagement with WoD as a fan has been with the fluff rather than gameplay, it's not terribly outside of the norm, lol.

                    The update to the Bane seems fine. I can kind of agree with previous comments that the Grave Soil weakness was perfectly fine, but I've also delved into some of the discussions that have come up around trying to determine how large an area you could draw soil from, what happens if your source of soil is lost somehow, etc. I think having to define your "domain" in some sense tries to solve some of those ambiguities while also opening up the concept more. Also, this is a point that the "Tzimisce isn't the Dracula Clan" crowd should lean in on, given that the need to rest in the earth of your homeland was taken straight out of Stoker AFAIK.

                    The Compulsion is a bit more complicated. Assuming that the Tzimisce is allowed to define what they are "greedy" for, instead of always being obsessed with shinies or just absolutely indiscriminate in shouting "mine!" and licking things, I think it's interesting. It makes me think of the weakness added to Koldunism in DAV20, where they had this Sith-like obsession with claiming new kraina and battling rivals for knowledge. But I can see that the people who are probably most hurt by this are likely the ones most hurt by most of the changes: the die-hard Metamorphosists, who probably hate the idea of having such mundane attachments hard-wired into their esoteric independence-obsessed trans-vampirists. But then, maybe they should view it as one of the challenges that stands in the way of Azhi Dahaka.

                    Let's move on to the meat of this conversation: the Disciplines.

                    To introduce, I started digging into this topic when I finally heard, a few days ago or so, that the Tzimisce lost Auspex. For my personal playstyle, my preferred Tzimisce, this was a sad and painful loss. But maybe it's because I mostly came to Vampire during the 20A years, and tend to view everything through the collective 'canon' that includes all the editions and was constructed after the fact, but I have always seen the Tzimisce as much bigger than just the esoteric Metamorphosists, or the Kolduns. Setting Dracula aside, characters like Rustovich and Radu, a warrior and a diplomat, have become part of the Tzimisce story as much as esoteric researchers and blood mages wielding Auspex. And the Modern Nights are full of Tzimisce that are not deep philosophers or occult researchers. I can understand an argument for a transition when the Clan *has* become defined by much more than sorcery and mad science.

                    But I think the story should be much more complicated, frankly.

                    There are few Clans that can make a better argument for having a wide array of specialized Bloodlines than the Tzimisce; the Banu Haqim are basically the other option, and look, they do that! Tzimisce Revised talks about how the old Metamorphosists like Yorak realized in the Dark Ages that they needed more than a bunch of isolated monster-hermits if the Clan was going to thrive, so they started embracing barbarian chieftains and feudal warlords to manage the more mundane affairs of the Clan, such as Shaagra. And we already have one example of the Tzimisce manipulating their access to in-Clan Disciplines through the Embrace of Revenant family members with alternative talents. Dominate Tzimisce, in this sense, are essentially a new "caste" set up by the Metamorphosists. But given that the undead who are actually attending to matters in the material plane are more likely to flourish and spread, you can imagine the more mundane Dominate branch of the Clan growing faster and eventually becoming the "generic" Tzimisce.

                    The irony here, which I find extremely amusing, but others might find infuriating, is that the Auspex Tzimisce, in this framing, would essentially be the genuine "Old Clan" of the Tzimisce. And obviously the story I told above does fly in the face of everything we know about the "Old Clan Tzimisce" up until now. But I've acknowledged before that V5 is often sloppy in how it handles things even when they really don't bother me in principle.

                    Still on the subject of Disciplines, but I feel like Vicissitude deserves its own little bolded header.

                    As my comments above might indicate, I do rather lean towards the sentiment that Auspex + Protean would have been a better choice than Dominate for Vicissitude-as-Amalgam. The idea of Dominate being part of Vicissitude smacks in the face of decades of conflict between the position of those two Disciplines within the Clan. When I first saw it, I thought Animalism, for its power over the Beast, made more sense, but then I realized that made Vicissitude more available to the Gangrel than might be preferred. I've heard the opinion that Blood Sorcery would be a good option, but I think that comes with complications. When you think Tzimisce and Blood Sorcery, 9 times out of 10 your thoughts go to Koldunism, and the last time it probably goes to Thaumaturgy stolen from a Tremere through horrific torture. And given how popular the "Vicissitude mad scientist" concept is, I could see forcing a mystical side into things potentially rubbing people the wrong way. Nor do I think that Koldunism and Metamorphosis should become so necessarily entwined, even though I love the combo personally. And I can kind of see what they were trying to go for with Vicissitude as a Dominate Amalgam, even if I'm ultimately unsatisfied with the result: you are Dominating the Flesh.

                    And here are some closing thoughts on Clan Culture, a mix of following up on things mentioned above and throwing in a few other thoughts.

                    As my comment about Grave Soil above indicates, I think the link between the Tzimisce and Dracula even during the early versions of the Clan is a bit stronger than some like to admit. But I haven't read a lot of the early material, so I don't know how much time the Tzimisce spent as purely-antagonist freak-shows without the details of a Clan Flaw being defined, etc. I don't think that the Clan being entirely reduced to Dracula is a good thing, but I don't think that it is reasonable to argue that any deviation from the original Necroscope-inspired Evilutionary Scientist version is heresy either. As someone who has always considered themselves something of a vampire fanboy, I did not know Brian Lumley's work existed until I became interested in the Tzimisce, so it's probably not a big enough pop culture influence that it should be allowed to dominate an entire Clan concept. Dracula is much bigger, and I don't think HE should be the end-all-be-all either. The FLUIDITY of the Tzimisce, their capacity for change and evolution, is their most fascinating quality IMO, and that is a trait that should inevitably result in diversity and mutation.

                        Old Clan vs. New Clan vs. New-New Clan

                        Honestly, as a long time fan of the Dragon clan, I have to say that the Old Clan Tzimisce have always kind of been a correction to a problem that never should have been a problem in the first place. The Old Clan Tzimisce was created for DIRTY SECRETS OF THE BLACK HAND to explain why there would be the creepy old nobility Clan (before the Ventrue were allowed to go back to being warrior nobles) could exist there with Souleaters in Vicissitude.

                        Well, a lot of people thought Vicissitude being EVIL was stupid and when they did up Vampire: The Dark Ages, the Old Clan Tzimisce just flat out were ignored. The Eldest and all of the Clan Culture included large amounts of Vicissitude as their central basis. Indeed, they revived "Vicissitude is a disease" AGAIN by making it so anyone who had Vicissitude was secretely possessed by the Eldest.

                        So, the Old Clan Tzimisce had their culture of hospitality and honor added to the mainstream Tzimisce Clan in the Dark Ages. I have a big fondness for this Clan as the Dark Age Tzimisce are the best Tzimisce. Vicissitude is a big part of it even if it was still too often played for "gross out monsters" like in Vampire: The Dark Ages: Redemption. I like the update because it goes away from "fleshy serial killers" and more to "Neo Feudalists" which I think is a concept that can be really interesting. They're now the Hoarder Clan, complete with old lady surrounded by plastic bags and newspapers versus apartment building manager as viable Tzimisce.

                        It's funny because an NPC I once made was based on a Tzimisce who owned an apartment building where every single apartment was rigged with cameras and microphones with extensive contracts that allowed him to control their lives to a minutia level. This actually fits it really well and I think works quite nice.

                        "You don't understand power, Grand Admiral Thrawn. Conquering worlds you'll never even visit isn't power. Neither is destroying ships and people and rebellions you haven't looked at face-to-face." ―Joruus C'baoth

                        The big appeal of the updated Tzimisce for me is the fact that they offer a lot of new options to the archetype and are easier to fit in existing games, which is something that should be supported.
