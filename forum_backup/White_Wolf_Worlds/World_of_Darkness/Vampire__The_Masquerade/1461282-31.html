                            I can't judge Paradox's plans, I can judge their results.

                            When I read 1st ed core for the first time, the Sabbat was described as basically Sauron's Army. But also as a rumor, something no one really knows about and many suspected to be just BS invented to keep Neonates from trying their luck in those cities. I loved it. I liked to imagine what the truth could be, as the basic description was too obviously bullshit. I didn't wanted to play a game where the villains were a bunch of psychos eternally on cocaine implying that Latin Americans, or anyone for that matter, live in Mordor and are too dumb for this to be a Masquerade breach.

                            The moment I started to hear that WW made the Sabbat be exactly what the rumors said, I got furious. It would be about a decade and a half before I gave them the right of the doubt and start to read things about the Sabbat.

                            And lo and behold, I found out that the Sabbat was more complex than that. It wasn't exactly fine, but it got to be far better than their initial depiction and than other Camarilla or fucked-up players made it appear to be. What's more, there was material that I could really focus on and, instead of just dump the whole concept of the sect, I could work with it to have a good adversary for my play style. One that didn't meant I'm a dumb mongrel living in Mordor.

                            Rio de Janeiro being Sabbat isn't the problem. What the Sabbat meant is the problem. Making the Sabbat more complex changed what it meant. Now we just aren't Mordor anymore because the White Knights of the North saved us, how grateful I should be... it isn't as if in the last time we've got a military coup followed by about 20 years of US-sanctioned dictatorship that we still feel the deep marks in our society and find secret mass graves from time to time.

                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                            I would actually be very interested in what WOULD be a very good way to address the issue and would love to hear pitches as its something I've struggled with myself.
                            For the Sabbat? Reinforce nuance instead of ditching it. The problem with a setting like WoD is that you're always talking about a representation of real people. This is not an utterly fictional world, it is a mirror to ours, so making associations is unavoidable. Every assumption of the setting will inevitably be an assumption about real places, real things, real people. And it is such on design, as the setting was created to be a social criticism.

                            You must understand the irl side of an issue to make decisions ad be prepared to own your decisions. Preferably consult with people related to that or let then be the authors. All over the place it is the same core problem: this is about real people. Real people live in slums, real people live in every country and continent, real people are brainwashed by cults, real people become extremists, real people see their loved ones becoming extremists, real people are in both sides of every conflict, no side is more real than the other.

                            So the solution can't be to ask which people won't care about their hometowns becoming Mordor, or which group is ok to treat as Sauron's Army. It is to accept that every conflict is nuanced and offer players the tools to explore and adapt such nuances, and do it walking with the people you want to represent instead of showing off your good will to them. And unfortunately, yes, that means the WoD won't have a lot of places for pure evil irredeemable villains. The real world don't offer easy paths for goodness, its dark mirror shouldn't, either.

                            OBS.: I live far from Rio de Janeiro, but my father's family is from there, so I know the city a little and went there several times.

And that for me is one of the huge problems of 5th edition overall. That makes it feel less like a game of its own and more like some fans (and fans who became writers) of vampire getting to feel vindicated on the internet and in LARPs, because the game now agrees with them.

                                When people note that part of their problem with V5 is that it seems basically like saying "almost everything significant after the corebook was a mistake and you didn't like or understand quality VtM gaming if you liked it" and how stunningly insulting that is, reviews like this seem to be replying to that with "well exactly!"

                                            My opinion of the Sabbat as a Sabbat ST and player

                                            I think I should share my opinion of someone who has run extensive amounts of Sabbat games and played multiple Sabbat characters. I have reviewed the book as a more general storyteller and someone who reviews books on their own artistic merits. But the visceral response of many people is not from the perspective of a neutral observer but someone who is heavily invested in Sabbat play and storytelling. I thought I would share my opinion from someone who has multiple characters from the Sword of Caine.

                                            The book is pretty damn useless.

                                            There's a few interesting tidbits here and there for Disciplines and angles for the Sabbat ritae as well as living with certain paths. I also like the idea of how the Sabbat might incorporate Thin Bloods and "Antitribu are now more important than Clan--the Tzimisce and Lasombra are nothing special anymore either." However, there's only really one way to use this book and presentation of the Sabbat if you are a die hard Path of Caine follower like myself.

                                            This book is about how the Sabbat died.

                                            I can easily tell you how to run a Chroncile about how the Sabbat got taken over by shovel heads, religious fanatics, and the most degenerate Low Path followers. I've actually run that campaign a couple of times because the erudite Nietzschean ubermensch vampires that I tended to play have always struggled against the lowest common denominator of the Black Hand. Perhaps why I was prepped for it, unknowingly, that the Sabbat were going to die.

                                            I never objected to Lucita joining the Sabbat. I objected to the Lasombra Trilogy depicting her as joining the stupid-evil Sabbat. Lucita was already an evil Elder. She just didn't need to become a "I kill orphans because I am BADASS" dummy as well as evil.
                                            However, this is not the story for someone who wants to see the Sword of Caine rise above its worst impulses. This is basically the story of Los Angeles by Night and the Anarchs where after they successfully acquired California, the Anarchs turned on each other to make a bunch of gang fiefdoms that are worse to live in as a Cainite than the Camarilla. I am 100% down to be able to tell the story of watching your faction become a parody of itself. However, there's no room for actual continued Sabbat play. It also is ONE story you can tell about the Sabbat. This is the Prequel Jedi period and rise of the Empire. If your ST isn't willing to let you kill Palpatine and Dooku/Anakin then it's going to be pretty depressing.

                                            The Sabbat of V5 are a parody of themselves. A not unbelievable parody that combines militia men, ISIS, gangs, and cultists but these are the Sabbat throwing away being Dracula and deciding to go all in on THIRTY DAYS OF NIGHT as well as VAMPIRE$. The 30 Days of Night and Vampire$ undead were always there. However, it is equivalent to the Brujah overthrowing Critias and becoming 100% iconoclasts. Which was actually kind of the story of V:TDA where the Brujah were getting dumber every century. I mean, Cristof is one of the smarter Brujah they embraced in the Dark Ages and Cristof is a moron.

                                            If you don't want/believe that the Sabbat's lowest common denominator will eventually overwhelm the secret manipulators then this will not be a story you want to tell. It's a story of the mob gaining control of the revolution and turning it into a Reign of Terror (and we were pretty Reign of Terror-y to begin with).

                                            Rats leaving a sinking ship is certainly a story arc to get with the PCs joining the Anarchs or the Camarilla (Or Ashirra or Inconnu) is there. I feel like a better story would have been to have given Blood Cult options to join. A "True Sabbat" that tries to rebuild what was lost or the Black Hand having become a separate organization from the swarm of locusts. I'm not afraid of tragedy in my vampire games, obviously, but if you're going to SINK a sect then you should include OPTIONS for past players.

                                            The Sabbat book should have had a whole ****ing chapter about, "So what does your Sabbat PC do now?"

                                                The idea that it "presents the Sabbat in a new way" when the praise for it, praise you yourself are quoting to compliment and note as yet another positive review, is being phrased as "restoring the original feel of the Sabbat" doesn't especially go together. Saying that the review you're trumpeting does not make a judgement doesn't feel possible when the very paragraph you quote of said revue comes off as pretty disdainful of how the Sabbat had been written post the core. Calling the V5 portrayal "purer" and statements like "just another Camarilla" are not things a person trying to sound benign about things says, as far as language. It's being judged just fine. You yourself devoted any number of posts in this thread to taking things to an outright moral dimension of why the Sabbat needed to be redone. There is an element of judgement that seems to unavoidably come with this praise, and it stands out. It makes the idea that people appreciate this book for building something up, instead of that they appreciate it for tearing something down, feel thin.

                                                It doesn't present the Sabbat in a new way, it mostly wants to present the Sabbat in the oldest way it possibly could with some added frills, and significant chunks of the book mostly exist as a Watsonian attempt to say why it makes sense to do so, as if to dodge the Doylist implications of what the underlying reasons come off as.

                                                The book doesn't add options, it takes them away, and puts visible effort into taking them away as far as if you wanted to opt into using V5 as someone into previous material. It was entirely possible to use the Sabbat as antagonists before, it was just also possible to use them in other ways. The idea that you can't possibly do both, that one excludes the other, comes off as deeply blinkered, and a statement that it's better when a game loses nuance and becomes basically about narrow absolutes, which, for VtM especially, is not great. Gaming doesn't actually have to be an either/or. It is in fact a lot easier to just not use extra material you don't like, than to have to come up with material out of nothing because the game has decided it no longer wants to help you out with that. In fact it's a pretty strong statement on that the energy and quality existed just fine to do both when "the Sabbat as only antagonists" is a markedly short, and thereby kinda painfully overpriced thing as a result.

                                                So, a related example: I used to hate the True Black Hand as something I found deeply moronic. Virulently hate it in that old, dumb, smug 90s gaming vibes way. I was even happy when they nuked it in revised. But after some time I realized that you know what? When it existed, I could have just ignored it. That blowing it up took away an option for people that liked that option. I realized that my getting to be right on the internet was mostly going "you know this thing you were enjoying? I am happy because now you don't get to enjoy it in the game going forward as far as material that helps you play or run games. I am happy at your expense." When you state happiness or approval for a game thing getting blowed up, you are unavoidably also saying "and thus I'm happy that people who enjoyed playing with their toy, got their toy taken away." That's just inescapable.

                                                So when V20 put it back? I realized that not only did I not really care, I actually thought "you know what, good for the people who wanted some support for doing a thing they like with a game they like." And me? I could just ignore it.
