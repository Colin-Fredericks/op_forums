        Instead of the usual, normal, prepared, and experienced construction crews another approach could be decided upon.                                         Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                                        Switching havens all the time as a security measure is high foolishness. You're wasting resources, telegraphing all the backup plans you have, and setting yourself up for failure since you really just need to install one tripmine and the guy'll fall one day or another. You have one really well managed haven, and then you have secret emergency havens you fall back to. If you're well-to-do and don't have so many enemies there's nothing wrong with having a well run second or third haven for convenience or to display wealth but it's not a security benefit.
                                        Beyond a certain level, Resources are endless, so wasting of resources is functionally a moot point.

                                        As for a trip mine, Vampires are unlikely to be killed by anti-personnel mines since they are designed to maim rather than kill mortals, so you would have to either use an anti-vehicle mine or rig an improvised incendiary trap, both of which have implementation issues.

                                        The anti-vehicle mine while directional requires the target be directly on top of it, which means unless you are in a rural area with dirt roads, someone will notice you jackhammering the vampire's driveway to install it, not to mention that unless you are controlling it by remote trigger, the mine isn't going to be discriminating in what triggers it. So, the mailman, the amazon driver, the ghoul who drives up to check on the property, or the mortal security service will all trigger it long before the vampire ever gets there. The scene in the first Godfather movie where the wife sets off the car bomb meant for Michael comes to mind.

                                        As for the incendiary trap, the tricky thing is hiding the incendiary materials from sight. You have to have enough material to create a fireball big enough to cover the distance from the hiding spot to the tripwire/trigger, most vampires will notice a 55-gallon drum of gasoline sitting on their front porch even if they can't see the tripwire. Sure, there are other materials that burn hotter and take up less space and thus could theoretically be hidden closer to the tripwire, but they are much harder to get ahold of and don't explode in huge plumes of fire like gasoline does. White Phosphorous for example burns very hot and will melt a vampire in short order, but it doesn't create a fireball, which means the delivery system needs a different set up than a tripwire. As with the anti-vehicle mine usage, this is likewise contingent on no one other than the vampire coming up to the house in question.

                                        On the subject of switching Havens, unless you are playing a character like a Nosferatu who lives outside of the public view, you technically should be changing Havens every 10 years or so to keep the masquerade intact since people will notice you are not aging. Unless you want to move out of the city, that means you have to maintain several potential havens to use in rotation, not returning to any given neighborhood for at least 20-40 years. That easily works out to three maybe four Havens just for time rotation, then a couple more that are hidden in case of emergencies, and then finally one or two in different cities in case things go to FUBAR levels of trouble. It is far simpler to buy all of these properties early on and switch between them, rather than trying to buy and sell them every decade.

                                        I know that turning VtM into a property management sim isn't what most people think of, but in truth, unless you are playing a wandering character like a Gangrel that just Earth Melds wherever there is open space, you kind of have to think in these terms.

                                                        Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                                                        I think you're overthinking the mine thing, it's just an example: you can do a whole bunch of things like fiddle with locks, change passwords, add more entrances, install cameras, mess with traps... that and it's not terribly hard to increase the yield of an explosive device or indeed use something properly incendiary like napalm, thermite or willy pete. Timed explosives are a good option too.
                                                        It's not a case of overthinking, so much as practical understanding.

                                                        Napalm is a jellied incendiary which doesn't really explode on its own, it is incredibly sticky and burns. It requires a proper dispersal device/secondary explosive to hit an area. Pouring burning napalm on a car won't do anything but ruin the paint and given enough time melt the plastic parts it is touching.

                                                        Thermite much like napalm isn't a standalone explosive, its just an incendiary. It burns at high temperatures, but that heat is condensed into an area only about as big as the volume of the Thermite. So, if you have a sandwich bag full of thermite, if you set it off, it will only burn what it is sitting on top of and cause radiant heat damage within a couple of feet.

                                                        In the case of both types of incendiary chemicals, the amount of damage is contingent on the volume of the material. Thus, a small ziplock bag of the stuff will burn out in a flash, with minimal damage to the surroundings, while a large canister of such material could burn for minutes on end. But once again the implementation of such a large canister makes creating the trap at the targets home base beyond impractical.

                                                        As for timed explosives, once again the implementation of such an attack is impractical. First you have to know which location the vampire is going to ahead of time to set everything up, you have to know exactly when it is safe to set it up, you have to know the exact travel time and arrival time so you can trigger the timer and leave before the vampire shows up, and even with all of that, if you are off by as little as 30 seconds, the target will be out of position to be damaged.

                                                        Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                                                        resources might be virtually infinite but keeping secrets doesn't scale that well. The bigger and more complicated your operation, the more things can go wrong. Changing havens every night just creating a host of vulnerabilities.
                                                        Your assumption is that the enemy has total knowledge of the entire system of havens. Using traps like this only work if the enemy doesn't know you are coming. If you poke around too much it tips them off, if you attempt to kill them and fail it tips them off. In both cases the target will then change their patterns and you are back to square one having no idea where they are or what they will do in response.

                                                        I would also point out that historically, never spending too much time in any one location has proven to be effective against large agencies or militaries wanting to execute the individual in question.
