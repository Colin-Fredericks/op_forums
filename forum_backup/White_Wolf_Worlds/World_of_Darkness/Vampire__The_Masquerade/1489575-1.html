    So, for those famiar with Gurps, particularly with 4e, everything has a cost in CP (character points).

    Now, there is the book Gurps: Vampire the Maquerade, but that's for Gurps 3e. The book is pretty much fine as it is, and anyone that likes the Gurps system and VtM setting can use it as it is.

    I have however made my own adaptation specifically for Gurps 4e, because I absolutely love how you can taylor make any kind of weird power or ability or very specific trait imaginable.

    In fact, I play ALL WoD games using Gurps 4e mechanics. I have also made adaptations for Mage the Ascension and in the Gurps forums I was publishing my entiŕe adaptation of Wraith the Oblivion for Gurps 4e (I must finish that some day... I just got a little bored, but I'll finish it, eventually).

    If any of you would like to see Wraith the Oblivion converted to Gurps 4e, it's here: http://forums.sjgames.com/showthread.php?t=180897

    It's almost done, all that needs finishing is the rules for haunts, relics, thorns for the Shadows and finally the many powers - althought the "magic abilities" are the easiest part, since it's only a matter of twinking the Advantages from Gurps. And the rules for Specters, but frankly, with what I posted there one can already play if they choose to. Im particularly proud for the rules over the Tempest Navigation that I made. But yes, it's not finished, and it's spread over a forum, so I need to place it in a pdf someday, but it can help to give you an idea on how this can be done.

    I particularly enjoy my adaptation of Mage the Ascension; for those familiar with Gurps Thaumatology, I use "Realm Magic", which essentially is like the Spheres; the difference however is that the Gurps system allows you to create your own "Spheres" and have them be balanced in terms of XP points.

    This has been absolutely AMAZING for me. Because now yes, the Hermetics have all the 9 Spheres. However, my Dreamspeaker have only 3: Body, Mind and Spirit (Body deals with the "illusions of materialism", thus it is roughly what in MtA would be Matter, Life and Forces, Mind deals with the "illusions of the senses", thus it is Correspondence, Time and Entropy - and no, the Hermetic "Mind" Sphere is NOT part of the "Mind Realm" of my Dreamspeakers, and Spirit is the "illusion of the self", thus is roughly what the Hermetic Spheres of Spirit, Prime and Mind are).

    My Technocrats dont have Correspondence and Time, instead having the Space/Time Sphere, and instead of Matter and Life, they have Biochemistry Sphere. Thus my Technocrats (and Etherites) have 7 Spheres.

    And my VAs have only the Data Sphere; everything for my VAs is Data (and it's a VERY expensive Sphere in terms of XP due to that).

    Besides those awesome changes, I also use "Sub-Spheres", which are far cheaper. For instance, "Pyromancy", for my "Fire Mages", is a Sphere that deals only with Fire Magik. Thus, my Pyromancer can create or control fire, but cant levitate objects. Likewise, my "Winter Witch" can specialize in the "Cryomancy" Sphere, and acquire control over ice and freeze for a much cheaper price than buying the entire Forces Sphere. Or a "Plant Druid" can have only "Plant Sphere" instead of the entirety of Life, or just "Bird Sphere", and so on so forth.

    So, in the specific case of VtM:

    First of all I have generation be a special kind of UB (Unusual Background). 13th gen is 0 cost, each gen bellow that costs 5 char points (CP) as an UB. So, for example, being of the 9th gen would costs 20 CP.

    I dont usually allow gen bellow 8, but if I did, I would charge exponentially more. So, to be a 8th gen costs 25; instead of charging another 5 points for gen 7, I would charge an extra 10 (so it would be 35 total). For gen 6, an extra 15 (so it would be 50 CP total). For gen 5, extra 20 on top of the anterior (so 70 CP total) and for gen 4, another extra 25 (so 95 CP total). That is of course, if players could even achieve that much, which probably would never happen (with gen 8 being the most).

    On top of this costs which are just an Unusual Background, the characters must also buy their "Blood Pools". For those familiar with Gurps Powers, this is just ER (Energy Reserve), and it costs just 1 CP per point. The Pool is related to generation, as is in D10 system. At 10 "Blood Pool", there is, the max amount of Blood you can have, you are at the 13 gen, and that is part of the "Racial Template" of vampires, so you dont pay for it (you already "paid" the points for being a vamp). At 12th gen however, you have a Blood Pool of 11, so you must pay an additional 1CP for having 1 extra in your pool (thus the 12th gen actually costs 6 CP total, 5 for the UB and 1 for the extra Blood Pool).

    At 9th gen you can spend 2 blood per turn, at 8th gen you can spend 3, at 7th is 5, at 6th is 6 and so forth, just like in the D10 system of the WoD, and the pool for 7th gen is 20, 25 blood pool for 6th and so forth, exactly the same.

    Now, human limits of attributes in Gurps are 20. So that's the limits up to gen 8. Now, it must be remembered that 20 in anything is already INSANE for Gurps, and ANY attribute above 16 is highly cinematographic - even Albert Einstein would probably NOT have an IQ above 17, and Bruce Lee hardly had DX above 16.

    For each gen bellow 8, those upper limits increase by 1. So it's 21 max attributes for gen 7, 22 for gen 6, 23 for gen 5, 24 max for gen 4 and finally 25 max attributes for the antes. Those attributes also have the Enhancement +25% to allow skills to default to those above 20; this means that a 3rd gen Ante with 25 IQ can learn how to use a computer in just a few minutes, and be better at it than most experts. It truly is insane.

    I also let Talents go up to 5 (as opposed to 4 which is the norm), and each gen bellow 8th also increases this upper limit by 1 point - thus a Methusa 4th gen can reach Talents 9 and a 3rd Ante can have Talents at 10, which is once again insanely powerful (as it must).

    For the "Disciplines", althought I play "fast and loose" with them, there are several powers that are leveled, and thus generation determine those upper levels.

                Originally posted by omenseer View Post
                Had a thought. That happens sometimes...

                If one made a solid conversion would they be allowed to put on the Storytellers Vault?
                That depends on the system you convert to and, more importantly, what do you describe of it in your work.

                But in principle, yes. If your conversion follows the rules of the Vault and you put nothing in the document that is protected IP or ™ of the other game, then it is ok. And by US (and Brazilian, for that matter, but US is what matters here) law, mathematical systems can't be copyrighted themselves, what can be are other aspects of a game.

                Even then, I would recommend careful thought being given towards each one. Make as few references to the other game as possible so you focus exclusively on WoD material and just translating rules. If a system has an SRD document, it is a safe material to use on this work. Beware the rules of places like D&D's DM's Guild as they're special cases just like the Storyteller Vault, so what they let you do there does not applies elsewhere.

                You may also simply approach both companies if you have something you think is worth publication for mutual benefit. While that's tricky, it isn't out of the realm of reason for them to agree, and then you have the leeway they afford you through an actual licensing, but be sure to have an almost finished product to show before trying, or a really solid project.

                And always be extra careful with layout, both of the book and sheets. Layout is a part of ™ and copying is considered infringement similar to identity theft, a © license isn't the same as a ™ use authorization.
