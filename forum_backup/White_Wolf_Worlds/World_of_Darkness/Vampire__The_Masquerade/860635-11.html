                    Originally posted by Telgar View Post
                    The game is not *designed* for that. You can use a knife to eat yogurt, too, it's just not the intended use of a knife. There are sandbox games. That's a term that has specific meaning in RPG design. There are games like Savage Worlds that can be adapted to any plot, any setting, whatever. They're sandbox games. You're given tools to build whatever you want, like LEGOs. VtM is not LEGOs. And if VtM was LEGOs, it would be one of the "built this thing from that movie" kits. Not the big box of bricks to enjoy with nothing but your imagination and physics.
                    I think you're conflating sandbox/toolbox settings with sandbox-style games. Requiem is an explicitly toolbox setting. Masquerade was relatively open-ended in 1e and 2e, went through a few metaplot-driven years during Revised, and has swung around to a "choose whatever option works for you" format for V20. I'd say V20 is more "choose your own adventure" than pure toolbox, but I digress.

                    Sandbox-style games can be run in conjunction with either type of setting. If I slavishly adhere to metaplot and turn a coterie of PCs loose in a 2001 New York City where the Camarilla just ousted the Sabbat and Calebros stepped down as Prince and Don Polonia still has a suntan problem, I'm running my game in a non-modular, out-of-the-box setting. If I have no overarching storyline planned beyond "have the world react to PC actions," I'm running a sandbox game.

                    Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.

                            Originally posted by Telgar View Post
                            VtM is not "Sandbox" at..
                            Hi!

                            I think you confuse sandbox and toolbox. Toolbox systems like Savage Worlds, Fuzion, Cyphersystem, D&D 3+, Genre Division, etc. give a rules framework and then you can do anything you like with it. Many of these toolbox systems have one or two main designermade settings (e.g. Numenera for Cypher), which are sandboxes to play with.
                            A sandbox system has a rules set and a broad setting, but the setting is openended for the gaming groups to fill in. VtM is definitely a sandbox system since there is a fixed background, but how you play it out is your groups choice. There is no OPP police coming for you if you decide to play a group of Gangrel in the hinterland woods and what they do for 400 years, instead of a city campaign with intense politics and most clans involved.

Worst comes to worst, you could always get the Shadow-eaten involved.
