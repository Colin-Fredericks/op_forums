    How to play a cleaver?

    06-02-2023, 07:42 AM
    I'm still working on my character for Vampire 5. Hopefully I'm going to have a session 0 tomorrow but I'm still unsure about the "predator type".
    Right now I'm considering the "cleaver" archetype, it thrills me but at the same time it feel like a huge pain in the ass, a bad omen and a walking on eggshells.
    Do you have any hint about how to play a good "cleaver"?
    And first of all, how my character can explain to his family, relatives and friends why he/she is always missing until the sunset?

      Best way to play a cleaver is to.. don't. But it's all good fun so let's move to the second best way: don't go anywhere near other vampires, which might have problems at the table, so the third best way: be distant, and target non-family members for feeding unless your family is like, really big.

      I don't play V5, but if I did, the Cleaver is a pretty good predator type to start with because it has nice bonuses... and after getting those bonuses you should abandon cleaving right away. But let's say you actually want to play a character with low EQ who's mentally health is dependent on his family being nearby, and you can't move to somewhere I've never heard of for some reason.


      -Buy a property adjacent to your family so you never get seen going into the same front door as the single person with the dog and two kids that look like you. You can build a secret entrance in there.
      Buy more properties around your property so you can say the last purchase is just a security thing. Maybe if you rent them out and have secret entrances into those properties too you can build plausible deniability as a sandman.
      -Make yourself useful and get into prestation debt, seriously,. if you owe people they'll want to keep you around for longer and might be more willing to overlook things. Of course they might just slaughter your family to protect you but that's a problem for later.
      -Change names of course.
      -Having dominate.. no wait that doesn't work well for V5... is bind the accusing tongue ritual still a thing? powers like that are good.
      -prepare blackmail and murder schemes.
      -please, please don't ghoul your family...

              Okay
              So...
              Yikes
              Yeah... it is not the best of Predator types for a first timer, don't get me wrong!
              But lets deep dive, shall we?
              The whole idea is from superstitions. In older times, funerary carriages were drawn through confusing routes on the way to the cemetery; this was because there was a fear that if the deceased became undead, they would find their way back to their home and murder their family.
              Quite simple, really
              Dark Secret: Cleaver as a Flaw means if you would be exposed you would be ridiculed by society for it, not to metion the tons of targets on your head
              A Cleaver's family/friends is a huge weakness that can be easily exploited should their enemies find out about it. The Second Inquisition leans most heavily on these predators because they can be the easiest to turn.
              The Giovanni in their whole history was the prime examples of a Cleaver if you think about it
              The Cleaver starts with a specialty either in Gaslighting or in Coverups, which means you must be adept at either manipulating your family/friends or must be skilled at erasing the evidence of your... misdeeds.
              There are several concepts
              The Gang Member, who still serves the homies as long as they provide him with a meal every now and then
              The Historian, who has followed their family for generations, maintaining it like a gardener maintains a tree
              The Boogeyman, who attempts to keep their family in line by controlling them through fear.
              Just to name a few

              One of the biggest things to remember for this predator type, is the fact, your family and/or friends will be the integral part of the story.
              You will give a job to your ST because he will have to make those thinking, living, breathing individuals that can be used to remind the vampire of their humanity, to contrast the darkness with which they usual deal, and to possibly be threatened by that very darkness; they are not just a simple herd with no life. - so I strongly suggest, try to help your ST and iron out the details together!
              The most extreme cleavers adopt children, marry a human, and try to maintain a family life for as long as they can.
              Cleavers often go to great lengths to keep the truth of their condition from their family, but some also maintain unwholesome relationships with their own kin.
              But secrecy is not actually required, with taking the risk, you can let them in the truth, they will be your Alfreds Batman! Your very own Shazam-family with a death sentence or a promise, they could join you one night in the Darkness if behave. Even could build a Cult from them, Settite style!
              The Camarilla actually forbids taking a human family in this fashion, and it frowns on cleavers as Masquerade breaches waiting to happen. - Wiser Kindred may massacre your family for your own good if they find out your secret and care what happens to you.

              The "easiest" way is to mimic the Giovanni to be honest when playing a Cleaver or similar very close Family, like a Ravenant Family who knows the "truth"​

                  Originally posted by Ipergigio View Post
                  Right now I'm considering the "cleaver" archetype, it thrills me but at the same time it feel like a huge pain in the ass, a bad omen and a walking on eggshells.
                  Do you have any hint about how to play a good "cleaver"?
                  You should pick a predator type that best suits the character you intend to play instead of the other way around. You can always discuss modifying certain aspects of a predator type to better fit your PC. If you are already having feelings that this is not going to be fun, it's probably a good sign it is not a good fit for you. IMO, the difficult aspects of your character should be the kind of things you like to roleplay as that is going to be a large part of the scenes you need to resolve. The challenges inherent in any predator type or character concept should be fun for you, not the things you desperately want to avoid facing in the game.

                  But assuming you are going to play a cleaver, the key idea is that the vampire hasn't accepted the need that he has changed and needs to change his life. Instead, he is pretending he's still the same person he was, just a little different. A cleaver is basically in denial and still attempting to lead his old life. That he feeds on the humans he knew while a mortal (because it is the easiest thing for him), is just one aspect of that.

                  It is possible that as your PC is played in the chronicle, that the PC will grow out of it and stop being in denial. The predator type just represents what he's been doing from his embrace until play begins.

                  So a cleaver will have had to make certain adjustments, but he's trying to keep on living his "life" as if he had not become a vampire. The most likely reason he feeds on the people he already knows is that it is simply the easiest thing for him. Those people already trust him, will let him into their house, may make excuses for him, etc. There could be other reasons - he's too cowardly to hunt on his own, too incompetent to master the art of hunting, or has developed some perverse thrill at victimizing the people he knew before - but they are probably a minority of the type. But they are options.
                  ​
                  Originally posted by Ipergigio View Post
                  And first of all, how my character can explain to his family, relatives and friends why he/she is always missing until the sunset?
                  Unless he is someone actually living with people in the same house (married, has children, roommates, etc.) this should not actually be a problem. Friends and even family may not even notice for a long time that the PC only shows up after dark, social events being what they are. People have to live their own life, and their schedules may be in conflict. There may be certain daytime events that people may expect the PC show up to (the kid's baseball game, someone's holiday party, etc.), but that can often be explained away by simply providing more details on why they cannot make it. Only seeing a friend at night is far different than someone thinking "Hey, this person is ONLY seen after dark by EVERYONE". Assumptions would be that, "of course Bob must be talking and seeing OTHER people during the day" (mechanics, neighbors, coworkers, etc.) even if I don't.

                  If there are humans living with you, then that changes. The only options you have is either move out, or go to the blood bond or some other means of mental control.

                  The other hard part is how can the character earn the money needed for a normal lifestyle. Even working second shift or third shift will usually cross over into the daytime at some point. So it may mean lots of part time work where the PC can only work 4 hours a night or something, perhaps with some other side hustle. Major changes in one's lifestyle or career might make more things difficult than simply only seeing friends and family socially after dark. It would be the kind of thing that gets people talking about "what's wrong".

                  So ask yourself 1) why your character is "cleaving" onto his mortal life instead of moving on sufficiently to minimize it, 2) why is he mostly feeding on the people he knows (probably his closest family and friends, though this may not be a requirement), and 3) how exactly he is feeding on them (do they not know, is it consensual, is it an abusive/co-codependent relationship, and so on). If you can figure this out, you can probably start filling in any other details as needed.

                  Also important to remember a predator type is NOT the only way the character feeds. Or even that this is mostly the way the character feeds. It is merely an important "specialization" of his hunting style that has provided him both strengths and weaknesses.

                      Explaining your nightly activities to your family:
                      • "I got a new job on the graveyard shift, it pays great." (and as a vampire, it's pretty easy to acquire extra cash)
                      • "Don't question me, woman. Wives do not question their husbands; know your place."
                      • Copious applications of dominate.
                      • Your parents are too high to care.
                      • Your relative has Alzheimer's, and either don't really notice or they're easy to gaslight.
                      • Flat out tell them you're a vampire.

                      Originally posted by Black Fox View Post

                      You should pick a predator type that best suits the character you intend to play instead of the other way around.
                      I don't know about that, characters generally have a starting concept, and family-orientated isn't a bad place to start building from. Like first work out why a person might be so attached to their family, what that suggests about their personality and skills and what clans might be drawn to such an individual*... and the cracks that foreshadow the future mess in the making.

                      ​​​​​​
                      ​​​​​​
                      (*With their new clan flaw forcing them to stay with their chosen "charges", Tzimisce are a rather obvious choice for clan. Ventrue as well, as they may be forced into the predator type by their weakness. Then the Giovanni bloodline is another highly likely candidate due to clan culture.)

                      People have already explained some of the thoughts that go into your character, so I'll throw in some ideas for characters who might cling to their families.
                      ​​​​​Some Cleaver concepts:

                      Child of a broken home who's grown up bending over backwards to keep it functioning and intact.
                      Perhaps there's a parent at risk of dying of addiction, another crippled by depression and siblings who will be stolen away by Social Services.
                      This character knows how to keep secrets, how to be loyal, how to stay in control in a crisis, how to play the peacekeeper. Now that they have power, they're going to start using it in a misguided attempt to fix things. And of course, that bottled up stress from bearing all that responsibility all their lives is now resting on the shoulders of the Beast...

                      Family patriarch/matriarch (possibly a controlling, abusive one) who is convinced the family needs them? A traditional nuclear family unit who's big into Family Values™?

                      Or maybe the family was tightly knit and loving, but poor, and now that one of them is undead, they're going to change that...

                      The reverse of the family head: a socially anxious mess who has grown up dependent on their family. Perhaps their entire social circle and emotional support is contained within their family. Possibly this was done to them on purpose.

                      Grew up in a fundamentalist home where they were taught to stay close because the world outside the family and religious community was dangerous and tainted. Perhaps they strayed from this and got turned and clearly their family was right and this is divine punishment. Now they're clinging to their family, hoping that if they find their way back on the right path god will forgive them.

                      Would be (or actual) divorcee who's repaired their marriage with a blood bond.

                      Someone who lost offspring or siblings to an ex or CPS or something is emboldened to steal them back in undeath.

                      Perhaps the vampire didn't originally have a family in life, despite wanting one, and now that they can no longer make a biological family, they're going to put the blood bond and dominate to good use and make their perfect family. (Vampire foster parents?)

                      Twilight, except without the rose-tinted filter of the hormonal and possibly mentally ill protagonist, so the ugly undertones of the setting are so much more prominent.

                      Prone to hyperbole
