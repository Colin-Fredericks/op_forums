                                                            I also think having the Antitribu be a moniker used by all Sabbat clans could help simplify matters and, as others have mentioned, it would certainly jive with the historical Sabbat philosophy of “actually clan-loyalty above all else was Bad™️ and it was used as a tool of the elders to enslave us, we should abandon it for the unity of Caine’s Sabbath”.

                                                            That said I do think there’s an interesting wrinkle that comes in from the trajectory of Sabbat history we get sketched out, with that attitude being more or less explicitly the feeling of the anarchic proto-Sabbat and their ideological descendants in the Loyalist faction. However I do think the notion of the significant chunk of Lasombra and Tzimisce elders who joined the sect in its early nights bringing a more conservative attitude that has endured adds an interesting element, where despite their lip service we see these elders not wanting to have to actually give up the benefits of clan power, especially if we interpret their addition as being largely due to a significant clique of Lasombra elders viewing the Tower as a Brujah-Toreador-Ventrue conspiracy.

                                                            This element also has an important effect on the Pander question IMO. The Pander’s whole story takes place in the strange narrative gray area where the Sabbat has just enough of its original ideology left to allow self-conscious Caitiff organization to exist and gain structural power but also enough traditionalism to have significant sections of the sect view the phenomena with extreme distrust and hostility.

                                                            I do think one could square this circle though, by having Anttitribu be embraced as a pan-Sabbat moniker while noting that internally the Conservative faction (which has disproportionate influence on the College of Cardinals and the Pricii) practices many pre-Sabbat traditions and customs in secret and to maintain clan-oriented patronage and power networks within the sect (particularly among the Lasombra but also among the minority of elders who defected from other clans).

                                                            Also, this may be a hot-take: I think it makes more sense to emphasis the Lasombra as the poster-child for this phenomena than the Tzimisce. The way the lore developed with the split between flesh-crafter Tzimisce and the Old Clan, it makes more sense IMO for the Tzimisce Antitribu to be true Sabbat in their ideology (given that they’re probably descended from the Tzimisce who rebelled against the Old Clan’s use of Dark Ages style customs and methods of domination). That said, I do like the idea of the Tzimisce being the most spiritually conservative clan. If we keep the idea that the Vinniculum was discovered by the Tzimisce before being spread throughout the Anarch Revolts, I think the idea of the Sabbat Tzimisce remaining significantly dedicated to the esoteric doctrines of the cults that originally developed and distributed the ritual, and that those occult doctrines should probably be emphasized as one of the major influences on the development of the Sabbat’s belief system and ideology.
