    How many Characters is "too much"?

    12-17-2022, 08:17 PM
    So, when thinking about creating a story and developing it through the course of a great chronicle, do you believe there's a certain "ideal number" of NPC characters in a story? I was arguing with a colleague, and he's of the opinion we should keep the number limited to at most half a dozen important NPCs as he argues too many would "make the players forget about them and not build a strong enough connection with them". While I like the idea of taking a "Game of Thrones" approach to stories, i.e. character's driven and full of deep characters, with detailed backstory, etc. as I want to "give life" to the world and story I'm creating. As such, I think that the story only gains from a good number of characters with a good backstory and drama etc.

    I'd like to know your opinion on this matter. How has been your experience with games so far? Have you ever suffered from having way too many characters or the opposite, having perhaps too few? Do you think a game can really suffer from having many "deep characters"? How do you think it affects the game you're storytelling? Is there a balance or an ideal number of sorts? I'm really interested in hearing similar or different approaches to the subject.

Each power player has an agenda that goes on in the background, whether the PC's are aware of it or not.

                      In my experience, players are very goal oriented as opposed to character oriented, so those characters not directly (and obviously) related to their goals get overlooked unless there is something about them that grabs their interest.

                      In addition, they have limited interested in even those characters they do care about. If someone is an opponent, they care about their capabilities and motives, not any convoluted backstories. Players in an RPD approach the game much differently than a reader approaching a book. Things a reader would love in learning about a particular antagonist becomes irrelevant if that same person runs a PC in an RPG.

                      I don't think the game suffers if the ST gives detailed backstories of many dozens of NPCs. However, I do think it'd be a lot of unnecessary work as so much of that characterization will never come into play. The optimization of time spent developing the chronicle and NPCs and the actual value to the PCs tends towards less work, not more in my experience. After a certain point of key NPCs, an ST would be better off utilizing his time in other aspects of chronicle preparation than character development.

                      Do the minimum necessary to figure out the NPCs role and motivation in the chronicle, and then only fill in the details if the players show actual interest in that character.

                      If you figure out how to do that, please let me know because I definitely spend a lot more time on NPCs than is needed in my own chronicles.

                          When I'm coming up with a Chronicle, especially one I plan to have last for a time, I'll try and come up with around 15-20 NPCs.                                       A good way to look at NPC creation might be to start with major characters, who potentially have other NPCs below or around them. The characters are all nominally allied, at least on the surface, which is why they're being grouped together. But if PCs dig in more, they may discover the different constituent parts of the group have their own baggage, priorities, and relationships.

                                      Examples:
                                      • The Ventrue Primogen and their brood
                                      • The Brujah biker and their gang
                                      • The Malkavian "street seer" and their following of mortal transients
                                      • The Tremere Regent and their chantry
                                      • The Nosferatu informant for the local warren
                                      • The Inconnu Monitor and their acolytes
                                      • Etc, etc.
                                      The exact arrangement is context dependent, and the NPCs clustering around the central one can be inferiors, superiors, equals, etc. Bound by common cause, sect or bloodline affiliation, magical or interpersonal ties, obligation, or brute pragmatism. The important bit is the NPC the party meets will be their nominal introduction to their group. The contact that can go between the PCs and the related NPCs. The players will only learn about or even meet the ancillary NPCs to the degree they take interest in them. Like peeling a social onion.

                                      Of course, no person is an island, and even groups the party shows little interest in can be MADE relevant through social entanglements that cross factional bounds. You know, just in case you made an NPC the party didn't get a chance to meet, and you'd really rather they did. Just make them an ally, enemy, or something else to one or more characters within a faction they ARE dealing with.

                                      Magestuck: The Ascension

                                              A story should only have as many characters as is needed to tell the story.

                                              For every writer who creates a truly in-depth world with interesting characters who interact with each other, there are a dozen writers who just stack random side characters into every nook and cranny of the story with no real benefit and often to the reader's detriment. So, making worthwhile characters is its own skill.

                                              In RPGs the number of characters is fluid because despite you as the ST crafting a set of memorable characters with interconnected backstories and plots, the PCs will inevitably become obsessed with a random no-name NPC that you threw together on the spot to give a scene some life. So regardless of how many NPCs you originally planned for, the PCs will always figure out a way to add more to the story. Best to keep the number of NPCs low and leave yourself some room in case the roster grows.

                                              Template character groups are a mixed bag in my opinion. This makes things very easy to produce in a short amount of time, but it also means the content is shallow and unrefined for the games needs. Template groups are to RPGs what frozen dinners are to home cooking, fine in a pinch, but unfulfilling and potentially unhealthy in the long run. Don't get me wrong though, there is nothing wrong with taking a template group and slotting it in as a place holder while you refine your ideas, but if that refinement never happens or the concept is never revisited, then the game design can fall apart.

                                                      Originally posted by Thoth View Post
                                                      In RPGs the number of characters is fluid because despite you as the ST crafting a set of memorable characters with interconnected backstories and plots, the PCs will inevitably become obsessed with a random no-name NPC that you threw together on the spot to give a scene some life.
                                                      Yep, you never know what NPCs will become important in a chronicle. I try to provide some level of flavor text, no matter how minor, to NPCs a player may interact with, and it is surprising how providing one little innocent detail makes a player interested in that character, and you must create background on the fly as the relationship becomes more important. In some cases the only reason that NPC existed was I needed some flavor text background for that scene and wasn't even a real character.

                                                      While I may describe certain important NPCs in full detail, for the rest I try to keep them within the "index card rule." Everything important about a character - description, motive, history, backgrounds, key dice pools, etc. - must fit on a 3x5 inch index card and have room left over. That boils everything down to their essentials. I may not need anything more. And if that NPC for whatever reason becomes more important, I can easily build the character up as needed.

                                                      I don't have index cards for every kind of character - many ghouls or other retainers might only be a brief phrase on some other character's index card, or might only just be an undescribed "dot" that I create on the fly for an unexpected scene. But if they do become more important, they may get their own index card or even full write up at some time.
