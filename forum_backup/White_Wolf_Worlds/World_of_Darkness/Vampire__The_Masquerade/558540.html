    Rworking the Kupala plot in Transylvania Chronicles

    06-17-2015, 03:32 PM
    I'm pondering STing the Translyvania Chronicles, and was thinking about reworking the Kupala plot angle, to make the whole thing seem a bit less contrived. I haven't quite finished reading the books yet, but I wanted to get some ideas down and out for discussion before they went out of my head. This mostly involves my own slightly different take on the Baali, which is still evolving, so may not entirely make sense yet. One big meta assumption/change here is that Kupala is a Sleeper.

    Basically, I envision making the Baali ongoing antagonists for the Chronicles, what with their connections to the infernal. Destroying a Baali nest at Budapest and finding hints pointing towards the tablet in their rituals and such would be the starting point. Much of Octavio's stuff would be subsumed in here, to the point where he's dropped as a character. Anatole may be introduced earlier, but possibly not.

    After this point, the characters may or may not build Tihuta Castle - I never understood why a patron asks them to build it if all they wanted was the tablet, given that they do so many fetch-quests anyway. It feels like a convenient point to introduce Zelios more than anything else, who could, again, be eliminated in favour of various geomantic hints in places that the PCs are sent over the course of other storylines.

    I also want to straighten out why the Tremere are so involved - they seem incidental, but involved everywhere. I think the key to figuring this out is reworking Saulot, given his probable involvement with the Baali. I know that Saulot's diablerie is the first sign of Gehenna, but....

    Hang on...

    What if Saulot wants the end of the world all along, and is the one orchestrating it all through Tremere?

    The Baali were a failed experiment thanks to Moloch, and so he starts gain with the Tremere, through his willing diablerie, hence why it is the first sign of Gehenna. So while the Baali are the evil devil-worshipping things, Kupala trying to impart things through them (whether they're trying to summon it or keep it asleep is a question that needs an answer here), so they're kind of the salvation of the world, ironically. Their involvement is why all the signs of Gehenna point to/are linked to Kupala, which is the source of Anatole's confusion over the prophecy (that the signs point to Kupala rather than Gehenna). They also provide an easy potential source of the geomantic information without Zelios - potentially being able to leech Kupala from these points, or being directed their by it in order to defend the parts it knows are its weak spots.

    Given this, what other changes are needed to the structure of TC to join up the dots? Anything that I've missed? Apologies for all the thinking aloud!

                                          Let your players come up with a clever plan for how to pull the fat out of the fire, that's their job. The GM just comes up with situations to throw at them (or at least, that's how I do things in my own games). What you should do, though, is adapt the game in response to your players' actions and choices. Whole point of playing RPGs and all, and where the Transylvania Chronicles fall so very flat. They're probably the most railroady set of adventures I've ever read.

                                          Even if your players don't make any effort to change the situation to their favor (smuggling out Maria, calling in allies, waylaying "guests" from ever arriving at the party), though... you should at least roll dice for the Baali and Giovanni trying to kill Maria. As I remember, the book just says 'Maria wards off the demons with her True Faith, then Ambrogino or the lead Baali-dude kills her.' No dice involved or anything, despite the presumed presence of PCs trying to prevent that very thing from happening. That's just dumb. Have the NPCs roll Brawl or Melee against Maria as normal, and give your PCs a fair chance to take them out before they can take Maria out (re: by using actual game mechanics when you're playing out the scenario). Whether she lives or dies, abiding by the fall of the dice is always preferable to the GM arbitrarily declaring "such and such happens, nothing players do can matter."

                                          Also, that "Last Cappadocian" thing is BS. There's Japheth, Lazarus/the Capuchin, Drenis, Angelique, Lady Constancia/Unre, the other Harbingers of the Skulls... probably a few others I'm not remembering off the top of my head, all before the PC in your game. Granted, maybe those individuals don't exist or won't be appearing in your game, but you certainly shouldn't feel obligated for Maria to be the world's last Cappadocian because the book says so. White Wolf's other writers didn't.

                                          Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.
