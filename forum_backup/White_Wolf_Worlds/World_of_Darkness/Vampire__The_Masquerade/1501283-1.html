                The biggest problem I see to the WoD getting back into a sort of mainstream limelight is that while it captured the Zeitgeist of the 90’s and rode it like a champ, it hasn’t done nearly so well adapting to the Zeitgeist as it has continued to evolve.

                A biggie in this regard is it’s failure to acknowledge that the rebels of the 90’s are now the establishment of today. The edginess of 90’s LGBT culture is now normal and celebrated by mainstream culture in the 2020’s. Media that doesn’t offer inclusion is lambasted in pop culture and the “Moral Majority” of the 80s/90s is a punchline in the modern days of clerical abuse scandals.

                The 90’s rebels are the now the CEO’s and politicians able to enact their beliefs through things like Diversity Equity and Inclusion and Environmental Social Governance scores to shape how businesses and organizations operate.

                Trying to claim the same social outcasts of the 90’s are still the oppressed outcasts just doesn’t ring true and without truth you’re never going to be able to capture that ephemeral thing that is the Zeitgeist that will ride you into the mainstream.

                If you ask me, the present spirit of the age is Atomization. Social media connects the world more tightly, but our youth have never felt more disconnected from each other within its web. The ability to define your own meaning for everything has led to a devaluation of even the concept of “meaning.” Teens are committing suicide at epidemic rates because they can’t find meaning in their own lives or fail to live up the impossible ideals of social media influencers and have few friends outside of their social media accounts.

                Meanwhile the world grows darker and their futures more uncertain; environmental and economic collapse, pandemics and escalating wars. They fear forming families and having children of their own because of what they believe they’d be bringing that child into. A youth of today could be forgiven for believing the Apocalypse is at their doorstep.

                Isolation and fear are the Zeitgeist of the present age, IMHO. If VtM wants to be mainstream again in the present day, it needs to offer material that speaks to that spirit; either in offering hope/purpose (ex. play up Clan as family and a way to be part of something… perhaps more mono-Clan scenarios in books) or in reaffirming their fears as valid (ex. not great for a ttrpg, but in a video game the idea of isolation and impending doom is something easy to work with).

                                                    I'm still not really comfortable with how "mainstream" is being used as a short hand for, "mainstream in English speaking cultures."

                                                    It's even worse to me as we continue to see that the attempt of media hegemony in the Anglosphere has actually increased how much general audiences are turning towards entertainment from other cultures. South Korea, thanks to it's long term cultural exchange with the US after the Korean War, has really mastered making live-action entertainment that's still accessible to Western culture audiences. You don't need to actually be all that open minded to be a zombie fan and love the stuff coming out of Korean that's helping to revitalize the subgenre. Squid Game took off just fine, and people not getting it weren't missing the point because of a language barrier (since plenty of people have missed the same points when English language satires make them). Parasite and Minari both had massive critical acclaim and English language audiences came out in droves for them.

                                                    Gen X, Millenials, and Zoomers are all used to watching stuff from other cultures. Gen X and older Millenials generally had the experiences of seeing how bad localization used to be and started actively pushing for less "creative" reinterpretations that changed things from the originals that didn't need changing (how we got the whole sub vs. dub wars). Was the American remake of Old Boy bad per se? Not really. But I've never met anyone that's watched the original and the American remake actually recommend watching the remake. If you really can't handle subtitles, the dub is still better than a remake that fails to actually do anything with the material to make it worth your time.
