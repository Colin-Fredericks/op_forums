        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

        Eh, I think that isn't what the book goes for. The book goes to elaborate lengths about the Paths and how they have replaced Clan Culture. It's just the Paths are sadistic, vicious, and nasty in this version. Which they always were but tended to get couched in flowery post-human terms. The Sabbat always reveled in their hatred and disdain of humans. This book doesn't shy away from that.

        Which is to say, "wights are just dangerous murderous predators" while the Sabbat is actively evil and malevolent.

        I'm talking more in the Doyalist sense. The narrative role that the developers intend Sabbat vampires to fill at the gaming table is much more similar to that of wights (NPC monsters you can't really interact with or redeem) than it is to vampires of a different sect. So from the base when considering the Sabbat in the setting it's better to do so with wights in mind than thinking of them as another vampire sect/faction.
