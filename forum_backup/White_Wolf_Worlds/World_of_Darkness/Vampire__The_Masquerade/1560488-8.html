                    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

                    Unfortunately I can't say any of this surprise me, because Ericsson was from start too easy to profile. And all those things are wrong, not from your part, but his.

                    V5 is not intended to be a depart in any sense, but a return, a pure 1st edition experience.

                    If you don't get this vibe from the game, it's a testament to how misguided this idea is from the start.

                    As many fans, he's a romantic that want return to a game that never existed but in his head, a curious trick our memory can play with us. This is not new or surprising, but is telling, because this kind of person is always chasing an ideal past that can't exist.

                    Hence why you get this impression: it is made to be a slightly improved 1st Edition, which isn't a new game by any standard, but it is made out of a fantasy full of things that weren't there.

                    For the reason of stopping CofD, it's equally misguided. Understandable, but utterly incorrect. The best time for both games was when V20 and VtR2nd were both actively releasing and reinforcing each other. Onyx Path had a lot of success doing just that. Paradox had zero to actually gain doing what they did, it was a plain bad decision.

                    But it was intuitive for someone with his mindset, because this kind of person despise what doesn't confirm to their vision and see as an obstacle regardless. He hated CofD on principle, as in his heart it is a thorn in the way of a truly masterful game.
                    AIUI, Ericsson has said he actually wanted to do VTR3e instead of V5. He was told to stick to WoD as the more recognisable brand of the two, but this explains why the system borrows so liberally from CofD.

                    But it's also worth remembering that Ericsson wasn't the only person involved in writing V5. Don't forget, Paradox specifically wanted Ken Hite to do Nights Black Agents from the vampire's perspective.

                    As you mentioned, they also had MRH, who has always had strong views about his games, but they also had Justin Achilli, who also has traditionally had strong views about the game. They were also told to include certain elements from the version of Bloodlines 2 that never was, such as Blood Resonance and You Are What You Eat l.

                    As such, V5 has similarities to both V1 and Revised, but also tries to do a bunch of new things all at once. While it aims to capture a version of the game that never really existed, it also often adopts (and exaggerates) Revised's approach to "rectifying" things (literally blowing stuff up for being "tonally inconsistent" without any hint of irony), being vague about things (intentionally excluding concrete dates and prioritising "mystery" over clarity), and ST/player guidance (wagging its finger at badwrongfun, etc).

                    After release, the books were also developed by a range of licensees, so tonally and quality-wise, they were all over the place. This is presumably why they're bringing the whole thing back in-house for the entire run of V6. Many people who stuck with V20 rules would've still bought V5 books just for the lore and any new ideas they could crib. But if you don't know whether the next V5 book is going to be another Cults of the Blood Gods or another Camarilla, you're less likely to take the risk and buy a copy.

                    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                    The result was exactly to be expected:
                    - the company has no know-how in the area,
                    - this is the pet project of a person with this profile,
                    - the cost, at the time, was mostly beneath notice for them. Not irrelevant, but low enough that it was worth a shot without engagement.

                    We got a game with lots of good things, because it does have a lot of them, but riddled with serious, naïve problems a serious publisher would have caught. They went through instead and we got the result.

                    [...]

                    What I can say after all those years is that the writing was on the wall the whole time inside Paradox. Time just made clear, and this is not just a WoD thing, that they have a severely bad management.

                    V5 abandoned most of its initial stances and is backpedaling, but this is all overall slow. Paradox burned bridges, pissed people, broke things they could use, and blundered around until investors had to intervene. Now they have to play chill, and WoD simply isn't a focus, as they had to admit they just don't know what they're doing.
                    I'm curious to see how they handle distribution going forward. Renegade seems to have slowed down distribution of V5 books (at least here in the UK). The most recent books are getting harder to obtain and there's often big time lags even when they can get stuff to you.

                    From my experience in publishing (having worked on ~300 titles in one form or another since 2007), distribution is becoming a riskier business, with storage costs going up, margins becoming tighter, and bookselling becoming much more fragmented as time goes on. On the publisher side, this is aggravated by rising paper costs, greater reliance on overseas shipping, and disruptions to the supply chain caused by tariffs and politics.

                                        As usual when it comes ot V5 mechanics... implementation is usually the far bigger problem than concept.                                                         Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

                                                        That's for sure.

                                                        Resonance could have been more focused on how to hunt for them rather than randomizing people. And be more freeform on the benefits rather than lock so tightly to humour theory.

                                                        It should be an optional rule in a sidebar somewhere. As it is, it's a rule that's both underdeveloped and clunky, so it fails on two counts. Dyscrasias are yet another thing layered on top that should probably die by fire.

                                                        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                                        Predator Types have a roughly stable scheme: 1 choice of Discipline, 1 choice of Specialization, 1 dot of Advantage. The bigger problem is with the ones that mix Disadvantages to give bigger bonuses. In theory this is simple to correct by homebrew or free dot placement.
                                                        The problem is that they finely balanced XP with the available chargen picks, but then completely blew it all again with Predator Types. The first dot in a Discipline is obviously not worth the same as the third dot in a Discipline, so just saying "add a dot of x" is sloppy and undoes their own hard work.

                                                        IMO, they should have gone with flat XP costs as a much saner option (perhaps with the fifth dot costing double, as in CofD), because then it doesn't matter. You can just let people allocate dots however they want. But even keeping the system they had, all they had to do was limit the bonus to the first dot in a choice of Disciplines. (This doesn't even have to be tied to Predator Type, though. You could just give people 2/1/1 dots to allocate at chargen, stipulating that the first three have to go into clan Disciplines.)

                                                        Personally, I would also give an optional sidebar that just lets you spend the equivalent points however you want. Going from V1 to V20, where you're relatively free to assign points, to V5, where everything is allocated for you, just feels so antithetical to the simplicity and freedom of the original system. Giving people a choice for freeform chargen is much better.

                                                        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                                        Loresheets need an overhaul of the rule.
                                                        Yeah, I like them, but I like Heavy Arms' suggestion of them counting as "lore" skills as well. One thing I think we do need is to move away from the 1–5 dot progression and let them be whatever makes the most sense. It cuts out on the boring stuff added in just to get five distinct Advantages out of it.

                                                        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                                        How you interpret your Predator Type is up to you.

                                                        What it really is is a package of starting dots and nothing else. Everything else that uses them are just ST helpers for feeding scenes where you follow a given Type. They don't even have to follow the one you chose, even if the assumption is that.

                                                        How it reflects character ethics is up to the player. Humans don't usually eat each other, except when they do, but that has nothing to do with the human capacity to reflect upon the ethics of personal activities and needs and try to approach them in a less harmful way.
                                                        It's perhaps useful the first time you make a character, but it never gets reinforced in play. Because you can't allocate the points where you want to, it just ends up being something you pick to get the traits you wanted all along. Which means a freeform approach would actually be better most of the time. To avoid the optimisation trap, you could just say, "Now pick some traits based on how your character approaches feeding. Pick one Discipline you regularly use, one specialty that helps you..." etc. Discuss it with your ST and you're golden.

                                                        Ultimately, though, it's still a meaningless pick that doesn't really reflect how people tend to feed in play. They work best as optional templates to guide character builds. If you want Predator Type to have more fangs (pun intended) it should have more ongoing mechanical weight. E.g., you heal one level of Willpower damage when you feed according to type. Maybe even let it heal aggravated Willpower! That'll certainly make people use it. This makes it more like the old Archetypes and fixes a problem with aggravated Willpower damage to boot.
