    Do you base your character on yourself?

    04-25-2020, 01:18 PM
    I was thinking about the do vampires have sex question in another thread.
    ​
    I'd guess it matters more to players than story tellers who are trying to identify with their character. I would guess most people invested in just one character for years have a bigger issue with thinking themselves impotent especially if they are basing the character on themselves. Maybe I'm off base, but it leads me to this topic.

    As a player I based most of my characters on whatever role I was making them for but some times I would make an awesome version of myself. I'd give him skills and stats I felt reflected an idealized version of myself. Anyone else?

      I think it really depends on the player and the game style you are playing.

      For instance I have STed games where a player couldn't wrap their minds around a character unless it was very similar to themselves, while another player in the same group refused to player a character unless it was as different as possible from themselves.

      Alternatively if you are just flat out playing a "play as yourself" game, that makes things even more confusing.

      When it comes to being invested in a character it really comes down to why you are playing the character. For some people the character is a therapeutic outlet where they can rage or behave transgressively with few if any repercussions. For other people the character is inspired by an outside source and they just want to explore the concept and its ramifications.

      The one thing I have noticed is if the player has IRL knowledge that is part of the character, then they automatically "sink into character" faster and more smoothly, which in turn helps the rest of the group do the same. So the investment isn't always being a reflection of the self as much as a point of automatic similarity by virtue of shared expertise.

                                                      No, and when I'm ST'ing I try to encourage players (especially newer ones) not to make characters that are too similar or too heavily based on themselves either. Over the years I've found that it makes it a little too easy for a player to identify their character if the character is based off of them, and if there's any kind of issues or problems with that character, they are much more likely to take that as criticism personally by seeing it as criticism of him or herself rather than the character. And having a player or the ST start criticizing another player (or at least having that criticized player feel the criticism is directed at them rather than at a character) is a great way to end up with serious drama and groups imploding.

                                                      So while having a character similar to you can make roleplaying a bit easier, as an ST I just find it presents a few potential big problems that I'd just as soon try to head off.

                                                      That's not to say I've never run games like that before. One time I ran a short 3 shot game where I had the players make characters based on themselves (even the same name and everything) and then.... bam, they all got gunned down in a shopping mall 10 minutes into the first session and then woke up in the Shadowlands as Wraiths. Made for a fun way to introduce the group to Wraith, but it was more like a one time gimmick than something I'd encourage players to do more often.

                                                          Not in general. The whole point of a role playing game is to get to play characters who think and do things differently from yourself; a come-as-you-aren't party, as it were.
