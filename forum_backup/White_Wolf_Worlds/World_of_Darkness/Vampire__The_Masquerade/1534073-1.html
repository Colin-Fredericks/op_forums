                Originally posted by Hermeticus View Post

                Going back to the Assamites, if İ get your point, İ agree: frankly, İslam is a late phenomenon in the history of the clan. Any characteristic of the clan should be rooted in much older traditions, like Mesopotamian culture (like Dur-An-Ki) and Persian culture (like the VDA20 Companion saying that most elder Assamites in Asia are still Zoroastrian, which was a nice touch). So in this sense, İ wouldn't link the black skin to İslam at all. But since jinn predate İslam, referring to them in whatever reason one gives for the skin darkening İ think is ok. But then it makes the Assamites very tied to the Arabian peninsula unless one uses other words for jinn,from other cultures, but that has complications. So, for this, İ might favour other ideas.
                Without getting into the "religious discussion" side of things for obvious reasons, that's kind of the neat part...even though it doesn't necessarily track by lore. The reason I brought up Zoroastrianism relevant to this is, if I remember my Islamic studies right, jinn under the Quranic narrative still exist according to Zoroastrian duality -- at least in terms of Muslim and kafir jinn. Which, if one squints hard enough, would suggest BH skin color as directly correlative to degeneration, and only indirectly correlative to age as older vampires tend towards lower road/path ratings.

                Even then, I'd argue despite what the text says, character illustrations and individual descriptions tell a different tale. Every elder and methuselah with higher road/path ratings has been depicted with lighter skin, up to and including Tegyrius and Fatima. But, that appears even worse on the "is this racist/culturalist?" spectrum at face value, even though those characters remain devout Muslims and would still therefore abide by the "good or bad jinn" duality, however it's contextualized. Tying skin color to morality is just bad optics before a Western audience, no matter how well-vindicated it might be considering non-Western belief systems in their own context.

                As far as tying vampires to specific geographic regions, and cultures, I personally see no problem with it so long as those regions and cultures are represented with due diligence and respect. Nobody's out here starting flame wars over Tzimisce being a caricature of Slavic culture -- but on the other hand, Tzimisce aren't exactly being depicted as hardcore alcoholics who listen to hardbass and wear Adidas track suits, are they. However objectively hilarious a Tzimisce gopnik might be, or how badly I want to play one now that I've thought of it.

                With getting into the "religious discussion" side of things...yeah, if I remember right, it's mostly Shia and Sufist Muslims who take issue with pointing to Zoroastrian influences on Islam. If I remember the narrative as I heard it right, aspects of Zoroastrianism are regarded as divine revelation, but Zoroaster/Zarathustra himself is considered a false prophet. Which conveniently gets around the entire question of whether Zoroastrianism itself had influence over early Islam as an organized religion. Which to me personally is a non-question -- religions syncretize and assimilate regional belief systems as they grow.

                Hell, contemporary Catholicism and Protestantism bear a lot more semblance to European paganism (and in the US, post-Renaissance mercantilism and capitalism) in form and practice than Judaism...and Christianity is a religion that started as a Hebrew eschatonic cult.

                                                    I think OOC it was done just to distinguish the Assamites from all other vampires. Someone thought it'd be cool if vampires became pale, then these would go the other way. Unfortunately it didn't seem to occur to the writer that the reason vampires are described as pale is because of a lack of blood, and simply turning the skin dark therefore seems strange.

                                                    In terms of IC explanation, I just assumed it was part of Caine's curse like all other clan weaknesses. The Assamites are a clan of diablerists who routinely murdered other vampires. The color black is already associated with diablerie - it is mentioned that vampires with Auspex looking at the aura of vampires who have practiced diablerie appear with black veins in their aura. For a clan of diablerists, it makes sense that black stain will increase and even influence their physical appearance. The sins of the father (in this case the Assamite antedeluvian) will be visited upon the children (in this case all other Assamites). It is a good way to warn all other vampires of what this vampire is and the danger it represents. Since it is part of the curse of Caine, it doesn't matter if that particular Assamite ever engaged in diablerie. It's a way to make sure the clan can always be identified by other vampires, and since the darker the skin represents an older vampire, that means others can use to guage how much of a threat that Assamite could be.
