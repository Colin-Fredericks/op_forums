    An interesting, if practically so-so ability of thin-bloods, is how they get a dot of a discipline based on the resonance of their last meal, two if it's Intense. So by choosing their blood carefully, they can swap discipline at will. I don't know that means they can choose their particular discipline power, but then I don't see how that works for potent vampires either. I see thin-blood alchemy as a refinement of this practice, which involves creating new resonances that confer powers to which other vampires lack natural access, because their vitae isn't flexible enough. Although perhaps it would be possible on a regular diet of given formula.

    All V5 kindred respond to resonance, but for thin-bloods, one of the effects is instant, tangible and reliable. I think what they lack is heriditary resonance and the ability to cumulatively develop their blood.

                                The Bahari part 1

                                Type: Cult

                                Synopsis: A cult of feminist misandrists (no they aren't the same) who engage in a combination of better living through torture as well as gardening.

                                Analysis: Yeah, this was delayed by about four months, which goes to tell you that I was 1:] Distracted by a deal with Podium Entertainment. 2:] Writing no less than three books simultaneously (Space Academy Dropouts 1 and 2, Daughter of the Cyber Dragons). 3:] Struggling with the holidays. 4:] Dealing with a most unfortunate fact about the subject matter. 4:] is the one I have to confess to.

                                I hate the Bahari.

                                I feel embarrassed about saying it because it's a silly thing but I have hated the Lilith part of V:TM lore for over twenty five years. Seriously, I've hated it since Revelations of the Dark Mother. I was a huge fan of the Book of Nod and practically memorized it but I absolutely detested the writing in ROTDM. I didn't care for the Path of Lilith and I absolutely hated her followers every time they showed up in Noddist stories. I think my ultimate moment of dislike was reading the adventure "Nightshade" in the Gehenna supplement.

                                That particular supplement had Lilith growing her garden of magic fruit, the PCs dealing with Lasombra and some other Antediluvians acting like Dick Dastardly, and the ending that they can either side with Lilith and dwell in wonder and glory forever in Paradise or be killed because they betrayed her. I feel like it was written by her publicist. I respect that she's Samael's wife in Kabbalah and has a "legitimate" role in demonology as well as folklore but it seems so damned weird.

                                I can't even say what precisely irritates me so much about Lilith's use in the setting. I don't actually mind Lilith in the Verbena, where she makes even less sense, and my players like Lilith just fine (I use her regardless of my dislike). I think its that the Bahari seem so damned inconsistent and I'm not sure what their deal is. They like Gardens because, I guess the Garden of Eden even though vampires are divorced from the natural world. Pain because? I dunno. Pain is a thing Lilith causes? Its just hard.

                                So I was admittedly delighted with the opening fiction here that included an extended, "Just so you know, we're aware a lot of Lilith lore is a recent creation and not actually feminist." It also manages to make Lilith interesting in vampire for me because it casts her as an unknowable eldritch abomination that her own followers don't necessarily understand. It's honestly how I'd prefer they do Caine with the idea that he's something much-much older than Jewish myth but the Abrahamic types have used their own legends to try to explain it.

                                I'm actually quite fond of this sort of thing and use it in my games a lot. The idea of a "dark supernatural truth" that religions and mythology are the interpretation of that isn't necessarily accurate gets used a lot when it's "pagan" myth but rarely for Abrahamic. It annoys me when that happens and it's partially why I once did a Call of Cthulhu scenario dealing with a cult that believed Yog-Sothoth was the God of Christianity and had a semi-decent argument. Maybe Caine is older than the original first murderer myth and his story got passed down to become the legend of Set and Osiris, Romulus and Remus, and Caine and Abel. Yes, there's some fratricide involved, probably, and some angelic beings but who knows what they are.

                                Same for Lilith.

                                Very cool idea, IMHO.

                                        I like the general notions of having a matriarchal cult (or better: loosely related cults) of vampires centering around Lilith instead of Caine, even if through the filter of the Book of Nod. Hell, I would love to go full Circle of the Crone and have Eve (or Zilah), Lilith and the Crone as figures of worship or symbolism. Thing is... Bahari really feel badly developed, they feel like cringefest with the whole PAIN (gotta love it, gotta taste it, gotta do it) thing... like, I get the idea that Lilith suffered a ton of shit from basically everyone except maybe Satan, but to love pain like that seems just edgelordism.

                                        So in the end the values of Bahari are lost on me. They want what? Wisdom? Power? Be alone and develop freely? Grow as a community? Grow their magic gardens? Get Magic? Bring Lilith forth like the Setite's do with theirs? I really don't know, and if it is just 'hey, choose yourself gamemaster' then it is one of those cases that I feel like it was a badly developed thing, not something I can use freely and creatively.

                                        I believe they can be a fun idea, and a nice option, but till now (and maybe even now with Cults of Blood...) they have been empty and without direction. Most of the time they are really out of place, like Sabbat (wtf?), Camarilla (yeah... may... no, don't think so), Anarchs (yeah I suppose); but they should've been bigger, much bigger. They have a Book of Nod of their own in and out of universe, but I still get the Heresy of Caine and it's crazy gnosticism better than the Bahari. I really think they can be fun, and will work a bit on them with the Cults of the BLood Gods book.
