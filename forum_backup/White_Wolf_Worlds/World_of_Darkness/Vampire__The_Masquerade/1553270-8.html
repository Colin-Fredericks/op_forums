                                    So, now I've finished my playthrough and overall... I would call it a mediocre action-adventure with a decent story. But really, pretty much every facet of the game is a mixed bag. The story is still the strongest part - even though it's quite linear, sometimes to the point of feeling like you're getting railroaded. That's still the part I liked the most, though.

                                    There are two obvious issues this game deals with: First, its name and second, its price.

                                    Is it a worthy sequel to Bloodlines 1? No, absolutely not. And I don't think calling it an RPG fits here. Like I mentioned, it feels more like an action-adventure type of game to me. I'd compare more to something like the Crystal Dynamics Tomb Raider reboot games. You have combat-scenes, a very basic skill-system and a pretty linear story (with some side-quests). But character-progression, skill-systems, inventory, story-choices... All of that is either completely missing or present in such a basic manner that RPG doesn't fit as a description of this game.

                                    But the second issue is the price. Does this game feel like a full-price game? Not really. If anything, just looking at the game, I'd feel more comfortable thinking of it as a solid AA-type of game. There's important stuff missing, like a FOV-slider, the option to turn off motion-blur and accessibility-options regarding color and the like. Hopefully, they will patch that into the game in the near future. But releasing a full-prices game without those options is bad. There's also the feeling that a lot of what this game has to offer in terms of gameplay feels shoddy or unpolished if you're expecting something on the level of a full-priced game.

                                    Combat and the Disciplines are... okay. I wish they had mapped light and heavy attack to two different buttons. You don't have a block which is weird but you do have a dodge. And later I figured out that you can counter attacks by dashing into enemies but it all felt weird. The telekinesis was useless in combat unless you activated vampire vision. And vampire vision is that typical detective mode feature from way back when Batman Arkham Asylum came out. It has a similar issue where you use this special vision to gain more information but at the same time you're also looking at an uglier version of the game-world. Here, in Bloodlines 2, you kinda have to use vampire vision to see the objects you can pick up via telekinesis. Later on, that got a little bit easier. Other than that, the flow of combat when people are getting thrown around due to your heavy attacks and the animation of feeding in combat (as well as the finishing animations) looks good and you feel strong in those moments. So that works. But it also gets repetitive quickly due to a lack of variety in the animations.

                                    For my playthrough, I picked Ventrue and... it did say easy. I played around with the other powers a little bit to compare as well. But the game wasn't kidding when it called Ventrue the easy option. By far, the best set of powers and the two Passives are very useful as well (temporary damage reduction after feeding and feeding heals more). If I were to play it again, I think I would pick Lasombra because that power-set felt the most interesting in my opinion. Tremere was also interesting, though. But the passives for Tremere aren't that good.

                                    The gameworld looks nice but the Resonance-stuff, the sidequests, the Masquerade-meter... Really, all of it is so rudimentary and boring that it just worsened my opinion of the game. The end-effect: Later on, I just dashed through the city to get to the next quest-marker. I had lost any interest in the game-world outside of the main-story-missions.

                                    So, overall, as an AA action adventure game, it feels okay. But I think on one hand, it's a miracle that this game came out at all after all the development-hell drama and there's nothing disastrously wrong with this game. On the other hand, though, there isn't a single aspect in this game that stands out positively either.

                                    So, for me, it's a 5/10.

Apparently, you would just tell that person: "Sucks to be you. I don't care. Maybe they will fix that later.". And what I'm saying is that if you're buying a full-price videogame in 2025, you're entitled to having the option to turn off motion-blur, for example. Therefore, if, as a reviewer, I notice it being absent, I'm gonna call this out as an obviously bad thing.

You need to have better standards. This is another general videogames-issue: The state of the game on day 1 matters. If it's shit on day 1 and gets better later on, that still means the publisher/developer was willing to release/sell a game in a state that's shit. And that's unacceptable.
