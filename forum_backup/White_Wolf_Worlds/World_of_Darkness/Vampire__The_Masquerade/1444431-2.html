The details are left up to individual Storytellers to decide, but the opportunities are laid out for you. And really, the idea that the source of power for the *undead* shadow-wielders might be associated with the Low Umbra and the manifest power of Entropy doesn't really seem like a terrible stretch.

                                                            The Lasombra is one of those clans that I think are strong in concept, but was improperly depicted in canon. And the V5 metaplot has really screwed things up for them.

                                                            The major problem with both clanbooks is that they (as well as the Tzimisce clanbooks) forgot they were clans of the Sabbat. They ignored how the Sabbat has been depicted as creating new vampires. And they failed to distinguish what elements the main Lasombra clan and the anti-tribu should have in common and how they're different.

                                                            I think there should be essentially three versions of the clan. All recognizably Lasombra, but easily differentiated from each other.

                                                            The first are the Lasombra anti-tribu who should be a direct descendant of the Lasombra clan in the Dark Ages in the same way the Camarilla clans are, with adjustments as a result of seemingly losing their founder and working within the structure of the Camarilla. Relic clan institutions like the Friends of the Night still exist, but are forced to operate in secret as it violates the Sixth Tradition. They would carefully choose their childer like their Camarilla counterparts. They would support the more conservative and oppressive factions within the Camarilla.

                                                            The second are the Sabbat Lasombra who represent the original Anarch Revolt. These were Lasombra who detested the oppression of the Lasombra elders of the Dark Ages and who fought for substantial changes in the clan culture. They would obviously hate an institution like the Friends of the Night, and they believe they have purged it in the “new” Lasombra clan. They would follow the practice of random embraces as described in the original Sabbat descriptions. They may not necessarily be Loyalists, but should be fervent supporters of the ideals of the Anarch Revolt, although perhaps acting in a “vanguard of the revolution” kind of way. More practical about using power than the idealists of the Brujah anti-tribu for example.

                                                            The third are those Lasombra of the Sabbat who joined the sect, but who fought against the Anarchs during the Anarch Revolt. Their membership in the sect is mostly based on cynical opportunism. They saw the bulk of their clan create the Sabbat. They did not see an opportunity for their clan to gain power through the Camarilla. So they joined the Sabbat in the century or two after the Convention of Thorns. They are very two faced, wanting to preserve elder power and old clan traditions in a sect that should be hostile to them. They have to balance things – appearing to create their children through the random creation rates, but actually selecting them as much as they can. They still practice the courts of the Friends of the Night in secret, culling other Lasombra in an attempt to rebuild the clan. But they still revile the anti-tribu as traitors for joining their enemies. They are the cancer and hypocrisy within the Sabbat, but their leadership has been critical for the sect's survival. In culture, they are a blend of the anti-tribu and other Sabbat Lasombra. They are equally fervent in their support of the Sabbat, but this is demonstrated in different ways than the mass of the Sabbat Lasombra. They don't support "freedom" in other way, but they are often at the head of other things that distinguish the Sabbat, like development of the Paths of Enlightenment, supporting Crusades, etc. This creates a lot of tension within the Sabbat Lasombra.

                                                            As for Disciplines, I can't comment on Oblivion, but felt certain levels of Obtenebration were too powerful. It should never have been a combat Discipline. Since the Lasombra already have two active, forceful Disciplines in Dominate and Potence, Obtenebration should have been a less forceful Discipline. I don't like its more cartoonish powers that resemble Marvel Comic's Darkforce rather than traditional vampire powers. Control over shadows and darkness should not mean the darkness can punch you.

                                                            Since I haven't read V5 I can only comment given my perception based on how people have reacted on these forums. But it seems the defection of Lasombra into the Camarilla wasn't properly predicated. I have not heard once how these defectors could have overcome the vaulderie and vinculum to do so, or why the Camarilla would have trusted such defectors if they were still in effect. This is just lazy writing.

                                                            If one wanted a situation where the Sabbat imploded, a Civil War broke out, and mass defection occurred, there is an “easy” way to justify that. And that is for the vaulderie to stop working and vinculum to no longer exist. This would happen suddenly and mysteriously. But it could work as metaplot and justify a lot of what happened. I personally would not have liked such a change in the setting. But at least it would explain why things happened.

                                                            I liked the original weakness of the Lasombra. It was a standard flaw of vampires so it needed to show up somewhere. I understand the concern of people that in today's age that the weakness might up being a benefit as Lasombra would seem immune to video surveillance. However, there needed to be more thinking behind it. The purpose of the weakness was to make the Lasombra more vulnerable as detection as vampires. So any changes should have built on that. At the top of my head, the easiest change would have been to show some kind of distortion on video equipment rather than simply being invisible so that there could be a trigger for people to know something went wrong. That has flaws too, but at least it doesn't change things too much and seems more like a clarification rather than a change.

                                                            If one really wanted the Lasombra anti-tribu to become more prominent in the Camarilla, it should have had more natural origins than a ham fisted metaplot worthy of professional wrestling. Show how the anti-tribu have benefitted the sept, accrued boons, built influence from a position of weakness, and perhaps even grow normally like cementing the Camarilla's position in Latin America. Then at the right time they made a pitch to finally be recognized in the Inner Circle and they were. And now they're a more prominent part of the Camarilla, whether you go with the original 7 clans (the only right choice IMO) or the ridiculous V5 version that now exists.

                                                            I don't care anything at all for the signature characters of an RPG. So Lucita is utterly irrelevant. In so far she exists, I'd prefer it to be as a background character representing something a PC could be. In this case, it'd be a PC elder, but that's fine. Elders as PCs go back to the Elysium supplement. Like many such sect defections, her defection to the Sabbat in the Revised era was not well supported. As a long time anti-tribu enemy, the Sabbat Lasombra should just want her dead. It's like the British welcoming back Kim Philby with full miltary honors.
