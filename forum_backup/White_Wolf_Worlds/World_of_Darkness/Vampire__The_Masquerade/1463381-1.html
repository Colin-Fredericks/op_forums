        One thing of note is the Camarilla and their preoccupation with the masquerade. A kid is a masquerade risk by virtue of just how much they're expected to change each year. If an adult doesn't change how he looks in a decade he's lucky, rather than a freak of nature. Adults tend not to stand out and Most people aren't going to be around long enough to really see an issue. , if a kid doesn't change in a year, nevermind a decade, then something is very wrong. Parents have a tendancy of noting the faces of all the local kids, they're easier to remember because there's just so many variables with how they look. Unless a child-vampire is an obfuscate vampire, they need to be put down for the good of the masquerade.

            I actually have a differing opinion on this regard. I don't think 20-30 would be a modern standard, I think it would be general.

            The age of adulthood changes with times and cultures, although in more complex ways than just adjusting the arbitrary threshold. But the Embrace isn't about getting an adult, it is about getting a proper candidate for immortality.

            Human development don't really change that much with time (except for the average age of start of the menstrual cycle). No matter where or when you live, turning to adulthood sill have you considered young. You're ready to take on responsibilities, you're not expected to be particularly good at them. Your body won't finish growing up until about your 20th birthday, and even then you're not at your peak, which is around 30yo.

            Such development stages aren't clearly defined but are well known. A young warrior at 18 is expected to challenge another at 26, but just because he's young and stupid. The young maiden is beautiful, but the almost 30 matron is in the prime of her mind and skills, and just physically superior, give or take a few exceptions. The young adult is ready to start their life, but have nothing to show off yet. Is it really the age you want to lock someone up in eternity? Is it the person you want to be an immortal?

            My personal take is that Kindred wisdom would be to take someone at a more mature age whenever possible. Exceptions abound and are tolerated, but they're not a rule after all. Passionate Embraces are the most likely to result in vampires below 25, and sure, then the adult age for local culture will dictate how this is received by the larger vampiric society. But "good" Embraces are generally of people between 25-35. Women are probably Embraced younger, but not by much when vampires can stave off the effects of time on mortals through other means.

            Ghouling stops aging for a month, but I rule in that the healing effects of the Blood are enough for a pre-industrial woman to keep her natural youth by being Ghouled only once or twice a year without withholding her entire physical and mental development. Of course, make what you will of her mental health with such arrangement. And this definitely happens to young boys, too. So the difference in age probably isn't that much among sexes for what would be considered a good Embrace.

                        Something that doesn't seem to be considered is the notion of night and sleep when it comes to under age embraces. Anything say 12 or under regardless of society would raise red flags for not being in bed and asleep. In first world countries parents are told to get their offspring into bed by 9 o'clock, while in third world countries the children are out cold a couple hours earlier since they are already being used for manual labor, probably on the family farm. A documentary on the american dust bowl back in the 1930's had a wonderful quote about children, "an exhausted child is a well behaved child". I can personally attest to the efficacy such approach.

                        So that just leaves 13 - 17 year olds and then you are back in the normal embrace age range. Though to be fair, if the game had to confront the reality of how embracing would work, no one below the age of 20 would probably be considered. As much as vampire fluff is all about gothic love and tragedy, the reality is that vampire clans would treat embracing like how players treat hiring NPCs in a video game. They probably pay attention to their stats and what they can bring to the table first, then their aesthetics a distant second.

                        Sure there will be the occasional wierdo who just really wants a certain scheme or concept, but truth be told, a person who would put that together in the first place probably wouldn't be embraced to begin with.

                        So that just leaves the ethics argument for those who accidentally killed a kid, but the act of embracing at that point is less about undoing a wrong and more about assuaging their own guilt. Regardless it would be incredibly rare.

                        In all likelihood there would be probably be a higher occurrence of Malkavians or deranged members of other clans with Obfuscate who use the power to portray themselves as child vampires, rather than actual children being embraced.

                                                    On the US, by the way, what happened to you is permitted by law and happens. US law allows children to work on family small farms and family business, in media or doing house chores at any age whatsoever, and on anyone's farm for short harvest from age 10, or indefinitely out of school hours from age 12. From 14 they don't need parental permission and can work at anything non-hazardous, at 16 they can ditch school to work. Few states have more strict laws, while others are exempt from even federal minimum.

                                                    Brazil have among the stricter laws on the matter. No work is permitted below 14, period. At 14 you can work as an Apprentice, a condition tightly regulated that requires approval from and good performance at school, among several rules and limitations. At 16 you can work non-hazardous day jobs, but your employee is still required to make sure you're going to school regularly.

                                                    Not that the US is the prime example on labor laws, Brazil is a typical case for developing countries, or that laws translate perfectly to societal norms. But laws are an indication of overall expectation in a country, they may be more or less followed, more or less ignored, but they're there for a reason. And looking at a broader (if not so detailed) data on as many countries as possible there are some striking trends.

                                                    Most countries set the minimum work age between 14 or 16. 13 is uncommon, but is there, while 12 is a rare outlier. Some have laws for work exempt from minimum age, but this work is almost always restricted to media work under strict control and regulated hours. A few allow unrestricted work below 16, but on every continent without exception the most common age for unrestricted labor is 18, with some countries setting it even higher.

                                                    What's more, there are few rules to determine who will be the exceptions to the trends. Work at 13? Is allowed on Kenya, Turkey, Switzerland and United Kingdom. When unrestricted labor is allowed? 16 in Egypt, but 19 in Morocco, 15 in Turkey, but 21 in Iran. Are the laws followed? What matters is that if they're there, at least a sizable portion of population think they are sensible rules, enough for local legislators to not feel enough pressure to change them yet. Societal rules may be different, but they're likely to vary depending on specific circumstances and not on a generalized standard all around the country, much less among all countries.

                                                        I live in Appalachia, in the USA, and I know a number of families that own farms and where the kids spend a lot of their days working on the family farms. That is neither here nor there, just a little statement regarding kids on farms.

                                                        In any case, in most nations, little kids wandering around by themselves at night is seen as unusual. Even taking you kids to Waffle House past most people's bedtimes can get the occasional strange look, or going somewhere with your kids during school hours, though vampires don't need to worry about that. This doesn't mean that the police will be called on child vampires who are out late at night, but people tend to notice little kids more, especially in situations where they don't expect to see them. This makes things more dangerous for child vampires in many ways. I suppose, on the flip side, kids are less likely to be suspected of crimes, but if the police want to take an unaccompanied minor into custody then that will be a major problem even if they don't intend on charging them with crimes.

                                                        Keep in mind that in the Camarilla every embrace must have the permission of the prince and an embrace without permission can potentially lead to execution for both sire and childe. For this reason, embraces of passion are going to be rarer and those that happen are more likely to end up in the death of the childe. Because each embrace requires permission, then it is unlikely that obvious minor children are going to get the embrace. Even if a potential sire really wants it, its highly unlikely that the prince would grant permission for this with all of the additional disadvantages and Masquerade risks that a child vampire presents. Even if some minor shows really great potential for the embrace, it's highly likely that the vampire would just wait until that child grew to their late teens, a very short amount of time by vampire standards.

                                                        What if there is an urgent reason to embrace a kid? Let's say that the local Tremere discover a 10 year old kid with amazing magical potential, whose magical talent would make them a nature at Thaumaturgy - but this child has terminal cancer that will kill them within a year and no amount of vampire blood or Thaumaturgical rituals will cure or slow the progress of the cancer, so the kid needs to be embraced to preserve their magical talent. Would the prince grant permission in that case? It depends, but even if the prince doesn't, the Tremere could take the child to another location with a friendly prince or none at all to avoid that rule.
