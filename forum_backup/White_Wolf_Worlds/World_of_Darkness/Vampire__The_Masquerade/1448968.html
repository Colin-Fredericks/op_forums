    About Necromancy/Oblivion

    05-19-2021, 08:11 AM
    A lot of the game's design seems to take it as a given that Necomancy/Oblivion is dark/evil even by vampire standards. Putting the original discipline in an independent clan, making the mere use the power give stains, all points to the idea that Necromancy is a discipline that is just tooo dark for a standard Camarilla game. I just don't buy it. There's nothing about talking to ghosts and creating zombies that is anymore evil than blood-bonding people, something any vampire can do, or Dominating them, something half of Cammies can do. It just feels like a holdover influence from older games like D&D that got ported to Vampire while making no sense in this world.

                      I don't have V5 so I can't comment on specifics there, but it seems to me that this may be an attempt to acknowledge that Necromancy should be dangerous to use, but in practice it isn't.

                      Necromancers are dealing with a lot of unknowns. They don't know that the spirit they are dealing with is a wraith, a wraith under control of its Shadow, a spectre, or some plasmic entity. Establishing relations with such spirits are dangerous as you are making it easier for that spirit to affect you. That's both an occult principle and something the Wraith game itself sometimes heeds (attunements and consorts). Interacting with the underworld could easily backfire. Doing things to wraiths to compel them to obey you, might only result in the wraith giving into their Shadow who can now use their Thorns for additional defenses. An encounter that should have been easy could now become quite menacing.

                      But from a Vampire perspective, there is little support given that necromancers are engaging in a dangerous practice that could harm them. The mechanics are set up that allow necromancers to harm or control wraiths, but little on how wraiths can fight back or how necromancers are making themselves vulnerable. Most Vampire STs don't understand the Wraith setting well or consider it as part of "their" setting. They just want to get back to the city, the Prince, vampire politics, etc. So necromancy is often seen as a power that only bring advantages that can't get negated (since there are little rules about how other vampires could defend themselves against it). For example, when I used to LARP, use of Necromancy often lead to a lot of player complaints because it created situations non-Necromancers couldn't deal with at all. So I think most likely the stains is an attempt to provide game balance and simulate some kind of risk of using necromancy.

                      As you noted, this may not make sense from a setting perspective. Talking to ghosts isn't an evil thing, even if it may be dangerous for many reasons. However, I do think things like animating corpses should be considered differently. That's violating a lot of natural laws, and I don't mind it somehow being tainted. At the same time, so should such things like a Tremere creating a homonculous or demon bound. I don't know how those are handled in V20.

                      Some things are always seen as somehow "intrinsically worse" even though the end results are the same. I've seen some D&D players balk at the idea that the use of poison is inherently evil (in most versions of the game) as opposed to blasting someone with a fireball. Yet in the real world, certain weapons like poison gas or weaponizing diseases are put into a different category. Therefore, I don't mind if certain powers, rituals, or even whole Paths are somehow intrinsically wrong in a way that Dominate is not. But certainly there are lots of powers in Necromancy that should be neutral.

                          While I think a lot of changes made in V5 are Dunning-Krueger related, and that oblivion causing stains because -dark energy- is -redacted- , it has always been the case that necromancers do some sick shit to get their magic mojo on. First, Messing with wraiths is usually inhumane, secondly you actually have to try and -awaken- this power. That means a lot of messing with stuff. You're both bothering wraiths, and doing a whole bunch of socially unacceptable stuff. Now, there's nothing fundamentally wrong with messing with corpses, because they're dead and don't have opinions, but there's something wrong about breaking taboos, at least, if you're someone who regularly breaks the rules for things like this, you're probably more inclined to question the worth of -actually important rules- Most importantly, when you're messing with the dead so much, you're isolating yourself from others. Spending more time trying to mess with the dead than the living won't send you to crazytown, but if you're the kind of person who's into that you're probably heading to crazytown, necromancy or not.
