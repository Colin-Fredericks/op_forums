    The simple Fortitude buff:

    Each rank of Fortitude a Vampire has grants them one additional Bruised health level.

    That way the power of Fortitude grants some consistency, even if your Soak dice don't work out or you're hit with unsoakable effects. It ensures that Fortitude remains a good investment at 3 and beyond, instead of suffering from a horrible case of big investment low reward. Who wants to buy Fortitude 5, really? An extra health level at least sweetens the deal a tad.

                We're getting tangential, but Fortitude in and by itself isn't the root cause of its own problem. Fortitude is fine, especially post-V20 where blood points can be spent to convert Fortitude dots into auto-soak, and armor rating was finally normalized to other WoD games to apply to (non-bane) aggravated.

                The root cause of the problem is combat overage. I've run without combat overage at my tables for over 15 years, and that one change fixed practically the entire combat system on its own. Action economy loses its premium and players can't brute force combat with huge dice pools and Celerity, players actually have to invest in Strength/Potence if they want characters to hit hard, thrown weapons are damn good by comparison, players actually use combat maneuvers (that aren't clench/grapple) for once, and players get to think laterally during combat to jockey for situational advantages rather than just declare attacks and defenses.

                    When did this change to Fortitude occur? Is that a DAV20 thing or a 5th edition thing? Because I know as of V20, Fortitude works just like Revised, with no option to spend blood to increase it's effect.

                    As far as overage (which I always called "overkill"), getting rid of it doesn't really solve the problem of Fortitude by itself. Fortitude has no active effect. It grants no utility, it doesn't help you win combat or other dangerous situations, it simply helps you "not lose". And it's not even really good at that. A few extra dice are effectively meaningless, since the rule of 1 is designed to make more dice matter less as you achieve larger die pools. A Specialty can overcome this, but in the case of Fortitude, that requires expending resources in a trait other than Fortitude (ie, Stamina of 4 or greater), which makes Stamina a far better investment than this Discipline (and that's IF your Storyteller allows a "soak damage" Specialty- I've encountered some who think the idea is rather cheap, like taking a Strength specialty that applies to "damage", but I digress). This is then countered not just by "overage", but the fact that Potence completely counters it's effect- and Potence offers other options besides raw damage. Potence users can apply almost inescapable grapples, jump higher, and try to smash you with very large objects that can inflict even more damage.

                    The fact that any weapon gives you extra damage dice alone is enough to make Fortitude a bit questionable. If Bob the Brujah has Str 5 and Potence 5, and picks up a fire axe that does, say Str +3 Lethal, the guy with Stam 5 and Fortitude 5 is already at a disadvantage, unless he's wearing armor of some kind (which at level 3, lowers his Dexterity by 1). And the fact that Fortitude doesn't resist anything but damage- there are a host of Disciplines, even those that can affect your body (such as Vicissitude) that don't care about your Fortitude.

                    Then you bring in aggravated damage, and while it's nice that you're allowed to soak SOMETHING, 5 dice tops against some Brujah's Str +1 bite is a very shallow advantage. Now this is all by design- Vampire is a terrible game for combat, and combat is DEADLY and a great risk for someone's immortality. You're supposed to avoid it. But the fact that Fortitude is one of the more common Disciplines, and it's really bad at it's job quickly makes players avoid bothering with high levels of it.

                    You're better off investing in 8th Generation and using blood buff to Stamina if you want soak (and even then, you're probably better off with Dexterity in most situations). I've toyed with all kinds of variations of Fortitude; in my current chronicle, odd levels add extra Bruised Health levels and even levels lower soak difficulties by 1, and I allow Stamina to soak aggravated, and it still can't help my Gangrel player avoid damage in combat the way the Brujah player can by simply having an extra action or two to dodge attacks.

                    Even if Fortitude automatically soaked damage, it's still worse than Potence. It would have to apply to things other than just raw damage to have a shot at being useful, because again, it has no other benefit than defense. And the sad part is, I want Fortitude to be good, but I know it's not.

                    Oh something I just thought of- if overage isn't a thing, then wouldn't it be more likely that players could make targeted attacks against unarmored locations more viable? Food for thought.

                    -11twiggins, the reason they changed it was they wanted aggravated damage to be scary. They wanted vampires to fear the sun and fire. Granted, the higher soak difficulties exposure can bring does a lot to do this, but I'm sure at some point, the developers saw some Gangrel ignore a burning building with 7-8 dice of soak and were like "no that's not right". This is actually an important advantage to the Gangrel, as I found out by allowing better soak in my game. Gangrel are supposed to be dangerous, but the low damage of claws, and their lack of Potence really hurts in a game where it's not an attack against a limited defense. In most games, a starting Gangrel can rip apart foes and that makes people fear them just a bit more.*

                    The fact that non-Gangrel (or Followers/Ministry) can rather cheaply get this same benefit is well known (to the point that the LARP community simply says "Gangrel do not teach Fortitude", a lie that has crept into a lot of tabletop groups. Go read a few 'By Night' books, and you'll see tons of non-Gangrel with Protean). I've solved this by switching Protean levels 2 and 3 (something I think Requiem did), and it's worked out great.

                    *What I ended up doing was allowing my Gangrel players easy access to a Devotion (Talons of Fenris) to give them a few extra damage dice and an armor rending effect. And using the Revised Gangrel Clanbook rules for stat buffs for their animal forms.

                        Originally posted by Lynceus View Post

                        When did this change to Fortitude occur? Is that a DAV20 thing or a 5th edition thing? Because I know as of V20, Fortitude works just like Revised, with no option to spend blood to increase it's effect...Fortitude has no active effect. It grants no utility, it doesn't help you win combat or other dangerous situations, it simply helps you "not lose".
                        DAV20. Adding Fortitude dice to Stamina-related dice pools (as Potence does Strength), and spending blood for auto-soak, were already common house rules to normalize Fortitude, and OPP finally just published them as part of the package as-written. It actually makes Fortitude quite appealing for the smattering of discipline powers that use Stamina.

                        ...A few extra dice are effectively meaningless, since the rule of 1 is designed to make more dice matter less as you achieve larger die pools....
                        That isn't the case at all. Any given die has a greater likelihood of resulting in a success than a 1, except in the case of difficulty 10 rolls in which a success and a 1 have the same 10% likelihood per die. And given combat rolls with few notable exceptions operate based upon the standard difficulty of 6, each die individually has a 50% chance of resulting in a success whereas it has a 10% chance of a 1. The overall impact of this is far less important than you make it out to be.

                        ...unless he's wearing armor of some kind (which at level 3, lowers his Dexterity by 1.
                        Yes, and this only has an impact on damage on a per-case basis in circumstances in which overage applies. Because with overage, attack dice are damage dice on a 2:1 basis, because excess attack successes convert into damage dice. Which means defense dice, as opposed to soak dice, and the actions spent to make those defense rolls, are at a premium because they also function for all intents and purposes as soak by way of reducing damage pools. This is precisely why attack/defense pools and action economy are king in WoD, and Dexterity double dips.

                        When you remove or nerf overage, that double dipping no longer applies and characters can actually wear armor without crippling themselves. Thereby, putting weapons and armor on parity.

                        But the fact that Fortitude is one of the more common Disciplines, and it's really bad at it's job quickly makes players avoid bothering with high levels of it.
                        I know it's "bad". I'm pointing out why it's "bad" -- it's ill-designed for the combat system it fits into. And the combat system is, itself, bad. Ask yourself, would the nature of this conversation fundamentally change were we talking exclusively about lethal instead of aggravated?

                        ...and it still can't help my Gangrel player avoid damage in combat the way the Brujah player can by simply having an extra action or two to dodge attacks.
                        Well...yeah. That's what happens when every success on an attack beyond the first, when the target can't defend, converts to damage dice. Damage pools skyrocket, and any level of soak the target may be capable of bringing to bear ceases to matter.

                        Oh something I just thought of- if overage isn't a thing, then wouldn't it be more likely that players could make targeted attacks against unarmored locations more viable? Food for thought.
                        Oh trust me, after fifteen years' experience running without overage, it absolutely does make called shots matter. The situation with overage is so out of whack, the difficulty penalty on the attack roll makes characters likely to have less damage dice on the resolution, because of how excess attack successes convert to damage. Remove overage from the equation, and those additional damage dice from called shots are a lot more valuable, in addition to the potential of bypassing armor, making the cost-benefit ratio of called shots a lot more enticing.

                        That makes combat better overall, since it gives players a bigger toybox that bring tangible benefits to the table.

                                        Maybe it's because I've been using a die roller program for online games for a very long time but large die pools have always shown to be very wonky. The fact that each die has a 1 in 10 chance to remove a success but only (usually) a 5 in 10 chance to succeed has always made large die pools act in bizarre ways. Firearms are a big example of this- I've often seen someone gather a large pool of damage dice through various means (sniping or point blank shots being prime examples, plus the fact that if you have no cover, the only way to dodge is to fall prone) and the results have always been...ludicrous. I recently saw someone roll TWENTY FIVE dice of damage after a successful Ambush and only get 8 damage successes, 5 of which were soaked. So I started to realize that, at some point, adding another die seemed to have a minimal impact on the proceedings. I started to consider this might have been the intent. My solution was to make 10 = 2 universal, and now specialties ignore ones (similar to the effect of Charmed Existence) and we still see bizarre results. If anything, the result of this chance is we've been seeing smaller die pools get more successes than dice rolled, while the larger ones still remain unwieldly.

                                            Originally posted by Lynceus View Post
                                            Maybe it's because I've been using a die roller program for online games...
                                            That's why. Random number generator scripts are only pseudo-random; they're deterministic, because they propagate a string of integers based upon a seed generated at the time the script was run. You can get more random results from scripts that rely on external data for seed generation, but the results are still going to be a string of integers based upon that seed. You can get truly random results from a computer, but I don't have the few dozen million bucks to buy a quantum computer and the infrastructure to support it, to do the same job a $10 set of dice does faster.

                                            I'm not going to blow smoke and rainbows, dice are deterministic too. They're just a lot less deterministic than a computer program, let alone perceptibly so.
