                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                            Apparently, it's slightly more complicated than that. All of the Sabbat Elders are bunkered down for Gehenna like Bezos and Zuckerberg while sending out the Sabbat as a massive cannon fodder army of Zerg while they bark orders via radio or telepathy.

                            At least according to Justin Achilli.

                            Which is still far far less interesting than the Old Sabbat and I had my criticisms of the Old Sabbat (The Sabbat's handling is what finally killed my faith in V5)
                            For real? Ugh, that is just... it's just painfully dumb. Like wow. I'm just sitting here shaking my head. I didn't like what they did with the Sabbat in V5, but it made a certain amount of sense.

                            The Sabbat's leaders have always been hypocrites. They talk about brotherhood and equality, but they hoard all the money, all the power, all the blood and then send their pawns off to die. They're assholes, just like the leaders of pretty much all of the groups in the World of Darkness are assholes in some way. That's just the "punk" part of the setting's DNA. And it makes sense - the Sabbat grew out of the Anarch Rebellion. Of course many of the early Anarchs were mad about the way they were treated by elders, but a lot of them joined in because they wanted the power, influence and prestige held by the feudalistic elders and didn't want to wait hundreds or thousands of years. The Anarchs who went on to found the Sabbat did so with the idea that, "We'll make our own sect with blackjack and hookers and I'll have all the power there that I would have been denied in the Camarilla."

                            So the elders in the Sabbat had the power. Then they fed their offspring and their offspring's offspring stories all about the big war, the crusade, that they were brothers and they needed to go fight and die. This worked great until the Beckoning happened. Suddenly all of the Sabbat's elders vanished, and there was a huge power vacuum and you had a bunch of younger vampires step up to take over. Unlike the old hypocritical Sabbat leaders who had had come out of the Anarch Revolt, the new Sabbat leaders had been Embraced into the sect. They were the true believers. They believed in all of the stuff that the old hypocritical leaders refused to do. They tore the sect apart, drove out everyone who wasn't ideologically pure enough (like followers of various Paths or the way the Lasombra saw what was coming and left), and then burned everything that was left to the ground to devote themselves to the cause.

                            This was a very obvious, kind of hamfisted parallel to conservativism in the US and other countries. In the US, many conservatives had publicly held views on things like shutting down the government, or banning abortion, but they never took steps to actually do those things. They were talking points to rally people, but the politicians knew how dangerous that stuff would be. But as they stepped down, they were replaced by people who had been born and raised on those talking points, and they thought that was stuff that they should be doing and suddenly you have a situation where the crazy people, who were not originally intended to be in charge, are in charge. It's not just a US thing, you're seeing it in Canada and Europe.

                            Again, I didn't like what V5 did with the Sabbat, but I understood where it was coming from, it made a certain amount of logical sense within the setting, and it's easy to draw parallels to events in the modern day.

                            But I guess now the Elders never actually vanished, they're just pulling the strings and burnt out their own sect and destroyed their entire powerbase, which they had spent centuries creating. It just feels pretty hollow and kind of stupid.

                                                        To complain some more about V5's Sabbat, it's also weird because the story is so convoluted and weird in its handling of things. It was clear they didn't know what they wanted to do with the Sabbat so they kept making weird uses for them like, "They all went to the Middle East to fight Vampire Gods."

                                                        They could have had:

                                                        * A devastating civil war

                                                        * They could have been the chief victims of the Second Inquisition due to their disregard for the Masquerade

                                                        * They could have split into multiple sects and blood cults

                                                        There's only a couple of hundred thousand vampires in the world even in the most overbloated estimation of vampire numbers so it's not like you had to do much to write away the Sabbat as a less prevalent part of the setting. Even if you didn't want them to be as prominent, you could just dial them down without destroying them completely or changing them utterly.

                                                        I remember when Chicago by Night was a few years away from release that I had the Lasombra become a Camarilla clan because of the spoilers and my players asked, "How did that work."

                                                        I said, "Well, the Lasombra Anttribu embraced a lot more and the Sabbat Lasombra had their numbers reduced by infighting so its now a more even mix. The former are now able to assert clan status and the latter can't wage effective war on their kinsmen. Maybe like a hundred defected."
