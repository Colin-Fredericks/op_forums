    The Wolfman(a Protean homebrew)

    06-12-2021, 04:25 AM

    I know what you're thinking: if you want to play a wolfman, why not just play WTA? The thing is, WTA's garou aren't much like movie werewolves. They are more like Druids than creatures cursed to transform into savage beasts. I've found playing a Gangrel closer to being a cinematic werewolf than playing a Garou. Heck, the 2010 Wolfman movie works waaayyyy better as the story of a Gangrel becoming the enemy of a sire who has gradually dropped to Humanity 1 or 2 than it does as a Garou story.

    Garous are also waayyy too powerful for the story of a hunted monster to work. A Garou would simply step sideways if it found itself in the situation in the above clip, and is waayyy bigger and more physically powerful than the wolfman.

    So since the Gangrel are already kind of Wolfmany to begin with, I came up with this power to push it a step further: https://www.v5homebrew.com/wiki/The_Wolfman

              Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post
              I know what you're thinking: if you want to play a wolfman, why not just play WTA? The thing is, WTA's garou aren't much like movie werewolves. They are more like Druids than creatures cursed to transform into savage beasts.
              Many of the famous cinematic werewolf tropes aren't handled well by traditional Garou PCs (although some of this is because too many STs and players ignore concepts like the Curse, the Beast Within, and other negative aspects of Rage). But they can be emulated much better with concepts such as Ronin, Lost Cubs, or Lunatics.

              But those are either left better as NPCs or part of a character's backstory, rather than a PC going through that in game.

              WtA probably needs at least ten good pages of mechanics and concepts in some sourcebook to help an ST run a chronicle where a PC goes through some of these tropes before final mastering themselves to be a Garou, how to integrate these kind of characters in a game where other PCs play Garou with pack totems and such, or running an entire chronicle of Ronin based on this.

              I think it would be very limiting and frustrating for most players, because the key concepts of cinematic werewolves are about things that would limit a PC's agency. And PCs want to make their own decision.

              Having said that, I don't think simply giving Gangrel the ability to assume a Wolfman form (essentially Glabro in WtA terms) makes Gangrel any more like the Wolfman than Garou are. Your objection that they are more like "druids" may indicate that a major problem you have is with Garou society, its Gaian religion, and environmental themes that often hijacks the game from being one of "savage horror". I definitely agree with you there. It's one reason I prefer some of the depictions in early 1e/2e that presented the Garou as fractured, partially demoralized, and many members were not good members in standing with the Litany in actuality regardless of their public status. As the game went on, Garou became much more likely to be honest followers of the Litany than vampires were of the Traditions, and so we saw far less NPCs in sourcebooks and stories ignoring portions of the Litany when it was convenient to do so. It also meant more concentration on external enemies (minions of the Wyrm) rather than a dynamic on personal stories and other Garou (something that would also happen in Vampire when sect wars dominated the setting). I wonder if that is a byproduct of player behavior as a result of tying Renown to Rank, or the general tendency to want to lionize the Garou as heroes because of the games' environmental themes, or simply that many players really don't want to be in a horror game, but be dungeon crawling murder hobos.

                  It's not just savage horror that WTA is missing, it's body-horror. I can not express fully just how disheartening the garou's total control over their form is from a body horror fan's perspective. The mechanic where you can spend one rage and instantly transform in the blink of an eye like a "Bewitched" witch snapping your fingers and transforming something in one instant cut is just....siiiiigh...to me. I deliberately avoid using it and make characters with a low transformation dice pool just so I can roleplay a slow snap-crackle-pop transformation like "American Werewolf in London" and "The Howling".

                  It's resulted in the frustrating realization that the only way to play a cinematic werewolf is to play a Gangrel and encourage the ST to give me routine frenzy rolls, so I actually get to roleplay body horror. Like, a couple of months ago, I started playing a game, and in the very first episode, I frenzied and my hands turned into scaly clawed hands like an eagle or lizard. I got to roleplay being utterly horrified that this was going to progress like "The Fly", since we were embraced in-game and my character didn't know the rules yet.

                  Maybe I'm just old. In my day, werewolf transformations were slow and painful and full of body-horror. Not like these new fangled "Twilight" werewolves who transform painlessly in the blink of an eye you youngins have. In MY day, you spent a good three minutes transformin', and you were screamin' in agony the whole time while your body popped and cracked and stretched, and you had to walk two miles in the snow, uphill both ways while ya did it!

                      Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post
                      It's not just savage horror that WTA is missing, it's body-horror.
                      I can see your point since body-horror is important to you. The entire issue then comes down to how many turns it takes to change form. Is it enough so you can roleplay out your PC's reaction?

                      For most players, this is likely not an issue. They don't want to wait multiple turns because that is "lost" actions which could prove fatal in any fight. So they like the rules that allow instantaneous transformation, and that's why they are in the game. I'm sure you know this, but you are coming from a different perspective because you want to roleplay that out.

                      You can add that to a WtA character too just by using the Curse flaw or adding a homemade Flaw. That's jury-rigging the issue, and you may feel that WtA requires too many of those to make it feel "right" for the setting as a whole. I view it mostly as a matter of emphasis - WtA does have a lot of concepts buried within it that does emulate the kind of things we've been talking about here and other posts, but tends not to portray them in most sourcebooks. It's something I can live with in my own chronicles by emphasizing them, but I understand why you don't.
