                            Regarding Tzimisce Culture and Organization

                            It is true that the Tzimisce were relatively organized, but it's also true that most medieval Voivodes were highly independent and at conflict with each other as/more often than not. If you did not have a terrifying demigod like Yorak ordering something to happen, getting the various Tzimisce warlords to cooperate was a task that'd make herding cats seem simple by comparison. Their rules regarding hospitality do not necessarily translate to camaraderie on the battlefield. And even if you could get most of them aimed at the same goal, like wiping out the Tremere, you couldn't count on them collaborating to be as effective as their numbers might indicate; some might even undermine their rivals to the detriment of the Clan's larger conflict.

                            Regarding The Power of the Tremere

                            It's important to remember that the first Tremere, while organized and possessing a great deal of supernatural lore, did not immediately have the broad knowledge of Thaumaturgy that they came to possess. They needed time to learn how to convert their Hermetic arts into Kindred Blood Sorcery, and even after a while the variety of Paths at their disposal was still probably limited in comparison to what they have in the Modern Nights. Which doesn't even include learning about the more basic features of the Curse and their other Disciplines. They were definitely at a disadvantage initially, and there's a good chance they wouldn't have made it, IMO, if 1) their enemies weren't relatively disorganized, and 2) they didn't have allies like the Ventrue and the support of powerful supernatural entities like Saulot and Kupala (Stars-Above and Root-Of-All were the aliases for them in the book about Ceoris IIRC).

                                        Originally posted by Hello View Post
                                        It makes a lot of sense when you think about why revenant families either defected to the other side, or killed their masters. The tzimisce are such terrible rulers, it makes you wonder how no angry mob with torches stormed their castles during the day. That's some strong plot armor right there.
                                        The Tzimisce often made heavy use of the Blood Bond, which means that regardless of how terrible they were as rulers, they did often have plenty of servants that would literally lay down their lives to defend them. While they lived in a castle. A random angry mob of villagers with pitchforks and torches is going to have a hard time breaching their defences, and if they don't succeed before sundown, they are in trouble. And assuming they *did* succeed, they'd better be able to rouse every other village in the region to their side, or the Tzimisce and their minions would leave their flayed corpses hanging from the rafters as a warning to the rest. If they were lucky; if they were unlucky, the Tzimisce would leave them in a state that would make them *wish* they were dead, as a warning to the rest.

                                        Setting aside their pride on its own as a motive, kine rising up to murder their Cainite overlord is *not* the kind of precedent that the Tzimisce are going to allow to stand for obvious pragmatic reasons. Organized or not, the Clan absolutely would collectively descend on the guilty party like a legion from Hell. It didn't become possible until the Clan began literally tearing itself apart during the Anarch Revolt.

                                        Regarding Revenant families, betraying the Tzimisce and living to tell about it is the exception rather than the rule. Ask the Basarabs. Or the Danislavs, who were literally thrown under the bus by one of their own... who became a Tzimisce as a reward for betraying his kin. The Ducheski are the only family known to have succeeded at such a betrayal, and they simply traded one asshole master for another slightly-less-asshole master. Which doesn't even mention the Vlaszy, who went down with their elder Tzimisce masters during the Anarch Revolt because (supposedly) willingly Blood Bonding yourself to your Tzimisce liege was part of their family culture. Most of the Revenant families have either remained loyal to the Tzimisce and the Sabbat, or they have disappeared.

                                        We need a Spider-man meme labeled as different Clans pointed at the others yelling "plot armour!"

                                        Really, it just mostly comes down to the fact that even the most organized Clans were not monolithic military empires that could genocide an entire Clan out of existence without considerable risk to themselves, assuming they even had the will to do it. In case you haven't noticed, the two most prominent examples of this being attempted 1) targeted Clans that were both highly disorganized and extremely peaceful compared to their peers, and 2) failed, often creating groups of extremely militant fanatics hell-bent on vengeance as blowback for even trying. In the case of the Tzimisce, they were extremely aggressive, moderately organized, and had a powerful home-field advantage in the form of a supernatural pact with a literal demon inhabiting their territory.
