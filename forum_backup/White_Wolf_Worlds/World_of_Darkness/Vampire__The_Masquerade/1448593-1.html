                The Mood: Gothic. The world and its powers that be are large and imposing. Age, size, and power over people reinforces the protagonists' inherent weakness in the shadows of their oppressive looming and the greater schemes of the world. The alliance between the corporate and political classes, the bureaucracies and laws, pop culture and the mindless mob. All of them are against you. So is your past and your own personal failings.

                The Theme: Punk. Be yourself and fuck anyone who has a problem with that. You are a person and an individual rather than a number or a cog. You - yes, YOU - are important and your voice is worth being heard, provided you are willing to speak up and accept the potential consequences of not following the crowd. Ultimately, the only person who can save you is you.

                The Genre: Noir is the typical default. It's an often bleak setting rife with cynicism, fatalism, and moral ambiguity. You're a Protagonist, not a "hero", and that's important. Your motivations are often inherently selfish, but odds are you're still more moral than the powers that be. Other people will try to use you for their own ends and vice versa. And this frequently results in a great deal of intrigue and tragedy. But also maybe the occasional well earned moments of triumph.

                This can easily veer into other areas such as Political Thriller, Heist, Mystery, Romance, Cosmic Horror, Splatterpunk, or violent Action.


                (In hindsight, I rather wish Vampire had done a series of sourcebooks about running the game in certain genres, including sample chronicles. Political Intrigue, Horror, and Romance would've been good ones. Maybe Action/War as well.)

                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                    For me, they always come in these flavors:

                    Gothic Punk is when you're a penniless Brujah and his runaway Goth girl Tremere girlfriend of 12th and 13th generation respectively living in an abandoned hotel because the Ventrue Prince murdered your mortal families after you revealed you were vampires to them. You now plot to get your revenge but need, first, to be hired by the Primogen to go sabotage the art gallery of stuffed human corpses (faked of course ) displayed by their rivak.

                    Gothic Horror is when you are a Toreador Embraced by a mad old Elder as their concubine who has successfully staked them, leaving them to burn, only to discover that you actually are not the person you remember them to be. No, your memory of your past was erased and you must use your wits and charm to pretend to be the monster you were made to be while trying to discover if you had a past at all.

                    Urban Fantasy is when your characters enter a strange and fascinating world of vampires, demons, werewolves, and magic living under the surface of the mortal world. The point is not horror but to appreciate the beauty, terror, and weirdness of the surreal. The World of Darkness as a whole tends to be invoked here. Marco the Malkavian is an occult investigator who follows clues only he can see to find missing girls stolen by Boogeymen, solve the passions of wraiths, and investigate the secret of Nod's mysteries.

                    Gothic Punk Urban Fantasy Horror is when you're named Bloodrage Stonecold, 7th generation Caitiff with Celerity, Daimonion, and Thaumaturgy armed with two silver plated pistols that shoot explosive rounds while wearing a bandolier of incendiary grenades. Plus, you have a katana that is also silver plated. You exist to hunt werewolves, Sabbat, and giant flesh monsters. You follow the Path of Badassitude that has the central tenets of "Fuck this shit."

                    I admit, I pretend to do the first two primarily but always end up with Stonecold.

                    I'm pretty much the same way. First campaign I ran in the 90s, I had an idea of the local Primogen, what the politics were, and who the major players would be in the Chronicle. Everything was prepped for a game of intrigue, dramatic reveals, and general skullduggery as the characters simultaneously wrestled with their own bestial natures. Within five sessions, the entire coterie was riding around in a copy of the Munster family car that played the show's theme tune when it honked, followed in a clown car by three circus performer ghouls (a fire eater, a strongman, and an acrobat). Not what I'd originally had in mind, but we had fun all the same.
