    Thumbs up or thumbs down these mechanic changes

    06-02-2022, 07:06 PM
    (1)Stamina giving health levels instead of soak dice.

    (2)Difficulty being purely the number of 6+'s, rather than a combination of the difficulty being anything from 3+ to 10 and the number of successes.

    (3)The introduction of Hunger Dice and the resultant Bestial Failures and Messy Criticals.

    (4)Humanity based around 3 sins rather than a 1-10 chart.

      For my two cents, mostly thumbs down.

      1. Health levels being almost always the same is part of the game balance, messing with that tends to very quickly skew things. While V:tM does sell itself on a social heavy play style, combat is too prevalent to ignore how quickly health level imbalance can change things.

      2. I can see the value of attempting to simplify Difficulty, but in truth if the diff number goes below 6 then many people don't bother calling for a roll.

      3. Never been a fan of those mechanics.

      4. The paths have never been especially good guidelines IMO, reducing to down to three sins doesn't really improve the system, it just changes the degree to which the path matters in game, which in turn means if you are cutting it down by 70%, why not just ditch the entire system?

      Was that the sort of feedback you were after?

                              Don't know if this is for comparing V5 to past editions or for backwards compatibility. Either or, here's my 2 cents.

                              1. In real life, different people are able to handle different levels of pain and injury. Both old and new represent this in different, but interesting ways. Ultimately, I prefer the old soak method.

                              2. No. While I appreciate V5 making "difficulty" fixed, it actually kind of made it worse in some ways. The old method of 3 to 9 is best honestly.

                              3. Mega no! While I love what hunger dice try to do, it actually kind of fails in practice. Hunger dice are not meant for systems where you roll often, which is ironic for a TTRPG. Also, fuck Messy Criticals specifically. They effectively turn an exceptional success into a botch!

                              4. Actually love this the most, although I'd personally make 1 change. Instead of calling it Humanity I'd call it Integrity. There wouldn't be much mechanical difference, except that Integrity is effectively flavored as one's conviction and control over oneself. Many vampires would still call it humanity, while others claim to follow their own paths.

                                      OK, so with better clarity on the desired feedback:

                                      Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post
                                      (1)Stamina giving health levels instead of soak dice.
                                      Thumbs up. Good fit for the general change in combat mechanics.

                                      (2)Difficulty being purely the number of 6+'s, rather than a combination of the difficulty being anything from 3+ to 10 and the number of successes.
                                      Also thumbs up. The older game had too many variables and something needs to go. Target numbers being set at X is the best of the options because it makes it much easier to understand the math of the game and how to value different adjustments. Needing to roll 7s instead of 6s for +1 dice of damage is not intuitive to understand if that's a good or bad deal. Taking a -1 dice penalty to inflict 1 damage is easier to see as, on average, a fairly good deal on average damage, but still tactically variable because of how V5 combat works.

                                      (3)The introduction of Hunger Dice and the resultant Bestial Failures and Messy Criticals.
                                      Thumbs down. Hunger Dice are too present leading to Bestial Failures and Messy Criticals as too much of a constant risk regardless of the stakes of the scene. The justification the "blood points are a boring gas tank," given by Martin I think loses both the utility of tracking set points over random outcomes,and how to connect the random outcomes effectively as consequence for other choices.

                                      (4)Humanity based around 3 sins rather than a 1-10 chart.
                                      Hard to answer as I don't entirely agree on the premise of treating the three Chronicle Tenets as equivalent to the old sins charts (there are other ways to get stains that apply regardless of Chronicle Tenets).

                                      But to give some level of answer to the intended comparison: thumbs up to negotiated for the group chronicle "morality" as a tool to help get the group on the same page as to the moral themes that will be present in a given instance, but thumbs down to the implementation and specifically of how it interacts with the Stains system. Most specifically I find the guidance on how severe a Stain penalty should be very lacking. Once you get to "how bad is this violation"as a ST, you have incredibly broad fiat to interpret and assign Stains for the action, and the players have to trust you to be fair and fun in it and have minimal recourse in the text regarding best practices to point to if they have trouble with the call.

                                                  1) Speeds things up. I think VtR showed the advantages of this quite along time ago. The lack of random variables has a downside, but also an upside.

                                                  2) This is good. Low target number was always preferable and it was not obvious for everyone how the math worked. Now everything is more equal. I like target number 6 more than 8 simply because it generates more success, which clicks gratification in my head. I think the VtR system is more solidly designed, but a target number of 8 was always a really tough thing in older VtM, so I do not like it.

                                                  3) It is a hit and miss. Hunger is a simple yet much better system. Messy critical and beastly failures, well… That quickly goes into house rule territory. This could have been done better, but the fixes are so easy.

                                                  4) Absolutely fantastic. The old humanity was such a contrived mess. Breaking someone’s bones was typically fine, but taking their money will doing it was not, and let’s not mention it you vandalized a window, you soulless monster. The last house rule I had for revised was running entirely on just virtues, with obvious transgressions being contested against conscience/conviction where they either failed and gritted through it with artificial penalties, or they succeeded and felt bad about it. A roll came up fairly rare as they all kept to a moral compass they had set out. There was no chance of wassail, but if someone had wanted to play in such a struggle, I would have tailored the game to it. Degeneration with all its limits were really harsh as it was written, and with the artificial codes it did not do what it was supposed to do, and drained the fun out. So a really good change, easily moddable.

                                                  But I think one of the best changes was to make combat etc contested rolls, instead of the sluggish attack/dodge circus.
