        Originally posted by SetiteFriend View Post
        I agree that Touchstones don't bring enough advantages. Outside of recovering aggravated willpower, there is little reason to interact with them. It doesn't help that many STs are just concerned with telling their own story and forgetting about each character's.

        I realize that V5 usually gives Willpower recovery as a carrot for this kind of thing, but it XP works too--I have seen this kind of scheme work really well in Blades in the Dark where you get XP for roleplaying in specific ways that are consistent with your character.

            I am biased, because I love Exalted Intimacies, but I think you could drop Convictions and Touchstones as they are written, and make them types of Intimacies (corresponding to what Exalted calls principles and ties, respectively).

            That means they can serve multiple purposes: regaining/healing WP, gaining XP, replacing V5's social combat system, and giving the ST hooks to create story ideas. You can just read Exalted 3e for how Intimacies work, and then adapt them yourself.

            They work sort of like Aspects in Fate, that you leverage to influence others. You can impose new Intimacies on people and weaken or strengthen existing ones too. Uncovering them also adds more to social scenes. WP damage becomes a last resort when you want to refuse influence, rather than the default. They're also great for coming up with ambitions.

            ETA: I snipped a big essay here. If you want more detailed rules, let me know, as it was looooong! I did save my original post.

                        As a rule of thumb, making the game harder just to have a bonus not be actually a bonus isn't a great design option. Imposing them as a requirement to recover WP will still feel as a hassle instead of a benefit, and risk problems when characters aren't able to do it for any reason.

                        Remember, the problem isn't that they don't have mechanics, is that those mechanics feel like a chore, not a benefit.

                        ​I think Heavy Arms' suggestions plus OP's idea work well for starters. Another simple solution is to include them directly in the, as CajunKhan described, fancy parties, detective work, and fighting. They don't need to be in-the-know or something to be characters with abilities, networks and goals relevant to the game and stakes on the story.

                        Making this isn't hard once you reduce the number of Touchstones to a more manageable 1 per player. Basically the players give you a host of NPCs for you to use, just use them. Make one the Mayor's secretary they must go through directly or not from time to time, another may be the city's main gun seller, the last is the coterie's landlord. Now those characters have other roles in the game than just being The Touchstone", which makes going after them seems less intrusive to the plot even when they are indeed being seek "just" as Touchstones.

                        But unfortunately the game never gave any advice on how to do this, it just goes through the "liability" route...

                            Something that I don't think is actually against the rules (and thus wouldn't require a house rule per se), but is kinda skipped in V5 while very present in VtR 2e:

                            Let players attached other traits to their Touchstones. One very simple way to keep them involved is by having them be things like allies/retainers/mentors/contacts/etc. While perhaps a bit much for VtM to copy directly? Make an Alfred to your Bruce Wayne. Or countless other examples. Comic books inspirations might mean some adjustments to the tone of your V5 game, but they are full of skilled-enough-to-be-useful characters that do double duty as emotional anchors for the main characters they're attached to. Even bright shiny superheros recognize that the stories need some more grounded characters to keep said superheroes feel connected to everyday people.

                            This can also make the liability side of things more organic and fun, as the players are designing characters - not mechanical bonuses - that are going to have some natural negatives to them. Lois Lane is a highly talented journalist with a nose for public corruption cases meaning she has contacts, informants, and connections all over one of the biggest and most important cities in the country... and exposing the secrets of powerful people is dangerous in the real world, let alone the WoD, requiring the PC to put in some work to keep her safe. Plus if anyone is concerned that Lois Lane isn't a dark enough example of how abusive being tied to a vampire can be, I invite you to dig into some of the horrific abusive shit Superman did to her during the Golden and Silver ages of comics to justify protecting his dual identity. Pick up any Batman classic graphic novel, and you're probably going to find out about yet another example of how Bruce treats the few people in his life that can actually connect to him as a human being like garbage.

                            You do not have to reach far to twist these from purely positive relationships to dysfunctional ones as befits the tone of your chronicle, and you get the added bonus of Touchstones the players want to bring into the story because they paid to have useful NPCs with their dots.

                                        Originally posted by Caldorian View Post
                                        In general, I like the system well enough. (It's not as good as VtR 2e, but still...) My biggest problem is that Touchstones don't really bring an advantage to the table; actually, they are only liabilities for the PCs, since the damaging or the loss of Touchstones can cause Stains. In fact, they are only relevant because they are loosely tied to Convictions mechanically. So, neither do they bring a narrative advantage to the gameplay, nor do they give the PCs any mechanical benefits.
                                        I don't play V5 so I can't comment on it per se. But I know that way back when my gaming group first started doing more roleplay oriented stuff - providing backstory to characters, giving them real personalities, having more goals than to just get more gold and XP - there was a bad problem with the GM going "ah, the PC now has a love interest (or a little sibling, or some other loved one) so I can put that character in jeopardy or kidnapped by my NPC villain". Now if that was only done once in a blue moon, that would be OK. But it quickly became the default move by every GM to every such character.

                                        The result? All the players STOPPED including such characters in their backgrounds, or building close relationships with NPCs. It was always a detriment that would hijack the game instead of adding fun to the players. So our campaign stopped being as good as it could have been because the ST could only use our contributions in the most negative way against us (I don't blame our GM too much, this is a common mistake by GMs before they have a lot of experience).

                                        From your description, I can see Touchstones being the same thing. If it can only be used negatively against the PC, players eventually become detached and sullen. There has to be the potential for something positive for the PC to happen.

                                        I don't necessarily think a single, defined mechanical benefit that always applies is the key. It might be. There might be a table of possible benefits the ST (or player) could pick and choose from (perhaps limited by their actual relationship or interaction with the Touchstone). Or maybe it is merely narrative or ST fiat (hey, that was a good scene with your Touchstone, increase your Humanity or eliminate a Stain). But there should be something. The more a Touchstone represents a negative experience (through mechanics or narrative), the more benefit (in some form) it should provide in some way. Otherwise, it's something that is going to become neglected instead of providing for a meaningful and fun roleplay experience (which I assume is the purpose is Touchstones).

                                                I like the idea of different Touchstones offering a different benefit. These are entirely unbalanced and untested, but you could have something like this:

                                                Person - Your Humanity counts as two dots higher for the purposes of using blush of life and blending in among mortals.
                                                Institution - Downgrade one level of aggravated WP damage to superficial if you spend time engaging with your Touchstone. If you spend your daysleep surrounded by the institution, downgrade two levels.
                                                Place - You can downgrade a single level of aggravated damage to superficial per day you sleep in your Touchstone.
                                                Family - As long as your family survives, your Humanity counts as two dots higher for the purposes of rising from torpor.
                                                Ritual - Whenever you spend time engaging in your ritual, you may heal one level of superficial WP damage. If you teach the ritual to someone else, you may heal one level of aggravated WP damage.

                                                            True Friends don't have to be Touchstones, and as I said, True Friend has a lot of other bonuses than just that.

                                                            I'm not sure it would be a good idea to make that benefit free like that, because it loses a lot of its meaning otherwise. It's putting a significant chit down to say, "this NPC is so important to my view of my PC, that I want as much control over them as my PC." When for the most part, NPCs are supposed to serve the whims of the plot more than the whims of one player.

                                                            If you want to move to a more default constrained GM mode of play, I'd do something far more holistic and copy games where player actions generate a meta-currency the GM can then spend to do things like put NPCs in distress; then Touchstones can just cost more than NPCs that don't have have any or much other mechanical rating to them.

                                                            IME and IMO, you want to avoid pushing the players and GM from being to antagonistic towards each other with the mechanics. You don't want the ST constantly frustrated because the Touchstones are never brought into anything fun because the players keep citing Touchstone plot armor. Neither side is having fun in that situation. The CofD method is restrictive enough players aren't really going to use it on every NPC they want to have around (and if they do that's a whole big reason to stop and have a chat). The ST can still do stuff they think is going to be fun with half a dozen other characters. The Doom/Tension/etc pool approach is a rules mediated trade off where the players get to exert some control now, for the cost of giving it up later. When the players decide how much ammo to give the ST, they tend to feel more engaged with how things play out.
