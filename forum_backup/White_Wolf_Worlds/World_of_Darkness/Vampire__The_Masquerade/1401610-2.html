            Note that Elders will still have considerably stronger vampiric powers than a fledgling neonate. Sure, they don't have 6+ dots in anything, but having a much better blood potency and a bunch of level 4+ disciplines can still crush said neonate without breaking a sweat. But do remember that powerful vampire elders were in a massive panic during the late middle ages when many young vampires kicked off the Anarch Revolt by killing off elders. In the end, the elders managed to contain it, but not without the loss of a considerable number of them. A half dozen angry neonates could easily take down a powerful elder if they are prepared.

            Now as for smart vs not smart ... the basic premise of V5 is that elders, while very experienced and powerful, really struggle with the rapid advancements of technology and global communication. But other than that, elders survived because they have learned from centuries of experience how to counter younger kindred by being one step ahead of them. Neonates adapt much better to changing technology and social standards, but can't hold a candle to the wind when it comes to politics, surveillance, currying favors, and making sure that any potential rivals do not gain an advantage on them. So in the case of your bomb maker, said elder knows what bombs are, knows or knows someone who knows how to get necessary supplies on the black market, and will have contacts in the police to set up surveillance on someone they suspect of being a possible threat. Not saying it's not possible for that bomb maker kindred to get away with it, but they would need to execute their plan impeccably and in absolute secrecy. If I'm GMing and a player REALLY does their research and plans things meticulously, I'd certainly allow them to get a shot at that elder. But if they act impulsively, I'd likely rule that said elder is aware something is amiss and has started actively seeking out your neonate's weaknesses with plans to destroy them if they pose a threat.

                    Originally posted by Rock113 View Post
                    AFAIK there is no 6+ traits in V5 now and many people have scoffed at this. So how will you express them of older editions in V5?

                    My brief thoughts:
                    1. Traits: Just like what they had done (Blood Potency, etc.) and Werewolf, raise some new traits and you can add them in your dice pool. Ancestor, Past Life, Renown and so on

                    2. Disciplines:
                    All level 5-9 disciplines of older editions are folded into the single level 5 and appropriate combo ones, but they are still required something effective (Generation or whatever) to learn, exp you spend depends on the strength, for example (I haven't converted the system):

                    Of course for me I will modify overpowerful disciplines, making them more reasonable

                    Tip:
                    1. I will also fold all forms of blood magic(Dur-An-Ki, Koldun, Thaumaturgy, etc.) into the single Blood Sorcery and just leave some options to different ones, making they have more differences in culture or region rather than actual changes
                    2. To some Clan or Bloodline Disciplines, I will refine them, making they have more differences in style rather than actual change. For example, I give make Tzimisce Protean, not Vissicitude, but this kind of Protean will appear more "flesh" and "horrible"
                    To be fair they aren't all folded into level 5's, several Elder Powers are lower then 5 but without the initial option of multiple options for each level they just shunted them to 6+ where you do have the option for multiple variants.

                                                But think about what you just wrote, though. Having a super scary Dominate power that can mass affect people that aren't even at the same location as her makes going up against her a totally lost cause. She feels threatened? Boom ... Dominate *everyone* in the district. Problem solved for her. Horrible problem for the PCs. I get that it's important for PCs to be terrified of such a formidable foe. Still *extremely* challenging, but it is attainable. It's also why The Beckoning is a thing ... it removes the most powerful NPCs from the local area and allows PC actions to *matter*. They can play a political game without worrying that the 5th generation thousand year old Prince of the city won't just instantly kill them all within seconds just on a whim.

                                                Now if you are of the opinion that PCs *should* face situations they cannot possibly have *any* control over because the antagonists are walking gods, then fine, stick with Revised/V20. Different strokes for different folks and all that.

                                                    No such power exists, but I digress: When you're good at a game, do you prefer playing on hard or easy? (and I'm not talking about novelty -extreme- difficulties )

Players are to look for smaller victories to prep them for the big ones, or to avoid loss. Vampire isn't about winning, you win when you slay god, and that's a far off goal. Vampire is about not losing, and clinging on with everything you got to make your unlife comfortable and achieve some small personal goals in the face of a storm. If you aren't ready to face Helena, don't piss her off, don't give her a reason to come down on you personally; Short of a linear campaign that's how you're supposed to play, how you're supposed to live your unlife. Methuselah's aren't a challenge that needs to be overcome, they're a potential challenge. You're supposed to navigate the web of undead living with some semblance of intelligence; V5 dumbing that down by leveling the playing field is not a good thing.
