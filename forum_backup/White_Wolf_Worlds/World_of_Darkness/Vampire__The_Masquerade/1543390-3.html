                                            Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

                                            Anyone who would do that would just end up being as bad or worse than Paradox.

                                            It is precisely this kind of depreciative treatment with the fanbase and previous material due to having a “definite personal view” of the game that got us where we are. Paradox does what they want with the IP and everyone else be damned.

                                            However you initially agree with the vision of the next one, as long as they hold this same attitude, it’s a matter of time for the same mistakes to happen.

                                            WoD5 has its fans and already has a reasonable amount of material and lore. The path a new owner can take without incurring in the same errors is to either start from WoD5 and get things on track without ignoring it, or to make it into an alternative setting.

                                            But that’s still a long way from here. Selling the IP isn’t a simple or fast thing. Who’s interested? How much is it valued today? Who has an actual working plan to make this investment and then work with it?

                                            The beginning of this whole mess was an enthusiastic CEO making a costly acquisition without any idea of how it would actually use it. He had a vision of how it would be a money-making machine in the future, but none in how he would get there. And it being outside his company’s main market played a huge role in this catastrophe.

                                            Now we need a company that either understands this market or really knows who does, but then this buyer will have to both be able to make the investment and have an actual plan to make it worth it in a timely fashion.
                                            Yet sometimes a brutal retcon is critical for a brand. D&D's Second Sundering effectively undid almost the entire Fourth Edition and its metaplot, and in fact allowed it to launch the most successful edition ever.

                                            You don't need a retcon-meta, where it says “this never happened.” You use two or three narrative devices to get things back on a better track

                                                Another option is to give up on making VtM/WoD about "now" and just have the default setting be the tail end of 1999. While that's 25 years ago from now, it is both a fairly pivotal era in modern history, and we have a lot more insight into what's going to matter now. It plays into the nostalgia of the WoD's original run (and it would hardly be the first time present media explores the past), with foresight into how to direct the games to what will still be relevant later.

                                                It also gives you lots of freedom to branch out in lots of directions and thus unify the player base. Does Y2K turn out to be a huge deal and the WoD explodes into a gonzo dark fantasy? There can be books exploring that. Does the WoD continue to mirror own our fairly closely as the powers that be ride the ups and downs of human history as they always have? Sure, explore that. Does the upheavals in human society lead to upheavals in supernatural societies that forces them to adapt to new eras? Do that too. This also drastically frees up the brand for different media, because anything after 1999 becomes a self-contained possible future for the WoD; whether is be one book, or a series of video games, or whatever.
