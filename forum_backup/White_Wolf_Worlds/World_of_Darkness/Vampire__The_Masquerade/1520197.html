    Baali discussion (V20).

    08-09-2023, 10:53 AM
    Hi there! I guess my profile picture might have already hinted my love for the Baali bloodline. I am here opening this discussion in attempt to delve into some of the intriguing and often debated issues that have come to light during my extensive years of playing Baali characters. My hope is that we can shed light on the less explored aspects of this bloodline.

    Let's begin by talking about the entities that the Baali worship. As we all know, the Baali have a penchant for worshipping and occasionally exploiting entities that offer them power. In the pages of V20, the spotlight is cast on their primary devotion to the Children of the Outer Dark. Now, drawing from my experience with Demon: The Fallen, I can't help but notice some striking similarities. But, of course, there are intriguing nuances to consider.

    The Children of the Outer Dark are depicted as entities that were buried beneath the earth, emerging later to utter blasphemous words that caught the attention of The First Tribe – the mortals who were destined to become the Baali in Ashur. This narrative seems to veer away from the essence of Demon: The Fallen, where these entities were cast into the Abyss for an eternal prisoning, rather than being interred underground. Furthermore, the depiction of the Children of the Outer Dark as the original inhabitants of Earth, predating the transformative acts of God, contrasts with the concept of celestial angels who were created in order to aid in creation, as detailed in Demon: The Fallen.

    But wait, there's more to ponder: if the Baali's devotion aligns with the Fallen, why is the wellspring of their power referred to as "The Outer Dark" instead of "The Abyss," akin to the Lasombra's description?

    Lastly, let's touch on the fact that the Nergali counterparts of the Baali have a tendency for worshipping the Archduke Namtaru – a demon associated with plagues and death. This might ring a bell with the Namtar visage, the Visage of Death within the Halaku House. However, it's worth noting that the Archdukes in Demon: The Fallen are distinct – Asmodeus, Abaddon, Dagon, Belial, and Azrael.

    What do you think? What would be your opinion on the matter?

              Same as the others, I also think the Children are something eldritch beyond the Fallen or even Earthbound. Qlippothic, to use a Mage term. Or perhaps Neverborn. I treat the 'buried beneath the earth' as a metaphor for a different realm (or realms). I personally like the primordial history story from Sorcerer's Crusade: Infernalism a lot- it's very open to allow all kind of different dwellers in the dark.
              I think of Namtaru as one Children, and thus differently from Demons. The nomenclature with infernal powers is messy, so if someone calls it a 'demon' I guess it's something for a catch-all term.

              As for the Baali themselves, I found they need some effort to make them work. Nergali tend to be very two-dimensional, so it's easier for me to start with Molochim or progeny of the Third. At least Molochim have a proper motivation, one I suspect they share with the Swarm brood. My main gripe with them is they aren't really given the tools they need to perform their main goal of keeping the Children asleep (until the Kraina of the Well in the V20 Tal'Mahe'Ra book), and Daimonion isn't a particularly well written Discipline. I've pretty much settled on Dark Thauma as Clan Discipline, and making the aforementioned Kraina a quasi-path. I would love to play a 'sensible' Molochim some day, but convincing an ST it could work in a Cam chronicle is a bit of a hard task. So if they appear in my own Chronicles, they are restricted to NPCs.

              There is more than a bit potential overlap between Baali history and several other factions; the Lhiannan could originate from a progenitor with one of the fragments of Namtaru. Kupala could be a Child, which would explain the link between Koldunism and what the Molochim are doing (as well as how it can translate into a Kraina). There's a Bahari path that focuses on Lilith as the mother of monsters, and Bahari history has some other tangents, as Shadeprowler pointed out. Ultimately, what the Children are exactly depends on what you need them to be in your chronicle to fit pieces like these together. If you cross over with Mage, Nephandi jive pretty well with the concept, and Molochim make a good third party in the mix.

                  It is not hat hard tho' to give them a Fallen-conenction ,but NOT the Earthbound
                  Earthbound require a reliquary, either in the form of a totemic object or a geographic location (Earthbound pg. 18 etc.; Devil's Due pg. 37-38) - they can possess flesh, but only very briefly. The Children, on the other hand, are embodied, painfully so, physically trapped in underground catacombs.

                  The Black Hand: A Guide to the Tal’Mahe’Ra states:

                  "The Children of the Outer Dark exist in a state of physical and psychic separation and imprisonment. Their flesh exists in the physical realm, embedded in the substance of the Earth itself in “tombs” that show no sign of human construction, and that have walls etched with the scars of their thrashings. Their minds seemingly exist elsewhere, adrift in a bloody sea of tormented horror that is to them the sweetest of ambrosia, from which they either cannot or will not leave unless coaxed to do so."

                  This is quite interesting because:

                  The Book of Madness: Revised (pg. 27):
                  "Deep in the heart of the Dark Umbra, at the very core of the Labyrinth that surrounds the utter Void, dwell the nightmares of creation. These are the true Malfeans, the things that crawled forth from the Universe's mind and gnawed out that first Labyrinth, and for millennia they themselves have been sleeping. As they slept, they dreamt dreams of power, dreams that took the forms of demons and urges and other, less sightly things."

                  The Black Hand: A Guide to the Tal’Mahe’Ra pg. 120:
                  The final layer of the Underworld is a rocky substratum under the Tempest, where meaning, coherency and sense of self are ground down into nothingness. The Labyrinth is comprised of the most broken pieces of the living world, a maze of infinite size and subtle malevolence, inhabited by ghosts who worship and commune with the void."

                  The Children are the physical halves of the entities, while the Malfeans (or Neverborn) being their drifting, sleeping minds.

                  So then, what are these entities?
                  ​
                  Houses of the Fallen pg. 183-184:
                  Those few Slayers who have directly encountered one of the Earthbound, including Magdiel in London, have spotted some uncomfortable parallels with beings who reside deep in Haven [i.e. the Shadowlands/Dark Umbra], according to some of the elder ghosts. Underneath the shadowlands, monstrous creatures move through a storm-wracked sea of memory with twisted souls following in their wakes. A few of the fallen harbor the terrible suspicion that these bloated, hate-filled creatures of destruction are the few rebels who escaped into the depths of the Haven and changed utterly by the untold millennia that have passed since their defeat. Like the Earthbound, they have become something incomprehensible to the fallen, and the Slayers who regularly deal with Haven are afraid. [...]
                  Charon and his lieutenants have become alien, dangerous beings. The ghosts talk of a place called the Labyrinth, where they were taken to be tested at times of stress and where those specters that were consumed by their own hatred reside in a twisted parody of the demons’ own existences. Most dismiss this realm as the part of the underworld closest to Hell, but a few wonder if it might not be the secondary Haven that Charon was building, No Slayer has yet volunteered to explore somewhere that could be the very walls of the Abyss.

                  The Children of the Outer Dark could be physical shells of entities that used to be those Fallen Slayers who never were imprisoned in the Abyss... but becae something... more (less)?
                  (And would align with the True Name/Celestial Name usage, how the Baali/First Tribe extracted power from them)

                                  Originally posted by Matt the Bruins fan View Post
                                  Phil Brucato's conception of tangible evil seems at odds with Richard Dansky's Wraith-centric one, and the former's view prevails in the new edition.
                                  That's a shame because it means the old World of Darkness, which tried very hard to have some kind of uniting metaphysics between all game lines, is now completely gone in favor of each game line being its own silo.

                                  The great thing about the concept of the Neverborn is that they can be anything that the ST wanted. The game was actually agnostic about that. All the Neverborn meant was that they pre-dated Creation and thus were never "born" in this world. You could take that to mean they were the traditional devils/fallen angels of Christianity. Or bizarre other dimensional beings outside of our time and space. Remnants of some leftover world previous to this one. Something unique to Wraith and its conception of the Underworld. Or more mythological beings like spawn of Tiamat or Apep or whatever being of formless chaos.

                                  Mage provided most of the metaphysics behind the World of Darkness, but Wraith was where its ontology and eschatology came. It also meant that the WoD indeed had its own internal logic, and that each game line - while perhaps limited in its own way without reference to the others - wasn't just flimflammery that was incompatible with the other game lines' flimflammery.

                                  I've always really loved that idea in the Book of Mirrors, and it was what I immediately thought of when the question is what were the demons the Baali worshipped. Whatever entities they worshipped, they were "dreams" of the Neverborn. But that isn't very helpful because the form of those dreams is what really provides the flavor. So what forms did these take?

                                  Of course before you can answer that, you must decide which Baali are you talking about. Because the Baali in the Clanbook: Baali (and especially those involved in the Children of the Outer Dark) and afterwords do not seem to be the same as the original Baali briefly described in the first Storytellers Handbook. Those first Baali, while being occultists devoted to the powers of darkness, were concerned with things like sin, damnation, summoning demons from hell, and so on. Those are not Lovecraftian concepts of cosmic horror which the latter Baali were more heavily associated with. True cosmic horror entities might be terrible and destructive, but they wouldn't care about the state of men's souls. The earlier Baali seemed more like people who worshipped Pazuzu as portrayed in the Exorcist - demonic entities that they originally believed were gods back in Sumeria. Those first Baali just appear to have been the first infernalists in a time when such powers were just seen as "gods" in a world of many gods, not demons opposed to the one God.

                                      That was in Clanbook: Baali, which was the first time the Children were ever mentioned or discussed in print. The idea of the Children being ancient vampires has been around for a long time.

                                      I recall one popular theory, that I still like, back when Clanbook: Baali was published, was that, after being exiled from the Garden of Eden, Lilith had children (with who? Who knows, but Lucifer was the big suggestion). Then after Caine and Lilith had their fallout, Caine retaliated by Embracing the children that Lilith had with Lucifer in order to slight her. This means that The Children are the Children of both Lilith and Caine (and maybe Lucifer as well). But the Curse placed on Caine by God was meant for humans, which Lilith's children were not, so rather than turning them into same sort of vampires that the Second and later Generations turned into, it shattered their minds and their bodies, leaving them sessile and insane but with vast and immeasurable power.

                                      Moving from this basic concept, the idea was that the being who turned the Baali into vampires was actually Lilith. She used the blood of one of her vampiric Second Generation to do it. Note that Lilith isn't a vampire, and The Children can't move or Embrace on their own, which is why the Baali's Embrace was so radically different from any other we've ever seen. That would make one (or all three) of the Baali founders 3rd generation vampires. People pointed to the fact that Daimoinon had received a 10th level power in First Edition, as evidence that there must be one (or more) Third Generation Baali out there, which would either mean they had committed diablere or had been Embraced by a Second Generation vampire.

                                      This means that the Baali are essentially the descendants of Lilith, Caine (and possibly Lucifer if he was the one who fathered children with Lilith) which is why they radiate unholy power to the degree that they do, far beyond any other vampire. Almost none of the Baali know any of this, but they were created by Lilith to take revenge for what Caine did to her Children against his own vampiric children and they represent her initial steps into the Jyhad and that's why they ended up being the enemies of all of the Clans descended from Caine.

                                      Later gamelines tended to push the Children closer to the Neverborn and the creation of the Baali more towards Saulot, largely removing the Lilith connection. But I rather liked that early theory and used in several of my games. I've been thinking about running a Ba'hari focused campaign, and bringing back that old idea of the Baali being children of Lilith (or at least the children of Lilith's Children) could be a pretty powerful story hook.
