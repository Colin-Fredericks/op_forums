    Wights Menagerie

    07-09-2022, 06:25 PM
    I'm brainstorming a sudden idea for making Wights more present in the game. Well, two, actually. One is to create "Path Traps", things that can stripe a vampire from Humanity/Path ratings to put them on their toes about how safe they are on any given rating and make Wights more of a thing.

    The other, and the core of this thread, is to elaborate more on the rules about Wights. Give them more of a sting and greater variation.

    For now those will come as "Wight Hacks", small rule templates to apply when a vampire become a Wight. You can use any as your new rule for them, or it may be that the circumstances of their fall or later development change how their work. You can mix or combine at your heart's content.

    As the thread goes on, if the brainstorming takes momentum, this can develop into more fleshed out systems.

    Originally posted by Template I: The Hulk
    Left alone, the Beast starts climbing its way to physical transcendence of the body it inhabits.

    The vampire spends blood on instinct as soon as it perceives prey to boost Physical Attributes, one full use of their Blood/Turn if possible, evenly spread between Attributes. If more prey is available, they'll do so again as soon as they finish the first meal. It can also activate Physical Disciplines with Blood as normal for combat purposes.

    Every time the Wight reaches a full belly, their body responds during rest with one Evolution from the list below. They happen in order, you take the top-most possible Evolution or moves down when it isn't applicable:
    • Hulk Complexion: the first (or second and so on) point of Attribute Boost in the night lasts the whole night, limited to the vampire's BP/Turn and Generation limit for scene-lasting Boost;
      ​
    • Attribute Increase: the lowest Physical Attribute is permanently increased as if by XP expenditure;
      ​
    • Discipline Increase: the lowest Physical Discipline is permanently increased as if by XP expenditure;
      ​
    • Elder Hulk: develop an Elder Power for Physical Disciplines of a level the Wight already has and approved for Wights;
      ​
    • Hulk Outburst: the first (or second and so on) point of Attribute Boost beyond Generation limit in the night lasts a scene, limited to the vampire's BP/Turn;
      ​
    • Generation Breakthrough: the effective Generation of the Wight reduces by 1. No one knows if someone else can benefit from this.
      ​

    At first there are just one Elder Power for each Discipline that Wights can get, but they exist in levels equal to the level they're acquired -5, so each goes from 1 to theoretical 5. Those levels are requisites for each other in order:

    Celerity - Alacrity of the Raging Beast:
    You have a pool of "Rage" equal to twice this power's level. Every time you spend Blood to acquire Extra Action from Celerity, you can also spend up to half that pool as if it was Blood for the same effect (using the normal limits for Celerity. You recover a point every time you sense prey, and the whole pool upon awakening.

    Fortitude - Toughness of the Unbeatable Beast:
    You can spend a Blood Point reflexively upon receiving any damage to add this power's level to the soak roll as automatic successes.

    Potence - Impetus of the Thrashing Beast:
    When you spend Blood for the basic Potence automatic successes, the effect lasts additional turns equal to this power's level.
    So, the idea of The Hulk is to make Wights physically impressive, but also a growing danger. The longer you let them live, the more dangerous they become. Tracking and fighting the fallen vampire becomes a necessity, but also an extremely dangerous proposition, specially if they manage to go unnoticed for some time. At the same time, the possibility of exploring such raw power may be too tempting for some vampires to let others destroy this thing, be it for its value as a killing machine or the possibility of finding out about its apparent decrease in Generation.

    Originally posted by Template II: The Mystic
    The Beast is the ultimate expression of the Curse. It belongs to the Blood and the Blood to it, it exists since the first vampire and spreads unabated with each Embrace. A Kindred is a Human being infected with vampirism. The Beast is vampirism, and the powers of the Blood belong to it.

    A Wight may not be the smartest user of Disciplines, but its natural grasp on them surpasses anything other vampires can achieve. It retains every Discipline it had and start to develop its own uses for them. It may develop smart routines, increase Discipline levels or learn new Disciplines faster than normal, develop alternative powers even below level 6, and even improve powers beyond what they should do at their given levels, either by making them stronger or reducing cost.

    Routines are easy to establish. A Wight may try any Discipline they have repeating something they've done before with it, or just try a crude new tactic. While it may not work well at first, specially given their usual short term focus, perfection is a matter of trial and error to improve timing and form.

    Increase and transmutations are driven by necessity. Once per night, if the Wight faces an obstacle or danger it can't deal with easily with the resources at hand, it can spontaneously change. The only important guideline is the most direct solution to the task at hand, given the Wights experiences and recent ideas on how to deal with it.

    Lets see an example of a Wight that developed Dominate for a long time this way:
    • Command: the Wight found out that a single command, "stare", solves most of its problems either by locking attention or just making the target less responsive, so this is its main opening routine. It improved the power so that this specific command can be given through any sound and work regardless of language, as long as the target listens the sound.
    • Mesmerize: useful to get the target out of a safe place, the Wight remembers of luring people to dark, closed places where they can't flee and bring others with them. It can somewhat still form in its head a phrase like "bring your friends to this dark, cozy place outside, there's something fun to show them there". Mustering the patience to utter this phrase and waiting in ambush is almost beyond its grasp, though. So first it developed a secondary version of the power that only implants this idea, but then only requires the place of meeting to be indicated, the rest is implied. More dangerous, it improved this new power so the target becomes a carrier of the effect, delivering it when they first meet whatever "friends" they have using the lower between their own dice pool and that of the Wight.
    • The Forgetful Mind: at first this power had little benefit to the hunt, until the Wight figured that maybe something should be done to ensure that the friends of the target won't stop them from going outside to meet the strange pale person. Its first improvement was to superimpose old memories on new ones to not waste time creating a memory. The example being the most common use the Wight makes, pick the memory of a person the target really wants to meet and change the memory of their current meeting to them seeing this person. The second improvement was to have the target do all the talk for it. And the third was to suppress the talk altogether while making this power happen together with their alternative Mesmerize. Now as long as the unconscious command takes hold, the target will remember seeing someone they would like to see anyway.
    • Conditioning: being able to have (slightly) more capable servants to carry out tasks and bring food and shelter is too good a thing for the Wight to pass the option. Its first task was to Condition itself to be able to let such "extensions" live as long as they're useful. It then developed a combination of this power with the Blood Bond that enhances both and speeds the process. It forces a select target to swallow three Blood Points in exchange for their lives and makes the roll. A single success makes the target feel the Wight inside their head and this figment is fully capable of concluding the process through the following nights. The target will feel the effects of the second and then third step of the Bond each subsequent night, and each night the initial number of successes is added again towards the Conditioning. The victim will seek refuge with the vampire, protect it, take care of their existence, and if push comes to shove they're usually 10 BP waiting to be drained, which is a net gain anyways.
    • Possession: this level was already achieved as a Wight, so it began already with a simpler task in mind, scout the area, Dominate people and bring back as much blood as possible. With this, from the outset this Possession had the curious effect of making the target able to drink blood and store it in their stomach, including Kindred teeth to do so. This is all there is to it for now, but maybe, if the Wight is killed during the effect, it can force a last ditch development to transform the body in its own prior vampire body. Also, maybe with blood in its stomach the target may be spontaneously Embraced if killed. In both cases it's anyone's guess what will happen and what the resulting creature will be.

    Blood Magic is particularly dangerous under the hands of the Wight. It can apply the same developments to Magic Paths and Rituals. It can even reduce the Blood requirements or increase the duration of effects, or in extreme cases incorporate rituals as permanent effects.
    My idea with the Mystic is to explore the theme of the Wassail as the vampire condition winning over the soul. Unfettered, the Beast is a mystical being much more in tune with the Blood than a ultimately human(-ish) mind can dream to be. It also makes the Wight an unpredictable threat that can have a variety of powers and always have at least one new trick under its sleeve, which is simply cool.

    Now I have an unfinished idea about a Wight that can hunt in coordinated packs, but the concept is still too blurry on my mind and I don't know how to do it without going too much like zombies. Maybe a hive mind as the Beast spreads fom vampire to vampire as if in the end it was a single entity since dawn of time.

                                  Originally posted by Template III: The Horde
                                  The Kindred think the Beast is a lone hunter. Not without reason, as it drives them against each other in so many ways. Yet, that is not corroborated by Wights on the wild.

                                  For whatever reason, those creatures seek each other and band together. A single Wight can go centuries without finding another, but they seem to sense the presence of their fallen kin at a certain distance and when they meet, they stay close, and the group can grow even more after that. Those gatherings are called Hordes to avoid confusion, or Packs for those who prefer the inherent sarcasm.

                                  Hordes are adept at sharing information and tactics on their narrowly focused goal and coordinating actions, indeed preternaturally good at it, despite rarely engaging in any social interaction outside hunting, feeding and seeking shelter to sleep. They get used to each other vocalizations in no time, developing a "language" with no common words but perfect accuracy. They develop group tactics and strategic thinking to hound and fell prey, specially supernatural prey. And somehow they seem to just know when a member of the group found or felled prey, found shelter or danger, or went down.

                                  Language:
                                  Wights are generally too simplistic to engage complex communication with normal people, but with each other they can parse complex thoughts with ease and few words, in a process that becomes all the more effective the more time they have together. In moments two Wights hunting together start to understand at least basic concepts, and after some hours exchange two or three words sentences with no problem. Those times are regardless of them stumbling on speech, and even a complete lack of a common language just adds one or two hours to it.

                                  In a week at most they're able to have normal conversation across any language barrier, in the midst of hisses and gibberish all the while hunting. In a month those conversations take half the words and in a year entire sentences of any complexity can be parsed with a single "word". In a decade they can communicate whole paragraphs of thought with just a glance, and in a century they can parse all the information they want through any sensory sign they leave for each other, like a broken twig or a smear of blood.

                                  Common sense says that big groups would have a harder time if they don't share a language, but whatever drives their mutual understanding only increases with numbers as they help each other along. Any new member has the time to get at a given point divided by the number of Wights already there in the group, and there is indication that the increased size also helps the oldest members to reach the greater understanding in less time.

                                  Tactics:
                                  Garou are still the masters of pack tactics, but Wights can come a very close second place, with special mention to their ease at coming up and setting up new tactics. The only thing they need to prepare a tactic is literally to describe it to each other, including who will do what at which time. That means their ease of communication increases the danger they represent in combat.

                                  They can't use this to create "new mechanics" as the Garou can do, but by following the tactic they can reduce each other difficulties in their rolls, any roll, as long as it is within the agreed upon tactic. Every action in a Tactic must have a clear purpose within it, and the benefit only exists as far as every member of the group involved in the action keep doing their part and having success. If one fails the benefit is lost until they can start anew or with a new tactic.

                                  At the start of a "round" (not necessarily a combat round, it may even been an entire scene with an action of any nature handed to each member) the Horde defines the Tactic to be used, the actions each one will take towards their goal. It also defines if it will be a Rapid Tactic or a Sustained Tactic. Rapid Tactics require the members to perform their actions with a "delivery", something that directs the action to each other, and each successful roll gives a -1 diff cumulative to all the others, resetting at the end of the round. Sustained Tactics are designed to wear down the target or put them in a growing disadvantage, and give a -1 diff that carries on to the following rounds and increases as the tactic keeps up, limited by the number of member in the group -1.

                                  Strategy:
                                  Despite their simplicity, Wights are very careful at selecting where to sleep or to feed, and how to split resources. They can mix and match members to form hunting parties if they're a big enough Horde, they can scatter through an area for a large amount of lesser prey and meet later to share blood, they can identify danger and stay away or coordinate its destruction. Some form small fiefdoms that resemble Kindred Domains, except for the complete lack of restraint or mortal infiltration even in comparison with the Sabbat.

                                  More worrying, as the Horde grows it can perform increasingly elaborate strategies and, against common sense again, be subtler. A Horde can collectively subdue some of the shortsightedness of its members for the survival of the group, effectively acting as if each member had half the number of members in Willpower for the purpose of overcoming its own instincts as a Wight. They can force themselves to mount watch for a scene, slip out of feeding to bring food to their shelter, face and even wield fire, and so on. No member will ever willingly harm itself or be required to do so, but they spend those points of their own "volition" to keep the group fed and safe.

                                  Self-Perception:
                                  Hordes of Wights seem to have a sense for its members as if they were almost a piece of their own body. Within a few meters the observations are staggering. When one member is presented with blood, fire, a threat, or a dark place to rest, the other will instantly look in that direction even without sensory connection to the first, showing expected signs of preparing to encounter roughly the same thing.

                                  This effect is faster and stronger the shorter the distance, but even miles away the other Wight will prepare to meet the other in a matter of minutes, either prepared to fight if they're in danger, or to rest if they've found food or shelter, according to their needs at the moment. More members increase the strength of this response and the amount of information, from a general "danger" to fire, strong opposition, many enemies, even to the point of knowing if they should look for it, flee it or meet at a rendezvous point.

                                  Destruction of a Wight is the most intense signal in this strange limited telepathy. Every Wight in the Horde will know what destroyed their member and prepare to get rid of the threat. Again, the amount of information depends on the size of the group, but now not on distance. A two-member Horde leaves a single Wight that knows how death felt to the other and maybe the face of the aggressor. A big enough group will know appearance, gait, Clan, Generation, smell, voice, the sound of your steps, the exact position at the time of the deed and the general vicinity, maybe even more details.

                                  Limits:
                                  Really big Hordes are rare. Wights aren't all that common to begin with, and only a fool would let one live enough to ever meet another. As dangerous as they are, Hordes have high profiles for Kindred, which makes hunting them somewhat easier. A fortress with a dozen Wights is the stuff of legends for most Kindred, although some tell of small Domains being completely overtaken and becoming fiefdoms of almost twenty Beasts.

                                  But some scholars ponder over the phenomena and the portents it brings if a big enough Horde ever forms, what kind of horrors this army would bring to the world. What if, in the end, the Beast is a lone predator? The Beast, a single spirit spread like a disease among the Kindred, trying to coalesce in a single entity with many bodies? Many dismiss the notion, and in modern nights derisively refer to those scholars as "Romeros", who should watch less movies and leave home to stretch their legs and meet people. But they wonder what ties this... thing has. To the Thin Blood. To the Ancients. To Gehenna.
                                  It still needs some hammering, the numbers and times are more placeholders than anything for now. My idea with the Horde, that I would, indeed, call the Pack to avoid going too much into Zombie territory, then reverted to the Horde because the Sabbat already have Packs, then decided to joke with the whole thing, well, my idea is to have Wights that get dangerous in numbers, but also complex. Different sizes for the group plays in different ways, from the small pack of ravenous beasts to hellish kingdoms of demons with no interest for petty politics and humanized evil.

                                  How intelligent Wights are on their own is something I didn't covered and mostly won't. I take mostly the same stance as CajunKhan on the matter, I think they're pretty smart when their emotional simplicity and sheer hunger isn't lowing them, but to each their own, I want each ST to use those templates in the way it fits their own ideas. The Horde is the first to really rely on a bit more of smarts, but don't read too much into it beyond what you need, being able to exchange complex information doesn't mean necessarily abstract concepts or that the Wights engage in philosophical debate during their hunt. The hellish kingdom may present a "king" to visitors, and maybe this king is as smart and devious as a Baali Elder or the demons they summon. Or maybe it can barely join two coherent sentences together after spending "willpower".

                                  Originally posted by Template IV: The Slasher
                                  The Beast is a thing of death and destruction bound to a wretched sack of flesh and emotion that calls itself a Vampire. But for all the limitations this pitiful condition brings, the Beast must recognize that it makes its life so much easier. The Kindred is a tool. I vehicle of chaos.

                                  For this reason, sometimes the thing that remains after the fall clings to a last shred of the former vampire. Do not be fooled, even if the person's soul still lives somewhere inside, they're not in control in the slightest. No, they're barely a personality now, existing for a greater purpose as a disguise. The Beast's own Masquerade begins.

                                  Such Wights retain pale remains of their former self, enough to keep a facade of normalcy and exist in the midst of Kindred society. They can keep short interactions, pass off as low Humanity and withdrawn, and even hold office, make decisions, pretend to care for something. Those of great willpower can even negotiate and give up immediate satisfaction for future gratification.

                                  At the core of the facade is their Mask, something that emerges from their previous Nature and Demeanor. The Mask simultaneously defines the drive for the Wights behavior, its pretension for higher thought and the guideline for its role-play. It doesn't have enough emotional complexity to hold distinct Archetypes anymore, but it sure has the acumen to derive all it can from a single one.

                                  Wights with a Mask believe in their stated standards and goals as defined by the Mask. To a Beast, pretense and belief are almost the same if things have to go for too long. For an Autocrat it may mean that it will bring ultimate order and peace to the world, or at least the city, and its every action is for this purpose. They can strike conversations about this topic with length and dept, and put all their sometimes considerable intellect into it. They can even show passion for hours, almost as if it was Blood, safety or danger, the other topics it can talk about with such intensity.

                                  Every action of a Wight must be towards their Mask's most direct interests. Even thinking then in long terms may require Willpower expenditure, although usually a dif. 6 roll is enough if gratification is expected within the night. Anything else it at most drags through, disinterested and emotionless, with short term focus and the attention span of a toddler.

                                  More importantly, though, the whole point of this facade is to reach prey the Beast can't on its own. Behind passion, conversation and decisions, the whole nightly life the Wight puts so much effort to barely maintain, the Mask demands its Undertaking. Someone has to die. Something must be destroyed. Blood must be drained in profusion and its lair secured. The Undertaking is an ambitious act of destruction commanded by hunger, rage or fear, through the lens of the Mask, and all the time the Wight must keep it on sight, for this is its ultimate reason to be. And then another and another. Anything else is just preparation for the most ambitious and brutal kill.

                                  Once the time comes, when the Beast can finally gorge, the facade drops and every last bit of the enormous effort it puts to pretend sanity goes keenly focused on the single task ahead. The Wight gains for the scene a +3 bonus and -1 dif. to every roll dedicated to accomplishing its bloody mission, and one extra benefit unique to each Wight, based on its killing method and previous life. Some examples are on the list below:
                                  • Working the Room: the Wight don't need to declare any defense action beforehand, no matter how many times it is attacked or it attacked, it has full defense dice pools against everything.
                                    ​
                                  • Unstoppable: the Wight suffer no wound penalties, ever, and do not enter Torpor due to damage, although it may need to heal extra damage beyond Incapacitated before the scene ends.
                                    ​
                                  • Deadly Distraction: the Wight can still muster charm and endearing words for a few moments without recourse to Disciplines, yet more powerful than them. It says something applicable to the moment and rolls Charisma + Applicable Ability, diff. 6, versus the target's Intelligence + Subterfuge. If the Wight wins, the target is momentarily distracted from any non-obvious signs of problems and drops any usual safety measures against their best judgement, to the point of being considered completely defenseless to an attack.
                                    ​
                                  • Lay of the Land: the Wight perfectly recall every single inch and detail of the area as long as it has spent one month studying it. It is enough to further increase its bonus in the area by +1 dice and -1 diff for hunting and navigating on it, ignore any difficult terrain or traps, pass unnoticed by non-supernatural inhabitants and even use shortcuts almost impossible to explore, seemingly coming out of nowhere.
                                    ​
                                  • Profiling: the Wight studies a person for about five minutes and rolls Intelligence + Empathy, dif 6. A target actively trying to act as something they're not may resist with Manipulation + Performance, dif 6. If the Wight succeeds, they have extra +1 dice and -1 dif on any roll targeting or contested by that person, and the person has -2 dice and +1 dif on any roll they make against the Wight or a trap or something set by the Wight against them, if the roll isn't resisted by the Wight. Sometimes the Wight strikes a conversation with the profiled target for an even darker purpose: with enough time and similar rolls, it can spread it maniacal worldview. If the Wight succeeds on this, the target's very Nature changes (this prompts a degeneration roll regardless of Path rating). With luck, it stops at this. At worse, this Nature will be the Mask of a new Wight.
                                    ​
                                  After some time considering how to make a semi-functional, hidden Wight, re-reading rules on them from VtM and VtR, I decided to play a twist on it and went to another CoD book I really love, Slasher, about serial killers. Most of the rules I still brainstormed by myself, the tough part was to describe how to roleplay a creature that is both a Wight, a functional vampire and a hidden monster among monsters. I expect to have succeeded, but wait for feedback. The list of sample powers, though, I adapted directly from the Undertakings in that book (which I really recommend regardless).

                                  My idea here is to explore the "wolf on sheep's clothing" concept directly. The Wight isn't just a lost and destructive being still living among vampires, it is an active and highly dangerous threat. It is there to tear the Masquerade, cause widespread destruction, and target other vampires for destruction without holding punches.

                                      Something I think would be interesting, though I'm not quite sure how you would do it without crashing into "so how intelligent is a Wight?" would be exploring the idea of that now that the Wight has won, as it were, i.e. the Beast has destroyed everything not itself in the vampire, would it feel compelled to try now to drag down other vampires with it, perhaps to thereby create a Horde?                                           Making wights more common/tempting

                                          1-Offer willpower to players with low humanity scores for when they indulge in something awful. The worse it is relative to humanity rating, the more willpower provided.
                                          2-Diablierie is not an automatic loss of humanity, it's a gamble. Players are far more willing to eat another vampire at 1 humanity when it's not a guaranteed game over.

                                          Making wights more terrifying:

                                          1 Wights aren't the same as the vampire in frenzy. They're the vampire stripped bare of anything that makes them a person, a human; They are cunning and calculating and every move they make concerns survival, and every skill that the vampire had is available to them as a wight. The Former vampire's hunting style often carries over into wightdom: A former Cassanova and masterful deciever will still try to seduce her prey as a Wight, Verbally she may be undercommunative, but the rhythm is still there and Presence works wonders, and if it goes disastrously wrong, as it is likely to do given how "off" wights feel to mortals, she'll adapt. Less social and particularly stealthy predators likely won't go through such troubles: Hell the prince might not even suspect there's a wight in the city for months and might not even confirm it for a year.

                                          2- Wights may hide bodies or use them as territorial markers, depending on the personality/skills of the vampire.

                                          3- Wights learn common disciplines/Protean fast. If we had to give them values, I'd say they reduce discipline costs by 2x current level (unless we want to use V1 discipline costs, then half the discount). They cannot learn knowledges. I'd like to say they can still use any thaumaturgy/necromancy the vampire knew, because with wights the Beast actually has the human soul captive (a reverse to vampires having the Beast captive) However they're reluctant to use such abilities because they're expensive/draw on the human.

                                              Monday
                                              The wight lay full and bloated from its last feast as the sun set. As the sun set, it roused into wakefulness, but luck was with the wight, it remained at zero hunger. With nothing to motivate it into action, it simply lay there the entire night, almost indistinguishable from a corpse except for its pleased expression, an expression that would be have been almost peaceful if it were not so uncanny valley.

                                              Tuesday
                                              Luck was with the wight again. It again remained at zero hunger and again simply laid in its grave all night.

                                              Wednesday
                                              The wight grew hungry. It rose from its grave and found a groundskeeper sweeping. It used stealth to pounce on him and drain him dry.

                                              Thursday
                                              The wight grew hungry. It awakened to find police tape in the spot where it had drained the man dry. It remembered what this meant as it retained its full human intelligence, but as there wasn't any immediate danger, it gave no further thought to the subject. It's emotions were now far too simple to allow focus on long-term dangers. It found no prey near the cemetery. It hunted further and found houses and remembered that those would be filled with sleeping prey. It crawled up the wall of a house to the second story window. Inside lay a man sleeping.

                                              It attempted to crash through the window to get at the prey, but found that it could not. It then remembered that it had developed a Flaw: it could not get into homes without an invitation. In frustration, it scratched at the window, making broken chalk sounds. The man awoke. The wight remembered that men could be "seduced" into becoming prey by doing certain things to your body and combining it with Presence.

                                              The wight attempted a "seductive" expression, but the result was a feral grin, and a glassy, hungry eyed glare. Such complex human behaviors were too alien to it now to imitate in anything but a crude, mechanical manner. It pulled down its shirt and caressed its breast in a manner it remembered as seductive, but was now as mechanical as everything else.

                                              "Invite me in!" said the wight, its voice full of eager hunger, "invite me in and I'll let you fuck me!"

                                              That's how I see wight behavior.

                                                          Originally posted by Template V: The Sinkhole
                                                          Every Kindred lives with the constant presence of the Beast. It stirs and gnaws at the edges of its cage, it manifests in hunger, instincts and basic emotions. As gravity it is a constant pull, resistible but unavoidable, unceasing and eternal, awaiting at the bottom of the pit of Damnation.

                                                          Said the philosopher once, when you stare into the abyss, the abyss stares back at you. For the Kindred, there's no clearer window into this abyss than the Wight. The Beast set free to control the body, the Human set free to rest from the battle. No matter the facade they put when dealing with such creature, no Kindred can ignore the questions pounding in their minds. What if it was me? What if I become this? Can I ever win? When will I lose? If I lose, then what? Can I live like that? Do I want to? Do I want to battle forever?

                                                          Interacting with a Wight is like interacting with your own inner Beast. It feels familiar. It feels horrifying and tantalizing at the same time. It gives the vampire respite from any pretense at humanity or however you call a higher, complex, thoughtful self. And through that, the Beast worms its way to the surface, it tries to connect with the other Beast, an equal, a friend or rival that achieved its dreamed apotheosis. For the person in "control" this opportunity to lose themselves in nihilistic contemplation is extremely dangerous, regardless of the conclusions.

                                                          Wights may have a lot of effects on other Kindred depending on their own unique traits and on how the interactions happens. Some are the Wight instinctively trying to reach the Beast inside the vampire, as an animal that recognizes another of its kind, friend or foe. Others are the Beast trying to reach out, reacting not to the actions of the Wight, but the actions of the observing vampire. All those have one thing in common: they drive the Kindred closer to joining their ranks.

                                                          The ST chooses as many of the following effects as they want for the Wight and defines when it is triggered, common triggers are suggested for each. In general the best is to have at least three triggers, one that arises in response to likely PC actions, one that just has a high likelihood by itself and one the Wight may actively try to impose. Tipping the balance towards any option will greatly affect how a given Wight plays out in your game. When a Beast roll is called, it can be either the Wight's Willpower (resisted by Willpower), 10 - the Kindred's Path (resisted by Path), or a flat automatic success:
                                                          • Obsessive Dreaming: make a Beast roll every time it is Triggered and record the Wight's successes above the Kindred. When the Wight reaches 10 successes, the Kindred develops the Nightmare Flaw with a twist. The dream is actually good, but grotesque, it features the Kindred indulging some action that would be a sin against their current Path rating. If the Kindred does indulges the action, regardless of the roll to keep Path rating, the penalty vanishes. The Flaw is short-lived, vanishing after 1 week, but the Wight keeps accumulating successes until then. With 20 successes the dream lasts a month, with 30 a year, with 40 a decade, and with 50 a whole century. If the Flaw vanishes, the successes go back to 0. This version of Nightmare is cumulative with the normal one, including the penalty. Sample Triggers: Long observations while taking notes of the Wight's activities; The Wight strikes conversation with the Kindred about its interests; Witnessing the Wight delight on a similar sin. Long observations/conversations may trigger every 5 minutes or some other amount of time.
                                                          • Ennui: when it is triggered make a Beast roll, any Conscience/Conviction roll made on the scene is at +1 difficulty for every extra success of the Wight, this penalty reduces by -1 each scene until it vanishes. Alternatively apply +1 difficulty for the rest of the night, plus one night per extra success. Sample Triggers: Stare in the Wight's eyes; Being touched by the Wight; Witness the Wight enjoy a sin above your Path Rating.
                                                          • Proxy Sinner: witnessing the Wight engage in a sin prompts the character to make a Degeneration roll as if they were engaging in it themselves, but at -4 difficulty. Make a Beast roll when triggered to have the effect in place for a number of scenes equal to the Wight's successes, and +1 difficulty on the Degeneration rolls for every excessive success. Alternatively, if the trigger is the action that would prompt Degeneration, you may start it at -2 difficulty on the Degeneration roll and make only this roll. Sample Triggers: Witnessing the Wight engage in an action the vampire engages regularly or used to; Trying to predict the Wight's next actions; The Wight offers something the vampire would normally accept.
                                                          • Faux Contrast: the effect covers a number of particular sins equal to the Wight's successes on a Beast roll. As long as it lasts, the sins in question do not prompt Degeneration rolls, the vampire realizing there's more to degeneration than simply committing the act. The vampire knows when the effect takes place, but not when it ends. This effect lasts 1 to 10 turns, minutes, quarter-hours, hours, nights, weeks, months, years, decades or centuries, at the ST's discretion or rolling 2 dice in secret. Sample Triggers: Witness the Wight debase itself in a manner the vampire never did; The vampire rationalizes, for themselves or others, why they wouldn't ever act like the Wight; The Wight invites the vampire to engage in an action two or more steps below their Path Rating and they refuse.
                                                          • Memento Damnationis: the vampire becomes too keen on the link between powers and curses of the Blood, and the Beast within both. Make a Beast roll, each success "taints" one Discipline of the vampire at the ST's discretion. Using that Discipline prompts a Degeneration roll at difficulty 7. Every night thereafter the character makes a simple Willpower roll, difficulty 6, to reduce the difficulty of this Degeneration roll for a single Discipline by -1. When the vampire uses the Discipline and makes the roll, future rolls are also made at -1 difficulty. All reductions are cumulative until the difficulty is set to 1, when the Discipline ceases to prompt Degeneration. Sample Triggers: The Wight shows greater proficiency than the vampire at a Discipline; The vampire drinks the Wight's Blood for Discipline access or other Elder Blood benefit; The Wight outmaneuver opposition through the use of Disciplines.
                                                          • Releasing Guilty: this effect works backwards, affecting past actions instead of future ones. A Beast roll defines the extension of the effect, going back 1 game session per success of the Wight. The vampire has to roll again every successful Degeneration roll from those sessions that would still apply, losing Path rating/Virtues as normal if they fail/botch, and feeling the release of the guilt as they become numb to their bad memories. Sample Triggers: The character talks to the Wight about guilt or similar topics; The Wight makes their inner peace and satisfaction clear through words or expression; The vampire witness the Wight engaging in similar behavior unopposed.
                                                          For a game that focus on the Wight as a means for personal horror, where the danger is less the creature they face and more the creature they're becoming. You can also use it for other forms of horror where the Wight is an infectious mindset, a disease of the mind that can bring an entire Domain down if left unchecked. Or simply to make it tangentially dangerous, forcing the PCs to come up with creative strategies to attack it without risking their own degeneration.

                                                          While the overall idea was incubating since the first post, and some posts here gave me more to work with, the actual mechanics for the sample abilities were inspired by the Stigmas from Changeling, abilities normal people may develop at very high Banality ratings that aren't magical, but just how their creative nihilism taints their interactions with other people.
