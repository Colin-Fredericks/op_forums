                            The #2 opinion is also a lot more nuanced in a fashion that doesn't support just dumping any vampire sorcery that isn't "bloody" enough (because you could replicate all the mechanical gripes with Thaumaturgy in Cruac if you implemented it with the same issues).

                            1) A big issue is that the secondary paths are all cheaper in XP costs. As far as "powergaming" goes in all this, it makes Thaumaturgy the best all-round option. It might not be as good as a specialist in one area, but it's the fastest/cheapest way to be good in lots of areas. Having Thaumaurgy (or any other blood sorcery) in-clan means raising your primary Path is also much easier to do. Not every "powergamer" problem is mechanics where you min-max into being the ultimate hammer, and then try to solve everything in the game as if it's a nail. There's also the Linear Warrior Quadratic Wizard concept (a D&D-ism, so this is not some VtM specific issue) where casters out-shine non-casters over time due increasing both versatility and effectiveness simultaneously where non-casters can only advance one category at a time.

                            2) This leads to a second issue where Thaumaturgy (but not all blood sorcery in VtM) has the same roll for every path activation. You max out one dice-pool, and you've maxed out activating every path; and rituals only add a second dice-pool which due to char-gen issues is easy to max separately. This increases the perceived issues with #1, but is a separate mechanical gripe that is a problem for people on its own (esp. people that don't like how it was clearly done to keep Thaumaturgy the "best" blood magic even as the game added more practices in).

                            3) Rituals are an unlimited source of additional powers; nobody else gets to just learn new powers for free like that (V5 added XP costs to these, but kept the overall problem by making amalgams count towards your total power cap while rituals don't). This furthers the gripes even more because, again, its the "one stop shop" effect. Why bother trying to find a Protean user so you can learn Protean 1 and 2, or all that XP for the Burning Hand combo Discipline, when you can learn a ritual to get a magical agg dealing sword for no XP, while your XP goes to other things other than access to agg damage non-bite attacks?

                            None of this though, is a case for, "the Tremere and/or Thaumaturgy shouldn't exist," because all of that is fixable. The XP price for secondary paths is just a number (or there's the VtR method where all of it is ritual magic and there's no need for paths and players have to pick XP on the Discipline to get more potent at magic, or XP on rituals to do more things with it). VtM introduced other models for activation rolls so characters can't master casting significantly more easily than mastering the multiple activation rolls for many core Disciplines. Rituals can be given XP costs in pre-V5, or V5 can unblock amalgams so rituals don't feel cheesy.

                                The other way to solve the matter of Rituals is to keep them free, but decouple it from the Thaumaturgy Discipline itself. Letting anyone attempt to cast it, so long as they know how. Anyone could, with research and the proper materials, do magic. It's just that groups like the Tremere hoard knowledge so jealously.

Not to mention, without being tied to a linear progression you have to buy with XP, it's harder to prevent characters from just bum-rushing high level Rituals. (Other than the ST putting up barriers to access, of course).
