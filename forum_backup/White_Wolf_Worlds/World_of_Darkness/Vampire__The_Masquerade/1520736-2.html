                Originally posted by Manfr View Post
                This is obvious, but still,...
                No "but still." If this is obvious, than just citing the ICv2 surveys of retailers is obviously insufficient for the claims your making.

                if Wod5 enters the icv charts for almost a year by now, based on mostly reprints of manuals which were financed by crowdfunding some years ago, I'd say that their cut on profits in the last months is probably satisfactory
                The only books that were crowdfunded for V5 were the ones made by Onyx Path (traditional preorders are not crowdfunding, and are not transparent), and some of the localization studios. It's impossible for those to be holding up the line on their own.

                Lets do a quick gut check here. To be conservative, let's say Paradox compensates (that is, the total outlay of the company including compensations and benefits beyond salary) the equivalent of $50K annually to ten employees (there are more than that, but their salaries aren't published and the specifics aren't public, and there's been turnover, etc.). That's $500K, a year, for the last seven years. If Paradox nets $5 per sale, they need to sell 100K books a year, just to pay their WoD staff (oh, and they didn't actually have anything to sell for the first two years). Now, fortunately for Paradox, they decided to offload a lot of one-time costs associated with RPs onto their production partners. Paradox doesn't pay the freelance writers, artists, editors, and consultants, Renegade/whoever does (but that's also why Paradox makes less per book, because Renegade needs a bigger cut to stay profitable on their side of things).

                And, of course, the estimates I saw was that Paradox paid about $4 million for the buyout. At $5 per book, that's... 800K more book sales to break even. That would man V5 is selling books on par with D&D 5e, or they're still not breaking even. Since WoD5 has ranged from not place in the ICv2 top 5, to 4th place at best, it isn't out selling Pathfinder/Paizo, so it isn't doing D&D numbers, so it hasn't broken even yet. Yes, they're also trickling in small things like licensing fees from their various business partners, but Onyx Path's payments to keep making Exalted is chump change in these sorts of equations, or Rich couldn't afford to do it.

                And I haven' even tried to account for things like the development hell of Bloodlines 2 and how much money got poured into the marketing hype for that.

                Also, an interesting part of V5 sales are also international, as Paradox has a much wider set of localization agreements, and even though those are not direct profits, they are still widely distributed revenues, with no attached entrepreneurial risk
                If there was no risk, everyone would be doing it.

                Want to know why most RPGs don't bother with that? It costs too much. Remember that English is already the most widely spoken language on Earth. After English and Mandarin, the number of speakers (both native and secondary) of any language drops by a huge margin. For physical books, which are already a slim profit margin product, localization into more specific language markets is threading a very tough needle: You need to have extremely good estimates of what each language's market is for localization is over how many gamers gave up ages ago and speak enough English to run TTRPGs out of the standard English text; or at least have enough people in their group that can parse the technical rules parts of it. Otherwise you have no idea what kind of print runs to be aiming for, and localization companies aren't set up to do that kind of market research.

                So, you can put a lot of money into researching individual language markets, localization partners, and also still have to work on distribution of physical product..aka have risk.

                                    There is no magic number, because success is a factor of net profits, not gross sales. A book that costs $20K to make needs to sell less copies to turn a profit than a book that costs $40K to make.

                                    For a company, there's also a consistency of sales. One mega hit book that sells a lot of copies, but the company can't replicate in new releases, means the company can't sustain itself, while a company that never has any giant hits, but consistently sells OK over many books can keep going until something big happens externally.

                                                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                            They could create their own entirely separate World of Darkness continuity.
                                                            So could anyone else. That's the whole problem for Onyx Path if Paradox did something like make the WoD into an open license property. Onyx Path's profits for these products are built on a level of exclusivity. They aren't Paradox, but they get to make official books, that are part of the official continuity. If you take that away, Onyx Path could still sell books (because Onyx Path is still the most trusted company when it comes to doing the WoD games well), but suddenly Onyx Path has to compete with lots and lots of other publishers. It's the 3.0 OGL boom and bust all over, but Onyx Path has way less money in the bank to endure it and then try to be the WoD's Paizo once the dust settles.

                                                            The reason I bring up Exalted, is because that's the one place where Onyx Path really is the only company that can do it; even if they don't own the IP. Unless you want to piss off a lot of Exalted fans by cancelling 3e early and then starting a brand new edition (and the devs who's work you'd be cancelling are part of the small cadre of people that actually know how to make Exalted, and they're part of those fans you'd be pissing off). Unless there's some pretty significant changes in the current state of things, there's no Exalted without Onyx Path.

                                                            If you make the system open, and suddenly there's a dozen version of, "If I was in charge of Exalted Essence, here's what I would have done," flooding the market... you're just driving Onyx Path to stop paying Paradox license fees and focus even more on the various Storypath games. Onyx Path has had a lot of time to build up those properties and not be reliant on Paradox like they used to be completely at the whim of CCP.

                                                            Whatever your ideal situation here might be, the reality would by that Onyx Path as a company just moves on, while some Onyx Path freelancers try to use their name recognition in the fandom to put out some books of their own.
