    I think my issue with the Second Inquisition was...

    10-28-2025, 05:25 PM
    The recent thread I made on how the Second Inquisition could end had me thinking about what the intentions were for the organization versus the expectations of fans.

    I had a problem with THE SECOND INQUISITION book and why I realized that I'd been reading the writer's intentions all along. Basically, for a lot of fans, the Second Inquisition was this massive police state organization that would be able to track down and systematically eliminate vampires across the globe via anonymous military strikes during the day and burning the buildings down around them. It's a horrifying situation that means that vampire society has to cease to exist.

    It's not that.

    Instead, the intention seemed to be Blade-ifying the games more. Basically, heavily armed guys in a scary gas masks charging at the vampires in the middle of the night and getting into big fights where the vampires tear them apart with disciplines. They're basically a replacement for the Sabbat as disposable mooks and also primarily meant to be enemies in the video games.

    This is related to the Sabbat in that I believe the changes done to them aren't necessarily for the tabletop game but to establish them as disposable mooks for future video games as well as spin off properties. The SI and Sabbat existing less as characters to play and more for providing enemies in lore. It happened in Swansong as huge chunks of the game is killing SI soldiers like Selene or Bloodrayne. Now in Bloodlines 2, you get to slaughter whole swaths of Hunters and Sabbat.

    Maybe I'm right or maybe I'm wrong but the assumption is that the Hunters are there to provide the game orcs or goblins.

    It's not a bad thing, games need antagonists, but in the SI book you have less discussion of the politics behind the SI and more talk about light grenades.

    Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

          I think the two problems with this take are, first, that it assumes there is a clear and cohesive set of intentions behind the SI, and second that the author's intention supersedes what's actually written.

          This is a gameline, even the very first book was written by many hands, and with input from even more people. The intentions behind are many and sometimes messy, and they change over books.

          So, my take? This may be true, but first of all the idea of a harder survival-style game was definitely there in the beginning. It's overtly spelled out in the core book, that the SI makes things hard and vampires now have to be careful when using tech, or not use it at all.

          Did this change in other books? Yes, it did, but that's not because it wasn't the intention. It is because ideas change.

          More importantly, the power of the SI was actually written in the game. It's in their resources, their knowledge, their long list of successful missions, the places where they are on top. This got more complex later on, but never went away.

          You can downplay the SI in a chronicle. That's not by any means an issue. At any given time, at certain places, they may be little more than scary mooks to kill. But in the setting you can't simply say they're not dangerous without either changing the setting through new developments or retconning a lot of it. This is the setting as presented.

          What is the SI? It is a bunch of semi-competent idiots for you to kill in droves in a video game. But also, it is this massive police state organization that would be able to track down and systematically eliminate vampires across the globe via anonymous military strikes during the day and burning the buildings down around them. And most importantly, it is everything in between.

          But it's not less than this. It is a definitive change in the background danger level for vampire society however you see it, that's not going away.
