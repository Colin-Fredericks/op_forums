                                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                        It becomes an uncomfortable bit of cultural imperialism. Also, the idea of the vampires being the inspiration for religions that have historical or mythological significant for present-day individuals.

                                        * Huītzilōpōchtli (This one irritates me a lot because it's, &quot;Oh, it's a Baali!&quot
                                        This one didn’t bother me because;

                                        A) it wasn’t A Baali, it was THE Baali... the first one to appear in the World of Darkness and introduced us to the very concept of the Baali.

                                        B) Cultural Imperialism isn’t just a Western European thing. The Aztecs were also colonial oppressors who moved into and conquered the region in the 13th Century CE (only about 300 years ahead of the Spanish Conquistadors) and began sacrificing members of the conquered peoples to their gods in what was arguably a political terror campaign dressed up as religious fanaticism.

                                        Frankly, them being in the thrall to an actual 10,000 year old demonic blood god makes them look better than they actually were... in the real world they committed those atrocities of their free will.

                                        One thing that often gets scrubbed out of the story is that Cortés didn’t go to war with the Aztecs and beat them with just his 500 men; he negotiated alliances with local conquered peoples who sent along armies in the thousands to assist Cortés in overthrowing the Aztecs. The Spanish conquest wasn’t good, but at the time, Cortés was considered the lesser of two evils compared to the Aztecs.

                                        At the same time I do feel that there’s a bit of a double standard regarding religion in fantasy. If a Christian has an issue with a fantasy setting where, say, the Norse religion was objectively true, they’re expected to just accept it as part of the fantasy setting. If a non-Christian has an issue with a fantasy setting where Christianity is objectively true though, the expectation is that the Christian setting must be changed to not be problematic.

                                        VtM was specifically built on Judeo-Christian cosmology. WtA was built on an animist cosmology. MtA was built on a variation of Gnostic cosmology. CtD is based more on the pre-Christian myths of the British Isles. WtO is, if anything, an agnostic to even atheistic setting.

                                        DtF is also Judeo-Christian, but given that Dante’s Inferno and Paradise Lost are two of the most defining works of popular literature relating to demons and, more importantly to the WoD brand, one of the few cosmologies where demons are presented as unambiguous monsters (vs. say, the Greek conception of daemons as being a general category of supernatural being that might be helpful or harmful depending on the daemon).

                                        The point being; if you really believe that Christianity is just a myth no different than the Greco-Roman pantheon; then the use of it as the foundation for a fantasy setting should be no more problematic than one where the Greek gods are presumed to be real (ex. Perry Jackson; and therefore Christianity is objectively false in that setting).

                                        Especially when WW only used the Christian cosmology for one of the big three with the other two outright rejecting it as true. That paints the picture of Christianity in VtM being a conscious choice based on the primary source material (most notably the novel that kicked off the whole vampire in popular culture in the first place; “Dracula”) than some sort of deliberate attempt to exclude (if that were the case one would expect WtA and MtA to also have underlying Christian cosmologies).

                                        And just to be clear on my argument in relation to the Kindred and Laibon... whoever the first vampire was, they probably originated in Africa along with the earliest humans (probably 100-200k years ago) and so would be a common ancestor to both Kindred and Laibon. Further, since their true identity has been last to history, this first vampire may as well be called “Caine” when speaking of them in the English language in the same way that our most recent common mitochondrial ancestor is called “Eve.”

                                        One thing that helps me in this regard is that the Catholic Church doesn’t proclaim a literal interpretation of the earliest books of the Bible.

                                        What they teach IS true is God as Creator of the cosmos (he may have done so by setting up the conditions of the Big Bang so that our sun and planet would form and evolution would eventually produce mankind) and the relationship of God and Man (i.e. a loving creator contrary to the Babylonian religion during the time the Bible was written down where Man was spawned from the blood of demons and therefore only worthy to serve the gods and their priest-kings as slaves). All the rest can be allegories and “just so” stories that explain why certain things are so.

                                        Also worth noting is that, regardless of when it is actually written down, every ancient religion references events from much earlier periods that came down to them in the form of oral tradition. The nigh universal to all cultures story of a prehistoric Flood, for example, almost certainly descends from oral histories dating back to the end of the last glacial maximum and subsequent rapid rise of global sea level by 100+ meters in a very short span of time that occurred in stages between 14k and 8k years ago).

                                        Even if only got recorded in the Hebrew Torah during the Babylonian captivity c. 600-539 BCE, the oral histories recorded were at least 5500 years older in the case of the Flood, and the oldest recorded part of the Bible (the song sung after the parting of the Red Sea... which speaks only of the horses and chariots of the enemy being cast into the sea by God rather than of them being trapped there as the parting of the sea ended) was 1000 years old by the time the Torah was actually assembled.

                                        The point being, just because it only got written down about 2600 years ago, doesn’t mean the foundations of the stories recorded in them are only 2600 years old.

                                        So, in the case of Caine, all that really needs to be true is that there was a First Vampire (called Caine for convenience) who came into being contemporaneously with early humans (i.e. he was a descendant of “Adam & Eve” and “Caine” in turn sired other vampires who preyed on early human settlements.

                                        Maybe the story was backwards and Caine only murdered his brother after he became a vampire (a new vampire killing a loved one in their first hunger-fueled frenzy isn’t exactly rare), but regardless, Caine’s association with the murder of his loved one is at least an allegorical truth of the vampiric condition.

                                        Likewise, a Flood of some type almost certainly occurred for the reasons given above... but the really relevant part is what it signifies for the vampires. The Flood is the point when all contact with and involvement of “Caine” in human and vampire history ends.

                                        Whether this is because “Caine” deliberately turned his back on his childer -or- because Caine suffered torpor and the body was lost in the flood; buried under 8000+ years of sediment with no blood to restore it -or- because Caine met Final Death when the flood washed away any protection during daylight doesn’t matter.

                                        What matters is precisely that everything before the Flood doesn’t matter... as the oldest recorded histories begin it is the Clan founders who take the reins. Whether Caine’s existence happened exactly as recorded in the Book of Nod or as the Laibon say it did is an academic argument because he’s gone from the story until the last days (and maybe not even then).
