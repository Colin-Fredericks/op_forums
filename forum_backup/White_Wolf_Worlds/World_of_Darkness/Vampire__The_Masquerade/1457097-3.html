    The problem with the whole "boxes within boxes" thing is that it sounds great for a spooky conspiracy the audience will never have to be sold on as a functional organization once you peek under the flaps. It neglects that for exerting any serious amount of control like this there's a level of infrastructure and numbers that you can't effectively scale below. Israel is certainly an outlier because it punches way above its weight-class in terms of population to geopolitical importance. A small enough group of Technocrats to hide with all those layers of conspiracy in a country as small as Israel is also too few agents to actually handle all the major things that need dealt with (even more so with the canon status of the Middle East in the WoD). One of the reasons to commit to a larger presence that's too big to rely on the "we just nudge from the shadows" idea, is also that Israel is a great staging group for agents that primarily work in neighboring countries.

            The books generally present the situation as being very dependent on how the Technocrats view a given hunter group's methods.                         Which is, frankly, why I run my Vampire and Mage games in separate universes.

                        In my vampire game there are no “Mages” (in the MtA sense; linear sorcerers, true faith, demon worship and agencies using experimental tech exist, but there’s no war for reality in my Vampire campaign) or “Werewolves” (there are Lupines, savage flesh-eating monsters cursed by the bite of another Lupine who claim Lycaon as their founder in the same way Vampires claim Caine)

                        The “Technocracy” that destroyed the Ravnos Antediluvian in my Vampire game was basically the “Five Eyes” Intel community, the nukes were conventional and when that failed someone was actually willing to listen to the Project Twilight crew and try using a hastily repurposed solar mirror from an off the books Znamya-like solar power project.

                        It’s been two decades and the Intel community has basically forgotten it even happened... a lot of Cam elders burned a lot of capital making sure the entire thing is buried under so much red tape in so many independent intelligence silos and classification layers followed by virtually everyone involved in the incident being “persuaded” to take early retirements that only Project Twilight’s heirs even know there WAS an incident beyond the official cover story.

                        It makes my Vampire campaign run a LOT smoother when I don’t have to worry about Mage and Garou shenanigans invading it.

                        (Conversely, in my Mage campaign vampires are mostly solitary hunters and there are no sects or Antediluvians or similar elements that take the focus off the conflict between the Mage factions... the Etherites do however have a theory that parallel Earths where different supernatural paradigms dominate exist... which is how any crossover I might ever run would actually work).

                                                    The big problem with the Camarilla is that their entire concept went through several changes throughout the years, and the games pretend it wasn't the case. In the beginning it was a conspiracy theory about someone pulling the strings of Princes and Primogen all over the world, but no organization truly extended from one city to the next in any formal fashion. Then it became an open ideology, still with any formal structure being just a conspiracy theory. Then an association. Then Vampire UN, and only after a time as UN they became a formal government with a structured administrative body.

                                                    So the Camarilla was all this at some time and, weirdly, all of them are somehow canon simultaneously, because the books never admitted a retcon.
