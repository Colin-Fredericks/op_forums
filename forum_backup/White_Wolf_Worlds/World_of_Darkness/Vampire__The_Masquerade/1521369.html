                      Vampire: the Masquarede built on Judeo-Christian elements originally, maybe thats why
                      God, Angels, Damnation, Cain, Abel, Adam and Eve, the whole Book of Nod
                      But important to mention, even the book points that out, this is what the Vampires believes at the moment
                      In the Dark Ages and prior that many vampire belived they are the descendants of Ixion

                      (Ixion married Dia, a daughter of Deioneus (or Eioneus), and promised his father-in-law a valuable present. However, he did not pay the bride price, so Deioneus stole some of Ixion's horses in retaliation. Ixion concealed his resentment and invited his father-in-law to a feast at Larissa. When Deioneus arrived, Ixion pushed him into a bed of burning coals and wood.
                      Ixion went mad, defiled by his act; the neighboring princes were so offended by this act of treachery and violation of xenia that they refused to perform the rituals that would cleanse Ixion of his guilt. Thereafter, Ixion lived as an outlaw and was shunned. By killing his father-in-law, Ixion was reckoned the first man guilty of kin-slaying in Greek mythology.
                      This act alone would warrant Ixion a terrible punishment, but Zeus took pity on Ixion and brought him to Olympus and introduced him at the table of the gods. Instead of being grateful, Ixion grew lustful for Hera,
                      Zeus found out about his intentions and made a cloud in the shape of Hera, which became known as Nephele (from nephos "cloud") and tricked Ixion into coupling with it. From the union of Ixion and the false-Hera cloud came Imbros or Centauros, who mated with the Magnesian mares on Mount Pelion, engendering the race of Centaurs, who are called the Ixionidae from their descent.
                      Ixion was expelled from Olympus and blasted with a thunderbolt. Zeus ordered Hermes to bind Ixion to a winged fiery wheel that was always spinning. Therefore, Ixion was bound to a burning solar wheel for all eternity, at first spinning across the heavens, but in later myth transferred to Tartarus)

                      And before that...?

                      Important to note, Vampire when came out was different - very different - than it ended up in V20 (and later V5) but carried on the strong connection of America - Judeo-Christian America - and that effected the whole line

                      Vampires are also not specifically mentioned in the Bible, though it is possible to interpret certain Biblical passages as referring to vampires
                      The closest is the Alukah - is a feminine Hebrew word that means "horse-leech", a type of leech with many teeth that feeds on the throats of animals. According to some biblical scholars, alukah can mean "blood-lusting monster" or vampire (Ironically, t is closely associated with Lilith or thought to be her direct descendant)

                      Of course Dracula had a great influence on the Game (as liek the Anne Rice novels)
                      In his novel, Stoker never explicitly explained the process of how Dracula became a vampire. The Count is loosely based on Vlad III Dracula AKA Vlad the Impaler, the 15th-century warrior and Viviode (ruler) of Wallachia and Transylvania who was infamous for impaling thousands of his enemies on long, wooden spikes. Stoker describes his vampire's past life as a "soldier, statesman, and alchemist... He had a mighty brain, a learning beyond compare... there was no branch of knowledge of his time that he did not essay." Count Dracula is said to have studied alchemy and black magic at the academy of Scholomance in the Carpathian Mountains; the implication is that to stave off death and achieve immortality, the Count used his arcane knowledge to turn himself into a vampire. This adds to how Dracula is atypical among vampires since most vampires in popular culture are sired by another vampire but Stoker's creation seems to have been a self-made creature of the night, and one with special powers other vampires don't possess.
                      Francis Ford Coppola's 1992 adaptation Bram Stoker's Dracula took the Count's origin to operatic heights: incensed that his wife Elisabeta's soul was denied entry into Heaven because she committed suicide, the warrior-prince Dracula renounced God and declared war on the Almighty with "all the powers of darkness". So, like in Stoker's novel, Dracula used black magic to turn himself into a vampire but he did it out of 'eternal love' for his bride

                      When Demon came out it alligned well with Vampire and reinforced that Judeo-Christian angle
                      The Time of Judgement even has a scenario involving God returning to pass judgement upon the world. Some vampires are chosen by God to go to an old church where they must remain for 40 nights. During this time they have a chance at redemption, and if they do redeem themselves they leave the church to see the dawn devoid of the curse of undeath and vampirism.
                      Basically you can make Christianity and other Abrahamic faiths as important to the setting as you want.

                                  It's considered Judeo-Christian because the term Judeo-Christian has been forced into the vernacular by Christian supremacists that want to delegitimize Judaism as anything other that a precursor to Christianity. It enables cherry picking Jewish elements with no concern for actual Jewish beliefs or practices, while still telling things from a Christian perspective.

                                  VtM is Christian coded, because the original authors saw religion as a force in society that did more harm than good, so it was subject to the WoD's satire; and they were born and raised in Christian dominated culture so that was the religion to target. It's not a Jewish game, despite some words from Hebrew sprinkled in here and there.

                                      It's judeo christian because in addition to mythological elements from judaism, it possess many elements from christianity.

                                      Gehenna for exemple, despite it's jewish name, is clearly reminiscent of the apocalypse from the new testament books, even taking whole concepts from that, like caine ruling his kind for a millenia of darkness, in the same way that jesus is supposed to rules the world for a millenia after the apocalypse.

                                      In the same way, golconda is a concept far more christian than judaic, the idea of a being fundamentally sinful and damned finding redemption in the eyes of god is a pretty christian concept.
                                      On that saulot has a pretty christ like role.

                                      In the same way, the demons and infernalists presented across the game are all pretty much based on the christian view of such things, with the baali being pretty much the image of a satanic cult.
