    Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post
    Well, you say the WoD, but it seems like more games don't use Strength + Intimidation as an off the shelf option (Werewolf, Mage, Changeling) than do (Vampire and Wraith). Also, technically, the procedure for breaking shit is a Feat of Strength - a Willpower roll (difficulty 9) to push yourself unless you perform some feat less than or equal to your Strength (with an unhelpfully small number of examples!). Being practiced in threatening others doesn't inherently make you more likely to break things, so you can probably see where this roll can kind of break down in play.
    I'm not following why breaking stuff is being brought up. I just slammed my tail as part of the intimidation. Body language + whatever I was saying (e.g. "surrender to Set or die"). Though the point of involving the tail off-game was to justify using the higher Strength rating of the tail over that of the rest of the body (the tail's rating is 3 dots higher). But it was an extra anyway: as far as my understanding of intimidation rolls is concerned, direct threats of physical violence are handled through a Strength + Intimidation roll whether you make a display of strength or not. I think the intention of the writers was to use the Strength rating to represent how physically menacing one appears based on muscle size and the like. It is a questionable system, since some forms of Strength (like Potence) do not necessarily appear as big muscles and some factors are ignored (ex: what if you're threatening someone by holding a gun, or even a sword? Would it not make more sense to roll the damage rating of the weapon you're showing to the opponent, rather than Strength?), but I don't think that one has to always put up a show of strength in order to do the roll. I am open to refining the system to include more factors, though.

            There's nothing wrong with the concept of a Strength based Intimidation roll All the WoD games are meant for the majority of the "dramatic systems" to be examples of how to use the rules to their full extent, not lock of creative uses of unusual combinations.

The body language and words are in-line with each other. People might say mutton is great to avoid the fight, but you're not really challenging their believes because raw strength doesn't challenge such beliefs.

                    That feels... very ahistoric. The time-frame of the Dark Ages games did not include a lot of wars with forced conversions. It's been a long time since the violent Christian hegemony succeeded in securing most of Europe, and only the Moorish expanding into Iberia (and the Moors didn't force conversions as a common practice, and the Christian counter offensive that worked hasn't happened yet), and Ottoman wars in the east were the only major religious conflicts in the setting.

                    The real world Inquisitions were very focused on rooting out fake conversions (the large number of them after the success of the Reconquista was the origin of the Spanish Inquisition).

                    The idea that "surrender or die," and "convert or die" would be seen as interchangeable doesn't really mesh with normal life in the time period the game is set in.

                    As to the game itself, Strength + Intimidation still doesn't make sense for forced false conversions. Religions don't have five second conversions, even for forced ones. Forcing a fake conversion to a religion your target has never heard of makes even less sense. Just like "surrender to King Bob" doesn't work if nobody you're talking to has ever heard of any Kingdom ruled by Bob.

                    If your intent needs to present these sorts of contexts to make sense, you're moving into Manipulation territory, not Strength.

                    And more so, I would not allow an Aura bonus if you're not actually trying to convert. The bonus isn't for getting people to say "hail Set" if you know they don't mean it.

                                Garou are religious fanatics who routinely interact with aspects of their religion, routinely fight horrible monsters, and are filled with supernatural rage. It’s hard to imagine a thinking being that would be harder to intimidate into conversion.

                                I would rule that such intimidation would be impossible. You might be able to make them run away or even say something that they don’t believe, but actually changing their world views to such an extreme degree would be impossible without some sort of powerful use of Dominate or Presence.

                                It does make sense within some contexts for Strength to be used for intimidation. If a strongman (Str 5) threatens to beat up a skinny nerd (Str 1) it’s going to be intimidating regardless of that strongman’s social skills. Then again, the difference in combat prowess between the strongman and nerd is apparent. If the nerd was carrying a gun, suddenly that Strength score doesn’t mean as much. All werewolves carry guns in this scenario from their various supernatural powers. Also, unlike the strong man, a super strong vampire isn’t obviously strong unless it displays that strength in some way. An Antediluvian with 10 Strength and 10 Potence may be stronger than a giant Wyrm beast, but that doesn’t really come across unless it displays that strength spectacularly.
