                Clanbook: Assamite (part three)

                And again.

                The Angel of Death
                Our second female character, and again, the write up seems to be somewhat gender neutral. (There are more than a few ballet references, but that doesn't really mean much, especially if you interpret the character as being Russian or Eastern European.) Anyway, this is a poor individual from the bad part of town who managed to work their way in with a gang, eventually moving pretty high up as an elite killer. And then got recruited by the Assamites. They view killing as some sort of pleasure high that they are really good at. Really, they feel like the main henchmen of the villain from a certain class of action crime-drama films. Mental is primary, Physical secondary, with Dexterity at four dots. Firearms and Melee are at three dots each, with Athletics, Dodge, Intimidation, Stealth, and Investigation at two. Disciplines are the same set up as all the others, with the extra dot in Quietus. I think having it in Celerity might've fit the concept better. Backgrounds are two dots in Generation and three in Resources. You'd think this character might have some Contacts or something. Maybe even a Retainer (a background that no character in this book has). Path is six, Willpower is eight. Overall, this isn't a bad character. I think the traits could stand some adjustment. Maybe make Physicals primary and Socials secondary. Buff up a few of the social/cultural abilities the write up mentions the character as having a strong interest in. As is, it's kind of one of those middle of the road things that doesn't stand out as being particularly good or bad, just there.

                The Avenger
                Kind of a weird one. From the title and character image (a guy with a maniacal look on his face firing off twin Uzis), you'd think this was going to be The Punisher turned into a vampire, but no. He used to be a soldier, but where, when and for whom is left very vague, but from context, I'm going to guess probably from one of those countries that decided Israel was an affront to the honor of Islam and tried to wipe it out as soon as it came into existence in 1948. Anyway, he got recruited into the Assamites and apparently became fanatically fixated on the clan, especially on all the affronts to their honor from the Camarilla, especially the Tremere curse. And now he does the usual Assamite thing, but laughs maniacally about how members of the Camarilla pay him to avenge his clan upon them. Somewhat to my surprise, he's Mental primary and Physical secondary. Abilitywise, he's mostly a KillNinja. Alertness, Firearms, Melee and Stealth are three each, with Brawl, Dodge, Survival and Investigation at two each. He has five dots in Disciplines, with Celerity and Quietus at two each. (Of all the characters, I would've expected this to be one to go all in on the clan's Special Sauce power.) Backgrounds are four dots in Generation and one in Status. I presume that's Clan Status. Weirdly, for a guy so balls to the wall gung ho for the clan's ideology, his Path rating is only five, making him tied with the Holy Warrior as lowest. Willpower is six. As I said, this is a weird one. I can understand the idea of having a character totally devoted to avenging the clan's past losses, but this feels more like a motivation for a character rather than a complete character. Almost as if you had a Brujah totally obsessed with getting back at the Ventrue over Carthage, but was otherwise your bog standard cookie cutter street punk who does street punk things and little else. He's also a bit hampered by having all of his freebies spent on extra Discipline dots. So overall, I think I have to put this one pretty low on the list.

                The Gamester
                I really love this character, and it's probably my favorite concept in the book. It takes the clan's stereotype and turns it on its head and inside out, into someone who approaches all of existence, including assassinations, as a series of puzzles to solve as part of an endless quest for mental stimulation. As a child, this involved various games and other pursuits before eventually discovering burglary and security systems. Eventually, they broke into a mansion that turned out to be owned by a vampire. This lead to being recruited by the Assamites, doing what they've always done best. The write up describes the character as a nerd type, and the artwork is youngish looking 90s geek girl (making our third female, and again with a gender neutral write up). And this is from before geek culture pretty much beat that concept to death. Mental is primary, naturally, with Wits at four. I'm a little surprised no freebies were spent to boost them even more. Investigation is maxed out to five dots, and Security at four. Stealth and Alertness are three each. Repair, Survival, and Computer are twos. I'm not sure Survival fits the character that much, and the other two feel a little low. Also, there are ones in Firearms and Melee, which more or less make sense, but none in Brawl even though it mentions having studied Aikido at some point. No Streetwise and Subterfuge at only one. And there's two dots in Game Playing, which I think could've been spent better elsewhere, even if might suit the character (in which case it should be higher, I think). And not all of the ability points have been spent. We have five in Talent, thirteen in Skill and only eight in Knowledge (two of those taking Investigation over three in violation of char gen rules). Plus two in Game Playing, which IIRC is a Skill. Disciplines are, again, the same deal as all the others, with the extra dot in Obfuscate. And again, I think those points could've been better spent elsewhere. Backgrounds are two in Generation, two in Resources, and one in Status. Path is at seven, Willpower at six. Like I said, I really enjoy this character a lot. I just wish the traits were done a lot better.

                The Scholar
                So, this book introduced the Scholar and Sorcerer castes to the clan, but pretty much as foot notes, with both focused almost entirely on trying to break the clan curse. So this guy is a specialist in hematology. He was recruited to try to help find a cure for the clan's curse, and eventually embraced. And now he works in a lab at an Assamite strong hold doing research all night. And that's pretty much it. Mental is primary, with two extra dots to raise Intelligence to five. Wits is four and Perception three. I really think Wits could've been left at three to save a few points. Medicine and the secondary ability of Research are maxed out at five. Alertness is at three. I've no idea why. Maybe to make sure he knows when his lab machines' timers go off. Science is only at two, and then he has the totally redundant abilities of Biology and Chemistry on top of that. Seriously, just spend the points to raise his Science to four or five. He also has Kindred Lore two, which makes a certain level of sense, and Firearms at two, which doesn't. And again, some of his ability points are missing. He only has one dot in each clan discipline, making him the only character to not have spent freebies on it. He has two dots in Contacts, and one each in Generation, Resources, and Status. Path rating is eight, making him the most hardcore devotee in the book, and Willpower is five. Quite honestly, this is the worst character in the book. His entire existence is based around a single activity in a single location and he never leaves to do anything else. I could see maybe a handful of ways to get him out into the world, but most of them honestly feel like the sort of convoluted situations in a bad action movie plot. But as he is, this is kind of a waste of space.

                And so, that's the 2nd edition Assamites. Weirdly, I ended up liking these more than I thought I would. But even so, with the exception of the Playboy and the Scholar, these are basically eight variations on a theme. Effectively, eight different ways to do "I kill people" as a character concept. One of the reason's Clayton Oliver is my hero is that he was able to take such a one note clan and, like some sort of alchemist, turn them into a fleshed out and diverse bunch that ended up being one of my favorite revised era clans. I don't think its an accident that my favorites out of this book - the Playboy, the Sportsman, and the Gamester - all feel like they'd fit in perfectly with the revised version of the clan. (And per CTPhipps even the Psycho Killer feels like he fits the Web of Knives OK, though I still think he'd make a better Sabbat.) But even so, most of these aren't terrible. If anything, they are just a little bit boring. Save the Scholar, who is almost useless as anything other than an NPC or MacGuffin.
                As far as representation goes, while three of the character images are female, all three write ups are gender neutral, so it could just be after the fact or totally accidental. It's a little hard to judge ethnicity. The Gamester, Sportsman and Scholar are all clearly white. The Holy Warrior image is clearly Arabic. The Playboy looks like he might be Latin American, as might the Angel of Death. The Professional might could be interpreted as black.
                I'm a little surprised that there was no Sorcerer, but given how the Scholar turned out that might be for the best. Also, as I long wondered, for a clan of contract killers, who handles their contract negotiations? Or their armories or other logistics? Surely that's not entirely left up to ghouls. (Especially seeing as none of these templates have the Retainers background.) As far as I could tell, all the ghouls are being trained as future contract killers. Some sort of Contract Lawyer or International Arms Dealer would've been a nice addition.
                As far as the second edition went, the Assamites were pretty much just predators. All of these characters are that, to some degree or another.

                Next, the Setites, who are evil and like-a da snakes.

                    One problem I have with both the Assamites and Setite clanbooks (and to a much lesser degree, the Tremere) is the idea that the embrace comes first, and then the clan indoctrination. In my own chronicles, those two clans ONLY recruit from mortals who have already been indoctrinated in some kind of cult or secret society controlled by the clan as a front. They work much harder to ensure future members are loyal and have already bought into much of the clan's culture. The character templates in these books don't reflect that at all. I think that was a major mistake and handicapped both clans as the game developed.

                    The Tremere aren't as cult oriented, but they still see themselves as essentially a hermetic organization. They've had to make certain comprises as a vampire lineage seeking power within the greater vampire "nation" in general and the Camarilla in specific, but they still need people who can work within a very structured hierarchy and can think hermetically in order to develop their Thaumaturgy. So they too prefer front organizations (although these are much more broad than the fronts the other two clans employ) to recruit future potential fledglings, although they are open to exceptions - especially in terms of people who already seen to have occult powers.

                    I've believed this since very early on, so after almost thirty years I'm fairly set on this.

                    Looking forward to your comments on the Setites since I LOVE Howardiana and always enjoyed early editions' embrace of it (even if I don't like how certain things developed). Given your profile pic and other comments, I think you do too.

                        The really weird thing about the 2nd ed Assamites - what became the Web of Knives - is that if you train someone as a human for seven years, then another seven as a ghoul, and then yet another seven as a vampire neonate before granting them the equivalent to the Camarilla's right of release, the end result is a level of near ludicrous over training that far outstrips what Vampire's basic character creation was set at. Probably even above what even the Shih rules from Demon Hunter X were going for. It's roughly the equivalent of elite special forces operators who are nearing retirement age. If I really wanted to play that kind of character, there's probably better games out there to do it with (Dark Champions springs to mind).

                        The Tremere pyramid as presented in their clanbook has a similar problem. While it sounds good on paper, when the Camarilla LARP organization tried to actually implement it in reality, it didn't work. Much like the Web of Knives, I think it works better as a smaller organization within the clan who are heavily fixated on a certain facet of clan identity. (It's part of the reason I prefer to refer to the Setites as that instead of the Followers of Set; as the former sounds like a lineage while the latter sounds like a specific cult. I want my Setite characters - and my Assamites and Tremere and others - to be able to do their own thing under the umbrella of the clan's overall theme instead of having their activities and goals dictated to them from on high.)

                        My own impression has been that maybe a third of embraces, even among Camarilla clans like the Brujah, Gangrel or Malkavians, are impulse ones (and a lot of those may end up being destroyed depending on circumstances). But the majority are done with some amount of care and foresight, including a certain degree of "grooming" and various tests before said Childe ever even encounters their sire in person. (I actually really liked the way the revised Followers of Set book laid out how potential embraces will have their lives slowly and methodically destroyed as part of a way of tearing down their preconceived ways of viewing the world and making them much more accepting of the Setite world view. I suspect that this happens to certain degrees among a lot of clans.)

                            Clanbook: Setites (introduction)

                            It's now 1995. This book lists two developers, Andrew Greenberg, whose departure I mentioned when talking about DSotBH, and Jennifer Hartshorn, who had, IIRC, been Wraith developer before being moved to take Greenberg's place. The book is written by Richard Watts, whose name I'm unfamiliar with from much else. Interior art is by Anthony Hightower, whose work has this sort of "if watercolors were in black and white" washed out feel to them. (It's not a bad look, but for some reason it strikes me as a style that would work better for Wraith than most other lines.) Joshua Timbrook is also listed, but it's just an opening page and the tiny bit on the back cover that I suspect were done long before.
                            So, all of the Independent clans, as originally conceived, were ... I think "problematic" is the current in vogue term. I've long believed that, in spite of being in the Players Guide, they were all meant to be NPC antagonist factions rather than actual PCs. They were all essentially 1930s pulp fiction villain groups made into vampire clans. In the case of the Setites, it's the creepy Egyptian cultists, popular in a lot of old mummy movies, among other places, and seen in the proto-Egyptian kingdom of Stygia in Robert E. Howard's Conan stories and their later pastiches. And one of those pastiches is James Earl Jones as Thulsa Doom in the 1982 Conan the Barbarian movie. However, the character Jones plays is actually much closer to the character of Thoth-Amon, the greatest of all ancient Stygian sorcerers and worshiper of "Father Set", who some have suggested may be one of Lovecraft's Great Old Ones. The character never meets Conan in any of Howard's works, instead being his enemy in a number of pastiche stories by later writers, especially in the Marvel Comics series from the 70s and 80s. This is probably where a lot of the early Setite influences come from.
                            (In actual Egyptian mythology, Set was moderately heroic, or at least kind of an antihero, being the super badass warrior of the desert who defended Ra's sail barge from the chaos serpent Apep every night. He was the "red god" of the desert sands in contrast to his brother Osiris, the "black god" of the fertile river lands. Weirdly, a bunch of vampires who idolize Set sounds like it should be a gang of Gangrel who dig on being barbarians of the wastelands and don't have any time to waste with your wimpy civilization.)
                            Added to the above, Werewolf had come along and introduced the whole Wyrm thing, and I guess someone decided that was now what the Setites were into, now casting Set as a worshiper of Apep and their entire bag being corruption. Why do they corrupt people and institutions and the like? For ... reasons. The clanbook doesn't really give much of a justification, other than an in character account of how totally awesome Set was in both life and after the embrace but none of the other 3rd generation were able to see how cool he was and made fun of him, and one day while he was out at Hot Topic, they killed the 2nd generation, including Set's sire who he totes adored, so he decided to start the Jyhad and use the other Antediluvians' corruption against them. (I'm kind of noticing how all the Independent clans have this "our Founder was an awesome hero who never did anything wrong so we're the bestest clan" thing going on. I think this may be part of why the Giovanni's "no, we really are total assholes and go f--- yourself" take ended up being so refreshing.) So, it's sort of that they're into corruption for corruption's sake, I guess.

                            Also, each of these templates has a little Egyptian hieroglyph type image with it, I think meant to represent certain Egyptian gods. I can only really recognize a few of them, and I think they start to repeat at the end, so I've no idea if they were meant to carry any deeper meaning or not.

                                Clanbook: Setites (part one)

                                Our snake worshipers. BTW, the character sheets give an option for Humanity or Path, but none of them specify which Path. In second edition, the options were Typhon (from the 2nd ed Players Guide), Ecstasy, and Warrior (both in this book). I'll try to guess which one.

                                The Bent Narc
                                A corrupt cop. Basically, he wanted to become a cop so no one would ever push him around. Instead, he gets to push everyone else around. And because he was perfectly fine with violating the law six ways from Sunday to make arrests, he got promoted to Narcotics, where he could abuse his authority even more. And eventually got embraced. Now he serves the clan by getting rid of criminal rivals and protecting their own drug importations. He doesn't really care about anything concerning the clan's deeper goals or philosophies, just as long as he gets to push people around and feed off of crackheads. He's Physical primary and Social secondary. His highest abilities are three dots each in Melee, Investigation, and Criminology. That last one feels a little redundant. I'm also not sure why his Melee is so high. All his other abilities are twos, including Brawl, Dodge, Firearms, Streetwise, Subterfuge, Survival, Drive, Security and Law. He's got ones in Stealth and Setite Lore. Given how little he seems to care about the clan and its goals, I'm not sure why he has that last one. Disciplines are one dot each in the clan powers. Backgrounds are two each in Allies and Influence, and one in Resources. You'd think drug money payoffs would rate higher. Given how he seems to like to feed off of crackheads, I'm sort of surprised he doesn't have any thing in Herd. I also feel like he might benefit from Contacts or maybe a Retainer/slave of some kind. His Path rating is five. Given the drug thing, and because it's mentioned under that Path, I'm guessing the Path of Ecstasy. Willpower is six. This isn't exactly a terrible character. The overall concept is fairly good, and the stats, while needing a little massaging, aren't that bad. I think I'd just prefer something a little closer to Denzel Washington in Training Day of Michael Chiklis in The Shield. The sort of character who is more proactive and goal oriented. As is, he feels a little more like a ghoul than a vampire.

                                The Closet Setite
                                This is basically a Caitiff - this is back in the day when that term confusingly/annoyingly did double duty for both the real clan-less and those who had just been embraced and abandoned - who figured out she was a Setite when she developed the first dot of Serpentis. And now she doesn't know what to do, because everyone had told her that Setites are bad. And that's pretty much it. I'm not kidding. All we get of a backstory is that her living days were "uneventful, ordinary, even boring." No, what's boring is this barely-an-idea of a character. There's just nothing here. And her character sheet is ... odd. Mental is primary and Social secondary, with an extra dot to bump Charisma up to four. Acting and Subterfuge are four dots each, and Empathy, Etiquette, Survival, and Occult are three each. Was she a Wiccan actress/con artist who moonlighted as a wilderness guide? She has one dot in each discipline. Backgrounds are three in Generation and two in Resources (Set only knows from what). Humanity is eight and Willpower five. This is just terrible. This isn't even a character, it's an idea for an aspect of a character. The character's abilities have no context, and honestly feel like someone just tried to max the ones that would involve pretending not to be a Setite. And I think what irritates me the most is that, when I say it's an idea for an aspect of a character, it's actually a pretty good angle. There just needs to be more to it than that. Maybe she was an ancient history/languages major, or a psychology student, or someone who's always made her way through school by cleverly cheating without getting caught, or the secret descendent of some cult priestess. Anything that would give me something to work with here. (Even better might've been a perfectly happy Setite who is just pretending not to be one for the purpose of shenanigans.)

                                The Corrupt Politician
                                If the Bent Narc was Corruption Archetype #1, then this is #2. The standard glib oily type straight out of central casting, worked his way up from local politics. And now he's a United States Senator. Present tense. As in he reached that office and then was embraced. I'm fairly certain that one of the top five ways to get your name on the Red List. And for said Senator to have a tragic plane crash. Anyway, whatever you imagine corrupt politicians doing, he does it. And much like the Bent Narc, he doesn't really care much about the clan's overall ideals and whatnot as long as he keeps getting to "live" the high life. Socials are primary, with Charisma and Manipulation at four each. Politics is at four also. Law, Bureaucracy, Finance, Etiquette, Leadership, and Subterfuge are threes. Disciplines are, again, one each. Backgrounds include Influence and Resources at four, and Fame, Allies and Contacts at two each. In spite of his equipment listing his staff (all of the ghouls), he has no Retainers. And I'd probably say his Resources and Influence are too low. Path is six. I'm again guessing it's the Path of Ecstasy. Willpower is four. I think if this had been a local politician, it might've been OK. A little cookie cutter, maybe, but still OK. But as a US Senator, no. How the hell do you work this character into a coterie? Or even a chronicle outside of the most specific of DC by Night ones? Sometimes the "cute" idea isn't the best choice. As I said, if you just ignore most of the write up and make him a local big wig, it's OK.

                                And I think that's about all I can take for now. Next will be part two.

                                    Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
                                    Added to the above, Werewolf had come along and introduced the whole Wyrm thing, and I guess someone decided that was now what the Setites were into, now casting Set as a worshiper of Apep and their entire bag being corruption.
                                    I don't think it was case that "someone decided that was now..." The Setites were always established as worshiping a "dark god." They were published in the first edition Player's Guide in 1991 (as was the first edition Werewolf corebook) as you noted earlier. In 1992, the first edition Mummy supplement was published that explained in further detail the (narratively unreliable) background of Egypt in the World of Darkness including the Setites, and we learn there that the Setites "serve Set as well as one of the guises of the Great Wyrm."

                                    I don't think this is the case of someone applying some retroactive continuity. I think a lot of things had been worked out in advance, and early White Wolf was slowly dribbling this information out. I think when the clan was created in 1991, they were intended to be servants of the Wyrm. Both Werewolf and Mummy, which utilize the Wyrm, must have also been in the planning stages while the Player's Guide was being written as well. I'm pretty sure when the World of Darkness was conceived as five different games, some thought had been given on the generic setting and how certain elements were going to play out in all of them before Vampire was even published. So this is not a new thing. It's been there from the beginning.

                                    Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
                                    Why do they corrupt people and institutions and the like? For ... reasons. The clanbook doesn't really give much of a justification...
                                    This was a problem in early World of Darkness. PENTEX and many other Werewolf antagonists associated with the Wyrm were equally ludicrous in their motivations.

                                    It's a problem with a lot of media in general in depicting evil. For example, the temptation of Luke Skywalker in Return of the Jedi never made much sense either. It's just a vague notion of the culture that if someone does something evil, then they go all out and become completely evil in everything. It's the same reason why in many movies when they show the antagonist, it's insufficient to simply make him a rival or personal enemy to the protagonist, they have to show him kicking puppies, using racist language, cheating at cards, and other offensive actions. It is lazy, but easy shorthand that "this is the bad guy, don't root for him. boo! hiss! root for our hero instead, he loves puppies and would never kick one."

                                    I think a major reason for this is the desire to not glamorize or excuse evil. Many people feel that if you try to explain why someone did something morally wrong that you are justifying it. So things are often dumbed down for the target audience.

                                    The Wyrm wants to corrupt everything because that is how you destroy things. His followers do that because they gain power by favor of the Wyrm (in theory) if they do what it wants. In practice, the Setites need to have some justification that what they are doing is great because in reality most people need some level of justification for their actions for their own minds, as well as something to feed to outsiders. You'll always have sociopaths and thugs willing to do anything just because, but long term people need some kind of ideological justification. This takes more verbal gymnastics, but it's not particularly hard. Self-serving rhetoric has a very long history. You still have people today spouting Communist nonsense because they're unable to look past the propaganda line to what they actually did. (In Vampire this becomes even more important because the benefit of any Path is that it allows you to control the Beast so that you don't descend into an animal mindset).

                                    Eventually the game would begin to envision the various servants of the Wyrm having more reasonable motivations and more effective propaganda. But things were very crude early on.

                                    Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
                                    This is probably where a lot of the early Setite influences come from.
                                    It is very obvious that Robert E. Howard's work was an inspiration for certain parts of the World of Darkness. Besides the Set motif, the corruption of the Picts and the transformation of the White Howlers into the Black Spiral Dancers is obviously inspired by Howard's Bran Mak Morn stories. Even in Vampire, the hunter Sullivan Dane sounds very similar to Howard's Puritan witch hunter Solomon Kane. Somebody really liked their Howard. Don't know who, but I wouldn't be surprised if it was Mark Rein Hagen.

                                    Like you, I imagine the four independent clans as being more appropriate as NPC antagonists. That doesn't mean it isn't possible to run a game with PCs as characters of those clans. It's just that they are going to be played very differently than the "intended" clans.

                                    As for the contrast between Vampire Set and Mythological Set, the game did need to do a better job. As you stated, for a long time Set was an honored god among the Egyptians. And he performed various mythological acts that were heroic (like defend Ra from Apep when the solar barque entered the Underworld). The god didn't start being demoted in Egypt until after the Hyksos Invasion when foreigners ruled Egypt, and since Set was considered to be the god of foreigners, he became discredited. But that kind of knowledge isn't popularly known, and certainly wouldn't be expected of the game's main fanbase. But if one does know it, you have to start making up head canon as to why. And head canon can go into many different directions. Besides my own version, I've heard several other great explanations. But the problem is that we're all exclusionary to each other, so we start having very different conceptions of the clan and the kind of vampire Set is. The key is to remember the clan isn't about the actual Egyptian god named Set who isn't tied to snakes, it's about Howard's Set which is envisioned as a huge serpent. If one goes the Egyptian route (as opposed to it simply being a veneer), you're going to get the clan and "Set" wrong.

                                    The challenge for the first clan book was how well they could blend the various elements together. They needed to both be true to the Howardiana inspiration, show them clearly as a Wyrm cult, yet show how their propaganda both inside and outside the clan tries to conceal that. If I remember correctly, it fails at that though there are some useful things to be found.

                                    Presenting things from an IC point of view has its advantages. But I thought too many of the World of Darkness sourcebooks did that. I think it's better to have a combination of IC perspectives and OOC/Game perspective in order to make certain things clear to the reader. This is less important for the PC side of things than it is for the ST side. The problem with the clanbooks is that they are used by BOTH PCs and STs.

                                                    Clanbook: Setites (part two)

                                                    More snakes. (Why did it have to be snakes?)

                                                    The Dilettante
                                                    A big time celebrity sex symbol. I'm pretty sure this was inspired by Madonna as she was in the late 80s and early 90s, save that this is an actress who tried to break into music and bombed badly rather than the other way around. Anyway, she had the full hedonistic lifestyle, with drugs, assorted boy toys, jet setting, etc. A Setite apparently selected her for the embrace with plans to use her as the focus for a cross branded promotion of film, music, and related merchandizing in order to spread corruption and evil. What kind of corruption? The corrupting kind, apparently. Yes, it's very vague in the way that the entire book is, with corruption for corruption's sake. (I do kind of get the general idea, and in hindsight, I find it interesting that it predicts the '00's issues of media culture selling young girls on the idea of being hyper sexualized.) Socials are primary, with Charisma and Appearance at four each. Mental is secondary, with Intelligence and Wits at three. Abilities are three each in Etiquette and Music, and two each in Finance and Acting. And that's it. OK, not really. But all the rest are Secondary abilities. Carousing, Poetic Expression, Seduction, Singing, Style, and Setite Knowledge, all at three or higher except Poetic Expression. While I at least applaud going all in on the secondary abilities one picks, I think this is a bit much. I'm not really sure why she has Poetic Expression. In this case, I'm pretty sure it's meant for songwriting, and this is the sort of singer who would hire people for that. I'm not sure why her Setite Knowledge is at three dots either. She's far more into hedonism than anything about the clan's inner workings, and I'll point out that a later template is a total hardcore priest of Set who's rating in the ability is also three dots. I honestly think that you could drop the Carousing, Poetic Expression, and most if not all of the SK and use those dots for things like Intimidation, Subterfuge, or raising Acting. (You could probably drop Singing as well and raise Music to four and take Singing as a specialty.) Disciplines are three dots in Presence and one dot in Serpentis. I don't think she really needs Serpentis. But every single one of these templates has at least one dot in it, because apparently much like the Assamites, if the clan has a Special Sauce, everyone has to have it, just because. Backgrounds include two in Contacts, four in Fame, and five in Resources. This is one of the few times I've seen a five in any background, but it makes sense with this character. Given how the write up implies her current activities are part of some master plan on the part of her sire, I'm surprised she doesn't have Mentor. Or a Herd of groupies and entourage of Retainers. (Those seven dots that went toward that extra discipline dot could've covered all of that.) Path is six, and I'm guessing it's pretty obviously supposed to be the Path of Ecstasy. Willpower is four. In spite of its numerous trait problems and points that could've been better spent, I actually like the character concept quite a bit. While the generic corruption for corruption's sake angle is a bit weak, the over all idea is very sound, and even works really well with the revised presentation of the clan. So over all, I have to call this a win.

                                                    The Egyptologist
                                                    So, I guess it would be inevitable that a clan so heavily tied to Egypt would have a template based on the scholarly study of that land's ancient history. This man was a devoted scholar and archaeologist, who apparently uncovered a lost tomb and was embraced by the Setite who was slumbering within. I confess to being somewhat amused by how much that feels like a classic mummy movie. (However, I should point out that this character is 13th generation. You'd sort of expect a concept and origin like that to result in at least a few dots in the Generation background.) He mainly serves the clan via helping move entorpored elders around in their sarcophagi by listing them as antiques. Meanwhile, he's really only interested in the clan and its lore in so far as what it can teach him about ancient Egypt, a subject he's fairly obsessed with. He's Mental primary and Physical secondary, with Stamina at four. His Manipulation is three, leaving his Charisma at one, which seems a little off. This is another character with a bunch of secondary abilities, and again going all in on them. Archaeology, Research, Search, and Setite Knowledge at three, and History at four dots. Linguistics and Survival are also three dots each, with Bureaucracy, Investigation, and Drive at two. I think this is another instance where you could trim some of the secondary ones, maybe by dropping Research and raising Investigation to four dots with the Research specialty, and dropping Search for a few dots in Alertness. Disciplines are one dot in Obfuscate and two in Serpentis. Backgrounds are three dots in Mentor, two each in Status and Allies, and one dot in Fame. As I mentioned, I think this concept could've benefited from several dots in Generation, because I'm not really sure how many 12th generation vampires were roaming around 1700 BC, but I somehow suspect those that were around were frequently referred to as "snacks". Path rating is five, and I think this is probably our first Path of Typhon (what was the "main" Setite path back in 2nd ed). Willpower is also five. I understand why this character exists. I'd probably have been shocked if something like it hadn't been included. But as he is, he sort of feels more like the sort of character that should be a ghoul rather than a vampire. An Egyptology fanatic whose thirst for knowledge is being used to take advantage of his ability to move sleeping elders and other ancient treasures around the modern world. Maybe a more interesting take would be this character with the added angle that he's now bound and determined to "correct" the errors and heresies of the historical record and return the Setites and Set to their proper glory in the academic arena.

                                                    The Exotic Dancer
                                                    And, again, I think this might've been inevitable. Curiously, it actually predates the film From Dusk Til Dawn by a year. So this is something along the lines of the Poor Little Rich Girl archetype, or at least probably Poor Little Upper Middle Class in this case. Apparently never wanting for anything, except for parental love and/or attention. So she started acting out at a young age, breaking things, hurting pets and other children, and even herself (I pick up a bit of a sociopath vibe here). As a teen, this turned to heavy drinking, drugs, casual sex, and the like, until she left home and became a prostitute. And then got embraced, apparently by a Setite who'd been following her long decline. Now she's a stripper, and not the classy kind at "gentlemen's clubs" either. More like sleazy dives, where she likes to seduce and "corrupt" (there's that word again) and eventually hurt the desperate and needy. She has a ghoul pet snake as part of her act, because of course she does. Her Nature is actually Masochist, so apparently she's still inherently self destructive. Social is primary, with Manipulation and Appearance both at 4. Physical is secondary, with Dexterity also at four at the expense of Strength being one. She's another with a bunch of secondary abilities. Athletics, Streetwise, and Stealth are all three dots, with Brawl, Dodge and Melee at twos. Dancing is at four dots. Given that Athletics are already so high, I don't know why you wouldn't just get the additional dot and take Dancing as a specialty. Seduction is three. Also at three is City Secrets. I never liked that ability, as it always felt like something that should be specific things a player should write down on their character sheet rather than an ability. She also has one dot in Camarilla Lore. I'm not sure why. Nothing about the write up says anything about her being a member or affiliated with them. (If anything, she feels like she'd fit in with a gang of Anarchs.) Disciplines are back to the one each plus one formula, this time with the extra dot in Presence. Backgrounds are three dots of Herd, and one each of Contacts and Fame. Path is five, and I'm guessing we're back to the Path of Ecstasy. Willpower is a whopping eight. I'm not sure how I feel about this character. On the one hand, it feels a little cheesy, in that sort of "look how edgy and mature we are" way I find a little cringe inducing (mainly I suspect because I remember how I used to indulge in it way back when). On the other hand, I do find the character's psychology and backstory somewhat interesting (if more than a little depressing), especially in light of what the revised version of this book had to say about how the clan will deliberately try to tear someone down prior to the embrace. It makes me wonder if this was some long term project the sire started when she was just a child, which is kind of disturbing. I think with some fine tuning, this could probably be a fairly strong character.

                                                    The Gangsta
                                                    Sweet Christmas, where to start .... It's your stereotypical '90s black ghetto street thug. And that's it. I almost don't want to do this one. His quote is "Mutha-fucker!" He was a drug dealer. He tried to rip off a shipment that was owned by the Setites and got embraced for his efforts. (I guess it was a set up of some sort.) Now he's ... a drug dealer. It says he has contacts in DC, the CIA and the Mafia. He has no Contacts background. He used to do steroids and work out a lot. Roleplaying hints include "quote Ice T and Body Count at every available opportunity." I'm going to take a wild guess here that Richard Watts is white and in 1995 knew little or nothing about gangsta rap or hip hop. I'm a little surprised the character's equipment includes an UZI and not a TEC-9 or AK-47. Also that it doesn't include some crack, a 40 of malt liquor, and some "ho's". Nature and Demeanor are both Bravo, so he's exactly what he appears to be. Physicals are primary, with Strength maxed out to five dots. Socials are secondary, with Charisma and Manipulation both at two and Appearance at four. I guess he's the world's best looking crack dealer. Intelligence is one. I'm really not even sure what to say about that. Abilities include four dots in Streetwise, and another four in Area Knowledge, which feels redundant. He's got three dots each in Brawl, Intimidation, Firearms, and Melee. And two each in Alertness and Leadership. Disciplines are the one each plus one, this time in Presence. Backgrounds are three in Herd and two in Resources. I mentioned the Contacts thing already, but his equipment does list ghoul bodyguards, and he has no Retainers. No Allies that might be his gang either. Path rating is five, and I'm guessing he's supposed to be the Path of the Warrior. Willpower is also five. I don't really like this character on a number of levels. While I'm aware that this sort of individual exists in real life (I've actually met more than a few of them in my life), it feels like it's being presented as a racial stereotype, possibly just because the Setites are the clan most associate with Africa and black people. And if you compare and contrast with the Brujah Rapper, it definitely comes off as the worse of the two. Plus I just have a hard time buying the idea that a Setite drug gang looked at this guy and said, "this is who we need to be our liaison with George Bush and his CIA cocaine importers."

                                                        I've always liked the Followers of Set and they remain one of my all-time favorite clans along with the Old Clan Tzimisce, Brujah, and Malkavians.                                                         I always felt the Followers of Set are one of the few groups that shoulds have vampires trying to be "good" because while they don't have to be, it's a clan built on people going out of their way to be evil by society's standards. It's why I feel like the Clanbooks failed to properly incorporate the Serpents of Light. The Serpents of Light are evil muthersuckers, don't get me wrong, but they believe themselves to be good despite being in the Sabbat. Because it takes a lot of mental gymnastics to adopt an ideology of war against reality.

                                                        I also felt like the Children of Osiris were something that could be properly redeemed into mainstream vampire-lore by someone like The Gentleman Gamer just by the fact a vampire-hunting cult of religious fanatics is exactly the sort of group that might be produced by the Setites pushing their Neonates too hard. Mind you, I don't think this is spelled out in canon but I think it's also been hinted that the Children of Osiris were created by Kemintiri. They were her weapon against the mainstream Followers of Set and just had a good discipline for staving off the Beast.

                                                        Part of why I like the Ministry update to the FoS is that it seems to be doing that in broader terms. Damballa isn't evil so the Followers of Set embracing a bunch of them and then trying to pass Set off as him resulted in a bunch of worshipers who want nothing to do with them. Worshipers of Eris may love Chaos but they're not going to subordinate their goddess to Egyptian mythology and so on. You can be evil and chaotic without believing that you should be EVIL in the name of CHAOS. The Church of Set in Cults of the Blood Gods amused me because I think Set would be less than pleased with an orthodox fundamentalist branch of his Chaos religion. Someone is doing something wrong.

                                                        One thing I'd love to speculate on as a Medieval historian and student of comparative religion is whether the Church of Caine began as a Followers of Set blood cult then went off into its own thing. After all, Setite Gnosticism is very similar to Church of Caine gnosticism with the exception of their messiahs.
