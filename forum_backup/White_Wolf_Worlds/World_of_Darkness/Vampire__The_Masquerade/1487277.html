    What should a vampire's innate racial abilities be?

    07-20-2022, 07:58 PM
    That is, what powers should it have without any Discipline dots at all?

    One of the more obvious to me is that they should be able to see in the dark. I've heard various arguments for vampires not being able to see in the dark over the years, and they all fail against the sheer "Dracula, Dead and Loving It" silliness of a vampire stumbling around in its victim's bedroom as it fumbles for a victim it can't see.

    What other abilities should be innate to vampires?

              VtR 2e's base package seems like it captures the essentials pretty well. I don't think we need the physical Disciplines as part of the package, as blood to boost physical rolls is part of the default in VtM and VtR. VtR 2e includes enhanced senses. Lashing Out covers the classic "vampire bares its fangs when suddenly confronted" without needing to go full frenzy to temporarily unleash the Beast on people.

              A more subtle thing is that VtR 2e doesn't have Clan Weakness kick in until you drop to Humanity 6 for the first time. This hits a bunch of pop culture notes where new vampires are somewhat blank slates compared to their more experienced peers and creates the potential for in-game hope that maybe you can escape the curse of being a vampire if you fight it hard enough (you can't in the long run though). It lets you play around with fresh vampires that take time to become "full"vampires for both the power and price it come with.

                  In my games I give vampires the ability to function in general darkness without penalty. There needs to be at least some light source (starlight or the moon peeking in through the curtains or a bit of light seeping under a doorway). If a vampire were a mile under ground in absolute pitch black darkness, they'd be in as much trouble as anyone else, but functionally their night vision is naturally comparable to a cat's. Then Protean 1 can give the vampire magic glowing eyes that will let them see in the deepest darkness, or echolocation like a bat at the player's choice. Another general advantage I grant all vampires is a -1 difficulty on all rolls that involve smelling blood, or when trying to track or pinpoint someone by listening for the blood pounding in their veins.

                  This generally means that vampires operate at better than human levels in the darkness, especially when it comes to finding and tracking prey.

                      Nope. Vampires don't get to see in the dark automatically. Unless the moon is gone, Nights aren't that dark. There's no pressing need to really force such a power on vampires.

                      The Awareness Talent encourages different types of supernaturals to avoid eachother.


                      Ghoul racial abilities (often overlooked)
                      Ghouls can increase attributes that cannot normally be improved (like Mentals) This is largely from Sires bestowing a fraction of their gains when providing ghouls blood.
                      That +1 strength comes from the same animating force that allows vampires to move around despite not having working organs. This animating force has a lot of extra features:
                      Ghouls don't bleed out like humans do. Some blood will escape from wounds, but it's significantly less than there should be. Being able to resist lethal damage also enables them to move on injuries that would normally keep a mortal still.
                      Ghouls get tired a lot less, and they operate a lot better than normal people when they're tired. Operating at the bare minimum is rather like sleeping for them, and they can perform long-distance athletics with a strength of 1 indefinitely.
                      Their ability to hold their breath or go without eating is greatly improved, especially when they're not doing much, but it isn't infinite like vampires.
                      Of course, they can use Vitae. Vitae can be used in place of any other magic resource: Gnosis, Rage, Quintessence, Glamour, faith ETC.



                      Vampires
                      -Vampires increase attributes by feeding from many, many people with good features over their long lifespan. IE who they hunt matters. They increase Disciplines by how they hunt.
                      -Unless they're really exerting themselves and tearing muscles (which they are more resistant to), they basically have infinite stamina when it comes to long distance athletics.
                      -Blood bond is great. Could use better explanations of how it works.
                      -Creating ghouls is fun and I wouldn't want that to change, but as said before, I think a Vampire's blood should work well to improve the ghoul in the long term. Much like a capitalist, the Vampire takes wealth from the masses and hands it off to his buddies.
                      -The Vampire's internals make for a great storage space. However, most vampires can't tollerate consuming anything but blood orally. It's best to cut yourself open or use the butt.

                          I don't see the need to give them physical Disciplines. It also takes away somewhat from the Clans that have them as their Clan Disciplines.
                          • For vision, I've played it mostly like vampires can perceive part of the infrared spectrum. This enhances vision in 'normal' dark scenarios enough, while they would still be blind in a cave or something. It also gives them a sense of body heat, which is useful to pick out other undead things without Auspex.
                          • Sense blood sources in proximity. No exact location, but presence. Vampires being able to smell out nearby prey is a trope I like.
                          • Increased pain threshold, immunity to heat and cold (to the point it starts to do damage), at the price of decreased haptic senses. Dead bodies.

                          I actually forgot whether or not those are already part of the default package. The rest (lick wounds to close, mini-amnesia with the bite, etc) is fine as it is.

                                      Alright. Gone through it.
                                      I think it's something all vampires can still work around.


                                      Malks, Toreador, Gangrel, Tremere, Tzmisce... all have their in-clan hightened senses out of the gate. So not an issue for them.

                                      The Ventrue are highly social predators with ample resources and thus very likely feed in illuminated areas.
                                      Brujah, Giovanni and Lasombra either go like the Ventrue and feed through manipulation or they act like Brutes and just hit the guy till he crumples, then feed; Not a problem.
                                      Followers of Set can follow the Ventrue way of doing things until they get their own sensory power.

                                      The ones we should actually discuss.
                                      Ravnos: There's not really a need for them to fumble around in the dark. They would benefit from heightened senses helping them Sandman, because that's a safe way to feed, but the clan for the most part is transient and naturally inclined towards risky behaviour: beating mortals down for blood, manipulating them, using animals when you get desperate; this doesn't need heightened senses.

                                      Nosferatu
                                      The big one. How can they live underground without some sensory power?
                                      Well, for one, I don't think they actually put themselves that far down there, at least when we talk Neonates. Neonate Nos likely either live like other vampires, in secure homes or apartments, or they live only slightly in the underground network. In modern times, you're not hunting underground, you're just sleeping there. Either you live near enough to the entrance/exit that simple by-memory navigation is fine, or you can delve with a torch.
                                      Nosferatu are the prefect ambush predators. Feeding from the sleeping while in the dark is great for them, but they can easily gravitate towards alternatives like animal use or violence while they lack Auspex/Protean 1.

                                                          One of the reasons I really like what VtR 2e did with Kindred senses is that they're so focused on finding blood. The default package isn't about being a cat, but about being a mosquito: homing in on food.

                                                          There's a thematic strength to it, and the default package creates room for good drama (as oppose to fumbling in the dark). Hunters can learn exercises to slow their heartbeat, develop sprays to disguise the lingered scent of their living blood, and get fresh blood to fill the vampire's senses and set a trap. The hunters can become the hunted with skill, the right tools, and some luck. Sensory Disciplines are then more important for stepping beyond being a bloodsucker and into things like avoiding ambushes.
