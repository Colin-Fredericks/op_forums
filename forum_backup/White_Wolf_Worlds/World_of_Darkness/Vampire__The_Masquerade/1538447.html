    Regional Setting | US Southeast

    07-11-2024, 05:09 PM
    EDIT: US Southwest

    Forum regulars may remember that some time ago I started a thread with the focus of considering New England as a regional setting for Vampire, with the intent to get into other settings later. Well that last part hasn’t really happened but I’m still thinking about it and I wanted to take a particular question to you all.

    How do you see the US Southwest as a setting for vampire?

    First, let me clarify a few things. Wikipedia lists Arizona, New Mexico, Nevada, Utah, Oklahoma, Texas, & California as Southwest, albeit with only Arizona & New Mexico as definitively Southwest, though I’d include Nevada & Utah in that. I can also see how certain regions in California & Colorado merit limited inclusion. Texas & Oklahoma one could argue are somewhat distinct because of how their history has greater ties to the Southeast or Plains West, but for the sake of this convo I definitely want to include Texas.

    According to Wikipedia’s listing of US metropolitan populations, of the top ten highest population metropolitan areas, three are located in the Southwest: 4. Dallas-Fort Worth, 5. Houston, 10. Phoenix. If one expands this to be the Sunbelt in general one can add 2. Los Angeles, 6. Atlanta, & 9. Miami.

    Suffice to say, large and important cities, with more than enough warm bodies to provide a robust environment for plenty of vampires. My issue is that, when I consider what the vampiric setting of the three primary southeastern metropolitan cities listed above, I’m somewhat at a loss. I’m sure that information has been offered to provide canon answers to sect affiliation and princes but imo the region feels underrepresented in lore. The Sunbelt represents a very significant region of the US in terms of population as well as political & cultural influence and so I feel as though it ought to be represented in my game lore, but there’s a blank area.

    I understand that tbh. For one, the games Midwestern tendencies aside, there are reasons for that. The regions which got the most coverage are also regions of the US with some of the oldest and most influential cities and cultural images (the Northeast, Midwest, & Pacific West). The Midwest has the Rustbelt cities, forged in the fires of the Second Industrial Revolution, providing a strong backdrop of gothic smokestacks for the Camarilla of Chicago. The Northeast has the metropolitan prestige, power, & gothic memories of New York City (whether in its Gangs of New York, Great Gatsby, or Godfather iterations) as a domain for the Sabbat pre-gentrification or Camarilla post-gentrification. The Pacific West has the counterculture and noir of California of San Francisco & Los Angeles to fuel the punkish Anarch Free States. Even regions which received less coverage, such as New England or the Southeast, can easily provide strong gothic imagery: puritans, witches, and old world history for the former & the macabre relics of the slavocracy and the ruined Kingdom of Cotton for the latter. I immediately have inspiration which comes to mind for the depiction of all of these regions.

    But what about the southwest?

    Thats the question I have and what I’m curious about other people’s thoughts on. I’ll share some initial thoughts of my own in a post to follow this one but I’m curious to hear other peoples ideas of what a Gothic-Punk Southwest would look like, what inspirations you might invoke, and what you imagine the vampiric domains of Dallas, Houston, or Phoenix might look like.

      Initial Inspiration & Thoughts

      •The first thing that comes to mind for me is Westerns. This probably isn’t a huge surprise, it was the first thing that the writers for Changeling came up with when designing the Kingdoms of Concordia too. Yet, while Westerns & the Old West in general should definitely have some influence I don’t want to have that be the entirety. Some thoughts.
      -Perhaps, when considering Westerns for a southeastern setting, one could play with the eras of the Western. One could play with and distinguish between the various modes of the Western from the Classic Western (‘Stagecoach’ 1939, ‘3:10 to Yuma’ 1957, etc.), Revisionist Westerns (‘A Fistful of Dollars’ 1964, ‘Once Upon A Time In The West’ 1968), or Neo-Westerns (‘No Country For Old Men’ 2007)
      -Further the classical images of the western and the cowboy might be interesting to mine for inspiration. It’s not a new observation that Cowboy conceptions about the are often more significant than the realities. Similarly one could explore how the images of the Cowboy were consciously invoked through the 20th & 21st century for various purposes.

      •Breaking Bad & Better Call Saul could provide a source of inspiration

      •The Works of Cormac McCarthy in general

      •An important element of the Gothic is how the concealed corruption and crimes of the past return to haunt the living (and, in fact, are as inseparably woven into our lives as ghosts are woven into a haunted house). Chicago by Night does this well for the Industrial Midwest by portraying a setting still haunted by the splendor and corruption of the Gilded Age, with trade unionists and steel magnates still ruling the streets of the Windy City alongside Prohibition Gangsters, Counterculture Radicals, & Yuppie Psychos. Similarity one can see how similar things could be done for the Pacific West (‘Chinatown’ style industrial cartels, ‘Hollywood Babalon’ style Vice and crime carefully concealed by the Studio System, Noir in general), the Southeast (the scars of King Cotton & the Slavocracy, the suffocation of Reconstruction, the specter of Jim Crow & the Klan), the Northeast (The bossism of Tammany Hall, the glitz and glamour of Wall Street in the 20s and the cocaine fueled frenzy of Wall Street in the 80s, the Art Deco shadows of La Cosa Nostra), etc. What can one offer as substitutes for the southeast?
      -One suggestion from the legacy of the Revisionist and Neo-Western is to carefully consider the consequences of the Anglo Settler-Colonialism (Native Americans) on one hand and the Mexican-American War (Hispanos) on the other. Both how the gothic perpetuates these conflicts as part of its historical penumbra and how the imagery taken from these historical contexts is used to define the self-image of the region and its leadership (I’m thinking of how mid-century Anglo leaders in the Phoenix Chamber of Commerce called themselves ‘Thunderbirds’ and wore turquoise necklace and jackets with intricate beadwork).
      -The contradictions of the midcentury booster notion of cities that ‘made the deserts bloom’; one could focus on the artificial tendrils holding these cities together in environments where they could not survive without robust infrastructure networks. One could play with this both as a sort of doomed Hubris (think the Titanic) as well as an obfuscation of the crimes that ‘making the desert bloom’ required (take ‘Chinatown’ as inspiration; although perhaps the full implications of that are more the territory of Werewolf)

      •Still, despite the reality of the region’s history, the Sunbelt is also a relatively new region of the country and its story may not be yet finished. One could use this to imagine that the Sunbelt domains of the southwest boast ambitious coteries of ancillae and the younger elders of the Camarilla who seek to harness the rising region to establish their own bases of power and perhaps to make their region central nodes of Camarilla power as Chicago, New York City, or London were in their respective periods. One could perhaps tie this into the libertarian streak of the region to have robust and unique Anarch organizations and tendencies within the region.
      -On the other hand however, if one imagines the rise of the the Southeast as coming from Postwar Development (1940s-1970s) and teachings its height amidst the oil crises and Cowboy booms of the following decades (1970s-1980s) than perhaps, invoking representations such as ‘Breaking Bad’ or ‘No Country For Old Men’ one could also portray a Sunbelt whose moment as the avatar of the World-Spirit has past it by and, just as with the Rustbelt of the early 90s, now one was doomed to dwell within the ruins of a now-gone golden age, haunted by specters of the past and worlds that could-have-been but never were.

      •One probably ought to consider how one imagines Mexico & Central America in your setting, as the circumstances of those regions would most likely have a significant connection to the southeast as a region in the WoD
      -I’m tempted to explore this in a sort of, “The Mexican-American War never ended…” type way, with the history of Anglo-Hispano interactions in the region, going back to the conquest, and the pre-conquest unity of the southwest with modern Mexico (as well as perhaps an older ‘Aztlan’) still colors the themes and narrative of the region in the present WoD (or a reconstructed 1990s)

      This is all I have atm but I encourage others to share their own thoughts

          Additional Inspiration Ideas

          So here’s a few more things that came to me while I was brainstorming things that might be invoked for a sketch of the region in the WoD

          •Mysteries/Land of Enchantment
          ​​​​​-When I was a kid one of my favorite movies was ‘Scooby Doo & the Alien Invaders’ which was set somewhere in the Midwest and featured, as you might expect, UFO folklore. So while of course one can key into a number of veins of mysticism for the southwest. New Mexico of course being the ‘Land of Enchantment’ and the themes of Native American spirituality and its images in the US consciousness (Ancient civilization, drums, fires, psychedelic substances, etc.; the finale episode of the second season of the X-Files ‘Anasazi’ is a good touchstone for this). Furthermore one can add the remnants of the Spanish Missions and its influences, the interest of various New Agers & Neo-Theosophists in the southwest (see stuff like Joshua Tree, Mount Shasta, etc)
          -The UFOangle however does not simply tie into the new age but of course the military-industrial aspect of the region. Other than the Gold Rush the Cold War is perhaps the most significant economic event in the region’s recent history. Roswell supposedly happened in the deserts of the southwest and Area 51 is out there as well, reported on by desert radio stations like classic Coast to Coast. Contemporary New Agers/UFO Watchers still flock to this region whether they’re hoping to study Utah’s Sk*nwalker Ranch or if they’re doing Stephen Greer-style Transcendental Meditation™️ in the desert. Of course skeptics have also long noted the curious overlap of areas with a high number of UFO sightings with proximity to dense southwestern military installations and experimental R&D sites. Regardless of the truth of any of this, the presence of the Military-Industrial Complex in the region also provides a rich vein of mid-century vibes.
          -Relatedly, in the conspiratorial vein, where was the Prince of Camelot killed? Why, in Dallas of course.

          •I’ve never seen the classic soap drama ‘Dallas’ but I have seen some of what I consider a sister series, ‘Dynasty’. While being set in the Rocky Mountain highlands of Denver rather then the desert plains of Texas, the show still focuses upon the intrigues of high level energy dynasties in the Sunbelt region, from the rough and tumble wildcat oil-riggers to the genteel barons of industry whose influence in Washington is capable of influencing National Policy. This era, still remembered in early White Wolf with its affection for the period archetype of the ‘corporate raider’ could provide further inspiration for material of the region.
          -Further the history of oil and energy is significant as well in showcasing the recent dynamism of the region. Regardless of one’s view on recent US political economy, the narrative of southwestern oil Cowboys helping to carry out a palace coup in the 1970s presents rich opportunities for depictions of political drama with connection to the region. It also helps to highlight how relatively young the region’s golden age is compared to that of the Northeast or Midwest and provides opportunity for unique sect dynamics. Sure by the 1990s the Halloween Massacre of the Ford years was old news, but what might it mean for the vampires of the Camarilla to see these ancillae in cities that count their age of majority in decades, attempting to challenge sect leaders in Chicago, Manhattan, or San Francisco?

          These are just some additional thoughts for inspiration I had, I might use these to try to sketch an outline of what I’d see the sects of the region looking like in the near future and I encourage others to share any thoughts they have

                          Something that helps me when trying to figure out the vibes of a chronicle is to go over the history of a region, particularly how it can be accentuated in a Gothic-Punk fashion, so I figure it could be useful to go over a historical sketch of the region. I’m not a professional historian but I am an amateur with interest. I’m stronger in some areas then others so I welcome the input of my fellow forum readers.

                          Early Days of New Spain

                          I’m not familiar enough to really sufficiently sketch out what the vampiric presence in the pre-Columbian Southwest would look like, although I’ve always thought it would be worthwhile to have an image of pre-contact vampires so we can better imagine the transformation that occurred in early modern American vampirdom.

                          To speak loosely I’d suppose that vampires would be more prevalent amongst settled peoples such as the Aztecs of modern day central Mexico and that they historically would have been more prolific amongst the settled agrarian states of MesoAmerica, the Ohio River Valley, etc; with nomadic and semi-nomadic groups supporting a much smaller population of semi-nomadic vampires who probably resembled somewhat the Noddist idea of Caine as accursed monstrous wanderer prior to the creation of the First City. Perhaps this idea of indigenous vampires as being in a more ‘natural Cainite state’ can partially explain the later infatuation the Sabbat (loyalists) would have with indigenous cultures.

                          Regardless, alongside Spanish arrival in the new world would come the first waves of Sabbat smuggled aboard their ships. These early Sabbat would probably be near Autarkis in their relative isolation. They would also be the first waves of refugees to the new world, fleeing the Camarilla-Sabbat Wars which arose after the Treaty of Thorns and which dominated much of Early Modern vampiric history.

                          (btw shout out to Black Fox, whose various write ups of Sabbat sect history have been greatly influential in my understanding of the sect’s development)

                          Anyway, this is not the place for an in-depth discussion of the Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire, which a Mexico by Night write up would be better equipped to handle. Suffice to say here that the instability caused to indigenous societies by Columbian contact and conquest would, I imagine, produce a chaos amongst indigenous vampires similar to that of what produced the First Anarch Revolt in Europe. This instability, which would set new world elder against new world neonate, would provide a prime opportunity for old world Sabbat arrivals to share the tools they had used in their Revolt (and refine them with the secrets and mysteries of new world recruits). I imagine the first wave of Sabbat influence, slowly emanating out along trade routes out of central Mexico, seeing proto-Sabbat Loyalists creating strong ties with New World Neonates turning against their Elders for whatever reasons, forming the bases for the first nomadic packs of the New World and influencing much of later Sabbat philosophy and custom.

                          (I imagine the myriad Spanish-Native wars that broke out from the 1540s onward would provide good cover and grounds for these early Sabbat proto-loyalists to make contact with and integrate with New World Neonates, from the Chichimeca War to the Pueblo Revolts)

                          However these wars and revolts, along with the expansion of Spanish Missions from the 1550s onward, including missions in the modern day US South from California to Florida, create an interesting dichotomy.

                          First this period is in what Immanuel Wallerstein might call the period early modern imperial expansion and rivalry between the Hapsburgs & Valois, ending with the abdication of Charles V in 1556 and the collapse into bankruptcy of both the rival European empires of Hapsburg & Valois which had arisen in Europe after the rise of the Age of Exploration. Some historians have posited that it was in this period that the Hapsburgs had the greatest opportunity to realize their vision of a pan-Christian imperium but were ultimately unable to realize it and so, according to Wallerstein, the imperial states retracted and the pull of history began to shift towards the rise of medium-size states.

                          This period, in the World of Darkness, is concurrent with the Camarilla-Sabbat Wars. In this phase I imagine that the Sabbat is still largely in its decentralized post-Anarch form, although the gradual defection of Elders, both for political and philosophical reasons shall gradually transform the sect. In this circumstance however, the influence of the Camarilla in the major urban centers seems to me that it would be ascendant, with its far more serious rivals being hunters, sorcerers, & recalcitrant princes of the night rather than the Sabbat. Rather then the Camarilla & Sabbat battling over control of these cities in midnight sieges, I imagine it is more likely to resemble efforts to sway the remaining independent princes to pledge towards either sect, with the Sabbat proper remaining a rural phenomena, using the social technologies of bandits and heretics for their survival.

                          Those recalcitrant princes who accepted entrance into the Sword of Caine did so for a number of reasons but I imagine that two were particularly important: first the feeling amongst the recalcitrant that they would not have sufficient power, influence, or prestige as latecomers to the Camarilla (this is often explicitly sited by Lasombra & Tzimisce elders); second, that they chafed under the proscriptions of Humanitas & Masquerade enforced by the Ivory Tower, a particular source of discontent for the sorts of elders & ancillae who would have considered themselves peers of the residents of the Black Monastery (who were probably very influential in attracting elder recruits to the Sabbat during this period; perhaps this is even the origins of the Priscus). However, even though recalcitrant princes of the Mediterranean region & Eastern Europe may have declared for the Sabbat, I also imagine that many of these elders, never accepting the Anarch conception of a ‘Total War Against The Sires’, would likely seek to maintain clandestine contact with their now-Camarilla peers. This is likely the origins of the phenomena of Sabbat cities which, in many respects, operate similarly to Camarilla cities; as well as the notion that certain elysia, far from the eyes of the young of either sect, may host congenial representatives of either sect.

                          What is the significance of this for the Southwest? Well I think the New World is important in how it establishes the practices of the sect. I imagine that elders and ancillae of the sort who ran Camarilla-lite cities in Europe would have been at the head of establishing covens in early Spanish population centers in the Americas. However, unlike in Europe where the post-Anarch Sabbat Rabble were ever on the back foot, the flux of Indigenous Vampiric Society & the circumstances of colonial frontier life would provide greater opportunities for proto-Loyalist Sabbat packs. I can even imagine these packs placing pressure upon their conservative sect leaders by aligning themselves with indigenous vampiric cliques during the various colonial wars of the period. This phenomena of heightened tension, where conservative Sabbat are not secure enough to establish a Camarilla-lite as they might have in Hapsburg territories in Europe, provides greater opportunities for the proto-loyalist Sabbat to make their conception of Sabbat life active in vampiric politics in the new world. The later histories of the Sabbat tell us that the first Civil War would erupt in the 1700s, but perhaps this is the revisionist historiography of later conservative Sabbat, who would like to imagine that they were not once forced to concede multiple points of the American strategy to the loyalists who, rather then allow the Sabbat to be molded in the conservatives vision, pushed back their revitalized notions of ideals which had their origins in the First Anarch Revolt, during the Long Night…

                              To continue…

                              Developments Under New Spain

                              In Europe, the general contraction that came along with decline of the Valois & Hapsburgs after 1557 would see a short-lived second renaissance for the north Italian city-states but would lead into the ascendancy of the United Provinces (the Dutch golden age beginning somewhere around 1600-1625), which would include the capture of the Portuguese West Indies trade by the Dutch. While 1557 was not the final end of Hapsburg ambitions (which would continue to be significant until at least the end of the 30 years war in 1648), shown by the Spanish Armada and such, it was the beginning of the decline of the Hapsburg states, a decline that would become even more obvious by 1651, at which point Wallerstein argues that Spain & Portugal were well along the process of a downward transition from a core to semi-peripheral state in the European world-system.

                              What does that mean for Spanish America, more particularly the northwestern frontier of New Spain, the latter-day US Southwest? Well, this is the contest within which it arose. The indigenous population in many of these areas would remain the majority until the turn of the 19th century and Spanish concentration would remain to the south in central Mexico. The Mission System would expand throughout the Southwest from the 1560s to nearly the turning of the 19th century, a relative hinterlands of New Spain where, despite the concentration of secular and religious authorities which may have ensured a conservative Sabbat in central Mexico, the frontier territories would provide plentiful opportunities for the development of the proto-loyalist Sabbat. This tension can be highlighted in the continuous, albeit scattered, indigenous revolts which New Spain saw from the 1550s through to the early 1700s, not to mention hostilities with independent nomadic indigenous groups such as the Comanche from the early 1700s onward.

                              The New Spain of this period would be interesting for a deep dive by a Mexico by Night source, particularly with the relative decline of commerce after the imperial contraction (exacerbated by the piracy campaigns of the English, French, & Dutch), the Spanish core in central Mexico developed ties with the ascendant peripheral zones of the West Indies & Piedmont Atlantic Planters. An interesting early sinew one could exploit for a Southron Lords/Knights of the Golden Circle style plot line.

                              By the early 1700s a significant event occurs for the entirety of the Iberian world: the War of the Spanish Succession. Wallerstein argues that this war breaks out in the context of the Franco-Anglo rivalry which had become the primary contradiction amongst the core states of Europe after the negotiated surrender of the Dutch to the English in 1688/89. (A note here, I think that the golden age of the Netherlands and the rise of England’s influence in Europe would be a good unexplored place in WoD vampire history, where neo-anarch young Turks within the Camarilla begin a campaign which reorients the vampiric underworld in Europe, but anyway. The consequence of the War of the Spanish Succession is that the French successfully place a member of the French Bourbon dynasty on the Spanish throne, albeit with significant concessions to England & the Netherlands. This brings about the era of the ‘Bourbon Reforms’ and while the Reforms arguably do not reach Hispanic America in earnest until 1763 (in the aftermath of the Seven Years War), I feel this is a significant and unexplored transition period in WoD.

                              IIRC Clanbooks Lasombra correctly, much of their dialogue on the Spanish years ends with the failures of the Spanish Armada and the transition of the clan’s attention to the new world in the late 1700s (after the end of the last of the Camarilla-Sabbat Wars). I feel this warrants further attention. The Lasombra’s power on the Iberian peninsula can be traced back to Phoenician times, so it’s not surprising that they adapted to both Germanic & Arab migrations into the regain. What is interesting is that during the formation of the modern sects, Iberia is a territory of the Hapsburg Empire, which is interesting in that the Middle Europa of the Holy Roman Empire is generally associated with the Ventrue. While perhaps this was not done consciously, instead drawing upon Anglo cultural memory of the Black Legend and the exoticisation of Catholic territories, I find it interesting that the Lasombra & Tzimisce are associated with the two sides of Hapsburg influence (Iberia/Southern Mediterranean & Eastern Europe respectively). Perhaps this can serve as a partial explanation for their tendency towards the Sabbat, a sense that if, alongside accepting the influence that the Ventrue core of Hapsburg Germany/Austria could extend over their territories, they consented to enter into a Ventrue led political coalition, the Camarilla, they would lose too much of their power to the Clan of Lords. It might also explain some of the causes for Lasombra rejection of the Camarilla, if they perceived Iberian Ventrue, with ties to the Germanic Ventrue core, as engaged in a conspiracy to oust them from control of their traditional Iberian territory. (Plus, as all this drama goes on, the contraction of the Hapsburg imperial ambitions provides ripe economic conditions for the proliferation of the Sabbat amongst a restive and impoverished rural population). Perhaps one can also link this to the Portuguese Revolt against the Iberian Union or the Catalan Revolt.

                              The War of the Spanish Succession brings to power the Bourbon dynasty in the Spanish Empire. As the above might suggest, I think it would be a worthy discussion for Vampire to consider in a hypothetical World of Darkness: France to consider how the dynastic history of the Valois & Bourbons is reflected in the developments of the Camarilla. France is generally considered one of the core heartlands of the Camarilla, alongside the HRE, at least in its early iteration as, in no small part, uniting the Toreador-Ventrue power blocs of those regions. Regardless in 1721 the Bourbons ascend to the throne of the Spanish Empire. Unlike the Hapsburgs, the French under the Bourbons had been able to recover from the imperial contraction of 1557, and was presently in a power struggle with Britain to determine who would be the premier core power of the European world system. A central part of this struggle was the competition to usurp the Spanish-Portuguese Atlantic trade for either power. This dynamic, perhaps reflected in a rivalry between French Toreador-British Ventrue may present the vampires of Spain the room to negotiate with the leaders of France via elder elysia & the Sabbat of Southern France. The Bourbon reforms attempted to modernize Spain with limited success but perhaps this effort, while characterized by some loyalists as a negotiated settlement with the Camarilla, would allow the conservative Sabbat to retrench itself. Notably the Bourbon Reforms would see the expulsion of the Jesuits from Spanish America.

                              The Bourbon Reforms did not begin to see full realization in Spanish America until 1763, after the Seven Years War which marked a crucial failure for France in its competition with Britain. This period was, in the northern frontier of New Spain still heavily influenced by the Mission & Presidio system, increasingly under threat from the native Comancheria. I can see this as a time when the Sabbat of the latter-day Southwest increasingly defined themselves in contrast with both the Penninsular Sabbat and the Criollo Elite Conservative Sabbat of Central Mexico, who appeared to be increasingly adopting a policy of compromise with the Camarilla and utilizing the organizational methods of the Camarilla to preserve order in their domains. In contrast to this the loyalists would have had ample opportunity, provided by the instability of centralized Spanish authority in the region, to take notes from the decentralized leadership of the Comanceria, blasphemous mockery of the Missions which could both serve Noddist ends and appealed to disillusioned natives & mestizo, and consider themselves the keepers of the banner of the ‘True Sabbat’.

                              All of this, in the lead up towards the Spanish Wars of Independence in the Americas and what lore tells us is the outbreak of the First Sabbat Civil War, a major battlefield of which may just be the latterday US Southwest…

                                  Something to remember is that none of the European powers were actually the significant power in what would become the southwestern United States. The Spanish claims were mostly on paper, not reality. Other than a few missionaries and tiny settlements, Spanish power was quite limited. Likewise French power was also quite distant and lax. Different Indian groups ruled here in reality, and from 1700-1850/1870 or so, the Commanche Indians were the top dog in Texas and the surrounding areas, often dictating terms to the Spanish/Mexicans and the French.

                                  You might want to go to the library or bookstore and pick up a popular history of the Commanche to understand the real history of the region before the Americans arrived.

                                  Very likely there is at best a limited vampire presence in the small settlements (perhaps none) during the colonial area. Any vampires that exist might be Gangrel wandering through Commanche and other Indian territory. If you want to tie that into the Sabbat, these would probably be Country Gangrel on the Path of Harmony who are de facto Loyalists, but otherwise have very little to do with the Sabbat other than that they will eventually see the incoming Camarilla vampires as enemies because they are following behind the American settlers. The "Sabbat" here will be very different than what is developing down in the urban part of central Mexico or in the cities of the US back east.

                                          Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                                          You might want to go to the library or bookstore and pick up a popular history of the Commanche to understand the real history of the region before the Americans arrived.
                                          I recommend SC Gwynne's Empire of the Southern Moon. It's relatively short, very readable, and gives a good overview of the Commanche from before they acquired horses up to around the start of the 20th century.

                                          Tangentially, I'll also mention the late Tony Hillerman's long running series of mystery novels involving members of the Navajo Tribal Police. They are a fascinating window in Diné culture and politics, including the relations with the neighboring tribes in Arizona, New Mexico, and Utah during the 70s, 80s, 90s, and early 2000s. A few of them were adapted into tv movies by PBS, and the current AMC series Dark Winds is based on said books. Also, Hillerman's nonfiction book The Great Taos Bank Robbery, a collection of true stories from the region.
