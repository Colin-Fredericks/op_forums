                                                The problem of citing the Tzimisce Voivodate as some exemplar of the efficacy or strength of Tzimisce rule is that it was something of a disaster in pursuing its goals or even maintaining regular cohesion by the time of the War of Princes certainly. The Dark Ages Tzimisce had massive issues with being hobbled by their sheer overwhelming pride and territoriality.

                                                Vladimir Rustovich, the Voivode of Voivodes was undermined and betrayed by his own broodmates despite being the head of the whole thing and the guy in charge of their war efforts, such that he would ultimately abandon said efforts to seek revenge on his own brood specifically and the Shadow Lords generally. An explicitly noted problem for the clan in their wars with both the Ventrue and Tremere, and a regular cause of losses within it, was that getting the clan to actually work together was a nightmarish process, where many Tzimisce would refuse to participate in unified efforts/subordinate themselves to another member of their clan/abandon whatever vendetta they might have with another member of their clan, or recognize any problem that didn't threaten their direct territory. It was a massive achievement of leadership that Rustovich could get his clan to work together, just at all. And then his own clanmates ultimately brutally, short sightedly sabotaged him despite the sheer damage that would cause to their war effort. Tzimisce pride and jealousy is a hell of a drug, to paraphrase something.

                                                It's one of the reasons the Tremere survived the Tzimisce's attempt to wipe them out, the clan's inability to fully get their act together due to the territorial mindset and vengeful pride that ran rampant within it. The modern clan (pre V5 obviously) has less of a complex in that sense, but the tradeoff there is the shift in overall clan culture has taken it far away from concepts like asserting leadership or cohesion to more of a focus on warped matters of spirit (there's a reason a huge proportion of pack priests, but not ducti, are/were Tzimisce)

                                                Ironically and at least to their credit, the old pride and territoriality of the Tzimisce was actually useful as far as keeping them out of the clutches of forces like the infernal (who were otherwise the strongest they would ever be as a corrupting force in the medieval era, overall), because the usual pitch of demons/infernal vampires/that kind of thing about being able to offer aid or power would crash hard into a Tzimisce reacting with grave, violent, murderous offense to the clearly insulting suggestion that they needed help from someone else.

                                                But otherwise? The Voivodate spent most of its time as a loosely organized institution with certainly individually very strong leaders, and a highly marked problem when they came together to do things like wage war as far as tripping over each other's egos and starting up a whole bunch of new internal vendettas.

                                                    So let me get back to the original idea a bit with a few of my musings about what this might look like if the Tzimisce lead, or at least were a prominent Clan, in the Camarilla.

                                                    Let’s go with the Anarch Revolt happening in much the same way as the canon timeline, but the Tzimisce leaders defeat their childer and maintain control over their ancestral domains and are able to unite the Clans to create the Camarilla. They become the most respected founding Clan and their intense interest in feudalism and the honorable aristocratic values informs the Camarilla system. The Ventrue participate as well, as reluctant allies and eventual rivals to the Tzimisce.

                                                    As time goes by, the Tzimisce loses power and wealth relative to the Ventrue and the Toreador, as mortal wealth becomes less based on nobles and land and more based on business. Ventrue are new money, Tzimisce old money, and this distinction in part defines the power struggle within the Camarilla. The Ventrue are more egalitarian in a sense, as they believe in upward mobility of those who have the ability to make money or influence people. Alternatively, they don’t believe in noblesse oblige. Ventrue supported central banks, stock investments, big corporations and trading companies, big markets and merchants, democracy, media corporations. The Tzimisce favor landed gentry, slavery, local politics, nationalistic/ethnic/cultural interests. The Ventrue are winning, as we can see.

                                                    The Tzimisce rely more heavily on the Revenant families in this setting. The Grimaldi are one of their connections to the modern business world, they also represent a large centralized organization that transcends feudal ideas of territory. The family goes beyond the land. The Zantosa also have a lot of wealth, both ancestral nobility wealth and contacts to the criminal underworld and the world of big vice. The Obertus are mystics and the Bratovitch are warriors along with the Vlaszy who are still alive in this timeline. These Revenant families provide a lot of diverse abilities and resources that keep the Tzimisce Clan from being entirely surpassed by the Ventrue. I’d even go far as to say that a high percentage of Tzimisce vampires come from one of these families and the Revenant family structures bind the Clan across domains that would otherwise have been isolated.

                                                    What about other Clans in this setting? The Ventrue, as said, are the primary rivals of the Tzimisce. They are in some ways like canon, but a bit more ruthless and less honorable, maybe even more supportive of Anarchs or similar movements, as the Tzimisce support absolute rule of the elders.

                                                    The Toreador exist and are also one of the more powerful Clans in the Camarilla, perhaps having a more modern power base than the Tzimisce and favoring policies of the Ventrue over those of the Tzimisce. The Gangrel and Nosferatu are both in the Clan and they have a traditionally more friendly relationship with the Tzimisce. Those two Clans lend their strength to the Tzimisce more so than they supported the Ventrue in canon and their support helps the Tzimisce maintain power. The Nosferatu help provide the Tzimisce with information about their enemies and the modern world. The Gangrel lend their strength to help as well, perhaps given command of wilderness territories by the Tzimisce, making the Gangrel feudal lords over their own rural domains. Malkavians are probably wild cards in this setting, much as they are in canon.

                                                    Can the Tremere be in a Camarilla lead by the Tzimisce? Who knows, but it might be interesting and appropriate if they aren’t. They Tremere could be seen as diablerists and caitiff by the Tzimisce and Camarilla leaders and they are opposed by the Camarilla, so they join the Sabbat. It makes sense considering their rise to power.

                                                    Let’s throw in another change. The more egalitarian Brujah won’t compromise with the new Camarilla ruled by the Tzimisce, who think that the youth should be obedient to the elders and even the more liberal factions are represented by the Ventrue. The Brujah stay with the Sabbat. While we’re at it, let’s have the elder Lasombra defeat their rebellious childer as well and they will join with the Camarilla as well.

                                                    So we have the Camarilla:
                                                    Tzimisce
                                                    Ventrue
                                                    Malkavian
                                                    Lasombra
                                                    Toreador
                                                    Nosferatu
                                                    Gangrel

                                                    And the Sabbat:
                                                    Brujah
                                                    Tremere

                                                        Originally posted by archderd View Post
                                                        on an unrelated noti find it hilarious that most ppl here defending the tzimisce clearly fail to understand the exact same thing the tzimisce failed to understand which caused their downfall.
                                                        I think you fail to understand that leadership and politics are complex, and so people may have different perceptions from yours without being necessarily more or less informed or accurate. Were it so simple we would lived in a far different world IRL.

                                                        My personal impression, first making it clear that arguments on both sides aren't equal either, is that you abide to a too restrict view of what leadership is. You're taking the Ventrue style and goals as the only standard to measure everything and everyone, and then it is obvious that the Tzimisce won't make it. But there are other forms of leadership and rule. You also have an extremely narrow view of the Tzimisce and who they can be, and refuses to accept that other interpretations are possible with the information we get from the books.

                                                        The Tzimisce are the archetypal aristocrats of Europe, especially eastern Europe. They rule through personal ties, family lines, ritual and tradition. And they recognize their key supporters very well, the difference is who their supporters are and how they operate. Traditional aristocrats don't rule through influence over institutions, they rule through influence over people and personal obligation. Extending tendrils over vast distances isn't their style, either, instead strengthening their control over their direct possessions and vassals. Finally, they delegate a lot, relying on others having the direct power as long as they can assure a reasonable degree of loyalty. You can take a look at aristocratic politics even now in Europe and you'll see that the way they do things is really diverse from the way common politicians do.

                                                        So yeah, a Tzimisce led Camarilla would be different from a Ventrue led Camarilla, because they understand and exert power in vastly different ways. The other clans would probably have a far more direct role in controlling mortal institutions because the Tzimisce would expect that instead of seeing them as rivals. They would have more interest in keeping ties to the princes of other cities instead of wanting those princes to be Tzimisce, as long as those Princes follow their traditions. Probably the Traditions of the Camarilla would be a little different, or at least more defined, and there would be guides on proper etiquette for formal meetings.

                                                        And yes, they would have less global power. Global power isn't their thing. Again, this isn't about saying that a Camarilla "ruled" by the Tzimisce would have a ruler as present in everyday affairs as the Ventrue are. Instead it would make itself more present in values like personal loyalty (tempered by the personal hate they frequently have toward the same people) and hospitality, and generally a more archaic aesthetics in the structure. Domains with a proper Tzimisce prince would be less common than domains with a Ventrue Prince in the official setting, and probably with a more structured hierarchy.

                                                        Keep in mind that it wouldn't be the followers of the Path of Metamorphosis. As every clan the Tzimisce isn't a monolith where everyone have the same personality and it wouldn't be the same people creating this structure. Camarilla Tzimisce would be the elders and followers of the old traditions of the clan, not the upstarts that created the Sabbat, Viscissitude or not. Also the Revenant families would have a profound impact, as they would be the primary supporters of Tzimisce influence, and they have a lot of the abilities the clan lacks.

It is probable that the most common Salubri in modern nights would be Warriors, although the changes in social outlook could mean that the Healers would be starting to gain traction since the 19th century.
