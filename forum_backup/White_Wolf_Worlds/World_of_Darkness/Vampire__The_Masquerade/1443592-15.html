            And part of it too is just a massive disconnect between people’s definitions of horror and drama. For example, I consider the whole “locked in a room with a child and starved until I lose control and feed” to be neither horror (evoking fear or dread) nor dramatic (those involved must have some choice to make that affects the outcome). For me such a scene is what I’d call “misery porn.”

            And because my frame of reference is heroism and drama, if I were writing the same scene the protagonist would either find a way to escape with the child (a more heroic outcome) or, if wanting something more tragic, find a way to cripple or kill himself before Hunger would drive him to eat the child. Because just suffering until you give in doesn’t make for an interesting protagonist to me. If I wanted misery porn I’d just turn on the news.

            And I don’t play to be victimized by some force beyond my control; I play characters who fight to maintain control and not harm innocents. With its hunger dice only being completely mitigated by murder, the ONLY enjoyable vampire I could play in V5 is one who, upon learning what they are, took an immediate sunbath.

            As written V5 is utterly unable to deliver an experience I’d find enjoyable while V20’s toolbox approach can. Being a consensualist in V20 is a viable approach. You can keep yourself satiated and therefore in virtually no danger of losing control when feeding. In V5 it’s a recipe for misery as there’s always going to be that one hunger die that will rob you of your agency and leave a consensual vessel drained... and again, that’s when the only viable option for me in terms of character interest is a sunbath.

            Not saying people who like that stuff shouldn’t have options for it, but it’s absolutely not for me.

                Happy to explain why I think V5 politics are better, though.

                Originally posted by adambeyoncelowe View Post
                I don't see how politics is easier in V5. Would you mind elaborating on that? Is it a matter of setting or mechanics? Because, to me, the Hunger mechanic forces a very narrow focus on feeding and blood, which actually lends itself to a street-level focus rather than a political focus. And yes, street-level can be political, but it's only, again, a small fraction of the politicking you could do in V20.

                For example, V20 has super-powerful elders, so you are quite often forced to think really cleverly about how to take an opponent down or make them lose prestige in your city. Games can also have a global as well as a street-level focus quite easily, which expands the politicking.

                If anything, the kind of politics in V5 is more about turf wars but otherwise *avoiding* the politics.
                In terms of both but in terms of mechanics, I think that people who attempt to state that the Hunger Mechanics ruins politics just flat out are doing it wrong. They've let their own interpretation of the rules get in the way of actually running the game and the politica are not in any way shape or form affected the way they think.

                Mechanically, just a few of the benefits that I think have been helped by the game are:

                * The adjustments to Mental Disciplines that emphasize more freedom for vampires in roleplay over Disciplines.
                * Rouse checks limiting the use of abilities on fellow vampires to dramatically appropriate moments.
                * Loresheets allowing the player characters to be neck deep in the politics of the game from the get go.
                * The transformation to appearance and how it functions being no longer a Attribute sink.
                * The de-emphaziing of powerful Elder Disciplines and focus much more on prestige and alliances.
                * The bigger focus on coterie-power and shared backgrounds.
                * Feeding changes mean that the Tradition of Domain is now something much more important to fight over when it was previously the ignored Tradition.
                * CbN and Cults of the Blood Gods also give other great optional rules sets.

                Lore wise, the differences are also beneficial for politics:

                * The allowance of Ancilla and Neonates to higher positions in the setting.
                * The conflict between Anarchs and Camarilla in the same city.
                * The limitation of Elder and Methuselah power, which I don't necessarily AGREE with but makes for more exciting politics.
                * The rise of cults and hidden sect allegiance.
                * Cities now can have a lot more variety in their structure and makeup.

                I'm gonna be honest, yeah, a lot of my praise is, "I think Requiem was always the better social game and V5 is now more like that." But it really was. However, it combines the best of V:TM with V:TR and the results are impressive.

                Originally posted by Damian May View Post

                Fully Agree.

                We gave up on ever going to Elysium in our games as the combination of MCs, BFs, Willpower Damage etc. made interactions with other vampires a losing proposition where social damage was certain, mental damage extremely likely and any plans you made could be randomly sidetracked when your nature betrayed you. If SPCs used the same rules as PCs the place would be a bloodbath every time.
                Really, I would have thought the Willpower benefits far outweigh the costs because if you can reroll a particularly bad dice in social situations you can avoid a lot of the horribly embarrassing situations you can in others.

                It's another reason I like the revision to combat as Willpower expenditures are great ways to make sure embarrassing failures are not going to disrupt the flow of a story.
