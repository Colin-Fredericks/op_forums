    The good thing about CoD's 2nd ed systems is precisely that they're not ultimately linked to morality. It's a subtle distinction that makes a huge difference in contextualizing what your rolls and eventual loss means. They're about your ability to cope with your condition and remain functional in society (yours or mortal). Each "Integrity" trait works differently because what it means to become dysfunctional will change for each splat.

    For normal humans, it is coping with Trauma, for Vampires it is being able to pass as Human, which is a very nice twist. No more having a high Humanity score means being a good person, or believing you're entitled to be a monster means you need to abandon Humanity. You can believe in your own goodness and strive to be good even at a low score, and you can be a despicable monster and still want high Humanity to be a wolf in sheep's clothes.

    Otherwise Morality will inevitably fall in this same place again and again. It may be fun for some people to just be able to reinforce on most chronicles that their own faith is objectively true, and now and then change things for kicks. But for someone that has to constantly deal with obnoxious claims to absolute morality irl, using religion to demonize others, to justify their prejudices and to deny others their own merits and say that good morals actually only come from their god, facing this kind of absolutism in a game is abhorrent.

    Now, with how the game actually developed with Paths and Roads, this use of several Morality Systems to try to curb the Beast is also a very interesting option that acquired its own history in the setting, and I think that has some value. In this, I agree that the best for VtM may not be to just apply VtR Humanity, but instead use DAV Roads. And then you can even keep the idea of Humanity becoming a default due to the rise of Humanism as a common moral background in the last centuries, which would make it less an objective moral system and more of a sign of the times.

    Mechanically I think the V5 system has a better potential than the strict Hierarchy from previous editions, but for now the Roads work better, V5's Humanity still needs a lot of polishing (and maybe becoming even more Road-like).

            Experience plays a lot into it, but you don't really need much to apply the main gist of it:
            • Keep in mind that Humanity isn't the only way, although it is the default and more common way;
              ​
            • You don't need to understand every Path, only the Path you'll use, from the perspective of the actual character;
              ​
            • The regular Path Follower isn't either a shiny paragon of the Path nor a knowledgeable sage of its philosophy;
              ​
            • If a player wants to follow a Path, let them do the hard work for you in researching and explaining it, then check;
              ​
            • Paths are complex, but it just means that followers also make mistakes and disagree, feel free to make mistakes;
              ​
            • Just keep in mind they exist, really. As background element they'll help you a lot without any need for exploration.
              ​

            This kind of work is manageable when you get the trick of simply not trying to grasp it all at once. We have a tendency to assume that we need to understand something comprehensively to apply it on our games, but actually just throwing in bits and pieces as they get interesting to use goes a long way.

                    Possibly gaining derangements from losing Humanity was a think for all of VtM 1e to V20, and was in VtR 1e as well.

                    Having Humanity zero being a fail state (like a character dying) is something worth considering how effective it is, but even if you want characters to be able to recover from Humanity 0, it needs to be a severe enough condition that PCs are rightfully scared of it rather than it being inconvenient. It shouldn't be a ditch that you accidentally fall into and then have to deal with the relatively minor hassle of pulling yourself up.

                    While there's a lot of reasons people ignore Beast: the Primordial from the CofD, it's Integrity trait (Satiety) is actually a really good model for alternatives to the Humanity approach. Hitting Satiety 0 is something you can recover from, and it's a fairly pressing issue because you're dying slowly while you're there. But it takes a considerable effort to do, and you're stuck without access to a lot of things that would help considerably as Satiety is also your fuel for a lot of your powers. It's an "Oh Shit!" moment to be stuck spending your last Satiety, because it's still really serious even if not instantly lose your character so.

                    It's also worth considering that if you keep Humanity linked to morality, the whole "gain a derangement" thing is actually really... not cool. That's why CofD 2e stopped that It's important to separate out doing "immoral" things from causing real world psychological conditions in people. You can keep some level of this (doing immortal things is frequently traumatic, and trauma has clear mental health concerns), but you need to be pretty careful with it.
