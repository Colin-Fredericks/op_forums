            I think it's fair to say that VtM is as much (or more) "horror-themed" than "horror" itself. At least, whether or not it qualifies as "horror" is going to depend on how you engage with it.

            A lot of the "personal horror" that it's supposed to invoke only comes if you really embrace (a rare intended pun!) the notion of vampirism being a curse: that (near-)immortality is depressing, that seeing regular people, even the ones you love, as "kine" is monstrous, and you treat the loss of your identity to the feral savagery of the Beast as a matter of "when" rather than "if." If you ignore or undersell these aspects, make it easier to play as a "friendly neighbourhood vampire" or really get into the idea of playing as a "monster," then it's much easier to treat the whole thing as a power-fantasy, which isn't especially horrifying when you're the one experiencing it.

            I think the concept of Video Game Cruelty Potential from TV Tropes has some relevance to the discussion, in the sense that people playing a game often put some emotional distance between themselves and the downright awful things that they sometimes do to the NPCs populating the fictional world. The World of Darkness feels less like horror if you treat it more like a generic power-fantasy RPG and don't put a lot of thought into what it would be like being on the receiving end of the sorts of things that a vampire can (and often will) do to other people.

            Imagine encountering something like a Szlachta or a Vozhd in a dark alley. Not your VtM PC, *you*. It's funny to imagine a Tzimisce turning a person into a chair while they're still alive when it's just a game, but what if *you* came home one evening and saw that one of your loved ones had been twisted into some horrible obscenity of exposed bone and muscle, clearly still alive despite how desperately they must be wishing for death...

            Along the same lines, imagine being chased through the woods at night by some *thing* that turned itself into a wolf right in front of your very eyes, like something out of a horror movie. Imagine being pursued by this creature, stalking you with a malevolent and human-like intelligence despite its beastly shape.

            Or being at home one night with one of your loved ones, and watching them just *spontaneously combust* in front of you, screaming in agony until the fire finally manages to kill them, and having no idea of how or why such a thing might have possibly happened.

            A lot of horror comes from suspense and helplessness. The fear of the unknown, or of inescapable cruelty and predation. VtM gives you less of this experience by default because you are (often) not the victim, but the monster. While there may be unknowns, you are less likely to be completely ignorant of the nature of what threatens you, even from an IC perspective. And to the extent that you are participating in a game with some expectation of giving you a "fair chance," you can more likely expect to be able to fight back somehow.

            I think it may be that part of why it feels so absurd to you is because you're viewing it primarily from the abstract perspective of a player who can put these things into his character's toolkit, and not imagining it from the personal perspective of a normal person suddenly having their life ripped apart by these strange and mysterious powers.
