            I found the books very variable. In my opinion the books go from best to worst as follows:

            Top tier
            Chicago by Night
            Constantinople by Night
            Transylvania by Night

            What puts something in the top tier is that the books present a bigger story about the world than merely the conflict between a group of elders in a given city. All three of the above books were massively influential for how the game developed, and with good reason.

            Well, I Liked It
            New Orleans by Night

            This had some of the best art out of any of these books, a few interesting characters, with enough in the way of interesting and unusual to make them interesting. Nothing to make it truly stand out, but I seem to have liked this more than anyone else.

            Decent Enough
            DC by Night
            Iberia by Night
            Mexico City by Night
            Milwaukee by Night
            New York by Night

            These are books that were perfectly fine, but just didn't have anything that interesting to make them stand out. New York by Night managed to be adequate, whilst also being quite boring (which, for a book about New York, was quite a feat).

            Could Do Better
            Cairo by Night
            Berlin by Night
            Dark Alliance: Vancouver
            Dark Colony
            Los Angeles by Night
            Montreal by Night

            These are books that weren't terrible, but had some significant flaws that should have been worked through in editing. Cairo by Night was rambling (and had at least one historic individual as a vampire, which was supposed to not happen in the revised era). Dark Alliance: Vancouver was developed as a Werewolf: the Apocalypse book, and doesn't quite get kindred society right. Interestingly enough, whilst most people hate Berlin by Night, I don't think it is irredeemable; you could use it as the basis of a decent chronicle with a few tweaks (such as replacing Rasputin, and all the Nazi vampires).

            What Were People Thinking?
            Jerusalem by Night
            London by Night
            San Francisco by Night

            How can you get a book about Victorian London wrong? Well, London by Night was written by a team of authors who seemed to have no interest in a) London and b) the Victorian era. Terrible maps, (mostly) terrible characters, and a dollop of Mary Poppins. Meanwhile, it would require an entire thread to outline the problems.

            Worst of all, however, is San Francisco by Night which seems to be mired in racist tropes about Asian settlement in California. I don't know why that book didn't earn more opprobrium from readers...

                            Well, I'll go on the byzantine train and say that Constantinople by Night is the Chicago by Night of the VtDA books. It offers such a plethora of characters, plots, avenues, tragedies and triumphs, alternate histories and interesting ideas. It also offers a new political system that wasn't present in any other material till then (and ever since). The plot of the Dream is great, and one of the best in the lore, that had amazing repercussions (and is way better than all the Brujah-Carthage stuff that is always mentioned and never explained, and just turns into Baali go birl).
                            Though I think the V20 Dark Ages Companion sequel is very weak. Basically everything is the same since the 4th crusade, except some new characters that mostly fill old or already-filled roles.


                            Dark Ages France By Night is also fabulous, and I also regret that is only in French (but I also regret that so many books were never brought to my language). Dark Ages France for vampire is way more interesting than any other area bar Constantinople. Lots of intrigue, mystery, exploration and variety.

                            Transylvania By Night is... cool, but it is spread WAY too thin. We have an entire book trying to cover with detail 1/3 of Europe and going only by single characters, instead of structures or societies. And for a book about "Tzimisce land" we get very little Tzimisce in comparison. Almost no Voivodes, surely no content for how the Voivodates work, or examples of Knezes. So cool ideas, but too pulverized for my liking, focusing on characters that many times are unrelated (which should be the basis of any By Night). Showing the Balkans, or just Hungary and Romania should've been more than enough.

                            On the other hand, I love Dark Ages Europe. The way it presents the regions and princedoms is very concise, and let's each region have a web of relations, alliances and oppositions, instead of a loose gathering of isolated individuals. It advanced a lot of plots, created new scenarios, and gave each of its covered cities and countries good themes. I only wish we had more of it.

                            Iberia By Night I thought was hot garbage. It should've been one of the most interesting regions, with Brujah, Toreador, Lasombra and Banu Haqim politics going wild. But it mostly is "Muslisms are Haquimites" and "Christians are Lasombra", period. There are no freaking Brujah characters to speak of (and even worst, they stick a damn Ventrue as a great general for the Lasombra, out of nowhere), and Toreador basically a passing fad. No plots , just Amicti Noctis deciding "ok, we'll go full-Christian", and no Banu Haqim putting the Laws of Haqim first (like Ur-Shulgi would many centuries later). There is no theme, no struggle, dilemma or campaign to take part in. It would just turn into War board game, which isn't functional for Vampire (just try the third chapter of Under the Black Cross to see how vampire open-war is very unfun to play). Besides, lots of important Lasombra characters aren't even in the book (like Eliser de Polanco and Tercio Bravo).

                            Damascus By Night I agree that is really underwhelming. Nothing in it justifies using Damascus, nor it has anything to do with Ashirra. The plot could've been in any city, and at any era nonetheless. For a book about Ashirra it has very little to do with it, and it does not use a venerable and great city like Damascus with any propriety. It's just the old plot of "degenerate Elder is actually a puppet of a Baali", which I tire heavily every single time I see it.


                            So Yeah, lot's of Dark Ages books. I'm running Giovanni Chronicles and Transylvania Chronicles, besides a Byzantine solo-player game.
                            And when my group play modern era Masquerade, We play South America, so I mostly read By Night books of the era for inspiration and structure, not for use.

                            But I agree that the Chicago By Night are very well realised, and for good or bad, sticked as the default way of Camarilla princedoms (which for me isn't that bad, Camarilla is supposed to be a sect after all, so a reproduciable structure only makes sense).
                            And that LA did the same, only mostly just bad, for the Anarchs. Besides, calling a little weak prince toppling a Second Anarch Revolt is a joke. The first one was a wave of consequence, that popped in many (entire) regions and became a continent-wide matter.


                            New York By Night failed to catch my attention, and I only looked into it after I played the Visual novel games.
                            The Montreal and Milwauke By Night are very interesting in their ways, and give new ideas to the table each, even if some plots are ... unsavory. The Sabbat feels well-realized in them.

                            I didn't read much (I could only read what I translated in google) of the Frankfurt By Night (Rheinesgold), but it also felt like a unique take on vampire politics. While I think Frederick first of all shouldn't be Barbarosa, and even more so, shouldn't be the most proggresive vampire in all Camarilla (he should be at least Brujah), his policies are very different from all other princes and turn Frankfurt into a very different place. Angiwar's stuff is also very cool. Frankfurt and Berlin should butt heads more.

                            Cairo By Night has interesting ideas and political layout, but it could've gone deeper, or been more clear in some parts. I find it a fun resource, and think that it should've been more important in the metaplot, but alas it has been left in the dust. It does fill like the double-layered style city of ancient powers fighting age-old wars, while newer vampires are having their own struggles that indirectly suffer the effects of the War of Ages. What I hate though is the cover, I just don't understand what the hell is going on in it.

                            Strange... When coincidence seems too convenient, I prefer to call it fate.

                                        I've actually been paying a lot of attention to the "by Night" books as part of a research project I was doing about representation in VtM. I don't have anything close to ready to say on the matter yet, but I do have some opinions. I think Damnation City for VtR is a better city book than any "by Night" book because it is more of a toolkit and guide to building cities and not a catalog of cool Kindred, but I digress.

                                        The first and second editions of Chicago by Night are understandably beloved because Chicago was the closest thing to a "default setting" that VtM had, particularly since the sample Gary chronicle from the first two core rulebooks tended to push games across the border. The Menele-Helena conflict set up the Jyhad as the primary driver of history and conflict. I know as a young storyteller back in 92-93, when Vampire was the game du jour among my friends, it encouraged a lot of metaplot that didn't give a lot of agency to the players. Second edition, by blowing up Chicago, attempted to create an instability that gave characters more room to breathe. Personally, I don't think it was successful because there are so goddamn many powerful Kindred running around the city, it was still hard for players to be the main characters of the story. I actually think the 5th Edition Chicago series (CbN5, Let the Streets Run Red, and Chicago Folios) did a decent job of opening up the city and making more room for the players, certainly a better job than CbN2.

                                        All of that said, one of the things that I think many of the later city books struggle with, and a lot of chronicles struggle with as well, is capturing the essence of a city. Instead, they focus on the Kindred as they interact in the Kindred society that developed over the 13/14 years of VtM's main production run. Milwaukee by Night and L.A. by Night at least tried to make the vibes different, even if they didn't perfectly capture the essence of the city.

                                        New York by Night was the most disappointing for me, probably because New York is the city I grew up in the shadow of, went to grad school in, and generally love the most. The vibrancy of the United States' largest and most diverse city was ignored for a focus on the runaway train that was VtM metaplot. It captured the zeitgeist of late-stage Vampire, but the characters and plot really could be placed anywhere and, for me, that is the wrong answer.

                                                    Originally posted by Gryffon15 View Post

                                                    2. This is my most unique take but I’ve got a special fondness for DC by Night. It was one of my first WoD books and I’m from the DC metropolitan area so I’ve got a fondness for the city (it’s far more than just Capital Hill!). The cover is iconic (even if only to me) and makes wonderful use of the Washington monument (or, as I called it as a kid, ‘the big pencil’) as a gothic landmark. Now that said it’s not perfect. Marcus Vitel is a somewhat gonzo concept but his long lasting popularity speaks for itself. Other than him and his brood though, the rest of the city’s vampires are somewhat shaky. Lots of potential though.
                                                    How interesting. I too, as a Washingtonian by birth, have a special fondness for DC by night. Peep my forum handle if you don't believe me.

                                                    First, let me say that I don't think its unusual for a native of a region depicted in a "By Night" or "Rage Across" book to have, shall we say, pointed opinions on its portrayal. It doesn't seem there was any sort of requirement for the writer to have lived in the region for any length of time. And, to be fair, most of these were written before Wikipedia was a thing (or at least widely used). But I still feel like, despite some big hits, DC by Night had several problems.

                                                    The Geography section reads like a tourist guide, with far too much attention on the various federal buildings but far too little attention to the neighborhoods where people actually live. The history section is phenomenal from the Colonial period up through the civil war. After that his interest in, ya know, the actual city of Washington DC seems to have waned. They misses out on a lot of the cities development as a center of (particularly African American) cultural expression and political struggle.

                                                    I don't want to imply the author is, like committing a racism or something. But such disinterest in the cities actual residents has knock on effects elsewhere in the quality of the final product. Simply put, next time I wouldn't have someone from Mechanicsburg VA (300 miles away) write a book about Washington DC.

                                                    In my opinion the fact it skips out on the cities diverse cultural history is made evident by the fact so few of the Vampires feel particularly tied to or part of the city. They are, as Gryffon15 put it, "shaky". Most of them are broad enough vampiric stereotypes they could be slotted into almost any generic fictional ST created city. Ballet dancer Toreador. Serial Killer Malkavian. Drug runner Settite. Le yawn! Even Monica and Cynthia black are just kinda...there.

                                                    No former intelligence agency Nossies? No Toreador with connection with any sort of black cultural product or era from the Chitlins Cricut through to Go-Go Music? No Brujah with any appreciable politics? In DC?!

                                                    That (Malkavian? Ventrue?) chilling in the suburbs pretending to be Bela Legosi because he knows Vitel's secret and wants to be under-estimated was a good one, though. We needed more like that.
