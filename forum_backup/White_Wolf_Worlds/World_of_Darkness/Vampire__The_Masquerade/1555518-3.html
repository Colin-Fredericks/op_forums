                                        Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post
                                        QUOTE=GreatHornedRatkin;n1557468]

                                        Thats arguably worse in terms of attrition. Trying to capture loyalist lasombra (who outnumber you) is difficult enough without drawing the other clan/bloodlines in.
                                        To be honest I'm not sure the Sabbat Lasombra outnumber the non-Sabbat Lasombra
                                        More like the other way around actually
                                        If you look at the factions:

                                        The Corsairs (Lasombra that have answered the call of the sea that seems to be inherent to their blood. They spend most of their time on the waves, often masquerading as pirates, and some never come on dry land) are never really been Sabbat or only a handfull of joined, they were independent or nominally Sabbat at best. Not Camarilla or not numerous in the Camarilla either but Alfonso López for example was an infamous Lasombra antitribu Pirate, renowned for his naval strikes against the Sabbat tho' he has been seen lastly in the 1960s

                                        The Faithful (Lasombra who draw their traditions from the once strong hold of the Lasombra within the Catholic Church. Ardent followers of God and believers in their own damnation alike, these individuals seek to fulfill the will of God in this world) I think had presence everywhere. Monçada is a good example

                                        The Kings and Queens of Shadow, Lasombra who took part in mortal institutions, exerting influence, gathering resources and in general keeping the mortal population unaware of the vampires among them always distanced themselves from regular Sabbat activities to maintain their Humanity. They were nominally Sabbat or not at all

                                        The Children of the Abyss, the Lasombra, whofollowed the ancient traditions of Abyss Mysticism payed only lip service to the Sabbat as the sect provides them with the means to pursue their arcane art.

                                        The Transhumanists, Lasombra who revel in their predatory nature and seek to perfect their undead state are a puzzling wlyd card since they quantify their various powers, work out taxonomies and otherwise seek to reduce vampirism to data. Many have a keen interest in mortal technology, particular in those that augment the human body, while others seek a perfect state without reliance on external tools. To be fair the Sabbat at best was an interesting experiment to catalog to them in my humble oppinion, not something they followed faithfully

                                        The Fatalists are those Lasombra that have stopped believing in freedom of Will. Many claim that their blood ties them directly to the elders and methuselahs of their Clan and these beings are in charge of the destinies of their progeny - this is everything the Sabbat is not. And more of a Camarilla thing...

                                        The Doomed were the exhibitionist lot among the Clan that do not believe in the value of secrecy and aimed to gain immediate and direct control of their environment, often without concerns to the kine. Many Lasombra killed those Clan mates on sight. They are... I don't know to be honest. But already member who had a target on their head

                                        The Black Angels also called Angellis Ater were the Lasombra who reveled in their state as servants of Satan and enemies of God, practicing Satanism and other socially repulsive things. Infernalist more often than not. Not something the Sabbat tolerated. Neither the Camarilla for that matter. Or you know, anyone else with a working braincell - Baali wanabees

                                        The only who were truly Sabbat if you ask me, wete the Crusaders who viewed the Sabbat as a religious and military organization almost to the exclusion of the Sabbat as an ideology of freedom.

                                        Then you had the "real" Lasombra antitribu or a faction led by Montano who already had a cozy place in the Cammarila and the Qabilat al-Khayal the Lasombra of the Ashirra
                                        And if you want to go a mile, Clan Lasombra Trilogy 2: Shadows mentions Khanom Mehr, childe of the Lasombra Antediluvian whose name is possible Persian, Indian or Thai origin and many belive the Lasombra from the East toward Asia are likely descendants of her.

                                        So all and all?
                                        I don't think the Clan as a whole was Sabbat to begin with, or loyal to the Sabbat and not jumping boat as soon as anything remotely interesting or better presented itself except the Crusaders​ and lets say half of the Faithful and maybe some Doomed ?​

                                        Of course it is just my interpretation but I think holds up
