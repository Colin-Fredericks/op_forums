    Originally posted by Manfr View Post

    The very same edition that gave justice to the Arabic and Traveller-coded Clans gave us Confederate Brujah. V20 also maintained the very same line about Nazi Skinhead Brujah, if I'm not mistaken.
    A quick check on the V20 pdf doesn't turn up anything that directly mentions nazi skinhead Brujah. The only possible in they would have is in the "embrace someone of the opposite beliefs" passage, which is explicitly done as a punishment.

    My impression is that a segment of the community was trying to justify its extreme rejection of "new hands" leading the franchise, by framing it into some sort of antifascist resistance, to deflect criticism about gatekeeping.
    Or it could be that the "new hands" are actively courting an old way of doing things that doesn't really help the games go in new places. And so it is - the cores are all chock full of one true wayism, making them the gatekeepers of a new era because they mandate "street level" play and focus and offer nothing else. It's just... gone, fwwp, cut off completely.

    I'd say that your considerations are probably more appropriate for the dynamic Elders of Requiem, but to a point they are still valid in VtM, perhaps not universally among Clans (we are told, for instance, that academic sexism was well alive in the higher ranks of Clan Tremere).

    Still, I'd say the difference between Elders and Neonates here it's very relevant. Neonates (which are the default characters you are going to play in V1, V2, Revised and V5) are brought into unlife full of their prejudices and life experiences, and may easily revel in their newfound powers. Lots of character concepts are based around these ideas.
    Neonates then get eaten by the system if they don't let go. Or they get sent on an innocuous "errand" they don't realize is a suicide mission, and go from undead to just dead. Again, not because the elders are high-minded idealists, but because they have "flexible ethics" and are set on a goal.
