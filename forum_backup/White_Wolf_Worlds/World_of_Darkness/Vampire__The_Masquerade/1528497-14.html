                        Cross of course is barely sought out by anyone to talk about anything because they still have problems with Thin Bloods, but since her grip on the Pier is currently tight enough to hold her own against everyone else with just Thin Blood vampires at her side, I'd say she's still stronger than Abrams as a Baron.

                        Other than those four, there used to be other three important factions whose current status we don't know. LA had one of the greatest Set temples in the US, but it fell some years before Bloodlines, still the Ministry may have numbers in and around the city. The Giovanni had one house that may or may not still stand. And the Sabbat had a priscus that had nothing to do with the infiltration the Fledgling wiped out.

                        Mages and Demons also have a very strong presence there, so in a crossover game there may be all sorts of interactions between splats. LA is one of the hottest spots for the Ascension War this side of the Gauntlet, with a lot of factions coming and going all the time. I'd say most Mages in the US and a bunch in Canada and Mexico will probably go to LA at one point of another in their lives if they survive more than 5 years. The local Infernal Court is, or used to be, the biggest one in the world, and the city was the focal point of the most high profile activities of Lucifer himself and two or three Earthbound.

                                    I particularly think Montreal and Ciudad del México are telling because in their fuck you attitude they didn't just disrespected fans and did poor writing, they effectively lost a golden opportunity to make really interesting stories.

                                    They focused on just making them Camarilla to have them as if they had always been Cam, and as a result they're pretty bland and generic when they could have been unique unstable colonies with plots and roles for PCs that don't exist elsewhere.

                                    Picture it: the Second Inquisition just had in the city one of their greatest, thorough victories. It has no surviving vampire power structure and any survivors are left in a danger zone where they must keep a low profile and extra care to survive. Even after a few years, while the SI has moved on it's safe to assume their vigilance in the city is still overwhelming. It's in this context that the Camarilla moves in.

                                    Local Camarilla is dominant, with no challenge to its claim, but that's not the same as an easy time. There's no old, well established influence in the city structures, and usual main targets are off limits as SI watchdogs. Local vampires are not powerful and stable, but adventurers or refugees fleeing their luck elsewhere to try the opportunities of a frontier colony style place. It is not a stable power, it's a tentative occupation of a still fresh battlefield.

                                    Sabbat vampires still wander around. They're under more pressure as the SI is likely to have more info on them, so they're in survival mode and not looking for a fight. Yet. But they're still older in the area and know more about it than the Camarilla newcomers. They're cut from the sect at large, or maybe acting as observers and spies, but compensate the lack of direct sect support with this greater level of integration in the city.

                                    Recognizing they history of the setting and the actual development of their own plot would get us something new and interesting to play.
