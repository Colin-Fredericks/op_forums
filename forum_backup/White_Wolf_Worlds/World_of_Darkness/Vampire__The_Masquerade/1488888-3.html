                The idea of being a "purist" for the lore of a non-interactive form of media boggles my mind. Being a purist about RPG lore? OK, I can get that, RPGs are a collaborative media and being a purist about lore is a good way to keep the group on the same page about things.

                Being a purist about novels just reduces the number of novels in the world. Most novels are the product of someone saying, "I really love story X, but I want to do my version of it instead." Even people like Tolkien had that as a motivation for all his world building: he wanted to take old stories from his younger years and do his own spin on them. Shakespeare's plays are full of retellings of older stories originally done by someone else. Etc.

                Reworking old stories into new contexts and new perspectives is an essential part of art and culture. Why deny yourself the chance to enjoy old stories anew as someone else takes a stab at them?

                Even more so when we're talking about an adaptation from novel to TV show, where changes from the source material were always going to be necessary because novels aren't screenplays designed to be read on their own and to be directly adapted to film/TV/etc.

                It's fine to say, "I have a lot of nostalgia for the older version, but little interest in new material in the same IP, so I'm not going to bother." It has nothing to do with some sort of artistic "purity" that older versions supposedly have (which they didn't because they're always knocking off something else anyway).

                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                            On my end, it's more like the fact that I don't understand why they keep changing stuff while claiming to like the material.
                            Every spend time with fanfic writers? Because... that's exactly what fanfic is all about: loving something so much you want to write your own version of it, which changes you would think would expand it in a fashion you think other fans would like.

                            Fanfic starts with "fan" for a reason. It's not haterfic.

                            It might not be your scene... it isn't my scene... but I know enough fanfic people that clearly love their fandoms, and then rewrite those stories all over the place.

                            The Vampire Chronicles deserve to have a faithful adaptation and every episode is more and more changes that aren't remotely similar to the characters in the books.
                            Why does it deserve anything? It's a set of novels that already got published and made Rice a lot of money. It probably can't get a direct adaptation because... the original books are actually not great for adaptation without significant alteration. Also Rice doesn't like a lot of the early stuff in it anymore and might want some changes and this is the closest she can get to signing off to her equivalent of Lucas's edit to the original SW trilogy. GRRM approved the ending of GoT... that didn't really make fans happier with it..

                            "Faithful" is always subjective (is the MCU faithful to the comics because it's faithful to the spirit of the characters, or unfaithful for taking huge liberties with the source material?).

                            [quote]If you're going to do a Louis who loves Lestat, isn't guilty about murder, and tortured by being a monster then why even call Interview
                            with a Vampire?
                            Louis loves Lestat on some level the whole time in the novels, he just eventually figures out how toxic their relationship is, he gets over needing to kill the feed quicker in the book than the movie, and according to Lestat, Louis completely self-aggrandized his story in the first novel too make himself sound like the good one of their relationship and play down all the stuff they did together.

                            We basically got the adaptation you seem to want already: the film, that played in this direction harder than the original book did (you know, they changed stuff).

                            AnubisXy's complaint? That I understand. Even if that's not what really happened, we've had so many things where an original story gets shoved into a franchise for sales purposes and we can tell, that you don't want to come off feeling that way whether it's just how you're handling changes, or because that's really what you're doing.

                            Pretty much all of Gen X and Millenials (Zoomers might not quite be there yet) have had some experience with "slap [franchise label] on this original story and we'll double profits, so screw new titles!" and seen how it only made things worse. It's a critique about a specific feeling the show is giving off, not a flat complaint about "change for the sake of change," when change is inherent in adaptations.

                            Why do you think you can do better than the author?
                            1) Most present authors think they can do better than the past versions of themselves. So I have trouble with any assumption that authors are inherently infallible here and you can't actually change for the better. Stephen King is notoriously snarky about film adaptations of his work, because he knows they're going to change stuff he doesn't think should get changed. However, when a change gets made that he likes? He's effusive in his praise. Because yeah, it does happen. The ending of The Mist is probably the most famous example, where he's been quoted as wishing he'd come up with that ending instead of the original.

                            So it's not really unreasonable to think that the original author can't be questioned about these things.

                            2) When it comes to adaptations? Frequently original authors don't understand the new medium and want to keep or change things that don't really work that way. The GoT point above is a great example. I'm pretty sure the last few seasons of GoT would have been a lot better if GRRM had just said, "the shows are going to be their own things to end in a way that compliments the show instead of worrying about how I plan on ending it." When GoT's team announced GRRM gave them the outline for the rest of story, and GRRM went so far as to say that if he doesn't finish the books, the shows should be considered canon. That put a lot of pressure on the show to do things that just didn't work right for live action fantasy drama.

                            So yeah, TV/film/etc. writers think they can do better than the original creator because frequently they can in the limited scope of adaptations.

                                                    Going to also urge people that are reluctant to give the show a watch. I'm not sure how everyone can get to watch a behind the scenes featurette that was on AMC but the creators of this show love, love, love the books. They were in constant contact with Anne Rice and her son, who is a very good writer in his own right and very protective of his Mom's work.                                                         The idea of being a "purist" for the lore of a non-interactive form of media boggles my mind. Being a purist about RPG lore? OK, I can get that, RPGs are a collaborative media and being a purist about lore is a good way to keep the group on the same page about things.
                                                        I don't see why you are struggling with it, you already seem to understand the answer when applying it to an RPG. The lore keeps writers making works in that setting on track in regards to how to make a work that belongs in that setting. For an example of what happens when they don't just take a look at The Last Jedi by Rian Johnson. He disregarded the lore of the setting in favor of making the story he wanted to tell and in doing so created massive plot holes that rippled back in time and introduced inconsistencies in already existing works of the setting. The biggest one being 'if using a hyper space jump suicide attack is so ridiculously effective why has nobody ever used them in other stories in the setting or even so much as considered using them? Why didn't the CSA have suicide droids piloting glorified missiles through Republic fleets using hyper space ramming attacks? Why didn't the rebels just have some ships suicide ram the first death star?" and so on and so forth. That is why sticking to the lore is important.

                                                        Of course, that doesn't apply to a reboot. In that case it is basically 'I like it the way it is and the more changes you make the more chances you have to transform it into something I don't like'.

                                                        Being a purist about novels just reduces the number of novels in the world. Most novels are the product of someone saying, "I really love story X, but I want to do my version of it instead." Even people like Tolkien had that as a motivation for all his world building: he wanted to take old stories from his younger years and do his own spin on them. Shakespeare's plays are full of retellings of older stories originally done by someone else. Etc.
                                                        You will notice that instead of doing something like 'A retelling of Beowulf by Tolkien'* he went out and made a completely new world and story all his own inspired by these older works. The idea that there would be fewer works in the world if people inspired by Tolkien went and made 'the duke of the circles' instead of 'Lord of the Rings as reimagined by random dude guy' is laughable. Very rare seeming is the adaption that makes significant changes while feeling respectful of the work its adapting as opposed to it being a sign they had no faith in an original story putting butts in seats and so hastily spray painted over an unrelated story with an existing popular IP without the slightest care for making it really fit as an adaption of that more popular IP.

                                                        *Yes I know he did do a translation of it from old English to modern English, not the same thing as a re-imagining.
