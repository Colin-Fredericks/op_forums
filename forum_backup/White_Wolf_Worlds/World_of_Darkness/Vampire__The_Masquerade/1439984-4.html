        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
        I don't see why there's any desire to make Vampire: The Masquerade less plot armory than other series. What attracts people to the story is not the threat of death to the main characters but the romanticism and mystique of the undead as well as their complicated politics.

        A vampire will survive 900 things that would kill a human protagonist so one would think that WOD characters would have a lot MORE plot armor.
        The threat of death isn't for the viewers, it's for the writers.

        If you really think about a storyline and realize that the protagonist could have stayed home and the antagonist, if left to their own devices, have defeated themselves, the audience is going to call BS. You can't have an appreciation for the politics in vampire if they make no sense or have no value as written.

        Most vampire politics are written in reverse order. Basically a writer wants a specific story to happen so they reverse engineer some politics to push the characters in a given direction. When doing world building, especially in an detail oriented book or tv series, you have to build the world, build the systems, build the politics, then only at the end you model how the characters interact with all of that. At which point all to often you realize that the characters probably wouldn't survive if they acted as they normally would or hadn't learned certain life lessons.

        This sort of shoddy modeling doesn't come across in the table top game because the players are in the moment and heavily invested due to it being their characters and their plans that they get to see interact with the world. With a tv series you might like or even love a character, but your third person perspective of the storyline unfolding makes the flaws that much more apparent. Hence why a lot of stuff feels like it falls so flat when adapted to the screen from table top games or even video games.

                    Originally posted by Thoth View Post
                    For my two cents, one of the major problems with adapting a game to screen be it large or small, is that what that games means changes from person to person and player to player.

                    The long parade of Dungeons & Dragons movies and TV attempts over the decades make it pretty clear that you can't hope to get close to a good product without a core storyline that everyone loves. Imagine Game of Thrones with soap opera level writing would be a high level of success, and thus far no one has even gotten close to that good.

                    Thinking back to vampire TV series like Vampire Diaries and Forever Knight. You can't even agree on what a vampire should or should not be as far as tone or substance. Kindred the Embrace as previously mentioned was little more than 90210 with fangs. Their interpretation changed things to such a degree that ghouls and blood bonding didn't exist, Assamites only work for Prince grade or higher, vampires can day walk as long as they recently fed, the brujah were all mobsters, the gangrel acted like brujah, the nosferatu had Protean and Thaumaturgy, the list of adjustments keep going..... Needless to say there are many pitfalls to over come when it comes to any IP and V:tM has many inherent issues.

                    Because V:tM can have any kind of tone, that means that which ever tone they pick for the show ends up excluding the fans who favor the other tones. Focus on the interpersonal drama and you get Vampire Diaries. Focus on the moral and societal decay and you get True Detective season one, focus on combat and you get the Blade tv series house of C'thon.

                    In other words if tolkien had this sort of adaptation problem the hobbits could be interpreted as anything from their current selves to being middle earths version of Pablo Escobar opening up the middle earth markets for "old toby" pipe weed to dominate the party scene. Then add in a writer who wants to use the hobbits as political statement for some hippie commune for a back to the earth working the farm vibe, after which some marketing directive will come down that the plot needs to be more inclusive and that Focus Groups of people who have never read the books want to see more inclusivity and representation because that is always the take away from such groups.

                    Perhaps I am being old and cynical, but then again if you believe the leaks regarding the development of Vampire Bloodlines 2, such issues seem to be inherent.

                    It seems to turn into a garbage fire that is utterly divorced from the source material so reliably and so fast, you could probably set your watch to it.

                    I don't know if this is a good idea but perhaps taking inspiration from the works of Lars von Trier would aid in how to portray adult subject matter in ways not done before in vampire media. Granted this would mean this show would have to get really difficult to watch with a bunch of graphic violence and sex stuff which would turn some people off.

                    I have reservations with Netflix but the hard truth is that they have 40 million subscribers, it's unfair they have that many subs but amazon dosn't but that's the reality. This hypothetical show has to be on Netflix

                    Also, I would go the MCU route and make this Vampire show one of 3 WoD shows set in the same city, with the other 2 being Werewolf and Mage (my favourite). Have it set in LA because Bloodline was in LA and Bloodlines is the most popular WoD media with the most popular WoD characters.

                    for antagonists Vampire should have Hunters, Werewolf should have D.N.A or Pentex, Mage should have the Technocratic Union. when the enevitable 4th show comes and brings characters from all 3 shows together there has to be a common enemy that threatens them all. Threat Null imediatly comes to mind, but of couse WoD has a large rouges gallary. The big Thanos villan could just as easily be Antidiluvians, Earthbound, Nephandi, a pissed off Incarna, Imbued, Natural Disaster, Cthullu, or anything really.

                    There's advantages to doing live action and there's advantages to doing animated, but I think you can get the best of both worlds if you do Rotoscoped animation ala "Undone" by Raphael BobWaksburg. Have it look like the Illustrations by John Van Fleet and a lot of people will leave their living rooms satisfied.
