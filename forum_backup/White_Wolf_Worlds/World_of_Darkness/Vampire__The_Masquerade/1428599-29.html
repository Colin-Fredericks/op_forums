    If, mechanically, I can no longer imitate others or create a series of alternative personas to deal with many mortals with obfuscate 3, that has very serious implications as to the story of my character. .
    If I had four in one discipline and four in another, and they're merged and I have to make sacrifices and cut away abilities that I use extensively, that has very serious implications to the story of my character.

    If thaumaturgy goes from an expensive array of powers I've sunk 200 xp into, and then I need to cut it down to five powers and maybe beg the ST for a lot of rituals that no longer have the same functionality, that's has pretty serious story implications. If Thaumaturgy goes from 'exciting' to 'just another discipline' as it has then that's a pretty big impact on the story.

    The 'everyone choses their powers' thing is just a completely different thing to 'everyone gets the same 1-5 powers' both in mechanics and in thematic connotations. The former says 'you are powerful and special' the latter says 'you are weak and insignificant and there are many more like you'. The later alone gave us incentive to diablerize our way down to 7th gen where we could transcend such limitations and have our own thing. But now the potential uniqueness of 6+ disciplines belong in the 1-5 rating.

    Fluff wise, chimestry worked on a completely different principle to obfuscate. Obfuscate just muddled minds to fool people. Chimestry made stuff out of illusory matter for everyone to see. Having them the same thing mechanically ruins that, and in turn ruins the occult/philosophy/religion kinda thing you build out of such powers.

        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

        I don't really think mechanics have ever reflected the in-story reality of the setting.

        Story trumps all.
        As an author you know stories without structure (i.e. rules) are shit. This goes double for fantasy (and VtM is urban fantasy) where consistent rules for the supernatural are key to maintaining suspension of disbelief.

        Even soft magic systems have rules that keep them from being the resolution to the story’s climax (ex. Captain Marvel’s powers are pretty much pure soft magic; the climax was her overcoming the conditioning/inhibitor; her kicking everyone’s ass and blowing the crap out of the Kree fleet was pure catharsis... conversely, Aladdin doesn’t beat Jafar by wielding the genie’s soft magic power, but by tricking Jafar into making a wish that, by the established rules would trap him in a lamp).

        So when one rule has been established and then at a later point you break it (without first setting up the loophole earlier in the story) it’s just bad storytelling. V5 changes a number of rules relative to prior editions (your V20 Thinblood suddenly uses alchemy instead of specific disciplines... Chimerstry is suddenly mind tricks instead of physical illusions) so trying to sell it as a continuation of the same story (versus a new story in a very similar universe) is, frankly, bad storytelling.

        If you answered the question “Why can’t the protagonist use [previously established ability] to solve the problem?” with “Because I changed the rules so he can no longer do that,” you’d be righly called out for crap writing.

        By contrast “I wanted to explore a ‘what if’ scenario so this story takes place in a different universe... the protagonist could never do that here so now we get to explore how they would overcome a similar problem using different tools” at least sounds like you give a damn about presenting a coherent story where solutions aren’t just pulled out of the writer’s ass in the name of “drama.”

                    How many of Taggie's complaints about V5 become moot (beyond the "I just don't like how this mechanic/change works" which is just personal preference) if its just acknowledged that V5 is NOT the V20 universe? How many of the things CTPhipps likes are lost (beyond "It MUST be the same exact universe even though every group plays in their own variation of it anyway") if its acknowledged that V5 mostly mirrors the history of the V20 universe except for the necessary changes to make the present V5 lore coherant?

                    I'd argue "most, if not all complaints" and "little, if any, losses" which is why I think its the most harmonious answer towards avoiding things like edition warring on the boards.
