    Originally posted by jamiemalk View Post

    One of the best things about Humanity was that it was so unreasonable. It didn't care if killing the bad man saved 50 people, you still took the check. It was glorious...
    Eh, I much preferred the way Bloodlines handled it.

    1) I think anything with the name Humanity should have as it's highest sin, killing for any reason, (i.e 1e humanity got it right) you want to be a killer, or a thief, or anything but a saint, take a path, doesn't mean I am right, and if anything Diablerie should sate the beast and make it quieter, restoring path, if you have the right one.
    What do you think diablerie is and its role in the game should be is I think a larger question of it, I believe. In my games, I highlight it permanently merges someone else's soul with your own in exchange for power. You no longer are ever going to be the same person you were before. You may suddenly like classical music, have memories of WW2 where you preyed on Slavs, and struggle with disassociative identity disorder if you didn't particularly have an iron will.

    If you are on Humanity and devour, say, someone on the Path of Caine then you ABSOLUTELY know for a fact that said religion is right or suffer some sympathy for it.

    If you are a Path of Blood 9 followers, devouring a vampire on Golconda will forever let you know that you cannot escape human sympathy, love, and redemption.

    The cost for diablerie is that it destroys your sense of self. Your identity. Who you are.

    But the reward is POWER.

    For other games, diablerie may be far less dramatic and is just absorbing some soul juice.

    Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.
