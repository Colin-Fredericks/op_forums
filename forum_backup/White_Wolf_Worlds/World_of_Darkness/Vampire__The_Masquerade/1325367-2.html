        Originally posted by Thoth View Post

        If you want the true sexist portions of the game, look at the story portrayals of the NPCs. Most of the despicable NPCs are male, while the female characters tend to have either a moral grey position (Pisha) or a victim status (the girl in the snuff film or the movie star & lama). VV tends to be a classy and sensual character who works in the adult entertainment industry, but the hard core porn characters tend to always be males.

        The female blood dolls in the clubs are all portrayed as relatively intelligent and at worst can be described as pleasure seeking individuals. But you don't get female characters who are portrayed as low intelligence or generally socially awkward. Sure the one female ghoul is whiny about trying to find her vampire patron, but the worst traits are more of a drug seeking addict rather than being socially inept.
        This goes back to what I was saying earlier. The female characters are designed to maintain an aura of sexuality. Even Pisha, a 230 year old cannibal, is portrayed as sophisticated and alluring. This isn't because Troika Games hates either women or men. It's because the female characters are meant to be attractive for a mostly male audience.

        VV is a women because she's supposed to flirt with the PC. The snuff film crew is mostly male to make the PC feel more powerful when they're all inevitably killed.

        The female blood dolls were created to engage in pseudo-sexual acts with the PC. The various male gangsters are (usually) at the receiving end of the PC's wrath.

        An attractive villain isn't one that most players will want to kill outright and females in Bloodlines are meant to be attractive. A female of "low intelligence or generally socially awkward" doesn't fulfill that purpose. The "despicable" NPCs are male (Andrei, Bishop Vick, Dennis, Stan Gimble) because they're the subject of quick missions that give the PC the option to kill them.

        The gender of a character is used to help develop their narrative. Tossing jacked male gangsters around like rag dolls is more fulfilling to a socially-awkward 15 year old boy than tossing around skinny female bikers. Likewise, a well-endowed vampire stripper flirting with you is more exciting than a shirtless man doing the same.

        This isn't necessarily sexist to either gender. The game just picked a marketing strategy and stuck with it.

                Of course I never said anything was wrong with enjoying a game with such things in it, but when people ask about "is it sexist", the answer is yes. Its kind of like how in TV and Movies if there is a female villain chances are that if they are going to get a beat down or knocked out, it will be done by another female because we are socially coded to assume any male who inflicts violence on a female is automatically the villain, but we laugh if a female beats up a guy.

                You get the same sort logic twists that make implied threat of rape to a female character the domain of the truly vile, but the threat of rape against male characters used by the heroes and even actual rape of the males played for laughs. Pop Culture Detective did a decent discussion video on the subject, not exhaustive by any means, but a good starting point. Needless to say, don't click the link if you don't want to hear discussion on the topic.

                Do I like Bloodlines, very much so. Is it sexist in several different ways, sure. Is that a bad thing, not really.

                        I think one of the problems with Bloodlines is a problem with the WoD in general:

                        Addressing negative societal problems by including them and allowing players to explore them, vs. perpetuating negative stereotypes and narrative tropes.

                        It's a hard line to walk in a videogame because computer games can only adequately include so much nuance while keeping it interactive. Is the game doing a good job ad highlighting the latent sexism in society - even Kindred society - by having it be there despite it not really being the point of the story? Or is the game indulging in that latent sexism; maybe even without much thought to it? Is there a way to give something close to an objective answer?

                        Some games cross a few lines and it's easy to say that they're sexist. Bloodlines dances around the lines a lot.
