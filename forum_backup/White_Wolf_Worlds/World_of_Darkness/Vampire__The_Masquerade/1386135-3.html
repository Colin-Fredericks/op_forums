    I think, amid all the Watsonian quibbling the topic of the SI encourages, its useful to analyze the Doylist reasons for its existence and its role in the setting:

    1) It’s meant to emphasize the importance of the Masquerade, especially after the game had drifted away from that understanding because of power creep etc., to where mortals seemed like chumps and vampires untouchable. Instead of retconning that stuff away, V5 basically says yes, vampire authorities get cocky and overreached, and now there’s blowback. Because underestimating mortals was vampires’ original error that led to the creation of the Camarilla etc., and it was time for a reminder.

    2) It’s meant to reflect how this sort of thing might look in a post 9/11 world, wherein major states have assumed a broad mandate to combat terrorism, they often cooperate and outsource those efforts, their operatives have extraordinary powers and little accountability, and large scale surveillance of civilian populations is now considered normal. It just doesn’t make sense that all of these things could have progressed in this way, yet none of it would have any consequences for vampires. In a way, the SI is necessary to make the setting feel like it’s progressed out of the 90s.

    3) It also emphasizes the cyclic nature of the setting, which is a big thing in V5 in particular. Vampires have been here before but failed to learn from it and keep making the same mistakes because they’re selfish and short-sighted. It’s what the Noddist myths try to warn the Kindred about. Now things have played out in a way eerily reminiscent of first inquisition, with vampires trying to use mortal hunters against each other, panicked elders throwing their childer under the bus to save themselves, a general doubling down on secrecy, and these badass creatures of the night being reminded that, in the end, vampires parasitize human civilization — they don’t run it.

    With those things in mind, it’s just a matter of taste how an individual storyteller uses the SI, and there doesn’t even need to be any uniformity. But as a concept it does make sense, and moreover it heartens back to the early days of Vampire when hunters were meant to be frightening.

        Originally posted by Reasor View Post
        A suspected vampire who's being tailed by the SI can almost certainly count on having their phone permanently switched on and transmitting to a recorder. That's at least one order of magnitude in difference from scouring the entirety of global communications and trying to filter literally all the data in the world to look for vampires. It's just a matter of how much work load can feasibly be tackled. In the real world, the NSA had to outsource their illegal domestic surveillance work to private contractors in order to manage it all, and that was with a threat that everyone believed existed.
        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

        Yes.

        When I wrote that my take on the subject was not, "The Second Inquisition CAN'T listen to your phone", my subject was, "The Second Inquisition has 7 billion people to listen to and probably isn't because there's too much data to sort through. When you're caught, that's a different story."
        The NSA doesn't really outsource its surveillance work, it outsources to get the tools but not generally for the use of said tools.

        And no, it's not that hard to filter the data. The phone companies know every phone call that your phone has ever made, have records of every text you've ever sent, and know your phones location down to at least the city block at essentially all times; and the SI has (or could easily get) access to that data.

        It all just sits there in the database until a phone linked to a vampire (or other target) is identified. Once that happens that phones history is correlated with the database as a whole; who did it call or text, what websites did it visit, how long were its calls, when were those calls made, etc. And most importantly, what other phones were in the same locations at the same time.

        With one phone you go from seven billion people to track down to a relative handful. You start building up a contact map and mapping out the social circle, overlaying it with the circles of other vampires. In very short order you have an incredibly complete map of the entire network.

        You also look at financial records for all of those phones. Who are they legally registered to, who is paying the bills, how are those bills being paid, what is the billing address for the phone (and associated bank accounts)? What other things are associated with those accounts or addresses?

        Let's say that the vamps are using VPN's and highly encrypted channels to communicate. That protects the content of the communication but it also makes it stand out like a light in a dark room. The worlds traffic is filtered first for anything using that particular encryption scheme and then further filtered to identify the nodes of the VPN network. Once the nodes are IDed, a tag is simply added to all traffic exiting those nodes and traced to the end users. Again, identifying the phones/computers used by the vamps isn't beyond their reach and once done those devices are tracked in real time.

        Hiding from the NSA alone is so hard as to be essentially impossible. The vamps, at least the elders and sects, have the resources to actually be able to do so to some extent though. Hiding from the NSA when they are working in coordination with the FSB, MSS, etc. becomes nigh totally impossible. You simply can't actually ghost your way through the telecommunications system against that kind of opposition.

        Even flat out owning the telecom companies (which the Cam probably does via the Venture) only works until that control is established; and then it just becomes all the easier to identify the network.

        Can an individual or a small group beat the system? Absolutely, if they have resources, knowledge, skills, and don't make mistakes. Can an organization the size of the Cam or Sabbat, and with their needs to be at least semi-public do it? Not a chance. There will always be a breach and once the first opening is found, the resources that the likes of the NSA can bring to bare as a casual matter will bring down the entire network.

        As for concealing this all from their bosses? Vampires get marked as a terrorist organization or two, are given a code name, and the entire compartment is buried as TS/SCI; and probably ghosted onto another TS/SCI project to cover some of the resource use. All that shows up in the records (assuming that you are one of the handful of people who could ever see said records in the first place) is a log entry in the database with a project codeword as authorization and access beyond that point requires that the individual trying to access it be cleared for that compartment already.

        For the intel work, more money doesn't need to be appropriated nor does new equipment need to be built. It really is as easy as some coding and the authority to bury one more routine task into the already extant infrastructure.

        You have something like the Society of Leopold acting as a clearing house for the data, probably with their own infiltrators or recruits actually in those intel agencies handling that end. And the Society was probably damn careful to make sure that everyone with the ability to identify their intel sources has True Faith so that they can't be mind fucked into turning traitor. They synthesize the intel into a mission order and whatever facts (shorn of any hint of sources or methods) are needed to complete that mission and then pass it on to a strike team. Any captured team member knows nothing beyond their own cell; they have their own training facilities, own armories, own communications networks, own bank accounts, own cash, etc. and orders/intel is received via dead drop (which might literally be a drone dropping a package on their back porch at two in the morning while they sleep).

The SI works much better as a catspaw for some number of proper elders/meths who have decided that the world needs to be reordered; there are too many neonates being made, vampire society needs to be reformed to keep up with the drastic changes in technology over the past century, and its a time for a bit of proper revenge.

        A bare handful of BH, Ventrue, and Nos Elders/Meths could have done it pretty easily. Once Ur-Shulgi awoke, the Tremeres nights were numbered; there was no way that he was going to let that cursing stand and so plans are modified.

        The Nos Elder ensures that the existence of ShreckNet leaks to the other Clans and that the Nos concede to the demands of the Ventrue (pressed by their own Elder(s)) to allow the other clans access. ShreckNet (the fake) gets purposefully leaked to the right mortals via the Ventrue and their pawns in the mortal governments. And when they are ready, the SI goes active. The BH with Ur-Shulgi at its head defang the Tremere and destroy their Chantry; and walk right into an alliance with the Ventrue without any issue. The mortals, obviously, prioritize the Sabbat as they are both easier to find and more monster but a great deal of the Camarillia riff-raff also finds the mortals breaking down their doors as Meths have no real use for most Princes who still count their unlives in a handful of centuries; if the vamps have any worth then they will survive the mortals anyways. The Lasombra leadership is right there on board with this plan, purging their own ranks and the Sabbat of many of the "problem" individuals while sliding into the new alliance.

        The Beckoning? The Ghennah War? It's amazing the lies that a handful of 4th and 5th Generation Meths working together can pull off. All those meths suddenly awaken and all the elders are moving to the ME for this war; what rich hunting grounds. What a great way to get the ignorant neonates and newblood (only a few centuries, why they are still practically babes) without sense to rush to a prepared killing ground - a killing ground on the doorstep of the clan of Assassins and where the mortals are very apt to look the other way and think nothing of chaos. But what about those who return high on power and with tales of the War? You think a Meth can't pull off pretending to be a young vamp who diableried a Meth?

        As for the Second Inquisition; the Cam still has all that middle management that needs to be cleared out, distracted, and played for fools. So the Ventrue string pullers pass down orders to cause chaos for the mortals. Dangerous viruses are unleashed, the markets and corporate books are played with the cause issues, etc. China and the EU are both sitting on economic bombs that could bring down their economies at any point and they have been in place for years; the Ventrue should have zero problem causing that collapse. What happens to China when its bubble bursts and the government can no longer feed itself? What happens when Iran and Saudi Arabia throw a war and imperil global energy supplies (which a vamp could engineer with trivial ease)? What happens when the Euro collapses and takes with it the entire idea of European unity? What happens in the US when the political process is turned farcical and social unrest is being pushed, when the cartels become more violent, etc.? What happens when the Pope is assassinated and replaced with a hardline conservative diametrically opposed to the policies of the previous Pope?

        All that nice shiny chaos to distract the mortals and let the SI tear itself apart after it has done its work; as the resources the SI depends on to be any kind of threat cease to be available. And then, after the SI's purpose has been served, the Meths get together and do a little housecleaning of their own to cut it back down to size as just another group of hunters.

        The only way that the V5 metaplot actually makes any sense in relation to the previous VtM lore & history is if the entire plot is basically Meths playing games for their own purposes. The mortals think they are doing something, the young vamps are all proud of themselves for throwing off their Elders and gaining power & prestige, the arrogant Elders are running around like chickens with their heads cut off as they got complacent; and behind it all the Meths remain, just as secure as they have always been and still playing their own games with a world that they control and have controlled for, quite literally, thousands of years.

                            You mean the First Inquisition that was started by vamps to kill other vamps and advance vamp goals until it got out of hand in conjunction with the vamp civil war that was going on at the time?

                            The First Inquisition that didn't appear to actually do much of anything when it comes to killing powerful vampires or reducing their ability to influence the mortal world.

                            The Masquerade is necessary for reasons that have little to nothing to do with the potency (or lack thereof) of the regular mortals. If Vampires rule openly then you establish an immortal aristocracy that is easily definable to the masses as other and that is pretty much anathema to the beliefs of those masses. Because new vampires are always going to be made, you can't play into the otherness as a means of establishing divine right. If you allow mortals to rule alongside the vampires then those mortals will, almost inevitably, end up vamp patsies or turned themselves. Why all this matters is that the kine become a great weapon for vamps that want to improve their position to grab. A vamp helps the mortals end the current vamp ruler in the hope of taking the rulers place; and until you get to the real top tier vamps, a large number of mortals is still a real threat.

                            On the other hand, mortals can be encouraged to fear vampires and think them fiction & myth. The powers that be among the Kindred can still do what they will with the mortals with little to no fear or interference in their business; but those mortals cease to be a viable weapon to be used against them. The hungry neonate simply won't be able to raise a mortal force large enough to be a serious threat to a proper Elder - especially not while keeping his plans secret. The younger vampires are at risk from relatively small groups of Hunters and their own predilections ensure that most mortals will be unwilling to work with those younger vampires. More importantly, the Masquerade and fear of the mortals gives the Elders a ready made excuse to end younger vampires; framing someone for a Masquerade breach is so very easy after all. It is also a great way to gain favors and control those younger vampires; they need identities, properties, bank accounts, etc. They need mortal authorities to look the other way; and who controls all of those levers of mortal society that the neonate needs if he wants to avoid breaching the Masquerade? Why the Elders of course.

                            The Masquerade is about control, but it is about controlling the younger vampires.

                            As for ruling the mortals? They still do, it's just that instead of being the King ruling from a castle they are the reclusive family with a large stock portfolio. Open rulership invites challenge, blame, and responsibility. Instead of, for example, being the Judge it is much easier to own the Judge by keeping his daughters cancer at bay. Instead of Dominating the Senator, you simply donate some money and use Auspex to get prime blackmail material.

                            It's a lighter touch but is all the more powerful, insidious, and inescapable for precisely that reason. Convince the mortals that you don't exist, divide their lines of authority to ensure dispersed (and redundant) control, and pull the strings while the mortals are secure in the knowledge that they control their own fate.

                                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                            My simple response is, "Thematically, Hunters being under control of vampires is uninteresting as a campaign element to explore. Humans should be more than just pawns."
                                            Why? Does it matter to the neonate (the vast majority of PC's) who is ultimately behind the Hunters? As far as the neonate is concerned, its some mortals hunting them; and to get to the vamp pulling the strings usually requires several iterations of unknowing patsies.

                                            Originally posted by Black Flag View Post
                                            Agreed, though there are some people to whom it's apparently very important that vampires rule and mortals drool. V3 did seem to promote that view, by implicaton if not explicitly, in contrast to pretty much every other edition. But as the last one before the hiatus, it's been very influential.
                                            Mortals are inherently disadvantaged against vampires in ways that can't be overcome. Vampires are immortal. They don't die of old age and, for the ones that last at least, think nothing of plans that take decades (or longer) to come to fruition.

                                            Start with the lowest level neonate in, say, the modern US. Who are they going to hunt? The average wage slave or the street corner drug dealer? An upstanding citizen goes missing and you get police investigations, missing persons posters, angry family, etc. The average gang-banger goes missing? No one really cares. Now what does said average gang-banger have on them? Cash and objects easily convertible to cash. With a little thought, said neonate realizes that Dominating or blood bonding that banger is all it takes to start rolling up the gang and locating their stash house.

                                            A vampire with nothing but the clothes on their back stepping into any city on the planet should have a few hundred thousand in cash inside a week or two. Seed capital is simply easy for vampires.

                                            Crime is easy when you can use supernatural powers to ensure the loyalty of your underlings. Once the money is laundered and the right paperwork drawn up, the vampire becomes just one more millionaire with a hefty stock portfolio watched over by a money manager at a major bank. And then comes the fun of compounded interest and investment returns when you have minimal living expenses and immortality. This is without getting into things like insider trading, stock manipulation, theft, etc.

                                            That wealth, and its attendant influence, builds year after year, decade after decade, century after century. And that is before you have an entire clan specifically focused on gaining wealth and using it to influence mortal institutions. Any Ventrue Elder worth the name will be controlling billions in assets; the clan as a whole will literally have a degree of wealth that makes the House of Saud look like paupers.

                                            Politicians get bought, civil servants get influenced, media is controlled, etc. In other words, all of the organs that mortal humanity uses to organize itself and control its combined efforts are co-opted by vampires with ease.

                                            ---
                                            Let's think easy examples. You're a vampire and you want to build influence. Now you could go and thrall a school Principal, for example. But he interacts with the government and other mortals of influence, he is also a potential mark for other vampires and may well show up at the same place as other vampires. It is also notable if he goes nuts. Or you can quietly influence the schools janitor one night to gain access to the building; where you install a keylogger on the Principals computer to capture his info, put a few hidden cameras in the ladies bathrooms to gather some CP to frame him with in the future, etc. With access to the student records you have their names, addresses, parents info, etc. In addition to getting a leg up on the competition via seeing who is liable to be useful in future (and a lot of PII to steal and use for your own benefit), you also gain a bit of potential influence. And when you want that Principal to do something? Well a phone call and some instructions and he is looking at a few gigs of CP of his students sitting on his school issue computer in a hidden file; he can play ball or a routine audit of the schools computer network is going to find the CP and his life is ruined. And those claims of innocence? Well those will be doubted when he commits suicide one day. Or, instead, he can do whatever it is you want.

                                            You can turn the police officer, or instead you can turn the police department's mechanic and a small tweak gives you real time tracking data on the entire police fleet along with access to their radio network.
