        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
        Taste is when something falls into an area of having both positive and negative qualities that appeal to certain segments of the public. Some things are objectively sexist (or even misogynist) but there's a lot larger area where it offends some while empowers other.
        The problem is that this definition of taste means that objectively sexist things are still a matter of taste. After all, an objectively sexist thing empowers sexists and offends people that don't like sexism.

        That's the problem with using "taste" as you are. It doesn't account for the tastes of people with beliefs we find objectionable as a society.

        Trying to draw a line between "matters of taste," and objectionable material doesn't work, because there are people who find objectionable material to their taste, and thus it's always going to be both.

        Originally posted by Grumpy RPG Reviews View Post
        I agree with the first part, but am confused with the second part.
        The second part? It's not aimed at the Camarilla book specifically. It's a more general comment that taste doesn't really justify saying that there's some sort of grey area that's just taste, because there's no way to avoid that justifying stuff that shouldn't just be a matter of taste.

        To an extent, I'm saying to this conversation, taste is irrelevant. Taste is just whether people like something or not, not if they find it objectionable. I don't like lots of things, but I think their existence is just fine. That's taste. Something can be to my tastes, or not, and still have enough sexist elements that I'd consider it sexist.

        While disappointing to some, in terms of VtM:B2, perhaps the best solutions is to down play the sexuality and sexiness.
        It's a matter of execution though. Toning it down might be better if they don't want to put in the energy to execute well (which is a lot), but there's a dearth of games that actually do it well and it would be good to see more.

        Originally posted by Thoth View Post
        Epilepsy can be an inherited disorder or it can be the result of brain damage from physical trauma or chemical exposure.
        Largely irrelevant to my point though (and only something that contradicts things I left out rather than something I said). People don't go around purposefully giving people epilepsy. People go around purposefully perpetuating discrimination in society; and unknowingly as well.

        Also for a long time epilepsy and its seizures had quite a lot of social negativity associated with it, it wasn't exactly uncommon to see epileptics being cast in a bad light by religious groups. So its not that far removed if you understand the history.
        Discrimination against disabilities is something I'm intimately familiar with.

        Having a condition isn't the same as being discriminated against for it; and you were expressly discussing accommodations for disabilities (reducing flashes or frequencies of light used in them to avoid triggering an epileptic episode), not discrimination against them (social mistreatment of seizures was not part of your post)..

        If the majority of people encounter discrimination, then they should be adept at dealing with the situation, like being stuck in rush hour traffic, sure its not pleasant, but not anything special either.
        The... lack of empathy here is astounding.

        First, that people experience discrimination doesn't meant they want to experience it more when seeking out entertainment. To use your analogy, I might be used to sitting in traffic, but I'm not going to go sit in traffic just for fun because it's a daily frustration.

        Second, discrimination is not some minor inconvenience. It's a traumatic experience that regardless of having successful coping mechanisms isn't something we should expect people to simply endure for the sake of other people's entertainment.

        Also just to be clear, are you claiming that 60% of people are discriminated against or just females?
        While relatively unscientific, attempts to poll all Americans on how many people feel they experience discrimination ranges from ~55% to ~85%, with more polls tending towards the low end. Of course meta-analysis is hard because phrasing, methodologies, and so on make it hard to make any sort of conclusive statement. I agree the number is probably towards the higher end in reality, but coming up with numbers to use in conversations based on something more than speculation is useful.

        So no, there's nothing more to try to read into it than that.

        Random side thought, if Vampire is all about power fantasy and wish fulfillment,...
        This seems a questionable premise.

        ...and what represents those things changes with each generation of gamers, does that mean that V6 or V7 will be about being able to hold down a job and pay the rent on time rather than sexual politics?
        I doubt it would change much.

        On the "jobs" side of things it's worth remembering that VtM was started before the Clinton boom. The late 80s and early 90s were, like now, a period of high unemployment, low wages, and economic uncertainty for the lower and middle classes. It was a huge influence on the punk parts of the game. The whole Anarch vs. Camarilla conflict represents that; even in the Revised mode of Anarches as barely anything more than a faction within the Cam.

        The Cam has always been the capitalists that horde the wealth at the top and string along the lower ranks with promises of raises, promotions, and more if they just stick to the game plan. The Anarchs have always been out to reject that and try to find a different way, but are stuck in a world that doesn't really make it functional to do more than play the capitalist game.

        "Sexual politics" has been part of human discourse and entertainment for literally all of recorded history; it's not going anywhere any time soon.

                Nobody's talking about changing the law (at least in most Western countries, and if we are we're talking about moving the laws towards more freedom in places that don't have it).

                We're talking about the freedom to criticize expression. If someone writes a bunch of books with a constant message of, "women can't get raped because there's not such thing as a woman that doesn't want sex from a man," the freedom to produce that doesn't in anyway protect the author from the freedom of the rest of us to call it out for being rape apologist sexist crap. It doesn't stop the freedom of stores to not carry it to avoid tarnishing their own reputations. Etc. It doesn't matter what genre the works are in, or how many copies they can sell anyway. It doesn't even matter if people are morons and buy the books just to burn them.

                The second artists are free from the consequences of putting out art that has negative impacts rather than positive ones, is the moment reasonable artists get drowned out by propagandists. Artists looking to push positive change with their art need us to criticize art. They need it to keep the stuff without good intentions shunned for what it is, and they need it to be kept on their own toes to make sure their intentions and ideals make it on to the page well.
