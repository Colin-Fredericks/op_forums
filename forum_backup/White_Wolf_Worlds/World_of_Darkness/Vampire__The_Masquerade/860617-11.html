    [This might be cringe-worthy, but let's get Meta, shall we?]


    166) The fascination with Japan and its culture was a completely genuine phenomenon in the 90s, not influenced by any vampires; mortals crave the novel and exotic. But if you want to know the origin of the practice among Kindred of carrying and wielding Katanas - in actual combat, on the streets of modern western cities - you're probably looking for the enigmatic vampire named David Blade (no relation). Or, as Katana enthusiasts among Kindred call him, "Master David".

    He showed up one night in 1990, with stories saying he'd traveled to Japan, braving the lands of the mysterious eastern vampires, and returned with ancient knowledge of Japanese swordplay. When occasion had saw fit to see his skill witnessed by a group of Anarchs, they flocked to him, wanting to learn how to use the Katana. Within a couple years, it seemed like every young Lick was wielding a Katana and wearing trench coats (for the record, the trench coats arose on their own, though David himself cannot argue the utility that came from a long coat that can hide a blade). Most of these practitioners weren't his students, but rather the knowledge spreading around as he taught what he knew and those students then taught what THEY knew. He's even admitted that nothing would have stopped Kindred from adopting the Katana on their own; he was just the earliest in the west, and the one who advocated its use. He also taught his students how to best utilize their Disciplines in conjunction with the weapon...though often, such lessons centered on how not to accidentally break their weapon from excessive application of Potence.

    As the 90s transitioned into the Aughts, western culture lost its fixation on all things Japanese. Greater connectivity through the Internet meant that David's knowledge was no longer rare, and people (living or undead) began to explore other forms of melee combat, including European blade arts (helped by a series of Elders, who got motivated to teach their childer how to use a "real" sword). Plus, with the Japanomania wearing off, people started to see David Blade as kind of...problematic; an American running around, championing the merits of "Glorious Nippon Steel!" (though he himself hadn't used that phrase since the very early days) smacked of cultural appropriation. This also seemed to be the time frame when "Master David" both grew more tired of taking students and more adept at Obfuscate. So he faded from view. He currently wanders back and forth between America, Europe, and Japan, taking very occasional students or dispensing specific lectures.

    But the question of his origins and Clan still lingered, unanswered. He seemed skilled in the physical Disciplines, but seemed most adept at Auspex and Obfuscate. This lead many to assume he was either a Caitiff, or a Malkavian with a particular monofocus. No one knows for sure, as he doesn't speak of it.

    The truth is far stranger, but far more understandable when one learns that David Blade always wears a bandanna. He's a Salubri, specifically a Wu Zao (or "Watcher"), having been Embraced back in the eighties when he traveled to Japan. His dedication to learning traditional swordplay from old masters caught the attention of a Watcher, who saw potential both in the Gaijin's skill and in the prospect of getting more eyes out in the West. One would assume David's caste-defined monofocus would manifest around his love of Japan as a culture, but it seemed to latch on to the art and application of Japanese swords. That he returned wearing bandannas all the time was chalked up by Kindred to him being a Japanophile (what modern folk would call a "Weeaboo"), allowing him to pass unnoticed despite that it conspicuously masked his forehead.

    Since the teaching business slowed down, David Blade (for the record, that's obviously not his real name; then again, no Kindred bats an eye at Licks with assumed names) has continued his blood-driven interest in swordplay. He's sought out old masters (mortal, vampire, ghost, and in records), and even braved vampire-kind's instinctive fear of fire in order to begin learning the forging process. True to his Watcher roots, he's sought magical secrets concerning the Katana; ancient scrolls documenting old rites, and delving into tombs and ruins for cursed blades. He may end up trying to develop blood sorcery, focused entirely on channeling magic through his sword. These are all long-term goals.

    Nonetheless, his activities have made and still make enemies among supernaturals. He's pissed off Mages of the Akashayana and Dreamspeakers, for stealing knowledge and Wonders. Many Elder vampires in the west despise him, for being the one who perpetuated an annoying trend among neonates, often leading to Elder final deaths. And naturally, the Japanese Wan Kuei despise him, both for being a bastard progeny of Zao-lot, and for being a filthy gaijin, coming into their territory and appropriating their traditional ways. Moreover, his ("stolen") skill makes him a juicy target for swords-vamps, looking to test their own skill and increase their prestige by besting "Master David" in a duel. He walks these nights cloaked in obfuscation as much for his desire for peace as anything else.

    He's a divisive figure, to be sure, but some Kindred (even his opponents) think the modern nights are more interesting for his presence.

                                        174) In life, he was a private detective in the most Noir sense. Complete with trench coat, hat, and smoking habit. And in the most Film Noir move imaginable, the chain of events that would kill him started with a pretty woman walking into his office. He learned later it was a Toreador, who hired him to investigate a missing person's case. The missing person, in fact, was the Toreador's prospective childe. The kidnapper was...the Toreador. And it wasn't a kidnapping.

                                        What the detective pieced together - but just too late - was that his entire investigation, including the descent into the seedy underbelly of the city, was the Toreador's attempt to determine how air-tight the childe's disappearance was to mortal society. To figure out exactly where the trail could be picked up by mortals, so she could come along and scrub that trail clean. He'd been a patsy to an absurd and useless plot, and now he was a loose end. He'd seen too much; he'd already figured out the whole "hidden vampire conspiracy" angle. So when he was cornered at gunpoint in the depths of a sewer, he was given the courtesy of one last smoke before he was riddled with lead.

                                        But while it was the end of his life, it wasn't the end of his story. His perforated body tumbled down a pipe and washed out to a gutter a quarter mile down the way, still barely alive.

                                        That's where his sire found him. A rotting carcass that stank worse than the sewer, but ambled on anyway. The Toreador wasn't the only one who followed the detective's case with interest.

                                        He's a Samedi now, but remains committed to the noir detective lifestyle. Unlifestyle. He works more night cases now, because of his particular condition. He relies on his Obfuscate to get him work, and his Fortitude to carry him through the fire and fury. He takes a special pleasure in freaking out Toreador clients, and shaking them down for all they're worth. He still stinks, and his clothes constantly need heavy laundering. But on the upside, he can look forward to a long career. AND he can smoke all he wants. His lungs already rot and grow back on the regular.

                                                176) Never came up with a name for the character, but he's one I mentioned to my table in a discussion about fun character ideas a few years back and got explicitly banned from playing. Ever. Let's call him, "Chuck".

                                                Chuck was a restaurateur and cuisine savant, winning his first Michelin star at 23 and having been awarded three stars before the age of 30. After securing a book deal and several appearances on food shows and marrying an up-and-coming model and influencer, he was well on his way to celebrity chef and social media darling status when the unthinkable happened: a hit and run car wreck that left his young wife dead and him horrendously disfigured.

                                                Just kidding! A Nosferatu nailed his car, intending to kill him and Embrace his wife as a Cleopatra. It was pure happenstance Chuck had a bit much to drink that night, so she was driving when the Nosferatu slammed into the driver's side which killed her on impact. Having to improvise, the Nosferatu set the car on fire and fled. Learning later Chuck survived the wreck and fire, but was left disfigured which would be the end of his celebrity chef career, the Nosferatu stole into his home and Embraced him. Except, Chuck came out the other side...wrong.

                                                See, Chuck learned nothing from the "humbling" effect of the Nosferatu Embrace. His Obfuscate worked on camera, and worse, his blood lacked the foul properties of typical Nosferatu blood, and he had an uncanny knack for Dominate. His sire accidentally slipped early on he was responsible for the car wreck that killed his wife, and ended his rise to celebrity. This, understandably, caused him to develop an intense hatred for his own sire, and he rejected his own clan to strike out on his own.

                                                For now, Chuck retains his restaurateur status, capitalizing on his story of personal tragedy, re-branding himself as a connoisseur of all things alcohol, and changing his restaurants' concepts to invitation-, membership-, and reservation-only luxury gastro lounges that offer haute cuisine alongside the world's choicest alcohol selections, in cutting-edge decoration and weekly offerings of performance and visual art from around the world, and in an environment that allows relaxation and discretion in his most prominent clientele.

                                                Chuck excuses his behavioral "eccentricities" (i.e. only being out at night, etc.) as business choices and the consequence of emotional trauma, and justifies his rapid improvement in appearance (Mask) as reconstructive surgery and make-up. Otherwise justified questions about his business strategies (Why only be open at night? Why prohibit walk-in dining?) tend to get pushed down due to whacko Pizzagate-style conspiracy theories that Chuck and his most-trusted associates may or may not be anonymously propagating online to discredit critics. The occasional drop of blood in a key patron's food or drink, or subtle command here or there, doesn't hurt either. Neither does it hurt that competing restaurants, pubs, and lounges tend to get knocked down by strings of health code violations.

                                                Chuck's relationship with other Nosferatu is...unique. He really doesn't like his own clan, and as a result he tries to cozy up with, of all vampires, the Toreador. He's not completely hated by them, his social niche serves its purpose, and they're willing to deal when it suits their purposes. On the other hand he'll never be accepted by them, and Toreador being Toreador, look at him under the best of circumstances as a novelty or pet, but most of the time he's an unwelcome interlocutor with a profile high enough they simply can't get rid of him.

                                                Other Nos find this hilarious, and they love to egg him on by behaving as deplorably as possible around him while keeping him completely out of the loop on their own schemes; most expect him to come around, eventually, especially once his attempts to weasel his way into the Torries' good graces inevitably bite him in the ass. It doesn't hurt Chuck's case that he's found a way to muscle in on territory typically controlled by the Toreador, and that really sticks in their craw. They're not too worried about the Masquerade for the moment, since he has a good grasp on how to maintain it and keeps the environment surrounding him tightly controlled.

                                                    [While the Bloodline as such is controversial, I'd like to take a crack at the DAV20 Niktuku. For extra points, one embraced in the modern day.]


                                                    177) He and his girl were the talk of school. She was the prom queen, him the handsome, self-styled bad boy. Until one night they were returning from the local drive in theater, when another car drove them off the road. He awoke at the wheel, a little banged up, to see a bunch of ugly sons of bitches - a pack of Nosferatu Antitribu - taking turns draining his girlfriend dry. He tried to stop them, but they were too strong. They stood over his beaten body, one of them prepared to "do a number on that pretty face" of his. He was only saved by the oncoming lights of a passing patrol car.

                                                    The police figured it was a passing band of miscreants, but the people of their small town didn't trust him. He'd always been a "bad seed". But they didn't get a chance to drive him out of town, before he made pursuit of his girlfriend's killers. From there, he spent two years traveling, stopping in small cities along the road and honing his skills as a vampire hunter. From backwoods folk, he learned to fire a bow; they were quieter than guns, and had a chance to stake the target outright if his aim was true. For a time, he mostly eliminated weak or small time licks; Caitiff and Thin Bloods, that he caught along the road. He wouldn't tolerate any of them to live.

                                                    At last, he tracked the pack of Sabbat Nosferatu to their resting place. He flushed them out with fire, slaying three. He was unable to best the last survivor, however. The lick who'd threatened to cut up his face proceeded to nearly kill the young hunter. Timely application of a flaming board drove the vampire off, but unfortunately also let the creature escape. Dying, he screamed at the monster, demanding it come back so he could kill it. Cursing and vowing revenge, though he was fast bleeding out.

                                                    Until a figure, as hideous as any Nos, appeared behind him, clapping in glee at the young mortal's cocksure display. This Niktuku had followed in the hunter's wake, admitting that he'd intended to kill the mortal (for robbing the Hungry bastard of potential meals). But seeing the young man's ferocity, tenacity, and beauty had stoked feelings in the Niktuku's cruel, dead heart. Feelings of admiration. "What will you do, boy hunter?" he said, a mocking grin made from behind a chapped, once-beautiful mouth. "What would you trade to exterminate your foes? Would you give up your life? Your beauty? Your humanity? I can give you new life, after a fashion, and the ability to feed on their strength. Would you willingly become a vampire that hunts vampires?"

                                                    Honestly, the old lick didn't need to ask. It was no question at all.

                                                    The young man remains a wanderer, a hunter, and, for the moment, beautiful. He's just no longer alive, and pursues his prey with an intensity borne not of loss, but of hunger. In his private moments, when the trail goes cold, he laments how hard it is to remember her face. It's been decades now. He takes the slightest bit of solace, knowing that if he ever forgets her completely, it will be when he's become so ugly that she wouldn't want him anyway.

                                                        178) A career sailor - occasional privateer - who in life operated in the Caribbean, he held a particular passion for scrimshaw and bone-carving. His skill at etching scenes into bones attracted the attention of his sire: a Cappadocian refugee who'd finally been run out of his hiding place in Europe by the Giovanni. When the Elder took shelter in Haiti, he discovered the sailor's remarkable craft in the stall of a night market clerk, who pointed the Cappadocian in the sailor's direction.

                                                        Turns out, it's not just Toreador who can appreciate good artwork. It helped first that it was bonecraft, and second that the sailor possessed a knack for conveying deep information in the carvings. It tickled the old scholar's love of history.

                                                        Since his Embrace, the sailor has made it a personal journey to travel the oceans, learning as much as he can about the world, the Kindred in it, and the art of bonecraft. His sire instilled in him an appreciation for recording events; bones outlast paper, and are much harder to burn. He's left hundreds of pieces lying around, documenting in sculpture many movements of the Jyahd over the years. At least, such that the sailor is aware of. Many necromancers collect his works, both for the insight they may gain, and for the joy of possessing such unique, well-crafted pieces.

                                                        He's also been known to study Kine, too. When he's had the occasion to befriend a mortal - because of his hunted "bloodline", he watches but does not readily engage with other Kindred - he will learn their personal stories. Then, when that mortal passes on, he'll return years later and etch those stories on their bones. One last, final testament to the people they were. He's also been known to do this on bodies he finds that catch his interest. If he cannot learn about such persons second hand, he can always summon their spirits and learn about it from the most primary of sources.

                                                        Whether it's to dodge the Giovanni, a desire for new stories, or simply personal preference, he is itinerant to a fault. He pilots a personal sailing craft from port to port, from South America to Africa, up to Iceland and the Mediterranean (though, for the Venetian necromancers, he doesn't stay there long), and down and around to India and Australia. Inheriting an animosity to the Camarilla Elders who betrayed his Clan, he has more sympathies for the Anarchs than anyone. Still, he'll play nice in whoever's territory he's in. No use rocking the boat. Not when he can fade into the background and watch events unfold.

                                                        Currently, he's parked his boat out in Africa, carving the stories of the Libertatian Fleet into the bones of Atlantic sharks and felled Savannah beasts that come out from inland.

                                                        Magestuck: The Ascension

                                                            179) A Malkavian who fancies himself a neo-Socratic. Complete with toga.

                                                            Originally a student of classical literature, this deep education into philosophy stuck with him following the Embrace. He travels from city to city, and hangs out at Elysium. There, he engages Kindred of all stripes - Elders and Neonates, Ivory Tower and Anarch, etc - in philosophical discussions. And like any good student of Socrates, he employs the Socratic Method. No statements of fact, merely questions. In the hopes of forcing them to examine their base assumptions, and realize how little they actually know.

                                                            Unsurprisingly, the reason he moves around so much is because of his penchant for pissing off the vampires in power. Multiple Domains have declared Blood Hunts on him, for no other reason than because his reasoned oration and insightful observations or questions have a tendency to make any and all vampires look foolish. However, that tendency to make them look like fools is often what saves him from Final Death. When a Prince calls for his blood, simply because he asked them questions, it often makes the Prince look bad, and/or incompetent. Not to mention that overreaction to the Malkavian - up to and including Frenzy - reflects poorly on any Kindred.
