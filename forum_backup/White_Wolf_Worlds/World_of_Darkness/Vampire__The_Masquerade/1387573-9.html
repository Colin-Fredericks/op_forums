    To be fair, even when making shovel heads, the Clan basically looks for people that have some resemblance to their stated preference. The Tzimisce seek out people like Wendy Wade who are upper class, intelligent looking, or cultured. It can lead to some serious mistakes but the idea is that if they do survive that they are going to be eventually brought into the fold of the Clan Culture.

    If you had a template for a Shovel Head, it would probably be Shovel Head and they would be described as someone who is being strongly encouraged to shed their human existence and brainwashed by the Vaulderie as well as the cult-like nature of Sabbat existence to become "More Tzimisce than Tzimisce." Wendy Wade is, for example, a perfectly excellent example of the Tzimisce: ambitious, refined, and utterly amoral.

    We something similar with Sierra Burace who is a perfectly fine Lasombra but also someone who has nothing really in common with her clan's esoteric interests.
    It's only if you think of the Tzimsice as a bunch of Cenobites that engage in nothing but flesh-related horrors that the shovel-heads feel like an afterthoughts. I got into a bit tiff about this in the Lasombra thread but I've always felt the Flesh-Monkeys (my slang for these) were a significant but fundamentally minority group in the clan--same as the Kolduns. Powerful, influential, and "famous" as Tzimisce go but not the majority.

    I admit I've always been a fan of the Old Clan Tzimisce far more so than the mainstream clan. I tend to prefer them because I'm all about the honor, oaths, hospitality, creepy castles, and Castlevania elements. The Revenant Families are much more important to my view of the Tzimisce than Vicissitude. The Basarab are my favorite Revenant families but each of them basically means the Tzimisce have their own sorta-Bloodlines.

    So The Count is my favorite of these by far.

        Is there a full definition for "Flesh Monkeys"?

        One thing I've tried to do in my own games over the years is present clans as being like these really weird extended families. (And while I do try to hew to certain overarching themes for each clan - passion for the Brujah, survival for the Gangrel, etc. - that sort of thing can manifest in a vast multitude of ways.) There are usually some wide spread traditions and customs among each clan, such as the Tzimisce's customs of hospitality and domain sanctity, and assorted blood ties and common traits, but also a bunch of internal factions and subcultures. In the Fiends' case, this includes the old school lords (basically the Old Clan, just without the name), the various revenant families, the Koldun witches, the metamorphosists, the Knights of the Dragon, the would be gods, the Balkan Muslims who exist on the fringes of the Ashirra, and others, as well as the various members who are just doing their own thing. And so on with the other clans.

                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                        To be fair, even when making shovel heads, the Clan basically looks for people that have some resemblance to their stated preference.
                        This is exactly what I disagree with. I understand that at this point, I am arguing against 25 years or so of continuity so it's a hopeless argument. I can't win by appealing to "canon". I'm not going to change the game. But the idea that the Sabbat is selective about who it turns really goes against what the sect should be. I'm fine with the occasional embrace being done because the vampire feels she must have that individual. Sabbat can be passionate and have an impulsive embrace as well. But it shouldn't be standard for the Sabbat.

                        When Kurt Barlow is turning the entire town of Salem's Lot into vampires, he isn't very particular about who he takes. That's the same for most other classic vampire movies when the movie often ends with the lone hero having to hunt down and destroy his friends before finally catching up the master vampire who was the cause of everything. That the World of Darkness vampires don't emulate those kinds of vampires is OK (and needed because Stephen King's story is all about taking the idea seriously that being bitten by a vampire automatically makes you a vampire would mean humanity is quickly wiped out is a serious objection to that scenario; it is why Bram Stoker made sure to show that something else needs to be done to turn someone). But it's the effect we're trying to duplicate, and the terror of that effect on us. That we could be eaten and turned into bloodthirsty monsters because we were in the wrong place at the wrong time. The Sabbat is more than just an emulation of classic vampire stories and movies. It has many other inspirations, but I feel this is a major inspiration and the game needs to reflect it. The shovelhead mass embrace is important in creating that. (I think that is also why we saw a lot of Infernalism in the early Sabbat books. They had infernalists because in the Hammer Horror movies, Satan worship was a very big element.) But we lost that early on because of these clanbooks.

                        And in that type of scenario the vampire doesn't pick most of his victims. Nor does it make sense that Sabbat doing a mass embrace of humans in order to get replacements are going to be selective. No one would say, "Before we start abducting people at random to feed them our blood, whack them on the head with a shovel, and dump them in a mass grave to see who comes out, it's very important we all try to find people we think would be good picks for our clans. That way if they happen to crawl out, and if they happen not to be Caitiff, that'd be really cool." I find that to be completely ludicrous.

                        Wendy Wade wasn't picked. She was turned by pure chance. "She made the mistake of walking home late from a neighborhood fern bar." She wasn't being stalked or targeted. She just had the misfortune to be out and alone late at night when a van of Sabbat were looking for random people to dump into a pit. If she had never went to that bar that night, or came home early, or was picked up by an attractive man and went back to his place, she would never have become a vampire.

                        And that it was a Tzimisce who turned her was also random chance. It could have been any member of that particular Sabbat pack. She could have been a Lasombra or one of the anti-tribu. In fact, "ambitious, refined, and utterly amoral" seems to work better for a Lasombra than the Tzimisce based on the templates we just saw.

                        I don't see the random turning of many of the Sabbat to be a flaw. It really distinguishes the sect from the Camarilla and Independents who do pay much more attention to their "candidates". Not all Sabbat are turned like this. Certain mortals will attract a Sabbat who wants that one specific person to be turned, but that is a different dynamic, and I think it weakens the sect as a concept if done too much. In fact, I think there should be a distinction in the Sabbat between Sabbat who were randomly picked, survived, and became True Sabbat versus those vampires who were "groomed." Wouldn't there be resentment? Wouldn't those who proved themselves consider themselves better? Wouldn't it make yet another point of contention between the elders and neonates or ultra-conservatives and loyalists within the sect? I think it would. And I think it'd be an interesting conflict for the sect.

                        One of the reasons the Sabbat sect ended up being changed as we went into Revised is because of this. People wrote the Sabbat clanbooks as if they were the Camarilla clanbooks, and didn't take the unique aspects of the Sabbat into consideration. So in wrestling with these contradictions, later writers simply decided to dump many of the original conceptions of the Sabbat and make everything more like the Camarilla, but with a different kind of hierarchy and more gore. That's when we started getting excuses that Sabbat pick their embrace just like Camarilla vampires as well as doing shovelheads, and then like your comment that even the shovelheads get picked.

                        The game developed in a different direction. I understand that. I just feel it was a mistake.

                                Clanbook: Lasombra (part one)

                                So, now it's 1996. This book was developed by Rob Hatch, so he's taken over as Vampire developer now. (IIRC, Jennifer Hartshorn was Vampire: the Dark Ages developer at this point.) Writing is by Richard Dansky (who would later go on to be developer for Wraith, Vampire: The Dark Ages, and KotE, as well as a lot of writing), and Elizabeth Ditchburn, who I think mainly wrote for Wraith. Interior art is by a lot of people, including Joshua Timbrook, and he appears to have done the template art as well. I've no idea how or why that happened, but it is a nice return to original form, especially after the deformed weirdness of Tzimisce. As a stylistic choice, this book is almost entirely black background with white type, because, shadows. This can make it hard for some people to read (and I recall Bruce Baugh mentioning it when talking about writing the revised version). This book is also notable for having one of the silliest covers in the entire clanbook line. Bald guy with a Black Hand logo on his head and his tongue sticking out doesn't exactly imply "highly competent political leaders of the Sabbat." This image became somewhat derisively known as "Scary Black Hand Face".

                                Characters are all made using the Sabbat rules, even though the regular rules are at the bottom of each sheet.

                                Antitribu Pirate
                                A guy who grew up loving the sea, and once financially stable enough at his day job to afford it, bought a boat and enjoyed sailing (presumably in the Gulf of Mexico/Caribbean). And one night he ran across a very old pirate ship and its captain embraced him. Now he's a modern day pirate, helping attack ships at night and stealing various cargo ranging from drugs and gold to electronics and computer chips. Overall, he enjoys being a vampire pirate, and sometimes his enthusiasm gets the better of him and he acts like an Errol Flynn character. Physicals are primary, with a 4/3/3 split, and Socials as secondary, with 3/2/3. There's also an extra dot in Wits from freebies. Abilities include Firearms, Melee, and Boating at three each, then Brawl, Intimidation, Streetwise, and Finance at two each. Also a handful of one dot abilities, including Navigation. Disciplines are one dot each, plus another in Obtenebration. Backgrounds are Generation at five dots, and two each in Mentor and Resources. I'm noticing a lack of Allies or Retainers to represent the rest of the pirate crew. Path of Harmony rating is four, and Willpower is five. (The bulk of the freebies got spent on backgrounds and that dot of Wits. I'm starting to notice that the background thing is maybe a detriment to the Sabbat characters.) I like this character quite a bit. He's fun and part of a modern genre I wish Vampire had explored a little more (it did to a degree with the revised Lasombra book and WoD: Blood-Dimmed Tides). If it has a down side, it's that I think this character might've been a little stronger if made under standard character creation rules, loosing either the dot in Dominate or the second one in Obtenebration to boost his backgrounds, and maybe fine tune the abilities a little.

                                The Cadet
                                A top student at one of the military academies embraced just before graduation after being scouted by his sire for a place in the Black Hand (the military branch, not the cult). Now he's the head of a Black Hand pack, putting his training and leadership to good use in the existing world of the Jyhad and ultimately aiming for a top spot in the Black Hand and the Sabbat. Physicals are primary, with a 3/4/3 split, and Socials secondary, with four dots in Charisma. Wits once again has an extra dot from freebies. I'm kind of surprised it doesn't just have Mental as secondary and use the extra dot in Charisma. Athletics and Firearms are at three dots each. Alertness, Brawl, Dodge, Leadership, Streetwise, Drive, and Melee are at two. Also ones in Security, Stealth, and a handful of suitable Knowledges. I'm a little disappointed by the lack of the Military Science secondary ability, as it seems like it would've been appropriate. Disciplines are the one each plus one, this time in Potence. Backgrounds are one each in Black Hand, Resources, and Sabbat Status. Path of Honorable Accord is six and Willpower is seven. Once again, this is a pretty good character. He's fairly well suited to the job he was created for, and would make a pretty good center to build a Black Hand pack around. As a matter of personal amusement, the character art looks like a young Clayton Oliver, who was an intern at WWGS back in the late 90s before becoming a writer, and I'm wondering how much of the character was inspired by him.

                                The Cat Burglar
                                Someone with a life long habit of going where they weren't supposed to and poking through other people's stuff just for the fun of it, eventually becoming a very skilled classic cat burglar. So skilled that they drew the attention of a Lasombra, who plucked them off the side of a building in the middle of a job to undergo the creation rites. Now they're an even better thief, frequently stealing from members of the Camarilla. Physicals are primary, with Dexterity maxed out to five and Strength at three. Mental is secondary. Athletics, Stealth and Security are all at four dots, and boy has it been a minute since I've seen a character with more than one ability above three. Alertness, Dodge, Streetwise and Finance are at three each. Disciplines are Obtenebration at three and Potence at one. Backgrounds are four in Resources and one in Contacts. Path of Cathari is five and Willpower six. I like this character a lot. It seems like it'd be fun to play, and its nice to see one focused on necessary skills instead of just all over the place. Incidentally, I keep referring to this character as "they" because the image is pretty androgynous and it's impossibly to tell if it's male or female.

                                That's all for this one. Next will be the rest of the clan.
