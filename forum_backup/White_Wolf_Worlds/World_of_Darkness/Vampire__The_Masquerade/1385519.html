    Namtaru

    05-03-2020, 10:43 PM
    According to the lore, Nergal unearthed Namtaru beneath Chorazin, then moved it to the labyrinth beneath Knossos.

    Later, in 636 C.E. Assamites waged a war against the Baali at Chorazin. There, Izhim abd'Azrael and 12 other Assamites were absorbed into the colossal hand of Namtaru as part of a ritual.

    Later, in 1242 C.E. Izhim was freed by his childe Enkara from Namtaru. Izhim called Namtaru “The Fallen One" and said that "Namtaru" was merely a word used in a futile effort to contain the infinite.

    So, if Nergal moved Namtaru from Chorazin to Knossos prior to the eruption of Thera in 1500 B.C., how is Namtaru still in Chorazin when the assamites go there in 636 C.E. and later in 1242 C.E.?

    I’m interested in your theories. I personally think Namtaru refers not to a single entity, but all of the “Children“ that the Baali worship (I know of the Decani and I don’t think it refers to them).

    I’m a writer. A storyteller. They’re my passions. They’re what I do. The stories I tell, they are my mythologies. Like the Norse tales of Odin, Thor, and Loki, or the Celtic fables of Lugh or Cú Chulainn, I want them to be the kind that people retell. They are the legacies I wish to leave behind.

      I don't have all my copies of the books anymore, so I can't say that I can recall whether somebody put some effort into cleaning the timeline up, but the sequence of events given on the White Wolf Fandom Wiki goes like this:

      Namtaru is discovered at Mashkan-Shapir, which was a real city in what is now Iraq.

      Nergal moves the beast to Knossos, a real city on the isle of Crete, and there loses Namtaru at the end of the Second Baali War.

      Namtaru later mysteriously turns up in Chorazin, a real city off the shore of the Sea of Galilee in what is now northern Israel.

      I have a nagging feeling that the initial discovery of Namtaru was moved from Chorazin to Mashkan-Shapir in a retcon. Maybe Chorazin is just the only place name that I'm remembering from the Baali's Dark Ages Clanbook, or maybe someone at either White Wolf or Onyx Path caught the issue you're describing and put in the effort to fix it.

                              BIGGER PROBLEMS
                              Hard as it may be to believe, the Nephandi have
                              other pressing concerns besides the relentless pressure
                              of the Ascension War. Malfean Nephandi time and
                              again find themselves charging into conflicts originated
                              by their allies, often fighting to hold off angered
                              spirits and shapeshifters while the tainted werewolves
                              who started the conflict are long gone. Tensions between
                              the two groups have been steadily rising for some
                              time, exacerbated, some Malfeans claim, by the actions
                              of other factions of Nephandi.
                              However, there are other Malfeans to worry about,
                              namely, the greatest and most powerful Spectres. Dwelling
                              in a Labyrinth of their own gnawing, these servants
                              of Oblivion (not the Wyrm) devote themselves to the
                              corruption of the living and the dead, at least when they
                              can rouse themselves from fitful slumber.

                              DEAD THINGS
                              Far worse, however, are the true Malfeans —
                              the ageless Never-born Spectres who sleep uneasily
                              at the heart of the Underworld's Labyrinth,
                              Created when Entropy was born, these creatures
                              gnawed the endlessly shifting corridors of the First
                              Labyrinth from the raw stuff of Creation.                               Now,
                              they slumber restlessly in the chambers and halls
                              they've carved out for themselves, dreaming dreams
                              of power and destruction.
                              Such dreams concern the Nephandi, and with
                              good reason. The uncanny number of coincidences
                              between the geography of the Underworld and the
                              Nephandi's own paradigm has raised many questions.
                              Recently, much to their regret, a team of
                              Infernalist researchers set out to discover the truth
                              behind this uncanny resonance.
                              The conclusions that this research team reached
                              both frightened and angered the reigning gilledeans
                              — and their Masters. Not one of the researchers
                              outlived his initial presentation of the team's findings
                              by more than five minutes, and every report,
                              note or voice recording made during the course of
                              the study found itself consigned to the fire. At least,
                              that was the fate of all of the research materials that
                              could be found. Certain materials, smuggled out by
                              Void Engineer barabbi to Convention acquaintances,
                              revealed the chilling truth.
                              In essence, the report claimed that the entities
                              the Nephandi serve are nothing more than the
                              dreams of the sleeping Malfeans in the primal Labyrinth.
                              The Labyrinths, titles and natures of all that
                              dwell Beyond are reflections and sleep-distorted
                              versions of the thoughts flickering through the
                              sleeping minds of the Never-born.                               And, when one
                              of the sleeping Malfeans awakens, much that he
                              dreams vanishes.
                              Both fear and excitement have sprung from this
                              last point, particularly among Technocratic mages
                              and the restless dead who spy on them. Researches
                              into the Shadowlands indicate that the oldest and
                              most powerful Malfean, Gorool, awakened just as
                              the Technocracy and Traditions commenced their
                              final assault on the Nephandi in 1945. Observers
                              from both groups remarked at the time that the
                              defending Nephandi seemed confused and weakened,
                              and many called on powers or allies that never
                              materialized. The implication of all this is that
                              Gorool's awakening destroyed his dreaming creations,
                              depriving those who served those dreams of
                              power in their hour of need.
                              Even now, a report from a gang of ghostly spies
                              sits on the desk of the Skeletal Lord of the dead.
                              Inside are the details of a proposed invasion of the
                              Labyrinth by a multi-Convention strike force. The
                              assault's stated purpose is simple: To awaken the
                              slumbering Malfeans, thus perilously weakening the
                              resurgent Nephandi. As for the effect that this tactic
                              would have on the Shadowlands, the Technocratic
                              assessment is chillingly simple: unimportant.
                              Worse still, though, are those Nephandi who
                              have heard snippets of this research, and come to
                              the conclusion that if they are only worshippers of
                              dreams of true outer darkness, then how much more
                              powerful could they be if they worshipped the things
                              that dreamed such horrors into existence?


                              Now while it's true that this is a Mage thing (even still, it's a very cross-over Mage thing) it's stillthe best cosmological explanation about evil entities in the World of Darkness. In this way the Neverborn and Oblivion become the source of every dark god, wyrm or anything.

                              Some fan on Werewolf hate this book because it makes the Wyrm an emanation of the Neverborns and Oblivion, but I think that a miscarried universe that predates the very notion of existence, something that never was (in a time were "time" wasn't, and "was" was a meaningless concept) and "was" before the Fiat Lux, is an excellent source for a "Unified Evil Theory"

                              It really does make much easier to fit Yama Kings, Maeljin, Wyrm, Demon Lords (and even the Fallen, with a bit of work) into one cosmology.
                              Especially because it doesn't make one religion true (like Demon the Fallen did), since while we could describe it as a Qlipphot, and use terms and nouns from the Qabbala and western mysticism, it's just a mere attempt of human minds to describe something that cannot be described. You could use any system of belief to encapsulate it.

Oblivion was originally part of the natural cycle; recycling old spirits so new lives could be born. The Weaver bound the Wyrm into its creation and the Wyrm shredded itself into the Hydra trying to free itself. Oblivion too shredded parts of itself into the Neverborn to escape its prison.

                                  Thus, the True Wyrm and Oblivion are one and the same entity. The Neverborn/Maeljin/Yama Kings are the same entities viewed through different lens.

                                  Bygones are thaumivores. They need to spend a point of quintessence every day to remain in the mortal world and possess various powers that ignore paradox. Vampires need to spend a blood point (valued at one quintessence in Mage) each night to remain active in the mortal world and possess various powers that ignore paradox.

                                  Vampires have ancients that slumber in secret places and wish to consume, at the least, their progeny when they wake. The Neverborn are ancient spirits that slumber in secret places and wish to consume the world when they wake.

                                  At one point in the distant past of Mage/Werewolf/Changeling there was a time when there was no difference between the mortal and spirit worlds, including the realms of the dead.                                   What if the legends of Caine and the Antediluvians are just a layer of reality that overlays the deeper layer that is the very concept of murder/destruction/Oblivion and its poisonous fruits/Neverborn/Antediluvians... and the vampiric condition is but a spiritual reflection of this primordial reality that sustains itself as all thaumivores do, by consuming quintessence that it has ripped from the very life blood of mortals?

                                  Just how deep do you wish to dig for truth? How deep before the madness consumes you?

                                              Originally posted by Penelope View Post
                                              This stuff is amazing! Cosmologically speaking. Thank you guys.
                                              Because it’s been a year ago, here’s a post I did that first explored some of the Orpheus/Oblivion/Wyrm connections;

                                              Originally posted by Chris24601 View Post
                                              The main way I dealt with it in my Mage campaign was to take one of the elements of Orpheus and run with it; Oblivion is a LIE. The idea that all of Creation can be undone is the Nephandic equivalent of a fairy tale. In one campaign the PCs were investigating and ended up on the edge of Oblivion itself... specifically at the spiritual embodiment of the Fallen Tower which was a Nephandic holy site that leaned out over the maw of Oblivion). What they learned through ancient writings on the walls is that the Oblivion was a myth created by the first Neverborn Malfeans..the first beings to reach its edge and falter in primal fear at the gateway to the unknown. This myth of eternal destruction filtered up to other spirits of the dead and the practices of Mages who associated with the dead and over time was corrupted by angry outcasts mages into a nihilistic credo that became the first Nephandi.

                                              It filtered up to the Fera as well in the story that the Primeval Wyrm had gone insane and was now consuming everything.

                                              The Nephandic Cauls, the Black Spiral, the Banes and corruption spirits all grew from the power of belief in this lie and further hiding the truth.

                                              The Truth? Oblivion/the Wyrm is what it has always been... the portal to birth/rebirth and the transition from one life to the next. The primal fear of the Neverborn is the fear of an infant being born from the safety of its mother’s womb, the only existence it has ever known, into the bright, cold uncertain world beyond.

                                              The Neverborn are literally the “never born.” Primeval miscarriages whose spirits were too afraid of birth and even more terrified of the unknowns of rebirth to pass through “Oblivion’s” maw and so are stuck in a stasis of their own making.

                                              The greatest irony is that the corruption the werewolves are fighting isn’t actually the Wyrm at all. It’s just the natural side effect of the Weaver’s efforts to keep the current world from sliding into the maw of Rebirth.

                                              By defying the natural pull of entropy, the Wraith Hierarchy, the Weaver Spirits (embodying man’s primal desire for a stable and knowable world... a fear of chaos and the unknown), the static vampires and the Technocracy all must build ever more elaborate constructs and schemes to keep it from all tumbling down... and it is the imperfections in this ever more rickety structure and compromises needed to keep it all afloat that creates the corruption the werewolves sense as The Wyrm.

                                              What the werewolves are actually fighting is not the True Wyrm, but the corruption in the construct built by Man to protect itself from the very primal fears of corruption/oblivion placed in them by the Impergium. The Nephandi are lunatics who drive themselves insane because their exposure to “The Truth” instills in them not a desire to clear away the rot so there’s room for new growth, but a nihilistic desire to “tear it all down.”

                                              Both the Banes and the Nephandi are self-destructive messes because they are NOT actually any sort of cosmic force of oblivion.

                                              The banes the werewolves fight are the natural corruption of a system that has grown to stagnant (like papering over the dry rot or applying another layer of bondo and paint to the rust on a car), but corruption keeps growing because the wolves are so focused on the symptoms, they can’t see the disease (i.e. the stagnation of the world) and even more that THEY are actually the root cause of that disease (the world stagnates because of its overwhelming fear of the unknown... of the monsters that lurk there and prey upon them... in short, the werewolves from days past).

                                              The Nephandi are the people who react to a cancer diagnosis by trying to kill themselves to just get it over with instead of devoting the time and energy it would take to actually cleanse the body of the poisons.

                                              In other words, the actual means of restoring the cosmic balance is to stop fighting “Oblivion” and allow the dead and corrupt to be consumed so they can be reborn. The hunt to destroy the “Wyrm-taint” and the Nephandi are just the two opposite extremes of dealing with it (“ignore it and hope it goes away” and “burn it all down” respectively).
                                              This was before I got into the Demon “layers of reality” aspect where concepts (murder) are people (Caine) are groups (early agricultural tribes) depending on the layer of reality you’re looking at. With that added in I think you could easily make the case that the very concept of the Antediluvians is synonymous with the Neverborn and Maeljin and the Yama Kings... it all just depends on which of the infinite layers of reality you’re looking at.

                                              This also might explain why there can be anywhere from 13 (three and ten) and 30 (three tens) of Antediluvians and its NOT a translation error... it’s a layer of reality viewpoint error. Caine is a singular vampire god who ruled a single advanced First City on one level while on another he’s the entire vampire race who descended in ones and twos upon many of the first communities as they moved into an agrarian society.

                                              In that sense it could be that Caine is the Antediluvians and the Antediluvians are Caine. Did the angels/spirits curse a single man (Caine) or an entire tribe (the Antediluvians)? Was Caine even a vampire at all or was his vision of the three angels merely the result of a very bad forced Awakening by Lilith and the vampiric state a contagious permanent paradox flaw that accompanied his paradigm of blood magic? Is the reason it is said that the Antediluvians can never die because of the vampiric curse -or- is it because they’re already dead and are now the Onceborn Malfeans sleeping in the Labryinth and whose dreams manifest as monsters like Ravnos during the Week of Nightmares?

                                              All sorts of ways you could take things if you choose.
