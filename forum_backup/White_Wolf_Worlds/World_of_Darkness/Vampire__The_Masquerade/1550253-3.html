    Something to be considered is the nature of the Palatine Treaty - if I remember correctly then it gives uncommon (possibly unique) status and powers to the Prince of San Francisco and to his Primogen, so it’s far from impossible that this would be a potent incentive for potent elders to become involved with San Francisco.

            I have not been able to find much information on the unique status of San Francisco. The Palatine Treaty was referenced in both A World of Darkness 1e and Anarch Cookbook, but we're not given many details. We need to interpret a lot. The Palatine Council is mentioned, but nothing else is known about it. I just assume it's just a normal primogen council, but renamed to denote the domain's status.

            My interpretation is that essentially San Francisco is an "independent" domain much like Siegfried's rule in Vancouver, but acknowledged by the Camarilla as part of the treaty in the 1946 Conclave (and thus potentially revoked by another conclave). It has to be allied to the Camarilla in some sense and probably bound to enforce the Six Traditions. But unlike other princes who are constantly worried about the Justicars and archons poking around in their business, Vannevar Thomas rules alone (although with the usual caveats of the primogen influencing him in some capacity). For now at least.

            Declaring cities as "free" is not unique. Siegfried declared himself as Prince of Vancouver "in the mid-forties," but rules without consent of the Camarilla. Prince Sarasine declared Sydney (Australia) as a free city in the mid-fifties also without consent of the Camarilla. Whether or not intended by the writers, it is hard not to imagine this was done in imitation of the Palatine of San Francisco except in their case it was not done under the legitimacy of a conclave. Both domains were remote in terms of the traditional power centers of the Camarilla, and such declarations would have been made at a time when both Princes believed the Camarilla would not make much effort to overthrow them for whatever reasons. I'm sure the Justicars intend at some point to reassert the Inner Circle's authority in both domains. It's likely in Petrodon's case that selecting Seattle as his base of operations was to keep eyes on both the Free States and Vancouver.

It's a fight that can be contained to just San Jose.

                            Of course, while geographically distinct from each because of San Francisco Bay, everything is within easy driving distance from each other. Kindred from the West Bay, East Bay, and South Bay probably all run into each other. They show up at the Vampire Club or other Kindred locale, make deals with each other, and so on. It's just that there are three separate power centers that control things in their third of the Bay, and characters have favored feeding places in "their" domain and less favored in other domains.

                            The more I think about this, I really like the way the Kindred of San Francisco Bay are developing. It provides a lot of opportunity for my PCs. For those who want to take the fight to the Camarilla, there are several options including a "safe" option for physical violence in San Jose. For those who want more traditional Camarilla salon scenes, there's the Vampire Club in SF which is open to all. If you want subtle intrigue, you have it. If you want a more tyrannical, heavy handed Prince to complain about, there's Prince Maxwell in Oakland.

                            I don't intend for the Bay Area to be the primary arena for the game. That remains Los Angeles. However, if players ever want to explore more or develop ties to actual Camarilla characters, it's there to be used. The rather wide expanse of the Free States covering multiple urban areas is a good way to distinguish it from other settings. Things typically take place in one city, and while there's nothing to theoretically stop PCs from residing in Chicago but travelling to Milwaukee, Rockford, Peoria, etc., that's rarely done. Both STs and players tend to hunker down in one metro area. While I could that too in LA, I like the sense that there's wide open freedom for anarchs to travel to nearby domains because the Tradition of Hospitality is not enforced. It means a lot more work for me, but I think my players will appreciate the freedom of such a large sandbox.

                                    I have terrible friends. One actually had ancient copies of the Dark Prince and Prince of the City novels from the nineties. So I actually read them to see what details I could pull from the city's NPCs. I read through them very fast and skipped bits so I may have missed some things. Apologies if I make any errors. There are also some continuity discrepancies between the books, but that might be attributed to unreliable narrators. There are two distinct groups of vampires in them that are very peculiar whom I want to discuss.

                                    The first is known as the Family. It's a group of vampires mainly from China who run most of Chinatown. It's headed by a huge, mysterious vampire in a demonic wooden mask called the Grandfather. The Grandfather's childe is a beautiful female vampire called Kwon, and her childe is a small, very young looking male vampire called Loo. They arrive in San Francisco in 1851 on a ship with all its crew dead (much like the Demeter in Dracula). Kwon ends up embracing an Irish-American crimp named Sullivan to help them navigate the unfamilar Western city of San Francisco. Although Kwon subsequently dies in the backstory, these three vampires of "the Family" have a somewhat important role in the city, and Sullivan serves as the protagonist of Dark Prince.

                                    The clan of these vampires is never mentioned. I assume it was meant to be some clan of the Kindred of the East when they were still conceived as mechanically part of the VtM line instead of the more Risen like undead of the Kindred of the East sourcebooks (which came out four years after Dark Prince). They are pretty much like any other vampires in the book. So how to translate them? I won't be using the KOTE sourcebook at all for my chronicle. The Family is clearly some traditional clan of Vampire so I am going with that. I still think they should be somewhat unusual and exotic for the traditional Camarilla setting vampires. So I'll be retconning them to be Lasombra anti-tribu vampires from Canton/Guangzhou. Most Chinese immigrants to California at this time came from the Pearl River Delta where Canton is located. In addition, Canton as a port was open to trade with much of India and the Middle East during the Song and early Ming dynasties. Very easy to justify that some Lasombra made his way to that port at some point in the Middle Ages and embraced someone local like the Grandfather. Separated from most vampire politics, these Lasombra would have never experienced the Anarch Revolt or the transformation of the clan into what it would become under the Sabbat. For reasons of their own, the Family decided to come to San Francisco during the Gold Rush, and they have remained ever since.

                                    I don't really think it makes sense for them to have embraced Sullivan as he would have been better in his role by being a ghoul. So to keep their numbers up to three, I'll keep Kwon surviving. Nobody actually knows that they are in fact "Lasombra", but have accepted them as being part of the domain. For lieu of any actual clan name, they just call them the Family. It's possible the Tremere (or perhaps some other elders) have figured things out, but if so it hasn't gotten out to the general Kindred population.

                                    The other peculiar group are three vampires descended from a line of Aztec vampires. The first is Hortator, an Aztec priest who was an advisor to Montezuma. He later becomes Vannevar's chief bodyguard and private counsel. Then there is Hortator's childe, Diego don Delfonso (a Spanish conquistador who came with Cortes). Then there is Delfonso's childe Domingo, a WWII era zoot suit hooligan. Delfonso has the honor of being the first vampire to reside in San Francisco (since 1777 shortly after the Spanish Presidio and Mission were built).

                                    Obviously Hortator can't be the Aztec priests's mortal name. It's a Latin noun meaning "inciter" or "encourager." It was also used as the title for the man who beat the drums of the galley slaves in the novel Ben-Hur, but I don't know if that is correct historical usage or something the author, Lew Wallace, made up. It's not clear to me why this character has been given this name, presumably a nickname used for him by some other vampire (Delfonso?) or self-applied once the priest learned Latin. It's also important to note that Hortator - since he was a human priest in service to Montezuma - could not have been a vampire long before he embraced Delfonso.

                                    These three vampires are all accepted by the local vampires of San Francisco. However, their clan is never mentioned, and in Prince of the City Vannevar even states that "his true bloodline was
                                    obscure, his clan unknown​". However, much like the Family, they are not treated as Caitiff.

                                    While as a general rule it's stated that the New World was free of vampires, by the time these books were written it was already canon that some vampires did in fact arrive in the Americas pre-Columbian contact. They also seemed to congregate in Mesoamerica, the first real urban civilization there. Diablerie: Mexico introduced the Wanderer (a Gangrel) and mentioned some other vampires, and the Chaos Factor introduced the Baali Huitzilopochtli. Both masqueraded as local deities. It's not clear if they sired childer, but these would be the best candidates for any line of local vampires that could have sired Hortator. Much later than these books, the revised Clanbook: Followers of Set introduced the Tlacique, a bloodline thought to be from the Setites. Giovanni Clanbook introduced the Pisanob, Aztec necromancers embraced by the Giovanni although I think they're clearly embraced post-Conquest. Still, it's possible Pochtli could have embraced Hortator who simply wandered off. For people who want to adhere as closely to canon as possible, these are your possible picks.

                                    For me, I'll be mixing some canon together. I'll be keeping the Tlacique, but making them a variant of the Gangrel except that their Protean defaults to the jaguar and not the world because that is the main predator in that region. Their in-clan Disciplines remain the Gangrel in-clan Disciplines, but they do have a heritage of indigenous Nahuallotl blood sorcery. Some of which Hortator knows. These vampires aren't popularly known/suspected to be Gangrel, but some elder vampires think they are most likely some ancient remnant of the Gangrel and thus afford them such acceptance. I'm sure other STs will select something different that works for them.

                                    I'm going to include these character into my version of San Francisco. They provide some nice local flavor, but more importantly I think they provide another reason why Vannevar's San Franciso was considered to be a hard target for the anarchs. If he has allies among some rather old, mysterious, and fairly unknown type of "Chinese" and "Aztec" vampires, it could give other vampires some pause. A surviving anarch whose coterie was wiped out in San Francisco by weird jaguar vampires and scary Chinese "sorcerers" would have some terrifying stories to tell.

                                                            Black Fox, I’d like to point out that Plato, grandfather of Western Philosophy, is said to have been born Aristocles and been nicknamed ‘Plato’ because he was an Absolute Unit (‘Plato’ meaning ‘Broad, thick’).

                                                            Heck, whole nations and generations have equated the word ‘Caesar’ with Imperial Majesty, despite ‘Caesar’ almost certainly deriving from a family nickname being ‘Hairy’.

                                                            Trust me, if there’s one thing History and Mythology has a long record of, it’s quite distinguished individuals being condemned to be forever remembered by nicknames.
