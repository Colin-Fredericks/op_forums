    I have an absolutely massive investment in VtM products. Getting me to switch over would take something truly extraordinary. Mind, I did say that about V20 too, though. Since I am a massive Noddist fan, things like the Lores and Becketts Jyhad Diary pulled me in. Although the Brujah sections disappointed. But I am not seeing anything like Beckett's diary here.

    The Bad:
    • I don't like the art and presentation. Very subjective opinion there. It reminds me of the change from the old Vampire cards to the new "webpage" looking ones.
    • Several metaplot developments are deal-breakers to me. Brujah leaving the Camarilla, and the whole Hecatea mess in particular. Fall of Vienna. Stuff like the last two ruins my sense of internal plausibility and suspension of disbelief.
    • Lack of elders and elder powers.


    The good
    • I like the idea of some clan curses like the Lasombra getting worse over time.
    • Touchstones are a concept I'll back-port for some types of games. The mechanics would be totally changed, but I feel the concept is strong.
    • The fiction is very high quality. I've borrowed books from friends just to enjoy it.


    The neutral
    • The Second Inquisition seems a natural outgrowth of increased kine handheld media capacity. I don't really buy the learning curve they've had though.


    The short version is, V5 wants you to play a very specific sort of game and is not an asset if you want any other sort of game. In fact it would be detrimental compared to previous versions of the game that can do more things. And it has a laser-like focus on the whole anarch, anarch versus Camarilla sort of games we were totally played out on by the mid-90s. It feels like it could have been a softback alternate setting for the people who want those games.

            Originally posted by Vamps Like Us View Post
            That's a lot to quote, but as someone who seems to like to discuss mechanics, do you think there is a way for V5 to fix the problems without putting out a new core edition, thus being V6?
            Oh, I can go on, and on, and on about the lore side of things too. My focus on the mechanics is that there's more objective facts about them to discuss in a neutral fashion. I have written massive posts about why the attack on the Vienna Chantry as written is wildly suspension of disbelief breaking for me, point out dozens of factors, write paragraphs about how to account for the WoD vs. our world, and so on. But in the end, lots of people like the fall of the Pyramid and are willing to overlook any iffy bits in the execution just as I've come to terms with plenty of meh parts of the metaplot in previous editions.

            To the question? Whether you want to call it 5.5 or 6, I don't think so. While some of the changes I would make are simple, and others would have to be extensive, I would estimate a need to revise ~80% of the rules on some level. If your errata starts getting to be 5% the length of the book or more... it's time to consider a new core book. The only reason I think you could get away with V5.5 is that there aren't that many V5 books, and most of the supplements aren't that mechanics heavy so they could remain useful with minimal to no errata (though there's some non-rules stuff I'd want to redo if I were in charge.... looking at you Cam V5).

            Do you think it's possible to get a more classic feel that some of us wanted?
            I think it depends on what you mean by "classic feel." I think there's a lot of potential in changes that aren't at all "classic" VtM. The idea of having both a Clan Bane and a Clan Compulsion for example is a novel inclusion in V5. In general, I think the Banes are a step up esp. the Severity concept. The Compulsions are great as an idea, but the execution leaves a lot to be desired, and are very responsible for some bad classic feels (ex: the Ravnos Compulsion completely destroys the attempt to salvage them from their racist beginnings).

            A new edition with a new feel is a good thing!

            I think the balance act V5 failed at, was bridging the editions. While V5 doesn't have to be mechanically backwards compatible with pre-V5, it should have been more conceptually so. As I've said elsewhere, there are concepts that make perfect sense in the previous editions that are hampered (if not outright denied if the concept is heavily focused on supernatural stuff) by the system. What I feel is important here is that if V5 says, "you can't play X out of the box any more," I need a compelling reason. Axing some of the racism hardboiled into some of the old stuff? Valid. Because you want the list of Disciplines to be shorter (even if they're getting bloated as new books at new powers too them anyway), or you want the new spiffy Hunger mechanics to make my character's undeath unbearable because they do something that involves a lot of rolling? Yeah, no thanks.

            Or would fixing the rushed out problems be too much of a move away from what V5 is for those that like it? I ask as someone who flipped through it, watched a youtube play session, and read about it but never played or read it cover to cover.
            I can't really speak for the V5 fans. I know there are rules changes I would make which I've suggested in various places that some V5 fans have loved and say they've adopted as house rules, others that V5 fans generally say that they're ambivalent towards and they wouldn't care either way, and some that are extremely divided in reactions.

            I think that balancing act is possible, but I'm biased in a fashion that means I'm not qualified to do it.

                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                        Weird, because I felt the whole Sabbat vs. Camarilla thing totally spoiled the Anarch vs. Camarilla thing. They removed the central conflict of the setting. Old vs. Young vampires and replaced it with Good Vampires vs. Bad Ones.
                        I think this phrasing is difficult for the conversation because it's far too black and white for what either conflict is about.

                        Let's remember that Smiling Jack, the strongest of the Anarchs in Bloodlines 1, is ~200 years older than LaCroix, Camarilla Prince. And lots of people cite Bloodlines 1 as returning to the Anarchs vs. Camarilla state of VtM despite being made far later in the original VtM run. People side with the Anarchs a lot because Nines, Jack, and Damsel are clearly "good" vampires, while LaCroix and the Sheriff are obviously "bad," and a few others are "bad" if you poke beyond their better PR.

                        The Sabbat and Camarilla have, as a major part of their conflict, the threat of ancient vampire blood-gods as an issue: the Sabbat considers them active existential threats, while the Camarilla don't think they matter if they're even out there any more. It's all about Young vs. Old, just on a scale of millennia instead of centuries.

                        The Sabbat (at least in Revised) can make a great case that they're "bad" by human standards, but as they're not humans any more, they're "good" by vampire standards. The Camarilla struggles to really make a great argument on either side of that.

                        I think it's more important to acknowledge what I've said, and what Trollroot is saying: being overly focused on one narrative isn't great for VtM. "Young" punk Anarchs vs. the stodgy old Camarilla was a great and evocative start for the game, but the game grew to be more, and it's better for before more.

                                                I greatly prefer the V20 mechanics... V5 goes far too abstract for my tastes and veers in places towards trying to be a narrative in real time (which can drag you out of character as you have to resolve aspects not from your character’s viewpoint, but from what is best for the narrative) vs. being played as events experienced by your PCs that can later be recounted in story form.

                                                I’m also not a fan of the rearranging of disciplines; particularly turning the straight-forward physical disciplines and turning them into grab bags of powers that often leave gaping holes; ex. Potence 1 in V20 gives you all the benefits of an extra dot of Strength; lift more, jump further, punch harder, etc. and a boost when you spend blood on it. In V5 you either deal more damage to mortals (but not objects or vampires) or jump a very specific distance (which is quite odd given how utterly non-specific the resolutions of other physical tasks are... it also takes no account of basic strength and so unless you have multiple ranks of Potence it ends up being significantly less than just a non-boosted Str+Athletics check could achieve in V20 (5 successes on a a difficulty 3 check will achieve results equal to that V5 Potence 1 power).

                                                The less I say about V5 Touchstones and Humanity the better; suffice it to say I much prefer the internalized Virtues to requiring your beliefs be dependent on mortal morality pets the GM is encouraged to screw with and because they must be unchanging you are punished for not being a creepy control freak stalker.

                                                Similarly, there’s no backstop on Humanity loss in V5. It takes real work at depravity to sink below a 4 in V20 (or any of the previous versions... which I think is ultimately what people mean by V20, at least that’s what I mean by it).

                                                I’m also not a fan of Rouse Checks/Hunger dice over the V20 blood pool; particularly the change where the ONLY way to ever be fully satiated in V5 requires you to murder your victim. Drink a pint of blood off a hundred people and you’re still hungry (instead of bloated like a tick)... kill one person by drinking five to eight pints and you’re full.

                                                To be blunt, in V5 you don’t actually feed on blood, you feed on murder with blood as an inferior substitute. The only way to ever be fully in control is to be a cold blooded murderer with morality pets to give you the conviction that you’re not actually so bad after all as you micromanage their lives so they remain stuck instead of being able to grow and change like healthy human beings do.

                                                My general feeling is that Humanity in V5 should be renamed “Delusion” as high Humanity doesn’t actually make you more moral since murdering and manipulating are required to maintain that state... it’s just how good you are at lying to yourself.

                                                Contrast the humane vampire of previous editions who can be fully satiated by animal blood, blood bags or small sips from volunteers (and because you can keep yourself “topped off” there is little to no danger of ripping their throat out) and who can allow the humans they care about to grow, change and experience full lives because their morality isn’t dependent on others telling them they’re a good person every time they experience a setback (i.e. take aggravated willpower damage or suffer a stain).

                                                High Humanity in V20 and before actually meant being humane; not hurting people or stealing from them and feeling remorse and a desire to repent when you did screw up.

                                                About the only mechanical bits I can appreciate are the better attribute spread (just as I find the CoD spread of power, precision and resistance superior), making Willpower entirely into points instead of also being a dice pool to resist certain actions, and decoupling Generation from the point buy of character creation.

                                                The latter I’ve even made a house-rule version of for V20 where Gen 8-16 are all trade-offs of raw power vs. fewer weaknesses that are roughly equivalent. Gen 7 or less still break that curve so there remains an incentive to diablerize your way up, but in terms of PC creation your Generation is now an independent choice instead of something to take up background dots.

                                                I also liked a few individual discipline powers and ported those over to V20.

                                                Now, one thing I DO like about V5 are the metaplot elements; though with the caveat that they work FAR better as events players are experiencing now instead of ones that happened 5-15 years earlier.

                                                For example; I just dropped the destruction of the Vienna Chantry into my chronicle. All the factions are in turmoil over who might really be behind it (and what this means in terms of favors owed by and to the Tremere). The Tremere Primogen (and head of the local Chantry) hasn’t been seen since a week before news of the attack hit and the Whip and next in line for head of the local chantry aren’t actually the same vampire.

                                                Just before the dust settles on that I’ll be dropping news of the Scouring of London onto the weekly gathering in Elysium. Vienna is happenstance, London is coincidence... now the NPCs will be bracing for confirmation of enemy action.

                                                The PCs haven’t encountered it yet, but when they actually follow through on their plans to take out the Sabbat Pack in the satellite tourist trap town to claim the territory as their own (thus side-stepping the local Prince) they’ll learn the Pack has been essentially abandoned by the Sabbat hierarchy who’ve gone off to the Middle East to fight and shortly after that some (not all) of the Elders will start to feel the Beckoning to the Middle East as well to build the creepiness of the event.

                                                I’ve got basically the entire V5 metaplot on an accelerated timeline for my chronicle and how each of the local vampires NPCs are likely to react to each one as they come. Playing out the effects of all V5’s backstory events is just far more compelling than reading of them in the past tense.

                                                V5 has some interesting ideas, and the older system did need SOME cleanup (particularly the combat engine), but V5 feels a LOT like the same sort of over-correction that D&D 4E was, including the shift towards more narrative-based rules, extreme streamlining of certain mechanics, and “video-gamey” changes to the powers and abilities.

                                                I do think there’s a middle ground to be found where probably 90% of both the V20/R/2e/1e and V5 players would be satisfied. But it will almost certainly take a D&D 5e-like compromise edition to actually get there as V5 has far too many assumptions built into its mechanics to serve as the compromise foundation.
