          My answer would be "always". However, I can't honestly answer your question because 1) I don't run or play in Sabbat games, and 2) when I ST (Camarilla games where the Sabbat are just NPCs) I prefer the original portrayal of the Sabbat rather than late 2e/Revised. Originally all (or almost all) Sabbat were put through the Creation Rites. The Revised era was responsible for saying that was not really the case and only used in times of war, and I felt it transformed the Sabbat into the Camarilla-lite.

          That built on something that began in late 2e when the clanbooks for the Lasombra and Tzimisce came out. By that time, Andrew Greenberg was no longer Developer, and I feel the new powers that be didn't really believe in the unique elements of the Sabbat. For some odd reason, those clanbooks presented these Sabbat clans as acting very similar to the Camarilla clans. We got very little of the unique culture of the Sabbat in them. Their version of the Lasombra and Tzimisce was basically the same as if they were Camarilla clans with little distinction between the Sabbat version and their antri-tribu. Prior to those first clanbooks, there was a very heavy emphasis on the random nature of Sabbat embraces.

          The first Sabbat we ever meet In Chicago By Night are random embraces. Phillipe Rigaud was just an random soldier in camp when Sabbat arrived and picked him after reading his unconscious mind. Although selected to a degree, it was just because he best fit their criteria for cannon fodder in some random army camp. Wendy Wade just had the misfortune of staying out too late at the local singles bar. When 2e CbN came out and we met a Nomad Pack, we see mostly other random embraces. Cameron was just grabbed off the streets and despite being a "loser" was embraced by a Lasombra. The other members aren't even given descriptions to their embrace although it's likely just as random. The only member who was actually selected for embrace by his sire... was the mortal servant of a Sabbat Infernalist and therefore picked to deliberately carry out the demon's goals.

          Now the original Sabbat Players Guide is a bit of a mixed bag. While conforming to the rule of random embraces in time of war, they do say that in times of peace the True Sabbat do choose "more carefully". But this is supposed to be done by two members of the Pack and "carefully" means they observe them for a few days or weeks. So not very carefully in my opinion. It is less random, but not too much. They just aren't spending the time needed to evaluate a particular mortal to decide if they are "worthy", and not all pack members make picks. It's still fairly superficial criteria.

          I think this works fine for the Sabbat. They have a great turnover (including even whether the person they picked will even survive the Creation Rites) so it doesn't pay to be selective. If you spent a long time agonizing over who to embrace, you'll be disappointed after your Lupine hunt destroyed most of the pack. Or if they mouth off and is slain in Monomacy. Or any other of the many stupid ass violence the Sabbat perpetrate and gets so many members killed quickly.

          I also liked it because if was the closest thing we had to traditional vampire movies when anyone the vampire fed on became a vampire. You didn't need to be "special" to become a vampire. You just had to have bad luck by being in the wrong place at the wrong time.

          That also fit an important theme to the Sabbat which is that humanity is unimportant. Only being a vampire is important. What one was like as mortal had dubious value as to how well they'd succeed at being a vampire. The housewife might become a better Lasombra than the MBA graduate. Whereas there was often continuity between a character's mortal life and their undead existence for a Camarilla vampire, there was just a complete break for the Sabbat.

          I liked that. It really defined the differences between the two sects.

          But the clanbooks and Revised just throw that out into the garbage bin by explaining that essentially the two sects acted the same except for perhaps the Paths of Enlightenment.

          I think that was a huge mistake. It's important for the vampires in the two sects to act very differently, and different approaches to selecting new vampires was important to that.

              My general problem with shovelheads is that I don't buy this method being so effective in making for Vampires that embrace the sect's ideology to any degree, or see reason to devolve into violence so thoroughly so quickly. To me it would make sense that a considerable number of shovelheads aren't as easy to indoctrinate as a Pack expects and I never personally saw the books address that.

              Does it make sense that many, maybe most work? In a fictional setting, yes. I know it has a lot of brainwashing techniques there. It is a quick succession of shocking experiences, a lot of shouting and ritual, and there's the Vinculum as soon as possible. But I also know that brainwashing isn't all that effective all the time, specially fast techniques based on shock treatment and not customized for the target. The result may be the person accepting some of the stuff they're told at first and engaging on some activities, but hardly with any conviction and it wears down almost as fast.

              That's a briefing of my personal problem with the Creation Ritae as presented. I would personally prefer that it doesn't only have failure in the form of people not surviving it, but also a number of shovelheads that rebel almost immediately or soon after, and others that defect the Sect entirely during their first nights, and then explore how the Sabbat deal with them (and their freedom from their sires).

                      Averages are simplifications for groups that have Variance. Chances in bulk become Frequencies. Embracing people at random doesn't mean just that most of those won't seem like adequate candidates, it also means they're nobodies from your perspective, but in actuality are peoples of all stripes, with all sorts of baggage, morals and ways of dealing with new situations. If 90% of the population will respond as you predict under such circumstances if they survive and only 10% will become a problem, then every single Mass Embrace with at least 6 shovelheads in the end is more likely than not to produce a problem, and I'm being extremely generous with the numbers, even considering all the things you stated, and adding that I don't think every Pack has a Priest with Presence (this is the price you pay for anarchy, not every cell will follow the book as obvious at it seems).

                      ​Take Thoth's example for instance. In my head it is ludicrous to even think it had a chance to work. Getting an entire military base is staking your chances against you, royally. They've had military drilling doing the brainwash thing for far longer and more consistently, they are prepared to deal with all sorts of attacks, they've been prepared to the possibility of death and they have very clear duties to follow. All the effectiveness aside, you only need one of them rebelling at the right moment to bring down the whole operation in any of many ways, and a high probability of having more rebels than not.

                      Paths after that have the same problem. One thing is for you to explain and paint a gruesome picture of what Wighthood is and what a Path can do to help. Another is the other person buying it. They have to believe you're honest, then they have to believe you're right, then they have to believe you have all the relevant information, then they have to lose all hope of unknown possibilities still existing, and then they have to really be able to muster the will to still go ahead, because they may have all sorts of reasons to really not want to from an emotional and even moral perspective, regardless of rational belief in this abstract threat (it isn't imaginary, but it is abstract). I don't see so many people buying into it, much less almost everyone in this position, specially if you consider how much dedication a Path demands.

                      It isn't an attack on the notion or on Thoth's plot. I think it was well thought and fun as far as a game goes. It is just that I personally do not buy it. Everyone has some things they find harder to suspend their disbelief, this is one such a thing for me. It doesn't mean I won't have Mass Embraces either, it is just that in my games it is far more complex to deal with the shovelheads, and every Pack has its own ways to deal with the actual results. The notion that shovelheads work almost seamlessly is another legend spread among the Camarilla. And the only reason my players never created shovelheads themselves is that my players never wanted to play as Sabbat. They're big fussed grognards that still see Requiem as a new fad that will go out soon and V20 (core) is just messing with a game that was pretty fine already... heck, we still stick to D&D 3.5 for the gods' sake.

                                      Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                      Does it make sense that many, maybe most work? In a fictional setting, yes. I know it has a lot of brainwashing techniques there. It is a quick succession of shocking experiences, a lot of shouting and ritual, and there's the Vinculum as soon as possible.                                       I agree. This is why those who have been put through the Creation Rites are False Sabbat for an unspecified period of time before they become True Sabbat.

                                      There has to be a large amount of failures. Not every vampire is going to survive the Creation Rites. A fair number of those who do survive will fall to the Wassail, or be destroyed by the pack if they don't seem to be advancing quickly enough on their first few dots of a Path. And given the Sabbat is usually conspicuous in their violence, many are going to attract the attention of those who will destroy them - hunters, Camarilla archon groups like the Wolfpack, Lupines, and any other number of enemies.

                                      I don't believe that those who survive the Creation Rites are going to be loyal to the Sabbat "just because". They'll be confused and disoriented and rely on their new "friends" for a while, but that isn't loyalty. The only way Sabbat brainwashing can work is in the long term, keeping them isolated and under watch. It'll likely be many weeks, months, or even years before their loyalty is assured. And in the meantime, a lot just aren't going to make it - they'll be destroyed at the hands of the Sabbat's enemies or by the Sabbat themselves because they obviously aren't working out. But I think the vinculum, the support they receive to help them withstand the Beast, love bombing and other cult indoctrination techniques, and the development of cool powers will help them adjust. They'll become loyal at least to their packmates, even if not to the Sabbat as a whole. (This is probably one reason the Sabbat is so fractious, and the sect always imploded.)

                                      The Sabbat isn't going to be able to rely on shovelheads to go after their enemies unless 1) they are able to make use of certain Disciplines to compel them, and 2) keep them hungry until the time they unleash them against their enemies because the Hunger alone will cause them to attack. This won't be easy and can easily backfire.

                                      The huge turnover of Sabbat neonates is one reason I have no interest to play or run a Sabbat game. Most players want some level of assurance their PCs will last long enough to get some solid roleplay. It is an extra challenge for an ST to do that while remaining true to the Sabbat. (And I understand why later Developers decided to turn the Sabbat into Camarilla-lite in order to avoid this problem. It just defeats the purpose of having the Sabbat as a setting element.)

                                      However, the game has always had a problem with this idea of assumed instant loyalty. There is a lot that gets passed over by many players and STs. For example, if you were embraced by the True Brujah in 2022, why would you care at all about the normal Brujah? You're not going to feel loyalty to some ancient antedeluvian who was destroyed thousands of years ago. You can't even argue that it will be done out of fear as the ordinary Brujah neither believe in the True Brujah or hunt them. Clan or bloodline loyalty should never be automatic especially the older the mortal who is embraced, and the more confidant he is thinking on his own. Clan culture and organization is only going to work over the long term. Loyalty earned in the short term will always need to be explained.

                                          The revised Creation Rites is something else from the original version. Most of how the Sabbat works was entirely upended for playability, even though you can still see a lot of authors (and players) stuck with the pre-revised ideas, which in my opinion is a shame.

                                          I like the term shovelhead, but even in revised, the way it is used is not to create new progeny, it is to create unliving weapons. No longer is humanity stripped, but it is more of a devastating blow to the psyche to be killed, embraces and then having to crawl up through the earth you were buried in - and then being herded and goaded towards an unknown enemy. It is probably also a tool of propaganda, from the Camarilla, on how dangerous the Sabbat is and how little concern they have for the masquerade, and innocent life.

                                          Have I as a player or as a ST been doing shovelheads? In pre-revised days, sure. Even in a post-revised LARP, but that was a sidetrack single-night event and none of the shovelheads survived.

                                          Otherwise, I can’t think of it. ST Transylvania Chronicles with a group that were exclusively Tzimsice, and they always choose their progeny. Granted they were elders when joining the Sabbat. They even murdered the Paris Sabbat for being slow, but they avoided mass embraces even then because of its unreliability. This was about 100 sessions without mass embraces.

                                          I played as a Sabbat, and I was the only one in the group to create progeny, two of them as an impulse thing related to the Toreador Antitribu weakness, with a third candidate being kept as a ghoul and undergoing the process of shedding her humanity and being indoctrinated into a path already before her embrace. The game ended before that, but she had probably been a ghoul for 45 out of the maybe 50 sessions, and would have gotten a normal embrace and hastened creation rites.

                                          Ran a game set in alternate LA, where the Sabbat had actually won when they fought the Anarchs. One of the player characters was a survivor from that and the closest to a shovelhead. Not buried, just embraced and shoved to the forefront. Earned his creation rites by surviving and being part of the siege. In none of the ~50 sessions did any player nor NPC perform a mass burial, but no player created a childe either. To be fair, this was just internal politics with no Camarilla (or Anarchs) involvement, and the only “hostile” presence was some Setites, but they were to weak to pose a direct threat, so the idea to suddenly produce fodder probably never struck anyone.

                                                      I haven't played or storytold Sabbat yet for my Players. But I think it works just the same as the classical "Mistake (maybe not) Out of Nowhere Embrace" that so many Vampire videogames do. And I don't even believe that shovelheads most of the time get indoctrinated. Like... what better way to fuck a city's Prince than leaving a dozen of famished, lost, mad, ignorant and guideless vampires in his little Camarilla town? It's like setting bombs, only that its Masquerade breaching as hell, and will make the entire city and the Cam society have to move a lot of pieces and pull favors to take care of. So I would definitely make a starting point for a Sabbat chronicle, be the PCs in this sect or not from that moment forward (in case they end up dealing with a very reasonable and calm Anarch, Autarkis or Camarilla).
