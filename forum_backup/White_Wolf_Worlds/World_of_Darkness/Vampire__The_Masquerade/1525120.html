    Vampire V20 Dark Age : So many questions

    11-06-2023, 12:11 PM
    Hi everyone !

    I am a new player on V20TDA and I am struggling on rules questions^^ I hope you can help me !

    I am currently playing in a new campaign and am still a human for now. Here are my questions :

    1) If I have a sword and shield, does it increase to difficulty to attack me to +2 ? (Means 8 on the first action, 9 on the second, and no more because actions that have a difficulty of 10 are impossible right ?)

    2) Isn't the sword really more interesting than anything else ? I mean, take the broadsword. STR+2(L) damage, with 3str min to use. Same stats as the hand axe, except you can use the sword with 2 hands to get +1 damage and also swords give the opponent +1 difficulty to attack you ? What if I wear 2 swords at the same time ? +2 difficulty ? Or is the axe really underpowered ?

    3) Also, 2 strength minimum to wear a knife ? What kind of knife is it ?

    3) I see in the armor table that they give a penalty of 0, 1 or 2. It's written that this penalty applies "initiative and all dexterity based actions". What does that mean ? Is it a +1 difficulty to dexterity dice pools ? Is it a 1 die penalty to dexterity dice pools ?

    4) After a successful attack with some weapon, what exactly do you add to the damage dice pool ? The amount of success on the attack ? (minimum 1, I guess) Or rather 1 sucess is spent on confirming the attack is successful and the rest of the successes add to the damage dice pool ?

    5) How exactly does work armor soaking ? Let's consider I'm humain. I can't soak lethal damage without armor. But what happen when I actually wear an armor ? Do I only use the rating of the armor as dice pool ? Do I add my stamina ? We had a real debate with my ST because since him, it's always been only armor rating as dice pool in all other games in world of darkness even though that's not actually written that way in V20TDA. Since I'm new to this game, I'm curious as to what the actual rule is.

    6) I've read that if difficulty reaches 10, then the action is impossible. So if during my turn I get attacked by someone wearing a sword and shield, and I declare a multiple action to defend and then attack myself, that means on my second action, the difficult is +2, and if I add the fact my opponent wears a sword and a shield it becomes 10, and I can't attack ?

    7) Same question if I attack first but I choose to aim a weakness in the armor of my opponent (difficulty +2 but no armor dice in the soak dice pool). The difficulty becomes 10, right ? (6+2 for sword+shield + 2 for aiming the weakness) so it's basically not possible ?

    8) If I choose to aim for armor weakness and armor doesn't add to soak damage, can a human soak lethal damage ? Does it still have only stamina ? Or no soak at all ?

    9) How long exactly does Obtenebration 5 power last ? There's no detail about any duration.

    Thank you in advance for your knowledge, I will probably have many more questions !​

      The rules of DA20 are somewhat patchy in terms of little matters like 'editing' which does make them harder to understand in places.

      Originally posted by Beastelion View Post
      1) If I have a sword and shield, does it increase to difficulty to attack me to +2 ? (Means 8 on the first action, 9 on the second, and no more because actions that have a difficulty of 10 are impossible right ?)
      ​
      And the answer is ... yes. Though this is something of a rules gotcha, as it's different in previous editions, and the additional difficulty of attacking an enemy with a sword is somewhat smuggled in via a table.

      Originally posted by Beastelion View Post
      2) Isn't the sword really more interesting than anything else ? I mean, take the broadsword. STR+2(L) damage, with 3str min to use. Same stats as the hand axe, except you can use the sword with 2 hands to get +1 damage and also swords give the opponent +1 difficulty to attack you ? What if I wear 2 swords at the same time ? +2 difficulty ? Or is the axe really underpowered ?
      ​
      Basically, the writers decided that swords were cool, so they made them better than everything else. So you are right, swords really are better than anything else, particularly as they oppose an attack difficulty.

      Originally posted by Beastelion View Post
      3) Also, 2 strength minimum to wear a knife ? What kind of knife is it ?
      A knife with badly-written stats? Remember, you can use a weapon when your Strength is less than minimum (which is why the word "minimum" is a bit inappropriate here) at a difficulty increased by one for every point of Strength you are lacking.

      Originally posted by Beastelion View Post
      3) I see in the armor table that they give a penalty of 0, 1 or 2. It's written that this penalty applies "initiative and all dexterity based actions". What does that mean ? Is it a +1 difficulty to dexterity dice pools ? Is it a 1 die penalty to dexterity dice pools ?
      This is badly-written, but a penalty in DA20 should be presumed to be an increased difficultly, rather than an decreased dice pool.

      Originally posted by Beastelion View Post
      4) After a successful attack with some weapon, what exactly do you add to the damage dice pool ? The amount of success on the attack ? (minimum 1, I guess) Or rather 1 sucess is spent on confirming the attack is successful and the rest of the successes add to the damage dice pool ?
      All successes on an attack, after defensive maneuvers, are added to the damage stat of a weapon. So, for a close combat attack with a sword, roll Dexterity + Melee, take the successes from this roll and add your Strength + 2 in additional dice (or Strength + 3 using the sword two-handed), and then this becomes your damage pool. So, if a character who has Strength 3 and Potence 2 makes an attack with 4 successes, the character will roll 9 dice, and then add 2 automatic successes for Potence. This is, of course, why you want armour...

      Originally posted by Beastelion View Post
      5) How exactly does work armor soaking ? Let's consider I'm humain. I can't soak lethal damage without armor. But what happen when I actually wear an armor ? Do I only use the rating of the armor as dice pool ? Do I add my stamina ? We had a real debate with my ST because since him, it's always been only armor rating as dice pool in all other games in world of darkness even though that's not actually written that way in V20TDA. Since I'm new to this game, I'm curious as to what the actual rule is.
      It is supposed to be that a mortal only uses their armour dice to soak damage (plus potentially Fortitude if the character is a ghoul), versus a Cainite who uses Stamina, plus armour, plus Fortitude. Did we mention that these rules really needed a bit of editing before publication?

      Originally posted by Beastelion View Post
      6) I've read that if difficulty reaches 10, then the action is impossible. So if during my turn I get attacked by someone wearing a sword and shield, and I declare a multiple action to defend and then attack myself, that means on my second action, the difficult is +2, and if I add the fact my opponent wears a sword and a shield it becomes 10, and I can't attack ?
      An ordinary action can reach difficulty 10, but you cannot choose to take an action at difficulty 10 if you have taken multiple actions. So, yes, rules as written, you can't block or parry and then make an attack, unless using Celerity.

      Originally posted by Beastelion View Post
      7) Same question if I attack first but I choose to aim a weakness in the armor of my opponent (difficulty +2 but no armor dice in the soak dice pool). The difficulty becomes 10, right ? (6+2 for sword+shield + 2 for aiming the weakness) so it's basically not possible ?
      Why, yes, as it happens. I mean, going into battle with armour, a sword, and a shield is a medieval classic, and was pretty effective in defensive terms, but there are a couple of issues with the rules.

      Originally posted by Beastelion View Post
      8) If I choose to aim for armor weakness and armor doesn't add to soak damage, can a human soak lethal damage ? Does it still have only stamina ? Or no soak at all ?
      No soak at all (unless the character is a ghoul with Fortitude).

      Originally posted by Beastelion View Post
      9) How long exactly does Obtenebration 5 power last ? There's no detail about any duration.
      ​
      Assume a power will last for a scene if not otherwise stated.

      Did I mention that the rules really, really needed an edit?

          Originally posted by James_Willoughby View Post

          This is badly-written, but a penalty in DA20 should be presumed to be an increased difficultly, rather than an decreased dice pool.
          I always understood the armor penalty to be a dice pool reduction. First, I don't think there is a general rule about penalties being increased difficulties when not specified: difficulty increases are actually always stated plainly to my memory in the book. Second, one can infer it's a dice penalty by its applying to Initiative as well: Initiative is derived from the dots in Dexterity (+Wits+Celerity) and similarly penalised by injury penalties, which are dice pool reductions. It's coherent system-wise to say the armor, too, penalises dice pools as well as the trait (initiative) derived from that pool. Also, an increase in difficulty would make armor way too disadvantageous, with all the other combat factors that increase difficulty. As a dice pool reduction, it can be seen that it remains useful to wear an armor, as you lose some dice on Dexterity rolls but gain more dice on soak rolls. This is consistent with the widespread use of armour in the setting and its perceived combat value.
          Increased difficulty becomes more and more an issue the larger your dice pool is. Warriors would get nothing done that way.

                              Originally posted by Hermeticus View Post

                              I always understood the armor penalty to be a dice pool reduction. First, I don't think there is a general rule about penalties being increased difficulties when not specified: difficulty increases are actually always stated plainly to my memory in the book.                               Increased difficulty becomes more and more an issue the larger your dice pool is. Warriors would get nothing done that way.
                              Increased difficulty is, on the whole, less burdensome for characters, as long as you don't have too many penalties. The system is, of course, broken, whichever way you look at it.

                              Let's have a look at a practical example.

                              Albert is a mortal mercenary (he's from France, so remember that the 't' in his name is silent); he has only been in the profession for a short period of time, so his statistics are fairly average. He has Strength of 4, and Dexterity and Wits of 2, as well as Melee 2. He's armed with a sword (what is generally called a "broadsword" in RPGs, including DAV20, but what military historians would call an "arming sword" or "knightly sword") and has some basic armour (composite armour in game terms; the Dungeons & Dragons notion of composite armour didn't exist in real life, but we will assume is it composite armour in a literal sense, such as a gambeson, with maybe a mail coif, splints, and so on). He has left his shied at home today.

                              Baudouin is a mortal peasant (he's also from France, hence the name); he has a bit of experience in fighting, so he has fairly good statistics for a peasant, with Strength of 4, as well as Dexterity and Wits of 2, as well as Melee 2. He has a pickaxe. He's also naked. We won't ask why; perhaps he's from a religious sect similar to the Adamites, or maybe he has run into Albert whilst bathing in a river. Maybe he's just a bit strange.

                              So, Albert and Baudouin run into each other, and end up in a fight. Who is likely to win?

                              The answer is clearly Baudouin. If Albert reduces his Dexterity by 1, due to his armour, the problems start with initiative. Baudouin will win initiative 55% of the time, compared to Albert winning 36%, and both characters acting simultaneously 9% of the time. Each attack of Baudouin will hit 80.05% of the time, with an average of 2.1 successes. His pickaxe gives him Strength+2 lethal damage which, with the successes of his attacks, will, on average 4.05 points of damage to Albert. The armour worn by Albert will not protect him, as a pickaxe has armour penetration of 3. Meanwhile, Albert will attack with a success rate of 73.99%, which is not too bad, with an average of 1.7 successes. His sword also gives him Strength+2 lethal damage; when we add the successes of his attacks, he will inflict 3.85 points of damage. Also not too bad, but less than Baudouin. Now, there's enough randomness in the system that either has at least a fair chance of victory in a battle, but Baudouin certainly has the edge, particularly as he's far more likely to strike first, and reduce Albert's dice pool further before the mercenary has a chance to strike back.. The moral here is that, if, as a mercenary you run into a naked peasant with a pickaxe, you better hope that you can either outrun him, or that you have remembered to pack your shield today; the extra difficultly imposed by your shield will probably save your life.

                              Incidentally, the situation would be worse if Albert and Baudouin were Cainites. As mortals they can't soak lethal damage, so the vagaries of luck on dice rolls are likely to have exaggerated impacts. With two Cainites, the ability to soak lethal damage will slow down the combat, and will likely allow the averaging of dice rolls to work in Baudouin's favour. Similarly, a battle of a hundred mercenaries with swords and composite armour, versus a hundred naked peasants with pickaxes, will definitely lead to a peasant victory; the benefits of initiative and the extra 0.2 successes will quickly add up (the peasants will collectively do an additional 10 points of damage on the first turn of combat, even if both sides strike simultaneously), whilst mercenary attrition will mean that the mercenaries will soon be outnumbered, with the obvious consequences.

                              So, what is going wrong in the combat system here?
                              1. Initiative as Dexterity + Wits + dice means that any penalties to Dexterity have a disproportionate effect on combat. In the first edition of Vampire: the Dark Ages initiative was run off of Wits + Alertness, which is probably a better starting point.
                              2. Weapons and armour really are not well defined, nor well statted, in this edition. People will probably object to the pickaxe here, saying that the weapon here is supposed to be a military pick, horseman's pick, maul, or whatever other late medieval weapon which is designed to punch through armour. But how are we to know this, as weapons aren't described? We will assume a pickaxe to be a pickaxe. Similarly, the stats really are not quite right for the weapons; a pickaxe (or whatever it is supposed to be) should do less damage than a sword, whilst punching through armour, giving more of a meaningful choice as to what weapon to choose. Similarly, armour would be better reducing specific Abilities, rather than Attributes. Wearing armour should not meaningfully reduce once's abilities in combat, nor should it reduce damage inflicted.
                              3. But the biggest issue here is overage, which is the conversion of combat successes to additional dice in the damage pool. If you go back to earlier editions of the game, armour would reduce one's ability to hit by a small amount, whilst giving additional protection. Similarly, there used to be trade offs, and some distinct differences, between a character with Strength 5 and Dexterity 1, and a character with Strength 1 and Dexterity 5. In DAV20, you would always want to be the character with Strength 1 and Dexterity 5, as overage will mean that the latter character is far superior in combat.

                              I do think that the entire combat system of DAV20 needs a re-write, but that's a problem for another day.

                                          I think generally the stats you chose for mercenary and peasant are questionable... for example, a mercenary is more likely to have Dexterity 3 than Strength 4, or he could possibly have both, and a peasant is unlikely to have the same dots in Melee as a professional mercenary. Even the sample human characters in the book show a significant difference in dots between warrior-types and peasant-types. Nonetheless, the real issue which I know you're trying to prove is whether, by penalising Dexterity dots instead of difficulty, wearing an armour makes characters paradoxically more vulnerable and likely to lose.

                                          Generally speaking, changes to difficulty are more influential on larger dice pools than changes to the number of dice, while the opposite is true for smaller pools. Where the cut off is depends on the exact difficulty and number of dice. From this point of view, professional humans with a dice pool of 5+ in their field of expertise would prefer losing dice to increased difficulty, while amateurs and people forced into an unfamiliar situation would prefer increased difficulty. For example, rolling 5 dice at difficulty 6 (no specialisation) has a 40% chance of success per die, averaging at 2 successes. Rolling 4 dice at the same difficulty averages at 1.6 successes, while rolling 5 dice at difficulty 7 averages at 1.5 successes. Better to lose one die than to add 1 to the difficulty. If I start from 4 dice, however, both changes will make the result 1.2 successes on average. If I start from 3, losing 1 die will give 0.8 successes, while increasing the difficulty 0.9.

                                          Also, regardless of whether the pickaxe is understood as a military weapon or not (but the miner wielding a pickaxe is unlikely to have the same Dexterity and Melee, or even Wits perhaps, of a mercenary, though exceptions may exist), this is a very efficient weapon against armor. In fact, it is due to the popularity of armour that the sword was not the primary weapon of many troops, who rather preferred weapons that could pierce armour. Many weapons in the manual have Armour Piercing, and I don't think that is a mistake.
                                          While fighting an enemy equipped with an armour piercing weapon was an obvious problem for armoured soldiers, armour was still better than no armour in most cases, because at least one would be protected from slashing swords, casual hits, etc. That would be the case at least for a few more centuries, until armour becomes finally obsolete. From this point of view, I don't have much issue with the game rules giving only a limited advantage to armour.

                                          Now, let's run a test with equally statted characters, one clad in Class Three armour (3 dice to soak, 1 penalty), one naked. They both have Strength 3, Dexterity 3 and Melee 3 (they're professional soldiers, but one was caught naked), Wits 2. They both carry a saber (which I consider a sword, otherwise we can say they wield a broadsword, shouldn't make a difference). Of course, we could arm them differently, with weapons with armour piercing, but then it would be less about dice pool reduction vs increased difficulty and more about armour vs armour piercing weapons, which I feel is not needed since armour piercing weapons are already intended to give victory over armour, as they should.

                                          Dice Pool Reduction:
                                          Armour Guy (AG) has Initiative 4, Naked Guy (NG) has 5. They both declare multiple actions since both want to parry as well as attack. NG rolls Dexterity + Melee (5 dice because of multiple actions), diff. 8 (because AG has a sword and because of multiple actions): average is 1 success. AG parries with 4 dice at diff. 7 (6+1 for the multiple action), average 1.2 successes. He's safe. Now he attacks with 3 dice 6-1 for the armour-2 for the second action) at diff. 9 (6+1 for the sword +2 for the second action). Average is 0.3 successes, so nothing.
                                          Second turn, NG attacks again (still 1 success), but this time AG does not attempt to parry. NG rolls for damage: Strength 3 + 2 for the sword but no successes carrying over from the hit (some would count the single success, including my ST, but I understand the written rules to be that the single success is not added to the damage). It's 5 dice on a difficulty of 6, averaging at 2 successes. AG rolls 3 dice to soak at difficulty 6, averaging 1.2 successes: he takes 1 health level of damage, but suffers no penalty yet. AG can now attack with 5 dice (no multiple actions this time) on difficulty 7 (just the +1 from the sword), averaging 1.5 successes. NG parries with 4 dice at difficulty 8 (second action, -2 dice, +2 diff), averaging 0.8 successes. Too little. AG rolls 5 dice on the 6 for 2 successes and naked guy is now Hurt and has a -1 penalty. Things proceed more or less the same until NG is dead. The armour allowed AG to save actions by not dodging or parrying, which led to his victory. Had NG worn a Level 1 armour, he would have averaged no soaking successes, leading to the same outcome. A Level Two armour would have given him the same penalty as AG but less protection, still averaging below one soaking success.
                                          If they both attack without parrying, NG rolls 6 dice on the 7 for 1.8 successes, then 5 dice on the 6 for 2 successes, and AG soaks with 3 dice on the 6 for 1.2 successes, taking 1 damage per turn; AG rolls 5 dice on the 7 for 1.5 successes, then 5 dice on the 6 for 2 successes, then NG can't soak and dies in half the time of AG.

                                          Had he wielded a dagger (for armor piercing), he would have suffered -2 to Initiative, -1 dice because a saber is longer. AG attacks first with 5 dice on the 6 (no multiple actions, let's assume he already relies on his armour for defence) for 2 successes. NG parries with 4 dice (-1 for weapon length, -1 for multiple actions to attack after) on the 5 for 2 successes. Safe. NG attacks with 3 dice on the 9 (+1 for sword, +2 for multiple actions), averaging 0.3 successes. If AG attempted to disarm NG, it would fail because he would score less successes than NG's average parry, so at this point they would have to do something else like clinching to avoid a draw. Let's say they both have Brawl 3. AG is still wielding the saber while he closes in to clinch, so the initiative order does not change (had he been unarmed, NG would have had the longer weapon), but NG can damage him with 2+ successes on a parry because it is an otherwise unarmed attack. AG rolls 6 dice on the 6 for 2.4 successes. NG parries with 5 dice (his dagger is still shorter than his saber, which gives him trouble even if the intended move is a clinch) on a 5 for 2.5 successes. Nothing happens. Had he parried as part of multiple actions he would have rolled 4 dice on a 6 for 1.6 successes, allowing AG to roll Strength 3 for damage, averaging 1 bashing damage, soaked with Stamina 3. Basically it's a draw-unless NG keeps parrying as a single action, eventually managing 1 success more than AG and rolling 4 dice for damage... average is 1.6 and the armor soaks at 0.8 because of the Armour Piercing. He can win if they keep doing this for a long while. This is not surprising though, because the dagger was a favourite weapon against armoured opponents, and victory has come after AG decided to clinch, otherwise the advantage of weapon length was evened out by easier parrying with the dagger.

                                          I think a spear in NG's hand would make him win, but I'm tired to calculate. I'm also digressing because I like these imagined scenarios.

                                          Difficulty Increase:
                                          AG and NG have both Initiative 5, roll 6 dice to attack or parry and 5 dice to damage. AG attacks on difficulty 8 (+1 for the enemy's sword, +1 for the penalty of the armour; single action, because if one does the maths, he realises that attacking and parrying or viceversa is pointless), averaging 1.2 successes, or parries with 6 dice on 7, averaging 1.8. NG can attack and parry (multiple actions) or use single actions. Multiple: attack first with 5 dice on 8 for 1 success (it gets parried unless he attacks after AG has already used his attack, but that means NG hasn't parried and has taken 2 damage, which means he rolled 4 dice, which means he rolled 0.8 successes to hit) and then parry with 4 dice on 8 for 0.8 successes - so it's pointless; parry first for 5 dice on 7 (1.5 successes) followed by an attack with 4 dice on 9 which misses. So multiple actions are pointless here for him too. Single actions: attack for 6 dice on 7 for 1.8 successes, which gets parried, or parry for 6 dice on 6 for 2.4 successes, which keeps him unharmed, but that's it. So, regardless of who wins the Initiative, it's a draw. If they both choose to attack and not to parry, NG will die first.

                                          Overall, it seems to me that reducing Dexterity is better for the armour wearer than increasing difficulty.
