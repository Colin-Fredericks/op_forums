    Originally posted by Gurkhal View Post
    That sounds like a really cool campaign. How did the players fit into this?
    It was a pretty long campaign, that turned gonzo and took just over 3 years to wrap up with several arcs. I started running the game about a year before the World of Darkness officially ended, so once that happened I decided to just have things go wilder and crazier because I figured it would be the last Vampire campaign I'd ever run. The game initially started back during the First Baali War. The players were in a small town that came under siege, and were ghouled and then Embraced to help deal with a Baali attack. They continued to fight against the Baali, gaining prominence. The first arc ended with the players launching an attack against a Baali well and in the process collapsed a mountain on top of themselves.

    The second arc took place thousands of years later, shortly before the beginning of of the Second Baali wars. As the Baali were starting to make their reappearance known, a kindred began researching and studying them and came across vague accounts of the player's earlier heroics. She ended up finding the mountain where the players died, dug them up and brought them out of topor to help deal with the resurgent Baali. The player wound up being at the vanguard of the gathered clans during the attack on the Baali base in Crete, and were instrumental in stopping Nergal from awakening one of the Children. We skipped ahead a bit after that, and moved into the Roman era. By this point the players were vastly ancient 5th and 6th generation Methesulah. They got drawn into the Punic Wars due to the Baali's influence in Carthage. The players were in charge of the Roman forces that destroyed Carthage and personally helped to defeat Nergal and Troile.

    For the third arc, we skipped up to the Victorian era. The players, several of whom were part of the Tal'mahe'Ra, had begun hearing stories about Soul Eaters. The Soul Eaters were actually part of Kupala. In this setting I had Kupala as one of the Children, but more awake than the others. It was trapped behind unbreachable mystic wards, but had figured out a way to minuscule amounts of its essence outside of the prison, and planned (over the course of centuries) to move its spiritual bulk into a wide variety of creatures, escaping from confinement. The Baali on the Path of the Hive were its followers, and they would willingly turn their bodies over to it. These Baali were the source of the Soul Eater infection.

    The players managed to take out a number of groups of Soul Eaters but couldn't do much about Kupala itself. Then we skipped up to the modern nights. the Hive Queen (the third Baali leader) had continued her plot to infect a vast number of people. The players finally managed to track her down and confront of her and kill her, but not before she pulled off her master plan, unleashing a global thermal nuclear war and wiping out most of civilization.

    Then the fourth arc took place about 100 years later and went completely gonzo, in a Fallout style post-apocalyptic wasteland. Cities were ruled by vampires who used their blood and powers to protect humans, while the wastelands were filled will all manner of radioactive monsters and soul eater horrors. The players turned Cheyenne Mountain Complex in Colorado into their base of operations and had taken over a big section of the remaining US military might. Meanwhile the Children had fully awaken and they and Lilith were moving to wipe out every vampire in the world. Lilith and her kids had managed to kill most of the Antedeluvians and many elder vampires, so the players (who were all 4th Generation by now, except for one who had diablarizard the Hive Queen and become 3rd Generation) needed to unite the remaining world vampires and take down the Children and Lilith. I basically just gave the players anything they wanted - Stealth Bombers, aircraft carriers, nuclear missiles, access to sattelite laser cannons, all of that. And then the last bit of the campaign was the players hunting down and killing and diablarizing the Children before finally taking down Lilith.

    The campaign ended with them as Second Generation vampires ruling over the remains of humanity on a radioactive world. It was pretty wild, but everyone had a blast. Looking back though there are a lot of things I would want to change. Like I didn't have the Bahari show up at all. I wasn't a huge fan of them at the time (though these days I really like them), so I'd want them to play a more prominent role. Of course, I don't imagine I would ever run another campaign like that, so it's more of a matter of wishing that I'd thought of something like what you wrote back when I was running my game.

                    I wasn’t satisfied with Deimos / Path of Four Humors / Path of the Twilight Garden as proprietary Necromancy for the Lamiae. While it does a decent job of supporting them as warriors, it falls short when it comes to support the bloodline as Bahari. The modern Nightshade Path from V20 is a much better at this, but is very situational and limited in some of its powers. Overall, I feel there should be the option for a Lamia to focus more on the Bahari side of their bloodline and be able to perform their service to Lilith.

                    I looked into rituals and practices of priestesses of Earth-Mother type goddesses, especially those who combine aspects of both life and death. There is quite a bit of historical material from the Mediterranean and Mesopotamia, and even some spells and rituals in writing.
                    Necromancy used to be a much more accepted practice and the relationship between living and dead appeared to be almost casual in some cultures.

                    I found there were some general themes in the practices related to Mother-Goddesses and tried to create a new Necromancy Path around these. The most prevalent aspect was divination, as it was often believed the ghosts of ancestors could glimpse the future. Some put the priests or priestesses in the role of psychopomps. The second was of course fertility and the related rites – the understanding that things must die in order for new things to live was a natural part of those rituals and ties into the necromantic aspect.

                    For an in-game background, I would imagine that this type of necromancy existed as a mortal sorcery first and was revised by the Lamiae after they became a bloodline and studied with the Cappadocians. I tried to center the Path around the concept of death (and dead bodies in particular) becoming fertile soil for new growth, and the practitioners acting as mediators between life and death.


                    Path of Fertile Grave Soil

                    • Haruspicy

                    By cutting open a living or recently dead being and reading its innards, the Lhaka can perform a divination. Usually small animals like birds are used, but a more powerful divination may call for a larger mammal or even a human.

                    System: For each blood point in the pool of the sacrifice, the haruspex may ask one question. Definitive answers are however rare, and in most cases a haruspicy yields vague premonitions or ambiguous portents. The storyteller should feel free to be oracular but take the ‘worth’ of the sacrifice into account.


                    •• Lilith’s Caress

                    With a touch, the Ba’ham can sprout plants or fungi from dead flesh, which pierce through the skin from within. A corpse will decompose in moments, as will any dead bodies animated by necromancy. The damage to a Cainite who is the target of this power is superficial, but the pain is excruciating. While the species of the growth is the Lhaka’s choice, thorns are often used to emphasize the pain. This is especially true for when Bahari use this power on themselves to enter the state of ecstatic pain necessary for certain rituals or sorceries, like Dur-An-Ki. While in this state, the Ba’ham is also able to catch glimpses of the Shadowlands and hear the whispers of the Restless.

                    System: When used on a corpse, it will simply be consumed by the growing plants within a round. Zombies and similar constructs suffer two levels of aggravated damage continuously each round until they are destroyed. When used on a Cainite, the target must succeed in a Stamina + Fortitude roll against 9 or lose their action for this round due to the immense pain.
                    If this power is used on themselves to activate another Blood Sorcery, it does not count as an action and the blood point spent to activate it replaces the blood point for the subsequent sorcery.


                    ••• Moon’s Blood

                    The Lhaka is able to secrete some of their vitae and transmute it into powerful elixirs. Despite the name of the power, the Ba’ham does not need to have a female body to use it.

                    System: The blood point spent to activate this power may be transformed into one of the following elixirs. Once transmuted, the vitae loses all of its other aspects. It should be assumed that the effect on mortals is permanent and will lead to extreme reactions. Elixirs do not have to be ingested, skin contact is sufficient.

                    Elixir Effect on Cainites (one scene duration)
                    A potion to turn a plot of land or being either fertile or barren. If turned barren, target can not create a ghoul, bond, or Embrace. Fertility potions can temporarily suspend any restrictions on these abilities.
                    A strong sedative or stimulant. Difficulties are increased or decreased by two.
                    A powerful aphrodisiac or intoxicant. Target can not spend Willpower. Willpower roll difficulty is increased by two.
                    A poison or cure. Poisoned targets will show signs of disease and vomit blood until half of their pool is drained. Cures will permanently rid them of a mundane disease, if they are a carrier.


                    •••• Imago

                    The Lhaka can make use of their undead bodies to push just a little further, leave their flesh behind and enter the Shadowlands as a ghost. Although not truly a Wraith, this form shares many of the characteristics and allows the Ba’ham to commune with the dead. On the other hand, they become vulnerable to necromancies and are in deep trouble should something happen to their body. Most Bahari treat the Restless with far more respect than the Nigrimancy of the Venetians does, and do not enslave or command them without cause.

                    System: The player must succeed in a Willpower roll against the local Shroud strength. As a fast rule, treat them as a vampire with an incorporeal form. The Lhaka must return to their body before sunrise, or be violently thrown back into it and suffer backlash which will leave them in torpor for the following night. The same happens should their ghost form be destroyed. Naturally, they can not take or bring objects with them.

                    Details for groups using W:tO rules:
                    The health levels of the Ba’ham become Corpus, which they can spend to pass through physical objects. Their vampiric nature carries over in that they are able to drain Corpus from Wraiths, although few would do so forcibly unless in distress. They can not learn or use Arcanoi, but can still utilize their Disciplines – for simplicity, assume the ghost form transforms their blood pool into a limited type of Pathos. They gain Deathsight and Lifesight, and the connection to their body is treated as their sole Fetter. Their deathmarks are those of someone drained by a vampire, but these are rare enough that only few Wraiths would recognize them. They have no Shadow, but are still susceptible to the Beast and powers that would target the Shadow affect the Beast instead. Similarly, use Virtues as a substitute for Passions if needed.


                    ••••• Twilit Bargain

                    One of the oldest forms of necromancy, the Ba’ham is able to trade aspects of life and death between others and themselves: the most well-known example in folklore is the story of the witch who would steal years of lifetime from children or young adults to become younger again. But the practice is far more versatile than this – for the necromancer, motes of life and motes of death become energies they can grasp and utilize.

                    System: To take or give a mote, the Lhaka must touch the target. If the target is unwilling, the player must make a Willpower roll with the difficulty of the target’s current Willpower. For each success, the Ba’ham may take or force one mote from or unto the target. It is possible to transfer directly between targets as long as both can be touched.

                    Example motes of life are: a pulse, breathing, lifetime (apparent age), Attribute levels (max. three per night), Health levels, physical or mental Merits, a physical sense, desire.
                    Many of these can be acquired from animals of appropriate size, and this is usually preferred to stealing the heartbeat of a human.

                    Example motes of death include: a disease or malady, pain, a physical or mental Flaw, dementia, depression, wounds.
                    Between vampires, motes of death include supernatural abilities like dots in Disciplines, but these can not be traded to mortals.

                    Due to the stasis of vampires, any changes made using this power revert when they awake the next evening. A vampire can not trade away their vulnerabilities to sunlight, fire, and faith, nor their Beast or thirst for blood. Changes to mortals however are permanent, and some Bahari have experimented with passing motes to plants.


                    Additional Rituals

                    Nightshade Grove (Level One)

                    The Ba’ham cultivates a garden on burial grounds, nurturing the plants with their vitae. Once the plants have matured, for every blood point invested they may harvest a number of fruits or herbs equal to their level in Medicine that imparts one of the following if ingested:
                    Deathsight, Lifesight, a level of Knowledge, protection from the powers of the dead, a level in a Blood Bond, or an enhanced version of the natural properties of the plant that can affect Cainites. The effects usually last for one scene. With approval of the storyteller, players should feel free to be creative with adapting mystical properties of plants from folklore into new effects.


                    Ancestor’s Tithe (Level Two)

                    Through ritual sacrifice or ceremony, the Lhaka can guide the ghost of an animal to the Shadowlands. While a simple beast may be a trifle for the living, to the Restless they are priced possessions of great value. Bahari use them to gain favor from the ghosts they commune with, or appease them if angered.
                    To guide an animal to the lands of the dead, the player must make a successful Intelligence + Occult roll against the strength of the local Shroud.


                    Hearth Warden (Level Three to Five)

                    Probably one of the oldest, most well known, and widespread rituals, Hearth Warden creates an ephemeral protector that is bound to a specific place. A domesticated animal is ceremonially sacrificed and buried in the foundations of a house or other place of commune.
                    After a successful Intelligence + Occult roll against the strength of the local Shroud and spilling their vitae over the carcass, the sacrifice becomes a ghostly defender of the site that will attack unwelcome intruders, whether material or immaterial. For stats, assume a seasoned ghost with offensive abilities that correspond to the level at which the ritual was performed.

                        I've made a couple of Lamia NPC concepts, since there are so few in the books.

                        Takamat

                        As the most trusted confidant of Lamia within the Cult of Lilith, Takamat received the kiss shortly after the Embrace of her siress and is said to be the first childe of the new bloodline. When Lamia went to their parent Clan at Mount Erciyes, she stayed behind to manage the affairs of the Cult.

                        After receiving a vision around the 4th century, she left the temple on a mission to explore the inner African continent and claim its secrets for the bloodline. She first did so in company of her ghoul, the woman later known as Tin Hinan. After a trek through the Sahara among a caravan of a local tribe in search of an ancient city now buried under the dunes, she established careful but friendly contacts with the African vampires known as Impundulu.

                        At one point, Lilith revealed to her that her ghoul’s destiny lay with the tribe they had traveled with, and Takamat allowed her to remain with them. In turn, she took two of the tribe as childer and it is said this was to mirror the relationship between Impundulu and Bomkazi. After Tin Hinan died, information on Takamat becomes sparse. Most likely she continued her expedition into the African continent, but never returned to the temple of Lilith to tell of her discoveries.

                        Takamat was a very devout Bahari, and had mastered the Disciplines of her blood. She relied on her authority and martial prowess in conflicts. Having also proficiency in Protean and Animalism, she was well equipped for the exploration of the rough lands she encountered.


                        Dihya al-Kahina

                        During her mortal years, Dihya was a military and religious leader of the Amazigh people in the Maghreb who resisted the ongoing Muslim conquest during the 7th century, and later that of the budging Ashirra. She received the kiss on the battlefield after her defeat at Tabarka, allegedly so late that her body had already started to decompose. Her focus changed from open conflict to guerrilla warfare and eventually espionage and subterfuge when it became clear that the advance of Muslim kindred and kine could not be stopped, and she maintained a small branch of Lamiae that did much the same.

                        Unsurprisingly, her later activities are shrouded in shadow, but some have drawn a connection to a Setite Sorceress who appeared in Cairo during an outbreak of the Black Plague. Whether or not they are the same person, Dihya was an accomplished sorceress (as her epithet implies) and practiced a mortal version of Dur-An-Ki before her Embrace, something that likely carried over to her undeath.


                        Giordano di Giovanni

                        Giordano had always been a misfit. A talented polymath of arts, music, mathematics, and philosophy, they were in constant conflict with their merchant family who valued vastly different qualities. Put to use as a courtier and clerk against their will, they smothered under the moral divergence between their own beliefs and those imposed by their role. Prone to bouts of depression, melancholy, and suffering from strong body dysphoria, they turned to the Dark Arts in search of answers.

                        Rumors started to erupt and Giordano, now a liability to their family, was sent to Constantinople as an envoy – a friendly euphemism for exile. Wanting to preserve the talents of Giordano more or less as a showpiece for their family to the Clan, the Cainite Giovanni arranged for an Embrace. When the designated Cappadocian sire arrived however, he flat out refused to gift the kiss and instead ordered his Lamia bodyguard to do it in his stead. Giordano now found themselves between Clans, in a foreign city, and desperate to rectify their mistake of striking a deal with devils. They are entranced by the Tzimisce and their powers as a way to free themselves from the limitations of their body, while unknowingly being watched by local Bahari who observe their development.
