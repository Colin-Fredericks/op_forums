            OK.... let me try to summarize:

            1) The V5 social mechanics eliminate any advantage to planning or trying to put your opponent on the back foot before you confront them. You roll your best roll against their best roll, and the major result modifier to determine the winner of a round, with no other circumstantial effects in play (ex: combat has cover modifiers against ranged attack that social conflict does not, and there are penalties for called shots).

            Though something I've become aware of to correct a previous assertion: You can get bonus dice in advanced social conflicts through the Maneuver action. However, this uses up one of your three actions, and you can't attack that turn. Thus it's only really safe to use if (a) you have large numbers on your side so someone can defend you or threat to attack if your opponent would otherwise take the chance to All-Out Attack you (b) you have the dice on your side meaning the extra dice are highly unlikely to get you more damage than attacking two turns in a row.

            And that presumes the ST doesn't decide your Maneuver takes setting up, as a Maneuver might not general bonus dice if the ST decides it's necessary to even try your intended action, or that you need to do a setup Maneuver and then you can do a dice-bonus Maneuver, and then you can finally attack.

            2) The V5 social mechanics models audience as weapon modifiers. What your social attack is, doesn't actually matter. If you have a devastating insult, but you only get 1 success total and it's just you vs. your target? They take 1 WP damage. If you put a whoopie cushion under a Primogen's seat and get one success, and it goes off in front of the Prince, it does 6 WP damage. The Prince finds this childish behavior completely unbecoming of a vampire and isn't at all amused? Doesn't matter, being mocked in front of the Prince is what does the damage.

            What's wrong with that? The mechanics don't model what they say they model. It's not just a matter of preference for style of rules, but the V5 mechanics are heavily biased towards specific social conflict options and not others with no stated rationale (and in fact contradiction to stated points of using social conflicts).

            Calling this "personal preference" is just deflecting. You can claim anything is personal preference is you're going to ignore what the goal of the mechanics are:

            "These combat rules are horrible, this is supposed to be a real world setting, but punches automatically hit, and automatically incapacitate targets, and you get to pick between non-lethal and lethal results, but guns never do more than one non-lethal damage no matter what, and if you successfully hit someone with a gun you lose all your health to lethal damage save one last health and you can't mitigate that at all!"

            "Well, that's just your preference about combat, I like it because my preference is for combat to be up close and personal and not for shooting people from range."

            It's not actually engaging with the rules in the context of what they're supposed to be doing. If combat tells me it's supposed to be results that resemble real world results fists should not be insanely superior to guns, fists even being close to competitive to guns is a concession to game over reality. If the social conflict rules are supposed to help me play out interesting political intrigue, back room one-on-one social conflicts need to have enough mechanical weight to actually force a result instead of giving one side no reason to take it seriously.

                        Originally posted by Trippy View Post
                        So, I will just note that on the previous discussion of V20 physical combat, that is also the major mark against it. It basically doesn’t model what it says it models - you roll lots of dice in a way that renders combat to a randomized and protracted outcome, where abilities do not actually provide as much advantage as they should. Additionally, the V20 system for social conflict is basically non existent by comparison to V5.
                        You asked what the complaints about V5's mechanics are. As I already said, most V20 fans don't particularly like the V20 combat/social mechanics, and that vast majority I've talked to would prefer something we consider better. The problem is that what we consider better are things more like Exalted 3e, CofD 2e, or the Storypath system: games where these systems that can get out of the way for people that want quick and fast resolutions, but keep enough meat on the bones for players that want mechanical support for a much more tactically minded experience.

                        A lot of us have also had a lot of negative experience so "social combat" models where all social situations try to work like combat, instead of recognizing how silly that is. It's one thing for a game like Fate where everything uses the same framework except for having a more than one "health" track to damage if that's relevant, but if you model all advanced social mechanics on combat, you fall short because "social" is as broad as "physical" and combat is not all physical action.

                        The utility of easy to remember fast resolution is when you do simple conflict resolutions. Advanced resolution systems are when you want to keep to the core idea of how the game works, but expand it out. Trying to keep all the advanced systems the same in the name of keeping how thy work simple inherently means selling parts of those conflicts short.

                        Point 1). Is there any reason why you cannot take an Advance to break down the advantage of putting the opponent on the back foot (as an Extended test)?
                        All an Advance does is more towards a narrative that doesn't involved head-to-head conflict. Trying to circumvent getting into a social conflict with a political opponent by doing favors for a mutual superior is when you use an Advance. It isn't for taking on an opponent directly (as noted, you generally don't use Advance in combat because once you're in combat you have to be contesting your foe(s) directly).

                        Basically, in my whole example story from before, the making an Advance part was all the stuff that lead up to a blackmail attempt and the NPC making an Advance to gather evidence the PCs broke the laws of Domain to counter them (because just Blocking them would have been kinda boring), that then stalls in V5 because trying to blackmail people doesn't really work in V5 (no audience - since you don't want an audience to blackmail - means it's significantly hard to do enough WP damage do get a meaningful opponent to concede).

                        It would be a prior Maneuver (p296) - not during the conflict.
                        A prior Maneuver is one that you have to take before you can take another conflict action. If you have a gun that's disassembled in a case, you take a Maneuver to put it together before you can use it. Investigating a political to discover a hidden vice that you want to exploit is a prior Maneuver to Advances to corrupt said politician to your employ. They are not stated as rolls that happen before an advanced conflict itself begins.

                        The stakes of any given social conflict are defined before any dice pools are collected or rolled.
                        Winning a social conflict requires one side to concede. If you only do 1 WP damage to them, and they don't concede, you have to keep at them. As V5 states, the only want to ensure concession is to fill them with Aggravated Willpower damage, so do Willpower x 2 damage, which you're not going to achieve in most cases without the audience damage bonus. After three rounds, since you're not supposed to roll more than three rounds and all, you have to be significantly ahead of your opponent so it doesn't get narrated as a tie, or a win in their favor. Which is, again, only really functional with meaningful audiences to give you the damage needed to show that things are decisively going your way (of course at the risk of that going against you too).

                        Lets say over three rounds it's looking like you're just going back and forth. You do 1 WP, they do 1 WP, etc. You're only up 2 to 1 because you got the first win. But they have two more Willpower than you - lets say 8 to 6 - so neither character is really done much. So, in deciding what should happen to narrate out the rest, the path set forward is an even verbal back and forth, but you'd come up short on endurance and the ST declares you end up conceding the social conflict and the stake go to your opponent.

                        Now we change up to having a +3 audience. After three rounds with the exact some rolls, they're impaired (down 8 WP out of 8) and you're doing not hot but OK (down 4 WP out of 6), with the impairment they might concede anyway, and the impairment penalty swings the even roll off in your favor rather than a coin flip, so suddenly the narrative switches in your favor and you get your stakes instead.

                        So, yes, that audience factor really does matter. Whatever stakes you set, you have to be in a position to win the conflict to get them, or your foe gets theirs instead.

                        Moreover, if you only roll a single success, that is the nature of rolling dice pools isn’t it?
                        Social conflict attack rolls are two characters rolling against each other. It's 1 success total after your opponents successes are subtracted from your own. It's still the nature of dice, but the issue remains the same with 1 success vs. 0, or 5 successes vs. 4; it might matter in terms of how to narrate the details of the actions, but the mechanical results are the same.

                        The reason to stress the "just 1 total success" part is how much the audience factor skews what is and isn't effective in V5 social conflicts. That cutting insult could be 5 vs. 4, and so however well delivered the retort blunted it significantly; but you only do 1 damage because nobody else was around. If the harpies were involved, it would jump to 5 WP damage not because the insult was batter, or the retort was worse, but because losing a verbal exchange in front of important people cuts deeper.

                                                Either it's solid enough that it doesn't need a roll, so fucking terrible it doesn't need a roll (perhaps a roll to save face?), or it's worth rolling for. *If the player isn't particularly good at articulating arguments, or he'd have far too much to say, he can just ask to roll.
                                                I'll also try to be lenient on people who're obviously not 4-dots in X trying to play 4-dots in X, though It is easy to forget sometimes.

                                                You can contest another person's roll, or you can make it singly. The issue that V5 flops it's face at is what to do when someone 'wins' a roll. social conflicts aren't about fucking willpower. Either you make your opponent concede, or if he's never going to concede, you make yourself look great and them terrible, or you're just fooling people. It's not about making someone depressed and sullen, that's the least important thing here. What If I know full well that my petitioning the prince will fail, but I'm doing so to galvanize a quietly resentful populace? What if I desperately WANT the story that makes me look like a fool to get out, so that what really happened never sees the light of court? Perhaps losing willpower for humuliation might be appropriate on occasion, but the way V5 does it is simplistic at best. V20 at least has the Beast try to frenzy at a public humiliation, that can excuse the willpower loss.
                                                You can roleplay your way through any aspect of V5 without making any rolls - including social conflicts. You literally have advice for diceless, freeform play to do this and there are different degrees about how much you can integrate dice-based systems - basic and advanced - in order to highlight scenes of play however you want.

Are you running on autopilot? Losing willpower doesn’t necessarily make someone depressed and sullen - it may just make them aggressive, swear and use capitalized letters when typing.
