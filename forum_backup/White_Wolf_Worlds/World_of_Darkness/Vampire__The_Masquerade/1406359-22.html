                        What is to be done?

                        Agatha “crazy little blonde Polish woman” Sterek and Salvador Garcia have a verbal go at each other, with Garcia getting the best of Sterek for once. This bit mostly serves as a thematic transition from the last few sections into the next ones.

                        Educate, Agitate and Organize

                        This section is a flier where in Garcia talks about how to foment dissent and revolution inside a Camarilla domain. It reads a bit like a shortened version of Saul Alinsky’s Rules for Radicals, with the caveat that Garcia’s take on it obviously includes a discussion of vampires, whereas Alinsky’s neglected that demographic for some reason.

                        Garcia expressly uses the terms educate, agitate and organize. These have been in use by political malcontents since the 1860 when they were apparently first used by (appropriately enough) real world anarchists. Notably, while Garcia might have a massive hate boner for the Camarilla, but he is not blind to the nature of the Anarchs. In the section he writes “If not in a Camarilla city, don’t worry. There will still be enemies, from wannabe-tyrants who want to make the city their domain…”

                        In his flier he offers a great deal of solid advice, such as avoiding dealing with elders if at all possible and not getting too hung up on all Anarchs needing to speak in the right speech-code.

                        This is a good section and provides a lot of potential, should a Storyteller and a group want to run a resistance and revolutionary style game. Even if they did not, dealing with Anarchs following Garcia’s advice could provide a lot of fodder for stories.

                        As an aside, it is interesting that Garcia thinks “vampire” is a vulgar term but “licks” is not offensive to him.

                                Build a Better World (for You)

                                Dalia Nakache provides this section which is about coopting mortal groups to serve as foot soldiers and cannon fodder in vampire fights.

                                In any case, Nakache discusses using mortal groups for vampire goals. She discusses a vampire using their social circle, and friends, or even a group of people they despise. They are all simply mortals and thus expendable.

                                Nakache is honest enough to write; “Most of us feel intuitively that the presence of an undying monster doesn’t make any political movement better. Our influence is toxic, yet we often continue to hang around with our old friends just because we like them.”

                                I cannot read this section without wincing. It is about vampire using mortal groups, in everything from creating riots to spawning urban violence, for the purposes of the vampires. The concerns and needs of the mortals are incidental. This is par for the course in the World of Darkness and in Vampire the Masquerade.

                                This summer America has seen a kind of rioting, urban violence, and general malcontent it has not sense the civil rights movement and the protests against the Vietnam War. There are specious, but widely believed, conspiracy theories about some malign and singular group behind the protests and the riots. The “Build a Better World (for You)” section is not bad. But reading it is uncomfortable.

                                Aside from the arguable squeamishness, the section is pretty good. It provides some solid story ideas for an Anarch group.

                                    Art

                                    This is not about a specific section but an observation about the art. I like the art in this book better than the art in the Camarilla book.

                                    The cover of the Camarilla is more memorable and striking than the cover of the Anarch book. But the interior art in the Anarch book is better. The Camarilla book is home to a lot of static art of buildings and things, such as on pages 11, 15, 17, 18, and so on. By comparison, the Anarch book is home to a lot of images of people, which are more interesting that just pictures of some skyscraper.

                                    The stylized images are interesting, like the ones of page 17 and 23. The image on page 30 is done in the style of Tom of Finland, which is fine but its placement at this point in the book feels a bit random. I think it is supposed to Sterek on pages 63, but in my head the character looks like Outstar.

                                    On page 114 in the original version of Anarchs is a picture of a Saulbri. In the edited version her third has been removed, which is a pity.

                                                            The Prince Must Die

                                                            So Tbilisi is an Anarch city. Tbilisi is the capital of the nation of Georgia. I will add that to the list of known Anarch territories.

                                                            In this section Natia Abakelia provides some guidance on subverting the Camarilla from within. She specifically advises infiltrating the Ivory Tower and turning its own methodologies and weaknesses against it.

                                                            She says “…if you really want to fight like a vampire, you must abandon honor and principle. Burrow deep into the flesh of the enemy and poison them from within.”

                                                            Two sections which stood out to me include;
                                                            • “Make it an offence to be insufficiently servile. When the Prince makes a joke, note who failed to laugh, then whisper to others about it. Your goal is to create a situation where the Prince makes a speech and nobody dares to stop applauding first because they’re afraid to be seen as disloyal.”
                                                            • “A classic trick is to be a stickler for rules. Always demand that the proper protocol be followed, no matter the urgency of the situation. Argue for a strict interpretation of the Traditions, against local initiative.”


                                                            The Camarilla is vulnerable to strategies like this and such moves could leave it moribund and unable to respond to Anarch moves, if the Anarchs have some patients and finesse.

                                                            There is a lot of good material here for Anarchs trying to divide and conquer a Camarilla domain, or even for Camarilla vampires trying to fight against such an effort.

                                                            As an aside, Abakelia states that the Tbilisi Anarchs broke from the Soviet Brujah in the 1970s. That is interesting as Georgia itself remained a communist nation and part of the mortal soviet sphere until the collapses of communism in 1990s. Even so, the vampires at least in Tbilisi slipped out of the grasp of the vampires in Moscow and Russia. That would be an interesting historical campaign.
