    Bloodhunt is apparently 5th Edition's biggest success

    07-18-2022, 08:20 PM
    Bloodhunt With Unexpected Success on Steam - gamepressure.com
    https://www.gamepressure.com/newsroom/bloodhunt-with-unexpected-success-on-steam/z4437f

    Well, I have to admit I did not see this coming but probably should have. Fifth Edition has had some serious issues with its spin off material and the present vaporware of Bloodlines was the biggest sorrow of the product. I really had hope for Swansong but it seems like the money-making success that Paradox Interactive wanted from buying the World of Darkness has been achieved in their battle royale game.

    It's not a Fortnite killer, hell no, but it IS a success and seems to be set for money to continue pouring in for the time being.

    It is the Mandalorian of their Disney purchase of Star Wars.

          I think it's worth considering that, esp. in the f2p battle royale genre, getting a good reception is not the same as being successful. While something players love, a purely cosmetics based monetization system needs a pretty big audience to have money "pouring in," no matter how much players like it.

          The problem is that you need a giant audience to make this profitable if cosmetics are really going to be the only money maker here. If, on average, Bloodhunt makes $10 per registered player per month... that's barely enough to support the dozens of full time employees which is a minimum of what it takes to run this sort of game at their current numbers. There's a huge risk at the moment of Bloodhunt not growing fast enough to sustain itself, or changes in monetization necessary become sustainable that alienates new customers and leaves a stagnant player base.

          Bloodhunt is absolutely having a great start. It needs to have a great next year or two before we know if this is really a success on a corporate level. There are lots of great games/movies/etc. that are really well liked, but bomb financially because they don't generate sales to remake production (and Bloodhunt could not have been cheap to make).

                              It's not really fair to compare Bloodhunt to Fortnite.

                              But, that said, assuming an average of around 3000 players per day, and if each player spends $10 per month, that comes out to a grand total of $360,000 a year. If you have 6 people on the development team and they're each earning $60,00 a year (which is not very much in the tech industry), that's the entirely yearly income of the game with nothing left over for servers, hardware or profit. And keep in mind that with a game like this, you need artists and programmers constantly making new skins, animations, etc, so that people can spend $10 a month.

                              Ultimately what it comes down to is how much money and how long the company is willing to invest into the game. There are some games that aren't super popular right off the bat, but word of mouth attracts more players and eventually they end up being profitable. Maybe that will be Bloodhunt. But the developer could look at those numbers and say, "Nah, it's not where it needs to be. Investing more money is a waste. Pull the plug," and it could end up dead before getting large enough to be self-sustaining, and it certainly wouldn't be the first time we've seen something like that happen.

                              Of course Bloodhunt was based on a fairly obscure IP (by mainstream standards) and even for people familiar with the IP, it's not the genre you'd usually associate with it. I'm guessing the developer considered that stuff when okaying the game in the first place, so I don't think there's any risk of the game being shut down in the near future, unless the numbers absolutely tank.

                              So basically those numbers aren't low enough for me to write the game off as a failure, but they're definitely not high enough for me to be willing to bet on the game's long-term future either.

                                              Look at the dates.

                                              The article you're quoting is from May. In May Bloodhunt posted 13K avg players a day with a peak of 29K users at one moment. Two months later, that's dropped to 1.4K avg players, and 3.3K peak players.

                                              Just look at Steams data that you yourself linked to: Bloodhunt is doing 1/10 of how good it was in May, and those May numbers were not runaway smash hit numbers by themselves. If things were consistently increasing from May? Bloodhunt would be on the fast track towards smash hit you seem to be reading it is. But the data is currently indicating a flash in the pan is a serious concern for the game. By next May, it needs to either demonstrate an astoundingly high microtransaction rate for the genre, or it needs something closer to 100K to 200K avg players a day, or the money on overhead and keeping a staff employed to constantly roll out new content and features isn't going to be there.

                                              Let me stress this again. Fortnite makes about ~$13 per average active user per year That is, on average, a player plays less than $2 a month there. And it's the biggest game in the genre. 3K avg players a day being "normal" for Bloodhunt would mean $40K gross (not net, gross) income off the game per year if players spend on it like they do on Fortnite. That's not enough to keep the lights on, let alone pay anyone.

                                              As Anubis pointed out, even if Bloodhunt players were paying twelve times as much money as Fortnite players, that's still a shoestring budget for a game under this model.

                                              There needs to be way, way, way more players of this game for it to be a success under this model. And the numbers are currently (if not sky-is-falling doom and gloom of a situation) going in the wrong direction.

                                              I get you want to find a positive light here, but the numbers are what the numbers are. Bloodhunt, however good of a game it is, might be too ambitious for its own good, because there isn't the money to backup the quality of the game as it currently stands. That's the harsh reality of this genre of video game. It's why so many of these games try to extract way more money out of players even if they avoid pay-to-win.

                                              Again, think Bloodhunt has the potential to survive this. Everything I've seen says it could be a solid game that finds it's niche and gets up to those higher levels of regular players needed for this type of product to work, because being a good game out of the box means a lot these days. But I can't tell how you can look at the numbers and see sunshine and roses.
