    Originally posted by Kakost View Post
    So, each one of us, we can only experience ourselves; we can never experience the other. We can run mental simulations to try to understand, but those are approximations of reality, not reality. When we exercise empathy, that's what we do - we simulate the pain of the other as if it were being inflicted in our own Ego.
    And that's why people work with advocacy for vulnerable groups, because empathy by itself lacks knowledge and context, and society frequently fail in providing those for how to deal with its minorities (or foreign cultures).

    You can't know how I feel. But you can understand because I can tell you. If I don't, then you can't do either, because your internal simulation lacks data, it has only your own assumptions and perceptions. That's the point with those labels: they're information about people, crucial information, even if far from all the information.

    So my point when I say that people trying to "see beyond the label" hurt me isn't that I blame them, but that their uninformed actions have consequences opposed to their intentions, and in this particular case the heart of the problem is precisely the assumption that the "label" is the problem, when it isn't. It isn't a label, it is information.

    The argument of "group identity is us vs them" is fallacious in this context, and yet used frequently because of people that use it out of context, frequently in bad faith. When we're talking about minorities, conditions, cultures and several other things, the purpose of the identification is to aggregate relevant information. Not all Autistics are the same, yet most of us share a number of perceptions, experiences and hardships that are related, directly or not, to our shared trait of autism. Our individuality shapes how each of us live through those shared aspects, but does not change that we share them. By identifying ourselves as a group we can help each other and help others to understand us.

    Especially because otherwise, going full on on the "beyond the label" mentality, you would have to understand each Autistic person from scratch, without any common reference, committing the same mistakes each time as you don't transfer data from one to the other. A single mistake isn't a problem, you can expect that I have a given common Autistic trait and be wrong, after making things clear everything is ok. But if you simply does not assume any trait whatsoever and have to learn everything from scratch, you won't commit just a few, it will be a problem, and it will repeat itself over and over with others you meet. That group identity will go a long way into helping you to anticipate things, and you're still able to adapt to personal distinctions. It is only a label if you make it a label.

    Just take those principles and apply to the others. Gays, blacks, Jews, Japanese, those people aren't just labels, but the identities in question inform a lot of things they have in common. Sometimes crucial information that can make a huge difference in their lives if you know or not. They're not made the same by identifying as a group and they're not necessarily taking an "us vs them" position, it is just about relevant facts. Sure, there are people that will assume such a position, but it doesn't invalidate the whole identity and the very important function it has in organizing people to deal with the very real problems they face.

    When you blame the "label", you risk making yourself the person that is assuming an "us vs them" mentality by proxy. Which is the intention of those who use this argument in bad faith.

    Originally posted by Kakost View Post
    As for what could the writters do, just "internationalize" everything. Make Assamite ninjas, Ventrue daimyos and ronin Brujah samurais.
    They are doing that. Have been doing that for a time, now.

    Yet, again, the devil is in the details and the specifics aren't that simple, but do matter.

    First big problem: how much should they respect the lore, how much should they retcon it? Not from the standpoint if the lore is more important than the affected people, of course it isn't, but at which point retconning it would cause more harm than good? People don't like retcons all that much, it breaks immersion, and this isn't less true for them. They may have their own solutions that will be thrown away with the retcon as it will throw away details about so many people's games. The cause of the retcon, explicit or implicit, may cause relevant reactions. The sudden change, making the culture that was a core concept become an afterthought, may offend and even reinforce prejudice more than help.

    It seems simple, but isn't, and their feedback is the most important factor every time it can be acquired, directly or not. You may not be able to reach to an actual person of the group, but you can do worse than read current research, articles written by natives on as many relevant subjects as possible, statistical data, etc. Nothing is perfect, but the more you seek information from sources that go beyond the basics, the more you build towards doing better than you did before.

    Just so you know, currently the Banu Haqim is being made far more diverse and less focused on being Middle Eastern, but they still have a history of presence (not of being the only presence) in the Middle East as a means to explain their lore. Alamut is the clan's ancestral fortress in the same sense that Vienna is where the Tremere main Chantry is located. There's also a whole other problem with the name where they tried to change it because the original one was a racist trope, but then the one they got came out a bit of a bad reverse translation. Assamite means nothing, just resembles Assassin, which is a bad joke (like, not a modern bad joke, a 12th century bad joke). Banu Haqim means "Children of Haqim" in theory, based upon a few Arabic tribal names, but it is kind of giberish. But until someone who actually speaks Arabic gives me a better option or explains why it shouldn't be used (Arabic has a very particular structure for how words change in context that I can't say for sure what's the error, just that it is there), I'll stick to Banu Haqim.

    Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
    I don't think you understand how much of a fucking monumental task it would be to give every culture/subculture their own cool bloodline and represent everyone in a way that's reasonable and fair.
    I'm quite aware of that and don't think they should have done that in the first place. In fact, I think even the 7 clans are an excess and prefer the 5 more general clans of Requiem, plus Covenants to cover some trope interactions like magic.

    But I do disagree that making representations is always bad just because we can't represent everything.     Namely, now that they're here, we absolutely can and should have good representations for the peoples and cultures already presented through the clans and bloodlines in the lore. Hinting that there are other variations in one way or another in different places and cultures not yet covered, so the list isn't exhaustive.     Originally posted by Kakost View Post
    I personally always hated the bloodlines businesses to begin with. To me there would be the clans and that's it
    Wouldn't happen anyway, for several reasons.

    After making the broad main character options, a game will naturally demand the creation of niche specialized options that cover some quirky and interesting themes. Of course as a player you can cover it up with the broad options if they're well designed and you put a little effort, but people will have ideas, will want new themes, and some simply won't have the kind of mind that takes for the effort, so making the specialized option is a great help for them.

    On top of that, the game needs to keep publishing books, plain and simple. It doesn't mean that every Bloodline or Discipline exists just to justify the book, at least not necessarily (some probably do), but since you'll cover a given topic in the book anyway, making up the Bloodline and/or Discipline as a way to put forward your theme and ideas is a given, even if you don't strictly need 'em. Just why not? The existence of unique Bloodlines in general, as a trope, was a given in itself, too.

    But I do agree that they aren't the best design for the concept. Mechanically speaking they're whole clans, and the too rigid Discipline structure and lack of other power mechanics made it almost mandatory to create new gimmicky Disciplines for many of them, simply because the authors didn't saw an alternative.

    Requiem did something a bit better, with Bloodlines effectively deriving from the parent clan as a simple template instead of a whole new splat, and their specific quirks usually resting in access to simpler advantages or a Devotion instead of a whole new Discipline. Yet I can't say I'm completely happy with it, just more than with Masquerade's take.

    Then again, though, I just got used to it. With the passing of time I learned to just accept the game as it is, and that actually improved my games. Bloodlines and Disciplines aren't bad for the game. They're far from the best mechanics it could have, but once I stopped trying to demand perfection from the setting I learned they can actually add a lot of fun with little effort, and options the players enjoy to have. All in all, I made my peace with them.
