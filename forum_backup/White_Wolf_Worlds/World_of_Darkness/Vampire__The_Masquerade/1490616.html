    They were always the Hecata

    08-18-2022, 11:33 AM
    5th edition V:tM has essentially had the Giovanni fall from full fledged Clan status and created a new Clan of sorts made up of the Cappadocian remnants. It's a pretty radical change. White Wolf doesn't really have the option of a complete retcon, since they want to build off of prior editions and metaplot. However, it seems a bit more logical that the Hecata as presented in V5 always existed as a Clan and that the Giovanni were merely a bloodline of the Hecata.

    This would eliminate rise of the Giovanni and the diablerization of Cappadocius. The Giovanni could still have arisen as a bloodline and family, but never became a Clan, which I feel was too similar to the rise of the Tremere and too total of an elimination of the Cappadocians considering the small and geographically limited nature of the Giovanni.

          I always thought the Giovanni were Cappadocians. So the issue of "Hecata" being a "new" clan is besides the point. It doesn't matter if the official clan name changes from Cappadocians to Giovanni to Hecata or anything else. It's always been the same clan, though with various sub-clans/bloodlines.

          This is not a case where someone from another clan diablerized the Antedeluvian (like Tremere and Saulot). Augustus diablerizing Cappadocious is like Gratiano diablerizing Lasombra, or Lugoj diablerizing Tzimisce (let's ignore that they actually did not for the time being). Nobody says that the Lasombra no longer exist and there is only now Clan Gratiano.

          Some might say, "Wait, there is a difference as the Giovanni destroyed the old Cappadocians, and thus now are a new clan!" But that isn't true. Simply killing all the old members does not suddenly transform the new members into a different clan. It's still the same vitae descended from the Antedeluvian. Furthermore, it was never the case that the Giovanni actually destroyed all the old members of the Cappadocians. This was always a timebomb waiting to go off in the setting. No mystery remains anymore. We know the Samedi are also actually Cappadocians. We know there were various other Cappadocians that survived. We know some of the Cappadocians imprisoned during the Feast of Folly survived.

          One unfortunate thing is that the Giovanni clan and the Dark Ages Cappadocians had different Discipline sets. I'll admit this has always bothered me. It also bothered me that Revised would often create sub-clans for many of the clans that also had different Discipline sets. (And that Sabbat versions of clans often did as well.) But by now, we know the Cappadocians had lots of bloodlines (Samedi, Lamia) so calling the Giovanni another isn't much of a stretch.

          Rather than being portrayed as different clans, it'd be more of a case of Cappadocian versus Cappadocian anti-tribu (assigning either name to the Giovanni or the surviving Cappadocians as you see fit, the politics of which group should be the "anti-tribu" is beyond this post). Except that the original clan finally won in the end and destroyed the diablerist.

          If we're to take the setting seriously, the idea that the Giovanni were a new clan was always a pretension. We know that the Giovanni claims to have destroyed the old clan was pitifully wrong. The real issue is that we probably need a revised version of the original "Giovanni Clan" description as presented in the Players Guide and Corebook to represent the actual, real status of the clan, as opposed to the Giovanni propaganda presented in the original. It wouldn't need much change - just a recognition that despite repeated claims of the Giovanni hunting down "the last Cappadocian", there is ongoing sightings of members of the original clan around the world.

          The issue of whether this group or that one was actually a "clan" or a "bloodline" was often one of semantics. One of the definitions was whether it had a third generation "founder" (as opposed to someone of less potent generation "founding" them), but the more important issue is that other vampires really wanted to keep clans limited to the traditional number of 13. So if the Tremere are clan, it means the Salubri are not. And if Augustus diablerizes Cappadocious and wants to rename the clan, then let him and if surviving Cappadocians don't want to be called that, then I guess it means they are a bloodline. Then the elders shrug and move on to the next topic of conversation.

                                      Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                                      I don't like all of them turning into the same clan, losing their unique weaknesses and identity however. That's boring.
                                      I have an inordinate need for setting consistency. So in my chronicles, I ruled that the Giovanni had the exact same Clan Weakness as the Cappadocians. But in addition to that, all the Giovanni ALSO had to take the Flaw Grip of the Damned which gave them the same the weakness as the actual clan weakness (almost, as Grip of the Damned didn't cause an additional health level damage to mortals). It meant all Giovanni characters had two weaknesses, but at least they got 4 additional build points because of it.

                                      So if you don't mind double dipping, you can easily assign mandatory Flaws to each of the bloodlines. That way you can keep the unique flavor of that lineage.

                                      Want Samedi to still look like decaying corpses? Give them the Monstrous Flaw.

                                      Want the Nagaraja line to still consume flesh? Give them the Conspicuous Consumption Flaw.

                                      Or if you don't think that Flaw really simulates the original weakness or don't like that the Flaws provide different build points, just customize the Curse Flaw (Curse of the Samedi, Curse of the Nagaraja, Curse of the Lamia, and so on) and give each one the same points.

                                      In my own chronicles, I often have certain Flaws (and sometimes Merits) persist in individual bloodlines within a clan. For whatever reason, their vitae is somehow tainted or special. So if a PC picks something like Repulsed by Garlic, I'll assign that to their sire, grandsire, brood mates, etc. as well. I believe this makes the lineages within the clans more special. So this was an obvious "solution" to making all the Cappadocians have the same Weakness, but still justify the unique flavor for that lineage.

                                      Although personally, I don't like the Nagaraja being part of the Hecata. Everyone else was at least some spawn of Cappadocious. The Nagaraja had a very distinct origin related to their being a bloodline exclusive to the True Black Hand. And unless one is planning to use that sect, it's best just not to mention the Nagaraja at all.

                                      However, one thing I do like is that disparate nature of these bloodlines while the Developers and Writers were putting these details down ad hoc had the inadvertent virtue of providing a lot of variety of concepts for a clan of Death that would not have been apparent at the start if you asked someone to design a Clan of Death for inclusion in the game along with the original seven clans. Decadent nobles practicing necromancy, cannibal bankers, priestesses of Lilith, ascetic monks, decayed corpses, and the like showcase how different clan members can be from one another. That's good for inspiration for players seeking to start their first character.
