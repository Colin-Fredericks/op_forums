    My interpretation of V5 is that it's central theme is a modern day adaptation of the Dark Ages plotline.

    * A modern day Inquisition hunting and destroying Kindred everywhere.
    * The corrupt and evil Elders throwing their childer at the Inquisition.
    * The Anarchs getting organized and more violent to revolt against their sires.
    * The Antediluvians stirring in the background.
    * A bunch of Sabbat getting together to destroy the Antediluvians, only to serve their purposes.

    Essentially, I define V5 as "Transylvania Chronicles, 21st Century Edition"

                                                        But in a more specific reaction, I think that V5 definitely is a more mature game due to a vareity of factors. Reframing the central conflict between the Camarilla and the Anarchs is a nice job of showing a economic as well as socially relevant interaction based upon the elite 1% as well as entrenched capitalist nobility vs. a more poor as well as struggling yet violent majority. The Thin Bloods also get analyzed as a concept of people who are closer to humans than vampires and may have advantages that could allow them to replace humanity while also leaving them vulnerable to the oppressive system they are found in.

                                                        Like MAGE: REVISED the game is very much about reframing the setting to be less about occult mystery and BIG SCARY MONSTERS than more about living in the "real world" as well as interacting with mortals on a regular basis. The existence of Touchstones is a rough one but make sure the player characters are FORCED on some level to not be totally divorced from human society but have at least one mortal friend to remind them that they can't just totally interact with vampires.

                                                        The depiction of the world is also much better with an international global view of things like the Ashirra, South America, Mexico, and other nations with a nod toward V:TM's massive global fanbase versus its original super-America focus. We also get more acknowledgement of the changes to modern society with the rise of the Global Surveilance State using the Second Inquisition as well as the changes necessary to maintain any form of Masquerade.

                                                        The renewed focus on feeding also encourages player characters to think about the people they use for blood as feeding rather than just resource management as well as the increased chance of frenzy as well as taking innocent lives. That's in addition to incorporating so many real life issues into THE ANARCH and THE CAMARILLA. I feel the game has also gotten back to being the dark progressive horror game of 1st Edition.

                                                        It's a bit more vulgar but I feel like its renewed its focus on personal horror and taking place in the "real world." Mind you, a large part of this is due to Matthew Dawkin's great writing and the Chronicles involved. RUSTED VEINS is probably one of my all-time favorite adventures and is focused on a drug deal gone bad and human trafficking. I also was a huge fan of THE SACRIFICE which is fundamentally a story about how the Sabbat has failed and how butt hurt this made Malenkov. Its resolved not by shooting up the place but by appealing to Malenkov emotionally and playing politics with Sierra.

                                                        So yes, much darker and more mature game. Like 20%.

                                                        I still loved the previous version(s), obviously but it's my 2nd favorite edition.

                                                        CULTS OF THE BLOOD GODS is perhaps the best written example of the darker and more mature game that it's become but CHICAGO BY NIGHT 5E is also wonderfully human in its dark and tragic stories.

                                                            Originally posted by Wingstorm View Post
                                                            ... So is the setting of V5, as a whole, considered NobleBright?
                                                            In short, no.

                                                            It is not.

                                                            You are mistaken about either the definition of noblebright, or the more-or-less mechanically mandated pov of V5 player characters.

                                                            Longer explanation... Noblebright is usually seen as one pole of an axis which begins at "grimdark", and gets more optimistic as it moves toward "noblebright".

                                                            From the very first result I got when I googled "noblebright":

                                                            "Why does the third of the three brothers, who shares his food with the old woman in the wood, go on to become king of the country? Why does James Bond manage to disarm the nuclear bomb a few seconds before it goes off rather than, as it were, a few seconds afterwards? Because a universe where that did not happen would be a dark and hostile place. Let there be goblin hordes, let there be terrible environmental threats, let there be giant mutated slugs if you really must, but let there also be hope. It may be a grim, thin hope, an Arthurian sword at sunset, but let us know that we do not live in vain." – Sir Terry Pratchett, “Let There Be Dragons” (A Slip of the Keyboard)
                                                            In V5, your players are not rooting for the humans to win and build a happy world by the end of the story.

                                                            Your players aren't playing James Bond, they are playing the nuclear bomb. Your players' characters are the goblin hordes. Your players' characters are the terrible environmental threats. Your players' characters are (in a few cases, literally) the giant, mutated slugs.

                                                            For that matter, they are the stone excalibur was stuck in, preventing a new king from rising among the Britons.

                                                            If all your players' characters achieve their goals, it is quite likely there is a very long list of humans who live/die in vain, or at least at the whim of the pcs.

                                                            (Of course, it's your game. Golden rule. Run it how you like at your table. Having fun is the first rule. Yadayada. If you want a world where the good guys can win, and the bad guys are likely to lose, take a look at V2 or VRev, especially Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand. It has been criticized by some -not me- as "Vampions", but is about as close to noblebright as VtM gets. Besides the Aaron Spelling tv show, of course.)
