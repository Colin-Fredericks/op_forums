                                Originally posted by Thoth View Post
                                The Tremere are utterly hated for eaten an Antediluvian, for the Giovanni it was Tuesday. I mean other than the Samedi and Harbingers of Skulls, does anyone really care about Cappadocious getting eaten?
                                Well, the Giovanni were supposed to be the "Devil Kindred". They were originally hunted and destroyed after their destruction of Cappadocious, and they survived only by making a deal with the Camarilla.

                                But I do agree that the game has done a pretty bad job on two accounts in regards to this issue.

                                First, what exactly were the obligations of the the Giovanni? This is one area where the original Giovanni Clanbook completely failed - it was merely an IC response that laughed at the Promise of 1528 and portrayed the Camarilla as suckers. That should obviously be discounted as an ignorant, puffing blowhard. If you make a desperate deal to save yourself from destruction, you pay dearly because you have no leverage. We can track back and assume some things - like the idea the Giovanni are only allowed to embrace 3 vampires per generation (and this is approved personally by Augustus) must have been part of the deal. But "stay out of Kindred politics" is so vague, it can be hard to determine what constitutes that, or what the punishment is for violation. In practice, I see most players (especially in LARP) ignore it entirely. In contrast, I put a lot of restrictions on the Giovanni in my chronicles and clearly present them as a second class "citizen" of any domain with many restrictions on them.

                                Second, why would other vampires essentially let Giovanni wipe out the other Cappadocians? And why exactly did the Cappadocians lose? Shouldn't there be way more of them? It's not like they didn't have many active Methusalehs at the time. At least with the Salubri, we know the other clans hunted down the Salubri as well as the Tremere. But the Giovanni were by themselves and simply couldn't conduct a widescale hunt.

                                Recently I picked up an old copy of the Giovanni Chronicles I - the Last Supper. It was the first time I read it. There's some interesting details in there that make this even more curious. First, it was the intention of the Founders of the Camarilla to include the Cappadocians in the Camarilla. As traditional allies of the Ventrue, this makes sense. Why abandon this simply because the diablerie was successful? Having an eighth clan would have strengthened them. Instead, the assumption seems to be that since Augustus is now 3rd generation, he rules the bloodline now and thus what he says goes. The Giovanni now "control" the Cappadocians, and thus determine the clan's internal policy. I don't think this really makes sense, and it is at odds with how the elders treated the diablerie of Lasombra and Tzimisce. But there's lots of problems with that sourcebook. It has some good ideas, but there's several things that make me go "No, that's stupid."

                                So in my chronicles, I go back to the old, old idea that the Black Death somehow affected some vampires too (the early game had some strange notions of various diseases affecting or even killing vampires themselves, but for the Black Death I say it had a supernatural component that allowed it to kill vampires too, it was not an ordinary disease), and state it was believed the Cappadocians (or their Founder) had something to do with it. So it made a lot of Kindred fear and dislike the Cappadocians. It also obviously made them a huge target for Anarchs. And the existence of the Feast of Folly meant there were a good amount of Cappadocian Anarchs too. Since Cappadocious was in a martyr mindset specifically wishing to be sacrificed in some kind of magical ritual, it is even possible he purposefully or inadvertently created such an impulse in other members of his clan, and that helped many of them be destroyed. So by the time the Camarilla managed to impose order and convened the Convention of Thorns, there wasn't much of the Cappadocians left. So rather than rally the few Cappadocians available and push the Giovanni into the new Sabbat, the Camarilla decided coming to terms with the Giovanni was preferable.

                                But that didn't mean everyone didn't detest them. (At least the Sabbat was anathema to Kindred society. The Giovanni were those that "got away" with it, and sometimes you have to be in the same room with them. The Tremere were disliked, but their propaganda that Salubri were diabolists and soulsuckers worked so most vampires actually stopped hating them for that.) Or that many Camarilla members hoped the remaining Cappadocians would be able to rally and ultimately win the struggle for the clan (which in V5 they finally did).

                                                            Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post

                                                            They can, although there is some effort involved, because Underworld stuff fades in the Skinlands. Some Wraiths (Proctors) can cache Relics in the Skinlands for a very limited amount of time, where they appear like any mundane object. That power doesn't work for Artifacts or moliated corpus, but soulforged stuff seems to be ok. On the vampire side, Ash Path allows for some inter-shroud shenannigans. Mages can probably jury-rig something to a similar effect.
                                                            All in all getting stuff from the Underworld into the Skinlands is a lot of effort for little return. Information is definitely the most useful commodity.
