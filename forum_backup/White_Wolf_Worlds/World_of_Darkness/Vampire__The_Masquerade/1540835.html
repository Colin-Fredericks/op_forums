    [V20 and before] Is Demeanor really useful?

    08-21-2024, 10:14 AM
    So, I'm asking a very broad and generic question, as the title suggests. On paper, the mechanic is good, but I often find that it is either not useful or overlooked.

    Mechanically: RAWisn't linked to any willpower gain. It's associated with rare or obscure powers/merits (like Akhu) that don't really come into play. I'm well aware of the widespread HR where you can gain willpower based on Demeanor as well.

    Roleplay: Demeanor is "how you behave," which fits perfectly with a game like Vampire, where everything is a "masquerade" between vampires.

    So, with this in mind, in the end, is it something your ST/Players refer to? Something they use? Something you use? I find myself almost always undermining it. As an ST, if I need to decide how an NPC behaves, I look at their Nature and consider their background. As a player, I gain willpower, experience points, and suggestions on "how to behave" from STs, usually based on my Nature or background. So, in the end, is Demeanor really that useful to be present at the top of every character sheet? It SHOULD add something to the game, but does it REALLY?

    (as ST) Do you enforce its use? Do you write it for every NPC?
    (as PC) Do you try to understand the Demeanor of NPCs to predict their actions/reactions?

    Is Demeanor really better than a "vice" system or some sort of urge/pulsion as in other gamelines?

          Demeanor is a roleplaying stat that is used to help keep your character's roleplay and interactions consistent during a scene. Demeanors can change but usually not within a short time, as that would be super erratic behavior (though this can be useful for a character like a Malkavian with Multiple Personality Disorder...) PC's should never know a NPC's Nature/Demeanor stat without some kind of supernatural ability to reveal it.That's meta knowledge that shouldn't influence the Character's actions.

          Mechanically Nature/Demeanor are equivalent to the Alignment chart from D&D. Its not wholly necessarily to the game, and the game can be played without it, but its useful in keeping a certain type of consistency.

              I've only see Nature and Demeanor come into play as initial roleplaying tools to help players better define their PC concept. After character creation, they don't come into play and nobody pays attention to them. As an ST, if I see a PC deviate too far from their Demeanor (or Nature), that will prompt me to ask the player if they are aware of that or want to change their Demeanor as its listed on their character sheet. Sometimes that causes the player to reconsider how they're roleplaying so they can go back to their original conception of the character, but more often they just change it. But that tends to be rare as most of the time they still play in a way that fits their character.

              Demeanor has never had any mechanical effect, and I and my gaming groups never thought it should.

              As both a player and ST, I do use the Nature/Demeanor system to consider the kind of character that person is. More devious or two faced characters will have vastly different ones while more honest and trustworthy characters have more similar ones. But as I said, this is really part of initial character creation.

              Nature, of course, is written that a character can regain Willpower whenever they fulfill their archetype. However, I rarely see my gaming group ever use temporary Willpower as an asset in game! They just forget about it. As an ST, I encourage players to use Willpower to get automatic successes and the like, but they never do. I think the players in my gaming group are more worried about NPCs trying to mind control them, and therefore want to hoard temporary Willpower to spend to resist. So they never seem to spend Willpower, always waiting for when it'll be "needed." I've seen that as a player and as an ST, in Vampire, Werewolf, and other WoD games. I think that the requirement of spending it BEFORE they know the results of their roll causes a lot of players to not want to "waste" it if it is not needed. I think if they could spend it AFTER they rolled, it would get a lot more use. Ignoring wound penalties get some use, but in the heat of combat, players often forget that rule.

              The people I play with just never seem to spend Willpower, so they never bother to regain it by fulfilling their Nature. So it just defaults as another initial character creation rule. When I play, I typically hold back some temporary Willpower for emergencies, but spend them when I absolutely need a success. That reminds my gaming group it can be done, but it never seems to encourage them to ever do that themselves.

                  It's a guide. Being there is generally useful, for some more than for others.
                  But enforcing it? I don't think I ever met someone who does that, and I think it would be a quite obnoxious thing to do. If the person actually playing the character don't find a need to go by it, so what?

                  Originally posted by Aydoo View Post

                  Mechanically Nature/Demeanor are equivalent to the Alignment chart from D&D. Its not wholly necessarily to the game, and the game can be played without it, but its useful in keeping a certain type of consistency.
                  I disagree. Nature, sure, it has very similar function.

                  Demeanor doesn't, though. D&D has a metaphysical weight to Alignment that's reflected in several mechanics and spells (at least in older editions, but some still play by those rules), and Nature has a similar function of being an important element for the game that rules and powers interact with.

                  But Demeanor is merely a guide, period. It has no weight that can be measured. It interacts with no system. And I doubt that punishing or rewarding its use would be a good idea. It is its own reward as a roleplay guideline.

                      The easier way I’ve explained it to my players is this: Nature is how you interact with the world. Demeanor is how the world interacts with YOU.

                      As an example: if I’m a police officer and my Nature is Caregiver, it doesn’t matter if I want to help people, because all the world sees is COP and they will treat me accordingly.
