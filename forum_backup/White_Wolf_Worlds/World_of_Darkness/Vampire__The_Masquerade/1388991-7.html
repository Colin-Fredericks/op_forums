            Originally posted by Taggie View Post
            VtMs Premise is being about Vampires...and then you find out later it's only Vampires based on Christian Myth, no other Vampires are real, most other religions are blood cults and thus totally invalid and only followed by mentally deficient members of inferior cultures, whose ancestors where suckered by the superior creatures of abrahamic myth. If the game was open with being Abrahamic-supremacist, that would be one thing, but it claims to be about Vampires......yea.
            I see a lot of posts on this forum like this, but I never had a problem like this. I don't think the game forces you to adopt Christianity or Abrahamic religion as objectively true. The fact that vampires ascribe their origins to a "Caine" is meaningless. While it makes total sense that Europeans in the Middle Ages would ascribe their origin to Cain, who was thought to be the father of many monsters, it would be absolutely ludicrous to think vampires from a pre-Christian tradition would ever think that to attribute their origins on the Holy Book of a small tribe of Jews. Especially those who were created before the Jewish people or the Kingdom of Israel ever existed. Obviously vampires in ancient Sumeria would NOT ascribe their origins to Cain. They would use another placeholder. And neither would the Classical Greeks or the early Romans.

            Do you really think that Lucian, the NPC Gangrel vampire who marched with Julius Caesar, and was literally in the first edition of the corebook, was told by his sire that the origin of vampires lies in the text of the Jewish people, who were a recent and marginal acquisition of the Roman Empire during this lifetime?

            There are only a few essential elements that need to occur (and even this is debateable), and any other details are simply flavor text. You need an antedeluvian world. You need a Deluge. And you need someone to be the original vampire that spawned all others. You can get that basic pattern almost anywhere. You can find it in mythology (the Greek Golden Age of Saturn, the Flood of Deucalion, and almost any number of figures - I like using Dionysus's death and resurrection at the hands of the Titans), occultism (Helena Blavatsky's Theosophism), or fiction (Robert E Howard's Conan and Kull stories). Hell, even Marvel Comics has it with Atlantis, the Cataclysm, and Varnae.

            The game doesn't do much to support this by including alternative interpretations. But there is some precedent. The Setites after all tell a different origin story. In my own chronicles, I routinely use older vampires relate the story in a different format. It's quite easy to do. And it doesn't need much imagination to do so.

            It just seems some people aren't able to read the text critically. But I immediately saw the potential for other interpretations when I first read the second edition corebook almost thirty years ago.

                    Seems pretty obvious to me it’s either a Christian-era rationalization of an older vampire myth, which may in turn have influenced the Judaic myth of Cain and Abel, or a Christian-era fabrication to begin with. There’s just too much about the Noddist mythos that has nothing really to do with anything from the Bible or related texts, apart from a few names. Much easier to believe it’s assimilation to Judaic tradition happened relatively recently (say, between one and two millennia ago). Interestingly, there’s no trace of the Book of Nod before then, as the earliest manuscripts are medieval and most are even later.

                    So either it’s a Gnostic-style late reinterpretation of Biblical elements to suit a vampire audience or a Biblical-style reinterpretation of a prehistoric vampire myth of different origin, which doesn’t survive in its earlier form. Either way it’s interesting but far from solid evidence of anything.

                        Um, I'm not sure how you got that.

                        "Caine" is simply a placeholder. It might be the Biblical Cain. It could be someone else in world mythology. It could be Varnae from Marvel Comics. Or Sauron from Numenor. Or someone else entirely.

                        I think it is far more likely that Lucian's sire would have told him some story like "You now possess a portion of the ichor of the gods, first seized by men during the Golden Age of Saturn. But when Jupiter overthrew Cronus, he cursed such men as he did Prometheus for stealing the secret of fire for Men. He attempted to destroy them in the Flood of Deucalion, but thirteen survived. The blood of one of those thirteen now resides in you." Or some other such variant. His sire might have used cultural elements from some other mythology he was more familiar with. Or he might have "translated" them into Greco-Roman myth in order to aid Lucian's comprehension.

                        This is how I assume the current version of the vampire origin legend became associated with Cain and the Bible. In the long centuries of Christian and Muslim rule of mortals, these would have been the associations new vampires associated with concepts like a Flood, and their own association with Cain as a father of evil monsters, like how he was referred to in Beowulf. Since this is what was "known" when the sects of the Camarilla and Sabbat formed, this single version of the story became the official one.

                        I'm sure someone like Critias who was embraced long before Christianity and would be familiar with many different pagan cultures, he heard many different variants of the same vampire myth - with various details changing, but a "core" group of facts remaining the same - there was once a single vampire from which all others were sprung; this was in the days before a Great Flood; only thirteen survived and all vampires are now descended from one of them.

                        Assuming the Biblical stories are true in WoD, the Jews would have obtained their information on the antedeluvian world and the creation in the same way they obtained them in this world (if you believe in their religion) - oral tradition from Noah plus any divine revelation.

                        Any stories told by vampires repeating what the clan founders told their childer would simply be in addition to that. Plus any other occult information revealed by any numerous ways about that world. You could have sleeping prophets like Edgar Cayce. Stories revealed by angels to the Celestial Chorus. Other remnants from the Deluge that survived. Lost artifacts and possibly even wraiths encountered in the Tempest or Stygia. Vampires aren't any more privileged in that regard. Except that the antedeluvians would have had first hand knowledge. But whatever they told their childer would not have been complete, probably misunderstood, and possibly deliberately misinformed.

                        Not to mention the retention of (corrupted) lore as recorded by all the other world civilizations who simply didn't preserve such knowledge accurately.

                        And if you want to go with some other culture's myth as more true, or your own version as the real thing, the same things hold true except the Jewish tradition also is corrupted to a degree and isn't as accurate as anyone else's stories.

                        But even if you want the Biblical stories to be true, it does not mean that anyone outside of that tradition would be using terms found only in Jewish lore. Thousands of years since the Flood cause any such oral traditions to change, and by the time you get to Classical Greece, Rome, Persia, or India, many details are going to look different. It's like how the Indo-European peoples had one pantheon of gods with certain names, and 4000 years later you have a dozen or more different mythologies derived from it. The Norse, Celtic, and Greek religions all had the same source, yet they seem like they are totally different. The rate of change might be slower among vampires than humans, but it'll still happen.

                                        Like what Black Fox is talking about, in my games I usually lean towards that before the rise of the Abrahamic religions, the use of the terms 'Caine' or 'Enoch' to describe The First and The First City were vanishing rare, if they existed at all they were likely limited to the Semitic peoples in there earliest proto forms.

                                        Vampires of the eldest generations recalled the stories of their elders from before The Flood and The Dark Father but a shroud of mystery cloaks that primordial age and so eventually by the time the grandchilder of The Antediluvian's grandchilder walked the night, the stories had split and transformed into a thousand regional variants.

                                        Now, after the rise of the Abrahamic Faiths and their successful claim to being the predominant cultural faith of the western world, the stories refer to Caine and Abel and Enoch.

                                        The part that I always come back to with thinking about it one way or another is this: do I prefer it being metacosmologically fluid in that The First Vampire is simply now referred to as Caine or if they have become Caine retrospectively due to consensus or if they, whether or not they always had been, as of now they were always Caine.

                                        Yeah, kind of a mind screw and not one I think I'll settle onto a personal answer for, both because the subject somewhat denies the pursuit of any one answer and also that I think either avenue can be used to the purpose of benefitting a story, either alone or together regardless of the seeming contradictions.

                                        Although what I ultimately tend to come down to is that Caine has probably accepted the title of Caine, but the name that Eve gave him when he was fresh from her womb has most likely been forever lost to the ravages of time.
