    [Roleplaying] Should all Kindred be murderers?

    11-01-2025, 11:06 PM
    Happy Halloween!

    A difficult subject ahead but an actual discussion topic for games that involve the subject of PERSONAL HORROR.

    *thunderclap*

    But I was curious how the subject of accidentally killing innocent mortals during Hunger or Frenzy comes up in your games. Either as something that happens during game or as a matter of Session Zero and backstory.

    For me, I think it's something to do as part of the establishing of vampirism as a curse. Sessions handled with player consent (they may not want to deal with) that at some point you've killed someone you didn't want to.

    So that every vampire has something to feel guilty about or brood about. Not all Kindred HAVE to feel guilty about it. Many won't or have put it past them.

    But this is the option for roleplaying.

    So I think when establishing a game, it's a question you should ask, "Who have you killed in the past you regret? or Did not intend to?"

    How about you?

      Most players have a general idea of how they want their character to approach hunting and feeding. This is my experience in the games I've played in. Some are fine with being real bastards. Most usually want to avoid killing except when it's purposefully done (for whatever reason). Only a few want their characters to actively avoid harming humans. I'm generally fine accommodating their wishes provided they support their choice by their actions during the game or downtime. Sometimes events of a game session take on a life of their own, and the players need to be fine if something happens they did not plan on.

      So I do think it is a good idea for the ST to ask their players some general questions about feeding for their PCs, but I don't have a list of any specific questions. I may ask follow up questions based on what they tell me.

          In general, it should be extremely rare for any vampire that's been around for more than a decade or so to not have killed someone, whether intentionally or by accident. The Beast's calls are just too loud for almost anyone to resist. That said, I don't have an issue with a player character being one of the few exceptions, if they don't want to have their character to have ever actually killed someone before. Sometimes players might want to roleplay draining and killing someone for the first time. It would be problematic if the player character is so against killing that, for example, if they accidentally killed someone in play their character immediately commits suicide or the player otherwise moved to stop playing the character or something along those lines because the player's attitude is, "I don't want to play a character that has killed someone."

          Life is pretty cheap in the World of Darkness, vampires are monsters, and the potential of killing someone who doesn't "deserve it" (whether intentionally or by accident) is something that the players need to be okay with when they agree to play a VtM in the game.

              Yeah, I think in a sense the question is fair, but not in its best presentation.

              First, there must be space for the player who don't want their character to have done it, be it because they want to have it happen during play, be it because they truly want to avoid it (although I'd agree with AnubisXy that you should at least be willing to deal with failure without giving up).

              Second, because it isn't relevant for every character. Even if the character isn't a consummate murderer, or even low Humanity, the occasional death may simply not be relevant as a story element for this character in the eyes of the player, at least not as a regret.

              At the same time, there are two possible related questions that may matter for some. A consummate killer may still have a death in their belt they didn't want (so there was the lack of autonomy), and for those who avoid killing, it may be more productive to ask about close calls, or how much effort they put into preventing deaths.

              In the end the answer is no, not all Kindred should be murderers. That's a rather simplistic view of what personal horror means to each one, or how they deal with it and how they cope. It also delves too much to my taste into Grim Dark takes of the setting and the idea that there's only one right, edgy way of playing it.

                  Yeah, this is a delicate subject to broach but is part of a larger question of how dark we like our V:TM games and how much the subject of them being dangerous monsters might come up in the game. It's actually something I was thinking of due to the Interview with a Vampire adaptation for AMC. Part of it being where Lestat takes the time to insult the idea of only feeding on the evil doer in the show when it was an important part of his revisions in THE VAMPIRE LESTAT.

                  I think this was worth making as a topic because it also led me to think of how the idea of the player characters getting forced to murder is indicated by 5th Edition versus the original ones. The inability to feed on animals for most generations, the difficult of bagging, the much larger focus on frenzy, hunger dice, and the fact that if you kill someone then that is the only way to reduce your hunger to 0.

                  I think part of is an attempt to hold down a more heroic and comic-book interpretation of vampires. The thing is that I think that's a perfectly valid if not PREFERRED take on vampires versus what others do. I feel like V:TM is fundamentally a very stylized action-orientated game that reveals in the power as well as the curse of the Kindred condition.

                      To answer the question: "Should all Kindred be murderers?" My answer is: Absolutely not.

                      Thankfully, none of the editions go that far. The interesting part between vampires and humans is that it isn't just about a predator-prey-relationship but also about a need for sustenance. The humans are prey - but they are also vital to the vampire as a source of sustenance.

                      How far you go in portraying that relationship is up to the table, I think. And these days, I think all that really matters is what the people at the table think. And choosing the edition should be up to the table as well. I think there's no fixed answer for every table as to which edition would serve them best. And lore-wise... it's the same, really. Just do whatever feels right.

                      But to come back to the original question: Being a vampire is not about murder. It's about being a supernatural predator whose prey are humans. And you have to navigate that relationship. Managing to get access to human blood doesn't have to imply you have to turn into a sociopathic serial killer. That's part of the fun, in my opinion: The parallel universe that establishes itself when a city accommodates vampires in its midst. And that's where the whole World of Darkness bit starts to work: It isn't about a monster chasing some innocent person at night, but rather there's a whole society of monsters with all sorts of conventions, norms and schemes operating in secret.

                      I think the vampires who come up with all sorts of pretenses and arrangements that help them justify to themselves what they're doing are more interesting than a vampire-character who's just going around killing people to drink their blood. I could see in a Hunter-game where you could prefer to portray vampires as the latter but when the PCs are vampires, I think delving into the former is more interesting.

                                      Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                      Yeah, this is a delicate subject to broach but is part of a larger question of how dark we like our V:TM games
                                      It's not a delicate subject for this game, murder is a focal theme of Vampire. It's just that this assumption doesn't necessarily makes for the best approach to start the game.

                                      Every Vampire is a dangerous monster, and this is part and parcel of the personal horror aspect of the game.

                                      But that doesn't necessarily means every one has already committed murder, and it's that extra step that limits what you can do in the game. The story of a vampire that still struggles against those impulses and hopes 9even if in vain) to never get there, becomes impossible if you just decide everyone has to have already killed.

                                      On how dark we want the setting, that's the problem again. An RPG must be as open as possible for the ST to adjust those things as the chronicle needs. First edition Vampire was definitely a less dark setting, because the characters being able to rise above it was fundamental to its design, but ultimately making all the bright parts unconfirmed meant you had a versatile darkness dial. When you make assumptions that lock the setting in the darkest option, you're breaking the dial.

                                      Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                      That's an interesting take on the subject because I've always felt, "are vampires predators?"
                                      As a literary device, vampires are abusers.

                                      How they relate to their condition, if they kill or not, or if this has a place in nature/Creation or not, those are secondary considerations.

                                      Vampires are abusers and that remains true in VtM regardless if they kill. What makes them monstrous is that they can't not harm mortals, or at least run the constant risk of it. As a society they do that in spades, at a whole another level.

                                      One of the problems with obligatory-killer vampires is that it actually diminishes how we can explore this theme of abuse and oversimplify it. V5 specially makes it terrible by having some vampires be necessary murderers precisely when they could be the most exquisite abusers.
