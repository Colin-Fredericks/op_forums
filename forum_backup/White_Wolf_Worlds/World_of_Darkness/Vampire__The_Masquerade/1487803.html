    Why are Vampires so calm with the fact that spirits exist?!

    07-24-2022, 07:47 PM
    This is something that has never made much sense to me in lore, you see a Giovanni/Tremere/Blood Magician summon Wraiths/Elemental spirits/Something else entirely out of nowhere, and your first reaction is "meh''?

    In every single clanbook, including the ones about clans with blood magic, they will downplay the abilities of the magic using clans. So you're telling me that no vampire who has heard/seen a Giovanni speak to a wraith isn't freaking out that ghosts actually exist and that there are vampires that can enslave them? Are you telling me that a vampire that has seen a Tremere summon a spirit using Elemental Mastery/Spirit Manipulation, didn't have their whole world view shattered by the fact that there are such things as spirits and that certain vampires can summon them? What about demons? The Red List is public to all Vampires, with three of them being known infernalists. Why are the Kindred in power the only ones worried, why is no one else freaking out over the fact that this is plausible proof that demons exist, and can be summoned, which means that Hell exists?

    The Baali Clanbook goes into detail about the creatures they serve/imprison/enslave, it explores the relationships different factions of the Baali have with these creatures, and how they view these creatures. No other blood magic clan does that with their respective blood magic tradition. The Giovanni whose signature power necromancy, which revolves around wraiths, are never shown pondering if enslaving the souls of the deceased is a bad thing, they never stop for a second and think "Am I going to hell for this?". What about the Tremere that are knowledgeable about the umbra or know paths/rituals that deal with spirits/umbra? They seriously never stop for a second to ponder where all these spirits came from? These are the same complaints I have about Assamite Sorcerers, Kolduns, Sadhana practitioners, Lector Priests, e.t.c. Seriously, no Lector Priest has ever wondered if demons were actually servants of Set?

    It's crazy that none of the books go into this!

    What do you think?

                  Because vampire magic does not invoke Paradox, it means they can show mortals that ghosts and spirits exists. I can see it being against the Code of Ananda, alongside Akashic and Euthanatoi philosophy. But the Order of Hermes would definitely want to experiment with this, to codify a semi reliable process.

However, ghosts remove the afterlife from an unconfirmed possibility into facts that were documented by blood magic for ages. Spirits not only add a new layer of complexity to all reality, but the Umbra encompasses entire dimensions, with nuances and laws of physics beyond the wildest imagination of sci-fi authors.                           I'm going to suggest something surprising: I don't think most vampires actually believe what you're talking about if they aren't the kind of person who believed in them to begin with.

                          You first have to deal with the paradigm shift that "vampires exist" obviously and that's going to be something that modern secular materialists are already going to be blown away. They might be able to adjust the majority of their worldview by believing vampires are natural predators, some sort of mutant, or otherwise creatures of science. These people are wrong. Vampires are magical and defy all conventional science to the point that you CAN scientifically study them but it would create an entirely new field of study as well as state the majority of what you know about others is wrong.

                          Which leads to point two, "magic is real." If you're a modern secular materialist, you're going to have enough trouble with vampirism but that's going to get into the fact that Tremere (and let's stick with the base Seven Clans to start with) can do something fundamentally DIFFERENT than what other vampires can with superpowers. They can mark a wall with a squiggly line in blood and then you flat out can't enter that place anymore. Plenty of vampires will try to say, "No, no, Blood magic is just Disciplines with a bit more weirdness."

                          But taking a step further, "Spirits are real." Well, after Point A and Point B, it's probably less of a leap than you might think. Because if you're not a modern secular materialist then you've probably come from a society that already believes in spirits. Plenty of cultures believe in angels, demons, and so on anyway. Many more believe in an afterlife so the idea that both of these exist is something that will probably be less surprising than you might think if you're anyone other than someone who dismissed these things as superstition in life.

                          Even then, I'd argue if you ARE a modern secular materialist vampire, you might actually dismiss these things because you know at this point:

                          * Mind control exists
                          * Memory alteration
                          * Magic that can do weird stuff
                          * Possibly illusion powers as the Ravnos are as common as the Hecata
                          * The real life Charlatans

                          Assume our modern secular materialist vampire, call him Fred the Brujah, hears that the Hecata are a bunch of mobsters (because the Putenesca is assumed to be the Hecata by 90% of people), that can talk to the dead and control their spirits. Are you going to believe that or that the rumor mill has gotten out of hand or they're a bunch of frauds like everyone else who "talks to ghosts"? (And aren't necessarily frauds in the World of Darkness anyway but are still probably 90% frauds).

                          But if you AREN'T Fred, by the time you find out about spirits, you've already been led far down the Rabbit Hole and may come from a culture that already believes in spirits and the supernatural that you might react to the discovery with, "Okay, sure."

                          I am a religious person in RL but if I'm a vampire, I might go, "Oh, Zeus is real too. I believe it."

                                      First, I think that simply the fact that they're vampires means they've come to terms that the supernatural is real. If vampires are real, then why not werewolves, wizards, ghosts, spirits, fairies, and all sorts of other things? So I don't think it's as shocking as all that for them. Now a newly turned vampire who has just encountered the supernatural may not know how much of the supernatural legends and stories are true and to what extent, but they'll learn fairly quickly. Their adjustment to this new worldview will have already occurred by the time they are released, and actually encounter other supernatural creatures.

                                      Brock, if pirates really exist that means Santa Claus and the tooth fairy can even be real, right? It's like all bets are off.
                                      Hank, nobody ever said pirates don't exist.
                                      So you agree with me that this is impossible!


                                      Second, when it comes to spirits and ghosts, most vampires will actually never encounter one. Or if they do, it's of a limited nature. The Gauntlet and the Shroud do a lot to protect the physical realm from them, and while their charms and powers might be impressive, most of the time what they do can be far easier replicated by other means. I don't think that's a "meh" reaction. It's just the difference between actually being in a warzone, and simply learning that there's a war in a far away country.

                                      There is also the issue that certain people (characters and players) who want to play down any kind of shock or amazement because they want to portray an attitude of "I've seen this all before, and it doesn't impress me." That is often a BS reaction done because they don't want to lose face, or think this kind of roleplaying means their character is a badass. It's boring roleplay, but it's unfortunately typical. Just for different reasons.

                                      Third, I do actually think that those occultist vampires who are conjuring spirits and ghosts actually discuss the issues that you are mentioning. The Tremere should have Hermetic theories on the nature of the cosmos and role of the spirits. Necromancers should have theories on the underworld and nature of ghosts. In terms of actual game play, these are the kind of discussions I do see happen between PCs and NPCs. Those are either good roleplayers, or new players who don't know the lore and want to understand. When PCs don't talk about them, it is generally because 1) they do know the lore OOC and just assume a lot of IC knowledge, or 2) those kind of discussions aren't fun for them and they want to get back to something they do find fun. But those are ultimately OOC issues and not IC issues.

                                      Lastly, the WoD suffers from a lack of mystery nowadays since so much of its lore is known. When the "real" answer is easily researched by players, it ends the kind of speculation that characters should be doing. This is one reason why I, when I am the ST, like to have NPCs explain things in different ways that don't match the established lore (though it doesn't contradict it either). Don't mention the terms of art of the World of Darkness. It's "another dimension" not the "Umbra". There are "cracks in reality" not "a low Gauntlet." The movie Ghostbusters is great with stuff like this.

                                      Sir, what you had there is what we refer to as a focused, non-terminal, repeating phantasm or a class-five full-roaming vapor.


                                      I find that when you eschew known game terminology for other descriptions, you are more likely to obtain more interest from your players.
