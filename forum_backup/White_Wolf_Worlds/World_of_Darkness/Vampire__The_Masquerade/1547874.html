    We have nosferatu, we have nosferatu today

    01-21-2025, 08:06 AM
    (Anyone who can tell me where that title came from and why it’s not actually appropriate to the actual subject of this thread wins bragging rights for at least the next five and possibly even ten minutes minutes).


    Fellow fans, as I’m sure you’re already aware a new Graf Orlock is growling, prowling and tearing his way through the streets of Wisborg, Germany (Not to mention residents thereof), and across cinema screens worldwide.

    Now given that this character is the namesake of Clan Nosferatu, one of the pillars of the Masquerade, it seems appropriate to ask “What should THE Nosferatu look like in VAMPIRE: THE MASQUERADE terms?”


    - For example, should he be a fully paid-up member of the eponymous Clan? (Especially in his newest incarnation, one could make a case for his being an unsociable Tzimisce).


    - When inserting Grak Orlock into Earth-Masquerade’s backstory should we go with the classic ‘Not Dracula, especially if Mrs Florence Stoker comes asking’ storyline or should we go with THE SHADOW OF THE VAMPIRE storyline? (Which depicts the original NOSFERATU being filmed with an actual vampire passing himself off as ‘Max Shreck’, with carnage ensuing).


    - Finally, how old and of what generation should Graf Orlock be? (Are there any vampires in the Classic World of Darkness who would make an especially appropriate site for this very notorious vampire?).

      Actually, one might also add “What does the former Hanseatic port of Wisborg, somewhere on the German coast*, look like in Modern nights?”

      For the record, it amuses me to imagine that the city itself would be almost empty of Nosferatu, but that the Clan would keep a ‘listening post’ in the surrounding region, apparently nervous as cats on the roof of a burning kennel about something in (or something that happened in) the city.

      Whether that would be a past humiliation or a current peril remains to be determined.


      *It seems fair to note that while the city appears to be inspired by Whitby (from DRACULA), a North Sea port, the original filming location was Wismar (A port on the Baltic Sea).

                          Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                          I’m also interested in learning more about your Interbellum Berlin campaign: might one please ask if you would be willing to share any details of that particular setting? (I know that the Prince of Berlin was horribly enthusiastic about the Nazi party, but little more than that).
                          It had absolutely nothing to do with the sourcebook Berlin By Night because I absolutely hated it. I only used a few (relatively minor) NPCs from it. I've had a long obsession with Weimar era Berlin and wanted to do it justice.

                          In my (prepared, but never played) chronicle, the Ventrue Prince of Berlin (who was not Gustav Breidenstein, but a Dutch Ventrue who came to the city after the end of the Thirty Years War) was killed in early 1919. He was replaced with a female Toreador who ruled the city until the Gotterdamerung of 1945 which saw the city levelled and almost all native vampires destroyed. I planned to run the chronicle only until 1939 because I figured the players would not want to roleplay the agony of seeing the city destroyed (as well as avoid painful issues involving the Nazi Party during the war).

                          The chronicle was tentatively titled "Red Berlin". I still have all my materials and may run it some day, assuming I find players who really want to explore that period and place. It was originally intended as a twenty game session LARP (one game for every year) back when I was LARPing regularly, but that never got more than one page of notes. I later expanded that into a full blown tabletop chronicle.

Elaborate tattoos of lush vegetation — flowers, vines, trees, grasses, herbs, etc. — in screamingly vivid hues cover every visible inch of her skin. Because she takes great pride in her body art, she wears clothing that allows her ink to be seen." (p. 164)
