                    The big problem with criminal activity is that the longer you're at it, and the more successful you are, eventually you're going to come up on the radar of law enforcement. This will be local police, but eventually it will include Federal agencies. Rats chew through wires and then someone robs a bank and a bunch of money goes missing? Yeah, the FBI is going to be all over that. Whatever ends up happening, that could end up being a a significant Masquerade Breach. The rewards are great, but the risks can snowball pretty quickly. Generally speaking this is why the Camarilla tends not to truck too much with the criminal underworld. For the most part it's the Independent Clans (the Giovanni and the Followers of Set) that have the biggest presence in the underworld. Among the Camarilla, only the Brujah tended to engage much with criminal elements, and then it was often the Anarch wing of the Clan.

                    That's not to say that the Camarilla never engages with criminals, but members would be encouraged to keep such interactions at a distance and short term rather than getting too caught up in that sort of lifestyle. If a vampire starts risking drawing the attention of Federal law enforcement (especially in V5 with the Second Inquisition) the Camarilla would quickly move to force the vampire to cut those ties - either by faking a death or just outright killing the vampire for the risk of drawing that sort of heat to the area.

                        Originally posted by Hello View Post
                        In VTM, a common trait is that the Elders have all the power/money and the young have none. My issue is that very few Neonates seem to capitalize on their inherent supernatural abilities to gain power/resources using crime.

                        Presence and Dominate can be used quite easily to form a criminal organization/gang, force mortals into giving you what you want, and to scare mortals into not moving against you. Auspex can alert you to the movement of law enforcement and victims, as well as help you obtain important information like the code to the safe where jewelers keep their most valuable items. In fact, every discipline can be used in some way to easily commit crimes that mortals would have serious trouble pulling off.

                        However, you almost never see neonates capitalizing on it.

                        Why is that?​
                        Depends on whatever group you're hanging with because it's pretty common for the Anarch groups I belong to, to have the players come from criminal organizations. However, I should note that you've actually just described the problem AND the solution.

                        Neonate: Hey, I'm going to rob three banks.

                        ST: You get all of the loot.

                        Neonate: What, really?

                        ST: As much as you can carry off. It goes off without a hitch. Now a bunch of ghouls and the Sheriff show up at your haven.

                        Neonate: What, why?

                        ST: Because you didn't ask permission to rob banks under Al Capone. They're also Ballard's banks.

                        EVERYTHING is someone's territory in the big city.

                                                        I think a part of why crime gets associated with neonates is that it makes sense thematically with what we’re told about the general relations of elders and neonates towards resources and influence: there is a scarcity of resources and the elders (and ancillae) have had the time to bogart those resources and the ‘legitimate levers of power’ (government, media, finance, major corporations, police, etc.) and all that’s left for the neonates is to either scrabble for crumbs from their elders or to carve out a niche for themselves in the illegitimate world (crime).

                                                        I think one of the issues that comes with that is that there is a struggle for STs to portray that dynamic of scarcity and hegemony of the elders in play. It’s one thing to say that all the major levers of power have been claimed by the local elders and ancillae but in reality the clever neonate can probably get their way to 5 resources even if limited to a select number of small businesses and interest in the C-list financial institutions that are too small and numerous for older vampires to have claimed them all. This means that, if played that way, even the average neonate within a traditional Camarilla domain can establish themselves in tremendous material comfort without needing to resort to the world of underworld finance. The only reason then for the typical neonate-anarch criminal archetype to take shape then is if they either can’t use legitimate avenues or they simply choose not to, whether out of arbitrary pique or a willful rejection of the establishment.

                                                        Now I don’t think the above is necessarily the only way to run it. I can imagine a careful ST combining geographical carving and allocation to create a situation where a young vampire is forced to either bend the knee or to become a de facto outlaw to get a leg-up and I think that would create an interesting setting, perhaps one more faithful to the intent of the first edition game, but I don’t think it’s the only way either.

                                                        The well-designed scarcity on the one hand creates a setting where the neonates either accept submission and chafe under the yoke in order to gain power or they accept the mantle of anarch and combine poaching, raiding, and criminality to carve out their own niche (which interestingly means that even as the Anarchs must be suppressed the elders probably have a greater respect for the Anarchs than they do the loyalists). On the other hand the less high-pressure high-scarcity domains allow those who are willing to play the game to gain a comfortable position with relative ease - the tensions then become one of a relative difference. What does it matter that you have 5 Resources if you still only have 1-2 Status and all the best domains are still under the control of the elders? This could create another interesting setting where a significant section of young vampires are satisfied to retreat into their own private concerns, some drop out of society entirely, but an ambitious few yearn for the opportunity to overthrow enough of their elders to gain a bigger piece of the pie. This could create a situation not unlike how Chicago by Night is depicted in early books.

                                                        Honestly, after some thought, I could imagine these as not being contradictory but rather a spectrum. A ‘healthy’ vampiric domain resembles the latter case where scarcity is relatively low and their is enough free resources available to buy off a section of the young licks and to keep either the ambitious or the Anarchs from gaining a significant section of followers. A ‘dangerous’ vampiric domain could resemble the former case where scarcity is relatively high and the young vampires must either enter society as toadies to gain advancement or reject society entirely and adopt the anarch ways (somewhat like how Denver comes off in that one early adventure book, Alien Ways iirc).

                                                        These are just my two cents on how a consideration of setting based on first principles could effect the answers one comes to from a question like this.

                                                            Most PCs who want such a criminal empire is going to establish that Backgrounds - buying the appropriate Influence, Allies, Retainers, etc. (justified in some way, and their Disciplines can be part of that) and have it operated in the background.

                                                            In general, players want to spend their time at the game table on things that cannot be established through Backgrounds. They want face time with other vampires, supernatural forces, roleplay with humans in their immediate circle, work on the ST plot, investigate chronicle mysteries or groups in the setting, and so on. In contrast, roleplaying through the minutiae of building up their "empire" from scratch either does not interest them, challenge them, or divert from things that are more fun. It's a distraction.

                                                            I can think of plenty of exceptions, but I believe most players (at least in a table top experience, I know Online gamesn or any games that allow for extensive bluebooking, can be very different) want to use their limited time at the game table differently.

                                                            However, I do admit that is can be trivially easy for vampires to get anything they would want from mortals. They just need to do it in a way that does not attract much attention or threaten the Masquerade (not even necessarily through committing a crime). A lot of vampires probably survive that way on a day to day basis. And sometimes PCs overlook such solutions, or overcomplicate such things. Or the ST is too inexperienced to prepare for such solutions or deal with them to problems they intended as challenges.
