                        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                        Why should the Court so readily accept the one who's a Kuei-Jin, but won't ever understand their ways fully, and refuse the Kin-Jin who came from the same people surrounding them and already knows how to interpret the whole spiel in one go?
                        At that point we're getting into religious territory. The idea that the Wan Kuei were returned from hell at the behest of the Jade Emperor, for the higher purpose of saving the world from the Yama Kings is central to the Wan Kuei's existence. This means that the Wan Kuei have very specific duties under heaven's mandate, whereas the Kindred do not.

                        Spiritually, the Wan Kuei have a divenly ordained obligation to assist other Wan Kuei in finding their path, even if those Wan Kuei are not from the local culture, whereas the Kindred are entirely outside of that divine mandate, regardless of what culture they grew up in. In fact, the Quincunx's aggressive attempts to spread the 5 "true" dharmas to all Wan Kuei, including places well outside of China, is a major source of conflict for the Wan Kuei and forms the basis of a lot of tension between the different Courts and the various heretical dharmas.

                        It would be similar to asking why the Prince of New York would be willing to extend the Traditions to a Kindred from Africa, but not to a werespider from the Bronx. At the end of the day, the Camarilla's traditions and rules are intended for the Kindred, not for every creature that happens to feed on blood. Similarly the Wan Kuei's rules and obligations are directed towards other Wan Kuei, not everyone who was born in Asia or everyone who feeds on blood (note that they would also accept a foreign Wan Kuei in their ranks over a werespider that was born locally).

                                                    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                                    My point isn't to deny this, but to remind that culture matters to how they think about someone. The post I was responding to was specifically about regarding the Kindred as uncultured.
                                                    The thing is, for the Wan Kuei, their culture isn't mortal culture. It's not about being Chinese or Japanese or Indian. It's not about being able to quote Confucius or the Mahabharata. Their culture is about being the Wan Kuei, the direct descendents of the Ten Thousand Immortals. People who have gone to hell and then crawled back out at the behest of the Jade Emperor. The Wan Kuei's culture is much bigger than geographical mortal cultures. That's also the big reason why they look down on most other supernatural beings. Other supernatural beings, whether shapeshifters or Kindred or mages, just happened to be born into their power or fell into it. The Wan Kuei earned it when they pulled themselves out of hell through their own personal efforts and that forms the basis of their culture.

                                                    That said, there are certain geographical elements - the Wan Xian were commanded to watch over Asia, only people who go to the Ten Thousand Hells can become Wan Kuei, etc. But the culture of the Wan Kuei is not the culture of mortals. Whether one of the Hungry Dead was born in China or India or Japan or Southeast Asia or even in America is much less important than the fundamental fact of being one of the Wan Kuei. Where your spent your mortal years growing up is not very important except to a subset of bigoted individuals.

                                                            Off topic, but the X-men analogy was always kind of awkward to me. Like, some mutants are walking nuclear bombs. It's understandable why someone would would be weary of a person whose power puts them and the entire neighborhood at risk living next door. And it's understandable why the government might want to make a list to track such individuals and their powers, especially when you have a subset of those people regularly carrying out criminal activity, mass murder, assassination, terrorism, acts of war. etc. Whether I agree or not, I can see the reasoning behind it.

                                                            Meanwhile in real life, there's no reasonable reason to not want a member of a minority or a member of the LGBTQ+ community living next door. Likewise, there's no reasonable argument for the government tracking such people.

                                                            While there was an effort to make the X-men and mutants stand-ins for Civil Rights, I don't think that analogy really holds up once you start examining it. There's no reason to be worried about some random person that's gay. There is a reason to be worried about some random person who can blow up your city by thinking about it.
