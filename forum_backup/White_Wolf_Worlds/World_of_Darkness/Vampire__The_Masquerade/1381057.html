    V5 isn't actually "True". It's not "real", and that's why it isn't for everybody.

    04-13-2020, 03:16 PM
    From a purely literary standpoint, V5 is very different from the past four editions. It doesn't actually reflect reality. It isn't a mirror of our world exaggerated by malignant supernatural forces, it's a whacky alternate history brought about by wishful thinkers. And as such it doesn't ring true, it doesn't resonate with everyone, because it no longer represents something real to them.

    Let me shamelessly rip this little bit off wikipedia about Hermetic theory.

    "That which is Below corresponds to that which is Above, and that which is Above corresponds to that which is Below, to accomplish the miracle of the One Thing." Thus, whatever happens on any level of reality (physical, emotional, or mental) also happens on every other level.

    This principle, however, is more often used in the sense of the microcosm and the macrocosm. The microcosm is oneself, and the macrocosm is the universe. The macrocosm is as the microcosm and vice versa; within each lies the other, and through understanding one (usually the microcosm) a person may understand the other.

    If we're to apply this to Vampire (and we really should, because the World of Darkness was once seeped in hermetic theory. Just look at mage) it's clear to see: The core vampire clans (and to a good extent the other large clans) each primarily represent an archtype of someone in society. The Ventrue are the Old Money, the Toreador represent the liberal elite, The Gangrel hold the country life... and so on. The Vampires are an intense microcosm for which to explore the Macrocosm (Society including non-vampires). One of them is above, the other is bellow. Older vampires reign over younger vampires, younger vampires reign over ghouls and mortal servants; CEOs rule over managers, Managers rule over employees. Methuselahs control princes, princes control kindred. The super wealthy rule over politics, politicians rule over people... I don't think I need to more into this.

    V5, alone among vampire editions, breaks this rule.
    V5 presents us a scenario where most of the elders have disapeared, The camarilla has ceded territory to the anarchs, and intelligence organizations are moving against vampires en masse.
    For this to work, in reality we'd need the world's multi-millionaires to leave (Bunkers? Space?) and stop using their power, Radical political ideologies have to gain ground (not just support, actual ground) and intellegence organizations need to start persecuting the elites that prop them up.

    Yes, as you can guess, none of this is happening. Maybe young people are leaning a lot more left than they used to, and old people are leaning more right (Note: Even young vampires are old), but that hasn't actually manifested itself in a way that would count. People being angry at rich and aware of wealth ineqauality doesn't mean that wealth inequality has gone away or that the system is changing for the better. That's just wishful thinking. The ruling classes aren't actually getting persecuted; beyond some flack on twitter and some fine memes, there's no real analogue to the way vampires are being hunted in V5.

    V5 gives us an absent group of super-elites, an upper-middle class persecuted by goverment organizations that are looking out for the little people. What part of this reflects todays reality? It doesn't. Which is why for a lot of people, V5 can't possible resonate with them. It's a false image that doesn't ring true. V5 has broken the fundamental laws of satire and the universe; V5 doesn't reflect our reality, it's optimistic wish fulfillment. If the fantasy specifically appeals to you, you may fall for it's comfort, but a joke is a lot funnier if it's got a shred of relatable truth to it.
