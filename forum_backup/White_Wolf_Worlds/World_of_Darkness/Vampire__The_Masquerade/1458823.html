                              Still broad. Put, lets say, a limit on Discipline dots.

                              If there is no limit on choices, so we don't have to give up something to get another thing, except the obvious, it boils down to this:

                              Generation and age - the lowest possible will always be the best option, there's no sense in asking. Low gen = more power without any drawback, because before V5 every drawback came from age, not Generation.

                              Clans - the only thing that makes them different in potential power is their list of in-clans. If the premise doesn't limit the character in any way about Disciplines, clans make no difference. It makes sense to ask which would be the strongest clan, but the strongest character possible is pretty much clan-agnostic.

                              Disciplines - they can be worse or better, but nothing tops having them all. Without some parameter to define this, that's the only reasonable answer: have them all.

                              That's why people are discussing already existing NPCs instead of suggesting a build, because framed as it was it is a nonsensical question.

                                              Originally posted by Shadeprowler View Post
                                              It took an Antediluvian to kill Samiel
                                              The Antediluvians have killed lots of people. I think what's more impressive is the fact Samiel killed an Antediluvian.

                                              Granted, he wasn't able to kill an Antediluvian permanently, but it's impressive for what it was.

                                              I think feats like Samiel's also make for a more interesting setting than "the Antediluvians always win against their lessers." That's not how it works with elders and ancillae, so I like that we have canon examples of that dynamic still being the case with Antediluvians and methuselahs. Some methuselahs (like Samiel) are probably comparable or superior warriors than Antediluvians, but smart planning and the right powers can render that skill advantage moot. Samiel might have killed Tzimisce, but he still died and Tzimisce was reborn.

                                              I never liked the idea put forward in The Book of Nod that each Antediluvian is an unstoppable blood god who even an army of methuselahs can't defeat. I mean, to your average vampire off the street, sure, they seem unstoppable, but Revised also gave us three Kue-jin methuselahs going toe to toe with Zapathasura. I liked that, and I liked the Technocracy being able to kill him permanently. One Antediluvian gave the World of Darkness more than enough trouble before he was put down. Gehenna is still scary because twelve (or more!) Antediluvians all rising at once is exponentially harder for the world to deal with.

                                              Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.
