            Originally posted by Manfr View Post


            RPG companies at the end are still companies, and have to make money. The IP acquisition was motivated of course by the idea that they would make a "Cyberpunk 2077" move with Bloodlines 2, but the Paradox CEO of the time was also a long-time gamer of WOD, and personally invested in the setting.
            ​


            The treadmill of the 90s too could be called a money grab, and it gave us a very deep setting. It wasn't soulless.

            The Wod20 editions could be called a money grab based on nostalgia as well. But they were works of love, gave us updated setting and rules, and restarted the classic Wod. They weren't soulless.

            ​
            As for the gamer argument ... Martin Ericsson is a gamer, Jason Carl is a gamer, Outstar is a gamer, they all are gamers. It is also frequently forgotten that Matthew Dawkins and OPP freelancers worked a lot on V5, and that Matthew was Creative Lead Designer for a while and its basically firsty brand ambassador, as the Gentleman Gamer.

            And yet, Wod5e took their risks in changing rules and metaplot. Had it been soulless, it would have looked much different and more conservative. Wod5e was realized by gamers and long time fans, helped by experienced game designers and historical authors and freelancers. Had it been a soulless money grab showing everything down the troath of old time fans, they wouldn't have started the Storytellers Vault.

            That said, editorially speaking, Wod5e is not a disaster: it has re-entered icv2 charts various times, coming in the top5, and it has wide international distribution agreements. It's also doing fairly well on Drivethrurpg. Kickstarter by Onyx Paths realized amazing products, whose reprint as physical editions has powerfully propelled Wod5e in the icv2 charts again, last Fall: but had not been for Paradox deals and Renegade distribution, they would just have stayed wonderful books acquired by ... 1000 or so Kickstarter backers, and later on an equivalent number of people on Drivethrurpg.

            To get my italian V20 distribution back again, another publishing house had to publish V5 ... and this propelled back interest in the franchise and moved the other publisher to print more V20 books.

            Nowadays, the WOD is widely available in a huge number of countries, languages and publishing formats, and also supports extensive fan modding. This, by itself, it's awesome.

            Unfortunately, Wod5e has failed in many of its original ambitions (uniting fandom, having a Cyberpunk 2077 moment, having a Dnd 5e big recognition...), and even though commercially speaking is doing well, it's just a sidenote on Paradox balance sheets.

            This, I think, is sad, missed chance: for a brief moment we had important designers, committed fans, long-time stewards of the franchise, and an international distribution network with huge transmedia ambitions working together to bring the Wod to its formery glory. It didn't work as intended.
            And I think the bottom-line of all of that what you described here is: WOD5E has failed.

            The main-reason for that is that saying "It didn't work as intended." is one thing but what happens in response is another. Sure, shit happens, WOD5E had its shit-show moments - so how do Paradox/Renegade plan to turn things around? One move was establishing that in-house-team and prioritizing Renegade for supplements. Seemed like a good idea back then. But Justin Achilli, the designated lead-developer, has left and Renegade's ability as a distributor has hit a snafu leading to delays (which could just be a temporary thing - but what if it isn't...?). And there's no strategy here beyond "Just trust us." right now. Can Renegade truly keep up with the publishing-schedule Paradox expects of them? Who is even in charge of developing the next corebook in WOD5E?

            One of the main-reasons I think WOD5E has failed is that there was a great moment with the release of V5 to actually make WOD5E a big thing and then that opportunity got squandered. Sure, that wasn't great but you can turn things around - and yet the history of WOD5E so far only becomes noticeable in the public eye when it adds yet another piece of bad writing to its seemingly ever-burning pyre of shame. It really has only gone downhill from there. People care about the V5 Corebook - but that's about it. I doubt it was mere greed that moved Renegade to give WOD5E books that hefty price-tag. It isn't Renegade's fault that shipping-prices are where they are but is Renegade actually still able to be the kind of distributor it used to be? I guess, we'll see just how bad things are with Renegade and if they managed to recover. As per the plans that were presented at the start of the year, Renegade is behind schedule. They claim their current problems will get resolved and therefore at the start of the coming year physical releases of all sorts will happen at the same time. Don't think that's a good idea but here we are. I guess we're gonna see when the first quartal of 2024 comes around. Theoretically, this will see the physical release of five WOD5E supplements in Q1 2024 according to the current schedule. Together with the hefty Renegade-price-tag, this doesn't sound like a good strategy.

                                                        And that's what I'm saying, Leandro16 . Is unfair this mash up. And that's a pretty common complain, they took Requiem mechanics and used in a bizarre way in V5. They work in Requiem because they're well thought and made to work and work in the scenario. V5 tried to take that and failed miserably because there are two different systems with two different premises. The paper a Methuselah is supposed to do in the Masquerade and the paper a high blood potency vampire is supposed to do in the Requiem are different. On the Masquerade, the low generation are an unavoidable threat, dealed differently between the highly hierarchical Camarilla and the way more "caothic" Sabbath. On the Requiem the threat is different, because you can voluntarily go in a torpor to drop your blood potency. You can't go from a lower to a higher generation because that would break the system. The fear of Gehenna is a vital part of the Masquerade. The fear of a 2k yo on Requiem is just not the same. It hit differently because he can be recently awakened of a volunteer torpor to be functional again. The blood thins with torpors, which is not supposed to happen in the Masquerade. Caine is a sword in the neck of every single vampire in Masquerade. There ain't a First Vampire on Requiem, despite of what the Lance of Longuinius and the Circle of the Crone may say. A vampire that knew the Christ that awakens in the Masquerade and the Requiem hit differently, because in the Masquerade is a blood hungry demigod that can wipe NY with a thought while in the Requiem may be a very cultivated low potency guy that can be lying through their fangs with bizarre memories that may or may not be real. The awakening of Ur-Shulgi remodeled the Banu Hakim formerly known as Assamites. The awakening of a vampire of same age that spent as long in torpor in the Requiem is just not the same. There are different games, which must be played differently, and throwing things as blood potency in the Masquerade is both ridiculous and counterproductive. Someone as Montano can't happen in the Requiem with very good reasons, because the scenario don't allow. A Gratiano in the Requiem wouldn't have the power to absolutely change, let's say, the Mekhet, because a Gratiano just can't happen in the Requiem. As I said, they're both good games with different premises, and trying to put mechanics of one in the other (blood potency, by chance) is not functional. That's the gist of what I'm trying to say.
