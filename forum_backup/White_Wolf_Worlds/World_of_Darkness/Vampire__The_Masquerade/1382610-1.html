                                        Here is my theory/headcanon on the origins of the Baali:

                                        "In the ancient nights, thousands and thousands of years ago Saulot the Kind went east in search of wisdom. So long ago was this that most of the line of Caine still dwelt in a single city. And all those millennia ago, if the Antediluvians had no human hearts, they were still closer to them than today. Especially Saulot, who strove to retain as much of a heart as he could.

                                        Saulot walked as kine do, for it was a pilgrimage. For many nights he walked through dry, empty lands where men were scarce or altogether absent. But one night he heard a song, the most beautiful he ever head. And Saulot followed the song through the sand and the rock and he came to a city. Green and fertile and strewn with lights it was, and Saulot thought it beautiful. And for a while he tarried there and sought the singer whose song he had followed. Time and perhaps other things have obscured the name of the place. Some say it was called Ashur, some say Ubar of the Pit.

                                        But Saulot found the singer. Rayzeel, a slave in that city. And Saulot fell in love and stayed for long years in that place. Freedom and much favor came upon Rayzeel as the consort of the Antediluvian. Saulot gave wealth, and comfort, magic and the wisdom of ages. But Saulot withheld from his consort one thing: Immortality and the blood of Caine. For he saw its immortality through murder as a curse, not a gift. In the end, Saulot continued his pilgrimage east, promising to return when his quest was complete.

                                        And Rayzeel wept, still mortal and considering that betrayal.

                                        In the end, Saulot completed his quest. He gained great wisdom, and entered into the state of Golconda. And he returned to that city, for the Antediluvian truly did love Rayzeel the Singer, beyond all other beings. But this time he did not hear song as he approached, he could follow the sound of screams. And he saw Rayzeel standing above the pit, and he saw what his love had done with the knowledge he had given. For Rayzeel had seen immortality up close, touched it, and knew that it was possible through blood and murder. And so all the power and favor that was given Rayzeel had been turned to the task. The first organ-pit grew in the heart of the city, and Rayzeel stood above it, crimson with blood and making a chorus of the victims screams.

                                        Saulot saw his love so, and Saulot fell.

                                        First in Golconda, he time in that state was the shortest of all who have held it. In frenzy he tore through the people, victims and perpetrators alike. Their broken, twisted and exsanguinated bodies he threw into the pit, and Rayzeel he left for last. And even that last plea did not move him "I did it for you. To live until you returned" His love went last into the pit.

                                        On the edge of the pit Saulot awoke from frenzy and realized all he had lost. Love, wisdom and golconda were all dust. And the Antediluvian fell to his knees and wept like rain. As he knelt on the brink, he sobbed forth words that have echoed down the ages to follow the kindred until they have been mistaken for Caines own words:

                                        "Do not embrace love"

                                        And as he spoke, his bloody tears ran down into the pit, and perhaps he knew. And perhaps he didn't. For the greatest of minds can be deceived by its heart, and Saulot had labored long at retaining his heart."
