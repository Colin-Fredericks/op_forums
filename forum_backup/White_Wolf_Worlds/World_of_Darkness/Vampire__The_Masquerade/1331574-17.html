    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    I agree that both the sect problem and conflict "solution" apply to any city and both sides. In fact, I think a "modern truce" between the sects out of necessity would be the ideal situation, rather than this Warcraft scenario with the Camarilla Alliance and Sabat Horde eternally at full, "kill on site" war.

    As you said, something more Requiem style.

    Not because the plot isn't good, but because the one-note war is bad for setting building and play. It limits options for both ST and players with little payoff unless you want this specific plot.

    My ideal city has both presences to some degree and a de facto truce in some form, with a border region that's either mixed or empty, but work as a buffer.

    From there characters do their shenanigans, so you can have people on both sides trying to get the situation hotter or colder for their own reasons.

    Only LARP I played on was kind like that and I had a lot of fun.
    Ages ago, I had the idea that if Lucita became Regent, she would facilitate just that. Called the Madrid Principle, it would allow Camarilla Kindred to coexist in Sabbat cities and maintain their own rules and Masquerade, so long as they didn't bother the Sabbat. The Sabbat were likewise told to leave the Camarilla alone.

    The Sabbat would maintain its own Silence of the Blood, with a Bishop of Silence for most major cities. The Bishop of Silence and probably the Sheriff would collaborate on keeping the peace across the city. The Rule of Silence would trump other concerns—the Camarilla can't complain about how the Sabbat keeps things under wraps, so long as it does. Elysium and the Sabbat temple/communal haven would both be treated as sacrosanct with no entry without permission from the relevant side.

    Coteries can petition the Archbishop for formal recognition of domain in Sabbat territories, on the same basis as founded packs, on the understanding that a Sabbat pack gets an equivalent parcel of turf in a Camarilla domain. Usually, though, the Camarilla would just have its own enclave in a Sabbat city (and vice versa). I didn't really think about split cities, Berlin-wise, but I think that could work.

    I can see both sides embracing an uneasy truce worldwide. Especially given the rise of surveillance culture, making it easier for mortals to hunt them (but I'd put this down to the Technocracy more than the Second Inquisition). There might be sporadic acts of terrorism and maybe even guerilla warfare in remote areas when things boil over, but most of the time, each side would want to just get on with nightly existence. I suspect more vampires would turn independent in such a situation, since they no longer need to use one of the sects for protection or vengeance against the other.

    Per BJD, Lucita is supposed to be mostly Status Quo with a few concessions to the Loyalists, via Vasantasena, and greater support for the Sabbat Inquisition, via her "sister" Maria Sandoza. She favours preventing Gehenna at any cost rather than a protracted Gehenna Crusade (as in V5). I figured the reason for this is that she would realise that slaying the Antediluvians would result in another Week of Nightmares happening—up to a dozen times, in fact, depending on how many Ancients are actually dead already—and she would therefore seek other ways to stem the tide.

    In practice, this would be business as usual, since the sect hasn't attempted to slay any Antediluvians in 600 years, but the growing Crusader faction wouldn't be happy. To prevent a civil war, you'd need to either aim them at something or lop off their head (Polonia in this case). My vote's on the latter. There'd be no peace otherwise.

        I thought of something similar, but with some differences based on my reading of the sects, because I really like the idea they're not just clubs with memberships, or formal institutions, but rather strong philosophies arranged around loose alliances and a claim over all vampires (and yes, that includes Kuei-Jin, some Fomors, Ananasi, anything, though those are unknown).

        The Camarilla isn't a group that enforces the Traditions. It is the idea of the Traditions. The city is Camarilla if it enforces the Traditions in some formal way, has Elysia and a top-down government.

        But I really like how for a time in Bloodlines LaCroix pretends to have absolute power while he actually knows how far he can push, and the Barons know they have power, but he's danger enough that they have to give some credit. Part theater, part reality, part the Little Prince's King.

        So in a Camarilla city, the Prince or Primogen (I don't use both in a city) claims domain over all vampires and establishes the Traditions as the ultimate law, but lives with the practical limits of power, probably bargaining with other power brokers.

        A Sabat city is one with a temple to the Church of the Sword of Caine and a Bishop. The same teachings of always are told, including rallying against the Traditions. Not against intelligence, though. If you're caught by the Sheriff being an idiot that's not anyone else's problem.

        Followers of the Church are pretty much the Sabbat we know, but "tamed" to live in society (tamed as in capable of doing so, neither just punk Cam, nor juvenile, unsustainable idiotic violence. Be a monster, but a smart monster. So tamed in comparison to some depictions). They don't like the Traditions, but neither is the Church requiring they tear them down openly, nor the Prince cares about crimes as long as they don't bring problems.

        It's a church and a state. The difference to the Invictus/L&S relationship is that they're at odds openly, even if not at war. The Bishop has a lot of political power, but won't ever claim secular command. The Prince enacts and enforces law but never claimed authority over spirituality.

        As for the other Kindred, they're whatever they want. The Prince requires obedience to law or hierarchy (curiously not both), the Bishop want you to believe and participate in mass and the ritae. Both keep tabs of likely loyalties, but as long as you're ok with one and not a problem to the other, you're perfectly safe. They don't want you to stay so neutral, do they?

        While this arrangement could be a new version of the setting, I do imagine this being the result of the last 20 years. They have areas of greater influence in the city, and sone very well defined domains, but the limits are fuzzy and unofficial, both draw territorial claim ignoring the other.

        Lucita I agree, would try to prevent Gehenna in its entirety as a pragmatic compromise. It may be the whole reason she's there. It's apparently working, but through continuous effort. Becket thinks it'll be a complete success since the end of time, since there will be no Gehenna anyway, but whatever floats your boat.

        Polonia may die to ease things, but the best thing from a global strategy standpoint to do with the Crusaders is to let them find targets. The Antes are such a vague goal that the most eager Crusaders are manipulated around to do war on whatever, while the smarter ones grow more frustrated with their own lack of clues than with the Church. Lucita won't ever have to deny them their quarry if they never find it.

        Finally, I'd shake up some other factions the game always implied they could have diverse membership, but just ignored:

        Giovanni - even before the Hecata, the Family is a family. Their mortal blood has always been of utmost importance to the Giovanni. But then, what happens when a Giovanni embraces out of family without consent? And when a family member is Embraced by another clan? I say the only answer is family. An outsider they don't accept will be forfeited the right to exist, flee or die, a Giovanni in Curse only. A family member will be rescued, and if they're still loyal to the family as they usually are, that's that, though it is unlikely that they'll get the right to Embrace.

        Ministry - the Followers of Set have been for so long dedicated to corrupt minds and hearts to the teachings of Set. They seem to be the most disastrously incompetent missionaries in the history of religion, since they never appear with an actual convert. They're one of the greatest rivals to the Sabbat, and have many Vampires of every clan among them. Those of Setite line also appear here and there fleeing after falling out with the religion, and while some are spies, some are not.

        Tremere - while rare, sorcerers of other clans are accepted if they show enough promise as students and loyal followers, while students of the blood that don't make the cut, are cut. The odd Sorcerer who truly escape is under Blood Hunt from their POV, and while Giovanni and Setites may have some leniency with those who accept their dregs, the Tremere are willing to declare war on a whole Domain for the crime of housing a liability.

        Banu Haqim complete the set. Their influence is more felt through their weight in the Ashirra, but individuals that show interest and really do believe in their cause are sometimes accepted as Residents of Alamut. Which is just a title as only the Web of Crazy Fuckers reside there now and all. The Sorcerers run a deep screen on the individual, then the Viziers and they're assigned a caste in the end. Those of the Blood who are not found sufficiently devoted after Embrace are also screened. If they're found at least just as good as other acceptable Vampires, they're graciously let go, though never with secrets of the clan in their minds.

        Case by case, similar situations should repeat all around: not everyone in a Gangrel gathering is a Gangrel by blood, not every Warren resident look like Max Schreck in expressionist makeup, and so on. Those aren't common, but they highlight the distinction between bloodline and culture, how far clans will go to hide their secrets by contrasting defectors, and how far they'll go to get their hands in such secrets by accepting those defectors.

                Originally posted by adambeyoncelowe View Post
                How do you imagine the development of this model to arise? Did it require a treaty originally, or did it evolve naturally? I can totally see the state/church duality of the Camarilla/Sabbat, though it would take some shrewd politics to get there. The impending threat of hunters obviously helps.

                If the Crusaders went off to fight a war, the remaining factions would be more exposed to Camarilla takeover, so I can see scholarly packs like the Shepherds of Caine being clever enough to strike an accord over fighting to the death or getting involved in sieges. Most of those Cainites are more interested in their spiritual explorations than fighting the Antediluvians anyway.
                I imagine it developing naturally. Gradually but fast in the sense of being "just" 20 years.

                A lot of cross-sect contacts already exist in the lore. That would increase during the years after the last news of Revised. The Crusaders have a lot in their hands with the Red Star and all that stuff. They can't waste time and resources with the Camarilla.

                Lucita is not a Cam hater. She welcomes any opportunity to focus on actual problems. She's smart enough to find the "traitorous contacts" and to shrug it off and see what they bring of useful. At the highest levels both sects find a common cause in pragmatic survival.

                No higher institution reinforces the Cam war policy anymore. Packs are indeed independent, and without this direction they lose interest at first and explore contact after. Princes are still not happy with the idea of close contact with anti-elder extremists, but under pressure of the same problems, mystical and mundane, they find something they like: Sabbat mercenaries.

                Eventually things calm down in the Signs of The End front. Not completely, though, and the Crusaders still have a hot track to follow. But enough. Hunters are getting more dangerous. If the developments of the early 2000s greatly increase the power of institutional hunters, at least some, the technologies of the 2010s start to give any hunter more access to information.

                Semiofficial accords start to happen. It isn't a big event, but several small arrangements here and there. First between minor packs and low hierarchy Guardians of Elysium, but sooner or later the higher ups see that they can't resist the tide. Most don't have any big reason to want to.

                The sects are also "stabilizing" in institutional nature with the leadership more focused on the big threats than night-to-night micromanaging. Princes and Bishops just want to take care of their interests, their personal turf and political style. As individuals their power grows, while the sects are more and more ideas rather than global hierarchies. The higher ups don't care of the important resources keep coming, and they do,more actually.

                When the big truces are openly signed between parties all over the world, the leaderships don't pretend to be responsible for this anymore. Its not a big global truce, but hundreds of regional or big cities truces. They're not equal, and here and there some open conflicts remain, but they are indeed more local grudges than anything.

                This is not a more tranquil world, just a less top-down sect structure. Leadership is strong, enough to not care. Don't cause problems, keep important resources flowing, and when they do come and ask something, you obey. As far as you can say, they're powerful enough to stop a primordial prophecy in its tracks and hold.

                Elders focus less on distractions forced upon them and more on their actual ambitions and rivalries. On many places, Princes and Bishops find out that bickering through influence rather than destruction makes for more wealth, of all kinds, reaching them.

                Anarchs are more fucked than ever. No more Anarch Free States, not because they fell, but because they had to organize for real to resist. They're proper attempts at administration, but not Cam enemies. To be an Anarch is again to be the rebel in your city trying to change what you think is wrong, now against both legal and spiritual oppression.
