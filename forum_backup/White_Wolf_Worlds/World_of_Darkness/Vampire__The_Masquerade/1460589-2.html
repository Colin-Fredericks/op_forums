                            Originally posted by Elphilm View Post
                            I'm beginning to think that the conflation of the Princes with the Camarilla, and the resulting redefinition of the Camarilla, has been one of the most pernicious influences on Vampire: The Masquerade.
                            I'm very sympathetic to that point. Many people conflate several things when speaking about the Camarilla. Sometimes it is perfectly fine to do so. At other times it leads people to trouble.

                            The first thing is the Camarilla as the overall government of the undead. This is limited to only a few things. The Inner Circle, Justicars, Archons, and the system of Conclaves (which are explicitly called by Justicars). When Princes complain about the Camarilla, they are talking about this. It's the incipient super-government that is interfering with their ability to rule their domains. This is the "debating chamber" (perhaps referring to both the deliberations of the Inner Circle and the Conclaves).

                            The second thing is to conflate it with the Princes ruling their domains. However, that is not an innovation or unique concept to the Camarilla as Elphilim says. It is almost hardwired into vampire society. Princes existed before the Camarilla. There are Princes who do not accept the authority of the Camarilla at all (like Siegfried in Vancouver). And Sabbat Cardinals/Archbishops are basically the same thing as a Prince just with another title. As are Anarch Barons (or any other title a vampire uses who rules in an anarch domain). But because Princes are the immediate and obvious authority figure, they get identified with the Camarilla. But the position by itself isn't Camarilla. Only if a Prince recognizes the authority of the Inner Circle in some degree is that Prince a member of the Camarilla. But that can be said about anyone.

                            The last thing builds on the above. Anyone who acknowledges the authority of the Inner Circle is part of the Camarilla. But there is a huge difference between someone who is merely a citizen or resident (your average vampire) and the actual government (the Inner Circle and its servants). Your typical vampires aren't a kind of civil servants or the militia of the Camarilla. They're just vampires. And this is why "Anarchs" were still considered to be part of the Camarilla. If they were just struggles with the local Prince, the actual Camarilla didn't care. Those that weren't were considered to be actual criminals or fugitives. The Free States were a threat because they explicitly denied the Inner Circle, not most Anarchs who were usually just inexperienced or local dissidents unhappy with the Prince who hadn't yet learned the right way of playing the game.

                            The issue is that for many people the game is about sect conflicts. Regardless of whether it is Camarilla versus Anarchs (or usually Princes versus Anarchs) or Camarilla versus Sabbat, they want the game to be about conflict between groups. Because most players are heavily oriented to combat, they gravitate to sect wars because sect wars gives that to them.

                            However, the game does not need to be about sect wars at all. I know many people originally attracted to the game because they wanted to explore all the other cool stuff inherent in a pseudo-Anne Rice style vampire setting. Combat had its role in these games, but the games weren't heavily into the violence. They were about personal horror, or exploring mysteries, dark romance, and many other things. They weren't soldiers in an army.

                            But the moment you turn everything into a sect war, then the ordinary vampires of the Camarilla have to get conscripted into an army and fight the other vampires in the minds of players. This is what leads them to conclusions like "the Anarchs are just being used as cannon fodder for the Camarilla!" or "the Brujah and Gangrel must leave because they're only used as cannon fodder for the other clans!" But neither of those conclusions actually make sense.

                            If Anarchs fight the Sabbat, it's not because the Inner Circle of the Camarilla told them to do so. It's because they are fighting for their own domains against Sabbat intruders. And the Brujah and Gangrel aren't cannon fodder more than any other clan. This is an example of OOC thinking taking over. Because we, the players, see the in-clan Discipline sets of the Brujah and Gangrel as being better combat oriented, and players who like to fight therefore gravitate to those clans, we see them as being more martial than the others, and therefore the frontline fighters. But that doesn't make sense from an IC perspective. Going back to Chicago By Night, how many Brujah could really be said to be warriors? Gengis might. But Hank Cave? He's a beatnik poet. Dooley? He's actually a con artist who joined the Black Panthers merely to get some street cred that attracted a certain type of white liberal to give him money. Anita Wainwright? She's a student protester. Blackjack is a union organizer. None of these people are actual fighters. Many of them didn't even have a Discipline set on their character sheet that would make them so. Yeah, if in danger they can fight - but so would Alan Sovereign the Ventrue financier, Rosa Hernandez the animal rights activist, any of the band members of Baby Chorus, and others. Certainly the Wolfpack would be warriors. But that is because they are Archons. Being Gangrel is secondary to that. Lodin, on the other hand, despite being Ventrue has lots of combat Disciplines and originally lead his coterie into killing his enemies during the Great Chicago Fire. In most games that I've been in, it's often the player of a Nosferatu who plays the heavy of the PC group. In another group it was the Ventrue Pinkerton Detective who was the most capable combat character.

                            After all, things like "that costs more XP than this, so it is not ideal from a character building perspective" is inherently an OOC perspective. People don't think like that in real life.

                            So who would be good at combat and actually do the heavy lifting for the Camarilla in the sect wars has nothing to do with clans. It has to do with their character concepts. At best, certain clans have higher percentage of those types than others for a variety of reasons. But that's very different than saying "Because we're in a sect war, everyone is conscripted and we're going to send those clans whose in-clan Disciplines make better fighters from a character creation perspective so you can make better use of that XP."

                            Originally posted by Elphilm View Post
                            I have never read a single paragraph that has convinced me that I should be using the Revised or V5 redefinitions of the Camarilla instead.
                            This is one reason among many that metaplot is a bad thing. It's one thing for one ST to take the game into the direction of constant sect wars. That can be fun. But it leaves the option for other STs to do other things. Metaplots though hijack the setting and divides fans because it essentially tells them, "No, the way you want to run your game is wrong."
