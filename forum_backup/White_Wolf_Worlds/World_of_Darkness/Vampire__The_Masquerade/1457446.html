          Read the Lore of the Clans history section for Toreador as the main basis. Starting with the Greek section through Constantinople, every place mentioned is associated with the Baali, and all of the mythological references connect to Lilith, Bulls, Ba'al, etc.. The Carthage/Rome section ends with “And of course we were the bad guys for bailing." This seemed like a wink about them being Baali. The initial section also talks about her getting into trouble with a love affair and having to flee. It mentions earlier that she had a twin and notes the lover was "as beautiful as she was," which seems like a clever way of indicating it was her twin. The whole forbidden love theme then gets repeated throughout the mythologies and stories of the following sections, particularly Crerte and Carthage.

          Petrichore is the opening word to the section, which refers to ichor (divine blood) and stones. One of the Greek myths it goes through involves a woman weeping for the loss of her children and turning to stone, which reads a lot like Zillah. Now the timing is off, but the whole book and in particular this section mentions that dates, names, and facts change, but they tell a greater truth. If you think about the stories being told as both working chronologically, but also giving you different aspects of a single story (through the way history repeats itself), you see basically a single repeating story that talks about the Second Generation, the founding of the Baali, the forbidden love presumably between Arikel and Malkav, her twin.

          Now if you read the other histories and look for the Clans and Progenitors mentioned by other ones, you'll see certain things repeating and connecting. Toreador, Nosferatu, Malkavian, Tzimisce, and Gangrel have interesting connections to each other. There's a lot of talk relating to 'splitting' and 'halves' relating to these Clans, including the Toreador section Tzimisce tearing someone in half. The Nosferatu section talks about ways to divide things that you might not expect, not identical halves. Abismiliard is mentioned strangely, and there's an implication that Nosferatu and Malkavian Antediluvian were once and split in half, hence the physical/mental deformation.

          In general the Toreador Antediluvian gets associated with Ishtar, Inanna, etc.. who often get later associations with Lilith, and this seems to be the unsaid name in all of this. I'd have to read again to be sure if it's saying Arikel is Lilith, or just a daughter of her. If you look at the Clans mentioned and the 'splitting' idea and mixed-gender role of the Baali (corruption of twins, but also the Heiros Gamos or Sacred Marriage) as well as the idea of Lilith as 'The Mother of Monsters' these would be the exact Clans you would pick ut.

          Also, 'Shaper' for Antediluvian is regarded as a title, just like Ashur. The city of 'Mashkan-Shapir' is one of the early cities in the fertile valley (note, the numbering here tends to connect with the Flood and the Generations), and it's a city associated with Nergal.

          Gangrel's section talks about Ennoia which is another name for Enoch. It says some interesting things about 'Mother' and Nosferatu being 'kin' of a certain kind. It also points out that they're not even claiming that Ennoia was the name of the Antediluvian, but that this is the name of the City associated with the Antediluvian. Note that the early cities are also noted as having a connection to Wells of Sacrifice.

          I haven't taken the time to build a full Mind Map here. There's parts I'm surely missing. There's also parts that are going to seem to be contradictory, in part because the narrators are unreliable. At times they are intentionally lying to you, and the book's intro makes this clear. I've got about 50 pages of notes, and that's just on the history section of 7 of the Clans (need to finish the rest.) But you definitely need to read between the lines and figure out the various literary, mythological, and historical references being made as well. Try to figure out what you're being told in each section, and ask yourself 'is the surface reading of this telling me anything of value at all? Is it the type of thing that an editor would X out if there wasn't something deeper being said?'

          An example of the kind of thing you'll find: The Toreador section talks about how Clans influence Human affairs less than they might have you believe. It says it's easy to influence /a/ Mortal, but a whole society? That would just be difficult. A few paragraphs later, it tells you about a single Mortal that was hugely important (relating to Christianizing Rome.) So yeah, the Toreador are saying 'oh, we don't do anything, nor could we' and then paragraphs later they're telling you exactly what they did and how they did it. You just need to do a close reading, and I recommend reading the intro of the book to get an explanation of how you might want to read it.

          The introductions to each of the Clan sections have a particular feel to how they are written as well. Look at the character traits being mentioned and what you're being told. You'll basically end up essentially with a bullet point list of Clan traits, and it seems like each of these might have been written with basically the form of taking such a bullet point list and turning it into narrative.

          So yeah, got a ton more, but start there, give your thoughts, ask questions.

              Here's my notes on the first 3 Chapters of the Toreador history section, to give you an idea of how I'm reading it. These were made as I went, I'm sure I'd have more once I double back after reading the whole thing.
              • History is the past, not tonight, out of style
              • Knowing rules to break them
              • Dramatic elements of history, act of storytelling, embellishments and making it about them
              • Founders name a debate of Noddist scholars, mentions Arikel as a possibility, along with the names of Ancient Near Eastern variations of a Goddess: Inanna, Ishtar, Astarte, etc..
              • Founder usually a sculptor, sometimes a bull-dancer (Bull of Heaven), even a man at times
              • Seduction of Enoch, seduce a Vampire, win the embrace
              • Romantic stories
              • “Atlantis of the Sands” the legendary city of Ubar is where this tale is set
              • Existing in harmony with Humanity
              • Pair of twins, arts, philosophy, prophecy
              • Sculpts Enoch’s image in a way so that it is mistaken for living by his own sister; which is interesting with with weeping stone and such
              • Referred to the Twins again as angels, which goes with a lot of the Book of Enoch
              • Twins are Arikel and Malkav, but apparently Malkav is not the real name, that has been forgotten to time
              • Art and Madness are related
              • Specifically mentions the creation of the 13 of the 3rd Generation, but mentions no one know all of their names, plus why does it matter?
              • 16 Cainites (2nd and 3rd Generation) with their Dark Father listed separately, which can easily imply other meanings.
              • Despite mentioning being Romantic and making embellishments, at least here it’s sticking to pretty orthodox numbers and detailing facts, which is unusual in these. Telling a story has advantages, and it might be that those come from Toreador tales
              • Arikel falls in love with Abismilliard, who’s noted as being beautiful (Nosferatu, not yet cursed)
              • Caine blames self for the deluge, seems to love humanity, but locks himself away from it as a punishment or protection
              • 12 of the 13 ask to embrace, but are denied the right
              • The 12 rise up and slay their second generation sires
              • Only Arikel is not among them, innocent, uncursed by Caine
              • This is referencing the Bull of Heaven, Inanna, and Gilgamesh
              • Arikel is told by Caine to flee North to escape the wrath of the other Cainites
              • Her Curse is actually spoken as a Blessing by Caine, that she will always love humanity and beauty


                Starting with the fourth paragraph is where we get heavy Inanna, Minotaur, Bull, Lillith, Baali references. But as you can see, I'm looking at what's being said, how it's being said, what's being referenced, and how it connects to the other Clan histories and how it difefres from them.
              • This is a reference to The Descent of Inanna; battling Gangrel and childer
              • This is a reference to The Minotaur; Minos does have other sons, but given the bull and sculpting references, this is almost certainly what is meant.
              • The Minotaur is a “Monument of their polluted love” is also a reference to Baal-Moloch
              • Malkav is pretty clearly Moloch; The Nosferatu section talks about a connection to Malkav and references splitting, one twisted physically and the other mentally; we’re also given the name Abismilliard as her lover, and note how when referenced above the narrator specifically mentions “a man as beautiful as she was” that is to say, her twin; Gangrel also references a connection to Nosferatu and speaks of them as kindred and references Enoch being a mother; Creating the original Baali spawning pit is probably a pretty good reason why Arikel fled North; her name also means ‘Engender Bushel’ basically a direct reference to the male-female Baali; it’s also a corrupted form the Heiros Gamos of which Inanna is directly associated; The names they’ve left out for this Antediluvian? Lamashtu, Lillitu, Lilith; so seems like the Toreador have a pretty interesting founding myth going on there that they’ve hidden within a story of Caine and have been very precise with the numbers
              • Reading another book, and really hoping that Arikel translation is just something unfortunate they overlooked, Black Hand book references Aretstikapha which can have the name Arakiel, so maybe an obfuscation of this; connects to Asakku / Vicissitude in-game, also the Toreador through Mikael; This connects a bit with other Vicissitude references; Name is drawing from Babylonian as opposed to Sumerian; ‘The Shaper’ is also likely reference to Mashkan-shapir
              • King Amphion that rebuffs of her affection is a reference to identical twins, name literally meaning ‘from two lands’; but more importantly, Niobe had 6 sons and 6 daughters, all of whom were killed when she boasted of her fertility to the Titan Leto who was only able to produce one set of twins, Apollo and Artemis; and here Niobe is listed as having 14 children, but that’s something that appears only in post-Homeric texts; Leto of course sounds a lot like Lillith, and though that’s not a common connection made by scholars, ‘the fruit that brings oblivion to those who eat it,’ ‘wife,’ and ‘hidden one’ are all attached with the name in the Greek, so seems like it to me; Niobe is also known as basically the original bereaved woman, and the killing of her children involves Zeus turning Thebans to stone, so yeah, that would be the weeping stone and would explain the Petrichore opening
              • We get told Amphion rebuffs, so Arikel embraces him and kills the children; and that’s why Amphion is the first Toreador line that all can trace back to; and then it adds a rumor relating to Minos in Chicago and of his childe Helena; no need to get into that, but it’s basically in ways of a repetition of a lot of the themes in these stories and also connects with what’s mentioned earlier about Minos
              • This is a reference to the Bronze Age collapse; but really what’s going on here is not a precisely linear story, we’re wrapping connecting mythological points together to tell something larger; and this is referencing the fall of the First City; and it’s also referencing the Flood via the mysterious to history Sea People associated with this particular collapse; also Baali
              • We’re said Toreador are cropping up in different nations

You don't really need much more beyond Wikipedia-level knowledge, to be honest. But look at the name of the Toreador mentioned in Carthage.
