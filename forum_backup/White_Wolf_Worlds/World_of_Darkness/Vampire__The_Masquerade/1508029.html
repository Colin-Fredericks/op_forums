    04-11-2023, 10:17 AM
    How do you all conceptualize emotion in your roleplaying as a vampire? I know it's a simple question, but it seems fundamental.

    What do you think immortal ennui is like? How do you think seeing an unchanging face in the mirror would affect you everyday, or the monotony of only ever standing under the stars? What is undead love?

    With these questions, I personally try to use the Beast as a reference point. It must color all feelings. I would imagine the Beast is kinda like a bulwark against boredom. It will always agitate you into some kind of action. Do you think it filters all passion and mundanity through a lens of violence? I think so. Love seems like it would be something to be feared as a potential kindling to flame in any domain. How does one even imagine the rage of the Beast? I know what it's like to be in conflict as a human, to feel your body tense in anticipation of possible violence as everything slows down and your heart beats faster, but how many times does that need to be multiplied to approximate the nature of Kindred, especially with no heartbeat to speak of? And how does one conceptualize terror as an undead monster?

    Of course, you also have the subjective perspectives of individual clans. If you were Hecata and could see the world beyond death, what would that do to you, make you more appreciative of life and/or cheapen it for you? I can't even imagine roleplaying a Malkavian; how do you even fathom insanity + the Beast and its incessant hunger? I can't, but I wish I could. And how are the Brujah even still in existence with such a dangerous bane? I would assume self-control must be the first and most foremost thing taught to each Childe.

    How do you think vampires feel?

      This is one of those topics that I tend to have a different starting point than most since I look at the setting and the context of supernatural existence first, followed by the reality of how the embrace mechanics allow for trait selection, and then finally I look at the concept of character creation in a gaming sense. I bring this up because many people seem to think in the reverse order.

      The WoD does not forgive error, weakness is punished with death or worse. Think of the WoD as a war movie, at the beginning all the characters are fresh faced recruits who have all these preconceived notions about what war is. Fast forward to the end of the movie and the incompetent and unlucky are dead, the survivors are hardened veterans who know what the reality is and what is required to survive on the battlefield. By virtue of the setting, anyone who is susceptible to emotional damage won't survive the first few decades, let alone centuries or millennia.

      Next let's look at the Embrace and how it skews the traits of the vampire population as a whole. Every clan has a set of standards which they value and look for in their potential candidates for the embrace. The ventrue look for competency in business, the assamites look for combat and assassination potential, the Tremere look for mystical skills, etc. But despite all of those clan-centric standards, all of them are survivors of the existential battlefield long enough to prove competent and earn the boon of siring a new vampire. So, this means you will look for candidates which have a high likelihood of surviving such a competitive environment, have the traits that the clan is looking for, and finally have some traits that that will bring the sire a benefit.

      Finally, there is the notion of character creation in a gaming sense. This is the part where a Player sees a character from a different piece of media and wants to play around with the concept or some aspect of it. This is what RPGs are all about, which is great. But in reality, the WoD as a setting doesn't really allow many characters to survive. Most people seem to look at character creation first, then reverse engineer a backstory, and finally look at the mechanics and figure out how to win at combat, or be a social character build, etc.

      Another way to look at the question of emotional processing of vampires, is to look at the difference of how a teenager processes their emotions verses how someone in their 40s-50s process their emotions. One is feeling everything turned up to 11, while the other has had the highs and lows ground down by experience. Given enough time you begin to understand what truly matters and what is just background noise. In the end you prioritize survival over ethics, you value long term security over short term gains, and you focus on gaining additional skills and knowledge to boost your chances of having an edge in any given situation.

      Will the Beast complicate all of this, absolutely it will. But the default mentality of the average vampire will be moved to one corner of a data set chart rather than being in a neutral starting position simply by virtue of the environmental factors of surviving the setting.

                  My preferred take on vampiric existence is heavily influenced by the old Kindred of the East sub-splat of VtM.

                  In that system the eastern vampires have concepts of being a mature and refined monster. The hundred years or so post undeath is called the "running monkey" stage where you are expected to run around reveling in your power, settling grudges from your mortal days, etc. But after that point you are supposed to settle down and start behaving like an "adult". While mechanically the Keui-jin had a lot of stuff lifted directly from VtM, there was a fluff level expectation to keep refining yourself, to keep learning, essentially to keep evolving as a creature on a physical, psychological, and spiritual level. While that can be seen as "gaining power" by some I feel there is a distinction to be made between power acculturation and personal refinement that just happens to gain power as a side benefit.

                  I disagree with the vampiric existence = ennui and mandatory shopping at Hot Topic line of thinking. I prefer the notion that the vampire just functions with a different set of parameters. For example, in most media the idea of the independently wealthy hermit who spends their time tending their garden, reading books, and managing their affairs, is shorthand for a quasi-enlightened character.

                  A vampire can do the exact same thing; the parameters just tend to be a little different. For example, rather than maintaining a garden, they maintain a power base of influence in the same fashion. Rather than noticing a particular flower or plant will turn into something special, they are grooming people, businesses, or political movements. The alien mentality that we attribute to the undead is in fact them just seeing human level existence like a rose bush or bonsai tree. It is something to be refined and appreciated, but not something to be overly concerned about if it dies or doesn't reach its potential.

                  The Beast just tends to push the vampire when it comes to survival and needs. A vampire with a full blood pool won't have a Beast demanding to be fed, a vampire who isn't in danger won't have the desire to kill everyone in the room, a vampire who is residing in their secure haven won't be worried about the sun coming up. Obviously, this line of thinking it relatively Path of Humanity centric, but it does function with most other Paths.

                  Once a vampire has a secure haven, a form of passive income, a blood supply, and perhaps a daylight servant, not much else is needed. Vampires heal so they don't need to worry about medical coverage, since they have an unlimited potential lifespan there isn't the anxiety about doing everything important while you still have time or in other words no such thing as a vampire mid-life crisis, vampires generally don't lose the ability to sire thus there isn't a baby clock to worry about, they don't exactly look more decrepit with age so they always are in their prime socially.

                  Basically, a vampire, once established, really can just focus on having a fulfilling and meaningful existence. Yes, you will have to deal with the occasional mortal world upheaval, and every now and then a vampiric faction will get uppity, but if planned for, such things don't really have to affect you.

                                                  I've been thinking about this one. It took a while, and now it's late so, God, I hope it's coherent.

                                                  It's not so much that vampires don't think or feel like humans, it's more that humans are relatively flexible beings and vampires live long enough to adapt to times and situations that humans never get the chance to: that's what "inhuman"/"alien" means in their context. Humanity as humans experience it quickly begins to inform a vampire's experiences less and less. Less than half way through your neonate stage most vampires have been vampires for as long, if not longer than they've been human. For ancillae, the time when they were human is as childhood to an adult. Elders have only hazy memories, like looking back at your early infancy. Good luck getting a Methuselah to remember anything about what it was like.

                                                  The shifts in thoughts are informed by three things; 1) the Beast, 2) their power, 3) their stasis.

                                                  1) The Beast is instinctual and responds to perceived threats to one's (un)life, and most vampires don't immediately realise they're feeling it at all. In an ideal situation you don't see the Beast at all. It's just quiet little things, like noticing the flush of a mortal's cheeks, being drawn to any signs of vulnerability, stepping away from a flame without conscious thought and the like. It's more obvious in reaction to a stressor. Going deathly still when somebody enters the room unexpectedly or there's a loud noise (potential threat). They start getting terse and bitchy when you surprise them. They really don't like cohabitation, and will instinctively seek to be alone when injured.
                                                  it feels like stress, but where humans start feeling our physical biological reactions intensify vampires feel theirs deaden. Their body slips closer to a corpse-like state and the mind tries to slips into a black and white state of survival vs threat of death/suffering. A bit like disassociation, I guess. They literally died, they don't want to remember what that was like or do it again.

                                                  2) Violence and injuries that humans find terrifying is like stubbing your toe for the undead, leading to shifts in priorities. If Jim frenzies in Elysium and tears your lower jaw off, making a big deal out of it makes you look like a petulant fledgling. For fucks sake, it'll grow back soon; Jim over there has to deal with the consequences of humiliating himself in public which is waaay worse. He's gonna be dealing with that faux pas all century.
                                                  Even leaving out disciplines they're stronger, faster, sturdier and that will start to warp the way they look at things. Do you remember being a kid and struggling to get up the stairs, or lift something heavy, and now you can do both those things without thinking about it? Most people need reminding when the things they take as a given don't apply to certain people/groups. So it is with kindred and kine.
                                                  (Also, can you imagine how annoying it is to deal with mortals? They're slow and can't bench press cars and if you touch them too hard the fragile blood bags squirt fluids and bone shrapnel all over the place. They're deaf and blind, and socially slow because they can't read minds or auras. Conversationally they just go through cycles of eat-shit-fuck-sleep-die interspersed with mediocre hobbies. By and large their lives are contained in a little bubble of safe, bland, sanitised ignorance that have nothing in common with yours. Sure, you've got your own interesting mortals you like, but it's like pets; My dog is fantastic, my neighbour's barking mutt is a pain in the ass. Or in the words I've heard from some parents about children "I hate kids. Not mine, but everybody else's." (which also applies to your fellow vampires' childer. Ugh, fledglings.))

                                                  3) Vampires are eternal, the world is not. Mortals die, power structures shift, societies come and go and vampires are the only ones still there. The only real (technically) permanent thing is your existence and those of your kindred, everything else is immaterial. You are a god, and the world is a game. You will play the game and fight to rise through the ranks - parrying, hitting, feinting... - and then one day you may fall again, which (assuming you didn't fuck up badly enough to die) is just an opportunity to start afresh. Reinvent yourself (new identity, new city, who knows), plot elaborate revenge, take the opportunity to try out some new tactics. Enjoy things while they last and then move on! Yes, maybe you'll grieve, but emotions run their course and in eternity that course is a mere blip. Relationships, too, change. Today's enemy is next century's ally and vice versa. With the exception of those individuals they genuinely loathe, enmity is a type of friendship for the undead. It will be satisfying to finally take down that jackass you've been plotting against for two centuries, but there will be a void in your life when you win and you will miss the scheming.
                                                  (Your alternative is to succumb to depression, in which case you're going to end up dead, on the train to wightdom, or napping eternity away in torpor. Just distance yourself from the world you can't be part of anymore, it can't hurt you then.)

                                                  What do you think immortal ennui is like?
                                                  Depression, burn out, etc.
                                                  People are stupid, they have been for centuries and they will be for centuries to come; your peers are tedious petty assholes recycling the same stupid plots over and over; you get stuck into a rut and need to go looking for something to jar you out of it. Usually results in stuff like in an entertaining disaster (as in it's entertaining for the bored vampire and a disaster for everyone else), upping the personal "difficulty rating" in ones political games, spontaneous childe siring, entering torpor and napping it off for a few decades so you can just drop yourself into an unknown world...

                                                  How do you think seeing an unchanging face in the mirror would affect you everyday,
                                                  You'd probably get over it, to be honest. I see pretty much the same face in the mirror that I have for the last ten years; remembering that one day I'll look in it and my face will be different is what seems weird (though by the time I have that face it won't be strange anymore, it'll just be my face).
                                                  So vampires by and large probably won't think anything of it, unless something reminds them. If it bothers you that much, the Tzimisce exist.

                                                  or the monotony of only ever standing under the stars?
                                                  And I rule it that even if a vampire finds a way to stand under the daytime skies, they simply find it stressful. The psychological and biological benefits of sunshine do not apply to the undead nocturnal predators. The only vampires pretending to be upset about the appearence of the night sky are Toreadors acting melodramatic for artistic purposes. Besides, the night sky changes more than the daytime sky. The sun is the sun. Stars shift and change, planets change alignments, the moon waxes and wanes and changes colour. How about aurora borealis? You will live long enough to observe many astrological phenomena. You can also use a telescope during the night for more interesting sights (...huh. Can you use Auspex to look at space?)

                                                  What is undead love?
                                                  Comes in two flavours:
                                                  A) mortal (you fall in love, you fall out of love, maybe you fall in love again some time down the centuries maybe not)
                                                  B) Immortal (blood bond. Two intertwined immortal nightmares essentially acting as one emotionally unstable whole stretched over two bodies, incapable and unwilling to be parted. The fictional ideal of love taken to its extreme - selfless and selfish at the same time, obsessive, all consuming, fulfilling, awful in its classic and modern meaning, dangerous, irrational and, frankly, completely batshit insane.)​​

                                                  (I do have thoughts about individual clans, but it's gone 1AM and this post is stupidly long, so I'll post it later.)

                                                          As for clans in particular, I imagine a Brujah still has his goals and his cause, and I think it's likely his sire chose him for that, once he's embraced I imagine it fills him with a sense of power that he can now do anything and that his ideology justifies everything, because he knows (believes) that he is right, maybe later on he will see how dangerous his new power is, how destructive it is, it makes him retreat and feel afraid of what he has become or maybe Seeing it as a resource or simply burying himself in his cause as an outlet, he might as well embrace that and his cause ends up becoming his justification for unleashing his beast. It might vary depending on the cause, but that's treading into dangerous territory and I'd rather not comment on that.
