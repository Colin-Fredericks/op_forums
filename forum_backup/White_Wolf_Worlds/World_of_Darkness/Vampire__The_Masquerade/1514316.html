      Complicated question
      Caitiff can purchase any Discipline at character creation, but thereafter have to pay six times current rating for any and all powers purchased with experience points. Even could access "exotic" ones, tho' the Storyteller have the final word, but in theory? The could have anthing - manifestation of Obeah, Mytherica, Daimonion and such are porbably result in a quick death mind you
      Necromancy Mortis, any form of Blood Magic are more of a "have an affinity" thing, even Clans with those have to learn and study them - but with proper story and background? Could work.

      These vampires have no inherent clan weakness, but no inherent disciplines as well. None of the typical clan markers apply to them - While Caitiff are Embraced normally by existing clans and bloodlines across the gamut of Kindred existence, something seems to happen between sire and childe. Either certain defining traits are not passed along in the Embrace, or some manner of imprinting is missing. As a result, the childe inherits none of their sire's clan's disciplines or weaknesses.
      Terms "Caitiff" and "Thin-blooded" are often considered synonymous. While there is considerable overlap, not all Caitiff are thin-bloods.
      The appearance of Thin-blooded have done nothing to soothe the fears of the Elders and many are more happy when there is a Caitiff less to prowl the night. Many cite the Book of Nod and claim that the blood has weakened to the point on which it can no longer establish the clan lineage. The appearance of the special Caitiff-like vampires known as Thin-bloods is also a growing concern and many Elders decide to wipe away every vampire without Clan protection.

      Many Caitiff in ancient times speculated that they are indeed the original vampires, heirs to the legacy of the Second Generation and Caine himself, because of the lack of ties - and could achieve anything. They are the manifestation, the quintessence of Caine's blood with all the potential

      The problems is, Vampire is a bit inconsistent... depending of the supplement even an abandoned childe would normally manifest the marks and weaknesses of their own clan.
      However, by swearing fealty to an elder of renown, it is possible that some Caitiff become adopted by a Clan. Many of those Caitiff are Blood bound and develop characteristics of the Clan they are bound to due to the regular infusion with foreign vitae....

      Then there is the Stoneman
      The Stoneman is a legendary Caitiff who is rumored to be the oldest one in existence.
      Some stories claim that he has successfully diablerized an Antediluvian. Others say that he is a lost member of the Second Generation who has traveled into the Orient. He expresses first-hand knowledge of Carthage, the Anarch Revolt, and similar events in Kindred history. Rumors are that he developed a host of unique disciplines on his own and is willing to teach them to those clanless that meet his standards.

      In short, they are so feared because they are anomalies and their existence is a threat to everthing the Cainite society built on; Clans, characterisitcs, Low or High status, origin stories, source of power, curses and weaknesess... and Cainites not really handling that well
      The scariest thing the Vampires could face is the dreadfull question: "What if we were wrong all along...?" - then everything crumbles around them and the existence of Caitiffs are basically that question manfiest...

          If the character is a Clanless Caitiff, she may purchase
          whatever Disciplines she wants, subject to Storyteller
          approval
          So basically, whatever the ST decides.

          When deciding how I think they work, I started by looking at the Setite Warriors. The bloodline is specifically created by taking a normal Setite fledgling and training them in Potence instead of Obfuscate. That's literally it. You teach a baby vampire a different discipline while they're growing up and they have it at in-clan costs.
          Implication; while they may be easier to learn due to blood ties, disciplines are a matter of nurture over nature.

          So, if that fledgling was abandoned, and nobody taught them their clan disciplines, they may well develop different ones. If they've a bossy personality, and/or are used to giving orders, they may develop Dominate instead. If they survive by going unnoticed, they're probably still going to develop Obfuscate. Since they're self-taught, the discipline is harder to learn than it would be if they'd been raised by a sire or other mentor, but since they know how to learn them on their own, they're capable of working out other disciplines too.

          Exceptions being blood sorcery, which aren't quite the same thing as disciplines and absolutely do require a teacher (or an infernal pact).​ I would also would not easily allow esoteric disciplines like, for example, Obtenebration, simply because that's a result of the Lasombra's innate ties to the Abyss. A Caitiff might be able to reverse engineer such specialty disciplines, and establish their own connection to the Abyss, but I think that would be an ability earned during a session if not an entire chronicle. It would also end with the Caitiff manifesting the No Reflection flaw to go with their shiny new discipline.

          Prone to hyperbole

              It's held ambiguous, but there are hints (ie it's an ST option) that Clan, Curse, and native Discipline spread are an instance of consensual- and/or naming-magic. Most prominently the ability to swap them by identifying as a different bloodline:
              While you can explain Clan offshoot bloodlines with an elder who created progeny after mastering a foreign Discipline, what happens with Baali Apostates, Caitiff, and Panders, or even synthetic bloodlines like Tremere, Nagaraja, and Gargoyles makes it very likely the whole thing is a magic property attached to the Name of the Clan.

              For a Caitiff this means they simply do not know their Clan-Name and can not identify as such. Thus, if you subscribe to this interpretation, they have no set Curse or Discipline spread.

                          Partly, Caitiff have no "In-Clan" Disciplines. No Discipline is any more or less innate to them. They're as close to "factory settings" Vampirism as you can get in someone who isn't Caine.

                          For this reason, other factors have far greater control over which Disciplines develop. The vampiric condition, despite being metaphysically tied to Stasis, is more mutable than we often give it credit for. A newly Embraced vampire can develop a Flaw that makes them allergic to garlic or repelled by holy symbols, seemingly for no other reason than because they believe that's how vampires work. Their preconceived notions about vampires gives them very real Flaws.

                          Stands to reason, then, that this works to the Caitiff's benefit at times. In most vampires, "the Blood remembers", as I like to say. In Caitiff, the Blood doesn't remember so well. So the Caitiff's beliefs about what vampires should be able to do dictate what powers manifest, or which ones they seek to cultivate.

                          While from a Storyteller perspective I'm inclined to limit starting access to many rare, Clan-specific Disciplines, if only for balance, I do think Caitiff randomly developing them makes for good plots. What's more threatening to the status quo than a clanless whelp who spontaneously develops strong supernatural powers?

                          The exception is blood sorcery, which works very differently to normal Disciplines. Blood magic is basically a hack, grafting a highly symbolic system onto the vampiric condition in order to exploit the powers of Vitae. You can't just cast spells, you have to commit to the sorcerous (un)lifestyle. You have to speak the works, make the signs, gather the materials, and observe the forms and bans. The particulars are wide open, but they all involve injecting "The Art" into it. Regular Disciplines just...work. They come naturally to vampires, whereas blood sorcery must always be learned.

                          A newly Embraced vampire is only going to start with blood magic if they had a teacher post-Embrace, or if they were already a Sorcerer/Mage pre-Embrace. This has nothing to do with being a Caitiff, and everything to do with having a body of occult knowledge upon which to base developing skill with blood magic.

                              First though: Paradigm, Consensus, and True Names are not concepts exclusive to Mages, so having an Avatar or not doesn't really factor into the equation I think. They are - together with Identity - universal concepts that appear in virtually all expressions of magic.

                              The Tremere are actually a good example. As an artificial blood line, they do not inherit the Curse from the poor sods they used to make themselves, because they created a new Name. Beckett spends a whole episode in the Jyhad Diary to figure out how a bloodline is able to detach itself from the Curse of their (alleged) parent Clan, which should not be possible. The Panders have a literal Renaming ritual that detaches new convert members from the mystical connections to their Clan. The Apostate ritual of the Baali works similarly, and even replaces the native Disciplines of the convert - both replace their Clan identity with a new one.

                              As for Disciplines, if the affinity for them would be strictly hereditary, every childe would inherit it from their sire. That is obviously not the case, as every Caitiff demonstrates, so there must be another mechanism behind it. In an older version (I don't remember which) of the game, Caitiff even used to be able to create their own Disciplines. This concept was later reworked into thin-blooded alchemy, vampires so far removed from their origins that their connection to it gets fuzzy. Again, there is a nice conversation between Beckett and a thin-blooded "Malkavian" in BJD about this, and how identity rules over what you are and can do.

                              Naming magic is of course just one way to explain all this, but it fits rather neatly as a theory.

                                  Dr. Douglas Netchurch in Beckett's Jyhad Diary had a few theoris regarding the Thin-blood and the Caitiff, he called Thin Blood Syndrome
                                  "While we call the condition “thin blooded,” this is a misnomer. The blood of neonates and thin-blooded is no ‘thinner’ than that of elders. Indeed, the physical qualities remain the same. What seems to cause the weakening of blood is a less efficient use. Elders achieve more potent results with the same amount of blood. Additionally, I found that while an average neonate can use all of her blood, thin-blooded are unable to. A portion of their vitae remains unresponsive when called upon to fuel so-called “powers,” heal wounds,or raise physical qualities. Their blood is not useless however, as I have found it diminishes upon rising: the “uncooperative” vitae is still used to fuel the reanimation of the body, but no more."

                                  "Thin Blood Syndrome exhibits in higher Generations, rising from a minimal occurrence in the 12th to universality in the 15th. We find a similar increase, though never reaching universality, with the Caitiff condition. It would be erroneous to say one leads to the other however. Rather, I believe the thin-blooded and Caitiff conditions are symptoms of the same underlying cause: a too-large distance from the Clan founders."

                                  "Patient Zero — or “Caine” — and his childer were “universal” Kindred, but we see different powers and weaknesses in the Third Generation. Furthermore, we see a similarly abrupt evolution in bloodlines. I believe Caitiff and thin-blooded are another such leap in our evolution. To wit, I mark these differences between the thin-blooded species and our own: minor resistance to sunlight (though not staking or other sources of damage, including fire,) a minor talent for prophecy, and a small aptitude in creating new powers. The latter in particular, has led me to believe we are dealing with renewed speciation. The Caitiff are the first step, as they lose their Clan characteristics. The thin-blooded are the next step: they gain new characteristics."

                                  Also, the Cainite Strain theory of his is also interesting

                                  "I’ve not yet been able to pinpoint the source of our condition. Based on symptoms, I believe we are infected with a spiritual virus passed down through blood. Unlike living viruses, this spiritual strain is unable to mutate and is rendered weaker in each new host. We may have reached the end of the virus’ viability in thin-blooded, as they cannot fully maintain the Cainite state."

                                  The problem of Resumed Metabolism itslef present an interesting wellspirn of theories

                                  "I have documented the growth of hair and nails, returned mortal perspiration and tears. I also found traces of white blood cells in one subject’s vitae and original DNA in another’s. To ensure this was not pre-Embrace blood, I fully exsanguinated both subjects. Studying their vitae immediately after, I found no traces of white blood cells or original DNA, yet both returned a month later. Lastly, three subjects experienced the return of menstrual periods or virile seminal fluids, allowing them to theoretically conceive mortal children called Dhampirs. The infection of non-Kindred by the Cainite strain has been documented before, from gestated hosts (creating ghouls) to unborn embryos (creating Revenants.)"

                                  "The brief emergence of mortal metabolism in thin-blooded yields an interesting perspective on the Cainite condition. Traditional belief holds that Cainite bodies are dead while the virus reanimates us. If that were the case, however, the lapses in thin-blooded should result in them being dead for short moments. That they instead resume mortal functions, indicates our bodies are alive and the condition keeps them in a suspended state. However, the nature of the thin-blooded Embrace contradicts this. While a normal Embrace takes only moments, a thin-blooded might experience hours or days between receiving vitae and reanimating. During this time, she is effectively dead. I find this contradiction intriguing and have begun to document attempted thin-blooded Embraces."​
