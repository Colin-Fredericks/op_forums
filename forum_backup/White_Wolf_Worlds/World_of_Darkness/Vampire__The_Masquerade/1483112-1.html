        In the interest of context, 1st Edition V:tM was this wild time where players were assumed to be Anarchs and Diablerie was assumed to be what Anarchs do. Two of the first published adventures released for the game were Diablerie quests to hunt down sleeping Methusalehs, and offered new Thaumaturgy rituals to make sure an entire coterie got to gain a Generation lift if the Methusaleh they took down together was powerful enough. Important NPC's were statted weak in order to ensure that hunting, killing, and eating them would be a plausible goal. Mithras, to cite one example, was introduced during this period of the game's history.

        I understand and don't necessarily disagree with the argument for strengthening characters who are weaker than an Elder player character who progressed through, say, Transylvania Chronicles would be. There's always going to be a part of me, though, that's tickled by what a cannibalism festival the game once was.

                    Both Diablerie books were written quite early in the game's history (1992 and 1993). Although the game marketed itself as a game of personal horror, White Wolf must have known that many players would be coming into the game from more combat heavy and dungeon exploration games like D&D. I imagined these books were an attempt to appeal to those kinds of players.

                    It makes sense. If you look at a lot of early Call of Cthulhu scenarios, they too are combat heavy, monster bashing in dungeon type environments. It took a while before Call of Cthulhu scenarios moved away from the D&D template towards a format more appropriate for Lovecraftian adventures and their rules set. Likewise, I think it took a while for STs and players to learn how a Vampire game is different from other RPGs.

                    However, I've always felt that most Vampire adventures/scenarios have generally been quite weak. They are always plot heavy and therefore railroady as opposed to presenting a situation to the PCs and allowing them to react however they want. As such, there were always NPCs who had heavy plot armor, and others made intentionally weak in order to encourage the PCs to take them out.

                                                Really I think if any Methuselah showed up in front of the Inner Circle making demands, provided those demands didn't go counter to the Camarilla's goals, the Inner Circle would just add all of that to the queue. It's not really worth getting up in arms over. If the Methuselah was making demands that would endanger the Camarilla, they'd say, "Sure thing boss," and then do fuck all.

                                                When that Methuselah showed back up later, he'd find the Inner Council and also several other Methuselahs (the puppet masters) or maybe even those guy's puppet masters (some of whom may have done the exact same thing, showing up in front of the Inner Council several centuries ago demanding to be in charge). And these eldritch methuselahs would make it clear to the newcommer that he can't just muscle in on their turf and the organization they've spent centuries growing, though if he wants to take a seat at the table there's probably an equitable arrangement that can be had that wouldn't risk Final Death for anyone.

                                                In the end, the Inner Council is basically composed of the vampires who are willing to stick their necks out and be front and center. Behind them are other, older vampires pulling their strings, and older vampires behind them. The whole point of the Jihad is that there's a conspiracy, but even as you unmask one conspiracy you find another one. Sometimes you have a Methuselah. Sometimes you have Baba Yaga. And sometimes you have Vasilisa. Any vampire who is too powerful for the Council to deal with will find himself dealing with other vampires who are as strong, or stronger than them, the ones who are sitting behind the Inner Council.

                                                Really I would just be very surprised if Artemis Orthia was the first Methuselah to ever do something like this to the Council.
