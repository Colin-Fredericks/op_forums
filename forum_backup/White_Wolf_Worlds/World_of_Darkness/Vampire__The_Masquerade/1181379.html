    Drowned Legacies

    02-01-2018, 09:42 AM
    I am rather taken with the small amount of information we have on the Drowned Legacies from Beckett's Jyhad Diary. Have any of you put any work into fleshing them out and making them playable? Or is there any chance that they might get their own book at some point in the future?

    I've put my start of an attempt at a work up for one of them below.

    Titlacauan
    The Titlacauan are a Legacy without a clear point of origin, being generally active in high society. Their unique gift gives them power over reflective surfaces, allowing them to peer through reflections, and ultimately pass between them. They believe that they have no singular progenitor, instead citing a group of vampires as responsible for their creation. Their curse links to their Willpower. As the Titlacauan grows close to frenzy, a trail of salty smoke oozes from her shoulders, and head.

    Disciplines: Celerity, Presence, Espejo

    Espejo
    * Mirror Sight - Look into one mirror and see out of another mirror that you know the location of.

    ** Mirror Speech - Look into a mirror to see and conduct a two way conversation with someone looking into a different mirror that you know the location of.

    *** Mirror Grasp - Reach into a mirror and have your hand come out through another mirror you know the location of with the ability to pull small objects back to you through the mirror.

    **** Mirror Haven - Step into a mirror to sleep within it in a similar manner to earthbond.

    ***** Mirror Walk - Step into one mirror, step out of another mirror which you know the location of.

      One Bloodline we created with a friend is the Quetzal line. They share a lot of history with the Tlacique (and may even be a "sister line" to them) and believe they descend from Quetzalcoatl. They have the discipline spread Auspex/Animalism/Protean, with a rainbow rattlesnake snake as a "land" animal form and a Quetzal as a "sky" animal form. They have the Gangrel weakness but usually acquire colorful scales as a result of their frenzies, or have feathers growing into their hair.

      Unfortunatly they disappeared with the conquest of America and one of their last, a spanish dude embraced in the 1490s, was tricked into turning into a Baali a few years after and cannot Embrace for fear of spreading Baali blood instead. He's currently working on a ritual to shed the Baali blood in order to resurrect que Quetzal bloodline, but isn't there yet.

                                      Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post
                                      See, I get a lot of people saying that Vicissitude is extremely powerful, but I just don't see it.
                                      Maybe the problem is crunch and fluff1.

                                      The crunch side makes Vicissitude kind of a good, out of combat, utility discipline, until it hits oooo when it really blossoms into a Lovecraftian death machine. Level 5 is mostly helpful for having highly inaccessible havens. Over-all, it's probably comparable to Protean or Dominate. A tent-pole power for clans that have it. Maybe best used by npcs, but not too gonzo.

                                      It's when the fluff (especially some of the implied, necessary secondary powers) is included that the whole thing spirals out of control.

                                      You can use Vicissitude to change your shape and the shape of others. Fine. So, why can't you just add or subtract physical merits and flaws to characters? I can give my ghoul a fully functional third arm, sprouting from their left knee, but I can't give them the Bruiser merit? I can change my own appearance, including increasing Appearance (which has drifted away from pure physical beauty since Vicissitude was first written), but still have to pay points for Blush of Health? I can't use Vicissitude to inflict Blind or Lame on a victim, but I can remove their eyes and limbs... which does no actual damage? The answer is, in most campaigns, you can do all these things. If a Tzimisce pc announces that he grabs a rival's ghoul and tears out her eyes and removes her legs, well, most ST would allow Vicissitude to do it. It just makes sense that you could, even though it's not explicitly stated in the rules.

                                      If you can change fat into muscle, or make bones thicker, why can't you change someone's physical stats, just like Appearance? "Game balance" is not an argument characters in-world would accept as a reason to not do something.

                                      Also, there are implied applications of Vicissitude that exist because it allows for biologically improbable changes to be made, which last and function smoothly. If you can create a fully functional eye in the back of a human's head, why can't you go to a hospital, find someone who has lost an eye in an accident, and build a replacement for them? Or, attend open heart surgery and just rebuild the heart into a healthy one? Or find a really old person and rebuild their body, into the body of a healthy, twenty-year-old? Sure, all of these things would take some time, and won't be happening on-the-fly in combat. Sure, they would all require the Medicine knowledge, but so does Vicissitude. They are all things that if you read the fluff of Vicissitude should be do-able.

                                      This all leads to a sort of power creep that just doesn't happen with "I can turn into a bat". (Despite conservation of energy/mass saying that should be a highly energetic transformation... at best you'd be a radioactive bat, at worst think "I go boom!". The coolest possibility, actually, might be a very, very, very fast bat. The weirdest implication is that when you change from bat back to human the room should get very, very cold.)

                                      Really, when you consider the full implications of changing any animal form you can see and touch into a new, viable shape, even if it shouldn't be viable, you're almost dealing with mage sphere levels of power and versatility.

                                      The only other vampiric powers that come close are certain paths of blood sorcery, most notably Conjuring, Alchemy, and Movement of the Mind. Weather Manipulation would come close if you could convince an ST what it actually means to manipulate ambient energy and matter on that kind of scale.

                                      Then, of course, as you mention, Temporis is the most over-powered, at least if the fluff and logical implications of the power are included. The fact that it makes the WoD into a non-time symmetric space... sounds like a small change to the laws of physics, but it matters. A lot. Cosmically a lot. Like the Trujah could break the universe. Or, rather, by definition, they already have and then it unbroke, then re-broke, and no one would ever notice, except that there is no such thing as velocity or movement or change or anything, really, because the whole universe becomes one giant Schrodiger's box and we're the cat.

                                      Temporis makes my head hurt2.

                                              Vicissitude can, in canon, create a "chitinous" carapace on a subject. Chitin, a long chain polysaccharide vaguely similar to sugar, is not available as a building block for vertebrates. It is so alien to vertebrates that most of us are allergic to it to some degree. The problem being that it is just close enough to us in the evolutionary tree (belonging to arthropods, fungi and such) that it is bio-available to vertebrates, but alien enough to have sometimes severe health effects. Chitin is why homes with roaches tend to also have people with asthma. Putting a large quantity of chitin on a human would lead to anaphylactic shock. If nothing else, it would be absorbed as really bad sugar, with who knows what health effects.

                                              That's a foreign tissue existing happily alongside human tissue, with no duration limit. Those are changes on a biochemical level. Serious biohacking.

                                              As for leg removal, the amazing part isn't that they remove the leg slowly. Humans can do that. It's that they do it without causing any health levels of damage. Major surgery without damage. That's a big deal.

                                              As for living furniture... I never understood that. It just seems so uncomfortable.

                                              Vicissitude is powerful. It's not a game-breaker, true, but the Tzimisce definitely won the super-power lottery. (As did the Tremere.)

                                                  Tying together the twin threads of the Drowned Legacies and the Vicissitude discussion here, I can't help but imagine a descendant of the Tzimisce doing exceptionally well in South America. For one, getting there must have come easily enough, or at least easier than some, given the tales of the Eldest swimming about during the Deluge. Furthermore, while the Tlacique have the whole "walking blood gods" thing pretty well under control, I think the bloody rituals across Mesoamerica feels like it would encourage all manner of depravity among Cainites. In fact, it wouldn't surprise me if the wars amongst the natives, especially the extinction of certain civilizations before European colonization, could have been akin to Carthage across the Atlantic. I'm especially attracted to the idea of Baali overseas - some Aztec rituals sound like they'd mesh well with consecrating a well or two.

                                                  In regards to dethroning Caine as the single progenitor, while possible, I feel that there is evidence enough of Kindred being able to sustain themselves for extended water journeys (aquatic Gangrel, for instance), that the narrative could accommodate them, but be vague enough to allow all sorts of details to be filled in by a Storyteller. For instance, I personally explain the strange nature of the Tlacique as them being descended from a Gangrel Methuselah that joined the Cult of Set to curb his more animalistic nature and basically installed himself as a god abroad to spread the dark word, taking the form of the Quetzal with his mastery of Protean. He's long since forgotten, but his descendants have fit into this oddly liminal space between Gangrel and Setite.

                                                      I tend to explain the Tlacique the opposite way: a Follower of Set found their way to the Americas, and embraced a local.

                                                      How that local developed Protean is another matter, and requires us to look at 1st edition Sorcerer. In that book, it maintained that when hedge wizards got embraced, their knowledge of Sorcery could carry over, if the character took a little time and effort to adapt them. While this is most evident with actual Blood Sorcery (both mortal and vampire sorcery use Paths, and both have similar Paths), it can also potentially manifest in other Disciplines. A user of the Path of Fascination, for example, developing dots in Presence.

                                                      The big narrative example the book used, though, was of a South American Sorcerer who had many dots in the Path of Shapeshifting. After the character loses access to their normal spirit talking ability (which they Paradigmatically tie into their ability to shapechange), they work and work and pray and pray, until they spontaneous re-develop the ability to assume the form of a Panther.

                                                      Once I read that, the idea of the Tlacique having been founded by a former Sorcerer turned Setite, who transferred their shapeshifting ability into Protean, became so obvious as to be basically canon. I refuse to believe otherwise, simply because it's the simplest and most elegant solution.
