                                      Vampire has Computers because it was written in 1991. This was before the home computing boom (largely pushed by the growing Internet boom) when the idea of having your own computer at home wasn't really "normal" yet. 30 years later most people have a more powerful and capable computer in their pocket than what was commonly sold in '91 to home users. The role and ubiquity of computers has change drastically in the past few decades; and Vampire didn't really age well in that regard. In the early 90s we knew computers were going to be important, so it was important to cover how good characters would be with them, even if the technology of the time didn't inspire the VtM authors into more detailed mechanics for the game (though Mage did get much increasingly more thorough ones.... but having a core Tradition be hackers necessitated that).

                                      Had Onyx Path gotten to make their idea for a new VtM edition, it's entirely possible Computers would have be removed from the game as an Ability (the CofD developers almost cut it from 2e but opted to keep it for consistency and facilitating programmer concepts).

                                          The problem is that modeling computer usage in RPGs has been a challenge since always, but one that most left by the wayside.

                                          Mage rules really aren't the worst call you can make here. It is not that you'll treat the computer as a Mage for all intents and purposes, but that those rules will give you more ideas on things to do, and a starting point for doing them.

                                          Other option is to treat individual programs or suites of programs as tools that allow or give a bonus to certain tasks, and the computer itself as a cap on how much of a bonus you can get out of it. The character then may customize the computer by acquiring or designing programs.

                                          You can look into other RPGs for ideas, but from the top of my head, the only one a remember with half-decent rules for computer using is Shadowrun, and that isn't an easy read, ever. Cyberpunk is moronic and I never understood all the hype around it, and I'm a fan of Neuromancer.
