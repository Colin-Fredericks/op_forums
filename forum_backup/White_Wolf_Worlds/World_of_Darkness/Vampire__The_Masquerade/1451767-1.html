And... the writers haven't done that here. They've turned it way down. It feels like depicting steamy sex in Harry Potter or moral ambiguity in Star Wars. It's inconsistent with the setting's tone.

        Moreover, unlike "a large vampire population can coexist hidden among humanity," it's not necessary. The Second Inquisition is perfectly capable of being a threat to vampires without its member organizations being such good buddy-buds. It works better as a label for a larger trend of intelligence agencies/militaries/et al independently seeing through the Masquerade, and hunters in general getting more dangerous, than it does as an international alliance of hunter/government organizations.

            Originally posted by Justycar View Post
            I like when real military spies share info about how fiction spies should be depicted
            Well, given the setting itself told us for years how these various groups operated, and it matched with how real-world spies and militaries and countries operated, I find the comparison rather relevant.

            Because the SAD, the Society of Leopold, etc had all been previously portrayed as paranoid groups. Much like real world spy groups are. And unlike spies, who if they're compromised there's usually some kind of paper trail to back it up, these supernatural investigators have to worry about actual shape-shifters, mind control, spirits watching them, and far more. Seeing these groups suddenly being all like "nah, it's fine, Joe Bob over there might have been dealing with vampires and ghosts for ten years, but I'm sure not a one of them put a brain worm in his head!" and sharing their intel in any way is far more jarring than finding out the FSB and CIA directors share all their files over tea would be.

            I don't like it when fictional spies who know Big Brother isn't the only one watching them and the guy they're about to talk to could be a secret agent for Dracula's secret cousin, act like that suddenly stopped being the case.

                                        Cooperation between security services isn't unusual. There was a lot of cooperation in the global war on terrorism, the coalition against ISIS, etc. There are long term ongoing security agreements between the US, UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand. NATO works together. There's an international effort against Somali piracy. These things aren't smoothly running Tom Clancy style competence pornography but realistically everyone is in a Coen Brothers movie and nobody is Jack Ryan, including the vampires.

                                        The only unrealistic thing about SI in this regard are the scope of the cooperation; it's apparently truly global, and not broken by existing international conflicts or travelling along existing alliances. It would be more realistic for there to be multiple Inquisitions of varying capability based around existing security agreements. The FSB is uncooperative with the CIA, for example, but the CIA and the BND work together all the time. I think it would be interesting if the book brought factional and internal division to the SI to reflect this.

                                        The other unrealistic thing would be the secrecy of it; an organization and campaign of global scope would be damned hard to hide, even if you were disguising it as counter-terrorism. But the secrecy of supernatural conspiracies in the World of Darkness is a genre conceit. You couldn't realistically hide the Camarilla, either; the urban fantasy/horror genre just sort of shrugs because it's the cost of doing business in a world both familiar and unnatural.

                                            I guess my thought is that, at the end of the day, if the Technocracy exists, then there's no reason why they wouldn't have a hand in the Second Inquisition. The whole thing is absolutely right up their alley. In fact, it would be a really weird if they weren't working with the Second Inquisition, smoothing things over between different agencies, providing information and material assets when possible, pulling political strings when necessary, etc.

                                            Also consider that one of the ideas for the Technocracy was that they had previously felt vampires didn't pose a major threat to humanity and since they had their Masquerade, the Technocracy figured they could leave vampires alone (at least until they had sufficient control over reality that they could crush them). However after Ravnos' awakening and the Week of Nightmares, the danger level of vampires skyrocketed and the Technocracy felt that they could no longer leave them alone, and decided to start making more overt moves against vampires.

                                            So something like the Second Inquisition was sort of already telegraphed, at least in prior editions of MtA. And then with 5th edition, the designers have always been clear that one of their goals is to have a unified, "one World of Darkness" so the Technocracy presumably exists in the setting.

                                            So for me, it's feels like it's obvious that the Technocracy is behind the Second Inquisition, and even if the SI isn't the Technocracy's puppet or anything, the Technocracy would still hugely influential to the point where if there's something that doesn't make a whole lot of sense on the surface (and there's a few things involving the SI that come across that way), then "The Technocracy did it," or "The Technocracy caused it to happen," strikes me as an entirely reasonable answer.

                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                    This is the same situation almost exactly except it's intelligence assets instead of Christianity.
                                                    But that's a pretty big difference.

                                                    The medieval church was greater than a nation. Rome often dictated who would be king, which nation could war with which, so on and so forth. Inquisition-era Christianity would be more akin to a single, unified intelligence agency. Seeing the Church cooperate with, well, the church itself isn't at all jarring. The top guys in the church would have good reasons to trust one another, and when you throw in True Faith and stuff, they can also be relatively (not completely) certain who is "on the take" and who isn't, at least among that upper echelon.

                                                    This isn't a single, unified group who got "in on the secret", if you will. This is multiple, disparate groups. They wouldn't all be able to call on a higher up with True Faith or some similar ability to see if the agent from the other group is under some whammy. They don't have a unifying belief system. They don't even all agree on what the supernatural threat even is. This has all been established lore.

                                                    It'd be more like if back during the OG Inquisition, the Christian church started suddenly working with Muslims, Jews, and Bhuddists, and shared everything with them.
