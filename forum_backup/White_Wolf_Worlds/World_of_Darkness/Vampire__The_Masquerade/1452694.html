    The XP Disconnect and advanced character creation

    06-17-2021, 05:43 PM
    Hello everyone.

    So I've always felt there has been somewhat of a disconnect between the amount of experience points (xp) being awarded basically 1 xp per night survived and the starting xp the books suggest when creating an older/advanced character than the default. Although, the books does state either at the end of each game session or chapter, and while it is definitely possible for a session to encompass more than one night, I think for the majority of sessions, it would be one session equals one night.

    The minimum amount of xp a character can be awarded for surviving the night, hence if a kindred were to be up every night in a full year, it would be getting 365 xp - which ties into the xp disconnect I'm getting to.

    According to the sidebar on p.79 in the V20 core book, for instance, it suggests between 0-35 xp for a neonate, I understand this as it basically marks the 0 value as the point of entry for neonates, where above 0 would be an older and more advanced character being created. However, it doesn't make any note about how much time it represents the kindred being active, nor if the amount of xp represents a value to be factorized in relation with age (in years). But as stated, if a kindred of whatever age can be awarded up to 365 xp for a full year of being active and surviving the nights, how does it make sense that a character should receive and xp amount that is significantly lower but represents a much longer time interval?

    The sidebar also states that as a basic rule of thumb, "idle" kindred should have a number of discipline dots equal to the square root of it's age. So basically, a neonate character at 100 years of age, should have at least 10 dots in disciplines, it starts with 3, so 7 to go, at the bare minimum, this is 10x7 = 70 xp. Player characters are active characters, so they would rapidly outpace this, which to my understanding translates into - they should actually be starting with way more xp than that. Besides this, the experience points it would've cost to increase to these levels, could've been spent on other character aspects instead, to reflect character progression in other areas than disciplines.

    Age Category and their approximate age intervals (in years), and xp intervals:
    Fledgling: 0-25| 0
    Neonate: 25-100 | 0-35
    Ancillae: 100-250 | 75-220
    Elder:250-750| 250
    Methuselah: 750-1000
    Antidiluvian: 1000+

    However, using age categories (status titles) to reflect age, is a bit ambigious, because it doesn't actually reflect age, what it really reflects is social kindred maturity level within the established kindred society, and not actual age. So creating an older and more advanced, using a status title to determine starting xp as opposed to it's actual amount of activity (for instance measured in years) is a bit odd and difficult to work with. Personally, I view age as something that the ST sets the certain amount of xp that is suitable for the chronicle, I think the elder background could be suitable as a reference point, but not something the players should actually spend points on acquiring.

    I think, having adjusted templates for the age categories is probably a much better way of handling it, and I wonder if something like this already exists for V:tM?

    With regards to the XP disconnect, what are your thoughts?

    //Inc.

      I think there are multiple levels of disconnect inherent in what the system is trying to do.

      On the one hand, people want to play the vampires from their favorite movies/novels, and the system is designed to allow them to do that in a fairly reasonable amount of time. For example, making Jerry Dandrige from "Fright Night" takes about 210 experience. That's a lot of xp, but you can at least get close in a long campaign.

      On the other hand, they want their NPCs to fit into general categories of Poor/Fair/Good/Excellent/Amazing, and if you gave them XP at the rate they get it while playing, any character over five years old at the longest would be in the Amazing category.

          It's true, because V20 actually awards xp per session, and in theory up to 5, with possible bonus xp at the end of each story. Even if you assume (correctly) that the average Neonate is not going out there and testing their abilities each night as player characters do, you're still going to end up very powerful before long. Originally, the game awarded you 1-3 xp at the end of each STORY, not Chapter, and that's probably more in line with those guidelines, as a Story could represent a large amount of time in-game. Personally, I like somewhere in the middle, usually 10 xp at the end fo the Story, with occasional awards during chapters. But after a year of play, this can make for very strong characters. This isn't unjustifiable, however, there are plenty of established NPC's in the setting who are much stronger than their age would suggest. What I tell my players is, if they ever look at someone's stats and say "hey he's not that tough", that most vampires, especially most Elders, are sandbagging and hiding their true power. What you see in a stat block is simply the most powerful Traits they have been seen using to date. Ultimately the decision of how much xp to give to your players is a personal decision that you have to decide upon- do you like the characters being slow to develop, or prefer them to quickly move into the sphere of Ancilla, regardless of their true age?

                  I think the "1xp per night" position is disputable. The one game that I've run had weeks of time passing by during a single session, with players getting 1-3xp per session generally. The point made above about activity is really relevant, because how active you are during a given time period, and the nature of that activity, could have a significant impact on the experience points that you get. A character shouldn't get an experience point for a night they just spent sitting on the couch, slurping from a blood-doll while they watch the news. In my opinion, they shouldn't get an experience point even if they are very active for the night, but the activity is routine or normal for them. Experience points should be the results of circumstances that cause characters to grow, not the simple passage of time.

                  Also, I think it's important to consider that those experience totals, regardless of what Age or rank they are supposed to represent, are meant for starting characters. Even within those various categories, there will be ranges in abilities and resources. The experience totals provided in that table might be suggested for the weakest or lowest-ranking characters that would nonetheless qualify for the rank/title in question. And having your new characters start at the low end of the average rank and ability for their age makes sense, actually; you want your players to have something to be hungry for (other than blood). Obviously a Storyteller can let their characters start with any amount of XP to begin with, but those numbers might make more sense if you see them as being made for characters that are still supposed to have a lot of growing to do.

                          Just some food for thought, the big issue with character generation and age versus expectation from my perspective as a player and as a storyteller, is there typically seems to be a massive disconnect between how published NPC's are statted, versus how players prioritize character growth and experience expenditure. Published NPC's almost seem to get dots in attributes and abilities for free, whereas players will (predictably) prioritize disciplines for experience expenditure, and dip in attributes and abilities only as needed. I do it, my players do it, players in other groups I've communicated with do it, and other ST's I've communicated with do it.

                          There's nothing inherently wrong with it, it just yields inconsistent returns looking at NPC stat blocks versus PC stat blocks. And, most elder NPC's will almost always be better-rounded characters than equivalently-aged PC's; they may not necessarily be stronger, because players are going to optimize their characters, they're just better-rounded and more organic-feeling.

                          I'm taking a couple measures in my TC game to counteract it, and so far the results have been damn promising.

                          First, I've increased maturation point awards from what's suggested in the DAV books, to three per decade with a fourth for proper blue booking. The caveat being, only half their maturation points can be spent on disciplines. Experience points awarded during the course of play can, of course, be spent however they please. So, that ensures they're building attributes, abilities, and virtues at a reasonable pace without really compromising discipline growth.

                          The second measure is, I've created a standardized formula for NPC generation. Discipline dots equal to square root of age, and apply the experience points it would have taken to buy those dots to attributes, abilities, virtues, and derived stats. It's worked out pretty well so far, as I've been able to create NPC's with it that pretty closely approximate published characters. I recreated Lucita using my formula with both Dark Ages and modern nights ages, and her stats were for the most part an exact match for what's published.

                          I'm still gathering data on it and doing regression analysis, but I'm coming closer to an actual mathematical formula that's a decent fit for approximating experience points as a factor of age.

                                              Another fix is to divide EXP into two types: Mundane and Sanguine. More total EXP can be given out without destroying character balance, since over a period of sessions a PC might pick up 20 exp to put into their Attributes/Abilities and 10 exp to put into their Disciplines, while 30 exp they could spend on anything would have likely all gone to Disciplines.

                                              This helps simulate a more broad progression of a player character - an aging Toreador doesn't just master Celerity, they master the viola and the spoken barb and the ability to keep up with what's in vogue. A player who loves to develop dubiously useful but highly flavourful abilities isn't punished by being compared to PCs with insane Disciplines, and a player who loves the mechanical side of things gets to enjoy investing in abilities that they otherwise would feel compelled to ignore by the shiny blood magic paths.

                                              I first tested this in MTA and it worked very well, with Mundane and Arcane EXP respectively covering things like Attributes and Spheres.

                                                          I'm surprised no one seems to like the rule that already exists: each trait can only be raised one dot per story. That rule, which has existed forever, means that even if you dish out 200 xp in the course of one story, each discipline can only be raised one dot. So if you begin with, let's say, Protean 1, Dominate 1, Potence 1, then the most you will have by the end of the story is Protean 2, Dominate 2, Potence 2, and 1 in all other Disciplines, and that's true even if you get 200, 300, even 400 xp. You literally have to spend the points to get well-rounded, because there's nothing else to do with your pile of XP.

                                                          By RAW, a character who received a literal billion XP in one story would, at most, have plus-one to each Attribute, Ability, Discipline, and Humanity/Path, while all the rest of the XP sat there unspendable until the next story.

                                                          I think maybe it never caught on because the end of stories isn't always clearly defined. Many games can be rather soap-opera-y, a series of subplots that get don't get resolved until new subplots are already in play.
