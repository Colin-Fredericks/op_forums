    Originally posted by Microcuchon View Post

    Any vampiric organization can maintain the Masquerade. The Anarchs do It; the Followers of Set do it. The Rsvnos do it. Even the Sabbat does it!!
    Goverment never have a problem acknowledging a higher power, as long as that power can not refute them. Medieval kings and emperors did it all the time (and the Camarilla elders are from the middle ages).
    Dissent is not a consequence of claiming to be the agent of a superior power. Power itself is the consequence. As long as we have power people we will fight for it, and contenders will claim to be legitimate.
    Finally, the fact that a story is contradictory doesn't render it impossible to use.
    I want to make clear that I don't think lore as written is WRONG, but highlight that other options were possible and I would have preferred them because, IMO, they would have meds more sense thematically


    "Any organisation can do X" never stopped organisation from basing their collective identity on asserting that only them and their method is able to achieve a goal.

    The camarilla asserts that only them can maintain the masquerade, and that independant clans need their supervision and that anarch are playing at the masquerade without actually maintaining it.

    Of course it's propaganda, but it's the core of the camarilla's identity.


    Yes, many civilisations acknowledged higher powers.
    Howether, these societies, and their ruling elites, didb't believed that these powers would ever come down to take away the thrones. Nor would communicate to the population outside of priesthood or saints and prophets whose validity was of course decided by the ruling body of religion.
    ​​​
    The antediluvian, on the other side, are presented as vampires, not faraway deities in heaven but flesh and bones beings as much as younger vampires. With similar desires.

    As such, acknowledging the antediluvian means you acknowledge that there's here, on earth, around 13 beings that have more power than your oganisation could ever dream of possessing, and with the mentality to seek to rule others.

    Which makes your own legitimity shaky, since at any moment, great-great-great-grandaddy can come back and reclail the throne Godfather's style, and since you acknowledged them, any neonates knows that you're nothing but overseer and that any night the real rulers may come back.
    In short, you'd paint yourself as a litteral placeholder

    Kings and emperors in history, on the other side, rarely claimed to rule the throne of gods. They were here because the gods allowed it or were chosen for it, and the gods have no interest in coming down to take the throne, since they're not humans and are ruling the cosmos.

    ​​​​​​
    ​​I would also add that, after thinking more about it, the idea that the camarilla could have claimed to be the steward of the antediluvians couldn't have possibly worked.

    For two reasons. One, they have in their rank the tremere, whose clanhood was gained by the murder of one of the antediluvian.
    Second, three of the four independant clans affirm that their culture and policy directly comes from their own antediluvian. Howether, they are independant, which contradict the claims that antediluvians wants
    a common society of vampire.




    You could brush it off by making the camarilla claim that only some antediluvians are for the camarilla, and that they are the keepers of their thrones.

    Howether, that shatters their claim of authority on all kindred, since now they only rules for the antediluvians of the camarilla clans, and have no legitimacy on, say, camarilla setites, ravnos, tzimisce or assamites.
    It also negates all hope of one day ruling other tzimisce and lasombra,. Since the clans based their modern culture on the deliberate murder of their progenitor. Who are the camarilla to claim to rule us for a vampire that we killed?


    The only logical solution is for the camarilla to claim to rule for the species common good, which, being an ideal, is a call to legitimacy far more solid than "these beings said we rule"
    Especially since said beings could at any instant come out of their nap and say "bullshit".

    "Rule by antediluvian right" is a far too shaky foundation for a group that hopes to rule forever.

                            Originally posted by Conradine View Post
                            In the WoD main story, Cain murdered his own brother and is utterly unrepentant about it. God offers him every occasion to repent, even giving him eternal life and sending three ( or four according to the Golconda legend ) angels to offer him forgiveness.

                            A common vampire Embraced against his will, who's worse sins were lying to his wife and cursing after a disliked politician, can pray and beg and cry and repent as much as he wants but 99% of time Heaven stay absolutely silent to his prayers and no angel is there to offer any kind of salvation.

                            So...
                            why?
                            The most basic idea is that God intended to punish Cain for committing murder by turning Caine into a vampire, but that's only one possibility.

                            I recall the Malkavian clanbook, in which a Malkavian recounted the Caine/Abel story but then suggested that Caine's vampirism was actually intended to be a blessing from God. Caine realized the "trick" that that God likes blood sacrifices, understood that human blood was more powerful than vampire blood, so he killed Abel, spilling his brother's blood as a sacrifice to God. God was pleased that Caine had come to such a realization and so God rewarded Caine by turning him into a vampire.

                            In such a case, the idea that Caine was punished was an idea that was created by humans (who, understandably, aren't too thrilled at the idea that God enjoys seeing them sacrificed on alters), and that idea ended up percolating through Vampire society as well. So these days many vampires view vampirism as a curse, because they view the first vampire as cursed. But if God intended Caine's condition as a blessing, then stories about God sending angels to give Caine a chance at "forgiveness" aren't true, and it also explains why regular vampires aren't ever offered "forgiveness."

                                    Even if you don't abide by any particular fictional vampire take on Noddist myth, the Bible itself has Cain both punished and protected: he committed a grievous sin, but before divine moral laws had been communicated to humanity. Cain was banished, but plead for some mercy, and was granted it in the form of the Mark of Cain, a symbol by which all would know inherently that to bring harm to Cain would curse themselves and their families for interrupting with Cain's divine punishment.                                         First let me say a few things as a preface: I generally ascribe to One World of Darkness, and assign hierarchy (as in, which takes precedent if another disagrees) on the settings in, usually, this order Mage > Kindred of the East = Werewolf > Demon = Hunter > Wraith > Vampire > Changeling; (The Reason that vampire and changeling are so low is, vampire is concerned with a very static manipulative subset so lies are par of the course and Changeling is literally retconnable in universe). Finally I go by literal interpretations wherever I can - I generally love the metaplot. Oh, and this does not concern V5, as that has disregarded most of it [In general, I approach V5 same as Requiem: Generally reused names, Fundamentally different game].


                                        Headcanon: It wasn't god who cursed Caine. It was, Adam, his father. It's quite clear that the firstborn had really insane abilities (see Lilith telling God to go f* himself, Eve controlling the Underworld as the lady of fate, etc). The Curse is born of Adam's guilt on ordering Caine to make the sacrifice (and setting into motion the Death of his then youngest son).

                                        Seriously, read the passage in the Book of Nod. Adam is always called Father, and god is always called the One Above. And the verse where the curse is described says:

                                        But my Father said
                                        "Cursed are you Caine,
                                        who killed your brother.
                                        As I was cast out,
                                        so shall you be"

                                        Now Lilith, the original rebel before Lucifer made it cool, teaches Caine how to circumvent parts of his father's curse (which effectively distances him from his heritage) and access his higher nature (his avatar) - what Lilith tries here is to make him like her, to deify him. So, he enters a seeking (he was never really a Sleeper, that wasn't a thing back then) and his higher self begins showing its power.

                                        Now, what about the angels? Well, remember than the first murder turned the war in heaven bloody. Then the psycho bastard who made murder suddenly begins exhibiting divine powers. The Angels cursing Caine are probably trying to get him to repent, to undo the creation of murder, to supplicate himself to the One instead of becoming a threat. Keep in mind that the Elohim don't understand Awakened wills very well, especially the loyalists - what Abel's murder was, was a spell adding a new law into the world (Murder is Possible). Oh, and Gabriel offers Golconda instead of what others did because out of the Loyalists he is the most rebellious - there's a passage in demon which has him potentially fall because he refuses to leave a siege (under orders from the One) in defense of a single human. Gabriel actively assists the Seeking, while the others try to impede.

                                        Then Caine comes back from the Seeking, understanding his powers, and Lilith asks him to stop pushing so hard because he is going to break himself. Aaand he doesn't listen, open himself up to the Night (keep in mind, Night here can mean really dark things) and comes into contact with a manifestation of the Wyrm (Beast of War) which gives him power, but also gives him a Beast - and thus solidifies the taint of the curse.

                                        So back to the subject at hand, why is the curse so punitive to others? Because Caine isn't just a product of the curse. He was offered partial freedom through Lilith and, more importantly, he never died. Caine isn't a vampire. Enoch is the first vampire. Caine is the dark Father, he is a Tainted, Cursed something but he does have an avatar and thus quite a say so on his own limitations. The rest are spiritually mutilated shadows of who they were, whose higher selves have been replaced by a piece of the Beast of War. Of course the curse sucks so much for them.

                                                    Originally posted by Muad'Dib View Post
                                                    Why do you place some types of magical persons and supernatural beings as definitely having more knowledge and being more intelligent, in regard to truths, topics, events, and facts considered and regarded as mystical or important ? Do you have clear and exact reasons in regard to the particular organizations, teams, and groups you mentioned ?
                                                    Clear and exact reasons? No, this is WoD, there's nothing clear and exact about it. But I have some reasons of a more intuitive nature, and I have some mostly thematic - and of course it is also an aesthetic choice, of what convinces me and what doesn't.

                                                    A pretty important part has to do with breadth of capability (what they can do, and do relatively easily) combined with a push towards learning about everything that isn't just them (effectively, how much each of these groups is pushed to understand the world). You can read on, but I doubt I have what you want; still, I appreciate the question:

                                                    Out of all these, Mage is the most expansive in capability and understanding - it's the only splat that is literally capable of making every other, going everywhere, and at the same time pushed towards learning and understanding. Werewolf goes next because of how expansive the reach of most werewolves is (their connection to the spirit world alone alows them so much knowledge the others don't have), coupled with things like the Mokole and Mnesis. Kindred of the East I mostly put second for a metatextual reason - it was whitewolf's first attempt for a semi unified cosmology.

                                                    Demon and Hunter are two sides of the same coin, and their information comes from similar (if antithetical) sources. They have access of a lot of knowldge, or they interact a lot with other hidden aspects of the world, but their perspectives are so biased as to make them very very difficult to use. Wraith is where it is because it's extremely limited in scope. 2/3rds of the spirits worlds are completely out, plus the material world is somewhat remote - wraiths know a lot about the underworld, and I trust in what they know, but they just focus on that.

                                                    Vampire are neither particularly introspective or particularly interested in anything other than themselves so they are not very good at describing the mysteries of the world. And finally, the grand exception: Changeling would be extremly good at it, but Changeling is literally the setting of actively forgetting and rewriting your lore. Maybe Dark Ages: Fae might apply though, haven't included that.

                                                    Hence, Mage > Kindred of the East = Werewolf > Demon = Hunter > Wraith > Vampire > Changeling; I absolutely get switching some of these. Some might put KotE first, others Demon. I just think that Mage is the one that needs the least effort to describe all of the others without conflict, so I put it first, but Changeling can do the same, for instance, just with a lot more clarity.

                                                    PS: Also I love the Bahari, so I admit my absolute bias for all Lilith related stuff :P
