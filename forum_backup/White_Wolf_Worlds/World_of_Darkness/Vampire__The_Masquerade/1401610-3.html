        Originally posted by Leandro16 View Post
        VTR is a toolbox edition designated to be played on multiple tiers (street level , city level and global conspiracy) and has crazier stuff than masquerade antedeluvians from the gehena it is the opposite of V5.



        3 dices is the difference between Horatio Ballard and Helena Dominate... A coterie of starting characters from the clan Nosferatu can fairly easily destroy Helena and even a goon with a lucky shot can so just add more goons and helena bites the dust.

        The problem of V5 wasnt tonning down the power levels it was eliminating them and triying to kept "the masquerade" in the tittle to get more sales and this generated a backlash from the comunnity.

        Ironically V5 could have worked as a different gameline or as a supplement with alternate rules for V20

        Please note that ancient vampires in V5 and V20 both roll roughly the same number of dice when using disciplines. It's just that in V20, these characters had 6+ dots in both attribute and discipline, whereas in V5, they are capped at five each ... but then add another bonus based on blood potency. So in reality, the only real difference is they don't have these crazy 6+ dot discipline powers, but on the other hand, 5 dot powers in V5 are overall stronger than 5 dot powers of old ... without drifting into GM plot territory by having game changing and railroading powers at their disposal. Player characters themselves pretty much never had access to these insane powers as PCs were generally capped at 8th gen and it's 5 dot limit. So in the end, all these powers served was to show players what they *could* have if they were themselves these powerful ancients ... or, as was pretty much ALWAYS the case in old editions, the ability to start chargen at 8th gen and then find excuses to diablerize.

        Note: Helena is by no means weak in the new edition. Not even close. She still has a bunch of 5 dot disciplines as well as many 5 dot attributes and abilities. Not to mention a high enough blood potency so that she gets an automatic 3 dice added to every discipline roll. And as I said, level 5 disciplines now are really powerful given they are meant to be capstones. For example, Majesty doesn't break against everyone if the majestic vampire attacks a goon. In fact, even the goon attacked is still only defending himself ... but would still struggle to attack her back. And combine that with, say, Terminal Decree, and you could have several dozen goons that are all cowed by her, unable to do anything but grovel, and watch helplessly as their leader goes blank eyed, aims his gun at his own temple, and pulls the trigger. Trust me, she's still quite badass. Without drifting into GM railroading territory.

                Originally posted by KarlB
                Simple: Because powercreep is a big problem and tends to lead to the downfall of gaming lines. PCs like to feel like they might have an impact, and if they know that an NPC has a power level FAR FAR above anything they could ever hope to accomplish or even counter through numbers, it just leads to jaded players. By toning down the power levels across the board, the players feel like they might have a chance without resorting to finding the most broken and imbalanced content to play with. For example that horrible horrible mess that was Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand.
                I don't believe this premise is true. And your example it's the perfect proof: Old DSotBH actually allowed you to start the game with a Meth or close (their had an "special" Generation Background), that's also an assasin of a secret society with True Magick and Werewolf-vampires among their resources - so it was the least de-powering sourcebook of all of Vampire that I know. It still was a disaster.

                On the other hand D&D 5e (a game that's very well liked among fans both old and new) has monsters with CR above 20. That's above what characters of max level can figth comfortably. And what lvl 20 characters can figth it's already WAY beyond anything lvl 1 starting characters can ever hope to defeat with their resources

                As evidence shows, this doesn't need to lead to 'jaded players'. Poor storytelling shoving high-end treaths to low tier groups, that both by common sense and Lore should be beneath their notice, it's what leads to jaded players.

                And I will admit that my experience with Vampire has seen many of these. I guess, it's possible V5 it's a (too little, WAY too late) reaction to the tendency of STs of days of yore to shove Meths against the players to control and abuse to create horror ... but this can't really be attributed to mere rules. This kind of antagonistic storytelling was encouraged by books - and it still kinda is, albeit in a different fashion (just see how V5 suggest STs "worth their salt" to treat PC Touchstones).

                You need a narration that's not about punishing your players (except when they like it :P ) to avoid this. After all, as has been said, Helena can easily break the PC if the ST wishes to force such a confrontation when they aren't capable of figthing her. I don't think that was ever the problem, though.

                Originally posted by MichelleCLight
                I found myself thinking of a good way to balance it was to limit XP players get to make sure they aren't creeping to the level of my Elder NPCs. And I think it's working.
                You give the players less than the 1-2 xp per session that V5 grant's them

                Personally, I think that the problem it's not so much that powers can't get over 5, but rather that

                1° Elders (and anyone, really) can only ever get 5 powers.
                2° This isn't tied to generation or anything inherently "elderly", but to XP - and this can't really be solved by making the XP gain even more abysmal for reasons I will mention below.

                As mentioned level 5 disciplines can duplicate some Elder powers. Problem is you can't ever have more than 5 powers in a Discipline (less than that if you use Amalgams). Helena has to choose between being able to order a single PC to shoot himself OR being able to dominate all of the PC OR have some other single high-tier gimmick (and once you see it, you know that she hasn't the other powers. Understanding the enemy skills it's the 1rst step to stop fearing them)

                Yet, as it has been noted the PC (that usually start as less than Neonates) likely will get smashed by anyone with a few lvl 5 Disciplines and BP, that's also smart enough to not get into their traps (as any elder should be).

                That's a problem in itself, because Elders deserving of the title weren't "anyone". They represented a power gap that couldn't get surmounted by XP alone, that could scare even Princes. That Helena isn't much above lesser elders, not really, it's a problem in itself for the narrative. Perhaps a bigger problem than being above the PC! - you see lot's of people being able to do the same as her, even if you yourself are trash and can't. Then why is she such a big deal for the Vampiric world? - Merely nerfing (even more) the PC doesn't solve this, you have to nerf the whole world (more than what V5 already does, judging from what people says about Horatio) OR make a house-rule

                It has been suggested that you could tie Generation to Disciplines, and allow more than 5 powers for low gens. I think that alone can be a huge step towards making Elders scary again. Being able to order mass suicide (and similar) it's above ordering the masses and having absolute control of only one person. And being creatives here, one could always invent Amalgams to reach power levels above what's in the Basic. Between these two, I think you can have elders almost as scary as those of the days of yore w/o breaking anything.

                                    Running V5 for a 1987 game:

                                    V5 is a great little system, but essentially it's advertising for the Paradox video games. So everything is video gamey. There's a reason the company that made the first three books doesn't exist anymore. It's a disappointment, but a fixable one. However, I started running it and I don't want to go through the pain in the butt of going back to V20. It takes some doing, and maybe they'll expand the line to the point I don't have to write my own rules, but that Summer 2019 release date on the Player's Guide is not encouraging.

                                    I run games before 1999 so I don't have to even think about Thinblood Alchemy (just play a ghoul) and the SI. (we already have the Technocracy) 90% of my problems with V5 can be excised with a time jump.

                                    That also means that the Beckoning hasn't happened yet, so what do we do for the elders?

                                    - Vampires can learn up to their blood potency in additional Discipline powers at the normal cost after they have 5 dots in a discipline. So a BP 3 Torreador has five dots in Presence and knows Awe, Lingering Kiss, Summoning, and Star Magnetism. That Torrie could learn the remaining powers of Presence up to level 3: Daunt for 5xp, Dread Gaze for 15xp

                                    -Anyone of Blood Potency 3 or higher can make their own Discipline powers. This, combined with the above, gives a lot of growth room for Blood Sorcery.

                                    -Whenever someone has a dice pool over ten and uses it, it weakens the local Gauntlet/Consensus/Reality. Any funny rules interactions are caused by this.

                                    OR

                                    -Just play V20.
