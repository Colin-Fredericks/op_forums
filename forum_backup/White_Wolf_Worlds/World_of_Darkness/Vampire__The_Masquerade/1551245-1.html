        To elaborate more on what the current approach towards WOD5E looks like, here’s an example Juhana Patterson (the current lead-developer of WOD5E) talked about: This was at Ropecon 2023, a gaming-convention in Finland and so he talked about pitching a book like “Helsinki by Night”. WOD has city-books, even fifth edition does with a book like Chicago by Night. But if he were to pitch such a book these days, Paradox would flat-out refuse to greenlight this book. Doesn’t matter how good the pitch is, they refuse to greenlight a book like that.

        Because the thinking goes: There needs to be one true canon in WOD5E. So, let’s imagine they were to greenlight a book like that. Now there’s a TTRPG book out there called Helsinki by Night, a V5 setting book about Helsinki. Next a videogame-developer approaches Paradox and says “Hey, I want to develop a VtM-game set in Helsinki.”. And then Paradox will say “But the game has to align with this TTRPG-book.”. The fear at the heart of this is that the videogame-developer will respond to that “Fuck that! Now I’m not going to develop this videogame.” And that’s why they will simply refuse to greenlight any pitch with a realworld-location. That’s why recently all the Renegade-releases use fictional locations instead. But on the flipside, when there’s a videogame out there which uses a real-world-location, you have to acknowledge those games’ setting and story-details in the TTRPG-books.

        The problem is that this approach hasn’t worked out. There haven’t exactly been a ton of videogame-developers out there with a deep desire to use the WOD IP despite them being careful. But I think that has less to do with the IP itself and more with Paradox’s current situation. Especially as a publisher Paradox has been a part of so many missteps and disasters over the last few years, sometimes it's hard to keep track of that whole mess. Most famously, you have things like the disastrous launch of City Skylines 2 or when Paradox created a studio (Tectone) to make a Sims-like life-simulator game called Life4You only for the game to get cancelled after years of work, effectively setting 200 million dollars of cash on fire. Oh, and everybody who worked on that game got fired too.

        And there’s Bloodlines 2, of course. The supposed flagship-title for WOD-videogames. That certainly is a prime-example of what one might call “development-hell”. But the most recent previews finally look like a game that could potentially be good. We’ll see in October… hopefully.

        It’s nothing WOD-specific but something way bigger. Who knows what exactly it is over there at Paradox that leads them to mismanage projects time and time again – but it’s noticeable. I mean, the Chinese Room reveal is another perfect example of why I don’t trust Paradox to manage this whole thing. They announced at the start of last year that there would be an announcement for Bloodlines 2, then later that year they revealed that the Chinese Room is working on the game now and that it would come out later that year. But first, it was delayed to “sometime during the first six months of 2025” and then it got delayed AGAIN to October 2025. So, if Bloodlines 2 does indeed get released in October, consider this: If they had simply waited one year (!) to start with the PR – everything would have been fine! They could’ve announced that there will be big news later that year at the start of 2025, they could’ve revealed that the Chinese Room was working on it during the Summer and in October 2025 they released the game. But no, Paradox had to mismanage the whole thing yet again. Because it was obvious to plenty of people in the Fall of 2024 that the game was very early in development. How that escaped Paradox’s notice, though, is anybody’s guess. Not that it’s very unusual for publishers to set unrealistic release-dates in the videogame-industry, I guess, but I doubt anyone’s time working on Bloodlines 2 was made better by rushing things like this.

        As for the TTRPG-books, I hope they simply stick with Renegade. Setting aside my opinions on the writing… Renegade has a good working relationship with Paradox, they handle distribution, they reliably put out supplements/adventures in a regular fashion and their sales are okay. Sure, it’s nothing nowhere what Wizards of the Coast with D&D and Paizo with Pathfinder make but it’s not too bad either. I don’t see any reason to centralize it all on White Wolf. Besides, hasn’t WOD5E’s publishing history not gotten convoluted enough already? Just stick with Renegade. Why does it feel like Paradox can’t stick with any chosen direction for more than a few years…? Of course, you’re not getting anywhere if who does what work on the IP changes constantly.

        My most optimistic take on the name-change is that it might help videogame-developers to get interested in the WOD-IP again because the idea of “working with White Wolf” sounds less troubling than “working with Paradox”. But that’s a very optimistic take.

        That’s why my preferred option would’ve been different: If it had been up to me, I would’ve gone into the opposite direction. I would’ve disbanded the in-house team aside from licensing and empowered Renegade to act more independently instead. As for the videogames – who cares if they contradict the books... Of course, there are guidelines to mind what makes the IP special but who cares if the videogame doesn’t acknowledge one particular character from a citybook or if the videogame has a different Prince in a city compared to what the book says. Sure, nerds like us here can debate this online what character is better or worse but in the grand scheme of things – who cares? What matters is to (hopefully) have a good videogame out there and a good TTRPG-book. And then let everybody sort it out for themselves what characters they use in their own game. Besides, you could just as well pull up books from earlier editions and compare them with fifth edition books and make a fuss about that. So why bother getting fussy about minute differences between videogames and the fifth edition books when fifth edition already is completely fine not caring about one true canon between all the editions?

        And in the fifth edition context, I’ve always liked the Renegade-output more when it chose a toolbox-approach. Just enable players and storytellers to pick and choose. That’s why I think books like In Memoriam (a book about playing older vampires) and Apostates (a H5 book about playing hunters who still have strong ties to hunter-orgs) are good additions to those gamelines. I’d much rather see WOD5E do more of that rather than centralizing it all on the new White Wolf and then do stuff again like “V5 is only about playing young Anarchs and H5 is only about playing street-level hunter-cells. Everything else is badwrongfun.”.

                    Originally posted by Saga View Post
                    Paradox has a political agenda and many will never consume VtM as long as that happens.
                    As opposed to the original White Wolf, who absolutely had nothing remotely resembling a political agenda.

                    ...

                    In one sense, any kind of large group of humans working together is going to have some kind of political agenda, because politics is ultimately just the consequence of human being interacting on a communal/societal scale. Negotiation and compromise are the result of individuals with disparate beliefs coming together to agree on compromises around goals and values that can hopefully lead to the collective managing to find a consensus that the various members can collectively accept, even if it is not the outcome that they would personally prefer.

                    In another sense, I think that the political agenda of Paradox as a company is really more of an economic agenda. Which is to say that they are primarily concerned with making money. There will a certain amount of influence coming from individuals that have a combination of power and personal opinion which pushes decisions in certain directions that are not chained to the bottom line. But as a for-profit company in a capitalist society, the decisions that Paradox makes will be significantly influenced by their calculations around which decisions will result in the most profit for their business.

                    If you think that Paradox is trying to sell you "woke," it's not because Paradox cares about "woke" causes, but because they think that most people that would be interested in their product are interested in "woke" ideas/causes/etc., and they are pandering to the customer base that they think is most likely to give them money. Which is just what for-profit businesses do.

                                        It makes a bit more sense if you consider the thinking of a for-profit company (esp. a publicly traded one, which has significantly different incentives than a privately owned one).

                                        A modern brand basically has to choose between to paths when they want a franchise: They either need to fully embrace the idea of franchise as "vibe" where an overarching canon is meaningless, but that increases the risk of any given entry in the franchise being a complete flop. The other option is to go full bore on one overarching canon that all media in the franchise abides by. This, by no means, guarantees any consistent level of quality, but has a much stronger tendency for "bad" entries to just do subpar instead of bombing; people will go see it even if just to keep up with the events in the franchise.

                                        Going down the middle road might work for TTRPG-primary businesses that are big enough to branch out into other media; because TTRPGs as an interactive and player creativity driven activity implies an audience that doesn't expect everyone to experience the story the same way. I'm free to run LA with my own completely homebrewed take on the city in my home games, so the "canon" TTRPG supplement and the video game being widely divergent isn't as much of a turn off as it can in other contexts. When a comic book based franchise says that a specific story arc is going to be the core story of a new video game, there's a lot less leeway the fans will tolerate for mucking with the story they feel that the company is promising.

                                        Paradox is not a TTRPG primary company, even with the newly revived White Wolf sub-company thing.
