    What service could the Ravnos provide larger society?

    06-08-2023, 08:24 AM
    I mean, aside from kindling and convenient catspaws.

    Like, the other independent clans cover a niche where you can deal with them in a (mostly) politically neutral fashion outside of the Camarilla-Anarch-Sabbat structure.

    The Children of Haqim loan out the warrior caste as hitmen.
    The Hecata are a politically neutral bank whose staff happens to be largely made up of mediums and exorcists, should you have a ghost problem.
    The Setites can deliver you literally anything with perfect customer service and a nice smile, zero questions asked. ("Amazon.com for vampires"; trades in humans, drugs, weapons, occult relics and other contraband with a side branch as a vampire airline/shipping business; "we will smuggle you and your stuff anywhere in the world.")

    All three clans practice some form of blood sorcery, if you don't or can't deal with the Tremere for your sorcerous needs, and can get pretty much the exact same results Thaumaturgy can, albeit weirder or less sanitary ways.

    Then there's the Ravnos. I like the Ravnos, I like their concept of reality as an illusion, their clan culture based on having a destined role to play, their weakness (not the V5 one, and after discarding their original ethnic ties), their use of Chimestry, their ties to CtD.
    But they don't... do anything. They don't have anything to offer. Honestly, they don't need to. It's just that Caitiff also sit in the spat upon, eternal outsider niche and I often wonder if the Ravnos could be more.

    So, if the Ravnos could add to vampire society in some fashion, what would they do? The things that spring to my mind are couriers of things you don't want to entrust to shipping companies or texts or something and general security contractors. Basic Chimestry is only dispelled when you're actively trying and able to disprove the illusion with proof, or if strains suspension of disbelief too hard.
    (They may also have a niche in interior design, for any interested Toreadors who would like some pretty illusions to decorate their parties or are designing Elysiums or havens.)

    Or jailers; trap your important, yet dangerous members of society in a lotus eater trap! Also doubles as a nursing home. Does your grandsire keep making everybody miserable because he doesn't like the 21st century and longs for the good ol' days of Imperial Rome? Nobody's going to kill or eat him for some reason? Well, send him off to live in a chimerical fantasy world.

    Might be reaching a bit. Anyone have ideas?

    Prone to hyperbole

      It depends of the setting really and how deeply you want to go in to their history and ties to the Kidnreds of the East
      In the Ravnos "Holy Text", the Karavalanisha Vrana​ (Wounds of the Night's Sword) described, The god's appointed protectors of humanity, the siddhittizaya, had been corrupted by the demons they were charged to fight and had taken to devouring the flesh, blood, and souls of mortals. Outraged, the gods cursed them, naming them asuratizayya or "countless demons" and declaring that from now on they would only find sustenance in devouring flesh and blood. This only encouraged the asuratizayya, however, who embraced their sins and gorged on humanity. Dismayed by this turn of events, the gods agreed to create a foe that would make the asuratizayya know true terror and curtail their gluttony. To that end, the gods took the soul of the man most grievously wronged by the asuratizayya and gave him new life, making him a monster so that he and his descendants might hunt the asuratizayya. Though many of the gods bestowed him with great gifts to further his cause, not all of them agreed to creating another dark force in the world. Three placed curses upon the man so that he should fear falling into temptation as the siddhittizaya had, and so the man would forever be threatened by the touch of fire and sunlight, and would also know an eternity of physical and spiritual hunger just as the creatures he hunted. Named Zapathasura, or "accursed demon", he was then sent out to drive the infernal hordes from the world.
      The asuratizayya are the Kuei-jin
      In the centuries that followed Zapathasura supposedly Embraced five others
      From each of these childer various lines of Ravnos spread across the world, many of whom ruled over the mortals while fighting against the asuratizayya. This war came to a climax in an apocalyptic battle, resulting in the death of both mortal armies, but with Chandraputra vanquishing the ruler of the asuratizayya.
      From Marizhavashti Kali, the Black Mother, the Old Lady of the Ravnos came the Brahmin and the Phuri Dae - The Phuri Dae are the only remaining caste of the Ravnos. Claiming descent from the Rroma, the Phuri Dae (literally "Old Mother") are seers and collectors of lore within the clan. The Phuri Dae use the Discipline of Auspex instead of Fortitude. The Indian branch of this bloodline are thaumaturges known as the Brahmin in the caste system of that region's Ravnos. There is also a family of power among the mortal Rrom known as the Phuri Dae; like the Ravnos Phuri Dae, they are renowned seers and lorekeepers.
      Chandraputra was the most loyal childe of the Ravnos Antediluvian and his general and war leader in his struggle against the asuratizayya. He is the ancestor of the Kshatriya jati (the military leaders of the Ravnos and ran the war against the asuratizayya)​
      He worked with the Black Mother to coordinate intelligence, from which he developed his strategies. He may be among first Indo-European conquerors who invaded India or may have ruled over the mortal city-nation of Harappa until it went to war against the asuratizayya stronghold of Mohenjo-Daro. During the battle, the two generals called forth otherworldly aid, forced "the light of four suns" to shine upon each other and turned the river valley red with the blood of thousands of mortals and Kindred. The final battle reportedly lasted for a year and a night, ending when Chandraputra took the head of his millennial adversary and condemned its soul to the most horrible of the many Hells. When his brood mates began to desert their sire, Chandraputra tried to restructure the clan in the wake of mass desertions and a complete collapse in morale. He constructed what would later become the mayaparisatya, the Path of Paradox, or the study of mayaparisatya.​​​
      Ravana embraced Infernalism and became the Yama King of the same name, king of Sri Lanka, lord of the Rakshasa, and a figure of great piety and great evil
      Ramessu descendants would eventually form the Vaisya caste (Vaisyas maintained the equivalent of a Masquerade in India and also managed mortal resources. The Vaisyas were effectively a military manager caste.)
      Some legends of the clan claim that Ramessu wandered into the east at one time, tired of the endless war between the asuratizayya and his Clan, and settled in Egypt during one of the times the Osirian League and Set battled again against each other. After his arrival in Egypt, he took a large section from the Setite territory when they tried to cast him out. He was able to do this because the Setites were fighting with the local lupines at the time and didn't have the resources to fight both. There, he gave birth to his own jati, the Alexandrites (initially native to Egypt, and were noted as being extraordinarily formal in matters of Cainite etiquette. Rumors also tell that they believed that Akhenaten was their founder, cursed by the gods for revering the sun over everything else with never being able to see daylight.)
      Hazimel, the Rakshasa​, the Ravnos claim was born "far to the North" and served originally as a guardian of a goddess dedicated to combating the asuratizayya. The asuratizayya eventually retaliated against her by turning Hazimel against her, causing him to kill her. Insane with grief, he ripped out his own eye and fled into the mountains, where he was discovered by Zapathasura. When the Antediluvian offered him vengeance, Hazimel accepted, forsaking his name for the title "Rakshasa". He is said to have bared his Eye as a nazar, which allowed him to see through falsehoods. The Karavalashina Vrana records that the Rakshasa became war-weary and confronted his sire with this. The two clashed and the Rakshasa became Zapathasura's second-greatest enemy, supposed to have cast his lot with one of his greater nemeses like Ennoia. In some versions of the story, he left India for either Africa or the Middle Kingdom.
      Zapathasura eventually took his Eye from him and imprisoned him until he would repent.

      These are the "basics" so to speak, and from this you can build a Ravnos Clan that are still try to do their job; fight against the asuratizayya/Kuei-jin, the Yama Kings, Infernalism, Baali and other Demons, not unlike the Assamite and/or the Salubri

      Depending where you are there are other Ravnos branches/jaits
      Bashirites - A branch that originated in a Ravnos known as Bashir. They believed in a combination of Christian doctrine and the tenets of the false Path of Paradox, and saw themselves as harbingers of the Apocalypse. They believed that Judas was the first of their kind, cursed by God for his betrayal with eternal wandering. Many of them later joined the antitribu. - they could "serve" as a doomsday cult
      Phralmulo - The stereotypical Travelers (guides and messengers mayhaps, like the Silent Striders of the Garou)
      Kalderash - A small family of traders that has insinuated itself into the Middle Kingdom and has managed to avoid clashes with the Kuei-jin. - diplomats and traders? Yes please!
      Phaedymites - A branch of chivalric Ravnos originating in the methuselah Phaedyme. The Phaedymites tried to manage to control the urges of their Beast and served mainly as couriers. Furthermore, they were sworn enemies of the Sybarites - ok, I let this sink in. Chivalric Ravnos
      Sybarites - Decadents that first arose in the days of ancient Rome, these Ravnos are responsible for the corruption of the original path of Paradox and their excesses were one of the main reasons that the Clan was regarded as better criminals.
      Yoryari - The Yoryari were a small splinter group of the Sybarites who are said to have founded a number of now forgotten philosophical variants on the false Path of Paradox, namely a belief that the primordial energy of change (weig) is held in all things, and that it needs to be released in order to transform reality.

      Not to mention, the Raktasadhu of India is the mutual alliance of the the Daitya, Danava, Nagaraja, Eastern Ravnos, and Wu Zao against the ever-posing threat of the Kuei-jin (mostly the Infinite Thunders Court of southern India and the Golden Court of eastern India.), the Camarilla, the Ashirra, the local Sabbat and the Tzimisce Methuselah Kartarirya and her Tzimsice)
      ​The Western Ravnos or even Eastern ones could try the same, serve a sect or society, form a mutual alliance against ancient enemies they all know too well
      Demons
      Tzimsice
      Baali
      Kuei-jin
      Anything that is not "us"

      Their ancient history, ties to the middle east/far east, legends, magic, reality shaping, and mandate from heaven a Ravnos or the Ravnos could be very usefull ,trying to fullfill their duty, use their caste system their "job" to integrate tehmselves to Cainite culture basically anywhere
      But even as the 'original Ravnos' idea, travellers and wanderers they could fulfill a similar nieche to the Silent Striders - messengers and guides and those are important things even in the 21st century​​

              Discount Gangrel.

              For real, there's a lot of postulation that Ravnos are like zealous hunters of other supernaturals. For Changelings, there's the thing where Ravnos illusions are real against them. For other supernatural threats... I don't really see it. I'd go as far to say they'd fare worse than more than half the other clans based on their V20 abilities. I think this could be a real cool angle to explore, but there lies another problem: Other supernaturals aren't really a bother for vampires. Werewolves stay out of the way while Mages and Changelings aren't even on the radar.

              There is one really, really cool 'service' feature the Ravnos got given in LotC: if they spit shake, that's a binding oath, and they'll get a red mark on their aura if they break it. That's a real cool feature, cool enough that I'm actually a little loath to keep it as a Ravnos only thing. But then again I'm a sucker for the road of kings and it's derivatives. Stuff like this would make the Ravnos ideal partners because you can actually put a lot more trust in them than other vampires.


              Where I think Ravnos really work though is that they're the real damned. Caitiff are just social pariahs, there's even a bit of jealousy there because the clanless don't have a weakness. But Ravnos? They're a pitiable existence that challenges conventional morality. Their Vices just bar them from high humanity attainment, they just can't do it. Camarilla vampires believe that if they make the right choices they can be good, it's difficult, but they can do it. Neonates want to believe they can be saints, that everyone has the capability to be a saint, and that damnation is a choice. For Ravnos, Damnation is a compulsion, their only escape is death or redefining what damnation is. For Ravnos, they can't choose their damnation, it's decided for them. their condition is fate, the people hurt by them are victims of fate. To Ravnos, the pretenses of a high-humanity vampire are vulgar and ugly, who are they to deny fate? Literally the most sympathetic villains in VTM, if you don't want to frame them as antiheroes.

                  For the Ravnos I think it benefits them to consider what they offer more on a narrative level than on a service/commercial level. I think that Ravnos could hypothetically serve as messengers or couriers or a particular type of mystic but I think it benefits to think about what they’d be in Vampire the movie or comic book or novel.

                  some ideas…

                  •I think Ravnos work well on the sort of peripheral role that the independents originally had, where they all had mercenary roles to varying degrees, and where the Ravnos in that role would operate as the semi-reliable trouble shooters who could deal with problems that others may struggle with

                  •I like the suggestion other posters have had as having Ravnos operate as vampiric-criminals rather than necessarily human criminals - they openly live among select companies of similarly desperate and scrappy nomads, they poach and thieve from other vampires without hesitation, they can act as trouble-shooters and saboteurs by their mere presence in the domain of another

                  •Ravnos on another hand also work well as another group that has a particular slice of hidden and occult lore than others; I think they’d do well with a combination of concern with prophecy and metaphysical tensions, the sort of thing that it seemed the ST character Baba Syn (if I’m recalling her name right, she was in Children of the Inquisition & Giovanni Chronicles iirc)

                  •(Basically my inspiration for the Ravnos is one-part Lost Boys type chaotic-free living figures and one-part characters that would fit in well in an issue of Hellblazer or the Alan Moore run of Swamp Thing)

                      Narratively, when I think of the the Deceivers I think of Changeling: the Dreaming and tales like the Lotus Eaters. Like having a nightmare, and knowing you're dreaming but being unable to wake up and desperately searching for something you can use to force yourself awake. Or going to a party where your charming hosts offers you all your favourite foods at a table with the lovely guest and you stay there forever, starving to death surrounded by the skeletons of everyone who fell into this trap before you. Dozens of people packed into empty rooms, trapped in dreams they never want to leave, while undead parasites feed on the prey trapped in their webs.
                      Maybe not exactly like that in game, with the exception of the final example, but along those tracks.

                      The reputation of vampires is that of banality, the death of dreams, but Ravnos are the opposite; Ravnos are bedlam for the denizens of the autumn world. They learn what you want and offer you the opportunity to leave this miserable world behind in exchange for a beautiful dream.

                      So, I suppose under this train of thought, they offer kine and kindred the world they want to see, keeping them peaceful and docile. For humans, that's the illusion of safety and blissful sedation within a predator's herd. For vampires, perhaps the illusion of the taste of food and the touch of sunlight (if the Ravnos remembers what that was like themselves, anyway), or the company of comforts and loved ones long lost to time and tragedy. The pacifying effect of the illusion of comfort and safety may also keep the Beast a little quieter and power hungry monsters from disrupting the peace, and stop elders from slipping into ennui, suicide and torpor.

                      I don't know what the word for this kind of role is... vampire therapists? Peace keepers?
                      I dunno, the idea of vampire jailors/care home managers is kind of growing on me.​

                          Thinking about the Ravnos Discipline spread, the thing that comes to mind for me, amusingly enough, is Entertainers.

                          The potential uses for Chimerstry as a means of providing entertainment are pretty much limited only by your imagination, and the limits the ST places on the Discipline. Illusions, light shows, traveling art displays, whatever you can think up.

                          As for Animalism, you now have your perfect beast-master for the Circus! A Ravnos animal trainer would probably make their mortal equivalent die of envy.

                          At first, I thought that Fortitude was going to be the odd duck here. But then I realized it was the best of all, the one for the most jaded of audiences, the ones truly desperate for something to shake them out of decades, or even centuries, of ennui: the Fire performers. Ravnos juggling burning torches, walking on red-hot coals, doing trapeze acts through flaming hoops. The kind of show that would require special preparations to make sure that the audience wouldn't suddenly erupt into mass Rötschreck; the performers would similarly need to be incredible specialists, with not only high skill in the Discipline but also high Courage to control the Red Fear themselves.

                          Obviously there are plenty of other functions they can fulfill as well, but that was the option that stood out to me and seemed somewhat unique.

                              The niche that the Ravnos fill is that of "dynamic group interactions" or in plain english, they have been designed to mess wiith the status quo when things get too boring. The Ravnos are to VtM what the Kender were to D&D groups.

                                      Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                                      Here's a question we may not have thought to ask: does a Clan/Bloodline NEED a social use? Or are they allowed to exist all on their own, for their own sake?
                                      Yes and no.

                                      Does a clan/bloodline need a social use in the sense that they play a role in world, not really. But does a clan/bloodline need to play a role in a role playing game because every clan/faction/sub group is supposed to provide support for a given playstyle, yes.

                                      I have brought this up before in other threads that several aspects of VtM design exist expressly to promote either story hooks or incite drama. If vampire society was designed to function first and be a game setting second, then the World of Darkness would be very different. Game design = social behavior trends, a good example of this is how games like D&D or Pathfinder have a murderhobo problem of killing everyone and taking their stuff. While WoD doesn't suffer from that to nearly the same extent if for no other reason than that XP isn't tied to a character's body count, instead it is tied to showing up and playing.

                                      If a clan or bloodline was released that didn't fit the coterie system or the political infighting default of the standard city power structure, most people wouldn't play it or claim it was broken.
