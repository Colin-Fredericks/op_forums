                            Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

                            The two tips I can think of now to give you.

                            First, think about what your characters really want. Not their goals, but their desires, that which makes them happy. Love, respect, family, peace. Some may desire power, those that find power itself intoxicating, but want is different from desire, most covet power as a means to have the lives they picture for themselves.

                            Second, picture how the natural consequences of their actions keep them from achieving those true desires, either by keeping them out of reach or by tarnishing them to an undesirable state. The character may hold to a number of beliefs that justify their actions and make them unable to see how they are sabotaging themselves, or may even see their own desires as a flaw, denying themselves out of pride.

                            An example would be a character that wants true friendship, but believes that only by achieving a high position they'll have the respect and attention necessary to make meaningful friends. Then just run in your mind the story of the character sacrificing relationships, making compromises with foes, and generally becoming and amassing around themselves the exact kind of people they despise.

                            Remember that "true desires" in this context are simple, but deep. They are the root cause of your beliefs, not the beliefs themselves. Frequently they are unconscious and they may be ill-defined, this is not a problem, as long as you can see the character setting the circumstances to make themselves miserable.

                            Let them free at the end to make others miserable as they lash out to the world, working their plans and beliefs, indulging their pride, and, secretly even to themselves, dumping their frustration on others. Know desires, let consequences run their course.
                            Thank you, I will start from here to re-visit my Tremere one shot set in the collapse of the Pyramid: wonderful ideas! Tremere are also very gothic in their themes, so I think they will fit.

                            Touchstones seems to be a useful tool in the context of a Gothic game, even though they are a bit difficult to use meaningfully in a single-session game, unless it's based on them.

                                        Regarding modern issues in the game, I am not opposed to that since they are necessary for worldbuilding but making them central?
                                        Well, making them central is a declared goal of the game in its every incarnation. But you already made it clear you don't like it and don't like either main genres, that's perfectly fine.

                                        I would like, though, that you don't call games that cater to social issues in derisive terms. That's unnecessary. Personally I'm not a fan of true noir, yet I'm not calling names on it despite being a popular take.

                                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                        Part of why Paradox is so international is they're alive and well....just not in America that just seemed to collectively say they were over in the 2000s.

                                        But Europe and South America?

                                        Huge.
                                        There are caveats, but yes.

                                        It's important to remember that both literary movements are very dynamic and can exist in many forms and shapes. They're also especially fertile to seed others. Noir itself is an example (already old, of course) of derived genre from both.

                                        But dead? They're among the most alive genres, despite a decline in the turn of the century. A huge number of media genres are subgenres to one of those two, as are many subcultures and a lot of people still cling to the main branches while giving audience to the sub-branches.

                                        Punk never became mainstream, and that's part of what makes it seems it is dead. The innate animosity towards posers since its inception makes Punk marginalized by definition, yet never dead, always present.

                                        Gothic did became mainstream and seeded larger culture far and wide. A huge amount of movies, series, books, comics and whatnot are extremely Gothic even when they don't say it out loud. Gothic played straight, parodied, deconstructed, reconstructed, reskinned, re-reskinned, Mixed with other genres, all the forms of Gothic you want.

                                                Originally posted by Grumpy RPG Reviews View Post
                                                Describing a city or the setting of a scene in lavish detail - a Gothic literature wallows in detail - will slow a game too much, and make it boring for the players. Likewise Gothic traditions often involve isolation will the VtM game involves a lot of socializing.
                                                But Gothic may and do indulge in socialization, as long as it isn't crowded. And the game expects vampiric population to be limited. Even metropolises have between 50 and 200 Kindred at most, many cities have just a handful.

                                                Of course, it isn't so obvious when you get the world-spanning conspiracies and sect conflicts of the full metaplot, but by most core descriptions you get that the bulk of Kindred society live in small, closed, incestuous circles, where you see the same faces over and over for decades.

                                                The standard setting is a city with a few dozen vampires and so little direct contact with the outside that visitors are an event on themselves. This is pretty Gothic. And the details you leave for props, something the game always encouraged, because here you got a point, but this is more of a media limitation than a genre contradiction.
