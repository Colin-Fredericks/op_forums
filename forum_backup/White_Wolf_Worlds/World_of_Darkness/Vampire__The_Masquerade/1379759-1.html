    I'll start with a list of phrases and words I wish were banned, under penalty of criminal law:
    • I would ban "passed away" to indicate death. There is no physical movement involved.
    • "Artisanal" is just a way of saying the maker lacks the cash to buy modern equipment. I hereby ban it.
    • "Hereby" is pretentious and unnecessary outside the unique context of a legal document.
    • "Unique" in any situation where it is not true, which is literally every time it is used.
    • "Literally", which is uniquely similar to the above word.
    • "Firstly". Adverbs are totally unnecessary in mostly every situation.
    • "Thusly", except when used by uneducated people trying to sound educated. We can't help it.
    • "Vayjayjay" and "peepee". Unless the speaker is three and still learning to speak, in which case the word shouldn't be used at all outside of a medical or hygiene context.
    • "Saga", except when used to describe stories from Old Norse, or comparing something to those. ex: "The story of how you got a flat tire on your way to work is not a saga."
    • "Enervate", "Irony", and "Nauseous". They don't mean what you think they mean.
    • "Awesome". Okay, fine, use it for a tornado you can see heading your way, but not for the nachos you ate while high.

    Which other words and phrases would you like to put on the no-no list? I'll promise to no longer use them, if you agree to avoid the terms I listed. F---dom fi---er will evermore spring from my mouth as "the eff-eff word".

    Before you do, though, I'd like to share one of my other pet peeves. In the Christian Bible (John 20:25, kjv), the Apostle Thomas says:

    ..he said unto them, Except I shall see in his hands the print of the nails, and put my finger into the print of the nails, and thrust my hand into his side, I will not believe.
    It has always bothered me that John doesn't believe in the resurrection. I mean, really, he was a writer of a gospel! If he showed a character talking like that, clearly John himself felt that way. Everything character portrayed by a writer is just a mouthpiece for the writer's views, and no character would ever say any little phrase that doesn't conform to the writer's views. So, John must have been a skeptic!

                        It is in a way. There's an Assad in the World of Darkness, but it's not the actual Assad; it's a fictional analogue existing in an alternate reality.

                        Using real-life events with real-life mass casualties as a backdrop for an entertainment medium is always going to have a problematic aspect. One can't avoid it entirely without turning the World of Darkness into the World of Candyfloss, and the bounds of acceptability are as much a matter of gut instinct as logic. For me it's partly about how many grieving relatives are still out there. I would recoil from using 9/11 (casualties c. 3,000) as part of a plot in a way that I probably wouldn't about the London Blitz (casualties c. 28,000). I see the logic of the Beckoning drawing elders to the middle east (region in turmoil hides their activities, plus it's the site of some of the earliest human civilisations and so a likely spot for the eldest vampires to have gone to ground), but it still makes me a bit uneasy.

                        So I remind myself that this is one of what GURPS would call the Infinite Worlds, and everything that happens there is fictional, even if it follows the pattern of events in reality
