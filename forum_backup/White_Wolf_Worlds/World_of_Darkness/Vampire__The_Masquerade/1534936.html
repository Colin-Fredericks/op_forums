    What are the Bogatyri?

    04-30-2024, 07:24 AM
    Or what could be the Bogatyri, considering we never had a proper answer to this one
    I'm curious of your ideas!

    So what we know:
    - The bogatyri (singular bogatyr) were personal guardians of the the Eldest
    - According Yorak -who know a thing or two - the bogatyri were not kine, Lupine, or Cainite.
    - Although they appeared mortal, they claimed lineage from Meru, a mystical mountain in India and supposed birthplace of the Arioi (what occult-scholars would later call the mythical Aryan race).
    (Worth tho mention, Meru was the mythical seat of the Wan Xian, their most holy site; it was a temple complex that served as the headquarter of the Wan Xian, right under the sight of the August Personage of Jade. Here, the rogue immortals turned on their own children, the Jin Hai, an act that finally caused their cursing. According the Kuei-jin, Meru was located on the Tibetan plateau.
    The Chi’n Ta believe that Mount Kailas is the remnant of Meru and that the Searing Wind attempted to take it with the aid of a corrupted Wu Lung, which sparked the Dragon River War.
    The Fera tell an entirely different story: According to them, Meru is the remnant of Toba, a supervulcano that erupted in Prehistory and unleashed the Banestorm. Toba lies in what is now a lake in Sumatra, meaning that the Wan Xian would originate south, not from the west of the Middle Kingdom)
    - Metal refused to cut their unblemished skin, while stone and wood forswore never to bruise them. (This is something VERY interesting and not unlike the Pact the True Fae in Changeling the Lost made with the elements of the word, except Iron who they pissed off... and a bit like Baldr, her mother took an oath from fire and water, iron and all metals, stones and earth, from trees, sicknesses and poisons, and from all four-footed beasts, birds and creeping things, that they would not hurt her son, but forgot the humble and a bit dumb mistletoe)
    - The bogatyri came into the Eldest's service when the Deluge washed a band of them against the Carpathians. In return for the Eldest's hospitality and-succor, they offered their allegiance
    - Each bogatyr possessed a skin pouch holding the flesh of the Antediluvian - when they fought for it, the skin pouch crawled over their faces and gave them the countenance of the Eldest. Their forearm bones extended into blades, while their tongues darted out like daggers.
    ​- When the Eldest fell to Samiel, the bogatyri scattered to the four winds to find it. They never returned, even after Yorak sent them messages that the Dracon had carried the Eldest embrionic form
    - Lambach Ruthven believes the bogatyri split and now guard the different manifestations of the Eldest. He claim he seen two under New York

    So... this is not that much
    They are an old race, that is for sure, existed before the Flood and survived it
    They appeared mortal but probably they were not
    They had strong mystical connections, matal, stone and wood outright refused to hurt them
    It is debatable if the ability to extended theirforearm into blades, like Barakka from Mortal Kombat, while their tongues darted out like daggers is a natural trait or the gift of the Eldest

    The word bogatyr is not of Slavic origin It derives from the Turco-Mongolic baghatur "hero", which is itself of uncertain origin. The term is recorded from at least the 8th century.
    In folklore, the bylinas (bylinas and starinas about bogatyrs and wizards, fairy tales and ritual lamentations – for a long time a whole cultural layer of the Old Russian song tradition remained alive in local villages) mention dozens of different Russian bogatyrs. Tales about bogatyrs, the strong men of the distant past, are among every Russian boy and girl’s favorite bedtime stories.
    There are three elder bogatyrs: Svyatogor, Volga Svyatoslavich and Mikula Selyaninovich.
    Svyatogor is the oldest and strongest bogatyr. Historians and philologists agree that Svyatogor is most probably a pre-Christian hero of Russian lore. Svyatogor is a huge giant who lives in the Holy mountains. When he walks, Mother Earth shakes, forests sway and rivers overflow their banks. Various bylinas tell that Svyatogor is either trapped in the earth (after trying to lift all the Earth’s weight collected in a bag) or immobilized in a stone coffin. In any case, Svyatogor is a personification of some ancient all-encompassing power. He’s too strong and too heavy for Earth to carry him.
    ​Volga Svyatoslavich is another pre-Christian bogatyr. His mother was a princess and his father was a magical serpent, hence Volga’s ability to transform himself into various creatures and understand the language of animals. There are many tales about Volga’s conquest of the foreign lands, Indian or Turkish.
    Mikula Selyaninovich is the ultimate peasant bogatyr. His patronymic, Selyaninovich, means ‘son of a villager’ and he’s invincible, because “mother Earth loves his kin”. It is Mikula who gives Svyatogor a bag with all the Earth’s weight, that Svyatogor can’t lift.
    All the other bogatyrs are considered to be “junior” bogatyrs. There are dozens of them in the byliny and, frankly speaking, most Russian people don’t remember them by now.
    Though not as heavily researched, the female bogatyr or polianitsa is a female warrior akin to the Amazons. While the female bogatyr doesn't quite match the men in strength and bravery, there are stories detailing instances where they save their husbands and outwit the enemy

    What do you think, what are the Bogatyri?
    Or what could be?
    Ever used one of these powerhouses? Even in modern nights something that immune wood, metal and stone is a formidable opponent...

      I never cared about the concept of the Bogatyri so they simply don't exist in my chronicles. They don't seem interesting to me. I don't think they add anything to the game that I like. In so far that their legend may exist, it's just more bullshit spouted by Tzimisce elders to make them and their creator seem more spooky and terrifying to the other clans.

      For some reason, the writers of the Revised era really, really liked the Tzimisce Antedeluvian so they created a lot of superfluous backstory for it. None of it interested me because quite frankly I've never liked the Tzimisce all that much. I never thought Vicissitude was that interesting, and in fact was counter to a game of gothic horror vampires. For years I wondered why this was even in the game until I finally discovered it was an attempt to emulate Necroscope style vampires. I still didn't like it, but at least I now understand why it was put in a game. Since I don't like Necroscope's conception of vampires, it was enough for me to decide to remove that Discipline entirely, give Protean to the Tzimisce, and adjust the setting accordingly. So that the Sabbat could still create the bizarre Sczlachta and Vozhd on occasion, I gave some Sabbat Tzimisce access to Bio-Thaumaturgy to create these Dr Frankenstein creations. Works good enough for me.

      I think the original details we were given about the Tzimisce Antedeluvian in 2e was more than sufficient for any ST. From the Lugoj entry in Children of the Inquisition we learned that Tzimisce had survived. From the Zantosa entry in the Storytellers Guide to the Sabbat we learned that family of Revenants were hiding the torpored body of the antedeluvian. That's more than sufficient for the ST to dream up plots if he wanted. I don't think all the other details we've been given make this vampire any more interesting, or make me want to use him more than any other antedeluvian.

      In so far that I use the actual bylinas and folklore figures in my chronicle, I treat the bogatyrs as I do most legendary heroes - they are most likely Mages of a now discontinued "heroic" Tradition that slew monsters and performed impossible deeds, using their Spheres to augment their bodies. Perhaps focusing on the Life sphere. The Bogatyrs, Beowulf, the Greek demigods, Melqart, the heroes of the Shahnameh, some of the Knights of the Round Table, etc. are all part of that. Those who aren't actually Garou that is or Mages of another tradition.

      I reject a lot of the - let's call it unique - contributions of the Revised era. What scratched their itch never did anything for me. So while there are some elements I used, I've created a lot of my own elements or built on certain things introduced earlier that weren't developed.

      I know it's not too helpful to you! So hoping you can get other posts that build on your interests.

                      Since Mt. Meru pops up in multiple cultures, there are quite a few options. If we take bogatyr as a sobriquet assigned to them in the slavic area, Wan Xian fit rather well as the heroic type - only, that doesn't really vibe with the character of the Eldest, imo. If they are paladins, I see them more as 'awful good' rather than 'lawful good'.
                      Meru is also the home of Devas and Asuras, the latter of which would fit better. There are types of Asuras that can change their shape, which would fit with the quasi-Vicissitude. The elemental immunity on the other hand makes it sound more like a Taoist thing, with them being something like offspring of the Heavenly Beasts.

                      In all cases that would make them some kind of demigod, so Amethyst's friend may actually be on to something with Exalted, considering the cross-over attempts between WOD and AOS some supplements made. Mechanically, I think they would make sense as the product of something ephemeral (Umbrood) and mortal. Hard to tell which one exactly.
