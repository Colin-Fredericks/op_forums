                                        Originally posted by Elphilm View Post
                                        I don't see anything ironic about that. To me, the 1st Edition core remains the beating undead heart of the game. I'm not going to tell anyone else how to run their games, but from my point of view, the seven original clans of the Camarilla trying to work together is what the game is all about. Even in 2nd Edition, the Sabbat and Independents are entirely supplemental material, meant for the occasional dip into ultraviolence before getting back to your typical chronicle.
                                        As a first Edition fan, the Seven clans are BITTER enemies that are fighting between the Anarchs and the Camarilla. They just have to work together when the Sabbat RARELY shows up.

                                        Characterizing a crossover splat like Under a Blood Red Moon, as much a Werewolf supplement as a Vampire one, as "one of the first major supplements" for Vampire seems disingenuous to me. The Players Guide to the Sabbat, for example, was released a full year before it, and is much more relevant to the discussion, even if I ultimately think it doesn't affect my point. Video game adaptations are even less interesting to me -- video games have always been casual with violence, regardless of the source material.
                                        Being a vampire is fundamentally about being a creature that is required to do violence as part of their existence. Even if they are the nicest vampire in the world, they must feed on the blood of the innocent (or not so innocent) and are one frenzy away from killing people. Their powers are also a constant temptation to do evil because they can warp minds and souls or physically hurt individuals.

                                        Using Elders and Princes as purely bad guys with zero nuance has no interest to me, so I can't really comment on that. But even if I agreed that Elders were all inhuman monsters, I would point out that a Humanity 0 vampire used to be a perfectly functional character -- as an NPC. The Humanity tracker was meaningful for player characters only.
                                        If you think evil means no nuance then I strongly disagree. Bluntly, I think the point of the game is to explore the concept of evil and damnation. Frankly, I think it's bad storytelling that darkness and corruption are something that you can't engage with as a player. Bluntly, I don't believe that player characters should ever be the GOOD guys in Vampire games. They can certainly TRY but a large part of what makes the game good is they're usually the lesser evil and possessed of their own terrible violent flaws.

                                        One of the things I encourage my players at my table to do is engage with this.

                                        "Who has your character killed that is an innocent person? Because at some point, unless you are REALLY wanting to play it otherwise, they have."

                                        * A bad feeding
                                        * A frenzy
                                        * Someone your sire gave you to feed on and drain dry
                                        * A case of mistaken identity

                                        All your characters are murderers. How you deal with that is part of the roleplaying exercise.

                                        Generally, I encourage my player characters to be 5-6 Humanity. They're the good vampires, actively fighting against evil. They're contrasted against the 2-4 Humanity vampires who are further down the slide to pure evil.

                                        If you want to play Humanity 8-10 then you should play a Thin Blood or high generation with deep self-loathing.
