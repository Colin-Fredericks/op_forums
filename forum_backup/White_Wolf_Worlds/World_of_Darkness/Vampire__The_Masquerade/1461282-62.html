                                I imagine that in the Sabbat, there are elders who are loosely aligned by Vauldrie (they may be cheating it) but are mostly spread out and keep to themselves rather like Camarilla vampires, and neonates who, if they aren't sharing havens, are likely in the same neighbourhood (in peace and with age, they spread out more). The Elders are actually quite wary of the packs of hungry neonates they have beneath them, so they like to play packs off one another, and they often feel threatened by Ancillae. A pack of neonates is a risk. A pack of Ancillae is a sword of Damocles. Elders will very carefully review anyone who makes it to 100. There are three possitibilities. Ideally, they'll try to form a great relationship with them in eager anticipation for them to enter the elder circle, Failing that; they'll reasign them to a nonthreatening or distant role. Third; They'll scheme to have the Ancillae killed.

                                Infernalists? Bringing them to your side isn't possible, their loyalty to a demon is paramount. Nonthreatening role? That's just delaying the rise of someone who'll become more powerful than you. Getting the infernalist killed is by far the best option.

                                        Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post
                                        I've never been a fan of the word "antitribu" myself, for anyone. I tend to think that citizens of each sect would consider themselves to be the real Gangrel/Nosferatu/Ventrue/etc, and members of the other sect to be the antitribu. In other words, both sides would use it as an insult for the other side. I've never bought that someone would call themselves something as cringey and self-demeaning as antitribu.
                                        I'm not sure I understand why antitribu would be a self-deprecating term (or even a cringy one, although that sounds more like a matter of personal taste).

                                        The Sabbat are ideologically committed to the destruction of the Antediluvians and all their non-Sabbat progeny. That is what being a member of an 'anti-clan' means. Saying you are Toreador antitribu is akin to saying "We are going to destroy Arikel and all of main Clan Toreador."

                                        At least, that is how I always understood the antitribu moniker in its original context (2nd Edition, Revised, and whatnot). There were always exceptions to the rule, of course, such as clan loyalty generally trumping sect loyalty in the case of the Nosferatu and Nosferatu antitribu. On the whole, though, claiming you were a part of an 'anti-clan' always came across to me like a proud declaration, not a sign of self-deprecation.

                                        I imagine this is why it was always emphasized from the start that "Lasombra antitribu" don't actually call themselves that. Non-Sabbat Lasombra are not ideologically committed to the destruction of the Lasombra Antediluvian (who was originally reputed to be already dead), nor to the destruction of all Lasombra in the Sabbat. The label "antitribu" simply does not apply to them.

                                                Originally posted by Elphilm View Post
                                                I imagine this is why it was always emphasized from the start that "Lasombra antitribu" don't actually call themselves that. Non-Sabbat Lasombra are not ideologically committed to the destruction of the Lasombra Antediluvian (who was originally reputed to be already dead), nor to the destruction of all Lasombra in the Sabbat.                                                 I would agree to this. But the most likely reason the Camarilla Lasombra were called the anti-tribu were for OOC understanding and "branding" that the label antitribu meant vampires who belonged to the opposite sect they were supposed to be. It was more a term for the readers/fans, not something best used IC by the vampires themselves.

                                                On a certain level, I think the Sabbat Lasombra and Tzimisce did not have the antitribu label because they had destroyed their antedeluvian and massacred most of the pre-Anarch Revolt clan members leaving the anarch Lasombra/Tzimisce triumphant. They were now the "new" "free" clan as the old had been destroyed. The other clans had not been successful at that so they were "just" antitribu. (I don't necessarily think it is needed to destroy a clan's antedeluvian to do so as some antedeluvians are popularly believed to be destroyed, but it would still be needed to destroy the existing structures and elders of the main clan so that the antitribu can be said to have triumphed).

                                                It's one reason I like the phrase Old Clan Tzimisce. It gets across the same point, but not using Sabbat terminology. As such, something like the Old Clan Lasombra in the Camarilla would sound better to my ears, but I'd prefer it to be something else that can get the same point across but not duplicate the term from the Old Clan Tzimisce. Maybe something like the Old Tradition, the Legitimate, Paragon, or the Venerable. Not quite as catchy as the Old Clan though.

                                                    Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post
                                                    I tend to consider I infernalism worse than Sabbat ideology, whatever the horrors of the sect they don't genrally represent an existential threat to life itself.
                                                    Demons aren't necessarily an existential threat, and most importantly the infernalist character may not think they are.

                                                    You are right that there are demons and demonologists that just want to end the world. Those are objectively worse than the Sabbat, even if you may argue that they're not more evil, since it depends on how you define evil.

                                                    But a demonologist that simply doesn't believe to be endangering life on this world, or even believe to be protecting it, isn't that kind of evil. And the demons are also a varied bunch, so they're not necessarily that kind of evil (or any kind, actually) either.

                                                    I already had a game where a character got pissed when he found out that he sold his soul to a demon that actually had high standards and didn't want to do evil for evil's sake, much less screw the world. The demon was just as disgusted with their relation when she found out her thrall was that kind of jerk, but they would have to work together anyway (we were both PCs, he was a Mage).

                                                    Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post
                                                    There are loosely speaking g 4-6 types of entity who could be consider demons in wod. 3 are committed to the annihilation of all life, 1 plans to turn it into hell on earth outstripping anything we can imagine the other 2 will just ensure you continue suffering after you die.....which the others often do as well. They're all worse than the Sabbat.
                                                    Not exactly that.

                                                    First of all, there is one type that is for PCs, and as every PC splat they can have a lot of other goals and considerations. You can't simply ignore them, and they're not doomed to evil.

                                                    Second, some of the other entities that can be considered demons don't have an actual agenda. As geared to cause suffering as they are, they don't always have it as a greater goal, as they don't have greater goals or have other, less comprehensive goals. Not all Wyrm spirits, as an example, serve directly the notion of bringing corruption to the entire world.

                                                    Third, in the end this is not about the reality of demons in the setting, but the beliefs of the infernalist character. Even the Sabbat is blind to whatever is the actual goal of those entities, so their beliefs on the matter are just that, beliefs. And for all purposes, unless the demon itself plays a larger role in a story, as happened with Kupala and entity in Hunedoara, the actual demon is largely irrelevant to all that.

                                                    By the way, for all its power the entity in Hunedoara was too big a loser to be a threat to the world. A demonologist that enters a pact in the firm and correct belief that they can gear their Investments towards the greater good, because the demon in question is an idiot, is worse than the Sabbat?

                                                    Originally posted by Elphilm View Post
                                                    The Sabbat are ideologically committed to the destruction of the Antediluvians and all their non-Sabbat progeny. That is what being a member of an 'anti-clan' means. Saying you are Toreador antitribu is akin to saying "We are going to destroy Arikel and all of main Clan Toreador."
                                                    If the term were consistently used as that it would make sense. An it does seem to be V5's take on the matter.

                                                    But the use of the term isn't actually that consistent. Some Antitribu didn't work like that at all, as was the case with Salubri and Nosferatu, and the Lasombra stand out if you claim this explanation, as they're in the sect's leadership and they definitely do hunt their non-Sabbat counterparts, so how can you say they're not Antitribu and the Camarilla ones are, instead?

                                                    The theory is good, but it doesn't match well with how it was actually used in the game. And I find that addressing this history matters even if I personally think the change is for the better (slightly).
