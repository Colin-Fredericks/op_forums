    Originally posted by Herr Meister View Post
    Hello everyone!
    So, this is a hot topic, somewhat. At least in every table I've been STing since I was a kid. How should we consider many of the functions of Presence to work, Presence is a discipline that's always bound to create some discussion about its funcioning, because on one hand anyone can resist it with a spendure of a Willpower point followed by a Willpower roll (difficult 8) and a single success, what many people(including MANY of my players that love to play social characters in my chronicles "since forever") complain a lot about, because according to them, it makes it "trivial to resist one of the most iconic vampiric powers". Also there's the fact of whether it should work in combat or not, some argue that you cannot use it during combat, others that it only shouldn't work after you attack someone and many think you could still use Majesty and attack a target and he would be still subject to its effects somehow. Also the fact that one needs to keep spending Willpower to resist it for as long as in the presence of the cainite using Presence.

    Throughout my years, I've been changing one thing or two regarding its effect and use, particularly during combat, where it's more problematic. I used to rule that only Dread Gaze and Majesty were possible to be used during combat, and Majesty, only as long as the one using it doesn't attack their targets. But, then I was arguing again with a friend of mine and that made me curious to see how other ST approach Presence and its use in combat. I'd be glad to hear anything you have to tell mir about how you rule it in your games.
    Disclaimer: I haven't taken the time yet to read the other comments in the thread. Just sharing my own take.

    I mainly play VDA20 and don't remember if it's framed differently in VM20.

    I take Presence to be a power by which a vampire supernaturally tampers with the victim's feelings, psychically altering them or projecting a certain type of influence that will affect people around him. It does not require the vampire to do or say anything particular other than maybe make a menacing face with Dread Gaze, but I like more the idea of being perfectly poised and chilled while turning on a victim's feeling of fear towards you. Way cooler. Be as it may, I rule that it can totally be used in combat, tampering with the victim's feelings directly and immediately.

    As for the roll to resist, I house rule that the number of successes is taken from the successes rolled by the vampire, not that a single success suffices to resist. The alternative just doesn't appeal to me at all.

            Originally posted by Herr Meister View Post

            I believe this way it gets too op, especially considering the limitations of Willpower dicepool.
            The point for me is that on average it should work.

            Let's look at Dread Gaze: its main benefit is reaped in the same scene and turn it is used, for example saving a social vampire from a physical confrontation. It targets one opponent at a time, so it does not help a lot when facing multiple opponents (a standard, not supernaturally powered, Intimidation roll might help more there - see below). If the opponent can resist for the turn or scene by spending a Willpower point and scoring a single success on difficulty 8, on average anyone with Willpower 5+ will succeed and attack the vampire for the turn, regardless of the vampire's trait ratings. Next turn the vampire might suffer from wound penalties, making things even harder.

            By contrast, the rules for social rolls are somewhat undefined, and in VDA20 there isn't even an explanation of how to physically intimidate a group rather than a single individual, which I consider a standard, common thing to try to do to avoid a fight, like when trying to scare off a gang, but the normal Intimidation roll 1 on 1 is Strength/Manipulation + Intimidate on diff.6 vs Willpower or Strength+Intimidate on the same difficulty. This means that, 1 against 1, a vampire will see his dice pool and traits count here more than on the Dread Gaze roll, and if it's a Strength roll he can even bear blood expenditure and Potence to bear. In VDA20, when trying to scare off multiple opponents, one could apply the modifier for fighting multiple opponents (+1 per enemy after the first, max +4), making things harder but possible, especially if one has automatic successes from Potence. The result is that, unless there are other factors and modifiers at play. In many cases mundane intimidation attempts have more chances of succeeding - especially for Potence weilders - than Dread Gaze.

            Sure, Dread Gaze might be active for multiple turns and force the victim to continue spending Willpower points until they leave the vampire's presence, but that's hardly relevant because often you really need it to work in the first turn.

                In general, the main problem with evaluating Presence and other socially-oriented Disciplines is that, while social actions should be central to the game (VtM is supposed to be more socially oriented than other game lines, since vampires are squashed physically by werewolves and I guess mentally by mages - whatever that means), the game system is underwhelming when it comes to defining social rolls and mechanics.

                Sure, that works if your playstyle is purely narrative, but then social Disciplines should be narrative, too. If you prefer a game where you can have a reliable system to compare the characters' capaciites and avoid dispute at the table, VtM's mechanics are too vague for mundane rolls and that spills into the comparison between using Disciplines and using mundane rolls, which then makes one question the usefulness of social disciplines and therefore the one thing vampires are supposed to be good at in the setting compared to other supernaturals. My VDA20 group (where I was a player, not ST) reached a near consensus that making social characters and investing in social traits and Disciplines other than Dominate was a waste of dots and experience... Sure, that has a lot to do with how the ST handled things, but it's a problematic outcome for a group that has been playing various editions of VtM and VDA for years.

                In Chronicles of Darkness there is a good alternative, in my opinion, because social rolls are better structured and many supernatural powers to influence others are framed as clear and sensible advantages to the factors in social rolls.

                    Well, I don't really know what kind of games you've been playing lol, but anyway, vampire games tend to bee more social than werewolf or mage, but that's just that, a tendency, not a rule. The whole "Vampion" trend the past already shows us that the game can be quite different, also when we have Dark Ages, an arguably more combat oriented setting, things can differ a lot. As I always say, the game is what the Storyteller and his group want it to be, some groups are more social, but even in those there are normally a few combats, etc. also even on a "Full Vampion" game, there might be a lot of social scenes and chapters, etc. again, the nature of the game is going to depend a lot on what the group want, there's no rule. From my observation, funny enough, social games tend to be the more present at one-shot stories normally found at events and the likes (especially live action events, naturally and so on), but "Vampion" tables tend to last longer, ofc this is my observation taken from my experience, but even in my experience it tends to vary a lot and I've seen A LOT of games over my 20+ experience, mostly as a Storyteller, but I also watched many of my friend's games and they would often invite me to play an antagonist on their stories, because since I was very young I was known for my taste for intense roleplaying and my friends liked to bring that to their tables sometimes (I even studied acting for a while when I was about 13-16).

                    Anyway, the thing is however our "approach" is, that is, the game style, be it more investigative, more social or more combat oriented, we need balance, however problematic the Storyteller System is, we can try to find balance in it in order to allow everyone to have fun and focus on what really matter, the story. For that I feel it's very helpful to have a stable and balanced system, so I think here in this example we're talking on this thread, perhaps a small buff compared to V20, making the resistance last 1 turn, instead of a scene, could be nice, but to ask for people to have more success on a WP roll, that they already spent WP to even attempt in the first place and that also has a target number of 8, is too much. I'm sure that 9 out of 10 storytellers would agree that it would make Presence borderline broken (but still not as broken as RAW celerity, probably the most broken thing in the entire line lol).

                    Tbh, the thing I really wanted opinion here is if it makes sense to be able to use Majesty after you attack someone, for instance, during a combat where you have already threatened someone's existence and then use Majesty and whether it should have an effect or not. This is probably the most polemic situation regarding Presence use in my experience and I'm still in doubt.
