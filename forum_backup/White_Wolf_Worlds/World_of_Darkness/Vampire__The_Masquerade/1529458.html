    (1st Edition/Classic Vampire) Reimagining Forged In Steel

    01-25-2024, 03:45 PM
    First off, shout out to CTPhipps for inspiring me to make this thread. I was re-reading his iconic "Let's Read Chicago By Night" on RPG.net prior to making this thread.

    One of the most iconic parts of the early days of VTM was the "Forged In Steel" mini-campaign setting included in the Vampire corebook in both the First and Second Editions of the game. It revolved around the starter setting of Gary, Indiana and the prelude to the iconic and extremely influential Chicago By Night chronicle setting.

    In fact, it even comes with the one-shot "Baptism by Fire", which in and of itself is also the prelude to the iconic adventure module Ashes To Ashes. And all too often, the Gary setting gets reduced to "And then they go to Chicago", and I get why. Gary is a small and run-down dying town and it wasn't meant to be a main campaign setting.

    At the same time, there's enough within the setting of Gary to run more than just a one-shot (usually some variation of "Baptism By Fire" or something), such as shutting down the Auction and taking out Williams, trying to resolve the potential issues of Detective Gregory Stephens knowing the truth about his sister, the threats that are William Shepherd and Sullivan Dane, gaining favor among the small court of Gary, taking down Modius and Juggler, etc.

    The "Blood at Dawn" scenario that came with the 1st Edition Storytellers Screen seems genuinely interesting and I get the feeling that it predates "Baptism By Fire" both in its development and when it's meant to take place in an "archetypical" Chicago By Night chronicle for First Edition.

    However, unlike the rest of Forged In Steel, Blood At Dawn never got reprinted for 2nd Edition. Everything else from the old Chicago By Night setting got reprinted in some form or another, whether carrying over into the 2e Corebook or appearing in the "Chicago Chronicles" omnibus.


    Side Note: I always imagined a "canonical"/archetypical Chicago 1E chronicle playing out in this order...

    Prelude > Blood At Dawn > Baptism By Fire > Ashes To Ashes > The Succubus Club > Blood Bond > Awakening: Diablerie Mexico > Milwaukee By Night > Under A Blood Red Moon. Dust To Dust works as an optional epilogue, of course.

    If I were going to order the specific adventures within The Succubus Club, I'd probably have either Annabelle's Party or Fundamental Differences be the first one and Death's Sweet Sting be the last one.


    Anyway, I'm thinking of possibly doing some alterations of the Forged In Steel setting, to give it some more meat and have the PC's do some more in Indiana before attending the party at Modius's decrepit mansion (and from there, heading to Chicago) and in fact, maybe the PC's actions in Indiana prior to the events of "Baptism By Fire" are what catches Lodin's attention to begin with.

    The problem is with Gary itself. There's more to do than what's in Blood At Dawn and the Forged In Steel section of the corebook, but not too much. Which is why I'm thinking of heavily fictionalizing the setting even more.

    In the 1st Edition iteration of World of Darkness, Chicago still has foggy cobblestone streets and old-timey cable car tramways. So it's not too much of a stretch to say that Gary (or an entirely different fictional Chicago suburb just over the state line in Indiana) isn't quite as dead as it was in 1992. This would make the city more diverse too, avoiding some of the potential racial implications as well.

    I've thought about replacing Gary with a fictional suburb combining elements of the real-life Indiana towns of Gary, Hammond, and Muncie alongside the more touristy parts of the Indiana Dunes and Lake Michigan. There's a part of me that wants to name this fictional town Gygax or Arneson, as a shout-out to CT Phipp's OG "Let's Read Chicago By Night 1E" on RPG.net, where he said that when he was a teen, he initially thought the town of Gary, Indiana was fictional and that the name was a subtle reference to Gary Gygax.

    The basic premise is that it's still a dying steel town in northern Indiana and a very short distance from Chicago. There was a massive US Steel foundry built near Lake Michigan that was a focus of a boom period in the 1950's and 1960's, but that the steelworks shut down around the 1970's. However, in the World of Darkness, the city government tried to offset this economic decline by promoting tourism to the natural beaches along Lake Michigan. The shift to tourism wasn't enough to prevent the town from declining nor did it prevent Modius from becoming an irrelevant husk of a Prince, but it was enough to prevent the town from becoming a near-ghost town like the actual Gary of real life.

    As such, the town is now a bizarre hybrid of a dying Rust Belt steel town and an overly kitschy tourist town (think along the lines of Branson, Asbury Park, Gatlinburg, Pigeon Forge, Seaside Heights, Myrtle Beach, or Atlantic City) with a boardwalk, arcades, casinos, mini golf courses, carnivals, an amusement park, pizzerias, ice cream parlors, dollar theaters, bowling alleys, the occasional novelty museum or roadside attraction, maybe a strip club. A lot of garish and gaudy colorful attractions contrasted with general urban decay and gothic malaise. Maybe the carnivals and theme parks try to play up a kitschy Halloween mall goth vibe? That would make things extra ironic.

      Hey,

      I think Gary, Indiana actually has a huge number of adventure hooks for play within it. Chicago is great and all but you could do an entire sustained chronicle within the smaller city without ever venturing forth into Lodin's domain.

      Some basic ones:

      1. Allicia and Modius: Allicia is a beautiful potential princess in a castle but also could well be going insane under the abuse of Modius. She could be a love interest or potentially just snap one day. Maybe she diablerizes her sire and tries to blame the PCs for it or asks their help covering it up.

      2. Evelyn and Juggler: Evelyn and Juggler have a very different relationship but a similar power dynamic. Evelyn thinks Juggler isn't her master, though, and it might be interesting if they try to rescue her only to find she's not interested or it's a test by Juggler. Gregory Stephens may also want to kill Juggler to "save" his sister and and end up getting killed.

      3. Mark Shepard and Gregory Stephens: The FBI agent and Gary Homicide detective may well be something that could cause all manner of problems for the Kindred in the city. Particularly since Modius isn't capable of covering up the dangers of a dead FBI agent.

      4. Michael as a Masquerade vilolation: Michael is a Malkavian with the mind of a child and absurd strength. It'd be very easy to do a "Mice and Men" style adventure where the Pcs must kill the High Humanity vampire to spare the Masquerade.

      5. Sullivan Dane stalks the players: He doesn't have to actually ever confront them but setting fire to havens, moving against them, and playing a game of cat and mouse might be far more interesting. Especially if he's more interested in seeing if they deserve to die or wokring against true evil.

          When I ran the Chicago chronicle, I just treated Gary like it was part of the Chicago setting. It was just one more neighborhood in a large city, albeit one that had its own "Prince" and could be a safe haven for any vampire that ran afoul of Lodin. As such, there were NPCs who often switched back and forth between domains, NPCs that mainly stayed in Gary but occasionally visited parts of Chicago (and the reverse - NPCs in Chicago who would visit Gary), and NPCs that stayed solely within Gary.

          The main hooks for PCs to want to stay connected to Gary in someway is 1) if you ever want to sire a childe, you need to be on Modius's good side (which is easily done) because Lodin isn't letting anyone sire outside of his brood, 2) Gary seems to be the place where shady shit goes on like the Auction which a lot of Chicago Kindred utilize, 3) it's a good place to lie low if you ever piss off Lodin or his minions, 4) it's the de facto headquarters of the Anarchs, and 5) anyone who wanted to arrive in Chicago and not owe something to Drummond (coming by rail) or Tyler (coming by airport) best utilized Gary as their initial stop going to Chicago (especially since Lucian was based there). Even for people just driving on the highways to there, I always told them that the place where to first go was always some run down hotel in Gary. That hotel's name was just open knowledge in the vampire community. If you go there, it automatically puts you on the list to meet the Prince and obtain Hospitality. If you decided to go elsewhere, there was always the risk of being mistaken.

          I had the South Side of Chicago be somewhat in contention. Lodin had the edge over the Anarchs there, but not decisively. So if a PC had any dealings on the South Side, there was reason for her to have to deal with people based in Gary to some degree. In contrast, Downtown, North Side, and the west were very strong in Lodin's camp. I planted some cool things in the South Side just to encourage players to want to do things there and thus keep in contact with the Kindred of Gary.

                              I don't think there's much difference between 1e and early 2e. There are only some minor game mechanical changes, and the setting is clearly the same. I think the real "edition" differences in pre-Revised Vampire is between the era of Andrew Greenberg (1991-1994) as Developer and post-Greenberg development (1995-1997). That second half of VtM 2e saw some reaction to some features people associated with the Greenberg era - fishmalks, vampions, and the controversy over Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand - as well as incorporation of new canon being created in the Vampire: the Dark Ages spinoff. It didn't result in continuity changes like what Revised did, but there was a shift in some tone, an increasing emphasis on battles between vampire sects rather than exploration of the world by newly made vampires, and increasing pushback by the gameline against "wrongfun" and trying to correct things they saw certain players doing which they thought clashed with the setting.

                                                          Apologies for the double post and double quote, but I'm genuinely curious about something.

                                                          Were Children of the Inquisition and A World of Darkness First Edition or Second Edition books? Both came out in 1992 and while most of the 1992 books for Vampire were 1e, a few of the books in the latter half of 1992 were 2E such as the V2 Corebook, Clanbook Brujah, and The Player's Guide To The Sabbat.

                                                          The reason why I ask is because when I look up those two books in particular, I get mixed messages on whether or not they were 1e or 2e. I don't own either book so I can't say for sure since 1e books have a very distinct look and feel to them. I'm going to assume that A World of Darkness might be one of the last 1e books, given there was a Second Edition version released later on, but Children of the Inquisition seems like it might be one of the first 2e books since it supposedly introduces some characters who'd later become part of the metaplot in late 2e and Revised.

                                                          With the wider Greenberg Era, the Vampire metaplot largely was confined to the Chicago area and this was still mostly true up until the very end of 2e/start of Revised.

                                                          Most of the major metaplot characters introduced in 1e were either only really part of that Chicago metaplot prior to V5, maybe got brief references in later Revised books (like the traveling Succubus Club rave started by Helena) or didn't become major metaplot figures until V5 (Carna and Agent Shepherd probably being the most notable examples)
