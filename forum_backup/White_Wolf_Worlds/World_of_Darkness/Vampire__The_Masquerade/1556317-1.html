    Originally posted by Gryffon15 View Post
    I also think that regional source books or just regional worldbuilding in general would be really helpful in general and that’s a big part of what inspired this project/thread for me.
    The problem with this is that overall it's hard to make good regional books/worldbuilding without local writers, and finding local writers is hard. Ask anyone who actually live in the places depicted in almost every By Night or Rage Across book.

    One of the reasons I don't forgive WoD for those terrible books is that at the time Shadowrun was doing exactly that: German: Deutschland in den Schatten (in English something like "Germany in the Shadows", but translated just as Germany Sourcebook) was literally given to the local fanbase for them to write, and it was one of the best area books for an RPG.

    So while I think more such books would be nice, I don't think White Wolf at the time should have done them even more than they already did.

        Handing regional sourcebooks to local fans would be the ideal, but it does present certain practical difficulties of it’s own (For example, Germany is a country with a whole nation to draw on, but what if there simply aren’t very many fans of the game in a potentially-interesting setting?) and rather dismisses the possibility that even a flawed resource may be interesting enough to attract the very fans who might improve on it.

        On a more cynical note, there’s never a guarantee that fans will be able to equal their local enthusiasm with their writing skills!


        I do, however, agree that a regional sourcebook will generally benefit when the authors consult local fans and that this will definitely be of help to non-resident authors trying to channel some serious Local Character.

            Speaking of local character, it struck me that whilst a ‘Lone Star By Night’ sourcebook might be a trifle too ambitious, a city sourcebook in the classic VAMPIRE style might be more bite sized (No pun intended).

            When picking a suitable focus for such an effort, my suggestion would be to look up lists of the biggest cities in any given area and then look up the most dangerous cities in the said area- because logically a city where a population of vampires prosper is one where the blood flows most freely in the streets.


            Based on some internet research Dallas (13), Houston (1), San Antonio (6) and Corpus Christi (7) all appear on the list of the most populous settlements in Texas AND on a list of the least safe cities in the Lone Star State (Dallas, Houston and San Antonio all appear on the list of the most populous metro areas to boot.

            From this shortlist, San Antonio particularly leaps out (Based partly on it’s strong association with the birth of Texas, but also because it’s Spanish name helps remind us that the roots of Texas lie outside the English-speaking world), if only because one can readily imagine it’s association with all the fame of the Alamo making it an excellent ‘neutral ground’ for meetings between vampires even before the city itself became populous enough to support a substantive resident population of Kindred (and tensions stemming from the clashes likely to ensue when a local vampire population grows large enough to exert Praxis over formerly-neutral ground could lend interest to local politics).

            Also, the title ‘GRINDHOUSE ALAMO’ suggests itself.

                                                    Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                                                    I’d suggest that cover each and every State might get a trifle repetitious past a certain point - perhaps going by regions (New England, Mid-Atlantic, Upper South) could be the way to go?
                                                    I would quite firmly disagree with this. One of the key problems with Vampire, particularly in later editions, is the dominance of what might be called “generic city by night”. We have the notion of the pompous Ventrue prince, rebellious Brujah rebelling, sneaky Nosferatu spies in the sewers spying, and so on. But look at the early location sourcebooks for Vampire and you see far more variety and creativity than what is now represented in the core rulebooks, partIcularly in V5. A good location sourcebook should represent specificity and depth in representing facets of a game world. The World of Darkness has existed for more than thirty years, and, even just working on pre-existing material, there are an incredible number of different domains which one could use to explore different aspects of the Vampire world.

                                                            Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                                                            I’d suggest that cover each and every State might get a trifle repetitious past a certain point - perhaps going by regions (New England, Mid-Atlantic, Upper South) could be the way to go?
                                                            I agree with James_Willoughby.

                                                            Doing the 50 states wouldn't just be an opportunity to better describe the distinctions of each area, which are many and worthwhile on their own.

                                                            More importantly, even, it would be an opportunity to do what WW couldn't at the time and explore how the fictional elements may vary, show how splats in different locations face distinct problems and find distinct solutions even to similar problems. As I said in another thread, we have a very, very big problem with four particular books, not because they're bad (although lack of locals and all that, they're quite messed up), but because they became staples for things that were supposed to not have a standard. A lot of how people think a Vampire city works isn't what's on the core books (especially the first two editions), but those four specific structures, rinse and repeat.

                                                            Also, I don't think dedicating each place to a main splat is a good idea. Every splats is everywhere from their respective perspectives, they don't usually limit each-other's presence even when they're not on good terms, as they spend most of their time hiding from each other. A state book may not have every splat cited, but should try to cover some bases and be clear that each one is, for their own account, the masters of the area. Or, if not, for quite specific reasons.
