        Also, most people don't want power over others, they want enough power to secure comfort and safety and positive emotions and whatever.

                                    Not really. The Ivory tower is really a hands off form of goverment, with 90% of power in the hands of local municipalities. The Cam operates on the consent of it's princes and has limited ability to enforce it's wishes among the thousands of princedoms, and essentially can't be too domineering lest princes succeed. Another issue is, obviously collecting your data so that they can watch for things that will alert mortal authorities who are also collecting data is... extremely counterproductive, and absolutely nobody would agree to it. What you're describing is farcical and any prince that tries such a thing would go down, and while you might like that tone with your game, because moustache twirling evil-stupid villains are fun, it doesn't sit well with verisimilitude.

                                    The thing is, the Camarilla is, in fact, a sect that was created as a necessity for Modernity. It's not a coincidence that the Camarilla, Sabbat and Giovanni were all created at the start of modernity, and that there aren't medieval or classical sects (cults excluded). The founders of the Cam were in a time of relatively rapid technological change: Crossbows were out, Guns were in, cities were urbanizing rapidly, merchants became powerful and the world became more connected, fuedal systems declined, globe-spanning empires became a thing, and a whole load of other massive changes. The Anarchs popped up because they adapted first and grew from these new changes, and the Camarilla was created to steer things in a favourable direction for vampires during these times. While the Cam gets strained in the much-more rapidly changing 20th/21st century, it shouldn't just fucking break, because it was literally designed for modernity and honestly, computers aren't that hard to wrap your head around, especially if you're immortal and loaded.

                                                        Re: Vampire: The Masquerade and Hunters

                                                        Hunters have been a major part of Vampire: The Masquerade but were weirdly the one thing excluded from Beckett's Jyhad Diary and that was really strange. I also felt The Hunter's Hunted II downgraded their dangerousness when Project: Twilight was upgrading it. Hunters are one of the very basic things about vampire mythology and we have them in Carmilla and Dracula as a major part of the storyline. Polidori's The Vampyre has a complete moron who utterly fails at hunting but that's also technically part of the story.

                                                        I honestly think the Second Inquisition is there not to utterly upend the series but just to make the Masquerade seem more important as a concept. Some players acted like the worst stereotypes of Sabbat because they didn't think Hunters or the Prince were a proper threat. Giving the government flame-throwers, thermal vision, and M4A1s to unload into vampire hunters makes them less of a joke.

                                                        Project: Twilight had a pretty good write-up of the government becoming more aware and more dangerous to vampirekind that could easily have led to the Second Inquisition. The Masquerade being broken was something very possible. V20, in one of the few critcisms I have of it, really didn't like this. It's why we had ANARCHS UNBOUND have the idea that suddenly Blood Magic was powerful enough and widespread enough that the internet couldn't be used to track down vampires.

                                                        I know when vampires are exposed, there's no game. It's Game OverTM but I felt that was a silly solution. Indeed, the handling of humans is about my ONLY complaint about V20 that was otherwise an immensely solid game, Anarchs Unbound was a great book and helped reform the Anarchs from the idiotic way they treatyed by Revised.
