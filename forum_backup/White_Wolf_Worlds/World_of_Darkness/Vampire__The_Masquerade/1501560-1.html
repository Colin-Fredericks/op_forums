    Originally posted by Gryffon15 View Post
    I’ve also been chewing on Black Fox’s posts about the Triad and I think it leads to some interesting ideas for New England as a setting. If I’m making any incorrect assumptions in your reasoning Black Fox, please correct me.
    I think you have some of the general conflicts right, but it is not how I would have articulated it.

    I do agree that the early "Ventrue domination" of New England implies something of New England being yet another fief of Mithras at the time since it's most likely Ventrue from England populating the region in early colonial times. Likewise, both Roman Pendragon and William Biltmore are shown to having angered the English Venture and their overlord Mithras, and them coming to New England and overthrowing the New England Ventrue implies a continuation of that rivalry/enmity.

    But I'm not sure how much residual loyalty the contemporary Ventrue of New England would have to Mithras. First, those ties to Mithras would have been severed 200-250 years ago. There isn't anything in Dark Colony that implies Mithras has been attempting to regain control of the region. Sure an ST could add that, but I don't think it adds anything. The situation is already complex, and I would personally prefer the local Ventrue to now being on their own. Second, Mithras has been absent since WWII, and certainly did not play any role in the setting at the time Dark Colony was published. I think Mithras would have more immediate concerns on his mind than faraway New England though he would welcome a Ventrue return to power there.

    I do like the implied idea that almost all Ventrue of the New England area are probably of the lineage of Mithras, and that could give an extended family atmosphere of the clan in the region.

    As for the Triad, I hold certain opinions. The first is that the term "Triad" is never used by actual characters. People do know there is some sort of alliance between Pendragon and Biltmore, but Warwick is generally unknown and a mystery. His ties to the others are not known. The term is best used only by the ST to understand the secret alliance.

    Now both Pendragon and the Malkavian Prince of Boston are a kind of feudal overlord of other minor Princes (of their own clans), but that is not unusual within the Camarilla. Even Lodin had some authority over Modius in Chicago By Night albeit Modius did retain certain privileges. And Mithras as Prince of London acted in the same way to the other fiefs of England.

    But I don't think these two groups of struggling with one another. Although that is an option as you pointed out.

    Dark Colony has a habit of saying "the Camarilla" when they really mean "the Inner Circle". That was quite frequent in early VtM sourcebooks. So I don't mind the idea that Pendragon is so dominating that the Justicars aren't really supervising him. Petrodon is all the way out in Seattle intriguing against the Anarchs, and Xavier wanders the Midwest and South, holding his conclaves in New Orleans. Both seem to ignore Pendragon and allow him to dominate New England, either because it is cut off by the Sabbat or that they don't want to rile the hornet's nest. But the idea of Princes upset over the Inner Circle interfering in their business was lead out in the earliest corebooks. With Pendragon we just have the idea that he's been more successful than most at ignoring them.

    Therefore, it might be interesting to present the opposition to Pendragon (through the Kindred of Liberty) as seeing the Camarilla not as a source of repression, but one of liberty that could be curtailing Pendragon's personal power. That might be a nice twist on the normal things. (Although I would probably change the name of the KOL as I don't think it's as clever of good as the writers of Dark Colony thought). It would be a good way of distinguishing the KOL from the Anarchs. The KOL is trying to figure out a way to strengthen the normal tools of Camarilla governance (Justicars, Archons, Conclaves) while the Anarchs do more typical Anarch things.

    So in terms of Camarilla politics, I would emphasize the resistance towards the alliance of Pendragon and Malkavians, while also emphasizing how the Triad's members are beginning to turn against each other, while everyone also fights the Sabbat, Lupines and other external enemies.

    Originally posted by Gryffon15 View Post
    All told this would give the kindred of New England a political landscape more analogous to a fusion of the English Civil Wars and American Revolution then decisively being the latter, but I think that makes things more interesting.
    That is an interesting way to put it, but I agree that it's not really a problem. I am actually not a fan of the idea of playing out a second American War of Independence in New England.         Here are my comments on the Malkavians.

        First, I really like the idea that Boston is somehow a base of Malkavian power in general. I don't know how appropriate that is for Boston, but someplace should be like that.

        Second, I also like the concept of a Welsh bard who somehow uncovered the truth about vampires and was embraced as a result, so William Biltmore is present. But I have him as the Prince of Boston, since he's not old enough to really be a power behind the throne. And if I want to move up the Triad to gaining power in the Revolution, he pretty much needs to become Prince of Boston at that time. He fled Britain to escape the wrath of Mithras, and as a result was a proponent of the American Revolution. As my own addition, I would also make him involved with a scholar of the Arcanum in Boston. Because the Arcanum Foundation House in Boston should be an important part of the setting, and I like the idea of setting up a stereotypical Anne Rice protagonist becoming involved with a character from the Talamasca. As a bard, Biltmore also fits that scholar archetype.

        Third, I am keeping Quentin King just because I like the name. Instead of making him a separate line from Biltmore, I made him Biltmore's grand childe (Skarvan is Quentin’s sire and Biltmore’s childe.) Rather than be from New York, Quentin was a police detective in Boston. As Biltmore is rarely seen and Quentin King handles almost all public tasks, many Boston Kindred feel he is the Prince. Biltmore’s name is known from history and some older Kindred insist he is the actual Prince, but since they only see Quentin King there is considerable debate whether “Biltmore” still exists. Technically he's just Biltmore's Seneschal. But Biltmore doesn't see anyone except Quentin (I would probably make Biltmore's Derangement Paranoia). So many people call Quention "Prince."

        The Six Knights were not embraced by Skarvan, but by Quentin himself, and is simply the name for his brood of childer (embraced for their rescue of him). They were part of Skarvan’s herd though. Per Dark Colony, one of these is his chief aid in Boston, and the others are “Princes” of minor cities. Rather than the silly names of Dark Colony, I would give them real personalities while keeping up the Arthurian resemblance. They’ve adopted name associated with the Arthurian legend, but are not obvious about it - Hector, Lionel, Mark, Elaine, Lynette, and Percy. They do see Quentin as a kind of Arthurian figure destined to lead the Malkavians to greatness, or at least to make Boston a new "Camelot". They're big fans of the Lerner & Lowe musical.

        One major problem with the character of Quentin King is what exactly is his derangement. He seems to be fairly competent, having been a fairly good fighter of the Sabbat early on. He is also mentioned as being an outstanding art critic, and as a result has become a favorite of the Toreador. So whatever he has cannot be too debilitating. I think in Giovanni Chronicles IV, it adds he has the Derangements of Schizophrenia and Delusion, but I don't think that makes much sense. Despite the Arthurian aspects of his character, that did not come from him, but from the Six Knights.

        So I think I'd make him an example of intellectualization. His defense mechanism is to shift into abstract discussions and logic about unpleasant things instead of dealing with things emotionally. This has helped him succeed as a "Prince", and it's one reason he's become an insightful art critic. The problem is that he is often cold and distant. And sometimes in times of great stress instead of acting decisively about a problem (like a Sabbat attack), he can waste time by over analyzing a problem instead of acting.

        I would not roleplay him so much like Mr. Spock but as Sherlock Holmes (which also harkens back to his background as a police detective.) I'd use a lot of Jeremy Brett's portrayal of the Great Detective as his roleplay inspiration. He doesn't have a Watson, but he does have the Six Knights.


        However, I do have one major problem. How can we deal with his weird connection to the Arthurian mythos and not make mention of a man with the last name of Pendragon? They have to be connected somehow. So far my best idea is that the Six Knights know that Arthur's (Quentin's) father Uther (Roman) is not a good king, and that Uther must go down before Arthur can become the "true king". So they are manipulating events so that Quentin can eventually replace Pendragon as a the paramount figure in New England. The Six Knights tend not to think logically, but symbolically.

        Incidentally that would make the Welsh bard Biltmore into a Merlin figure. If we continue to go down that route, they might rename many vampire characters into an Arthurian figure if for some reason they think he fills that roll, and then try to manipulate that person into fulfilling their legendary destiny. That might lead to a fun scene where PCs hear these NPCs talking about "Uther", "Merlin", "Mordred", "Bors", etc. in a way that seems utterly confusing, not realizing they are speaking in code. It would make them seem more delusional than they truly are. But a canny PC might pick up on what they really mean.

        I have not played the Swansong computer game, but I hear it does provide a loose continuation of the Boston in Dark Colony. And by loose I mean while it acknowledges past canonical characters in its history, it pretty much throws all continuity out and starts completely from scratch. It is pretty much its own thing, and ignores any real heritage from Dark Colony. However, I would try to salvage any names and concepts that I find inspiring. Unfortunately, I don't there is much there for the Malkavians. The PC of Leysha and her relationship with her childe Halsey is OK, but rather generic for New England. That could be any Malkavian in any city. The same with making her a "seer", her past unknown, and having her hail from a completely different city (Lyon, France). She could be acceptable provided we move her embrace to Boston and provide an actual background for her. Likewise, we can always keep the name of Richard Dunham and change his generation and lineage accordingly, perhaps make him the real sire of Leysha. But this is more easter egg type stuff than really utilizing the characters as depicted in the game. I'll mention Swansong more when I talk about some of the other clans.

            Speaking of the Malkavians, this reminds me of a thought I’ve had about New England as a setting, which I mentioned in the intro post but might as well go into a bit more detail about here.

            So, to start off, this concept is heavily inspired by Alan Moore and his Lovecraft Cycle, but not just that. I’d also say that things like Kubrick’s adaption of ‘The Shining’ and the concept of Mysteries in Mage the Awakening have a role in forming this idea as well.

            Basically the idea is to have one of the plot hooks or mysteries of New England as a setting have to do with some sort of effect of the Collective Unconscious. That the region has some sort of cosmic rippling effect of narrative and symbolism which overlays eldritch secrets and mysteries. I mention it here because alongside the Nosferatu & Tremere being served by the investigatory angles of this concept, it naturally flows through the Malkavians. In my mind, it would serve as a mysterious but evocative force, which helps to explain how Clan Malkavian maintains its prominence in Boston. That the Arthurian Mythos the clan uses, is itself one set of symbolic masks which serve the purpose of being more comprehensible representations of a larger phenomena which the Malkavians are sensitive too, in particular with Biltmore.

            One of the other reasons I’m fond of the idea of an eldritch under current for New England, aside from feeling it compliments the region’s feel fairly well, is that I also feel it provides a good basis for drawing other threads into play. It gives another reason for the occultists and theologians of the Sabbat in Montreal, the Arcanum, and sorcerers (& Mages, if an ST feels so inclined) to be active in the reason and to instigate plot hooks which arise from the efforts to unravel the Mysteries found within New England.

                            One issue with Dark Colony is that it does not do a good job distinguishing what are the different domains in the region. It has a lot of information about that, but it is scattered all over the place. There is no map showing them. And it lacks a good coterie chart of the different domains and how the Princes view each other. But this is information an ST definitely needs. So what happens if we look at a map, look up some quick populations in Wikipedia, and compare it to Dark Colony?

                            Vampire Population per Dark Colony is 152. This represents the number of vampires the mortal area can “support”, but then said the actual number can be higher! (How exactly can there be more vampires than the area can support?) Breakdown is Connecticut 35, Maine 20, Massachusetts 60, New Hampshire 18, Rhode Island 10, and Vermont 9. These are spread over 24 listed cities, but even here it says not all cities may have vampires. This number represents a higher population than it should be, so it likely presents both Camarilla and Sabbat vampires. So an ST has a lot of work to do to sketch this out, and the demographic data isn't very helpful.

                            Massachusetts
                            The biggest metro are is, of course, Boston and would probably have around 50 vampires. Another 10 could be in the metro area of Worcester which is far enough away to be a distinct metro area and a sizable population. This perfectly matches Dark Colony's suggestion of 60.

                            Connecticut
                            The 35 vampires of Connecticut broadly reflects what we would expect from the three metro areas. And let’s face it, these could easily be considered to be one domain. Hartford - 12, Bridgeport / Stamford Metro - 9, and New Haven / Waterbury - 8. This is 29, near but under Dark Colony's figure. I'd probably just put the vampires of 30 and call it a day.

                            Rhode Island
                            Rhode Island surprisingly should have more vampires than Dark Colony listed. Providence Metro could field 16 vampires. I think this includes Newport. Much like Connecticut, it's best just to see this as one domain.

                            Vermont
                            Dark Colony is schizophrenic on this. While giving us 9 vampires, in the separate section of Vermont it implies vampires here are rare. The figure of 9 probably represents a Sabbat pack that visits the reason from time to time. We know from Rage Across New York that Vermont is home to the Morningkill Royal House of Silver Fangs, and they probably exterminate any vampires here who foolishly stay here. Vermont just lacks the population and density to house vampires per the conventions of VtM. It's largest metro area, Burlington, only has 200,000.

                            New Hampshire
                            The four cities on New Hampshire are probably only around 5-6 vampires with only Manchester having more than 1 using the standard Camarilla one vampire for every 100.000 mortals in a metropolitan area. Even with one or two more Camarilla vampires out there, that means the other dozen or so must be Sabbat packs.

                            Nashua/Manchester/ Corcord is best seen as one “metro” zone based on population density, so is probably one domain. It can also be seen as the northern end of the Boston metropolitan area. Concord is actually a bit isolated from Nashua/Manchester, but it is the seat of Pendragon. So he’s like the tip of the spear.

                            Maine
                            ​Maine is another mostly rural and wilderness state with low population, but it has two metro areas. Portland could have 6 vampires and Bangor 2. Although canon says all of Maine is Sabbat, the Portland metro area is right next to the New Hampshire "metro" area with Portsmouth being a link. It's also not too far from Boston. So I would make Portland part of Pendragon's domain, and possibly make it one of the more contested areas between him the Sabbat. Bangor and backwoods Maine is Sabbat territory when they're not being killed by Lupines.

                            So we have five domains total. Who are their Princes and about how many vampires should be in each domain?

                            Boston - Biltmore/Quentin King is Prince. 50 vampires total, a large number of which should be Malkavians. I would make them the largest clan in the city. Because Quentin and even Biltmore are a bit non-traditional Malkavians, I'd use some variety in describing the other Malkavians. They would not be as loyal to Quentin as the Six Knights, but they like that one of their own is Prince.

                            Worcester - Sir Praxis, one of the Six Knights per Dark Colony. The other Knights’ principalities are likely better seen as personal domains held in the domain of the Prince of Boston. They aren’t large enough to house multiple vampires and are either in or close to the Boston metro area. 10 vampires total. If we want to keep the area as a stronghold of Malkavian power, the Malkavians should be the largest clan here too.

                            Providence - Warwick, though it is unclear whether this is publicly known, but if not who do people believe rules in Rhode Island? Obviously the Nosferatu dominate here, but I would also place the main Tremere chantry here although the chantry does have safehouses in other cities, particularly Hartford. 16 vampires total.

                            Hartford - Upton Rowlands, officially “Prince of New Haven and Hartford” per the book. I think it sounds better than Prince of Connecticut. 30 vampires. The Ventrue are the largest clan here, although that clan does have members in a few other cities that have much less power than would usually be expected for them. In so far there is a traditional Camarilla domain in New England, it is here.

                            Southern New Hampshire/Portland - Pendragon as "Prince of Concord", which is more of a nominal title. He's prince of a lot more than just Concord. Other vampires in New England understand it is a courtesy title that does not reflect the actual reach of Pendragon's domain. There's around 12 or so vampires in this area. Most of them are probably Brujah loyal to Pendragon. Maybe half of them are his own brood or lineage while the others are just loyal followers. Pendragon’s New Hampshire just does not seem large enough despite his personal importance in the region. A rural based vampire is fine, but the Brujah are not the most likely clan for it. So Pendragon’s Brujah are probably crammed into the Nashua/Manchester area (or Portland) and merely visit the rest of the state’s towns on occasion.
                            ​
                            ​That is around 118 vampires - Camarilla and Anarchs. That leaves the other thirty to forty vampires in the region to be Sabbat who probably spend more time fighting Lupines than they do the Camarilla. When they do fight the Camarilla, Pendragon's group of Brujah warriors probably win more often than not, and they should be a very effective coterie of fighters.

                            With this, an ST can start organizing his domains and building populations for each one. While the standard practice is to include all Camarilla clans in a single domain, I think it is best to keep some domains segregated. Only Boston is really big enough to hold all clans. Pendragon's domain should just have Brujah. Ventrue are mostly kept in Connecticut. Tremere should only be in Providence (under my chantry at Brown University idea) and Connecticut. Providence is the center of the Nosferatu, but they should probably be represented in other domains as well (though possibly in secret).

                                        On the subject of New England, I had been doing some reading through the books to learn more about the First Sabbat Civil War, as it seemed like it was situated to be correlated with the United States Revolutionary Wars and their aftermath, so considering the events of SCW1 would be relevant to the discussion of New England. After doing said reading, I now thinks it’s a “yes and no” situation.

                                        I do think that when one looks at the old Player’s Guide to the Sabbat and it’s history section, it’s pretty clear that SCW1 is chronologically correlated with the US Rev. The premise seems to have essentially been this: the new world was first been the domain of the still largely Anarch-ic Sabbat, they had attempted to harness the power of the US Rev to further their own ends, but disorganization led them to succumb to infighting and ultimately cause more domains to be lost then were won. This doesn’t seem to quite line up with what we end up learning about.

                                        For one thing, many US city books have the Camarilla have pretty much been there from word ‘go’, either with ambitious neonates/ancillae or roaming elders. However I do think that in many cases, in the earliest nights, we can assume that most of these are paper princes. Theoretically those early colonial domains had Camarilla princes but in effect the Sabbat packs who roamed the continent could do as they pleased, mostly with impunity. Montreal by Night (and New York by Night to an extent but it’s a bit confusing to read) show that pretty well I think; although perhaps this is less universal and more meant to be an indictment on the French Camarilla as being ineffective in its colonial enterprises (which might also take on some interesting implications when considering PGttS’s claim that France has the largest number of Sabbat in Western Europe). So we can imagine most of North America, with some exceptions, being de facto Sabbat albeit the occasional de jure Camarilla prince. Frankly I imagine that in these early nights, savvy would-be-princes would have an easier time calling themselves Anarchs or Autarkis and providing basic conveniences to wandering packs then claiming to be Camarilla.

                                        Mexico City by Night however I think has one of the more interesting cases to consider. If it is to be believed then it seems to me what was called the ‘First Sabbat Civil War’ was more a consequence of the political circumstances in New Spain more than anything else. After all, the SCW1 didn’t seem to negatively impact the Shepherds of Caine plans for Montreal or Ecaterina the Wise’s plans for New York City. Rather it seems that the infighting between Lasombra and Tzimisce and the struggles with Camarilla coups was more pronounced in New Spain more than anywhere else and that it was the conflicts that came from the difficulties their that spun out into sectarian conflict (at a greater scale then it had probably always existed at any rate). What MCbyN leads me to believe is that the SCW1 spun out from the infighting of Tzimisce and Lasombra elders vying over some of the only good population centers the Sabbat was entrenched in, in New Spain and having that spiral out of control throughout the sect as the leaders on either side use favors as well as political, spiritual, and blood influence to call in allies that ripples across the Americas (and presumably the Old World) and adapts itself to whatever the local conflicts of any particular region were. This only coming to an end when enough of these sectarian elders recognized the uptick of Camarilla entrenchment in the Americas, having for the last few decades been thinning the packs who previously placed limits on Camarilla growth, declaring that hostilities had to end and the sect had to regain its strength (as well as using the claims that disorganizatios, traitors, & infernalists were at fault to help justify the development of a centralized path ecclesiarchy and the formation of an Inquisition).

                                        Now, going full circle, how do we relate this to New England? Well Dark Colony tells us that the Anglo-Camarilla Ventrue had power in the region, also as has been described in this thread. A coalition of clans, particularly Brujah, but including the Tremere begin to conspire to contest the Ventrue Old Guard for control of the region; and those Brujah & Gangrel involved rally their broods and blood-kin for reinforcements, with a non-insignificant portion of those who show up being Sabbat who hope to work with the rebels to boot the Ventrue and then take power in New England as the Sabbat. We know that doesn’t happen, and taking the adjustment that Black Fox has provided, we can imagine that it was Pendragon and the Triad who suspected/discovered the Sabbat plot in their midst and rooted it out before the packs could seize an advantage in the battle and overcome both the Triad and the Ventrue. (Alternatively perhaps it was Warwick & Biltmore who outed the Sabbat conspiracy before it could take action).

                                        Something I’m curious about though is how to interpret Dark Seline’s role in such a turn of events. The implication I got from Dark Colony is that she wants to eventually see New England fall to the Sabbat so that she can carry out her vision. I can see two potential avenues for explaining this: 1. one of the other methuselah intervened to ensure the potential Sabbat coup was aborted, 2. Dark Seline allowed Pendragon to defeat the Sabbat (perhaps they didn’t have the juice to outright succeed anyway) to build his credentials and allow him to weed out rivals until it was just a matter of him being the last thing between her and control over New England.

                                                The Nosferatu are one of the more detailed clans with three members. I think Warwick is a great character. And I love the imagery involved in Trajan's Web. Strand Masters. Veins. District Overseers. These are evocative terms. This is all great stuff. In my chronicles, while every Nosferatu warren has its local "information system" where they exchange gossip and some easy spying, there are also actual spy networks within the clan, and Trajan's Web is the largest and most successful. Trajan, Warwick's sire, is an incredibly important Nosferatu whose Web covers most of Europe and the Mediterranean beyond. Warwick is his top agent in North America who is slowly trying to bring in key Nosferatu in other North American cities as part of the Web. In my opinion, it's a shame other material did not do more with Trajan and his Web.

                                                            The Brujah are another clan with three described NPCs. That includes Roman, his spymaster Jacques Sirque, and Pug Jackson.

                                                            The Brujah are traditionally depicted as the largest clan in the Camarilla (and indeed among all vampires regardless of sects), and it makes sense to do the same in New England. With an estimated population of around 120 Camarilla vampires, we could have as many as 24-30 Brujah in total. These would have to be divided among three main groups - Camarilla Brujah loyal to Pendragon (predominantly in his southern New Hampshire-Portland domain), Camarilla Brujah that are anti-Pendragon, and Anarchs. Let's look at each of these groups in detail.

                                                            Pendragons's New Hampshire/Portland domain probably holds between 6-12 vampires, the vast majority Brujah because he needs them to fight the Sabbat. And if this is the guy who dominates much of New England, we could be talking about half of all Brujah being loyal to him. However, we know very little about his followers. We know Pendragon is a natural warrior and leader, and Jacques is a respectable spymaster. But all his other followers are nameless goons. In the single "story" adventure of Dark Colony, his Brujah followers aren't even given names - just identical stats as "Redcoats" divided between "Lieutenants" who lead 2-3 "Dogs". These aren't characters, but orcs - disposable goons that only exist to fight the PCs and other NPCs and to be killed by them.

                                                            I'll probably have one of the squads routinely visit Boston and Worcester, to rest and relax from fighting Sabbat, to make sure that vampires don't put too much pressure on the mortal population of New Hampshire, and Portland, and just to keep tabs on his ally, Biltmore. Another squad sometimes raids northern New Hampshire or Maine to kill Sabbat. The Prince of Boston just deals with these rotating Brujah visiting his domain because it is worth it to keep the Sabbat away. And on some occasions, a war coterie leaves Boston to help Pendragon out. Likewise, Pendragon is helped by various individual Gangrel who dwell in the New England woods and hate Sabbat too.

                                                            In order to keep Brujah casualties to a minimum despite them constantly fighting Sabbat, I would say that Pendragon utilizes the visions of the Nosferatu Prudence Stone in Boston to arrange ambushes. Biltmore passes on that information to Pendragon, and Pendragon exploits this knowledge. Many times a Sabbat pack attempting to perform the Creation Rites and create a large number of vampires are attacked in the middle of it, leading to the loss of an entire pack or much of it. (I would interpret his name "Pendragon" not to be his mortal family name, but his vampire name / nickname as a result of his wanting to depose Mithras and rule the island. Other than in Liverpool, the Brujah were weak - typically used as "patsies" by one of the other clans.
