    Juggler's Anarchs may be one big coterie, but it is actually a group made up of several factions (all under his leadership). The Caitiffs Gordon and Priscilla are in it only to use Juggler (and vice versa). Maldavis, Dooley, and Anita are only it because it provides them protection. Hank Cave and Damien (plus Neon) joined only because they are dissatisfied with the older leadership (the text says Elucid instead of Damien, but I think that was a mistake given the actual coterie chart and the members list). Uriah is there simply as Lodin's dominated agent. Which means that the only Kindred who truly want Juggler as leader is his own childe Evelyn and his secret master, Tyler (whom he is blood bonded too).

    I also want to say that this time around reading CbN, I realized some things I had never thought before despite the text telling me everything I needed.

    First, Juggler isn't actually an Anarch. There is nothing in his history that mentions any prior involvement among the Anarchs. His name is not mentioned in either the 1960s or 1980s. We know Juggler was in the area since at least 1983, as it is mentioned that is when a young Evelyn first met him. We also know that Juggler only became a "leader" less than a year before a Chicago chronicle is supposed to begin. Helena woke up from torpor a year ago. That was when she decided she needed a pawn to take control of the Anarchs in Chicago. She selected Juggler for that role, and instructed her servant Tyler (who is blood bonded to Helena) to blood bond Juggler to her and begin instructing him. I imagine Juggler has only been an "Anarch" for only three to six months or so. He is doing it to please Tyler who is actually running the Anarchs through him.

    This would also explain why the entire coterie doesn't seem to trust Juggler although he is their leader. At this point, they don't know what to make of him. He wasn't there in the 1960s or 1980s despite residing in the Chicago area. He has no reputation or credentials. He was just the "Party Man" who liked to have fun (his Nature is Jester). I imagine Helena selected him because 1) his antics are the kind of thing that would upset Lodin, 2) he is known to the Chicago area Kindred at large, not a complete unknown, and 3) he's a blank slate in terms of his politics so he could easily be set up as the next leader in some kind of political awakening.

    Second, these Kindred are now following Juggler for one reason - he seems to be a good leader. He's declared himself to be against Lodin. He seems to have some leverage over Modius, giving him a safe haven. He has set up a safe spot for this group of Anarchs in an old ore smelter at Gary. And furthermore, Tyler is giving him instructions on what to do based on her own experience as an Anarch in the 1300s-1400s. To other Kindred, this seems like a man with a plan. The plan is making progress so far, though it is still very, very early in it. They still don't know if they can trust him, or if he'll end up another failure, but they are allowing him to prove himself. And so far, he has. Of course, it is not Juggler who is leading this. It's Tyler. Juggler's Demeanor is listed as Conniver, but I think that has to be a new demeanor meant to impress the others with his conspiratorial prowess. I imagine before this that "the Party Man" was more like a Gallant or Bon Vivant.

    Third, while the real goal of the coterie is to bring Juggler to power, this is not the actual goal of the plan. Helena already controls Lodin and the Ventrue, and two of the Primogen (she thinks its three, but it is really just two). This entire plot is to remove the Anarchs from the control of Menele (through his control of Critias and his pawn Maldavis and other Brujah). If and when Juggler strikes, it won't be to kill Lodin. It'll be to kill Critias and perhaps Inyanga as well - those elders closest to Critias. Killing Lodin doesn't do anything for her. At best, she just replaces one pawn with one another. Lodin would be an acceptable casualty if Critias and others are taken out, but not if Critias isn't killed. Furthermore, we know Tyler lusts after Critias's potent blood. Critias is the real target of Juggler's Anarchs, not Lodin. Of course, I don't think Juggler himself knows this himself. This is a secret that Tyler has not revealed to him. But when this group strikes, Critias is going to be the target.

        In terms of what happened in Chicago, this is how I see it. ”A year ago” Helena wakes up from torpor, and she begins to orient herself. As "Portia" she runs into Juggler and evaluates him as her pawn. She then informs Tyler to blood bond him and set him up as the next Anarch leader. The plan being he'll attack Critias, allowing Tyler to diablerize him. Tyler eagerly accepts and arranges to meet Juggler and then blood bonds him. Let's say that happens around six months ago. He then begins to enact Tyler's plan for him to become Prince.

        The first step is to begin establishing credentials as an Anarch. He embraces his herd member Evelyn to show he is defying a Prince (Modius) and to have another Kindred ally. (In Evelyn's history, she met Juggler in 1983, but it is very clear she has only just been embraced in 1991. Juggler has Herd 5. It makes the most sense he selected someone from his Herd that he liked and thought could best help him.) Juggler then starts hanging out in leftover Anarch hang outs like Hank Cave's Blue Moon coffee house. He begins to speak out against Lodin and states the Anarchs need new leadership after Maldavis' failure. Cave and Damien begin hanging out with him. He takes them to his safe house in the abandoned ore smelter in Gary. He has a gang watching out for it. He begins to reveal his plans (i.e. Tyler's plan). Juggler now has Evelyn, Hank, Damien, and Neon hanging out with him. Five is a nice little gang. When Gordon Keaton hears about this, he thinks this could prove useful to him. So he takes his bonded childe Priscilla with him, and Juggler now has seven members. That's when Maldavis and her leftover allies, Dooley and Anita, appear. Once Maldavis arrives, Lodin sends Uriah to join and keep him informed. This is the status quo as Forged in Steel begins when Juggler arrives and presents his new childe, infuriating Modius. This is important in furthering his Anarch credentials.

        While I would have previously run Juggler's interaction with Modius as something that has happened many times in the past, showing these character's long term history of antagonism. Now I would do it differently. Modius is shocked because this is not how Juggler has acted before. Sure, he's always been an annoying loudmouth who causes some trouble, but this was always how "the Party Man" had fun. He was not really challenging Modius' authority. Now he is since he's created a new childe without permission. Juggler doesn't seem to have any other childer, so he hasn;t created any before in Gary. It's a definite escalation from his previous behavior.

        Maldavis will clearly try to take over the leadership of this coterie at some point. Her two allies still believe in her, but to everyone else she's just the person who got all their friends killed. A failure who couldn't hack it when it mattered. I'd run Uriah as someone who completely backs Juggler in order to rebuild his Anarch credentials. A bit angry over how Maldavis screwed things up, but not to the point it'll prevent any relations with Maldavis as he needs to learn her personal haven so Lodin can kill her. Maybe try to become Lodin's second in command, who can then help Maldavis get back power and thereby regain her trust.

        This is a lot different from how I would have run it before. I would have played up Juggler bluffing and acting like a buffoon, and part of the Anarchs for decades or longer. Not someone suddenly being taken serious and being rather brand new to the Anarchs.

        The coterie is of limited significance to the Free States or the Anarch Movement. But I think it is a good example of compromised Anarch cells elsewhere. Kindred thrown together who have nothing in common but their enemies, and which have members who are agents - witting or unwitting - of their enemies. This is why so many Anarch coups fail.

        CbN 1e doesn't have much to tell us other than witness that traditionally the Anarch strategy was to put one of their own as Prince. That likely became obsolete once MacNeil and others created the Free States. I'll get back to that in a bit. But next up is CbN 2e which introduces some new things that are more interesting.

            With 5E, its interesting to note that Menele leaving has resulted in Helena flat out deciding she doesn't CARE enough about the Anarchs to bother controlling them. It also has resulted in the Anarchs getting actually talented leaders in Evelyn Stephens and Anita Wainwright.

            The thing is that free will means that Helena will probably just slaughter them all when she gets annoyed, barring unexpected miracle like a drone strike on her or the intervention of Narissa Blackwater (who probably cares nothing for the Anarchs).

                    Chicago By Night 2nd edition has some additional information that may be of interest.

                    Moreso than the first edition book, here is where we see Anarchs wanting to become Prince. The contenders to replace Lodin include Maldavis, Juggler, Damien, and Modius. It's also mentioned that the new Anarch Carlyle (more about him later) also considered making an attempt before deciding it was not realistic. This is presented without controversy. It's clear that the term Anarch Prince is not meant to be an oxymoron. It's natural. It's what one does with that position which determines if the person is a sell-out or not.

                    There's little change to the original Anarch coteries mentioned in first edition despite their losses in Under a Blood Red Moon. Hind's Socialists were all wiped out except for Blackjack who is now listed among Juggler's Group. Modius is still around, but without that coterie. Still we're told that Blackjack is still a supporter of Modius for Prince, and not Juggler. There's little change in Juggler's group except it has a lot less members. The Nihilists remain the same despite their losses except that Damien is the de facto leader instead of Levesque.

                    The main change is the introduction of Carlyle's Gang who has come to Chicago from the Free States. This is where it gets interesting. Carlyle is the leader of a small "gang" of Anarchs. This coterie has travelled all across the United States, including Chicago several times in the past. It has battled various Kindred of the Camarilla and Sabbat on behalf of the Anarchs. We're also told that they have the blessing of the "Anarch Council". His coterie mates seem to be volunteers or recruits to undertake these dangerous missions under Carlyle.

                    If Carlyle and his coterie is on missions for this Anarch Council then it must mean there are other coteries doing so as well. That is useful information.

                    The existence of an "Anarch Council" is interesting. Someone please correct me if I am wrong, but I do not recall any Anarch Council mentioned in LAbN. That book does mention a "Revolutionary Council" that ruled LA in the aftermath of the coup against Don Sebastian, but it was dissolved after the establishment of the Status Prefectus. To reconcile this, I posit that there is in fact an actual Anarch Council. But its purpose is not to administer the Free States, which is done by its system of barons and rants. Instead, the purpose of the Anarch Council is to support the Anarch Movement outside the Free States. To use an analogy, if the Free States are the Soviet Union, the Anarch Council would be the Comintern.

                    I imagine the membership of the Anarch Council is different than the leadership of the Free States. Obviously Jeremy MacNeil is there as well as his close mates including Crispus Attucks who is listed as a character in LAbN and Marguerite Foccart who is mentioned but not listed. Salvador Garcia would obviously be a member too (but his #2 in La Hermandad would probably not be). I would also include Smiling Jack if he's ever in LA when it meets. But I don't think Mohammed or David Geduld would be invited here (or any of the other lesser barons). They are strictly local factors, and they probably would not be trusted as true revolutionaries. I'm not sure whether Louis Fortier would be a member. I would think not, but I could believe that he may show up occasionally to provide advice or information.

                    I'm not sure what other canonical NPCs might be members. I imagine one would either need to dwell in LA or be able to visit frequently (like Smiling Jack). Someone like Anita Wainwright could be a member in some capacity, but she's isolated in Chicago as of 1993 and wouldn't be a member. But it would make sense that Carlyle would be one of her main contacts to connect her with the LA Free States and thus the Council. She might be one of the Anarch Council's assets or contacts. Likewise Robin Leeland in Europe wouldn't be a member, but he must be corresponding with the Anarch Council while being a local Anarch leader in Europe.

                    I like the idea that there is an "Anarch Council" based in the Free States. It gives Jeremy MacNeil something to do and explain why he remains a major player in the Anarch Movement despite being a rather bad leader in the Free States itself. It also explains why Crispus Attucks and Salvador Garcia (and possibly Marguerite Foccart) are so often absent from the Free States. They aren't just absent running their own thing. They are coordinating activities for the Council.

                    An interesting bit about Carlyle's Gang is that a member is an important member of the former Brujah Council of Russia, Andrei. He fled Russia when the Shadow Curtain was imposed by Baba Yaga, but ran afoul of the Sabbat in New York City where Carlyle saved him. Andrei thus owes Carlyle a life boon, and is helping him until it is paid off. Obviously Anarchs still value boons. This has two implications. The first is that this story combined with the Russian Brujah Council observers in LAbN is pretty strong proof that the Brujah Council was not originally seen as being Anarchs. Perhaps sympathizers, but clearly something different.

                    The second thing is that in my conception of the relationship between David Geduld and Jeremy MacNeil, I neglected the impact that Geduld saving MacNeil's life would have. It's not a good idea that MacNeil owes Geduld a life boon. We could either say that Geduld magnanimously dissolved that boon in gratitude of MacNeil's accomplishments; that Geduld used up that boon to gain MacNeil's support in establishing his own barony; or that MacNeil quickly repaid that debt by saving Geduld's life in a later part of the Sabbat invasion of LA. It might also be some combination. As long as it eliminates the idea that MacNeil is indebted to Geduld, I'd be fine with it. But that such a debt once existed, even briefly, before it was discharged is something I do like - at least in my version of their relationship (which is completely absent in LAbN).

                    ​There's some other things I want to mention, but that is it for this post. See everyone soon.

                                    I am glad some people are finding it useful. Of course, I'm using existing material and extrapolating from there. It's easier to do that than come up with stuff right out of the blue. (I do have some original ideas of my own, but think it helps people more to improve existing source material.) I was actually surprised that there were some foundations in LABN to do this as the characters were often bland and rolled into each other. But there are some nice distinctive hooks buried in the book (and other early Anarch material).

                                    At the level of your local Camarilla domain, your typical group of Anarchs will just be vampires (usually neonates) who don't like the Prince and want to get rid of him. You can have individual motivations, but you don't need anything more. The examples given in CBN are good enough. There's usually not enough Anarchs that competing ideologies will divide them. They are all forced to work together in varying degrees to overthrow the Prince or simply survive.

                                    But if you want an actual Anarch domain, then you might want actual political differences beyond personalities to distinguish one group of Anarchs from another. It's certainly what I wanted to originally see in LABN. Or if you have visiting groups of Anarchs coming into a Camarilla domain to recruit or support, it's a good way to distinguish this coterie from another. (Are the PCs persuaded more by a visit by Smiling Jack and his buddies, or by Salvador Garcia and his adherents?) And if you want to portray the Anarchs as an actual movement that exists beyond "remove this Prince from power", then you want to actually have examples of how it is presented to vampires.

                                        The Curious Case of Anita Wainwright

                                        Anita Wainwright is one of the oldest Anarchs in the setting, appearing in first edition Chicago By Night. While there are various ways we could talk about the character, for this thread we're only concerned about her role in the Anarch movement.

                                        Anita is only of limited importance in Chicago. She had no real influence or control over the Anarchs there, and was merely a supporter of one of the Anarch leaders. But she has connections with the Anarchs outside of Chicago. She arranges safe spots for Anarchs visiting Chicago, and she can put local Anarchs in contact with Anarchs in other cities, including the LA Free States. In other words, she's like a secretary for the Anarch Movement as a whole. In fact, she has a list of Anarchs in almost every major city in North America.

                                        For some reason, she even has contacts in the Sabbat, specifically New York. In Under a Blood Red Moon, we're given this contact as "Bishop Gustov". And it's not just a connection as she's also been given a "secret code word for Sanctuary" that apparently all Sabbat know. We're told that if Sabbat PCs destroy Anita during the scenario, that they will earn the wrath of this Bishop. So she's clearly favored by this Bishop as a useful ally, pawn, or asset.

                                        Bishop Gustov is one of the few details we're given about the New York Sabbat in the early game. In Rage Across New York, we're also told about Bishop Violet Tremain and Priscus Shawnda Dorrit. Of course, once New York City is better described later in Revised, Justin Achilli does not bother to reference to any of these characters. So this is left as trivia. But in regards to Anita, we have a mystery. How did Anita attract the attention and favor of a Sabbat bishop? What is she providing the Sabbat in exchange? Why would she have such close ties to the Sabbat when so many other Anarchs view the Sabbat as an enemy? How did initial contact between these two happen? All unanswered by the text. The ST will need to decide on her own.

                                        We can only speculate, but I would assume that Bishop Gustov is most likely a member of the Loyalist faction and sympathetic to the Anarchs in general. There's no proof of that. He could just be like other Sabbat and is simply using Anita to undermine the Chicago Camarilla, but if so I would think he must still present himself like a Loyalist. Anita doesn't seem like someone who is sympathetic to the Sabbat. But her connection is not inadvertent. She clearly knows her contact is a leader of the Sabbat. She has to have some deep ties if she has some kind of secret code word that general Sabbat would recognize as "don't kill me, I'm an ally". However, that does not apply to the local Sabbat scouts in Chicago who seem to oblivious to her. Her connection to Gustov must be personal.

                                        In terms of her personal politics, Anita is all over the place in Chicago. An early ally of Maldavis, she followed her into Juggler's coterie. But by 2e CbN, she supports neither of them as Prince, choosing instead to support.... Modius of all people. She seems to recognize Juggler as being a blowhard, and has become disillusioned with Maldavis's leadership and potential to become the Prince. Likely, whatever attracted her to Maldavis was a result of Critias and Inyanga's support in the 1980s. Without it, Anita just doesn't see Maldavis the same way. But why she would support Modius is perplexing. It's possible this is a result that Blackjack is now a member of Juggler's Anarchs after the destruction of the other Socialists. Blackjack is listed as one of the few supporters of Modius, and Anita might simply have decided that Blackjack is one of the few other Anarchs she respects politically and decided to support him as a result. (The coterie chart nevertheless does not connect these two characters). But that she would do this rather than give Maldavis another chance is telling. This could mean she's not very ideological outside her general Anarch loyalty, that she's just pragmatic, or that she's just very cynical and perhaps ruthless.

                                        It is said she can arrange transportation to the LA Free States, so we can assume she has ties to the "Anarch Council" mentioned in CbN 2e. It is even possible that her role as "secretary" is as a result of her services to the Council. There are probably other vampires like her in other cities, but she is likely much more important to the Movement simply because of her location in Chicago, which was a kind of "capital" of the Camarilla in North America. Given the LA Anarchs being fierce fighters of Sabbat, I think it's a given they do not know of Anita's contact with Bishop Gustov.

                                        While not mentioned, it makes the most sense that as of CbN 2e, Carlyle's gang entered the city using Anita as their main contact to orient themselves in the city and find an initial haven. It's too bad this is not made explicit.

                                        My original thought was that David Geduld might have been the person to tie Anita in with LA, but this cannot be. Geduld arrived in the Free States in 1965 while Anita was embraced in 1968. These two likely have no contact. More likely Lodin might have leveraged Hinds old Anarch contacts as a means to put Geduld into some kind of contact as part of his original cover story. Somehow convincing Kindred like Blackjack or Jurgis "because of stories I heard Hinds tell" might have been the key. They were old Anarchs who could have verified enough of the story to provide Geduld with enough cred and initial introductions so he could not have gotten killed.

                                        So Anita must have developed her contacts all on her own although, like Geduld, maybe she utilized some pre-existing contacts between the old Socialists coterie and the Free States. Nevertheless, I would make her one of the "Anarch Council's" important contacts. Not indispensable, but someone they find useful. If one of them wants to know what is going in Chicago or elsewhere, they use her regardless of whether it is Salvador, Smiling Jack, Crispus, or one of their agents.

                                        I don't have any V20 or V5 books she's in so I don't know how she was later developed, though my understanding is that she's become a much more important figure. If so, any future leadership role is not apparent from her original presentation in CbN where is definitely an important, but secondary figure to the actual Anarch leaders in Chicago.

                                            Phoenix Society Part 1

                                            This post is about the possible role of the Phoenix Society in the Anarch movement for a chronicle.

                                            I'll admit I did not remember them at all until Microcuchon mentioned them in an earlier post of this thread. I was so unimpressed with NObN I may have only briefly looked at it a few times afterward, and never read the entire thing after that first time.

                                            Originally posted by Microcuchon View Post
                                            Actually, I think New Orleans bN mentioned a secret society of Anarchs clases The Phoenix Society, and that would be the name I would use, not to make it too obvious.
                                            So I had to borrow a friend's old copy of NObN and see what it contains. I don't think the Phoenix Society has been mentioned anywhere else, but if people know it's been name dropped or explored in a more recent sourcebook, I'd love to hear what it has. For now, I'm just going by what NObN says about it.

                                            It pretty much says the writer of NObN really liked the song "7" by Prince. And that is about it.

                                            Now technically we are provided some aspects. First, people don't actually no who is behind or what it's purpose is besides killing elders. The Camarilla suspects it could be any of their enemies - Sabbat, Anarchs, Setites, or someone else. Second, the Phoenix Society targets the Justicars itself. They want to kill all of seven of them, hence their use of Prince's song as a "calling card" (this could have been a good topical flavor text back in 1992-1995, but is really dated and doesn't make sense to keep doing). Third, they are enough of a threat that its members are on the Kindred Most Wanted List, but it's unclear if there are any actual individuals on the list because of their membership, or if "Any Member of the Phoenix Society" is just a slot they keep open on the Most Wanted List. Fourth, Anarchs from California make up most of its members.

                                            Now this isn't a bad little concept (dated references to a Prince song besides). But I don't think it's implemented very well.

                                            First, we really don't have any good characters as members. We just have generic "attackers" in the book's overly long scenario/plot, and "the General" - an incredibly generic character who has no name, history, or anything interesting about him; but who exists in the book solely to provide the next plot coupon for the scenario. We know he was embraced in 1794 by Robin Leeland which puts him squarely into the Brujah Anarch camp, but this is as generic as it gets since Leeland spawned a lot of Anarchs. And that's it. We're also given another member's name "B" - but I can't find any actual reference to a "B" in the book. It's not mentioned in the characters section, and I don't think it's in the scenario. If someone can correct me, I'd be glad to know.

                                            Second, we're not told why the Phoenix Society is so feared. We're not given any amazing exploits they've done. Despite their targeting of the Justicars, it doesn't seem to have actually killed any. They have killed members of the Prince's Councils of New Orleans, and had one failed attack on Justicar Xavier. But that only gives them a local flavor as a particularly aggressive group in New Orleans. Presumably they come here simply because Justicar Xavier holds triennial conclaves at New Orleans. Is Xavier just angry because of ongoing failed attempts at his life? If so, that doesn't seem like a real reason to put them on the Kindred Most Wanted List, or why they are so feared.

                                            Third, there is some strange history there. We're told that Prince Marcel suspects they were responsible for the City Exchange fire, and that he is correct. But nothing more is talked about it in the section of the Phoenix Society. The section of the Prince's Council coterie (right before the Phoenix Society descriptions) mentions the City Exchange fire happened in 1841 and that Prince Doran destroyed the Anarchs responsible for it. This isn't even mentioned in the history section of NObN. It's not mentioned in Prince Marcel's character description because it occurred 25 years before his own embrace. It is mentioned in the geography section where a brief background is given that it was a (real, historical) hotel that burned down in 1841. But these mentions are scattered all over the place. And its relevance is downplayed as a result.

                                            It's therefore strange to be told that a group responsible for a hotel fire in 1841 is comprised mostly of "California Anarchs" and likely predates the conclaves held by Xavier. I'm not sure how long Xavier's triennial conclaves have been held. I don't think it's mentioned in the corebook, and I don't see it mentioned in NObN. One would think such a longstanding and important event would be documented.

                                            Even the reason for the name "Phoenix Society" is unknown. Is it associated with with the fire at the old Exchange Hotel? Something else?

                                            There seems to be some very important things missing, and this likely explains why the Phoenix Society doesn't seem to be referred to in other sourcebooks. Everything about it - its history, membership, actions - is bland and uninteresting. Having a solid concept doesn't help you if its actual details are lacking.

                                                Phoenix Society Part 2

                                                Microcuchon decided to use this name for the earlier idea of an anarchist brotherhood lead by Salvador Garcia. I'll go in a different direction. I want Salvador's group to be its own thing. And since Salvador was embraced in 1892, he post dates the earliest known activities of the Phoenix Society. He should have his own group, not one he took over.

                                                So here's what I'll do.

                                                So far, what the Anarchs lack is some kind of Leninist vanguard of the revolution - a close knit group dedicated to the cause who would prevent it from being corrupted by outsides and instill revolutionary consciousness among other vampires. It's supposed to prime Kindred for the rebellion, and when it finally happens they will provide leadership in order to coordinate everything. Instead of communists, they're Anarchs. Instead of fighting the Tsarist secret police, it's the Justicars and Archons. And like actual Leninists, I'd make them really intolerable, evil bastards. Since they are the vanguard and must purge false consciousness and corruption from the movement, then it means their right to rule cannot be questioned. So they are not just opposed to the Camarilla, but pretty much any other group of Anarchs that don't accept the Phoenix Society as the leaders of the Anarchs. That means everyone from LA - Jeremy MacNeil, Salvador Garcia, Smiling Jack, etc. are their enemies too.

                                                Now that doesn't mean the Phoenix Society is trying to will nilly kill them. Instead, they exist along them and argue a lot. Until the Anarchs destroy the Justicars and the Camarilla, the Anarchs need a big tent and must work with all these other Anarchs who are going about things the wrong way. Instead, the Phoenix Society must constantly struggle to "lead" the Anarchs by getting the others to do what the Phoenix Society wants best. The Phoenix Society has a very definite idea of what the Anarch victory is supposed to look like - and it's certainly not the Free States under MacNeil's leadership, Fortierist style reform, Smiling Jack's Free Brethren, or anything else discussed earlier. So these "errors" will need to be corrected.

                                                Now, I'm not actually sure what kind of institutions the Phoenix Society wants. I'll have to figure that out. But what's important is that the Phoenix Society continue to guide and lead the revolution even after it happens lest it be "lost". So in effect it'll be a dictatorship of the Society. That's OK because the failed history of the Anarchs - from the original Revolt all the way to now - show they need the Phoenix Society. Of course, there is a very big discrepancy between how the Phoenix Society talks and what they actually do. So many Anarchs are just useful idiots to them.

                                                I decided the name Phoenix Society comes from the idea that they are the old Anarch Revolt rising from the grave like a Phoenix. They idolize the original great Anarch leaders before they failed. They failed in two ways. They failed either by surrendering to the Camarilla during the Convention of Thorns, and those who continued the fight failed by becoming the Sabbat which has obviously become some deranged death cult. It'd be like if the Sabbat never developed its Paths of Enlightenment or its blasphemous ecclesiastical hierarchy.

                                                The best existing model for them in the game would be the character of Dominique in the Who's Who Among Vampires: Children of the Revolution. Here is something relevant written about her:

                                                "The Ventrue Antitribu Dominique is the leading proponent of vampiric accountability. She has made powerful enemies within the Sabbat over her stand, but has also won the backing of many influential anarchs who her as the conscience of the sect, and who credit her with keeping the Sabbat from turning the Earth into a living hell."

                                                So they are very close in nature to some (though not all or even most) Loyalist Sabbat. They are in theory, much more humane and protective of humanity than the Sabbat would. There are probably many Brujah here who want to create a "New Carthage". This does not mean Dominique herself would want to join them. For any revolutionary vanguard organization would exercise too much control to suit her. I'm just saying on the surface there is a lot of overlap.

                                                We also don't have any Anarch organization with ties to the Loyalist Sabbat, so this group would be a good match for that. Perhaps certain Loyalists even consider the Phoenix Society to be their natural allies, and the best way to return the Sabbat to its past stance. But there's still a lot of distrust on both sides. But there's enough contact among the Phoenix Society's highest membership that it is not inconceivable that certain Sabbat Loyalists might help them out against the Camarilla (or even other Anarchs) and the Society might help them in another Sabbat Civil War - at least on certain tactical levels.

                                                If we do go down that route, this would be a good place to put Anita Wainwright. It would explain why she has an ally in the Sabbat (we would need to make Bishop Gustov a Loyalist), and it would fit well the comparison to her and the Symbionese Liberation Army and other radical violence. Her membership in the Phoenix Society would be kept secret for the time being. She might have been recruited after Maldavis failed bid for power in 1986, but by 1991 she had not had enough time to recruit others to the cause in Chicago. Thus explaining her constant change of support between various Anarch leaders (Maldavis, Juggler, Modius). She doesn't believe any of them will really succeed, but she needs to bide time before helping the Phoenix Society establish a foothold in Chicago.

                                                    Phoenix Society part 3

                                                    I'd distinguish the Phoenix Society from other Anarch groups by making them older by far. They were already a well known group by 1841's City Exchange Fire in New Orleans. They were around at least since the French Revolution, maybe older. I'll have to think of a good starting date and story behind it. They thus have a very strong Anarch pedigree and reputation. It'd also explain why they are on the Most Wanted List. After several centuries, they might even have a real Justicar's death on their hands. I'd also have the group's organization and idea change over the years. Their devolution to a Leninist style vanguard party is a result of long and bitter experience at the hands of the Justicars and Archons.

                                                    They predate the California Free States by a lot. They resent Jeremy MacNeil immensely, seeing him as an adventurer. They think he got lucky and that his actions cannot serve as a model to other Anarchs. At best, like the Paris Commune the Free States are going to be crushed. Or that MacNeil, or perhaps someone else like the reprehensible Fortier or Geduld, will become a Napoleonic figure. They wouldn't be far off when it comes to David Geduld.

                                                    The Phoenix Society was not represented in LA at the time, and thus missed out at getting any credit or glory for it. So its members are rather upset and jealous. In other cities, they always remind the Anarchs that "MacNeil is not going to save you" and point out to the failed Anarch actions in Chicago as proof. The Free States are a bad model - only the Phoenix Society has the real answers. But of course, they'll cooperate with any Anarchs in the meantime. They'll just constantly point out in revolutionary logic why everyone else is wrong and will get the Anarchs killed. Only they have the experience to defeat the Justicars, Archons, Princes, and Inner Circle. After enough anarchs get killed in failed uprisings against the local Prince, there are always those who will start listening to them intently.

                                                    They are also a group that has members everywhere in the world because of how old they are. They were recruiting for a long time, and long term members are now in many places in the world. They don't have a success like the Free States, but they do have a long record of killing Camarilla agents. So they do have accomplishments they can speak about. So they still have credibility in the Movement as a whole.

                                                    MacNeil and the others in LA are not big fans of them. MacNeil smells the whiff of tyranny about them, and Salvador probably remembers what happened to the Spanish Anarchists during the Spanish Civil War. But there are members of the Society in LA, ironically likely in MacNeil's area. And they do have a member on the Anarch Council that plans Anarch activities outside the rest of the Free State. They are a too important piece of the movement too ignore even if they are rather unimportant in the Free States themselves.

                                                    Like the Bolsheviks, members of the Phoenix Society use aliases and nom-de-guerres to hide their identity. Not a few members, including the leadership, are better known as upstanding members of the Camarilla whose identities as Anarchs are unknown. Thus except to the leadership of the Phoenix Society, nobody knows that the Brujah primogen of Cleveland is actually "Danton" of the Phoenix Society who took responsibility for the destruction of one of Petrodon's Archons. That would explain such a boring name like "the General." Everybody knows "the General" by name. Nobody knows who he actually is (except at the highest levels of the Phoenix Society).

                                                    If many anarchs seem themselves as freedom fighters, then the Phoenix Society is the terrorist organization of the Anarchs. They have kind of lost sight of what they are actually fighting for because of the means they have had to adopt to survive. They are better defined by who their enemies are than what they would actually do. But like a lot of such organizations, there are always people who are incapable of seeing that and take their words at face value simply because the words are very nice.

                                                    A distinguishing feature of the Phoenix Society in a chronicle is that they are actually a legitimate threat. Perhaps not to actually overthrow the Camarilla in a city, but in killing important Camarilla vampires. These are not a bunch of losers like Dickie Fulcher, or (dangerous) blowhards like Smiling Jack. Nor are they so absorbed by unrealistic ideology to be incapable of governing if they do ever gain power. The problem is that they actually aren't Anarchs anymore since they'd war on everyone else in the name of preserving the revolution. Some Anarchs see the danger in them. But most don't either because they are too naive, are fellow travelers, see them as necessary for victory, or think everything will be resolved amiably after victory. Their supporters seem them as THE heirs of the Anarch Revolt who have credible achievements, even if they didn't get the lucky break that MacNeil and the LA Anarchs had. They represent a credible alternate.

                                                        I happened to be rereading the first Clanbook: Brujah, and saw another thing that is interesting. As I mentioned in an earlier post, early material implied the existence of some Anarch Council that is not mentioned in LAbN.

                                                        Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                                                        The existence of an "Anarch Council" is interesting. Someone please correct me if I am wrong, but I do not recall any Anarch Council mentioned in LAbN. That book does mention a "Revolutionary Council" that ruled LA in the aftermath of the coup against Don Sebastian, but it was dissolved after the establishment of the Status Prefectus. To reconcile this, I posit that there is in fact an actual Anarch Council. But its purpose is not to administer the Free States, which is done by its system of barons and rants. Instead, the purpose of the Anarch Council is to support the Anarch Movement outside the Free States. To use an analogy, if the Free States are the Soviet Union, the Anarch Council would be the Comintern.

                                                        I imagine the membership of the Anarch Council is different than the leadership of the Free States.
                                                        Clanbook: Brujah provides more information on this council. It is not called the Anarch Council like in CbN 2e. It is just a council of "major anarch figures." This probably what CbN 2e is referring to, but this was simply forgotten by the writers of LAbN. Quite strange.

                                                        Anyway, we're told Jeremy MacNeil formed the council. It includes MacNeil himself, the leaders of the Seattle, Sacramento and San Diego Anarchs, and one member from the Nosferatu, Gangrel, and Ravnos clans. Those three clans are the non-Brujah representatives of the Anarchs. The council is open to democratic influence, letting any anarchs in the area participate. The Council usually meets in downtown LA's city hall building, but have no set place or agenda, and usually meet in small, informal groups.

                                                        That last bit heavily implies this was the intended government of the Free States. The book also mentions various "Brujah gangs" fighting each other, but not anything like the Barony system introduced in LAbN. The Free States seem even more anarchic as the book says since no one is forcing everyone to present themselves, that Caitiff enter freely, Tremere sneak in and out, and Setites set up temples (note the plural). This all contradicts LAbN where the gangs do control their territories even if only for feeding purposes. And the idea that enemies like Tremere and Setites are able to enter and leave at will or set up shop doesn't seem likely.

                                                        We're told Toreador "infest" Hollywood and Beverly Hills, with Gangrel roaming the hills north of Los Angeles, and Nosferatu in east LA. Again LAbN does not reflect this.

                                                        We just have to accept that even early canon contradicts itself.

                                                        However, I would use this information as the basis of an "Anarch Council" formed for the purpose of exporting the revolution. It's a seven person council of 4 Brujah, and 1 each of the Gangrel, Nosferatu, and Ravnos. MacNeil himself is obviously a council member. But what about the rest?

                                                        Well, I'm not keen on the idea that the "leaders" of Seattle, Sacramento, and San Diego Anarchs are included. The only such canonical character we know of would be Tara who is scheming to defect to the Camarilla and be recognized as a Prince. Both Seattle and Sacramento would be portrayed as Camarilla cities with Justicar Petrodon even being based out of Seattle. Obviously there can still be Anarchs there, but any local leaders would not make good members of a Council based in Los Angeles. Such a Council would also leave out other important Anarch figures from its leadership.

                                                        I do think we have some good Brujah who should be on the council. I personally would choose Salvador Garcia and Marguerite Foccart as two of them. Smiling Jack would in theory make a good member, but he seems too ill-disciplined to be a good team member. Instead, I'd make him an unofficial member in the sense that he does stop by and tells the Council some things whenever he does appear in Los Angeles, and sometimes agrees to their requests. Crispus Attucks would make sense, but his presence would be redundant with both his friend MacNeil and sire Foccart as members. It works best as having some other Brujah faction represented. I don't like any of the other canon Brujah characters in LAbN as such a leader. So I'd just make up my own, or grab another canon character from elsewhere. I would be interested in hearing others give their recommendation for membership.

                                                        We also don't have any canonical candidates for a Gangrel, Nosferatu, or Ravnos member. LAbN does not mention any Gangrel at all, and the single Nosferatu and Ravnos characters mentioned in that sourcebook are not appropriate. So again it's either create your own character, or find some canonical character from elsewhere. I don't think these council members "represent" their clan, but their presence does serve as a way to communicate to these clans that the Anarchs could be trusted to look out for their interests. Presumably each of these Anarchs would have substantial Anarch credibility even if they are not at the level of a MacNeil, Garcia, Foccart, or Smiling Jack.

                                                        These members don't need to be based out of LA (Foccart isn't, but I assume she is relatively nearby), but should be able to easily meet in LA when needed.

                                                        (I suppose we could use Irena of LAbN as the Ravnos on the Anarch Council. But if so, I think it indicates the non-Brujah members might only be tokens and that Brujah domination of the Anarch Council is even more than it looks.)

                                                        Since MacNeil formed this Anarch Council himself, he probably selected people he already trusted or in the case of the other clans, a combination of trust and political necessity. All other clans are obviously excluded from any leadership among the Anarchs. But it seems interesting that MacNeil decided that the Gangrel, Nosferatu, and Ravnos were too important to the movement to exclude entirely. But this is clearly a Brujah driven leadership.

                                                        The exclusion of the Ventrue means not only an important local figure like David Geduld is excluded, but Garcia's second in command Jesus Ramirez, and Louis Fortier also have no role. It's understandable why and makes sense, but it is an important enough omission to point out. Probably the last thing Brujah Anarchs want is the possibility the Ventrue assuming leadership of yet another group. Likewise the Anarchs wouldn't see the Tremere as any possible ally.

                                                        The exclusion of the Malkavians and Toreador is less obvious, but still understandable. There should be a significant number of Toreador Anarchs, but overall that clan's culture is oriented towards the salon culture of the Camarilla. The Toreador are usually seen as a pillar of the Camarilla along with the Tremere and Ventrue. Malkavians either aren't capable of collective action, or when they do it is for reasons enigmatic to others. I don't think the Anarchs lose a whole lot by not throwing these clans a bone.

                                                        However, by being so Brujah dominated, it does help cement the idea to other clans that to be an Anarch means accepting Brujah rule, and that the main reason the Brujah rebel so much is that they simply hate the idea of ever having to obey someone who isn't a Brujah. It's not an attractive stance and probably undermines the appeal of the Anarchs to the other clans.
