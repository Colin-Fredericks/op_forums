    What do you think of human touchstones?

    05-15-2021, 06:08 AM
    In my experience, they have been a failed mechanic. I have been in multiple games where the human touchstones literally did not come up once in a six month plus campaign. That part of the sheet might just as well have been left blank.

    There's politicking and detective work and murdering to do, and touchstones are hard work for an ST to work in. Ironically, the non-human touchstones I have in the games where I've been allowed to use the Long Road Out of Hell supplement come up waayyyy more often, and can be fit into most stories far more smoothly and routinely, especially since Beast Marks are so much fun to roleplay.

    For example, one of my touchstones on one character is the New Orleans graveyard where I got my creation rites. So I do things like cleaning up litter and graffiti at the graveyard, which has lead to conflict because the Hecata claim that graveyard as domain. So I've had to bargain as the Hecata is more powerful than me, plus I would likely end up destroying the graveyard even if I managed to win a war for it. I've got Animal Instinct as a Beast Mark, which has lead to "Pulp Fiction" situations, where I frenzy and murder in very inconvenient locations and have a hell of a time covering it up. Gangrel powers are really NOT good at covering up murder, especially when your only Animalism powers are Bond Famulus and Sense the Beast. Gangrel, good at committing murder, not so good at covering up murder, especially in the city. One can see why they mostly stick to the open road.

    Here's the key thing, though: all of that happened because the ST allowed me to play a character on a Path of Enlightenment with non-human touchstones. It's more fun for me, and easier for the ST to integrate into the story than human touchstones. It was very easy for the ST to simply decide that the graveyard where I got my rites had fallen into Hecata hands over the ensuing decades. Easy and fun conflict creation.

    With human touchstones, everything is very awkward. Integrating them is awkward. Threatening them without killing them is awkward and requires a lot more plot armor than a graveyard. The flaw to human touchstones is that they are very fragile. The flaw to non-human touchstones is that they require one to take Beast Marks. In my experience, the Beast Marks are, by a huge margin, the more fun flaw to roleplay. And that's before you get into the problem that ST's mostly seem to forget Human Touchstones even exist, because they aren't a part of the Detective-Story/Action-Adventure/Political-Intrique/Cosmic Horror story they wanted to tell in the first place.

    By and large, ST's want to tell Detective-Story/Action-Adventure/Political-Intrique/Cosmic-Horror stories. Player characters are there to be plugged into those stories. If you can create a character whose details enrich those stories, wonderful. If your character's details get in the way or are simply irrelevant to that story, then those details will often be brushed aside, and, in my experience, human touchstones are at the top of the brushed-aside list.

    My graveyard touchstone enriched my ST's story about sect and territorial conflict, so it got integrated.

    My human touchstones in other games where the ST would NOT allow me to use Long Road Out Of Hell? I think....let's see...I had one conversation with one abouuuut...six months ago? Was completely irrelevant to the plot...only happened because I specifically told the ST I was going to go visit him...aaaand he moved right back into limbo after that conversation. That ST was telling a story about mystery and politics in Paris. My very mundane human touchstone was irrelevant to all of that, so he just kind of got forgotten.

    But maybe this is all just me. Maybe I keep getting ST's on roll20 who are bad at integrating human touchstones. What is your experience with human touchstones?

      the issue with touchstones in general is that the only reason the game gives you to interact with them is to make sure your ST isn't going to fuck you over through them,so a player needs to come up with a reason to interact with it that's unrelated to it being a touchstone. the advantage of non-human touchstones is that it's alot easier to come up with a reason to go interact with them, like in your example a graveyard, that requires maintenance, that's a good reason to go interact with it.

      with human touchstones this is harder to do worsened by the fact that most ppl playing vtm are socially awkward nerds.

      that's the issue on the players end, the issue on the STs end is that humans are always going to be somewhat removed from the story because the players are usually dealing with stuff that the average person isn't supposed to know about (either because it's illegal or more likely they need to uphold the masquerade) so you usually end up having to force them in there or just do the same thing every time, get them involved because of their connection to the player character.

          I think there's a few things at work (and this is me, so I'm more talking about my experience with Touchstones from CofD as I don't play V5).

          1) Roll20 open games tend to be a bit more "on the rails" because as a GM prepping for one you don't know who you're going to have in the group. One of the reasons I think you don't see lots of public games there for a bunch of systems, is that they're not systems that lend themselves to sitting down with a bunch of strangers and just playing as fast as people can put some finishing touches on their PCs to make sure the group is cohesive. Unlike, say, a convention game w/pre-gens, there's an expectation of still making your own PC.

          2) This, of course, makes it a lot harder to integrate Touchstones that don't fit the GM plans (or really any sort of background element), and the situation isn't as conducive to the assumption a lot of games have of more in depth player-GM planning sessions to collaborate on a story and setting where all the Touchstones have a more natural place. If I were running a game with Touchstones for an open game on Roll20, I'd consider it my job as the GM to make some space for such things; I know they're coming because they're something every character is going to have. I would probably have some notes for prospective players about it. "While taking place in one city, this game will involve a lot of movement. Plan for your vampires to not sleep under the same roof too many nights in a row, both in terms of their personal traits, physical things like Havens, and NPCs associated with their characters so they make sense being accessible in such circumstances." You don't need to highlight Touchstones, but you do prompt for ones that are easier to integrate. Though this is coming from a lot of experience with the CofD system and doing pickup games with it It might take some time for this sort of thing to work its way into the V5 Roll20 GMs if they're used to earlier VtM and other traditional games where the expectations are different.

          3) And, something that's really hard to do because V5 isn't as much of a help as it could be.... games that want human Touchstones to work have to incentivize it. With the current V5 mechanics, it's hard to get the players to want to bring the Touchstones into the story because.... why bother making the effort? As someone that's a huge fan of the CofD implementations of Touchstones, I want people to be fans of the concept. Making it more player facing so the players want to put in the work instead of putting it on the ST's shoulders is important to getting it to be fun.

          So... even with being as much of a critic of V5 as I tend to be, I think calling the human specific Touchstones a failure compared to other options a failure might be a bit off, but I think V5 should do some course correction to help make the players want to engage with Touchstones rather so much of the burden being on the ST. If your players buy into the default mode of V5 without extra incentive, I'm sure this is far less of a problem, but IME it's rare to get a completely group all on the same page like that.

                                                  A touchstone is an anchor that gives you a reason to keep going and anchors your soul/sense of self while the beast tries to gnaw on it like a flimsy chew toy. At least, that's how I interpreted it.
                                                  My personal opinion is that I think something is lost when you narrow the selection to people. It's something personal and complex reduced to something too restrictive and simple and it should be about the pc as an individual. A human being is not going to work for everyone, and it's certainly not going to for those vampires whose paths disregard humans altogether - or outright forbid interaction..

                                                  Even if our attachments start with people, they evolve or transfer to things. A ring is important because it belonged to your grandmother, who raised you. A song on the radio reminds you of your best friend when you were a child, and you still looked forward to the future. Your grandmother died last year and haven't seen that friend since you were twelve, but that doesn't mean your time together stopped mattering.

                                                  And like your graveyard touchstone (which sounds like fun), we attach to and gain meaning from things that aren't people.

                                                  There are suicidal people who hang on for the sake of their pets, or even because they want to know how their favourite show ends.

                                                  Places. An environmentalist in life has the local park for a touchstone, they make sure the nature within is protected and that gives their existence a purpose.

                                                  Nightly rituals.A vampire identifies with their comunity. He patrols the neighbourhood, takes up litter picking and generally dedicates an hour every night doing the rounds in his neighbourhood making life better there (or trying, at least). One vampire rereads her favourite book, the one that kept her company when real humans refused.

                                                  A member of an endangered or dead culture takes strength and meaning from keeping it alive in a way that only an immortal can. Maybe their touchstone is a place, object, ritualised activity or job position based around that?

                                                  Religous vampires may treasure their faith, a feeling that when genuine can be extremely strong. They're attached to an object of religious significance. Juzu beads worn with frequent use, a pocket bible bookmarked with favourite passages. A Jewish vampire fights off torpor on Sundays to observe the Sabbath. A muslim vampire gets up every day for prayer.
                                                  Or, since this is VtM, change the above to something relevant to the Noddists or Set or something.

                                                  A vampire who's too old to remember what it was like to be human probably can't relate to a mortal, but they might find the middle ground in a fledgling or a young neonate.
                                                  The other non-humans might work in a fucked up way... though I would definitely rule out a Garou... Although now that I think on it, a vampire with a werewolf as a touchstone sounds absolutely fucking hilarious, if incredibly short lived.

                                                  So anyway, other things touchstones could be, in my opinion: nightly rituals, songs, specific stories, jobs, objects, places, animals...
                                                  Hell, how about a plant? I know somebody who's kept a plant alive for over fifty years because they took it as a cutting from the garden it came from, and that garden had special meaning to them. It's amazing what you can latch onto.

                                                  To me more variety is more interesting.


                                                  I also really think that despite the books suggesting using them for drama, that touchstones should be treated as the opposite for most of the time. While there certainly will be dangers and conflicts, it's primarily a way to inject brief moments of meaning and possibly peace and happiness into a miserable, nihilistic setting.

                                                  Prone to hyperbole
