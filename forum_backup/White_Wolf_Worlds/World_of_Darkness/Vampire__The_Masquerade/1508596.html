    So which alternate banes are you using, and which aren't you using?

    04-16-2023, 10:00 PM
    I'll be using the Nosferatu alternate bane. I'm actually kind of excited to see it in action.

    I'll be using the Ministry alternate bane for Serpents of the Light, and keeping the original bane for the main clan. I'll be treating the Serpents as a bloodline, basically.

    I'll be using the Lasombra alternate weakness for Anarch/Camarilla games, and the original bane for Sabbat games.

    I'll be using the Hecata alternate bane. I just like it better than the bite thing.

    I'm iffy on the Malkavian alternate bane. It makes them blander, but also more newbie friendly. So this may be a game-by-game decision.

    I'll mostly be keeping the original Gangrel bane for most games, but I may use the alternate in games with a certain melancholy mood, inspired by "Let the Right One In". In such games, body horror is less appropriate than the isolation created by feeling driven away by every happy group gathered around a fire, every person walking by with a hint of true faith. The alternate bane is that of a lonely outcast, and could work in games about childer who know nothing about vampire society.

    I probably won't use the alternate Ventrue bane, simply because I'm not sure HOW to use it properly. Perhaps if I'm a player in a game where it is used well, I can learn to use it in interesting ways, but for now it's a no.

    Won't be using the Ravnos alternate bane. Just seems to require too specific a situation to come up.

    Will be using the Tremere alternate bane. I like it slightly better than the original, though neither are particularly interesting.

    I will be using the Tzimisce alternate bane. It seems more engaging and fun.

    Won't be using the Brujah alternate bane as written, but will houserule something very close to it but cleaned up. There is the core of a good idea there, but the details need work.

    Won't be using the Toreador alternate bane. It is complete garbage. There isn't even the core of a good idea there as there is with the Brujah.

              My ST used systems like this for weaknesses:

              Ravnos had an unhealthy compulsion to get into dangerous situations, so if he had a fight or needed to sneak in he needed to make a Willpower roll to not screw things up by jumping into danger like a idiot.

              Tremere had useless blood outside their bodies which prevented them from forming blood bonds or creating ghouls. It's also harder for them to create other kindred.

              Malkavian had either schizophrenia or amnesia.

              Lasombra has blurred reflection, same for photos and cameras, technology works with problems near them which limits usage, plants die gradually near them and corpses putrefy faster I guess.

              Saulot had addictive blood and the problem with the third eye, it could even be hidden with a bandana or hat, but it gave a -1 penalty to perception if you did it.​

                  In case anyone's wondering, the alternate Banes are described within the VTM paradox wiki's entries for the clans:

                  Assamites/Banu Haqim
                  Brujah
                  Followers of Set/The Ministry
                  Gangrel
                  Hecata
                  Lasombra
                  Malkavian
                  Nosferatu
                  Ravnos
                  Salubri
                  Toreador
                  Tremere
                  Tzimisce
                  Ventrue

                  I personally don't own the Player's Guide and from what I've heard about it, I don't plan on buying it unless it goes on sale for less than half of its current price. I do have some thoughts on the alternate Banes as described in the previous thread and the links above, though.

                  The Banu Haqim Bane's okay-ish as a concept I suppose, even if it's rather basic: it makes sense that the clan that's notorious for turning their blood into poison has poisonous blood. I'd say it's too easy to sidestep, though, since there's no need to expose mortals to your blood at all if you aren't planning to make them ghouls or thralls. At the clan level that can be a significant liability, but at the individual level it may not even be noticeable. If the Bane were flipped around to be a re-tread of the old Tremere curse upon the clan and vitae from other clans/bloodlines was toxic to them, that might be more meaningful since it makes diablerie an even more risky prospect, introduces nasty consequences even from just learning non-clan Disciplines, and it's the sort of thing that other vampires could potentially use against them even if they do everything right. Plus, between the Hunger Compulsion, hunger frenzies, and the clan Compulsion, it's hard to go 100% cold turkey.

                  The Brujah alternate Bane seems weird and half-baked to me; as written (or at least summarized on the wiki) it's about as much of an advantage as a disadvantage. Actually maybe even more of an advantage: getting consistent access to bonus Aggravated Damage on attacks would normally require the use of level 3 to 5 Discipline powers in this edition, and tossing around Aggravated Willpower Damage willy-nilly can mentally cripple others fairly quickly, especially if piled on top of the effects of social combat rolls. I'd be interested to see a rules revision for it, but not to the point of putting in the time make one myself since it doesn't seem like it'd play all that differently from the original Bane anyway. They're still just struggling with violent impulses that they can't fully control (or I guess maybe energy in this case - it's hard to imagine hurting someone with an Awareness roll without eye beams being involved). It is kind of funny to imagine a Brujah rock band literally blowing the minds of their audience at the height of an epic guitar solo, though.

                  The Followers of Set/Ministry Bane is serviceable, but I still think it's a better fit for the Hecata.

                  I don't think the Gangrel's new Bane suits them and to be honest it'd be more annoying than evocative on any clan as far as I'm concerned. The other frenzies at least compel engagement with what's going on around you, while terror frenzies/Rotschreck just drive you to get as far away as possible. Admittedly, that can be a source of drama in and of itself, but I think it'd get pretty repetitive after a while and the whole point of the Bane is that it'll happen fairly often. A better way to represent the horror of the Beast never being far from the surface of one's thoughts and actions might be to give them the Obvious Predator Flaw to reflect unconsciously predatory mannerisms and leave them subject to a corresponding Compulsion (Harm Compulsions for fury frenzies, Hunger Compulsions for hunger frenzies, and Paranoia Compulsions for fear frenzies) after succeeding on a frenzy roll unless the margin of win is higher than their Bane Severity: there's more room for thought and tactics with Compulsions, and contrary/unrelated actions are still possible with a penalty.

                  The alternate Hecata Bane is either crippling if you don't build your character to avoid it (either by forgoing Background altogether or taking predator types that just gift you the dots you'd otherwise have to spend ungodly amounts of experience on) or negligible if you do. Even setting that aside, though, in the core book there are only six Flaws that fit into the specified categories for the Bane; two of them flat-out prevent you from getting dots in the associated Backgrounds at all (No Home and Destitute) and four of them are Haven Flaws. It seems like the Bane was supposed to be fairly open-ended in terms of its specifics, but unless the player's guide doubles or triples the amount of these types of Flaws that are available, it'd up being pretty samey in practice if there are more than two or three Hecata with this Bane in the chronicle.

                  The Lasombra Bane makes sense for them on one level, but I think it swings too much between irrelevance and catastrophe. It'll often be barely noticeable until their Humanity drops to 5 or so; before then they're probably looking at two or more Stains before Remorse rolls are made most of the time if they're using Oblivion fairly regularly, so they'd probably be looking at only one or two dice per Remorse roll anyway. Once Humanity dips lower than that the Bane becomes pretty serious, but this is also the point where it starts to turn and undercut Lasombra themes in my opinion: they're supposed to be commanding, imperious, and self-possessed, not constantly freaking out over their lost Humanity and impending degeneration (which is what players will probably do if they don't want their characters to become unplayable wights). Maybe it'd be better to just lean into the fact that they're the clan of Night - apply their Bane Severity as a penalty to dice pools to wake up/function during the day, and if that alone would be too circumstantial, make them also roll Rouse Checks twice and take the lower result when they're exposed to bright lights or something.

                  The alternate Malkavian Bane's decent and I don't think it's even that much blander than their standard Bane in V5, since under its rules, 'mental illness' for the clan just means stacking penalties on top of penalties when you screw up. Given the 'subtle but eerie manifestations of supernatural power' explanation for the Bane, though, I'd probably be more inclined to assign it to the Tremere (or maybe even the Lasombra or Hecata). It'd also work for Nosferatu, of course, but my preference for alternate Banes is that they steer clans toward different experiences in-game and I think 'being creepy' is close enough to 'being ugly' that I'd like to look elsewhere for them.

                  As others mentioned in the player's guide thread, the new Nosferatu Bane does not play nice with the idea of them being firmly established in sewer kingdoms and insofar as it also can penalize social interactions, it's redundant with the original Bane on top of that. I'm also not convinced that Obfuscate still makes sense as a clan Discipline for someone who has a pet locust swarm following them around wherever they go. That said, one thing that I do find interesting about the Bane is its reference to Animalism being able to counter its effects. Between the way thatBlood Surge bonuses and Discipline power bonuses scale with Blood Potency, this means that Nosferatu elders are probably the only elders that have an easier time of it than neonates and fledglings where the clan curse is concerned (I'm sure that's very much unintentional though).

                  With the alternate Ravnos Bane, if it comes up at all it seems like it'd be hard to put the genie back into the bottle. Then again, the name has to be used against them, which means that the other party not only has to know the name but also know that the Ravnos is there right now. Given that Ravnos have Obfuscate, they can make illusions, and courses of action that don't involve Discipline use aren't penalized at all, I guess it could be argued that they have enough ways to keep the penalties from applying constantly. Toss in a Tzimisce buddy to fleshcraft them back into obscurity from time to time and it's pretty much a non-issue.

                  The Salubri Bane from the player's guide seems like another half-baked idea to me. On the surface, it seems to fit the idea of them being seekers of Golconda, but in practice its binary effect only discourages being a *little* self-indulgent: the penalty won't increase no matter how much or how often they overfeed. On top of that, despite referencing asceticism, it does nothing to discourage wallowing in actual luxury (not that a clan curse really should - this Bane is just a stupid idea from top to bottom as far as I'm concerned). To be honest, I think a third eye that weeps copious amounts of blood when using Discipline powers is Bane enough on its own: just update the Stigmata Flaw to V5 (if the player's guide hasn't already), slap it on them, and apply their Bane Severity as a penalty on rolls to conceal the eye's presence. Even covering it up can only go so far when using Disciplines makes blood ooze out of the vampire's forehead, and who knows? Maybe people can actually feel it looking at them even when they can't see it; that seems like the kind of thing that'd help make a clan unpopular enough for most Kindred to look the other way when the Tremere start hunting them to near-extinction.

                  I don't really feel like talking about the Toreador's new Bane or alternatives to it any more than I already have elsewhere.

                  The new Tremere Bane's pretty insignificant. Sure, it'd be handy to be able to make use of Blood Surges more easily, but jacking up the cost that much just means they won't be used 99% of the time and that's pretty much it. Spending Willpower can cover a lot of the same ground, the Tremere can get Retainers and coterie mates to shore up their rolls with teamwork bonuses, and there are plenty of Discipline powers that do similar things anyway. Having them take unhalved Superficial Damage equal to their Bane Severity from their Blood Surges might encourage more weighing of options, especially if they could reduce the damage by willingly applying a lesser bonus, but even then I find it more appropriate for a relatively minor Flaw than a clan Bane.

                  It makes sense that they'd go with this Bane for Tzimisce and I've seen the idea of it floated around in various places for years, but to me, the need for invitations actually undercuts their status as supposed lords of the land. A Dragon's powers fizzle out in a peasant's hovel because he didn't have their say-so before stepping in? That just doesn't work for me. There used to be a Flaw called Faceless in previous editions where their features would twist and shift from night to night: an easy way to port it would be to have the Tzimisce roll their Bane Severity and base the extent of the change on the number of successes rolled - something like passing for a close relative with only one success, a distant cousin with two, and a total stranger with three, with successes in excess of the vampire's Stamina rating also causing Superficial Damage. Hunger dice could still apply and outright inhuman changes could result from bestial failures/messy criticals (in addition to other effects that they trigger).

                  The alternate Bane for the Ventrue is somewhat fitting, though I might also have it trigger based on disparities in Status in institutions that they recognize as legitimate. I also would make the penalty scale up to Bane Severity based on the degree of difference in Generation and Status (they'd stack for this purpose) between the Ventrue and their 'superior' rather than have it start there. The funny thing about this Bane is that it makes Ventrue who have it about as intrinsically submissive as a vampire can be without being one of the Tremere's Gargoyles, so I could see them being looked down on pretty heavily by their clan-mates who don't.​ The only problem is that this isn't really a Bane at all if the Ventrue isn't around people who are higher up on the food chain (even if this seems unlikely to be the case in a standard VtM chronicle). Two things come to mind as ways to address this: 1) the Ventrue has to spend Willpower to directly attack other vampires of equal Generation and Status (but not those who are equals by one standard and inferiors by the other) except in self-defense, and 2) the Ventrue treats their Status as a Touchstone in terms of the risks associated with it, incurring Stains when it is diminished or lost. There'd also need to be a consequence for lacking Status altogether in the case of #2: maybe they'd function as though Impaired for the purpose of Social rolls, Willpower rolls, and rolls involving Composure and/or Resolve under these circumstances.​
