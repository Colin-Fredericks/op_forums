    Reworking Spirit Manipulation (for fun)

    07-18-2022, 10:27 PM
    (This is more for fun than anything else)

    Spirit Manipulation:

    1) Hermetic understanding- Combination of Hermetic Sight and Astral Cant

    2) Voice of Command- Becomes a level 2 power, but the rest stays the same

    3) Engling Fury- Spiritus 4 power that allows a vampire to devour spirits to regain willpower.

    4) Entrap Epherma- Uses both Revised and V20 powers (just for more options)

    5) Other Side (Heavy inspiration from Spiritus 6)- This power allows a vampire to transport themselves into the Spirit Wilds of the umbra. While in the Spirit Wilds, this power masks the users vampiric nature. The vampire in quesiton is instead is seen as a shadowy spirit of hunger and death, they wont be classified as a bane, but they definately wont be seen as a regular spirit. The vampire, now spirit, will recieve many different types of reactions from the denizens of the spirit wilds. Some may run away, some may fight/challenge them, some may even greet them as "kindered" spirits or as potential allies. Garou and other shapechangers will be intrigued by such a unique spirit and many will either try to research or interact with the spirit. If the vampires finds itself in conversation with another spirit or shapechanger, they are encouraged not to talk and instead listen, if they do, it is asked that they reveal nothing about who they truly are. Also, in this form the vampires voice is extrmely raspy.

    In this form the vampire is unable to use any disciplines. However, any type of blood magic that does not require a physical form can be used.

    Now I know there are major issues with this path. Some may call it a bit too OP, which it is. But then again, how often do people actually use Spirit Manipulation? No seriously, how often does that occur? Because I've heard people use elemental mastery waaayyy more than spirit manipulation.

    So anyway, what are your thoughts?

      The big problem is that over the thousands of years experience that the Garou have with the Umbra, they won't be intrigued or need to research this "spirit" at Path Level 5. They'll recognize it as some vampire interloper by the 21st century. I would also think that whether it detects as the Wyrm or not would be based on whether the individual vampire would be of the Wyrm (based on its Humanity). It should not suddenly be immune simply because he is transported to the Umbra. And detection as the Wyrm should make Garou and spirits think it is a Bane.

      I understand you are likely trying to make it normal for the vampire to explore the spirit world and not be immediately attacked by Lupines and such, but I think it assumes too much ignorance on the other side. I think level 5 needs some work to achieve a good balance.

                  Duality is weird, going straight up to the Umbra is ok as a niche power. And it isn't OP at all, because you're just making access for another place for the characters to explore. By itself the concept of the 5th level is ok.

                  Also, the problem with Spirit Manipulation isn't its power level, it is the whole "Vampires are never welcome by anything on the Umbra" shtick of the game, which takes an option that already isn't the first thing someone is looking for when playing Vampire and makes it completely uninteresting. I'm personally of the opinion that this was a bad design choice since the beginning, but it is what it is.

                  Let's apply a bit of Occam's Razor here. If you want the vampire to turn into a spirit, turn it into a spirit. It will follow some Spirit rules and that's about it. If it is a Wyrm-spirit, well, those are as normal in the Umbra as any other Spirit. There's already a rule stating that Kindred exude Wyrm-taint based on Humanity, you don't need more than that, if they're a Bane, they're a Bane. If this aspect isn't completely needed, just don't make them Spirits.

                  Do not define through the power how anyone will react, you're taking from the player the ability and responsibility of interacting with the environment and NPCs to define their relationship. Let the PC act however they want and the NPCs however makes sense. Particularly because in the Umbra anything can be dangerous even if not from the Wyrm.

                  Why does the Vampire loses access to Disciplines when using this level 5 power? Auspex 5 does that because the focus is exploring the physical world unhindered and unmolested. This here is about completely going into the Umbra, the place where everything is possible and every splat is more free to just blast magic around in some fashion. I would rather amp some effects if changing anything at all instead of nerfing the vampire in a place already hostile (not just to Vampires, by the way).

                  Finally, you need to define the crossing. You were so worried about making the vampire not hunted that I see no rules on how they cross, what they need, what they roll, how much time it takes. Those are important factors that can make this potentially op or not at all.

                                  I think the real question is ultimately what is the purpose of Spirit Thaumaturgy/Spirit Manipulation in your game? What does it add that other Paths don't? Why does it exist?

                                  Honestly, I think the only reason it exists is to give vampire PCs the ability to do crossover stuff.

                                  The original Spirit Thaumaturgy was more general in its involvement with spirits - it included things that would clearly be part of the Penumbra in Werewolf, but it also included ghosts. I think the general idea was that the World of Darkness was going to be a connected world with all game lines, and that things like nature spirits and ghosts are part of the Gothic tradition and individual players may be interested in that. It was first detailed in 1991 before any of the other game lines came out, but was clearly informed by the development of the first Werewolf core book.

                                  When Spirit Manipulation was introduced (I think) in 1999's Guide to the Camarilla, it is clearly the same kind of Path, but changed to better reflect the actual Umbra setting in Werewolf and eliminate any duplication with Necromancy, which had been changed from a true Discipline with level 10 powers to a similar system of magic as Thaumaturgy.

                                  If you are not interested in crossover, the only benefits it gives are the ability to get a spirit to perform a task for you, and the ability to create fetishes. These can be useful things, but tend to be a pain in the ass for STs. Any kind of crafting ability can easily imbalance the game, and for some STs it will mean needing to master an entirely new section of the setting just for this one power set (whether it means mastering the lore of Werewolf, or creating your own setting to replace it).

                                  The first levels simply lead up to the spirit slave or fetish making ability, really making them useless as their own powers until you get to the one that is useful to you. The last power becomes irrelevant unless it's purpose is to allow the character to learn more about the spirit world so he can get better use of the lower powers.

                                  Since the activities of the spirit world is irrelevant to vampires, I am not surprised if this does not get used much. The XP would be better spent elsewhere, and reduces the burden on the ST to bring in an entirely new setting element that only exists for this purpose.

                                  Since Revised, the Vampire game has clearly gone in the direction of avoiding crossover and keeping things solely with vampires as much as possible. That really eliminates any need for a Path like Spirit Manipulation. If you disagree and want a crossover power, the Path is fine. You can fiddle with it if you think it'll improve things. If you don't want crossover, I think you really need to figure out what you want the Path to accomplish in a primarily vampire game where these concepts are not important. Rather than spend time of revising Spirit Manipulation, you may just want to think about how to roleplay vampires astral projecting. That's already a spirit world of sorts that vampires have access to, and it's one that is rather neglected in every chronicle I've ever been in. STs tend to run it as essentially empty, and let the players use it mainly as a kind of spying. There's untapped potential there.

                                  Spiritus was a bit different, because it's intended purpose was to make the vampire survive the wilderness easier. It was like a more limited form of Spirit Thaumaturgy, but with clearer benefits, presumably to defend the vampire against Lupines in their home territory, the wilderness.

                                              I think the problem is more that in modern nights, the Path exists in somewhat of a vacuum. During the Dark Ages, calling on a Grain Spirit to increase the yield for your thralls or a Bane to go after your enemies was useful. The first use-case got really out of fashion, and the latter could raise suspicions of being Dark Thaumaturgy (and there are way better Paths for curses).
                                              If I were to rework the Path, I would first try to establish a framework for it to fit into- the first connotation one has with animism is probably the shamanistic tradition, which is a poor fit for the Tremere (save for outliers like the Telyavelic Tremere). But the hermetic tradition has a long history of summoning and binding spirits, too. The difference being that hermeticists favored to call on either spirits of knowledge in order to further their own, or servants to perform industrious tasks.

                                              Personally, I would see a reformed version of the Path - for the Tremere - firmly on the side of dealing with Weaver Spirits, which would be the most useful for them in modern nights, and trade the specialization for greater control over them. There is also little incentive for a vampire crossing the Gauntlet, considering most things on the other side don't tend to like them. The average Tremere mindset (imo) would be to not bother with it when they can just call and control their servants from this side.

                                                  I disagree that the Tremere wouldn't explore the Umbra simply because it does have secrets and sources of power and advantages. If you do introduce the Umbra to a Vampire game and do introduce a means for Vampires to access it on their own terms, then exploring it would pretty much become a necessity, to search for treasure and knowledge, to seek alliances, even to simply secure the Penumbra around your haven and be sure that your enemies aren't using the Umbra to gain an advantage over you while you're not looking.

                                                  But I'm not completely against a power that exists solely for crossover purposes. Crossover isn't a crime. It just have to be thought with this in mind. A power for crossover is something whose existence should be predicated on the chronicle being a crossover in the first place, so if that's not the case the player just can't get the power and that's pretty clear on it. Then, though, you cover your bases for what you think a crossover needs.

                                                  Curiously in this regard I think that being able to cross over the Gauntlet only at level 5 is stupid. If your game uses the Umbra, that is just a place, the PCs need to be able to get there with relative ease. Unless level 1 let you find and use natural portals or something to this effect, crossing over in some fashion should be level 2, 3 at the worst case. The rest of the Discipline needs to give you actual advantages in dealing with spirits instead of just being preparation for the only actual use.
