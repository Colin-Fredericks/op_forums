            I'd give the game an 8 out of 10

            I like the "choice of game" style, and this one is the currently best vtm one.

            For the positives

            Interesting plot

            While the game has a set direction, there's variety in how each situation can be accimplished

            ​​​​There's hidden unique situations, such as a potential friendship with a hunter if you make specific actions in the prologue



            For the negatives

            Minor shades:

            The character creations gives us a lot of personnalisation for our background, but it doesn't matter in the long run. As a tremere, Dr caul could have embraced me as a help on her quest to regain the lost magehood of the tremere, or to mix thaumaturge with advanced science, and that never comes into the game, even when I meet her again. Same for the ravnos site.


            I'd also advise anyone to play in the gm mode which allows you to see which characteristics are rolled.

            Biggest issues

            The game is very long. Which is not be a problem in itself, except if you want to try finding out specific routes, you have to do aaaaaaaaaaaaalllllllll it. And even while ignoring the reading what you already known, it takes a hell of a long time. The game would really, REALLY benefit from a save system. Or a selectable chapter system. Especially since the achievement list give you incentive to replay the game.

            And most importantly.
            ​​​​​
            Fucking julian

            In my first playthrought it was bearable, since I was interested in his plot was going.

            But afterward he's untolerable, you can't ever get him to shut up, no matter how much you try you can't avoid his dialogues, it feels like a writer's favorite, and as said above, he is a textbook Gary stue.

            The "betraying the kindred for the second inquisition" was worth it just for his reaction to the bretrayal and the chance to end him.

            The game would en a straight out 9/10 if we could get Julian to just shut up for the majority of the plot and only appear when his plot is relevant at the end

                I am divided on this game.

                On the positives

                Big clan selection Including game altering ones like Caitiff and Malkavian.

                Low humanity actually makes you more monstrous, in mentality and feeling.

                Picking to perpetuate your existence and accepting what you are, and the like actually has effects.

                I can kill most of the people that annoy me and am given many opportunities to lie.

                Negatives

                Hunger is far too limiting as far as I can tell even though I do not like V5 you are supposed to get rouse checks but it seems as if your character just fails almost every rouse check meaning every use of an ability almost always increases your hunger. This is basically like being a thinblood in v20. As you with a blood pool of five are equivalent to a thin blood with a pool of ten due to their doubled blood cost. This is even more egregious since your character is of a respectable generation 11th. Lower for Nosferatu.

                Disciplines in general feel rather weak especially the social ones, if you spec for a social character you are better off just using social abilities than presence or dominate, which sort of defeats a big part of the point of having them... Using disciplines alongside your social skills should give you even better outcomes, especially presence yet they really do not seem to most of the time.

                Julian, by the dark father I utterly despise him, every time he shows up I long to end him. The fact that you can only kill him in one ending and that ending requires you to fully betray your own kind irks me endlessly. He is a Masquerade risk, and worse than that he is a horrifically manipulative little leech, the fact that no one tries to kill him at all makes me suspect he was the author's little pet. (Sort of like Beckett if I am honest. Though far more annoying.) The type of characters I play would never associate with someone like him shared past or no.

                Diablerie, I have nothing against Caine's loophole but forcing the player to play a diablerist is certainly an interesting take. Especially with how whiny and repentant the character seems to be at game start? Like from the background options given I cannot understand why the character even committed Diablerie if they were going to whine about it so much?

                                I am not quite sure if it is hard or fast but most references to Diablerie say something about the vampire choosing to commit the act, Requiem as far as I can recall explicitly spells out that the vampire's beast will not commit diablerie. Could be mistaken as I have read and played quite a few different books and games over the years.

                                I think the act has to be conscious for a couple reasons.

                                1 in first and second edition it is presented as a choice.

                                "If a character wishes to continue drinking the blood of a vampiric vessel, even after all the blood is drained, he must roll Strength against a difficulty of nine. Each success indicates that the target loses one Health Level. No blood is actually drained, but instead the very spirit of the vampire is taken from the body. Once all the vessel's Health Levels have been taken, she dies the Final Death, and her life-force is passed along to the character. If the vessel was not of an earlier generation, this does not mean very much. Such cannot be done with monals, for their life-forces have already been extinguished by the time they lose all their Blood Points."

                                2 Revised has this to say

                                "Once a vampire’s body has been drained of all blood, the
                                true struggle begins. The diablerist’s player makes an extended
                                Strength roll (difficulty 9). Each success inflicts one automatic
                                health level on the victim (the victim cannot soak, and damage
                                is considered aggravated). When all the victim’s health levels
                                have been drained, the victim’s essence is taken into the attacker
                                and the emptied body begins decaying immediately.
                                A vampire committing diablerie is quite vulnerable to
                                attack. Total concentration goes into the struggle to draw forth
                                the essence of the victim, and stopping for even a moment ruins
                                the chance of capturing the spirit. All attacks against a vampire
                                attempting diablerie are made versus a difficulty of 2.

                                Upon successful completion of diablerie, the diablerist is
                                overwhelmed by euphoria, and a Self-control roll is necessary
                                (difficulty 10 minus the character’s Humanity) to avoid frenzy."

                                Note the text saying to avoid Frenzy, why would this be included if one could diablerize in a frenzy?


                                All this being said there is one character I know of who supposedly diablerized while under the Frenzy Al-Hakim from Jerusalem by Night. There may be more in other books, but as I said I do not have everything.

                                Though I would argue much like the existence of other weirdness in the fluff like humanity zero vampires functioning not as wights and entire cities being blood bonded through wells, that mechanics and fluff do not always mix 1 to 1.

                                So if you want to have frenzy make diablerie possible for drama I say go ahead, as some stuff does back it up. I just believe that the hunger frenzy would not justify it (no more vitae) the rage frenzy would view it as too slow and would rather kill the thing ticking it off as fast as possible (diablerie takes a good amount of time and a number of rolls) and the fear frenzy doesn't make much sense for it.

                                These are of course my beliefs.

                                                    Not letting me quote you for some reason apologies. I hope you see this.

                                                    Humanity zero has as far as I can understand it always been complete loss of self to the beast. Wight is not the correct term for first edition I admit it was one of the terms used for referring to mortals in that edition.

                                                    Granted it is important to note that the beast was different in first edition.

                                                    A couple excerpts from first edition.

                                                    "Humanity: The amount of Humanity a Vampire possesses is vitally important to the character. Vampires need to retain their sense of humanness as much as possible or grow more distant from the human world and thus from their own roots. The less human they become, the more monstrous they get. Eventually, they lose track of what they are and have no means of keeping in touch with any other aspect of their nature but the Beast within."

                                                    "Wassail The Final release and the last Frenzy .
                                                    Occurs when the last vestiges of Humanity are lost and a Vampire plunges into a world of chaos."

                                                    And most notably.

                                                    "The Beast is symbolic of the dark side of the human spirit, the shadow (Jung's word for the suppressed darker half)."

                                                    You are still losing yourself, but it is not to some bestial incarnate of Caine's rage, but rather you have fallen to your shadow, the twisted reflection of yourself made manifest and sentient via the embrace. You are not evil; you are technically just not you anymore since the symbolic Shadow has taken control.

                                                    This actually very important since Golconda originally involved accepting the beast/shadow as a part of yourself in order to stop it from taking you over.

                                                    "Once Golconda has been reached, you are at peace with yourself. You no longer exist in a life filled with self-horror and self-pity. You have finally mastered the Beast within by
                                                    accepting that the Beast is a part of you. There is only one major benefit for having reached Golconda, but this one is enough. When a Vampire reaches Golconda, they no longer Frenzy. No Frenzy rolls need ever be made for as long as they remain in Golconda."

                                                    Later editions made the Beast mindless, but that was not the intent of first edition you are correct on that. However, you are also technically no longer yourself at humanity zero. Mindless you may not be, but you are effectively gone I would argue.

                                                    The sabbat in first edition were not very fleshed out and were listed as humanity zero but what would become paths/roads were hinted at in the original Storyteller's handbook.

                                                    "However, members of the Sabbat seem capable of their own kind of virtues. They possess an intense loyalty to one another. Indeed, among more knowledgeable members of the Camarilla, rumors fly regarding Sabbat mystics, who spend their unlives in quests after knowledge about the history of the Kindred. (Path of Caine?) Others talk about honorable Sabbat leaders who strive desperately to keep the Vampiric world from going up in smoke. (Path of honorable accord?) Some members of the Camarilla even whisper that greatest of heresies -that there may be much the Camarilla can learn from its eternal enemies." (Path/road system in general?)

                                                    And brainwashing after stripping humanity is clearly listed in the core book. (The central step of entering a path/road?)

                                                    "Their initiation procedure is designed to destroy any of the vestigial will in a new Vampire. The Initiates are slain, slowly and painfully, and then given the Blood. This Blood doesn't come from one member of the Sabbat, but from as many as are present at the initiation, combined into a chalice. Once the Initiate has been given the Blood, he is buried alive. He must crawl out in order to live. Those who do not, spend eternity buried under the earth. The process of digging oneself from a Chapter Seven: Setting grave tears the Initiate's Humanity from him and opens him to the Subjugation and brainwashing of the Sabbat."

                                                    God, I miss the original creation rites. Though this form of mass embrace caused a lot of Caitiff it being tied to the Vaulderie was cool, thematic, and very monstrous. Suitable for their original role as antagonists.

A rather distasteful book but one I do possess. This is notable because this book came out after the formal creation of paths. Even if they did not have the paths/roads done for all of the clans/sects listing them as humanity 0 is a huge mistake. One of them was Kemintiri who was actually even brought back from being a wight if I remember right.
