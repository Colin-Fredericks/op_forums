        If those who Sabbat who follow the Path of Honorable Accord cannot tolerate the "NuSabbat" as real Sabbat, then the choice is simple. Either engage in a Fourth Sabbat Civil War to defend their ideals and defeat their internal enemy, or somehow reconcile their Path with the NuSabbat accordingly. (The end result of the civil war might be the defeat of one side versus the other, or the permanent division into two warring sects. Past civil wars did last for many decades or even centuries). Perhaps going into some kind "exile" remaining loyal to a Sabbat that no longer exists and perhaps rebuild the sect. Different followers of the Path might justify all three. But defection to the Camarilla seems pretty impossible IF you want to remain on the Path. (I have no problem with someone abandoning the Path because he comes to some personal crisis and decides he can't be part of the Sabbat anymore, but he'd have to start a new path.)

        The only way members on that Path could defect to the Camarilla is pretty much to abandon the Path entirely and adopt another one. I can't see them adjusting the Path to some other means (like a variation of the Path that some Camarilla archons might be using) because they've already committed treason and dishonor and pretty much destroyed the foundations of their Path. If they commit the ultimate betrayal of their Path once, they can do it again. It'll no longer be effective at holding back the Beast. It'd be a mockery of the Path to betray its foundations, but somehow remain on it constantly justifying your original betrayal by remaining within the Camarilla.

        An otherwise "honorable" Sabbat who is NOT on the Path can defect without the blowback that comes with committing sins of that Path. He'll be seen as dishonorable, but won't suffer Path violations. (And as I said earlier, there might be a few Path followers who were following some extremely rare variant in the Sabbat that did not incorporate the Code of Milan in it. I would still think betrayal of the sect while remaining on the Path extremely unlikely, but conceivably there could be some really strange version that squares the circle. It's just extremely unlikely.)

        The thing about a code of Honor, is that it gives its followers respect by its society because it commits its adherents to certain absolutes. Death before dishonor. A follower of the Code of Milan might justify many things, but betrayal of his sect by joining the Camarilla isn't one of them.

        The captain must go down with his ship. The samurai who is ordered by his lord to commit seppuku must kill himself. The knight bravely charges into a doomed attack because his king commanded it. That's what people with "honor" do. (Sometimes "honor" demands strange things. If you're a soldier you are not suppose to run, but if the unit is being wiped out and you are given the regimental colors to protect them, you're then expected to escape so the dishonor of having your colors captured doesn't happen. So we're not talking about absolutes here, just what the people in that situation consider the honorable thing to do. And of course, different societies can have different ideas of what honor entails.)

        Certainly there are captains, samurais, knights, soldiers, etc. who don't do the "honorable" thing that their society demands. But they won't be seen as honorable. They'll be seen as dishonorable.

        Because of their failure at the Battle of Midway, Admiral Yamaguchi and Captain Kaku decided that honor demanded them to go down with the IJN Hiryu because they had failed their men and Emperor and they wanted to atone for that failure. Whether that was truly needed by the dictates of Japanese honor is debatable. But one thing they did not do is defect to the Americans.


        ​At this point, I think I said everything I mean about this scenario. I understand monteparnas thinks differently. While I would be willing to listen to a PC attempting to justify his Sabbat Path of Honorable Accord Lasombra defecting to the Camarilla and keeping on his Path, I can't think of anything that would cause me to say "Yeah, that works." But I always remember to never say "always" or "never". I might be surprised by the specifics.

                            This starts to feel less about if those following Honorable Accord could move to the Camarilla and more about particular clashing stances on the V5 Sabbat plot, with this discussion as proxy (with "no, you can't, it should be plot and mechanically impossible and auto wight whoever tries, they should have to stay and fight for the sabbat" feeling basically like a note of that if it was acknowledged they could, it would be like agreeing the V5 plot was okay, therefore it can't be agreed with to be possible. And I say this as someone that is pretty solidly on record as disliking almost everything V5 pretty thoroughly. And of course there's what feels like a flip side of "well paths/this specific path are bad anyway, it's great that players can't have them in V5, and your path requiring you to now die, or auto wighting you if you don't is what you deserve for being on this one".) Ultimately, as far as V20 (the system being referred to for use), from a using the actual mechanics perspective, it boils down to at most two path checks for the decision at the time from even a stringent a reading of conscience/conviction checks, and thereby at worst, losing a couple of path dots. You don't actually keep making endless path checks for a particular act once you've done the particular act, that's not how that works. Nor does a single act (for example, defecting) require multiple checks for every single potential morality violation it includes. That's not how that works either.

                            As for griping about something being rationalization? Again, that's part of how the check works. Part of making the check includes the idea that you are able to justify the transgression to yourself and come to terms with it. Those are almost actual verbatim words from the description of what a morality check is. The game we are talking about, as long as the theoretically Honourable Accord follower has a rating of more than, let's say 4, would entirely allow for the notion of defecting path followers being able to both defect and keep their path, even if they fail two path checks (and again, by actual rules, it's really just one act, and thus one check, even making it two checks is already going to a land of overinterpreting the rules here, as though V20's particular take on morality does not align with how some people want the take on it to be), without now being 2 more away from wight-dom. And it's not for certain they would fail them checks at that.

                            For me, my perspective here comes down to "how would this feel as a player in a campaign if this happened." If the ST goes to me "guess what, the Sabbat is now completely different than you knew and pledged to, and in fact as a sect is proclaiming you and everything you believe in to have been wrong and casting you out/wanting you dead." Then it feels entirely fair to go "Well then, if the Sabbat has betrayed me and become a thing of dishonour, my own ethos allows for the possibility of renouncing it as far as if the entire group has become dishonourable, which I thus do." And that an ST reply of "Alright, that does fit within your worldview as far as you've explained it, but it also feels like a rationalization to justify something you know is wrong, make a path check for renouncing the sect for reflecting how much you really believe it and how much it is regardless a measure of spiritual degeneration that you're crashing into, and maybe a follow up one for actually joining a different sect full out." would feel like a fair enough response.

                            A response instead of "No! You have to either keep being Sabbat and probably die or roll and fail path checks until you become a wight!" is the ST being kind of a jerk. Like picture telling a player something like that. And sure, an ST in the first place going "you know that sect you built your entire character around? That sect has deemed you and everything you believe in completely invalid", is pretty bad too, but allowing for a varying response within worldview that comes at potential cost is still better handled than the alternative. I have regardless on an anecdotal level found there are games that do things like want to use V5 plot elements but still keep the V20 system, sizable games even, so pondering this whole thing in a "what would actually feel fair to a player on a level of a game people play" level still feels worth considering.

                            Also, if we're talking V20, I know it's been noted before, but to remind, Honorable Accord is specifically named out as a path that is noted as being followed by "a rare few Ventrue Elders", and otherwise apparently being a thing some Archons and Alastors follow as well, as far as the Camarilla. The V20 take on Honourable Accord is less "this was created by the Sabbat" and more "people on Chivalry/Kings in either sect evolved their beliefs into this Path, it's more popular in the Sabbat because the Sabbat is the sect that does paths as its deal". For an Honorable Accord follower who has just been betrayed by their entire sect, that whole thing being the case is certainly something that might get some notice for "well perhaps then I could find a way to exist in that sect now and maintain my honour."

                            There's otherwise a deeply punitive element to this discussion that feels off. Like it's actively looking for ways to be harsher than even the game actually encourages reading its take on things to be and it's worth taking a step back and remembering that this is also a game people play, not just a narrative to be discussed. A take that boils down to "well, you picked the wrong page in the Choose Your Own Adventure with no indication it would be, so now you have to die" doesn't feel great for me.

                                            They are still people, not computers. Quite capable people, by the way, if they achieved a reasonable ranking in the Path. And just as people they are perfectly capable of weighing exceptional actions on exceptional circumstances. Also of taking such actions even if in calmer nights they thought they never would, because saying you would never engage in something you abhor the notion is still easier when you're not in the grips of the actual circumstances that would call for it.

                                            MarkK got it on spot. Paths aren't closed cultures, and they're not meant to have no room for failure, even against their greatest sins. A Path follower still exists in a broad world and have contact with other ideas. They're not a cult, because cults are meant to be closed systems, stripping members of as much contact with the outside world as possible, Path followers are meant to operate along other people as usual. And they're fully expected to have bad days and falls from grace. It isn't about absolute discipline, it is about staving off the Beast, a battle that is always ongoing with good days and bad days, victories and losses.

                                            A major loss isn't meant to be the end of the war against the Beast, no matter what that loss entails in the outside. And the rules are supportive of this, not of a tight interpretation where a particular sin stripes you of any hope of ever attaining higher ranks again or toss you directly into Wassail. Why would the Honorable Accord be the only Path that works this strictly? That's not in any way what the books say.

                                            Even if you assume the only reason for following your entire clan is personal fear. Not, I don't know, loyalty to the clan.
