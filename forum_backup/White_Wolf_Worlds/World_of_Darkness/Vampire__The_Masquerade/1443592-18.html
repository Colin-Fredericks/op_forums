            Ok, many things going on and a very heated discussion. I won't address all the topics that have shown up but will go with my take. My background is the one of someone that has read and played a bit of V20, later run the V5 Chicago by Night setting for a while as a ST and currently playing a Berlin by Night Campaign with a Ministry PC.

            Writting this I realized the V5 Companion errata addresses or reinforces some of what is being questioned, so kudos for them!

            Personal Horror: I love the hunger dice and what they accomplish:
            • You cannot calculate how many times you can rely on powers, relying to them is a temptation that may come to a cost. On Blood Surges, this has been reinforced with the V5 Companion errata
            • You are always hungry unless you kill. That mostly means, as mentioned before, that you will be mostly always hungry. You are a Vampire after all.
            • And yes, that single die may cause catastrophic damage. This means that yes, you are playing a vampire, and even the nicest of vampires cannot escape the curse. This does NOT mean you are to Frenzy in every roll, and you can still play the cool vampire. Hunger, Rage, Clan compulsions, Messy Crits... are for you to play out or the ST to decide.
            • Players unable to keep their cool: as an example, my PC made his final argument in a tense political conversation doing a manipulation roll that Messy Critted. The ST decreed Hunger Compulsion. My PC took, as a remark of his arguments, a vessel to dance and feed. The target of my machinations joined and fed on the same vessel as well. The scene called for some eerie waltz music to start playing and the camera to zoom out while the two vampires nearly drained their victim. Hey, a Messy Crit turned a mostly "cool" attitude into a "larger than life cool" attitude. Of course, when leaving the room, my PC discreetly called a retainer and urged him to call a doctor.
            All the possible outcomes from such rolls need both the player and ST to "read the room" to decide for the most appropriate one, whether to shake the whole scene or reinforce it as dramatically appropriate. To me, they are not a bug but a feature. They can boost the history and enhance the themes. I understand some players may not like it and keep the control at any time on both resources and PC behavior, but to me making them juggle constantly with their beasts is a benefit. After all, this is a vampire game.

            Political Horror:
            • I guess depends on your campaign. If you want to play an elder's game you'll struggle. V20 may work better.
            • If you want to play Neonates or Ancilla though... Princes have Absolute power in their cities, they need to reinforce it more ruthlessly, both at the "play nice" and "make them fear you" levels to keep it since they are not so old, and Ancilla and Neonate players are in the game. There is still a lot of power to get, but more dynamism. To me, this benefits political games, doesn't hinder them.
            • We haven't played by the Social Conflict rules so not much to say there, but to my understanding, they do nothing else but add tension to whatever is at stake, including the PCs themselves. That calls itself for Political Horror as well.
            So, in my opinion V5 boosts this "vampiriness", and I can understand that the ones that don't want the system to get in their way doesn't like it. I personally do.

            On Blood potency, I like that higher generation vampires are not stuck on a power level and age can make them stronger - to a point. Also I like how the higher Blood Potency levels make Elders much more powerful rather than neonates rather than higher disciplines. I am not fan of the 5 powers per discipline restriction though, and I think it would need to be balanced with higher BP granting more powers, but that is a minor complain.

            About ease of travel, movement and many other stuff... I find it is very relative to the interpretations of the setting. I might be wrong, but to me, it depends more on ST interpretation than system per se.

            My favorite V20 books:
            • The Core book
            • Anarchs Unbound
            • The Black Hand: A Guide to the Tal’Mahe’Ra
            My favorite V5 books:
            • The Core book
            • Chicago By Night
            • Cults of the Blood Goods
            • V5 Companion, with some cool ideas, although I take it more as a completion of the core book (that is, Companion :P)
            Oh, and one last thing... I feel that in this thread there are more V20 defenders than V5 because... V5 are just happy with what they've got. We may like V20 and not feel like criticizing it because it is a cool setting and rules. In my case, I just like the changes introduced with V5.

                        V5 Pros
                        Bestial Failures and Messy Criticals are very fun new mechanics that are fun to roleplay.
                        Compulsions are fun to roleplay, and provide a vast improvement over the old black and white, frenzy or fine mechanics.

                        They also more accurately portray how vampires seem to lose control in fiction, where they visably lose it, but rarely go completely berzerk. As I post in another thread, Jerry Dandrige's behavior in the nightclub perfectly fits into the Bestial Failure and Messy Critical system, not the Frenzy-or-Nothing system.

                        It fits in with Barlow's behavior in "Salem's Lot" as well, where he can't resist executing his servant very cruelly over one mistake, despite immediately thereafter calling him the best servant he's ever had. There's a definite "I should have let that slide. Why couldn't I let that slide?" vibe of regret to that part of his letter. This is just a wonderfully inventive mechanic for portraying the lack of self-control various fictional vampires possess.
                        ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                        Combat is much faster and cleaner. One roll per round instead of four. And no picking out all the damned 1's.
                        ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

                        Animalism and Fortitude are vastly improved. Animalism used to be garbage after level 2. I always hated that it didn't give you more power over swarms of beasts after level 2, when ever increasing control over swarms of beasts is the whole reason I wanted Animalism in the first place.

                        Fortitude now has cool powers that allow you to either duplicate the living-statue nature of old Anne Ricepires and Twilightpires, or the berzerker ferocity of the more animalistic vampires. Huge improvement over a handful of bland and waayyy overpriced for their usefulness soak dice.
                        ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

                        V5 Cons
                        You blew up lots of institutions and characters I didn't want blown up. I liked visiting the great halls of power in vampire. It was kind of like playing superhero video games and visiting Stark Tower, and the JLA Hall of Justice and the Hall of Doom and Arkham Asylum. You blew up Mexico City and Montreal and a great many NPCs I liked meeting. I can't express how much that angered me without a stream of curses and rude gestures. And I don't for the life of me understand why. It's not like their existence interfered with games in local cities. They just provided a cool richness to the world at large.


                        ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------


                        Mooning over Bella Swans. I hate human touchstones. I hate them with a passion. They make my skin crawl. Every time I'm forced to interact with them I want to kill them in such a cruel manner that even a Tzimisce would be taken aback.

                        ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------


                        The absence of Paths of Enlightenment. Without the Long Road Out of Hell homebrew, I would have had no means of running/playing Paths of Enlightenment. I adore Paths of Enlightenment. They allow for truly varied character types, instead of every single character being the Louis/Night Knight type, just with a different powerset.
