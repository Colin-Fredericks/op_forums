    Originally posted by Dogstar View Post
    VtM only works as a setting when the world revolves around the vampires in a certain way, so, when building your own canon, you need to be mindful of the associated tropes and make sure not to break them.
    Yes and no. You can maintain the feel, the genre, and the tropes all while changing the numbers around. For example,

    City A: Fluff typical vampire city with many if not most clans and bloodlines represented, all struggling against each other to maintain or gain power while surviving the next thing that goes wrong.

    City B: Mono-Clan city where only one clan/bloodline resides there permanently. While there may be the occasional power struggle between clan mates, the true danger comes from outside the city from the other clans, other city states, or other supernatural's.

    The first city is what you see in almost every VtM book in an attempt to front load as many plot hooks and ideas as possible to get new players creative juices flowing and make first time STs have more of a "turn-key" experience. The second city keeps all the threats the same, but changes things from "what promotes story stuff" to "how would that work best while maintaining the vibe".

    It is an example of how you can modulate the power of characters without making changes to the actual mechanics balance.

They are structured as rigidly and narrowly as they come. And many 'choices' or 'crossroads' are freaking irrelevant. Like do you want to destroy or save the Eldest Tzimisce? The damn system doesn't help that at all, neither does the chronicle books or the metaplot really (many finer but important things that we should know are not found anywhere).
        When the PCs are not Elders, they should take part in big changes and have agency in tilting the scales, or making some momentous decisions in the thick of it. Elders can plan and execute their changes, Ancilla and Neonates can do it in the moment.

        I find it important to each game table customize their own World of Darkness setting. Give it their own aesthetic, their own plot, their own cointinuity. Otherwise it can feel too tied-down by the real world (and feel just mundane and static instead of living and changing) or an ideal metaplot.

            Here's a couple of ideas I've been toying with for some time, but never managed to incorporate into a chronicle:
            1. Folding the Anarchs into the Sabbat—as in, the Anarchs are now the form assumed by the Sabbat in those cities where it has yet to establish itself as the dominant faction. Instead of Revised era's "sect wars", every territorial concession the Camarilla makes to the Sabbat assumes the appearance of yet another Revolt: instead of one, two or three Anarch Revolts, there's a hundred small revolts taking place every time young & discontent undead decide to oust the local Camarilla. I imagine Sabbat/Anarch control over cities lasts relatively briefly after each revolt, perhaps concurrently with periods of relative decadence and infrastructure collapse (if one wishes to keep the setting "true to life", of course).
            2. Resurrecting the V1 oddity of princes not being an inherent element of the Camarilla. I've always liked the depiction of undead society in Constantinople by Night—it's the only sourcebook that managed to convince me its characters, and the constellation of character relations laid out in the book is unique to that city, instead of being a local variant on an established template (another Ventrue prince, and a Toreador harpy—hooray...). Maybe this is because the sorucebook was a Dark Ages product, but I see no reason why you couldn't do something similar in a modern setting.
            3. Folding the Giovanni into the Lasombra—or, rather, making the Giovanni the descendants of those Lasombra who did not join the First Anarch Revolt. The Giovanni's habit of only embracing family members would've originally been a characteristic of the Lasombra clan as a whole, abandoned by those members of the clan who went Anarch; maybe make them a Camarilla clan while you're at it, too. Also, perhaps doing something similar with the Gangrel and the Tzismice could be fun.
            4. Moving the date of the establishment of the Camarilla to the 1730s, the time of the Austrian vampire hysteria that brought knowledge of vampires to Western Europe (the word "vampire" apparently entered English in 1732). Also, the early 18th century is dead in the middle of the Age of Enlightenment and just a few decades before the start of the Atlantic Revolutions; I suppose the First Anarch Revolt could be seen as a sort-of undead analogue to ideological & political changes in mortal society.
