                    The Technocracy is an interesting storyline to view with the metaplot as they become increasingly impotent the less evil they become. In 2nd Edition, they were an all powerful conspiracy that ruled most of the world but due to Revised making them no longer full-on baddies (regardless of what horrible evil shit they were doing in the NWO Revised book's opening), they've lost 90% of their influence and members so they're more like the Traditions now in that they're rebelling against society versus dominating it.

                    The SAD was probably the richest of the Syndicate for example and they've defected to become full-on Nephandus for example while the rest became Threat Null.

                    So, while they certainly can HELP the SI, I'd argue that in a shared WOD universe, the Technocracy is funding the SI as a proxy organization (and ignoring the Society of Leopold's extensive Celestial Choir-backed influence [or Knights Templar]) because they can't do the kind of military operations they used to.

                    As I pointed out in one of my games, though, a bunch of the SI being clones like Victors or MIB would explain how they can apparently send hordes of disposable troops and no one in the US government noticing the 300 or so losses on US soil. Which is a lot even for America.

                        That's quite an oversimplification of how the Union changed.

                        Nowhere was it ever denied their crimes to begin with. Guide to the Technocracy (a 2nd Ed supplement) makes it perfectly clear that a game of Union agents is a lot about trying to fix it from the inside, because its crimes are real and are many. Also, the idea that the Technocracy isn't in full control of things (even so we can have other enemies be a danger, like the Nefandi) is old news in 2nd Ed.

                        But what weakened the Technocracy was specifically the Avatar Storm, the same thing that weakened the Traditions. They just had more to lose. The notion of corruption within is also a recognition of the place of both the Nefandi in the game and the Wyrm in the Metaplot.

                        Anyway, backing up from the Union can help in a number of ways, but not everything. It's a matter of theme and mood: if you give the SI overtly Technocratic resources, they become a very different thing to deal with. It stops being about the enemy we read about in Vampire and starts to be about a crossover with this whole new splat with a lot of elements and themes that may be great at a game of "Vampire With a Twist", but just doesn't mesh well with standard Vampire.

                        It isn't bad per se. I just wouldn't classify it narratively as the SI, even if they identify as such in the fiction. I'd classify it as another option, where another splat comes and sets it all down.

I was a huge fan of PROJECT: TWILIGHT (a Werewolf supplement) and part of that book's premise was the US government was controlled by agents of the Camarilla, Technocracy, and Pentex all tripping over one another while not realizing they were doing it.                                 If I were to use the Technocracy in a Second Inquisition game, I wouldn't use it as a case of introducing massive super tech and so on but as a bunch of hints that the SI are getting resources that they shouldn't have and shouldn't exist. Things like large scale hidden military bases, advanced technology (but not sci-fi, more improbably Tom Clancy with a vampire strain), and a lot of soldiers that someone should be missing. Enough that the Kindred can suspect it might be someone like the Tremere or Ventrue or Endron behind it.

                                Mind you, the difference in scales should be leveled to an extent as well. Without the Umbra, the Technocracy is far more on the level that can be opposed by vampires.                                 And this being Vampire, the most cynical ending might be the best one where the Technocracy and Camarilla make a formal treaty about the Masquerade and the SI loses its benefactor.

                                                In any case, I do think the Second Inquisition winding down instead of winding up works better for the long term health of the setting. I will also give credit that the game has managed to finangle a lot of plots out of the game.

                                                Swansong and Fall of London were probably the best uses of the SI.

You also get a sense why they're not the good guys even when faced with vampires given their lack of care for collateral damage.

                                                Fall of London because it took the Vienna theory and had Mithras do it for London. It's a lot easier to believe they could beat the Kindred of London when it's a Methuselah being MASSIVELY petty to his former followers.

                                                But if I had to give my favorite use, it was probably "Flyboy" of Chicago by Night as just the idea of a Thin Blood being used as a mole offered so many possibilities while also making it clear that once he tasted the immense power of being a Kindred, he'd want more not less.

                                                Still, I don't think of it as a permanent setting feature.
