        Shadeprowler has done an excellent job of summing up the connection between Koldun and the spirits of nature. However, I would like to complicate that account a bit. Specifically, the idea that Koldunic Sorcery should make Garou and other Fera rethink their opinions of Cainites due to the connection between Koldun and the land. It's not something that comes up too often, but some sources do describe Tzimisce sorcery as corrupting the land and the spirits under its influence:

        Originally posted by Player's Guide to the High Clans, p.185
        What is certain is that Koldunic Sorcery is not gentle or kind to the earth, any more than the Fiends are gentle to the beasts and men who walk upon the earth. Just as the Tzimisce fleshcraft servants to tend their masters better, so too do koldun warp the land and its nature to serve their own ends. Such gross violations of the natural order offend the Lupines, but the Tzimisce have endured too long to flinch from the ire of cringing dogs.
        This quote doesn't quite make the same explicit point, but it does get across that even the Koldunic Sorcery practiced before the Anarch Revolt and the adoption of the Ways was tainted to some extent:

        Originally posted by Tome of Secrets, p.45
        Tzimisce balk at the idea of serving any beings, let alone demons. Yet, something infernal exists in the kraina. Koldunic Sorcery is the raw power of the earth, but also contains the spite and discord of demons — the most infamous being Kupala. Cursed blood should not mix with nature, yet this is the fundament of the koldun.
        It's also important to not that a common feature of the Koldunic rituals listed above frequently include the act of feeding vitae to the spirits you are bargaining with. If you buy into the idea that the Kindred condition is a Curse of some kind, then Koldunism as practiced by the Tzimisce seems to at least partially involve spreading that Curse to the spirit world. And the local Lupines are *not* going to look favourably on that.

        Part of why I spoke earlier about exploring the "implications" of the spiritual nature of Koldunism is that the source material doesn't really go into much detail on the matter. All of those rituals Shadeprowler listed above that involve bargaining with spirits, and there is no official discussion of how Koldun interact with or influence the wider Umbra, beyond the Kupala's Night ritual mentioned above that allows the Tzimisce to destroy the lesser kupala spirits.

        In my personal headcanon, I have tended to view the long-term practice of Koldunism in a region as having a corrupting influence on the local spirits that deal with the Koldun, becoming tainted by the dark power of the blood mage's vitae. While whether or not the Curse of vampirism is actually Wyrmish, I imagine that the effects definitely ping the Wyrm-sense of Garou in the same way that most Kindred do. In particular, the spirits under the Koldun's influence become "bloodthirsty," looking for opportunities to draw blood or injure people, and the Umbral environment itself becomes bloodier and more predatory.

                                vampires are considered evil and unnatural from any point of view
                                I'm firmly of the opinion that something can not exist and be unnatural, they're two exclusive concepts. If it exists, some laws of the universe are enabling its existence. If that were not so, it couldn't exist outside of the human imagination. "Unnatural" is simply a word that means "I don't like that this exists, and I want it to stop existing." And if we're going with the Abrahamic lore and saying vampirism was created by the almighty god of the WoD you cannot get more natural than that. Vampires are totally natural phenomena of the WoD, and that's because the WoD is innately horrific and fundamentally broken place. A symptom of the disease, rather than a disease in and of themselves, if you will.
                                (Sure, the books can call them unnatural all they like, but I disagree with them; that's not a thing.)

                                (This is more of a person interpretation rather than anything I think was intentional by the writers, but I also question the notion that vampires are dead. Like if; a) they're truly dead (they're undead, it's a grey area with them suspended between the states of death (torpor) and life (blush of life). The increased vitality of the highest generations could be taken to suggest that the curse is suppressing vitality, rather than animating a dead thing. Thinbloods didn't die any less than the lower gens, and their vitae should be too weak to animate them to such a degree if it's the curse alone that keeps the body moving) and/or b) that the body is the vampire, rather than the thing inside the body (vitae). You can damage the body to hell and back and it won't make much of a difference, because the body is simply a tool, but if you suck the last drop of vitae out of the heart (or destroy the heart so that it can't contain the heartsblood anymore) then it dies, because the parasite has been removed from the host. In a case where that hypothesis/headcanon is correct, the "walking dead" bit isn't a hurdle because vampires aren't dead.)

                                Nor is anything vampires do particularly unnatural; drinking blood, theft, murder, desecrating corpses, brainwashing, domesticating other species for your own use, slavery, ruining the world around you for your own benefit, violent fight/flight responses, vicious dominance hierarchies and so forth are all things nature is perfectly happy to invent on its own and if nature has an issue with vampires doing these things, nature is a hypocrite. Vampires just have power unseen in the real world and specifically affect humans, that's all.

                                The Wyrm vs Weaver thing is iffy. Overall, they seem far more Weaver aspected more than anything else, but they do lower the Shroud rating with their presence, which maybe suggests something somewhat Wyrm-ish about them if they're causing an entropy effect. Then again, nothing says they can't be tainted by two thirds of the Triat at once.

                                And none of this is to suggest that vampires aren't collectively an awful blight on the world, nor that the other splats don't have good reason to consider them evil considering they're actively harmful to them (either because they'll eat you (humans, hunters, changelings, mages) or because they're worsening the corruption of the world that you're trying to survive in/save (wraiths, garou), just to list two of many reasons).

                                --

                                Also more on topic; regardless of power levels, the main thing Koldunic sorcery has going for it, in my opinion, is how damn cinematic it is. A city facing the wrath of a powerful elder can be plunged into constant darkness under a seemingly eternal thunderstorm, as floods and fires and earthquakes rock the world, animals turn on their owners and go violently insane all over the town for no comprehensible reason, spectres run rampant, people are reported to disappear; sucked into the earth, only to reappear drained of blood, with their faces in a rictus of horror. Horrific visions appear in bodies of water, a cruel voice whispers on the icy winds that appear during what was supposed to be a midsummer heatwave... power be damned, it's great fun. Masquerade shattering fun, but fun, and a great backdrop for a story.​

                                Prone to hyperbole

                                                    One of the most delightful things about the World of Darkness, Orkar, is that it's overflowing with liars

                                                    Even in the very rare instances where the authors come out and give you answers to the Big Questions, they will usually 1) push very hard that they are offering one interpretation and you are welcome to do your own thing instead, and 2) present several contradictory options.

                                                    So for example, with Vampires, most of the material is going to push the Caine/Book of Nod message. But you're also going to have the Setites disagreeing. The Laibon also have some opinions they'd like to share. And that doesn't even consider that the Garou story about the Bloody Man may actually be the right answer.

                                                    And then if we do settle, 100%, that we're going with the Noddist interpretation, which one? The modern "Book of Nod" and the "Erciyes Fragments" have a lot of differences between them, which is the accurate interpretation?

                                                    Now, take all of those questions, and apply that level of skepticism to the generic background myths handed out to the Garou or to Mages. At the end of the day, none of them are ever more true than the Storyteller wants them to be; beyond making sure that the mechanics and the lore, the crunch and the fluff, line up with each other, the Storyteller can do pretty much whatever they want so long as everyone is having fun.

                                                    So if it seems a little strange that some of us are pitching things completely at odds with what the books have said, it's because the books are intentionally unreliable at times

                                                    Also, the Tzimisce claim that Koldunism is the oldest blood magic, and figuring out the specific order of many prehistoric events in the World of Darkness is rather tricky. So don't take the Baali's claim about doing it first at face value, especially since they're Baali :P

I'd have to double-check how long it takes for lesser Blood Bonds to fade, but there's a good chance that performing the ritual once per year would not push the Bond past level one. And a level one Blood Bond simply isn't that powerful of a connection; it gives the regnant a bit of an alluring "je ne sais quoi" and that's about it.
