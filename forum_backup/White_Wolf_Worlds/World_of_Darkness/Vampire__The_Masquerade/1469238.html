    How Gothic is you Game?

    11-30-2021, 04:40 AM
    Sometimes in those threads of ours someone reminds us of the Punk in Punk Gothic. The struggle against The Man and all that.

    But I notice a lot of trends in the posts that are pretty much against the other side of this coin, the Gothic. Where are the Gothic tropes? The themes of the genre?

    Maybe most people here are used to the Gothic aesthetics, and think it kind of stops at that. Or that Gothic = Dark and Brooding. But me, I'm a huge fan of Gothic Literature, a complex genre with a long history that predates black and leather by quite some centuries.

    A genre about the nature of evil and the importance of choice. About villains with mundane dreams and fears, made dangerous by obsession and pride and plagued by the consequences of their actions. About temptation and Free Will, where redemption depends but on making the right choice, and accept the price of doing what's right instead of what's convenient.

    There was a reason Golconda wasn't so elusive in 1st edition. I don't think it should be. I don't think everyone and everything should be corrupt. Gothic isn't Grimdark and Grimdark is childish (on purpose, by the way, it does not take itself seriously, not when well done).

    I said in another thread some of the principles of the Gothic genre. Moralism, yes, but of a Humanist tone and through a naturalist lens. Evil isn't sin against God, but against other people, imprudence begets catastrophe and evil is self-punishing. Choice matters. Past behavior is predictive of future behavior. You reap the fruits of what you do. Change is hard, but never impossible. But denying it is easier than accepting responsibility.

    What others think of it? How do you understand the Gothic part of the game and how do you play by it?

It would only mean something in the context of a greater goal, a choice to action instead of inaction.

                          This reminds me of two of my own characters, the Gaia worshiper Salubri and the Sabbat Tzimisce Doctor. Both had high Humanity, one actively seeking ways to improve the living of others and the cause of her faith, the other trying to avoid problems, pretend callousness and deliberately choosing actions that he could use to justify not risking his hold on the Beast. She was a Golconda seeker, possibly with a chance. He was just a coward, no matter how competent at his farce (and he was quite).

                                      Originally posted by Manfr View Post
                                      Now that I think of it, I probably never had a Gothic campaign, and seeing your pitch, I'd definitely like to play one.
                                      The two tips I can think of now to give you.

                                      First, think about what your characters really want. Not their goals, but their desires, that which makes them happy. Love, respect, family, peace. Some may desire power, those that find power itself intoxicating, but want is different from desire, most covet power as a means to have the lives they picture for themselves.

                                      Second, picture how the natural consequences of their actions keep them from achieving those true desires, either by keeping them out of reach or by tarnishing them to an undesirable state. The character may hold to a number of beliefs that justify their actions and make them unable to see how they are sabotaging themselves, or may even see their own desires as a flaw, denying themselves out of pride.

                                      An example would be a character that wants true friendship, but believes that only by achieving a high position they'll have the respect and attention necessary to make meaningful friends. Then just run in your mind the story of the character sacrificing relationships, making compromises with foes, and generally becoming and amassing around themselves the exact kind of people they despise.

                                      Remember that "true desires" in this context are simple, but deep. They are the root cause of your beliefs, not the beliefs themselves. Frequently they are unconscious and they may be ill-defined, this is not a problem, as long as you can see the character setting the circumstances to make themselves miserable.

                                      Let them free at the end to make others miserable as they lash out to the world, working their plans and beliefs, indulging their pride, and, secretly even to themselves, dumping their frustration on others. Know desires, let consequences run their course.

                                                  How Gothic is my World of Darkness?



                                                  Very.

                                                  I personally think the Gothic element of V:TM is more prevelant than the punk element. Yes, there's a lot of conflict between the Anarchs and the Elders, the young and the old, and the rich versus the poor but the game doesn't necessarily force you into the position of the underdog. You can side with the decadent Toreador, the ultra-wealthy Ventrue, or the duplictious Tremere just as well as the other side. This is a Gothic Punk game where, if it was a cyberpunk game, you could also be a corporate agent as well as the plucky underdog.

                                                  The way I handle V:TM is that the cities are divided between the decaying slums and the gleaming towers. That the Camarilla is full of inbred decadent and corrupt old fossils that are always plotting against one another because it is literally the entirety of the point of their existence. There's all sorts of hidden secrets, terrible abuses, and tragic pasts. I encourage all of my players to have melodramatic PAIN and ANGST.

                                                  My vampire society is full of Eyes Wide Shut parties, childer abused by their sires, secret affairs, deals with the Devil, corruption, and unspeakable drama across family lines/bloodlines. Guilt and horror at what you are constant themes with almost no vampire not suffering some existential horror at the bleakness of their existence and filling the void with lust, power, hate, or pleasure.

                                                  I mean, if you're a vampire, you have almost certainly committed some horrible unforgivable act that haunts your nightmares. I encourage all of my players to have some terrible guilt from the Hunger or tragic backstory: abusive sire, dead family, murderous frenzy, or something else that is a terrible secret. Kindred society is built on tragic romances, obsessions, seduction, envy, revenge, and murderous jealousy. Every Kindred is obsessed with these larger than life emotions and willingness to break every taboo.

                                                  Kindred are romantic and tragic as well as horrifying as well as disturbed.
