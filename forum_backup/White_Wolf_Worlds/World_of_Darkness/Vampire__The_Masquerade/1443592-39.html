                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                    Because Modiphius wasn't getting their books out fast enough.
                                    That's only one step in the chain. And it certainly doesn't explain a confidence in the product as an artistic success, even if it supports your later point that V5 is, to Paradox, just a hype builder for other more lucrative projects (of course, even if a bunch of the existing transmedia projects are probably more profitable than the TT books just because TT books are shit for profits, it doesn't mean anything in that area has actually returned on the investment yet).

                                    The multiple global printings certainly help.
                                    Number of printings in a vacuum is a fairly meaningless data point because it's simple to inflate. A company that orders print runs of 1K books, and is on its fifth print run isn't outselling a company that does 10K print runs and is just about to start a second.

                                    This is also tempered by multiple print runs each coinciding with a significant errata (as in either a major addition such as the safety tools appendix, or a minor change to a core mechanic like tweaking certain values from 0-5 to 1-6) worked in. Even if they're just putting those in each print run, the fact that it keeps happening isn't great, and it gives a negative impression.

                                    None of the publicly available data backs up the print runs as being equally large as the second on (aka the first main print run after the first initial preorder one). There's nothing wrong with doing small print runs after a books first big push... that's when most physical sales are going to happen and you don't want to carry a large stock if you don't have to. But it means "hey another print run" isn't the same as, "hey another print run that means we sold as many books this time as we did the best time."

                                    And it doesn't address anything regarding the performance of the supplements.

                                    Even then, it's stood the test of time financially after gross grandiose overspending.
                                    No, it didn't. It's budget was completely in-line with the rest of the Star Wars movies of the same era, and where all the others were massively financially successful, Solo managed to barely squeak over the line in global box office sales to end up with a higher ticket sales than production budget. Which by no means indicates that it actually turned a profit because there's lots of other factors in movies that go into it. Just managing to avoid a project going into the red doesn't mean it "stood the test of time financially."

                                    No, it's instead the foundation for video games and mass media including television shows as well as movies.
                                    Then why even bother with the level of investment of TT support? Paradox doesn't need this headache to make video games, shows, and movies. The original VtM run had lots of mass media stuff just fine. They could have easily ask Onyx Path to do this: Onyx Path was already planning a new edition, Paradox just needed one that would be friendly towards their other goals, and then Paradox could have but in the money for marketing, distribution, and so on that Onyx Path doesn't have to push VtM back into the lime light.

                                    If it's primary purpose is to be a foundation for other media, being an artistic or financial success is, generally, meaningless. It needs to be a PR success first and foremost. Pissing off a bunch of fans in the name of artistic vision is not a strong PR move.

                                    Though I do find it weird that this is the point being argued when my point is, "I'm really bummed out by people who think it isn't successful as if that is something they think is even possible to believe at this point."
                                    Maybe because you make your point in a fashion that invites counterpoints that you don't really intend?

                                    If all you want to say is, "it doesn't matter that anything after the successful hyping of V5's initial release isn't a major financial success, because VtM is back as a brand now and non-TT products are what really matters," you shouldn't be making comments about how artistically successful V5 is as a gameline.

                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                    I'm pretty sure that V5 was designed specifically to be a Video Game adaptation.
                                    Doubtful. While there are video game influences (Martin literally said some of the V5 ideas were things that were taken from the failed MMO design stages), that game isn't particularly well suited to video-game translation. It actively increases the amount of reliance on GM judgement calls, which is the hardest thing for a video game to handle well. Video game AI is absolute shit for trying to figure out if a fight should be a single roll off, a three round capped conflict, or say "screw the book, Five Act structure is actually a thing to, lets let this combat go a bit longer." "Keep fighting until a metric is accomplished," is something much easier on a video game.

                                    Also, quite a few of what is in V5 is an adaptation of what was in Bloodlines.
                                    That seems quite the stretch. Lets also remember that the cult darling Bloodlines is the heavily modded and fan-patched Bloodlines that changes some of these things (like options to make the Sabbat a faction you can join...).

                                    Choose between Anarch and Camarilla
                                    And the KotE, and "none," which don't really work well for V5 right now.

                                    Lots of Hunters that kill you if you break the Masquerade
                                    Always been a thing, and they're Society of Leopold hunters, not the SI.

                                    Generation is an irrelevancy unless its a plot point
                                    Generation is extremely relevant in V5 even if it's no longer selected individually and all the mess that goes with that.

                                    Much simpler Disciplines
                                    Bloodlines kept, and even expanded the number of Disciplines that are a single power that gets better each level like Potence/Celerity/Fortitude. V5 made Disciplines more complicated by having different multiple different powers at each level. This claim makes no sense if you compare Bloodlines's implementation of Auspex or Obfuscate compared to V5's takes on them.

                                    Very fast combat
                                    Bloodline's combat isn't particularly fast. You're not storming the SoL stronghold in Bloodlines and only doing three rounds of combat. Now, in Bloodlines, if you spec for stealth/combat you can tear through most enemies like butter and the boss battles aren't too bad on time, but there's still waves and waves of them as some points, and if you try to make a non-combat character... those things can just be utter slog-fests.

                                    And, of course, they're "fast" in completely different ways. You can be really fast at BL combat be having strong FPS skills (getting lots of head shots and knowing how to avoid attacks without having to care about your stats). This is not the same as, "we capped combat at three rounds to keep things moving."
