    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

    * The Souleater concept feels out of step with the themes of V:TM and more werewolf.
    To be fair, the Tzimisce and the Shadow Lords had an intertwined history, back and forth explored in DA books, VTM books and Werewolf books, so in a strange yet fitting way the Souleaters for that age/edition mindset with the echoes of the lingering crossover "friendliness" of the early editions were quite understandable and... logical
    I mean the Gangrel could have been easily tied to the Wyld in similar way, even have Protean as a Wyld infection not unlike of the Black Furries' Metamorphic Plague or link the Brujah via their weakness to Frenzy to the Beast-of-War, but no other clans really had a presence so emphasized than the Tzimisce
    It was an interesting angle and personally I think The Black Hand: A Guide to the Tal'Mahe'Ra gave us a nice "compromise" in the form of the Path of Asakku (with a neat little link/possibility to Aretstikapha and the whole Qlippoth concept a neat Lovecraftian style)

        I admit I actually note that A LOT of Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand filtered out from the Black Hand to the rest of the line and it doesn't get any credit for it:

        * The Bahari and Path of Lilith
        * The True Brujah
        * The Soul Eaters actually became the main focus of Revised's handling of the Tzimisce Antiediluvian and his Gehenna scenario. Except instead of Vicissitude being Banes infiltrating people, it was the means for the Eldest to infect people.
        * The Old Clan Tzimisce
        * The Nagaraja
        * Revenant Families

        It seems like a pretty large chunk of the book became pretty important to the metaplot and backstory of the game in general.

                Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                So instead of using this stuff outright as written, I now like to think of DSotBH as more of a showcase of the kind of mysteries you should be creating for your own version of the setting. I keep some things that were introduced in this sourcebook, change others, and ignore anything I don't like.
                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                I admit I actually note that A LOT of Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand filtered out from the Black Hand to the rest of the line and it doesn't get any credit for it.
                This was also my experience and that of friends. When the book came out, the general attitude was it was too gonzo and a resource for munchkins. Yet, everybody seemed to like some idea in it. In retrospect it feels like an overflow book with concepts omitted from various earlier books that were tied together with a narrative for the sake of publication, and they bundled up some things that didn't really fit with others. So cherry-picking and re-contextualizing was what most did with it.
                That so many of the ideas work on their own tells me that they are generally good ideas, and the problem was more with the presentation. I think the biggest points of conflict for most were the Souleaters and the overpowered Temporis together with the over-the-top setting. Having so much crammed into a single book also limited the space for development and background of the ideas, which may actually have been a good thing for when they got developed in later books.

                I generally like the V20 version as they tried to make the hodgepodge assembly somewhat more coherent, but it ultimately suffers from the same problem that so many of the things feel weirdly out of place when put into a single pot.

                    I'm a little late to the party on this thread, but here goes. I viewed Dirty Secrets of The Black Hand as a flawed mess that had an interesting idea behind it with a sect made up of elders and ancients who are manipulating the Sabbat and Camarilla for there own goals to serve what they think is the antidulivans will. But the setup was subpar and some of the ideas were not that great if I go by all the comments from old reviews of the sourcebook.

                    What I liked were the sect believing they were serving the will of the 3rd generation and how they would play off the two sects off of each other and you could play potent elders if you wished and they had creation rules for that. I found the idea of Chatterlings interesting and creepy about young men and women being raised since childhood to become Kindred at early adult hood and would make even the most jaded kindred take a step back and might even feel sorry that these folks were denied a normal childhood and showed how even more inhuman these beings were.

                    I liked the Old Clan Tzismce and Nagaraja lineages and what they brought to the table and I liked some of the new revenant families they created.

                    What I didn't like was the Soul Eaters subplot being the source of the Fiends signature discipline and the introduction of the True Brujah bloodline with there signature power. I could get behind a line of Brujah that would lose there emotions over time as their founder was very cold in the beginging before whatever breakdown he had to create two distinct lineages. I could accept a line that lost there emotions, but I couldn’t get behind Temporis as it fit better in a Mage or Changeling settings.

                    I know that this sect saw themselves as "good" guys but you knew and saw right away it was a falsehood.

                                                    The one thing I never liked about the Tal'Mahe'Ra, besides (just like the Black Hand) the orientalism aspects of it, was that many times it's very confusing where the Hand Without a Sun commences and where the Black Hand ends. Like both using Seraph as a title makes everything weird. Having infiltrated members on the Black Hand, but hardly being clear how and when it is done deepens de confusion.

                                                    Although I think the Inconnu end up being 'just a thing' and not a phenomena, not something that acts and moves, they manage to be surrounded by mystery, by an aura, some form of purpose. The TalMaheRa in my view never could be more than the 'I'm cooler than you cuz I'm from the secret faction inside the secret faction inside of the sect, inside the secret society of vampires'. Cuz not only is a hotpot of unique Bloodlines, but full of exclusive magics, wizards, mummies, and powers, and they have the OG vampire HQ just for baller more status.

                                                    But when it comes to the idea of a conspiracy that worships the Antes, and is as much of as paranoid fanatics as the Sabbat, but instead of destroying them they want to stand on their side, I think they are awesome. I love the idea of their struggle against the Lost Tribe for control of the Black Hand, although that basically is unexplored in the metaplot. Although that sometimes just muddles very heavily the Black Hand itself, and the Sabbat by an extension.


                                                    What I like most, though, is cloaking them in as much of secret, conspiracy and esoteric mystery as possible. One of the most consistent eyes and hands of the Eternal Struggle, comparable to the Inconnu. With the very meta-understanding that they, in all their knowledge of Enoch and Noddism, still aren't 100% sure if the Antediluvians are all what their knowledge and legends relate them to be. They are like too-deep and invested to back down now, but it is still a big freaking gamble if all their work, their horrendous acts, their centuries-spanning plots, will bear fruit, or be hubris.
                                                    It is very Personal Horror and Gothic for them to know not if their Gods exist, if they know Their design, or if they are of any relevancy to Their plans.


                                                    And yeah, they should be very few but apex in power and knowledge, and also have taken part in some of the most Darkness of the World of Darkness out of pure research, functionality and projects. To me they are a cooler, grimmer and material-oriented version of the inconnu, that want to escape from the Curse by being in the side of the Divinely Cursed, instead of wanting to escape it by forsaken and somehow surpassing the Curse spiritually.

                                                    Strange... When coincidence seems too convenient, I prefer to call it fate.

                                                    -Blood Omen: Legacy of Kain d=
