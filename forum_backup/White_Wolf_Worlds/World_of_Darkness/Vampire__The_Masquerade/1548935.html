    Running the Free States - 5 - Setites

    03-04-2025, 12:30 AM
    A big part of the backstory of LABN involves the Setites, and the lone Setite NPC is portrayed as an antagonist. However, the Setites are quite underdeveloped as a setting element. So we’ll discuss them here. First though, a brief history of canon.

    The Setite Dawn Cavanaugh arrived in Malibu in 1923 and established her first temple there in the Malibu Hills. She was very successful in attracting adherents and helped make Hollywood a sin city. We’re told that in the days of Don Sebastian Malibu was a major Toreador hangout and a hundred Kindred could gather there spontaneously for a night’s evening. (It’s likely at one of these parties was where the Blount sisters’ father/sire was embraced in 1923.)

    Then in 1944 the Garou of the Black Furies tribe that controlled a caern in Topanga Canyon wiped out the Temple of Set in Malibu as it had corrupted a number of Garou and Kindred. The Garou wiped out many elders who dwelled or visited there. The exact date of this was December 21, 1944 which was the winter solstice. Other than that being the longest night of the year, I’m not sure if that has any special significance. Although if we go by Werewolf: the Apocalypse, there is the winter solstice seasonal Rite of the Winter Winds which ends in a Revel where the Garou remove anything that threatens the caern. So there might be a religious significance to the timing of the Garou attack on the temple. It may have been serendipity that the Lupine assault coincided with the anarch attack (as well as the slaughter of Don Sebastian and other elders at his ranch by what I assume was Christopher Houghton) all on the same night.

    Dawn was not at the temple at this time, but in San Francisco on business. Her biography states she may have been tipped off. If so, it was likely one of the corrupted Garou who warned her. However, it is possible she may have blamed MacNeil’s anarchs as they were heading there until warned off by a female Garou that they had already done so and that Malibu was now free of vampires. She might now have known who actually did it. Dawn stayed in San Francisco for the next two decades, but moved back to Los Angeles after the failed Sabbat invasion. She established a second temple in 1969, this time in the Hollywood Hills.

    This second temple was betrayed by her acolyte Nicholas (likely sometime in the seventies) who wanted to remove her so he could take over. However, his plan failed when he local anarchs to whom he revealed the location of the temple, notified MacNeil and he lead an attack on the second temple that wiped it out. Nicholas likely hoped a small group of neonate anarchs would attack and die in the attempt, but it would provide him an opportunity to slay Dawn himself. Instead, Dawn was once again absent, this time being forewarned by someone in MacNeil’s coterie.

    Dawn fled to Chicago and left that city after the events of Under a Blood Red Moon to gain revenge on MacNeil and then establish a third temple (in that order). Her preferred method is to seduce and corrupt vampires, presumably because they can corrupt others more easily.

      Before I get into any serious discussion of the topic, I took one look at ‘Malibu Hills’ and vampire surf-and-turf, then thought “BAYWATCH David Hasselhoff Vs Just Another Snake Cult - who wins?”*

      Truly, I have watched far too much BAYWATCH in the last year or two.


      *Answer: The Audience.


      Anyway, back to the actual topic:-

      (1) It’s interesting to note that while Dawn Cavanaugh hasn’t done very well in the Free States, she has a long record of prospering - or at least operating for long periods - in Camarilla cities.

      I wonder if her training and experience as a Victorian embrace specifically-trained to infiltrate & subvert European colonial society makes it much harder for her to operate outside polite society? (The Anarchs may occasionally be civil, but I doubt the Free States qualify as polite society by any definition).


      (2) Interestingly, Dawn Canaugh is of the same generation as Hesha Ruhadze (8th) and Abu Ruhadze, the latter’s Sire, is noted as a Setite who “… also worshipped the Islamic faith” (Possibly suggesting a syncretic approach, merging Setite principles with Islamic theology or vice versa, possibly only suggesting an unusually strong cover as a Muslim holy man - albeit a presumably Unorthodox one).

      Given that Hesha Ruhadze was embraced in the late 17th century and his Sire was still doing the rounds in the late Victorian Age (Dawn Cavanaugh was embraced in AD 1898, Abu Ruhadze is noted to have adopted his newer name in Modern Nights), so I’m tempted to suggest pencilling in Abu Ruhadze as the Sire of Dawn Cavanaugh.

      This is, admittedly, because I’m not sure how to chase down a list of Followers of Set from the Seventh Generation, but it does make sense that a Follower of Set with a strong Islamic connection and a potent Cult of Personality would be able to persuade a Sheikh who participated in the Fall of Khartoum and survived the downfall of the Mahdist Movement to part with one of the women from his following.


      (3) Incidentally, Cavanaugh’s background as the daughter of a British officer captured in the Fall of Khartoum (family killed in the sack) and given over into the household of a Sheikh is vaguely-possible, but given that she was born in AD 1882 and Khartoum fell early in 1885, there’s absolutely no way she remembers any of it personally.

      It also bears considering that this is exactly the sort of backstory a young woman plucked from North African obscurity by a Setite because of her red hair and then sent to infiltrate European colonial society all across the Middle East (In the interests of subverting it) would conjure up as part of her legend - a legend that might prove equally appealing in 1920s Los Angeles, given the immense popularity of (Amongst others) Mr Rudolph Valentino as THE SHEIKH.

      That it’s a backstory calculated to appeal to Victorian tastes to a degree that makes it hard for a more modern age to swallow might offer an interesting angle on the character - as someone from a very different time and place who has increasing difficulty accommodating herself to modern society (“Hence Camarilla city = no probs” as a wag might put it).


      In any case it seems reasonable to sum up the experience of her confirmable early life as a teenage girl from the household of a North African sheikh, possibly from the Sudan, who was picked out by an Elder Setite (Seventh Generation, remember) because Red hair is strongly associated with Set and presumably because she was (or at least looked) white enough to pass muster in European circles.

      Whether she was actually the daughter of a British officer or had been anywhere near Khartoum in her life is an question whose answer remains lost to Dawn Kavanaugh (Who would most definitely have adopted that name only after becoming a vampire: if she had a Muslim name, it seems to have been dropped a long time ago).



      (4) An equally interesting question is how much of a connection Dawn Kavanaugh kept up with her Sire and the Old World Followers of Set - my guess is that, having gone from a mere handmaiden (possibly a slave) in the tents of a back country sheikh into European colonial circles (I.E. The upper crust and higher leadership in political, financial and to a degree social circles) across the Middle East (and as the daughter of a British officer ‘martyred’ alongside the celebrated Gordon at Khartoum), she might well have gotten used to a degree of comfort almost impossible to maintain as an active subversive in the days of the Great War - hence her move to the Western Hemisphere, which might well have dovetailed neatly with Setite interests in destabilising the Great Power least affected by the First World War.

      This would suggest that Cavanaugh would maintain a generally-positive, albeit distant connection with her Setite elders: on the other hand, the very fact she sailed off to the other side of the world might suggest the desire for that connection to be distant and respectful on her part (and it’s not impossible that her repeated failures in LA - especially the debacle precipitating the fall of the second Temple - might have persuaded the Followers of Set to cut Dawn Cavanaugh loose).

      So the question of how closer her ties to the Setite elders might be remains an open one - though I lean towards the notion that Dawn prefers to avoid close attention from her superiors, at least at this point in her career (It now occurs to me that Nicholas’ bold move against the boss might have been prompted by the suspicion that their elders would be more interested in a success than in questioning exactly which hands they received it from).


      (5) It seems highly likely that the longest night of the year would be the perfect occasion for a massive party of vampires to … well, to do anything, really … and given that the mass gatherings in Malibu had tended to be supremely indulgent, it’s not hard to imagine both the Anarchs and the Garou taking advantage of such a distraction, the Anarchs striking in the core of LA while significant elements of the Toreador leadership were busy elsewhere and the Black Furies popping the local ticks whilst they were rather the worse for their indulgences.

      That this would - at least for the Garou - neatly coincide with one of their many annual rituals involving a killing spree would, of course, be either a very happy coincidence or a further blessing (depending on how one looked at it).


      (6) It’s interesting to wonder if it might have been one of the corrupted Garou or Kinfolk who have the Black Furies their actionable intelligence - I’d bet on it being a Kinfolk spilled the beans to Dawn Cavanaugh though, if anyone actually did (Given that a Garou getting in touch with one of the Kindred at a time when the local pack is planning an offensive would be very, very noticeable - and extremely dangerous, when the Garou in question are Black Furies rather than Shadow Lords).

      For my money making Dawn Cavanaugh aware of only one, rather than both, of these impending offensives makes more sense on a character level - keeping a safe distance whilst the local Authorities and the local rebels work out who gets to run things, then coming back to help rearrange things once the argument is resolved (Hands free of blood to boot) is a decent plan, but making your way to San Francisco when your temple and your followers are about to be wrecked by Mother Nature’s Hairiest War Machines in Malibu smacks of failure, rather than sound policy.

      Put another way: If Dawn Cavanaugh laid on a Vampire Beach Party at the Temple of Set in order to arrange for her assets to be covered while she kept a safe distance from the brewing strife in LA (All the better to present herself as an innocent - or at least a neutral party - in the aftermath), only to be blindsided by aforesaid Werewolf War Party, then this presents a fairly convincing picture of the Fog of War turning a reasonably shrewd ‘hand’s off’ policy into a disaster.

      Especially if Dawn being ‘warned in advance’ was part of a deception plan calculated to remove the Kay clear-thinking vampire at any Setite festivity (aka the Head Priestess) from the equation, all the better to wreck the Temple and it’s congregation.


      That notion rambled on a little, but I do like the idea that whilst Dawn Cavanaugh’s absence was planned, the plan was based on incompetence intelligence (and possibly on a deception plan, though whether of Garou or Kindred construction I’m not sure).


      (7) While one enjoys the fact that the ever-subtle Setites were absolutely wrecked by the frequently fangs out, fur on fire Black Furies (‘Amazons Vs Snake Cult’ is purest PULP, even when you remove the ’werewolf’ and ‘vampire’ from the equation), some small part of me would have loved to see a shout-out to Vic Stryker and his Eagle’s Nest pack (Burning out Setites is absolutely one of their party tricks and the gang deserve a Last Hurrah before oncoming tragedy hits them like the end of a Golden Age).

      Not least since there’s nothing like brutally wrecking a Follower of Set’a entire world to put a spring in a Silent Strider’s step (and very little more calculated to bring out their Best efforts).


      (8) Interestingly, the date given for Dawn’s return to LA is AD 1965, so it would seem that the establishment of the second Temple was the fruit of several year’s hard work in the wake of the Sabbat Attack.

      I wonder if any Setites built their bone fides with the Anarchs by playing some role in the fighting? (Or at least in the mopping-up).


      (9) There doesn’t seem to be very much on Nicholas anywhere, but the mental image of his being very much an LA local to Dawn Cavanaugh’s ‘blow in’ has been consistent part of my mental image for the character from my first conception of him - your description of the circumstances surrounding the fall of the first Temple make me wonder if he was one of the rare survivors of the attack (and also whether he might have been behind claims - true or false - that the High Priestesses’ absence in San Francisco indicates that she knew the attack was coming, then abandoned her followers & rivals to perish*).

      Incidentally, it’s taken me this long to get a strong mental image for this character, but the mental image of him as a very Sam Spade Private Eye (I.E. a profoundly mercenary chiseler with the face of “…a friendly Satan”) as played by Mr Willem Dafoe has struck me and refuses to be shifted.

      I’m assuming that a Setite would embrace a mercenary PI as a source of intelligence and a means of disseminating disinformation (Or at least arranging some serious blackmail), as well as acquiring somebody who not only knows where the bodies are buried, but knows how to exhume them for fun and profit (Also that ‘Satanic PI’ would make an excellent, rather subversive villain for Film Noir).


      Incidentally, should Nicholas need a surname, I’m assuming that it should echo some neighbourhood or district of Los Angeles (To emphasise his nature as a local boy who would have been a local hero, if only he weren’t such a heel - or perhaps as a subtle way to hint at an alias … or both).

      Nicholas Baldwin? Nicholas Hancock? Nicholas Hyde?


      *The possibility that she knew the attack was coming, but moved to save herself alone, might be equally damaging (Though a great deal depends on exactly when and in what form the warning arrived).


      (10) Incidentally, the second Temple being located in the Hollywood Hills circa the late 1960s gives me a strong ‘Manson Family’ vibe - I wonder if making the Brujah convert who may have sold out Nicholas to Jeremy MacNeil (and warned Dawn Cavanaugh to be elsewhere) a Manson Family type would make sense?

      An alternative that occurred to me was an African-American trying to get away from White civilisation and back to Mother Africa could well have fallen in with the impeccably-Egyptian Followers of Set (Having been embraced by Clan Brujah as more of a Black Panther type).

      Actually, making him an more authentic Nation of Islam-type than ‘Papa Crypt’ might well explain how Dawn got her hooks into him (She having been raised Muslim, albeit apparently low on the social pyramid) - as well as explaining her ‘In’ to modern LA circa the early 1990s (Back in the days when the Islamic world was mostly the stuff of Arabian Nights to the wider USA, prior to 9/11) in a way that sets up possible plot hooks (One assumes that ‘Papa Crypt’ would take an interest in somebody hitting at least a few of the same notes as his own preaching and would, above all others, be able to pick out somebody who talks Allah, but may be thinking of a very different god).


      Anyway, that should do by now - hopefully none of my notions are overdone!🙂

      Before I go, might one ask if Dawn Cavanaugh’s Nature & Demeanour are listed in any of your sources? I can’t find them online and would appreciate the useful insight into a characterisation that they offer,

                  Dawn should be a formidable opponent, but is nothing really special mechanics wise. She’s not even an ancillae in LABN, being just under a hundred years as a vampire. Her character sheet is fine, but nothing special. The one advantage she has is that her heart is located in an urn at the Grand Temple in Uganda (so she can't be destroyed by anyone in LA). Since Dawn does not have Serpentis 5 (she only has it up to rank 4), we can assume her sire removed her heart and this would compel her to be very loyal to the clan elders (since they can destroy her). It has to be her cunning that makes her dangerous.

                  The other reference to Setites in LABN is in the biography of Sir Ralph Hamilton where it says he had some contacts with Setites in the past (he has Serpentis 3), and would be in deep trouble if exposed. We’re not told what this big scandal is, but presumably he was involved enough to be taught the signature Discipline. Since Sir Ralph is from London, I’ll connect him to the Setites in London By Night which is set in the Victorian era. The one Setite depicted, Halim Bey, was embraced fifty years after Sir Ralph, so I’ll use Halim’s sire Sirian instead as the Setite who initially groomed Sir Ralph. Or perhaps go back to the original Setite clanbook and use Sir Marriot D'Urban.

                  By herself, Dawn seems unlikely to be a major threat. There is only so much an ST can do with a plan of her seducing someone close to MacNeil so he’ll betray and help destroy him, and then she starts a third temple. It’s likely LABN’s author intended STs to have Dawn blackmail Sir Ralph Hamilton, but I don’t think that it would make that much of a difference. Even adding a mole among MacNeil’s coterie doesn’t give her that much of an edge.

                  So we’ll need to be creative in order to make her a real threat. I’ll probably make her at least the short term main antagonist for my chronicle, and have the PCs slowly uncover the menace. But I'll need to boost her up a bit.

                  What do the Los Angeles Kindred know of Dawn? I doubt very much she uses her mortal name. It would make sense to use different aliases when she was priestess of the two temples. But do they even have a description of her? She wasn’t around when either temple was destroyed, and it isn’t clear that anyone even knew there was a first setite temple since it was the Lupines who destroyed it.

                  I ask because it’s important as to how Dawn will proceed. The safest option is that nobody has a name or description of the previous Setite temple leader. Even a generic description like hot mid-teen jailbait would expose her. Any PC hearing such a description will quickly guess who she is because PCs know they are in a story and always looking for Chekhov’s Gun.

                  Having said that, I must note that Dawn is not the only Setite in LA. LABN states that the Setites already have one temple in Beverly Hills and a second in the Encino hills overlooking the San Fernando Valley. Dawn's return simply hints at a third temple. The temple don't necessarily need to help each other, as it's known that rival temples are frequently at odds. But in terms of building the Setites as a faction in the Free States, it's important to note that. The failure of LABN to provide more information on the clan in this regards is just one of its many weaknesses.

                      Black Fox, thank you for giving me Dawn Cavanaugh’s Nature & Demeanour (It occurs to me that a key reason for her failure to establish a lasting presence in LA may well be her tendency to destructive, rather than constructive, intrigue - conspiracy being a poor substitute for leadership, particularly when one conspires against rivals within your own organisation).

                      (1) It’s interesting to wonder if Dawn Cavanaugh is made more or less brave by the fact that her missing heart makes it very nearly impossible to kill her permanently - after all, when you can’t stay dead you can suffer so much more.


                      (2) If I remember correctly, Sir Ralph is one of the Brujah idealists - which might qualify him for the role of the mole who warned Dawn Cavanaugh away from the destruction of the second Temple, though for my money he might work better as a false lead in the Mole Hunt, given he doesn’t seem especially close to MacNeil (Though as a Brujah with some Setite connections, he might be exactly the right Sire for our Mole).


                      (3) I wonder if, at this point in her career, Dawn Cavanaugh works better as someone who gets the plot moving in a way that starts to unravel the Setite designs in LA - it would be a logical progression (or decline) from someone who goes from the founder of Setite fortunes in Los Angeles to more of a liability, through a wealth of infighting and more than one forced retreat.

                      Especially given that her core ambition is the foundation of a ‘Third Temple’ in LA when there are Setite temples already in existence in that territory (Strongly suggesting that either she is not aware of their existence or, more likely, she is not in control of these outposts and not likely to assert any control over them in the near future).


                      (4) As noted above, there’s at least some grounds to believe that ‘Dawn Cavanaugh’ may be an adopted alias - and I’d bet money she tends to think of herself by an Islamic name, when she thinks of her mortal identity.

                      It might also make sense to muddy the waters by showing a certain amount of confusion over whether the Temple of Set’s leader was male or female (Vague memories of Nicholas and Dawn struggling for primacy becoming a historical controversy).

                      It does seem reasonable to make the only fixed constant in the vague recollections and friend-of-a-friend accounts that flaming red hair (Our Heroes need a few useful hints to go on, after all).

                      Incidentally all honesty, being so discrete as to leave not even a distinct recollection of her strongly suggests that a failure to stand up and be counted was a viable survival strategy, but a key precondition of Dawn Cavanaugh’s failure to establish an enduring Setite presence and maintain unchallenged leadership over her own brood (Presumably a comfortable majority of the Clan in the Free States).


                      (5) I think it would be quite important to establish who leads the Beverly Hills and Encino temples, because it seems highly likely that it is they and not Dawn Cavanaugh who have shaped the fortunes of the Clan in Los Angeles since the destruction of the second Temple (Another question: do these temple pre- or post-date the destruction of the Second Temple?).

                      If nothing else, we must wonder if these are childer of Cavanaugh herself, of her rival Nicholas (Presuambly her own own Childe) or possibly even fresh blood imported into Los Angeles by the Setite Elders to make good Setite failures in the area (or some combination of the above): the exact answer will seriously affect the shape of local politics and intrigues across the Free States.


                      (6) Finally, one has to wonder how aware the Sabbat, Camarilla and Setite assets in the Free States are of each other’s operations - it occurs to me that, if they were aware of each other, they might well launch into a Shadow War less spectacular than the nightly action movie at the heart of the Free States, but more vicious.

                      A Camarilla/Setite intrigue might be especially interesting (and damaging), since both could well be willing to sic the Anarchs on each other.

                              The Setites in LABN are clearly the high priests of the Wyrm version of 1e/2e, not the genericization of the clan we would see beginning in Revised. So while the Setites are generally talked separately from other minions of the Wyrm, we may be able to make use of some of the Lupine content.

                              In particular, there is a level two Pit underneath Griffith Park that contributes to LA being a “haven of the weak and corrupt”. There is a Black Spiral Dancer Hive there, and sometimes other Wyrm creatures make their way to the surface. I don’t think it is a coincidence that Dawn’s prior two temples were located not too far from there. (As are the other temples in Beverly Hills and Encino.) It’s not mentioned that Dawn knows about this, but it’s easy to justify Dawn discovering this and allying with the Dancers. While I wouldn’t send a pack of BSDs against PCs, I could see myself using one BSD on some kind of mission that the PCs may come across.

                              Rather than waiting for MacNeil to be destroyed (which may never happen in a chronicle), I think it’s best for the chronicle if Dawn already sets in motion building a third temple. That provides a lot more things that the PCs can encounter.

                              The first question is where should it be located? Dawn seems to prefer temples set in the Santa Monica Hills. The Malibu Hills and Hollywood Hills were both located there. Both were also relatively close to Griffith Park. Both places are also relatively affluent, but they aren’t among the most expensive neighborhoods like the Platinum Triangle (Beverly Hills, Bel Air, and Holmby Hills). That may be because, per Setite Clanbook 1e, that the typical temple is located in a “desolate” or unpopulated area in order to hide it from enemies. We can divide where Dawn might “entertain” or corrupt versus where she conducts her religious rites and has her haven.

                              If I want to go with the same pattern, I am thinking of putting her next temple in Los Feliz, a hillside neighborhood also in the Santa Monica Mountains just south of Griffith Park. That neighborhood also includes the site of the infamous Manson Family murders.

                              I figure before Dawn herself returns, she’ll send trusted minions to begin the process of finding fronts, surveying sites for the future temple, arranging construction materials, grooming herds, and so on. Some of that can become suspicious activity the PCs notice and can “thwart” without revealing Dawn.

                              Then she’ll arrive, perhaps as just another Kindred looking for freedom in LA. That will allow her to meet other Kindred and look for potential dupes to corrupt. She’ll mainly keep to herself and only gradually expand the cult.
