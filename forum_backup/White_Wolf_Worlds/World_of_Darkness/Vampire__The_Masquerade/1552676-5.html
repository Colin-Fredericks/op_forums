            Originally posted by Shadeprowler View Post
            I respectfully disagree
            Not the many people hated Ur-Shulgi but the personality and uselessness-part
            Personality isn't background, or motivation. Usefulness, neither.

            The problem with Ur-Shulgi isn't that he was the worse at being like that, but that he was the worse means to cause the impact he caused in the setting.

            Most of those vampires you mentioned are blank slates. It's not that they don't have personalities, but that the game didn't make one for them, it's up to the ST. Which isn't a very good thing to do, by the way, but is still usable.

            Ur-Shulgi is personality-less by design. He's described as not caring about anything beyond his life's mission. He has no quirks, no interests, he doesn't talk a lot, even his political views are simple to an extreme, without complexity or nuance, or even much reason beyond "Haqim said so". His motivations aren't absent, they're instead geared towards justifying him being like that and never straying from it. It's indeed incorrect to say that he has no personality, but the problem is that his personality is to be obstinately shallow and boring, even if with a convoluted story behind it.

            Worse, and what really makes it bad, is that his power level is also constructed and justified to make things without a story. He has a huge impact on the setting, but the way it plays out is boring. He does everything without any effort, preparation or actual plot. His doing is the plot, all of it. He isn't either a villain that makes things through an interesting story, nor an apocalyptic event people have to prepare for or navigate. He's the meteor that falls, everyone dies, the end.

            And he's useless because while you can investigate his ancient backstory and have to live with the consequences of his actions, nothing really changes with that. Learning his story doesn't change what he is, and don't actually shed new light upon his actions, they're still exactly as it says on the tin. His followers can't be reasoned with, and he can't be reasoned with. The only thing that changes the outcome of an encounter with any of those people is if you'll convert to their cause or not, and that's binary: if you do, no deviation is allowed, if you don't, they try to kill you, the end. If Ur-Shulgi himself is present, you die, no roll allowed and that's it.

            He doesn't really impact the setting at large, either. Yes, his followers are around killing people, so what? They don't gather meaningful resources, they don't conquer land, they don't command nothing, they have no political agenda or interaction with any faction living or dead, they don't stop to meaningfully talk, they don't care about anything at all outside a very simple and strict mission of genocide through frontal engagement. They're otherwise just there in Allamut waiting for the next mission of wanton murder.

            On the purposes he served, few people actually deny, but that's not the point. At the end of the day, Ur-Shulgi was a literary device, a tool to achieve those purposes in the setting. But he was a bad tool. The purposes themselves were valid and needed, but they could all have been achieved through other means. Ur-Shulgi was the straightest and most boring tool for the job.

            So the point isn't that Ur-Shulgi literally had no personality or purpose, but that he was overall a very bad execution, and one deliberately designed to be exactly what it is with no room for innovation.

            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
            Which is different from the above, I believe.
            More or less. And to be clear: your personal position in the matter is perfectly ok.

            What I want you to consider is that people disagree with some of your points.

            This disagreement comes from a few things:

            1- The Technocracy was made to be both fascists and defenders of actual science and modern society. Our opinions on this depiction do not change the depiction itself;
            2- First Edition Mage was quite luddite (in the derogatory sense, yes), so it did thought actual science and modern society were inherently corrupt and unsalvageable things;
            3- You can't change those aspects of the game without changing it too much. Mage was fated to forever have a faction that's anti-fascist, but harbors science deniers, and a faction that's fascist, but promotes actual science, with each being respectively against or in favor of modern society with everything it has, good or bad;
            4- Mage is a game about individuality, so both faction need nuance by the very nature of the game, we like it or not.

            So the movement you're very adamantly against of them being the Nazi Federation is basically the only option for anyone that want to recognize, within the premises of the game, that modern society and actual science have something good about them.

            Is that the real world? No. In the real world fascists are usually science-deniers and traditionalists that reject modernity in any way that isn't convenient for them. IRL fascists wouldn't ever be the Technocracy. But Mage isn't real life, for better or worse.
