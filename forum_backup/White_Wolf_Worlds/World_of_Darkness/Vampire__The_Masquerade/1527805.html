    Info on the Warriors of Glycon

    01-02-2024, 10:48 PM
    Hello everyone,

    VDA20 introduced a new bloodline of the Followers of Set, the Warriors of Glycon (not to be confused with the yet-to-emerge Warrior Setites). I like it very much, but the only content on them I can find is what is in the core manual, in the clan appendix. Is there anything else on them elsewhere?
    For example, I would like to know more about their name. I have read Wikipedia's page on Glycon, which seems to be a snake deity whose cult began in modern day Turkey in mid-2nd century CE and didn't last very long. There were earlier snake cults both in the area and in Macedonia and elsewhere, of course, but the name "Glycon" seems tied to this cult in particular. Now, a Macedonian-Anatolian bloodline of snake-warriors makes total sense to me, especially considering that Echidna and Typhon are also linked to that geographical area in myths. However, mid-2nd century CE suggests a rather young bloodline, and the VDA20 manual states that they tend to be found in North Africa and are the current "rulers" of the clan - though not for long. This does not go well with the idea of them being such a young bloodline - Set himself disappeared in 33 BC or CE, so the bloodline would have emerged after his disappearance and somehow gained leadership of the clan in North Africa despite their name suggesting a Macedonian and Asia minor origin...

    Thanks to anyone who can help me find more material on them, or flesh them out a bit. So far I have a headcanon about Set travelling to Asia Minor, Macedonia and Greece after his infection by Apophis / Embrace and struggling there to control his Beast and his physical form, siring the Warriors of Glycon and Witches of Echidna in the process and giving rise to the many part-human-part-snake-related myths in the area. Later he returns to Egypt and sires the priest line. In this headcanon, Glycon and Echidna are two early infants of Set, and the cult of Glycon is not the origin of the bloodline's name, but a spilling of their name into Roman religion due to the actions of some clumsy member of the bloodline. "Typhon" is the combination of Set and his yet-to-be controlled Beast. I am aware of the theory on Typhon being a 2nd gen vampire diablerised by Set, or 3rd gen if Set was an infant of the Gangrel Antediluvian, and I am not rejecting it in principle. It would make sense for a Gangrel to have a Beast that makes him assume a combination of animal features, as Typhon was described like in myth. I just haven't figured out how exactly i want to interpret these ideas in my headcanon.

                  One might also ask oneself where Kemintiri - and Nefertiti from Berlin by Night - figure or not among those legendary 13....
                  Definitely. It depends on how one chooses to run Set's story and the clan's origin, so it's very much up to the ST.

                  Were the 13 all embraced after Set's defeat by Horus and the Osirian League and after Set exterminated his surviving childer and followers to cancel all evidence of his defeat? Or were some embraced before?

                  Did Set indeed exterminate his first group of childer and followers?

                  Were they really 13, or was that a symbolic number chosen by the Methuselahs who survived the fall of Tanis and Set's disappearance?

                  Are disloyal childer of Set like Kementiri included in the count (like, they are still holy because his blood flows in them) or excluded?

                  Etc.

                  But the introduction of two new Setite bloodlines in Dark Ages (and the Witches of Echidna survive into the modern nights and are mentioned in V20 supplements) means the early history of the clan probably has to be partially rewritten, and the canonical V20 lore has to be regarded as what Setites younger than 700 years have been led to believe about the history of their clan. Especially because in VDA20 these two bloodlines are not presented as some rare and politically insignificant groups: the Warriors of Glycon were the clan's rulers; the Witches of Echidna were popular even outside of the clan, especially in Hellenistic times, as oracles and mystic priestesses, and the clan's magic comes from their line, even though they were quite liberal in teaching it to others, especially before their extinction.
