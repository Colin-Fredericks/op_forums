            Originally posted by Hello View Post

            From what I remember, it's usually done for ghouling specific targets or blood bonding other vampires. No one is going around trying to make it a large scale practice.
            As always, there needs to be a definition of what we're calling a "large scale practice" or a "specific target."

            But one example that I feel certainly complicates that notion is a fellow named Hedeon Yaroslavich. He's admittedly from some earlier content, so there might be debates about whether or not this particular tactic would work, depending on edition:

            The villages near his castle are populated to capacity with ghouls who drink from vitae-tainted wells, thereby becoming Blood Bound to Yaroslavich.​
            There's room for semantic debate, but I think one vampire using his Vitae to brainwash several villages worth of people goes beyond the typical scope of a term like "specific target."

            And as people have pointed out, *this* is the kind of Vampire behaviour that, metaphorically speaking, "makes headlines"; a Golconda hermit who subsists entirely on squirrel blood probably doesn't meet too many other supernaturals, and those that do meet said Vampire probably aren't going to count as much more than hearsay when it comes to determining the collective impression of Vampires in their community.

            The hate is a little exaggerated, but it's not unreasonable. Other Splat absolutely do horrible things, but (again, as others have pointed out) they are generally able to justify it within their own perspectives, as a "greater good/lesser evil" kind of thing if nothing else, whereas most of the terrible stuff that Vampires do to people are just bread-and-butter parts of the conditions necessary for them to exist. Werewolves and Mages are significantly hampered if they make respecting the autonomy and well-being of others a priority when they pursue their goals; Vampires are almost fundamentally incapable of existing without abusing the physical and psychological well-being of others. Werewolves kind of come close with just how much horrible stuff is a consequence of their Rage, but Vampires don't really have the excuse of fighting a forever-war against the End of the World to defend themselves.

                            Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post

                            Rage, or combat forms gratis for all Garou? Because everyone having Celerity for a steep discount is surely daunting, but Crinos and Hispo surely make it existentially horrifying.

                            (Ananasi blood shenanigans would probably freak the Kindred out more if they knew the full extent of the spiders' capabilities in that arena.)
                            Sorry, this is still a misunderstanding. I'm not talking about the combat viability of each.

                            I am talking about the threat that each Splat poses to Ordinary People, just trying to live their lives. And more importantly, How and Why they pose a threat. I'm talking about a comparison between the Beast and Rage as something that makes the members of the Splat threatening or downright dangerous to regular human beings who have not done anything to provoke a supernatural monster to come into their life and upend it, but might have it happen regardless.

                            The OP has argued that prejudice against Vampires is excessive because the various criticisms that are directed at Vampires can be pointed at other Splats as well. The point I, and others, have been trying to make is that even if other Splats do cause as much harm to regular humans as Vampires do, there are factors that make it easier for them to avoid the criticisms directed at the Kindred.

                            Firstly, harming regular people is more a choice that can be made or avoided by Mages and Werewolves, but is almost impossible for a Vampire as predation against humans is a necessary part of their unlives; the amount of effort a Vampire requires simply to avoid inflicting harm or psychological manipulation on regular humans as a necessary byproduct of their existence requires a lifestyle comparable to a normal human becoming an ascetic hermit.

                            Secondly, many Mages and Werewolves that do inflict harm on regular humans often have a "Big Picture" argument that morally justifies it from their perspective. I'd say that the number of Vampires who see themselves as fighting an existential battle for the fate of the world, thereby justifying the evils of their continued existence, are probably in the minority. And the ones that are in that minority are going to include groups like the Sabbat, who aren't going to sell that argument very well.

                            All that said and done, it's worth noting that a majority of Garou probably hate Mages only a tiny bit less than Vampires. And Mages don't so much hate Vampires as mistrust them. Again, it's a bit of an exaggeration to say they're the collective punching-bag of the setting. They're just kind of to the wider setting what the Setites are to Vampire society; implicitly untrustworthy and dangerous, even if sometimes you find yourself really needing something they have to offer and might need to deal with them on some level. Just make sure to triple-check it from every angle.

                                    Originally posted by Elphilm View Post
                                    Honestly, in its early stages, the World of Darkness seemed to be quite amenable to crossovers, but that element was downplayed heavily after around 1994-1995, when all of the original writers and developers had either left the company or were on their way out.​
                                    Yes. In the first few years of the game, the Developers clearly meant everything to be able to crossover and they encouraged it. However, some of the early crossovers were not done very well. They subverted the expectations of each individual game line, and created a different feel as a whole than what people wanted for a game that was based on classic supernatural monsters. So there was a pushback from the fan base, and I think that lead to a walk back from crossover that was later cemented very hard in Revised.

                                    I think one reason this happened is that players took the Litany far more seriously than they ever did the Traditions. The original description of the Garou showed a people in the beginning of a cultural collapse. Younger Garou were challenging the Litany, and too many elder Garou were hidebound and out of touch. There may not have been a War of Ages like in Vampire, but there was certainly generational (and lots of other) conflict. I suspect the Developers originally believed that the players would adopt a more pragmatic and self-interested approach to the Litany, and that there would be more openness to things like Garou and Gangrel working together. Or Shadow Lords working with Giovanni in the underworld. Or Bone Gnawers cooperating with the Sabbat (!) against mutual enemies.

                                    But players did not play like that. They ended up taking the Litany very seriously. And STs ran the elders as better leaders as well. (As an example, I've never seen STs roleplay Silver Fang NPCs as being poor leaders that are causing other tribes to consider removing them from the leadership.) So PCs didn't make exceptions for things like having allies or friends with vampires. And STs did not either. So the idea that a crossover style chronicle (not even meaning PCs are from the two groups, but simply that vampire NPCs could have a real role in Werewolf chronicles other than an enemy to be killed) quickly died. I know it does happen in some chronicles, but that is very much the exception.

                                    I think one reason (but not the only one) is that by tying in the Litany so heavily with Renown (especially Honor), it created a strong incentive to obey the Litany. Players hate losing XP. And Renown is simply another kind of XP earned in play. Why risk losing that hard earned Renown by making exceptions for this or that vampire? So the game in actual play turned over very different than how the setting was originally portrayed.

                                    However, since I always preferred the early description of the setting than the latter description, there is some appeal to me of some level of crossover between vampires and Garou... provided it makes sense. It shouldn't be normalized. But some Garou (concentrated more in certain tribes and camps than others) should have some secret connections. They just need to keep them secret.

                                    And I like the idea that in some capacity, a smart Gangrel who doesn't create trouble can somehow find ways to "run with the wolves" in some capacity. The problem is figuring out reasonable ways this can happen without making the Garou to be total idiots or just not give a crap about this Wyrm minion.

                                    Like a lot of things, I often feel that if the Developers had written their products to provide more concrete examples, things might have turned out differently. If they had include an occasional Lupine NPC in a By Night book, or a Leech NPC in a Rage book that portrayed some of these (crossover not intended to be enemies) examples in a way that added to that game line's feel rather than distracting it, things might have turned out differently.

                                    What would have happened if there was a vampire NPC shown in Rage Across New York that would have made a good ally against the Seventh Generation? Say by someone who would be useful against Van Gelding? Or a Garou NPC and Gangrel NPC who were in a Romeo & Juliet situation (perhaps because they were lovers before either became the supernatural creature)? Or a Lupine of the tribe of Thunder who operated as an assassin of Kindred in Dark Colony who would work for multiple Kindred in order to gain sufficient boons to accomplish a goal? Honestly discuss the difficulty of such characters in these situations, but provide legitimate reasons they have been overcome.

                                    I think this topic deserves its own thread in either game forum, but I don't want to hijack this one.
