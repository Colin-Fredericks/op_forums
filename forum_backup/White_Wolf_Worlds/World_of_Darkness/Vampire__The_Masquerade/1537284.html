    Using the novels as game supplements for V20

    06-12-2024, 09:24 PM
    I did a bunch of reviews of the novels as they came out and that cluttered up the forums but I do think the novels were a fascinating source of adventure ideas and world-building for how the setting is 'supposed" to work. Many times they were very different from what was going on at my tables and the depictions of the canon characters was incredibly different between the books or authors. Theo Bell in Clan Novel: Ventrue is different from Theo Bell in Clan Novel: Brujah or his own trilogy. But that's worth investigating on its own.

    So this is a thread devoted to discussing the possibility of using the various novels with Onyx Path Publishing's written material. Basically, short little discussions of what we liked from the books, why, and what we didn't. Throw in anything you liked and why.

      Dark Prince and Prince of the City by Keith Herber

      1. These were the books that predate the later handling of San Fransisco toward the end of the line with the Kuei Jin turning that into a police state. Vannevar Thomas was originally one of the first Princes of the city and weirdly he was ignored for Kindred the Embraced (not so strange given Aaron Spelling would want his own world) then ignored for official supplements (which seems to have just been them not caring). Vannevar would eventually show up in V5 but his backstory in the games there is largely irreconciable with the one in the books.

      2. One thing about Prince of the City is that it's one of the first books that presents the Prince as a sympathetic figure who is not necessarily a "bad" Elder. Vannevar is a lot nicer character than the majority of the vampires around him and seems to want to do justice for his kind rather than serve as a complete bastard. It's a very different portrayal of a Prince versus, say, Lodin and perhaps why he got replaced. He still does some evil crap like the fact that Carlotta the Ravnos does a sexy "blood dance" that results in a mortal being murdered for his entertainment but he lacks most of the sadistic bullying qualities you've to expect.

      I believe Vannevar is probably as nice as a Prince can be while still maintaining power so Humanity 5-6. I don't think he was ever statted up officially.

      3. We never learn Sullivan's clan and only that he was embraced by Chinese vampires that don't have their Clan identified. They don't function like Kuei-Jin and they're certainly individuals that turn vampires the old fashioned way but it's possible they could be some of the 1st Edition ideas for non-Camarilla Clans of the East. This is actually hinted at as something that may be even more common than it seems as another vampire, Delfonso is said to be descended from a South American vampire line versus one of the Thirteen clans. Which is funny because he's deeply Catholic but possibly not a Cainite.

      I think this would mean that Delfonso would be one of the "Drowned Legacies" that were introduced in BJD.

      4. The book was setting up an invasion of California and San Fransisco by "Eastern Vampires" even way back when in 1994 as The Family was there as an advanced guard. Prince of the City also reveals that they have Necromantic powers and the ability to raise zombies (that they planned to use as cannon fodder ala the Sabbat). I'm inclined to think that the Family are actually Eastern Cappaodicans using Mortis. It's a very interesting contrast to the Kuei Jin and clear they're as much motivated by money and territory as any other vampire. Also, something they've been working on since the 19th century.

      Basically, the 13 Clans replace the Kuei-Jin in this universe but they've long since gone one to become their own Bloodlines and sects.

      5. The Sabbat are a surprisingly good mixture of absolute evil, crazy, and occult darkness. Keith Herber somehow manages to actually make the fact they literally have Sullivan eat a baby (disguisng it with Chimestry as a koala) as a creepy but believable scene rather than the gross out evil that it really is. We also see how their Masquerade breaking was supposed to function as they wanted to use it to force the Camarilla to expend their resources and expose their pawns so they could be eliminated and thus weaken their hold on the city versus simply an exercise in EVIL.

          Clan Novels part 1

          Yeah, this one will probably three or four parts and just some generalized observations. I recently re-read the books as I posted up until Assamite. Some good, some bad, and some mid.

          1. The Sabbat Crusade is a very interesting setting for a Chronicle and there's really enough to do for an entire campaign from almost every single Clan in the books. The Sabbat will end up taking all of the East Coast, including Washington D.C., but end up losing New York in the process. It also causes them to lose several significant elders like Moncada but they ultimately gain Lucita in the process (theoretically). The player characters can fight the Sabbat, be Sabbat, hinder the war effort, or attempt to profit off of it just fine.

          2. The Eye of Hazimel is something that functions like the One Ring and is an interesting Macguffin that you have to wonder if it was inspired by the LOTOR or something more D&D-like ala the Eye of Vecna. Player characters would have to be morons to cut out their eye and put it in but I've seen them do way stupider stuff. I asked Justin Achilli and he basically said that its powers are allowing the wielder to be able to channel Hazimel's powers and it just so happens that the Ravnos Antediluvian has Vicissitude and Koldunic Sorcery, which is an interesting thing given the Tzimisce Antediluvian's presence underneath New York. Maybe Hazimel was a lover/rival of the Dracon way back when or other ancient Tzimisce.

          3. In hindsight, Jan Pieterzoon's feeding restriction reads like edgelordery for the sake of edgelordery. It actually worked as a genuinely horrifying revelation and showing Jan was not a "nice" guy we never get a psychological or backstory reason WHY he would need to do this and th focus is primarily on HIS pain. Given the difference in how SA is handled in fiction these days and how I try to handle (i.e. don't because I write entertainment and know RL survivors), I feel like he could have had it either done much better, tied to his backstory, or something done entirely. It's why I think Jan is dead-dead in the 5E canon (that I don't care about anymore--my VTM canon now ends with "Cult of the Blood Gods")

          4. I actually think it was surprisingly well done, the opening chapter of CLAN NOVEL: TOREADOR because Leopold's nightly unlife had more insight into how 1st Edition V:TM was probably "supposed" to be done than most of the rest of the books. It explained how Leopold lured them, convinced them to allow him to feed, and then got rid of them before daytime. It also showed how tedious and uninteresting actual sex was to him. The revelation Leopold was a Tremere Antribu kind of undercuts also he was the ultimate 13th generation Toreador stereotype.

          5. I think the Sabbat's handling in the books weren't really that good and kind of fell into stereotype. The Sabbat War Party leaders are all vampires who'd have been at home in Buffy the Vampire Slayer, being barely controlled savages and the Elders are all scheming undead horrors who hate each other as much as the Camarilla. Which may not be a WRONG interpretation but lacks much in the way of nuance. I much prefer the Vykos hinted at in Beckett's Jyhad Diary who actually was coming out of an centuries-long set of brainwashing.

          6. As I recall, Ramona was pretty controversial as she was used for the Gangrel book rather than Beckett. Honestly, I think she was absolutely needed for this as it's rather stacked with Elders and all of them are treated with way more sympathy than they probably deserve. Ramona's book is one of the best just because she's trying to replicate that Bloodlines-experience of surviving and maintaining her humanity. Her "romance" with Zhavon also ends tragically but not gratuitously. Basically, someone had to be the Neonate.

                  Clan Novels part 2

                  1. I think Fatima is a character that reads very different in 2024 than 1999 but that may be me rather than the woman who has changed. Basically, in 1999, I took her unwillingness to abandon Allah for Haqim to be noble and a sign of her redeemability. In 2024, it seems a sign that Fatima is a big old hypocrite. She's still a diablerist murderer and doing it all in God's name, which is blasphemous to the actual religion, so the fact she's a Path of Blood member clinging to God doesn't actually make her any different than Ur-Shulgi. Ur-Shulgi presumably knows the WOD Allah has damned them and condemned their actions even if you don't believe he's a Baali-spawn.

                  2. Lucita is an interesting character in these books because her childish rebellion against Moncada is absolutely justified but really has massive unsympathetic elements as well. She murders multiple people to try to make Moncada feel bad including Moncada's slaves and ultimately only gets her revenge because Fatima pushes her into it. I think the Clan Novel Trilogy and Dark Ages: Clan Novel afterwards treats her as a much more interesting more nuanced character. But she's really awful yet entertaining in her book.

                  3. Awful and toxic as it may be, Lucita and Fatima for Pride Month. I'm not beings sarcastic either as I think they're a fascinating romance of two people who only can relate to one another because the other wants to kill them but can understand their lives as undead assassins. I also think Lucita and Anatole's relationship is romantic but that's largely because of David Niall Wilson's handling of them. It seems Lucita has a type in the very religious.

                  4. I'm going to be honest, I had no idea what the Malkavian novel was about or how it handled Anatole.

                  5. Hesha really is a fantastic villain and his book is probably the best of the entire series for how utterly loathsome and yet charismatic the man is. I'd say that he's the perfect Belloq to Beckett's Indiana Jones but I'd argue that Beckett is Belloq as well, so Hesha is even more so. It's a shame we never got a follow up to Elizabeth Dimitrios' plot as I think there would have been some fascinating stuff to explore there if she took refuge with the Anarchs and he was suddenly a major Anarch leader.

                  6. I think Khalil is actually a weird character to have a perspective on because he's every conceivable awful Ravnos/Romani stereotype but insists, "No, that's me, not Romani in general, Ramona!" Which isn't really a great defense. He's also a rampant diablerist with one of his attacks set up to invoke SA in a way that Jan just went literal with (hiding in a woman's bedroom, watching her undress, attacking her, and drinking her dry). Honestly, if there's one guy to see dead forever in the books then I'd say he's a perfect one to be. ANY Ravnos would be a better Siganture Character for the Clan.

                                          Clan Novels part 3

                                          1. I actually think Aisling Sturbridge is an interesting character to look back on with different eyes. I used to think of her as the top young up and coming Tremere but it occurs to me that given se was in charge of the New York Chantry at the front lines with no support that she's actually someone the Tremere have never particularly liked since that seems like a suicide posting. Given she's described as flippant, emotional, and rebellious--this might explain why she's there.

                                          2. Calebros is a character that I think is a fairly one-note Nosferatu Elder but that doesn't mean he's not useful. I'm kind of sad that he's not in the New York visual novels or live series. He's a guy with a level head on his shoulders and absolutely doesn't want the job of being Prince. I also like how when confronted with existence of the Antediluvians, this incredibly knowledgeable and intelligent character--decides it's the Niktuku.

                                          3. Marcus Vitel is a character that I think was killed off in this book because there was a general attempt to sweep away any characters who were thought to be "silly" in these books. So much so that Christof and Theo Bell killing him (we tend to forget that Talley the Hound was there and I actually think that means they could have pulled ir off) was done just so they could pretend DC by Night never happened. The problem was that, magic pin aside, Marcus Vitel was pretty close to what people wanted from Lasombra Elders. I'm glad they retconned him as surviving, 2000 year old RL historical personage or not.

                                          4. Monica Black also never gets her fate resolved (I attempted to write her back into WOD 5th Edition but, well, such didn't happen and a better character was made in her place in CHILDREN OF THE BLOOD). Almost everyone assumed Monica Black was the survivor to and not her sister because, well, her sister was with the True Hand.

                                          5. Speaking of Christof, there's not actually a way I think that he could technically be in the novels as an Archon and also in these books with the way the time period is going. Still, it's a lot of fun having him there and doing what he does best--taking down millennium old Elders with his parties of much younger vampires. Like Drizzt Do'Urden, he's not really the kind of character you would want to show up at your party to do what he does best but he's kind of a funny in-universe anti-Samael Haight. Just a guy who is inexplicably awesome.

                                          6. Theo Bell sadly doesn't really get much of a character in these novels and I think he's much better off in the Clan Brujah Trilogy for character development. I'll get to those books in time but he just sort of is, "Point at Sabbat, Kill" throughout the novel. We never really get a sense of why he does what he does or what he thinks of the Camarilla.

                                                  Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                  The second is that Jan is a product of the Enlightenment and his arguments are going to ones the players might have if not for the fact they know they're wrong. Basically, "Evolution is real, most of the world is not Christian or Muslim, the Earth is older than X, and just because progenitor vampires exist doesn't mean that Caine the First Murderer exists." Certainly, Montano isn't going to talk about the Second City or so on and even if he did then he's a guy capable of lying like all methuselah.
                                                  Players certainly have a lot more knowledge about the setting than characters so this is always a problem. It would be a lot easier to dismiss IC claims about the antedeluvians except for all these canonical characters which we know had actual contact with them no less than 500 years ago.

                                                  For that reason, in my own chronicles I don't have any NPCs who have any direct or second hand knowledge of them. I don't use any 4th generation vampires at all, and any active 5th generation vampires only got that way through diablerie, not the embrace (so not even they could have gotten knowledge about the antedeluvians from sires who knew them directly). Sometimes that means I knock down certain canon NPCs down one generation, but I find that doesn't really alter the intended use of those characters.

                                                  The three diableries of the Anarch Revolt era does cause some issues.

                                                  Tzimisce not too much because Lugoj and Lambach were not childer of the antedeluvian, "Lugoj" disappeared shortly after his claim diablerie, and Lambach was a renowned liar. So any of their claims can be talken with a grain of salt.

                                                  Lasombra is much harder because of Gratiano's rather recent embrace and Montano's long existence. I dealt with that by simply making Montano be someone who claims to be Lasombra's childe, but it's impossible to confirm. Everyone knows he's of potent blood, but there is some suspicion that an antedeluvian could not have been active so recently as to have embrace someone around the time of the Thera eruption. And it was Montano who embraced Gratiano - doing so by orders of the slumbering antedeluvian supposedly, and teaching him according to how Lasombra wanted. The Castle of Shadows was Montano's seat of power, and there were may rumors it was because he was protecting the antedeluvian's body there. In any case, Montano went into torpor shortly after the formation of the Camarilla, and his active role after that was taken over by one of his childer who never liked Gratiano.

                                                  Cappadocious is really the only exception because in my chronicles he was seen as the most active of the antedeluvians. His periods of being awake from torpor always seemed to occur at the same time of major plagues (he supposedly converted to Christianity during the time of the Antonine Plague, and the Feast of Folly happened during the Plague of Justinian), and he was blamed for bringing the Black Death to the world (which I went with original lore that the Black Death could infect and harm vampires as well). Augustus was embraced with Cappadocious's blood, but in a cold embrace by Japheth using the blood of the torpored vampire under the slumbering commands (via dreams) of the ancient. Japheth was merely the most ancient Cappadocian still awake and therefore head of the clan, but could not possibly be a childe of the actual antedeluvian because such an old vampire would have to be in torpor by this time. The actual diablerie of Cappadocious by Augustus occurred much differently than in the Giovanni chronicle adventures (which in my opinion are quite awful and poorly designed). The main reason I associate Cappadocious with plagues is simply to provide some measure of dread in the imagination of players of what the awakening of the antedeluvians might bring during Gehenna.

                                                  That's what I do to try to keep some measure of mystery IC so characters who deny the continued existence of antedeluvians aren't seen as inherently stupid. And the subtle changes of canonical lore is usually enough to inform players themselves that they can't rely on canon to know the mysteries of my games' setting.
