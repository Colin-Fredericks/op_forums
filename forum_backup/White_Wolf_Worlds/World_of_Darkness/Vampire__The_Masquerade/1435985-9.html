I have plenty who are complete scumbags and monsters but I actually generally make the Society of Leopold Lawful Good with a bunch of Lawful Neutral rather than Lawful Evil torture prone psychos.

                                        This is because the Society of Leopold actually is one of those organizations that I rule genuinely gets patronage from the divine (or something very similar to it) in their setting. The torture and murder prone Inquisitors are best thought of as Fallen Inquisitors.

                                        Sullivan Dane is pretty similar to how I like my Inquisitors. He's an ultra-serious, not particularly social man, and you wouldn't invite him to a party. He only drinks water with Grape Soda considered to be one of his personal indulgences. Dude has no interest in women (or men). Elvis sounds sinful to him. However, Sullivan is genuinely out there to protect the innocent and while he thinks all vampires are a threat to the living, he's pretty much kept busy going after the worst of them.

                                        I actually have a couple of adventure hooks about the fact that Sullivan is NOT happy with the Second Inquisition as he predicts (correctly) that the government will give them all the money and guns they want but it will correct their "sacred purpose" with their own agenda. They will become less about protecting the innocent and more about killing as many vampires as possible. The current Inquisitior General being someone with no True Faith because she's down with results over goodness.

                                                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                                I prefer Inquisitors who are happy to do A because it's a shitty-shitty world. I just weirdly don't do that with the Society of Leopold who you'd think WOULD be a bunch of torture-prone assholes but usually aren't.
                                                I tend to play Inquisitors on the batshit end of the spectrum and don't provide much middle ground on the subject, because I don't see how nominally high Humanity characters would associate with them willingly while preserving their Humanity. But on the other hand, I don't restrict Faith behind Humanity rating for mortals and treat it as any other available Numina. I simply don't see a reason for a low-Humanity zealot bastard to be unable to manifest their "faith" as such in a universe in which other, less metaphysically staunch mortals, can pull summoned gubbins out their butts, chuck fireballs, or teleport about practically at will.

                                                And, the high Humanity ones don't "hunt" as such. They're going to take their time, investigate, figure out the inner workings of their "targets", and do their best to foil the truly monstrous ones' plans as best they can without racking up a death toll. A city's Diocese, various (genuine) charitable organizations, or something like a whisper network of former ghouls, can wreak Hell on a vampiric population in ways that don't involve bodies hitting the ground. Those are the ones I make capable of performing Miracles, as opposed to the former.

                                                Which I suppose is a bigger, more structural, problem with how hunters are represented and treated in the game. Whole lotta ink spilled over killing vampires, not a lot spilled over mitigating their influence.

                                                In terms of the HtR spectrum, on one end are the Waywards, Avengers, and Defenders. On the other are the Martyrs, Innocents, Redeemers, and Judges. One group believes they're firmly in the right and the ends justify the means, the others are quite a bit more introspective and circumspect.
