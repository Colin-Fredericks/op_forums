                                        Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post

                                        Oblivion is probably the discipline that is the most editorial mandate on it. Its a power which explicitly wouldn't exist either in lore or mechanics unless unique disciplines are banned.

                                        It kinda strains the internal plausibility of the setting by its existence.
                                        Agree
                                        Obtenebration was something that was defined and refined over decades and actually made sense - and actually explained really well the Clan Flaw
                                        Purely focusing on Vampire - tho this worked with other game lines, like Demon, Werewolf, Mage, etc - the spource of Obtenebration was the Abyss, the Darkness before God decreed "Let there be light,"
                                        The Abyss is the Unmaking, the non-space that was never meant to exist within Creation, but always precedes and surrounds it.
                                        The Abyss Mysticism of Lasombra sorcerers (especially in Dark Ages and V20) explicitly ties Obtenebration to the Darkness before the Light of Creation.
                                        Their power comes from reaching into this darkness — which precedes reality and God — and thus their very blood is corrupted by anti-reality.
                                        The clan flaw is thus a sign of metaphysical infection: not a weakness, but a scar of truth. They are not "unseen" because of trickery — but because they exist in partial contradiction to being.
                                        The Lasombra flaw in pre-V5 editions — being invisible to mirrors and electronic recording — has often been brushed off as a "shadowy" gothic flavor but actually they were not seen because they were not really there.
                                        Their reflection isn't absent because of physics. It's absent because reflections require presence, and the Lasombra, being suffused with the primordial, pre-Creation Void, are anti-presence. This puts them closer to the Abyss than to Creation — closer to the absence of meaning, light and substance
                                        No mater what game line you take the Abyss - the source of Obtenebration - is something primodial or "anti-reality"
                                        Thus the Lasombra clan flaw was the byproduct of this, their connection to Reality was weakened, they were not "real", their bodies were just an illusion like in the old Vampire legends and that why mirrors and devices did not "saw" them- they were just ideas, the willpower of the Vampire manifest in body that is material, and thanks to his own beliefs and the World concensus (alá Mage) they had the vampiric weaknesess, but in truth they became something that simply… Isn't, thanks to their connection to this primodial Void.
                                        The Lasombra Don’t “Exist” — They Are Imposed Concepts
                                        The Lasombra are sustained not by physics, but by belief — a belief that is fraying. Hence their reflections fail; hence they appear as gaping holes in photos; hence they must manipulate reality politically, because they are losing the ability to exist metaphysically.
                                        They’re barely real — and that is why they hunger so fiercely for control. The Abyss hollows them out, and power fills the hole.

                                        The Xi Dundu, the Laibon offshot of the Lasombra Clan weakness could be explained similarly. The weakness of the Xi Dundu is similar to the Lasombra weakness of casting no reflection; the Xi Dundu cast no shadow of any sort. They also have a secondary clan Flaw thanks to their own meddling - they originally hailed from the Congo until a rite gone wrong forced them from their homelands - they must not be touched by their native soil when they rest else they are greatly weakened; this is perhaps a further reflection of their original banishing; and a curious contrast to the Tzimisce weakness. But focus on the original, the lack of casted Shadow
                                        The Xi Dundu’s lack of a shadow can be viewed as a cultural, metaphysical variation on the Lasombra flaw, rooted in the same Abyssal metaphysics but refracted through a different lens: one of identity, exile, and self-erasure rather than elitism and arrogance.
                                        Reflection is how the world shows you to yourself. The Lasombra have no self-image, no echo in light, because reality rejects their presence. Their connection to the Abyss unravels their metaphysical existence.
                                        Shadow, however, is how you shape the world: it’s your outline cast onto reality. It’s a mark of presence, of interaction with the physical world via light. The Xi Dundu don’t leave a trace — they exist, but the world doesn’t respond.
                                        Thus:
                                        The Lasombra are unseen by the world; the Xi Dundu are untouched by it.
                                        They are barely real, but in a different direction — not denied self-recognition, but denied impact. No imprint. No echo. No resonance.

                                        This makes their lack of shadow eerier than the Lasombra’s flaw — because people notice shadows missing instinctively, and it marks them as outsiders not only to society, but to the very laws of nature.
                                        Their presence doesn’t alter light; light passes around them, touches them — but doesn’t respond.
                                        In Mage terms, it’s like they are not part of the Pattern enough to register weight or pressure. They aren’t convincing enough to cast consequence.The Lasombra do not reflect because they are no longer seen.
                                        The Xi Dundu do not cast shadows because they no longer impress upon the world.
                                        Both are sustained by Abyssal willpower, not reality.
                                        ​​Both seek domination, control, or ritual mastery — to assert meaning where none was given.

                                        The Kiasyd should be a paradox: Born of the Abyss, Yet More "Real"
                                        But actually they are not a flaw in this
                                        The Dreaming is the realm of infinite potential, imagination, archetype, and myth. It is not bound by physical laws, but it is dense with meaning and identity.
                                        The Kiasyd are half-Abyss, half-Dreaming.​
                                        Their Fae blood does something Lasombra blood alone cannot: it anchors them in myth, gives form and narrative to what would otherwise be unstructured negation.​​
                                        The Glamour (Faerie's creative fuel) provides the structure to give shape to the unstructured darkness of the Abyss.
                                        Marconius and his descendants do cast reflections and shadows because, despite their Abyssal origin, they are also made of Dream-stuff, which demands identity. Where the Lasombra are unraveling beings, the Kiasyd are "mythic beings" — strange, alien, but storied. The world can “see” them because they have narrative weight.​

                                        ​​​​This refined and defined peak metaphysical horror in Vampire was thrown out the window​

                                                    There are also too many Clans/Bloodlines for the number of Disciplines if you start cutting all the unique ones and want to keep the spreads more unique. It's one of the reasons V5 had to unify the Hecata in order to justify one Discipline for them.

                                                    It's also a bit weird to single out the Lasombra when VtM 1e gave unique Disciplines out like candy in the first place. Gangrel and Tremere both got one in the core book, all the Sabbat and Independent Clans got one, the first Bloodlines introduced all got one.

                                                    There's a bunch of retcons in VtM (Abyssal Mysticism being a thing that always existed rather than a new innovation when it was added to the books for example) that were actually meant to (and had the effect of) reducing how special some of the group-specific Disciplines where. We saw a proliferation of different blood magic traditions to break the Tremere's lock on it. The earlier attempt to make a universal "Clan of the Dead/associated Bloodlines" power by Necromancy being redone to work like Thaumaturgy with each group just having favored paths was also part of this.

                                                    We have obviously not like the totality of the end result, but if you want some place in VtM to point to some clear favoritism towards different groups... there's way better examples (some of which are edition specific, some aren't).

                                                        Among Discipline changes, Oblivion was certainly a special case.

                                                        I don't completely agree with Shadeprowler because a lot of it is theory instead of canon, which is fine. But I can't say it's just "Special Discipline of Specialness" as it isn't really more special than any other gimmicky unique Discipline around. It's their gimmick, and open to be used as plot point or not like many others.

                                                        But the actual point is that it isn't, by any means, Necromancy. However contrived the conflation of Death powers into Necromancy, it still made far more sense within the fiction than Oblivion, and it kept the Paths still somewhat distinct.

                                                        The problem with Oblivion is that it conflates powers that have few-to-no thematic coherence, and irreconcilable metaphysical roles. Their narrative niches barely touch each other except by the very controversial one of "scary power".

In fact, conflating Necromancy within Thaumaturgy would have been a far better (if not really "good") choice, and let Obtenebration be either its own thing, or a Trademarked suit of Alternate Powers to other Disciplines, like Potence.
