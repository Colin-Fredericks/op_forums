        Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
        The Sabbat can't be a death cult and vampires that only exist to revel in evil. That's not how cults work. A death cult is there to program you into thinking dying for the cause is morally good.
        And that's a big part of what the Paths always were. On a mechanical level they're there to anchor your Humanity by calling it another name and giving other sins. But on a storytelling level they do a lot of other things. They relay the notion that fanatics have a system, aren't just evil for the sake of evil. They are strict codes of conduct. They show what final goal the believer seeks that justifies their actions. They make the mindset of those monsters relatable. And they don't weaken the role of Humanity in the game, but reinforce it by contrast, showing at the same time what it means to abandon Humanity and that anyone staving off the Beast is still humane in some fundamental way.

        Giving deep cultural descriptions to the Paths is nice, but removing the rules weakens all those storytelling roles, a lot. It is outright saying that they're evil for evil's sake, something the Sabbat never was and isn't that interesting for an entire sect. Heck, it wasn't enough even for the Baali, even they got deeper than that because it is boring. By this move the Sabbat, the game's representation of religious and political extremism, lost nuance and turned into simplistic evil, not more, but simpler evil, their belief systems set aside as mere color for their actions, their lives meaningless, and by not being meant to ever be played, unrelatable. Non-persons.

                The Revised Sabbat had it correct: most Sabbat were on Humanity because most Sabbat were still new to the Sect and still going through the indoctrination process. Achieving a high bearing in your Path made you more elite. That is how the whole psychology of these things works. You get drawn in via cycle of escalation.
