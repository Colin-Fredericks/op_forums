    The Warrior Caste should not have been able to take control of the Assamite Clan

    02-20-2023, 01:24 PM
    V20 lore of the clan states that the Warrior clan was able to take control of the Assamites because they had superior numbers and fighting skills. What I don't understand is how the other castes could simply let that happen. The Visers I can kind of understand, they were diplomats, not fighters, but the sorcerer caste is a whole different story. The sorcerers had something that is coveted by almost every vampire, Blood Magic, their specific school was called dur-an-khi. The paths found within this school of blood magic include Spirit manipulation, lure of flames, movement of the mind, path of conjuring, Path of warding, Weather control, and many more.

    Auspex was at one time an in-caste discipline for the sorcerers, so they probably had a good amount of people running around with Auspex 5, which allows you to use the powers from many different paths, all from the safety of the astral plane. Combining that with path of Spirit manipulation, they could perhaps create a sizable army that they could summon at a moment's notice against the warriors, all of which can be done from a safe distance away. And IMO, if the school of blood magic you practice requires you to, as stated in the wiki, "call upon ancient covenants with spirits" to fulfill your desires, then Spirit Manipulation better be a primary path for every sorcerer.

    Something, else that irks me is that the reason the warriors have a much larger number of embraces than the other two in the early nights of the clan, is that it was hard for the visors and the sorcerers to find individuals with the right qualities, such as political skill or occult knowledge. If that were the case wouldn't it be best to simply... teach them? Instead of waiting around hoping to find someone who miraculously possesses the qualities you are looking for, it would be far better if each Viser or Sorcerer, took at least 1-2 young apprentices and taught them what they needed to know, as well as prep them for the embrace. They could even create classrooms to teach these kids. Nothing too big, but enough to grow their numbers.

    What do you think?​

      There's a reason for the inaction of the sorcerers during the warriors takeover, and ironically, it's dur-an-khi itself.

      If you're interested, it's mentioned in both pre V20 books on blood magic.

      See, for practitionners of dur-an-khi, the way to learn more paths is through an exctatic trance caused either by vitae laced drugs or other methods, once in this trance, the sorcerer enter a vision of a realm they call the stair to heaven, which is composed of massive stairs where the sorcerer faces mental trials and meet with spirits representing gods to negociate with them for blood magic powers.

      But the stair also contain what the sorcerer caste call stations, massive places symbolizing the planets and their influence , and the sorcerers' goal is to attain the eighth and last one, which they believe will offer them either absolute power or some form of transcendance.

      The thing is, for millenias, they were blocked at the moon station, which appears as a paradise. It is some spiritual trap, keep the sorcerer enamoored in sensual pleasure to delay his quest of further revelations.

      But the sorcerers though that this place was actually the promised heaven, so they spent millenias in trance, spending their time in the arms of spiritual concubines, until very recently ( the books doesn't precise exactly when) a debate amongs caste led to the discovery that this was not heaven, but just a first step, and that ignoring the pleasures of the station led to new levels of the stair.

      So in resume, the warriors were able to take over because the vizirs were simply diplomats, scholars and bureaucrats, and because the sorcerers were partying for the ages.

                              I think it makes a lot of sense. No, I think it makes perfect sense and was inevitable.

The new loyalist Aassamites are more radical and don't plan to be discriminating that much with their targets, so if their vizier replacements aren't as good it doesn't really matter as much.

                              -Assamite sorcerers are spread thin. They're rare and their services are demanded in a wide range.
                              -They aren't anywhere near as unified as Tremere, the way they practice magic differs substantially from sorcerer to sorcerer, and thus big internal divisions are likely.
                              -Assamite sorcerers are entirely dependent on the other two castes. Not only are they the main customers, but they also provide the resources and security needed for their operations
                              -few sorcerers are leader types. They're smart people, but bothering with that kind of thing will take time away from magical studies. It makes sense to let the other castes take the lead.
                              -Ur-shulgi is the OG assamite sorcerer, him rising is theoretically a good thing for them.
                              -The anti-religion rhetoric of the loyalist faction is a good thing for them; Islam stifles Dur-An-Ki so any pushback is welcomed.

                                              The warrior caste "took control" of the Assamites because originally they were the only Assamites. It was not until Revised that concepts like the castes and the Viziers and Sorcerers were established. So they were added retroactively to continuity. We then now had the issue of who were these other castes, what were they doing beforehand, and why we had not known about them before. This lore exists just to explain this retroactive continuity which is why we now have "history" like this. It is completely an OOC need to reconcile the retcon with canon. It's not something that was organic IC.

                                              If you don't like the new explanation as to why the warrior caste was previously dominant, go with what you think best if you are STing a chronicle. The only person you need to make happy is yourself (and your gaming group).

                                                  If you really NEED an in-universe rationalization for an out-of-universe retcon, consider that the other castes probably didn't WANT to have a war with the Web of Knives. Who would? The Warrior caste are the ones who specialize in murder - specifically murder of other vampires - and the Web of Knives are the most enthusiastic about it.

                                                  It's not a big stretch to assume when the Web of Knives stared asserting themselves, the Vizier and Sorcerer castes were willing to go along with their policies because it was easier that getting into a fight. By and large, the extremists were directed OUTWARD, at non-Assamites. History has shown us that moderates will readily let the extremists do what they like, so long as it doesn't put themselves at risk.

                                                  (Brief aside: not only are Sorcerers a smaller caste than Warriors, they're also way harder to make. A newly Embraced member of the Sorcerer caste probably had little to no magical knowledge or skill prior to that. Whereas the Warrior caste can pick any soldier, killer, or even brigand off the streets and be partway to making them an Assamite Warrior. They don't need years of tutelage just to get any results at all, post-Embrace. So while Sorcerers have an innate skill bottleneck, the Warriors don't have that problem. They would ALWAYS outnumber them. Viziers, meanwhile, have neither combat nor magical experience, most times, so they don't contribute much during an actual fight. Logistics, sure, but not when swords start swinging.)

                                                  Another angle one might consider is that non-extremist Assamites, outside the Web of Knives, probably still felt humiliated after the Anarch Revolt and the resulting Tremere Curse. The Clan not only got beaten, but got their Clan shackled. Sure, many Assamites probably didn't care much about not being able to Diablerize. But it's the principle of the matter.

                                                  The Sorcerer caste especially would feel humiliated. They were bested by upstart hedge magicians in the field of the arcane, in a curse they couldn't wriggle out of. Imagine you're an Assamite Sorcerer, heir to magical praxis made perfect in the days of Suleiman the Wise. Now imagine some foreign peddler of cheap charms, whose lineage started a mere few centuries ago and was STOLEN from multiple separate Clans (in various capacities), got the right to lay a hex on you that you couldn't subsequently break. No matter what rites you performed or how you meditated.

                                                  You'd probably feel bitter, angry, and spiteful enough to listen to the fanatics in the Warrior caste preaching a holy war. You take the devil's bargain, because your pride as a magician won't allow you to let that grudge go.

                                                  It would take centuries, and the heightening of extremism to the point the Sorcerers and Viziers felt threatened, for them to break with the Web of Knives. Especially when Ur-Shulgi breaks the Tremere Curse seemingly effortlessly (it was actually a rather involved ritual, but Ur-Shulgi pulled it off very quickly after returning, when centuries of Sorcerer caste work couldn't). Without the Diablerie replacement the Sorcerers held as a bargaining chip, their position relative to the Web of Knives becomes significantly less secure. (The exact order of events is unimportant. Just the threat of Ur-Shulgi breaking the curse is enough to make non-fanatic Assamites reconsider the nature of their relationship.)
