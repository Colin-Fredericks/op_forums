                Originally posted by MarkK View Post
                Sure, but I think that in exchange they lost a lot of distinctiveness.
                I don't, because that's not what I understand to have happened.

                Nothing in the change has that much of an impact on their original description. Everything the Leanhaun believed and did works just fine with the less severe weakness, and so does their reason for wanting Endless Winter, as the problem is the aging, not the Rhapsody itself.

                Quite the opposite, the only thing that really relied on the need for Rhapsody specifically was their overly romantic approach to it, but while it was a very interesting pathos, especially for an Unseelie House, it was greatly overdone because of the requirement. You loose a lot of the punch of a heartbreaking love tragedy when you have to get one every month. The new take allows to explore greater variety, while still remaining within their ethos towards Glamour taking, and in fact you can extrapolate the romantic approach to all forms of Epiphany that would then make for a greater variety of takes on Leanhaun excess, rather than focus only in the archetype of Leanan Sidhe.

                What removed the Doomsday Cult aspect was not the Rhapsody thing, but that C20 removed the metaplot of the Shadow Court. Leanhaun were an Apocalyptic Cult because they were written almost exclusively in the context of "this House is for use with the Shadow Court", so a version of the game where that Court is not that important won't work this aspect.

                And honestly? Good riddance. I personally see the Shadow Court as one of the worse things they added to Changeling. It was there to force the game into the apocalyptic tone of other lines while ignoring how much it had nightmare fuel and space for such plots without it. What the Shadow Court did was not to add this kind of plot to the game, was to make it mandatory when it shouldn't be.

                C20 overall has a hard time to condense every aspect of the Leanhaun in the core book, but that's actually the standard corebook treatment being applied to factions who originally had a dedicated book. It's standard fare for WoD20 and it's still compatible with old lore. And if you play a Shadow Court member or Shadow Court style Leanhaun, everything they were before will apply to the letter. But I personally think the Apocalyptic Cult part isn't required, isn't the most interesting aspect of playing Leanhaun, and even in itself works best as an occasional plot that can then get your full attention rather than a standard metaplot.

                Originally posted by MarkK View Post
                I always found the Balor more of an overall problem in that unless everyone are playing Balor, or Thallain I guess (ugh) you are everyone else's antagonist by almost default.
                Take what I said above about Leanhaun and increase a hundredfold, I despise the old take on House Balor. They're seriously way better in C20 with the sheer antagonism toned down in favor of a performative antagonism that may of may not be hiding actual malice in plain sight while they act as competent politicians for a change.

                Overall previous Changeling sourcebooks had the "50 Shades of Nightmare" problem: authors who didn't understood that the game's whimsy was not a denial of evil kept adding villains and trying to build an equivalent to the BSD and the Nefandi that Changeling didn't really need, and would naturally subsume in itself because it is made for utterly despicable evil to not be a problem or antithetical to whimsy. So the next author would feel the same "gap" and repeat, and we ended up with what, four or more types of "Uber Villains" sitting mostly useless there?

                Adhene, Thallain, the Shadow Court, agents of the Fomorians, all were attempts to make for a play style that were actually so basic that being made only for that just made them generic and uninspired rather than dreadful. Sure, Thallain are the worse offenders by a mile, but still.

                C20 was doing an excellent job overall toning down the Evil Villains of Villanic Evilness take to instead emphasize the previously secondary aspects that were actually way more interesting. So we have Balor as an ambiguous player working with obviously performative edginess that may either be just style or may be a double deception strategy and you'll never be sure, the Adhene got lovecraftian weirdness amped, the Shadow Court is optional and if does work better as an option you can apply surgically rather than assuming as metaplot true antagonists... And the Thallain don't really have much where to go, I guess, we kind of have to suffer them for completionism's sake.

                Sure, corebook treatment and lack of books for the line in C20 does have a cost as we see with more complex concepts like Leanhaun, but I do think it was overall well done and heck, Balor was an exceedingly welcome and refreshing change.

                Originally posted by MarkK View Post
                I did otherwise want to note on "aren't vampires intrinsically shitty?" that even a bit into Revised, there was an explicitly in game material notion for some that "A beast I am lest a beast I become" was a false paradox, was an answerable riddle. And the answer was "no". That's in game material.
                As I said elsewhere, the actual rules even made being an ok vampire relatively easy. Between managing feeding to avoid harming people too much, burning Willpower to not risk Frenzy and many other little things scattered through the game, you don't have any real impediment to be a good person while still being a vampire.

                Players who enjoy the grimdark take complain about this, but those are not failures in design. They are what the game intended all along. The Kindred society repeating endlessly that you have no choice when you actually have was the point all along.

                The description of the condition as one that entails inevitable degradation isn't given by an unreliable narrator just for style: the narrator is wrong. He was wrong all along.

                What staying at high Humanity actually require is just that you put in the basic effort. Evil isn't unavoidable, it's just convenient, and Elders want to convince themselves and others that they didn't fell so low just because it was more convenient. They want to believe it was greater than them when, in fact, it was their choice all along, their human, mundane choice.

                So much so that you'll see a number of NPCs who are better than this without any kind of surreal effort. They got toned down as editions went on to keep things ambiguous and give space for groups that like to play the edgy dark style, but there was a reason why you had non negligible amounts of vampires that were good simply and genuinely. And I find it way more compelling than just "Vampires are all cursed to be horrible", which is just childish and monotone to me.

And not as widely focused on after they came out as a lot of other stuff in the line. (To people who love those books and adventures. Look. It's crowded and not everything fits with itself. It doesn't help that there have been writer comments floating around that described what seemed like a stressed, rushed process. There's a bunch of things in C20 I'm entirely partial to and I'm glad to see them, but there are any number of things that I otherwise throw my hands up in the air about.

                            I'm not saying those problems weren't early, but it's more that they appear in books that are rather dissonant from the core.

                            Truth be told, there are very few Changeling sourcebooks I liked in any edition, and that impetus to give it BSD/Baali/Nefandus analogues is one of the reasons.

                            C20 is better in my opinion, but I get what you mean and can't say I actually gave a lot of attention to the Shadow Court anyway. As I said, I find it a bad piece of lore to begin with.

                            Simply put, the regular court system of Kithain has plenty of space for all sorts of factions. What exactly does the Shadow Court adds that can't be done within the regular courts?

                            You have all those terrible nightmare Fae like the Thallain and stuff and... you can have core book Kithain that include Infernalist Eiluned, Unseelie Fionna partying with Black Spiral Dancers, and whatever you wanna do with Red Caps. You can literally and easily homebrew an Oath to have a regular Changeling being the frigging demonic force behind some of the Super Evil Factions in the other game lines.

                            With everything the core Changeling offers in every edition, I always had the impression that most sourcebooks were both contradictory with the base game to an uncomfortable level and for no gain. Their plots feel a lot more a try hard than adding much value, with excessively poetic text making up for very bland ideas.

                            Exceptions exist, but that's what they are. The Leanhaun are a good example and I find most of their writeup very interesting, but Thallain and Balor are more representative of the usual fare with those books.

                            And yeah, my praise on C20 is mainly for the core book and what it did with the basics. The sourcebooks are better than average for the line, but that's a low bar IMO.
