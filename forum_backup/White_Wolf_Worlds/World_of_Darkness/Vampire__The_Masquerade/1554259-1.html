    1st:

    Honestly my entry here is a technicality because I like the fleshing out they further get as time goes by, but.

    Menele

    (Some of this is owed to a sequence from revised where before it got ditched as a plot, you realize Menele has actually learned and grew and is trying to walk his talk. What is his ultimate masterstroke against Helena and her line? Not to kill them, not some personal heinous revenge, not some staggering brutality he rationalizes as for the greater good. To just bloodlessly knock them all into torpor so he can try to move on with his unlife. He doesn't want anything from this fight anymore but to extricate himself from it. That's the exact kind of 'defeat my enemy' master plan a high humanity ancient vampire should be busting out. "Nope. Nope, just torpor, not even 'technically someone else will kill you, leaving my hands clean', just torpor". Earlier editions Menele very much suffers from failed execution in his writing for what he's supposed to be, or certainly what I think the intent with him was compared to how he comes off in practice, but that one side plot arc brought everything together for me for what the idea of him should be)

    2nd (including Dark Ages 1e as it were, I suppose):

    Michael

    Belisarius

    Thomas Feroux

    Elieser de Polanco (What can I tell you, I like almost all the 'so fucking help me, we are going to be professional, and we are going to be dignified. Oh, just me? None of you are capable of that for ten seconds and it's just going to be me? Well I'm still going to be that way' types. Particularly when they are completely self aware of the world they live in is not and will never be any of those things, or even rewards being those things, but fuck it anyway.)

    Vladimir Rustovich (Someone who is thrilled by the idea that werewolves kinda sorta reincarnate or at least have immortal souls because it means he can get revenge *forever* is doing Dark Ages Tzimisce correctly)

    Mithras (this has as much to do with Victorian Age Vampire, Dark Ages Vampire, and some revised stuff as well, but this is the edition where he gets going really, so)

    Anna Comnena. No she gets basically no screen time or personality to speak of, but the Alexiad is one of my favourite works that I regularly re-read and so she doesn't need it, I know her voice. And Vampire Anna Comnena is such an awesome fucking idea that I love her for her potential, even if it was never realized ;p Honestly kind of art imitating life a lil bit! (At least, obviously, in her opinion *cough*)

    The Order of Bitter Ashes. Not any one specific person from it, I just loved the idea, imagery, vibes and eventual tragedy.

    Revised/Dark Ages Vampire/Victorian/Etc:

    Antara

    Polonia (yes yes, he's from 2nd, but he comes into his own as an interesting guy in revised such that I feel later editions completely waste him)

    Esclarmonde the Black (I have a thing for almost all the vampires that try with clear eyes to build a praxis based on good ideas and on sheer personal competence come *this* close to pulling it off, before getting completely fucking torn down by other vampires due to their own shittiness. Because the problem with vampires is, you know, they're vampires. To paraphrase Margin Call, one of the most vampire movies that ever vampired without actually having vampires in it, you can build that extremely useful and sensible bridge as a huge success, some people will still demand to take the stupid way around anyway)


    Honestly no one exclusive to V20 or V5 made much of an impression on me, and it felt like either edition often dropped the ball with various pre existing characters.

    I don't think I'm being super fair to V20 there though, part of its whole thing was de-emphasizing individual narratives until the very end of the line, I can't really blame it for doing its thing, it just felt less dynamic that way. But it was trying to be less dynamic by intent, until the end there.

        I think V20 primarily managed to make it's most interesting character beats in the Children of the Revolution and Dread Names, Dread List for the NPC write-ups. V20 wasn't very NPC focused but they certainly managed to make quite a few memorable ones. Beckett's Jyhad Diary also had a bunch of fascinating takes on the Signatrue Characters that I don't think really got a chance to shine but had a lot of ideas that I liked in their updates:

        * Marcus Vitel becoming Emperor of Washington DC by being a populist anarch leader ala Caesar. It turned out that most Anarchs didn't give a shit about a dictator as long as they got theirs.
        * Vykos having been warped by having the Dracon's soul in their brain for centuries and finally getting it out.
        * Lucita having a "sister" who is the head of the Sabbat Inquisition (which had been ignored because they wanted to get rid of the diabolism subplot throughout Revised).
        * Lucita being set up as the next Sabbat Regent and regaining at least some of her sanity.
        * Isabela Giovanni falling into the Setite's hands.
        * Ramona joining the Anarchs (who could have seen that coming!)
        * Hesha finally coming close to getting the Eye of Hazimel and having no real idea of what to do with it now that he has caught the car.
        * Critias as Prince of Chicago because being "first among the Primogen" IS basically the job.

        As for 5th Edition, I am biased to OPP (shocking) but I don't think it ever got any better than Chicago by Night, Children of the Blood, and Let the Streets Run Red.
