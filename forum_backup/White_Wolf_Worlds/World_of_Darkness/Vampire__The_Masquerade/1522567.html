    Werewolves seeing through Obfuscate in VDA20 and WTA20: a new perspective

    09-14-2023, 10:15 AM
    Hi! I know this issue has come up in other threads, with one of the most frequent answers being that there is no set system to determine whether werewolves can see through Obfuscate, but generally one compares the werewolf's rank (as if it was Auspex) with the vampire's Obfuscate rating if the werewolf has a gift to see through supernatural concealment. This is the take that most stayed in my memory, though others were offered.

    Now, here's my problem: we are playing VDA20, but using WTA20 for werewolves rules. We are not using other supplements from either line. I went through the gifts in WTA's core manual and... I can't find any that would make me say: this would apply to Obfuscate.

    Those that I recall now:
    Sense the Wyrm: first, this seems to give vague impressions about the presence of Wyrm taint, not to pinpoint the location of a vampire. Second, Unseen Presence (Obfuscate 2) works by making others unconsciously oblivious to the vampire's presence unless s/he draws attention to himself or herself by affecting other things. It's so deep that people unconsciously move aside to make the vampire pass. The vampire is not actually invisible, but is rather ignored. I don't see why even a lupine sensing the Wyrm should not unconsciously ignore the vampire, even though technically he senses his Wyrm taint. As for Vanish from the Mind's Eye, that explicitly fools all senses, so I don't see why a sense like the Wyrm-sense should not be fooled as well. Nothing in the description of Sense the Wyrm says that it pierces supernatural concealment.

    Sense the Unnatural: this has the same issue as Sense the Wyrm. It does not, in principle, pierce supernatural concealment, and even if the lupine senses a vampire is near, he would be brought to ignore that sensation by Obfuscate's mind influence.

    Scent something (can't recall the proper name): a power that lets you swap.your sight for your smell to smell invisible entities. Again: Unseen Presence doesn't actually make you invisible, it makes you ignored, so I don't see why using smell would pierce it. Your nose smells the vampire, but your mind ignores that input. And as for Vanish from the Mind's Eye, it explicitly tricks all senses, so smell offer no special help against it. This scent power seems great to fight spirits or some type of mages maybe (not sure) or those who use magical invisibility rather than the mind-influence of Obfuscate.

    Which other powers am I forgetting? Am I reading this wrong?

          As for Auspex, I agree it is weird, but I think the deal with it is that Auspex by itself, regardless of its specific powers, pierces through mental illusions as well as illusions of the senses, yet it does not pierce through metaphyisical or mystical veils (ex: it doesn't let you see spirits or ghosts beyond the Gauntlet / Shroud). And in fact, all Auspex powers from 2 to 5 concern the mind in some way: sense emotions through auras, though also see the supernatural nature of those around you; sense psychic residues on touched objects; read people's minds and send thoughts into them; project your mind to perceive far away things (this is no more proper astral travel in VDA20, but just a projection of your consciousness elsewhere in the physical world). So it kind of makes sense that Auspex in some way protects from mental illusions. At lease to my understanding.

                      I think you can skip a lot of the uncertainty around specific Gifts if you remember that piercing Obfuscate isn't automatic, it's a roll-off. Having an appropriate power is a prerequisite to getting to make the roll, not automatic sensing the vampire. It's not like Auspex 1's specific mechanics are protecting you from mental influence in general, it just contests Chimerstry and Obfuscate because the game needed some counter to them.

                      Garou Gifts aren't organized into neat packages like Disciplines where you can just say, "having anything is this package counts as being able to counter Obfuscate."

                      Given how the rolls work, there really isn't a serious danger with being liberal in interpreting Garou Gifts as qualifying. A fresh Cliath isn't detecting an Elder Nosferatu just because they have Sense Wyrm. If you want some middle ground, just have it be the Rank of the Gift not the Rank of the Garou that dictates the mechanics of the roll off. A level 1 Garou Gift even in the hands of an Elder werewolf isn't going to be as good as a level 3 Gift.

                      Forcing Garou to have a mental-defense Gift isn't in-line with how the VtM rules are actually phrased: "Auspex enables Kindred to perceive many things beyond the limits of lesser senses.
                      ​Among its many uses, Auspex can detect the presence of a supernatural being who is hidden from normal sight (a vampire using Obfuscate, for example, or a ghost) or pierce illusions created by the Discipline of Chimerstry." It doesn't say that this works because Auspex is actually a mental defense power, it says it works because supernatural perception powers can overcome mental deceptions.
