    Are there traces of the so-called "Second Inquisition" in V20?

    08-18-2021, 08:47 AM
    Maybe this will get covered in the Second Inquisiton book or has been mentioned and I didn’t notice, but I was flicking through V20 a while back and looking through the witch-hunter section. The Arcanum and the Society of Leopold are regular hunters, but the others stood out to me and I just realised why.

    Because the NSA and organised crime syndicates were being manipulated behind the scenes. A handful of agency high ups were shown behind the shroud, thinking they were seeing an alternate dimension full of "negative bodies" (clearly where "blank bodies" came from). Then they get supplied with a scanner in which vampires appear exactly like wraiths (which is very interesting, but another topic). Now they think they're fighting against an invasion of body snatching extradimensional beings.
    Meanwhile groups like the mafia were approached by “angels” who turned them against their vampire leaders. These angels seem remarkably like they may be an organised group of Caitiff.
    I haven’t had the chance to read Hunters Hunted I or II, so there could be more hunter groups like this

    It seems in recent history there was a supernatural force collecting pawns and nudging them into place. Or maybe multiple forces, perhaps working together or maybe their unrelated plans just got away from them when they collided and the hunter groups noticed each other.

    Could be Hunter: the Reckoning. Could be antediluvians. Could be a regular bunch of vampires whose schemes got out of control. Sect wars gone wrong, perhaps. Could be members of a faction within a certain global conspiracy who could get access to totally-not-magical hypertech that wants all vampires dead, like, yesterday. (I mean it probably shouldn’t be the actions of another splat, but I seem to recall that the aim is a cohesive world of darkness, so)
    Could be all of them and more. It’s Masquerade, the conspiracies have conspiracies have conspiracies, after all.

    Either way, by the present day it seems these groups have started to notice each other and exchange information (although without a doubt they lie, disregard given information as nonsense, hide stuff and stab each other in the back as much as their targets).
    And so the vampires dub this “cooperation” the Second Inquisition, and like the first one it’s a totally manageable situation except for the fact that your fellow kindred are happy to point them in your direction if it benefits them.

    I suppose powerful supernatural influence would also explain the efficiency of such a patchwork “organisation” and a SchreckNet breach, though I don’t think the latter is necessary. I'm not sure the first one is necessary either, vampires have plenty of idiots who are greatly talented when it comes to screwing their whole species over by being selfish bastards who overestimate themselves and refuse to learn.

    Do you think this will come up, or am I seeing things? Because there’s some mysteries here and I haven't seen the answers to them (and honestly, maybe they should be left mysteries to play with).

          Have you read Hunters Hunted II? It's a solid book with plenty of ideas.

          as for the SI
          It can't be overstated how much of a political and logistical issue it would be for the various hunter factions to unite to the extent that the SI implies, without even getting into the barriers that V5 removed (The Vampires simply had better tools for dealing with organized hunters before V5; Obfuscate, Auspex and Dominate were all much, much stronger)

          1- You can't have the entirity of an Agency like the CIA or FBI go after vampires, because these organizations have information available to the public; Not only would the heads of these organizations fear reprisals from vampires, but they very much benefit from the corruption that vampires bring about. The United States can't even get good public transportation because of the Car lobby, now imagine US officials endorsing the hunts of people who got them their jobs, bought their homes and whom spend millions on them per anum. And they're not even getting good publicity for it. It's not happening.
          2- Yes, Rogue members of these big organizations can branch off and do work in secret, but this severely limits funding and how big they can get. They're also not going to have much of a sense of trust, so they're not likely to get in touch with rogue cells from other big groups, especially those outside their home nation. In the real world, military and intelligence alliances do not work well out of a reluctance to share information, just imagine how that'd work out with rogue fragments of big organizations.
          3- The SoL, the premier hunters, would be considered a terrorist organization, so they wouldn't have great relations with Big-Gov types. They're also religious quacks, so no dealing with the Arcanum.
          4- Technocrats and lupines can win battles against vampires, but they're doomed to lose wars and they know it.

          Thematically. Hunters have always been underdogs. It gets weird when suddenly the hunters are big government and they know what they're doing and they're outfitted with weapons from Blade. Which is a shame because V20 did great in adding diversity to hunters, and now they're a nebulous blob.

Money goes to your opponents, any media willing to be critical of you, any lawsuits filed against you, and your dirty secrets are up in the air.
                                                          One very good advantage Vampires have over normal rich folk is Ghouls. Now, Offering your supporters immortality is, for the most part, a bad idea. You never need to reveal you're a vampire, and Ghouls are a masquerade Liability, especially when it comes to high profile figures. The real wonder of Ghouls is their absolute willingness to do anything to please their master. Vampires can find ghouls that can cater to any desire, no matter how sick and twisted. Enough blood addiction and anyone with low willpower will happily allow themselves to be abused for the master.

                                                          Specialists like Tzmisce and Malkavians really excel. Not only can Tzmisce provide custom products, they can also deform people and then hold their treatment hostage. The Malkavians can really just drive people insane. Death is less worrisome than losing one's faculties. Strategic use of Passion can ruin careers and families, and Haunting is ample warning that one needs to do as they're told. Dementation easily discredits any victim.
