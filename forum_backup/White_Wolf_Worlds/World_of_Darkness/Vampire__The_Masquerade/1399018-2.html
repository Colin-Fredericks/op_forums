            Mutual blood bonding makes sense as a strategy, in-world, in that it provides at least one vampire you can sort of trust, and prevents outside blood bonding. I mean, if you consider multiple mutual blood-bonds, this strategy seems to have worked for the Sabbat for roughly five hundred years. Arguably, it's the main reason the sect lasted as long as it has.

            The problem is, for the actual players playing the game around a table, having two vampires in a mutual bond leads to two characters who ignore everyone else and tend to retreat from plot interaction, rather than moving toward the plot.

            "Wait, your plan might lead to my beloved being endangered? Nope. We're out. We're going back to our haven to watch a Gilmore Girls marathon. Have fun storming the castle!"

            So, the game has set up a world with vampires who, for little reason, think of a mutual bond as a perversion.

            Forget the Golden Rule. The Platinum Rule is: "That which moves pcs toward the action is good; that which leads them away is bad."

                                            It hit a lot of people much harder because while you're not going to ever see a guy leading an army of ghosts to exterminate a city so he can harvest their souls as citizens for his Necropolis built out of their bones, the idea of a guy who has the Charm of "Beloved Celebrity" using said status to gaslight a woman is an entirely different thing. The movie Gaslight is all about a woman's husband fucking with her sense of reality in hopes of keeping her under his control until he can get her committed (which was a real danger in the period).

                                            If it suits your game (and only IF--see Monty Cook's Consent supplement:https://www.drivethrurpg.com/product...sent-in-Gaming ) there's a lot to be harvested out of vampires using their powers as well as the Blood Bond in profoundly petty ways. Less "take over the city" and more, "wield abosolute power over your loved ones."

                                            Blumehouse's THE INVISIBLE MAN remake is one of the most successful of them because it really did an excellent job of showing someone using a superpower to utterly terrify and abuse someone. It also backfired as quite a few audience members had horrifying pasts with abusive partners. It doesn't even have to be a partner as Desus could have used his "Presence 5" on, say, his children. The only JESSICA JONES season worth watching (I'm a Trish fan, can you tell?) is the first one. That is the perfect say to show what a Kindred can get up to with Dominate, albeit a bit OP and there probably ARE vampires who act entirely like that.

                                            We have abusive parents and the tricks they use to gaslight their children showing up in the SHE-RA remake now for instance.

                                            For those who like "low stakes" psychological horror, then there's plenty of inspiration to draw from--just don't make your players not have fun.
