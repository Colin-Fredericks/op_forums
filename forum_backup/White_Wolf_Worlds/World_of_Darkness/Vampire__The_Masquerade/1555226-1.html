        I'm not sure that's really the case...

        Paradox bought the WoD IPs a decade ago. They threw a lot of money at it, and seemed like for the most part they let the creative teams take the wheel over investor driven short term thinking.

        The problem is that in any economic system, there has to be some sort of feedback system to ensure people aren't getting caught in a sunk-cost fallacy and keep putting resources into a project that's draining more than it can hope to add.

        Too a large extent Paradox is suffering the results of never really enacting an abstract or concrete long term plan for the WoD. Without that plan, product quality has been all over the place (at best) as there isn't a strong coherent creative vision at the helm of all of this leading the products being disjointed. Success has been nearly impossible to capitalize on because what was successful isn't being carried into how future products are made, while failures have racked up and driven potential audiences away. That plan is, basically, necessary to ensure that product quality in the first place.

                Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post
                On reflection this whole thing feels like an example of the downsides of capitalism were the quality of the products are secondary to abstract long term plans.
                While I definitely understand the sentiment, that's not the case here.

                In addition to everything Heavy Arms said, Paradox is simply making a very bad job at diversifying their products and are reaping the consequences. Those certainly take a capitalist flair to them, but something would happen one way or another.

                There's no mincing words here, they simply don't know how to do things. They know how to make their particular brand of game and that's it. Their success is more the result of being good at the right task at the right time than knowing how to manage anything of their current size or doing anything at all besides their strategy games.

                V5 is actually a love project that could have been so much better, because it was given mostly free reign and far more money that it needed, really.

                But capitalism or not, you need to know how to manage things. You have to set realistic goals and assess your situation regularly. You have to study how things actually work when you try to do something new. You have to allocate resources with a minimum of logic. You have to know when to pull out of a failed experiment. You have to adjust and learn from mistakes and successes. And they failed at all those things.

                Capitalism is at fault when you have a good thing going on, on good hands, but those things derail because quality doesn't equate profits, and that's certainly a thing that happens a lot. But in this case it's was something good being delivered into incompetent hands 10 years ago, and that happens at any economic system. All the problems that ensued are 100% Paradox's fault for being fools, not because capitalism (quite the opposite, as their money actually sheltered the IP for 10 years from the consequences of bad management).

                And that sheltering may still happen. As I said before, TTRPGs are small change for a company their size. What happened now is that Bloodlines 2 is not a TTRPG and brought a whole 'nother level of loss, so this sheltering is at risk, but they may be able to still hold to it if they can argue that keeping things at a lower profile (i.e. TTRPGs and licensing without compromise) is a safer deal than trying to sell it for a loss (which is exactly what will happen if they sell it now). They can still convince shareholders that if they don't waste that kind of money again, WoD5 is too little a thing compared to their margins. That just became a harder sell.

                                My take on the subject is that the video game department is working at the expense of the tabletop game but it really isn't Paradox's FAULT despite disagreements fans may have.                                     At the end of the day, the real value of the WOD-IP is in its ability to make videogames with it. Because no matter how successful the books might end up being, the money you're gonna make selling those will be peanuts compared to a successful videogame-release. And since Paradox is primarily a videogame-company anyway, I would assume focusing on videogames was always the goal. And that's why they wanted to start with the flashy idea of making a sequel to the cult-classic Bloodlines.

                                    But I think the way this then unfolded led to another leadership-failure by Paradox. Let's say it breaks down 50/50. They spent 30 million dollars on the Hardsuit Labs version and 30 million on the Chinese Room version. In reality, it probably breaks down differently and the 60 milion is also just an estimate from bank analysts anyway but for the sake of argument, let's go with that. During the entire time, they've been rolling out WOD-games, I'm sure it must have influenced Paradox's decisions that they were always thinking "We spent 30 million on Bloodlines 2 - and we have nothing to show for it.". And I feel like that's why these WOD-games rarely went into AA-territory like Swansong or Earthblood and instead, it just has been mostly visual novels and text-adventures. You never got any really big releases that made a splash. And it didn't help, of course, any time someone wrote about a new WOD-videogame the "Remember Bloodlines 2? What a mess..."-talk came up - which put a bit of a damper on the whole affair. So I don't think that approach ended up helping the WOD-brand get more recognition.

                                    The WOD-videogame-lineup has been sitting this entire time in the shadow of Bloodline 2's development-hell-fiasco and now Bloodlines 2 has finally come out - just in time for Paradox to lose any interest in producing a WOD-videogame-lineup, it feels like. Originally, Bloodlines 2 was supposed to be the flagship-game that showed off the potential of the WOD-IP. But the way in which it was released now was more about Paradox getting a burden off their chest rather than being a signal for any greater plans. They've finally done it, they've released Bloodlines 2 and there's seemingly no interest to capitalize on that. Which just feels like yet another poor management-decision by Paradox.
