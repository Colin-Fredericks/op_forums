        Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
        In the short term, yes. How many potential future deals are at risk if Bloodlines 2 continues to languish in development hell and Bloodhunt flops?

        If the WoD IP consistently proves to not be worth it for other video game companies to want to pay for the licensing, Paradox's whole business model for the WoD is in trouble.
        The original Bloodlines flopped, but eventually made it even and today can be considered a success.

        Yet, it killed Troika Games. Why? Because its flop meant they couldn't get a contract for another project. They went down the following year, not because the game wasn't making any money, but because wasn't making good PR with publishers.

As far as anyone can tell, while the game industry had a peak during the Pandemic, WoD is dragging down. Paradox itself just had its best semester ever, but not thanks to anything WoD-related. They are devs and aren't making any WoD game themselves.

        Currently Paradox is running a project of pre-approved licensing with no strings attached so people can develop games using the IP and not have it as canon, then negotiate canonization if it goes well, similar to the ST Vault. No big name is interested, the IP is yet to show promise, and games continue to not contribute a lot. If this trend isn't reversed by the next releases, then the only thing maintaining the IP will be good will.

                        Just as a quick add on rather than to re-edit, since I mentioned superhero comics to live action movie adaptations. Did you know the movie Logan is in fact adapted from a story from the comics (a frankly bad and overrated arc of comics in my own view)? A movie that in final result almost completely resembles nothing from its source material? Logan owes as much to the Western Shane as it does Old Man Logan, which as "adaptation friendliness" goes, includes such details as the Hulk committing incest-rape on She Hulk to found a clan of inbred hillbilly Hulks. There are references otherwise in the comics story that run ludicrously deep at times.

                        The movie somehow found a way around this and a wide variety of other finely ground or gross ass things or both to tell the story it wanted to tell. In fact it goes so far as to yoink and mass alter characters from other entirely different stories that had nothing to do with Old Man Logan, stories and characters that are themselves convoluted right out to nonsense.

                        And yet, without said comics, the movie would not exist.

                        Adaptation friendliness really does feel like a mirage. If a game developer/film or television producer/whatever sees even a conceptual germ of something they like in a thing, or more importantly, can sell, all the details surrounding that thing did not stop them from seeing it.

                        (Also, Shane itself is based on a 1949 novel that it took a few liberties with. And Laura from Logan is herself a riff off of X-23, who first appeared in the cartoon X-men Evolution, that was itself a loose adaptation of various X-men comics. She was then brought into the comics themselves as a character after the cartoon, which is where she was noticed by the people who made Logan, to include in their movie. And if you could follow that entire chain of who was adapted from what to where to make a given end result, congratulations, you're ready to watch Tenet.


                        Or just understand that adaptation friendliness is a thin concept as far as making it more likely something will be adapted and be successful at being adapted).

                                                        I'll try to be even more clear about something to get around the whole reductive "you just can't handle depiction of evil" implication that seems to be regularly trotted out when the discourse leans this way.

                                                        The Camarilla, certainly from when I preferred the writing for it, is an ornate machine that kills innocent people by routine. Far less monstrously than the Sabbat, certainly, and by far less frequently, but still horribly. The Camarilla as an organization has accepted that hey, gots to keep that Masquerade, and if that means people gotta die, welp! And the insidiousness of the Camarilla is they can make that sound reasonable. Do you want the Masquerade to drop and the world to light on fire and you and everyone you feel feelings for to die? No? Well then, this is one of those egg breaking omelets childe.

                                                        And once you accept that awfulness into yourself, the other bits start coming down the line as though they were waiting for you to open the door into your soul. Oh, someone accidentally killed their vessel? Well it's not like that happens too often, it's not like we're engaged in Sabbat style bloodbaths here. And don't you want the Tower to stay fed, and strong? What if it stops being strong? Are you ready to deal with that? Are you ready for the world to break? Of course you're not. And you know how much worse it could be, don't you? You feel that snarling within you, you feel that hunger. We built an entire world out of chains just so you could wrap those chains around it, childe. Just so you still have a way to go through the motions and have one, or two, or even three moments every so often where those motions mean something. Play the games, and play along, and we'll all be chained together. Yes of course some more lightly than others. You want to hold onto your humanity, don't you childe? Tell me what about the human experience was ever fair. Now bow.

                                                        The evil of the Camarilla as I prefer it, is an evil of expediency that cloaks itself in stylized politics and even ideals so that you can tell yourself it's too big to do anything but live within. Order and society writ so large, yet so rationalized you can convince yourself it has a meaning larger than you and that you didn't do so blindly, that you work for it, but just as importantly, it works for you. Instead of acknowledging that it is feeding on the meaning you give it. That you can tell yourself it is so powerful that really, you had no good choice. An evil of all the horrible little compromises you make because they made sense to make, and only when its too late do you step back and realize the mountain of wretchedness all those compromises have built, and the worst thing of all is the knowledge that it hasn't killed you, either in body or in soul. That you truly could keep going this way forever, because there's always just enough of you left over to hold on at the end of the night when the dance, be it played out in a salon or a sewer, stops for another sunrise. The evil of the Camarilla comes of all the people who accepted "A Beast I Am, Lest A Beast I Become" without even really trying to shout back a no, and instead reaching angrily to violently pull down anyone who does shout to trudge back with them through the mire. And then getting just enough of a reassuring "hey, good job" pat on the back for that motion from the people who set the whole thing going in the first place.

                                                        And all knowing in their hearts that all that is, for a certain use of the word, true, they react in different ways. Some embrace the games, or even worse, the corruption and the feuds, the climb and hoarding of power, because even the biggest loser gets a moment they can tell themselves is a win, and look! It's those one or two or three moments that you tell yourself meant something, that you felt something. Some do the best they think that can to use the system for something actually resembling its own right sounding delusions, even as that system compromises them, hoping that if they achieve even one good thing for someone they care about, or even better, someone they don't care about, that it will be worth all the awful things, worth knowing the Tower grinds the people who push too hard from within it into nothing. Some are just trying to live and can't you understand they're just trying to live? Can't you let them just have five goddamned minutes to live their (un)life? And most are all these things at one point or another, because even with a Beast, the thing about being still human, about even just giving lip service to the idea of your humanity like a good little Camarilla would, is humans are a mess, and terrified of that messiness. And they will go to great and terrible lengths not to face it, to feel safe from it, and give great and terrible power to something that will let them live with it from one night to the next. The Camarilla asks for so little! Just let it rule you, and you can have everything you really want.

                                                        Look, you asked to be protected, and the Camarilla protected you. You cowered before the Tower; it was frightening. It has put its hand on history, it has turned the mortal world to its will, and it has done it all for you. Frankly the Camarilla is exhausted from living up to your expectations. Hasn't it been generous?

                                                        Now bow.

                                                        And get rid of that body.



                                                        Direly overwrought ultra amateur prose and Jareth paraphrases aside, this is not an evil that, y'know, *works* with the balkanized, moron, half assed crime family, evil for evil's sake Camarilla. There needs to be enough of a measure of power and competence that there is an actual sacrifice and challenge in not going with its flow, even just long enough to do one good thing, for however long it lasts, even knowing that you'll end up right back where you were within the Tower. There needs to be that sense of noir (and I think the game lost a lot for having thrown its sense of film noir over the side). There needs to be enough depth to playing its games that you can tell yourself there is a reward for the fleeting moments of when you win them, even at the horrible costs of doing so. The world without them needs to feel like it could scare you enough that you turn your face away from the world, and back to the Tower. The evil of the Sabbat is in your face, or certainly at least more openly jarring. The evil of the Camarilla is the ending of the Godfather when the doors close in front of Kay as she is helpless to do anything but be shut out from how far Michael has fallen and yet somehow still functions under a mask of humanity, and his having rationalized every single step that took them to that point. To be pulled in that deep requires there be something compelling about the pull, even and especially when you know that pull is a lie that gets stronger when you believe it.

                                                        There is otherwise something vital to the Camarilla as far as being this ornate global thing that has the sheer gall to say "all vampires are us" and to pull it off enough that their enemies blink for even just half a second of "oh shit, maybe they are?"

                                                        The Camarilla needs to actually have some moments of symbolizing something in the first place so that symbol can be shown to be hollow. It needs to have ideals, even warped ones, so that it can be shown to be grotesque hypocrites about them. "Can I work with this? Can I force this thing to work for me? Is the mistake even asking these questions in the first place and not getting into the goddamn car with my mortal girlfriend and taking my chances?" doesn't work if the Camarilla is too pathetic, unsubtle, or just inane to make someone have that struggle.

                                                        And on a much, much, much less serious note? I like ultra stylized sociopolitical gaming with vampires. When there's evil in that, I'd prefer evil in those terms. That's enjoyable for me. When the Camarilla are just a cluster of vindictive clowns in a shattered world, that feeling falls riiiiight apart into nothing.

                                                        So, for instance, that bit with Cecily Banes and the Caitiff? It's dumb excess for the sake of being dumb excess. It's just shock value trying to act like it is being deep. It's what people who dislike the Sabbat, reduce the Sabbat to being when they talk about why they believe the Sabbat are dumb. It's what people mock M. Night Shyamalan's "what a twist!" endings for. "LOL, I tricked you into killing an innocent person in a way that makes the lives of the people they care about worse also, lololol! Lessonz!! (tm)" There are so many other ways that could have been done. And when it comes in a series where the Camarilla are otherwise pretty much a bunch of backstabbing dolts who at times get killed for a joke, I mean, sure, but don't expect me to invest in any of it. If this is where someone wants to trot out "well it's supposed to be satire", well again, hey sure, but that has nothing to do with my being unable to "view the Camarilla as evil" and more to do then with my making a mistake of taking the game at its word when it told me I could do more with it than laugh at the dumb dumbs.
