    Where to learn from the Houses Goratrix - Tremere conflict?

    06-14-2024, 03:31 PM
    So, working in a setting, I came with the following idea:

    The city has changed of hands several times during the centuries: From regular Princes during the war of Princes, to Anarchs, Sabbat and Camarilla. While the core of the city has remained independent, its allegiance has paid lip service to different sects or converted forcefully.It might not make total sense in canon, but has gone through a "Change everything to change nothing" process.

    Not everything was lip service, and different Tremere factions have occupied the chantry once the previous members have been kicked out. Most chantries left magical artifacts and knowledge behind before being defeated, so the same chantry, a medieval hospital, has been used over and over.

    Of course, every time a new faction has occupied the chantry, it has tried to remove all previous wards and set their own. Some, when being forcefully "evicted" have left deadly traps for the newcomers. With time, the situation was unsustainable and the place (or at least its basement/catacombs) has become a magical hazard.

    The place was abandoned by the Tremere (and as a Hospital) by the turn of the XXth century to a new massive hospital, and the Tremere (now the Camarilla ones) took what they could towards the new place. The old chantry though still keeps lost lore and artifacts.

    Now, besides the Camarilla V5 sourcebook and wod wikis, do you know where I could get more information on the conflict between the two houses? I know that between campaigns, novels and sourcebooks there are a ton of places, and it is a bit overwhelming. Honestly, I don't know where to start, or a way that I could have a fairly good idea without needing to read it all. I have a feeling that at this point, information is so spread that a "Tremere encyclopaedia" would be a great idea.

    Thanks!

      The short answer is that most of the history of the Tremere has had the majority of the Tremere united under the Pyramid of the "main" Clan. House Goratrix used to be the only big exception, as the Sabbat/"antitribu" alternative, and they kind of had to kerp their heads down due to not being popular with other factions in their Sect. Most other "factions" within the the Tremere were isolated pockets like the Telyavs or secret societies (many of them infernalist) that mostly tried to avoid being discovered whike pursuing their particular agendas.

      Large factions of Tremere openly competing against each other, rather than politicking against rivals in the Pyramid, is largely a V5 innovation and there won't be much information elsewhere.

      The DA book "House of Tremere" is probably the best source on the origins of the Clan and the rivalries among the "founders" of the Clan, such as Goratrix and Etrius. There are other sources I might recommend, but I'm less certain of their value.

              By the canon lore, a situation as you describe would most probably mean the city changing between Sabbat and non Sabbat, period.

              Even if some of the minor groups within the main clan had occupied the chantry for a time, upon losing it they would make a thorough clean up themselves before leaving, as to protect their secrets, otherwise they would be in far more pressing trouble than losing a city.

              The whole point of the Tremere in previous editions is their structure under the Pyramid. The clan is tightly controlled by its Inner Circle, every member effectively blackmailed to stay in line, through blood rituals. "Heretics" play a very dangerous game, to the point that most are willing to die with their secrets.

              Instead of using the Tremere Houses, you could make this story work with other sorcerer clans and lineages. Kolduns from the Tzimisce clan, Assamite Sorcerers, Giovanni Necromancers, Followers of Set, in Legacy many groups outside the Tremere practiced some form of Thaumaturgy, and while they're generally smaller, each is particularly sought of by certain parties.

                                      A primer on Vampire Magic Traditions for such a setting, may anyone want to follow a similar idea:

                                      Lilith/Crone Magic
                                      - Only attested in myth, with just very powerful First Humans said to wield it. Ancient lore does attribute to Caine mastery over Blood Magic with the help of powerful teachers. This can have a lot of interpretations, but mostly that all sorts of forgotten prehistoric sorcery traditions may exist.

                                      Dur-An-Ki
                                      - The oldest tradition actually known to Kindred society, it dates back to the Second City by lore about the clan's foundation, brought by Haqim himself as part of their foundation. It's a very ceremonial style derived from covenants with old gods from Mesopotamia. While formulaic, it's not naturalistic like Thaumaturgy, and have a more personal, custom, artistic approach and is frequently performed in drug-induced states.

                                      Koldunic Sorcery
                                      - Practiced from ancient times by the Tzimisce, it's said by some to predate Vicissitude, though neither of them is claimed as the original blood signature of the Eldest. Koldunism is shamanic and deeply linked to the land itself, divided in five Krainas, elemental paths of magic, all with a theme of communion with the landscape, the region, its fundamental nature.

                                      Sihr
                                      - Originally created by the Banu Haqim, spread to all clans in the Middle Ages, and practiced today only by the Hajj, a faction of Nosferatu that protect the holiest sites of the Muslims, it is a Faith-based, exclusively Islamic magic. The practitioner can't be from a vampiric variety of the Faith, instead belonging to a regular branch of Islam and studying the Qur'an.

                                      Necromancy
                                      - Actually itself a collection of traditions though they have less emphasis on methodology than in object: Mortis investigate the state of being a corpse, Deimos manipulates bodily chemistry by the Theory of Four Humours, Thanatosis is dedicated to decomposition, Nihilistics studies the Underworld as a dimension, Nigrimancy is the summoning and control over Ghosts in the Material World derived from Roman ancestor practices, and Apombo focus on the very ways the dead connect with the living world. Those are not all the Paths of Necromancy, are its main traditions of magic.

                                      Abyss Mysticism
                                      - Communion with the Abyss through the use of Obtenebration, this sorcerous study develop distinct rituals as they invite an otherworldly shadow-stuff to reshape and mutate the body of the practitioner in the process of the magic.

                                      Mithraic Mysteries
                                      - Developed by the Ventrue Creteus (himself an Old Form practitioner), an archaic but standing sorcery form based on the reenacting of myths from the Mithras Cycle, known in detail only to the members of the Mystery Cult and enacted only during ceremonies at a Mithraeum.

                                      Sadhana
                                      - An ancient form developed in India by mortals and adapted to the undead by the local Ventrue for practice by undead Brahmin. Along with the study of the Vedas, intercession of Hindu gods by alternating propitiation and defiance, and a lot of formulaic rituals, this tradition puts huge emphasis on austerity, sacrifice and meditation in challenging conditions. Many are ascetics.

                                      Akhu
                                      - Ancient Egyptian Blood magic practiced by most Setite/Ministry high priests. It requires a ritual Blasphemy-Shrine, corpses, effigies a lot of myth reenactment and a special brew of red beer made with a relatively small add of blood.

                                      Nahuallotl
                                      - Developed by the Tlacique, a Native American Kindred lineage believed to descend from the Followers of Set, it requires additional sacrifice in blood and organs from mortals to appease a host of gods. Its power may be greater if the sacrificial mortal is true believer and volunteer.

                                      Wanga
                                      - A syncretic tradition from the Afro-Caribbean Vampires, developed by the Nasyon San An for use by all vampires of Afro-American syncretic faiths and expertly used by the Serpents of Light. It may or may not be derived from Akhu, it may or may not have been developed by the entity known as Baron Samedi.

                                      Thaumaturgy
                                      - Derived from the practices developed by the precursors of the Mystic Order of Hermes and taught by Lord Tremere to his students since before the foundation of the Order in 767. This tradition developed more magical Paths than any other, coming from a prestigious lineage of True Magic systems and adapted to the Blood by one of the most brilliant Mages in history, the same who, with Bonisagus, developed the practice of Cértamen. As Blood Sorcery it was developed in the 12th century, but still contain a lot of Hermetic principles and even some say, new developments derived from secret contact with allies among the Order continuing today.

                                      Old Form
                                      - Actually an umbrella term for a huge number of smaller traditions derived from mortal magic that come and go over the centuries, Vampires who practice Old Form start by adapting occult practices of the living of a given area, usually their own living people, and with time may keep it at that or learn new mortal traditions and theories, forming a more eclectic personal take on magic. In a sense most traditions start with Old Form and specialize, but practitioners that eschew the last step stay Old Form.

                                      Inconnu Practices
                                      - This tradition differs in that it requires another as a starting point. It's unclear if the Inconnu is taught by peers to transcend the initial tradition or if this is a natural evolution among them, but the Vampire starts to ditch components in their magic while adding time and meditation, using patience to let the fewer elements do the work. And they keep doing this until patience is the only remaining component, a practice made of pure meditation to produce any magical effect.

                                      Infernal/Dark Magic
                                      - It can take the form of any other tradition, and Thaumaturgy is common today, but studying is not required. Infernalists find summoning rites that work with intrinsic power, and then bargain with the malevolent spirits so contacted for the knowledge of magic. Whatever comes is more a manifestation of the spirit's power than of the Blood and study of the sorcerer, known through alien intuition and corrupt with unknown effect.

                                      ======================================

                                      Traditions from the 20th and 21st centuries

                                      Old Skool
                                      - Actually a variation of Old Form, this term is used to highlight when it takes a character of reconstruction. This is not strict, and many will use those names interchangeably, but the idea is to call Old Skool when the sorcerer learned by piecing together a tradition they couldn't get an actual teacher, usually because they all died and only fragments remain. Practitioners aren't called Old Skool individually, though, but by the specific tradition they pieced together and that most also follow as personal philosophy.

                                      New Age
                                      - A more proper tradition, follows similar steps to previous syncretic systems. It's in its infancy and so has not coalesced into a unified form, but it's recognized by unifying Asian religions, European occultism and pseudosciences.

                                      Punk Sorcery
                                      - Developed by the Brujah, based on emotional intensity. It's occult lineage is old, with modern elements coming from philosophy, especially schools of Ethics and Epistemology. Sorcerers are unified by goal, though, and an apprentice is judged by political ideas more than tradition purism.

                                      Hacktivist Thaumaturgy
                                      - This tradition was specifically developed by a cabal of defector Tremere, the Digital Draculas, and taught to anyone approved by the digital movement The Red Question. It adapts regular Hermetic principles through the application of modern Information Theory to work within the digital world. It's not about using computers as ritual components, something any tradition can do, but using digital data as the medium within the magic exists.

                                      Thin-Blood Alchemy
                                      - Those of weakest Blood always developed a number of strange properties. Some develop a unique Discipline spontaneously. Others have a number of weird precognitive powers. Many of them had no idea or method to their abilities, it just was. But in recent times they learned that their Blood can power recipes mixing arcane ingredients with some principles of Humours theory. While it isn't actual Alchemy, it takes the name, and with the spread of the practice the occurrence of other weird powers reduced.
