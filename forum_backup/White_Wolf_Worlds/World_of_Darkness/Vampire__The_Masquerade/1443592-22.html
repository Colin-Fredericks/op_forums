        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
        The short version is that you can pump up your stats a large amount, heal a lot more damage, and require far less effort when using Disciplines. Theoretically, this does eliminate most of the problems of Attributes over 5 if not Disciplines.

        Example:

        According to V5 rules, Helena has Blood Potency 7. This means that she can add 4 dice during a Blood Surge.

        Which means that she naturally has Strength 5 and Dexterity 9 but goes up to Strength 9, Dexterity 9, and so on. The big difference from other is you Blood Surge one Attribute Pool at a time versus one Attribute. So you improve ALL your Physicals, Socials, or whatnot with one use of it.

        If we still had appearance, she'd "normally" be Appearance 5 and naturally one of the most beautiful women on Earth (I use Angelina Jolie as a basis because why not) and yet would be Appearance 9. However, she can still up her Socials with a Blood Surge to the superhuman level she's famous for.

        The big difference is that supernatural potency is not "on" for Methuselahs like them at all times. They have to activate it.

        So V5 rules wise, Helena hasn't actually "lost" any of her Superhuman Attributes. Just Disciplines.
        This, IMO, is fairly poor mechanics design.

        First, it's less intuitive. Appearance 9 is four more dice over Appearance 5. Got it. I can read that off the NPC's statblock in a single line. The bonus from Blood Surge at Blood Potency 7 is... what? Also, I have to remember that new rule is a thing, when this is a new edition for a very old game. It just doesn't do as good a job conveying the NPC's capabilities at a glance. Helena's stats say Charisma 5, and my gut reaction is, "Oh, so the fabled Helen of Troy isn't leagues beyond mortal women. Even though that's what the developers want."

        Second, it's much less cool to have to activate a power, or to pay a cost for a power, than to have it free/always on. In my experience, free/always on powers are VASTLY more appealing to players. If I were playing a superhumanly beautiful elder PC, I would much rather have Appearance 6 than be able to spend blood to roll extra Appearance dice to the same numeric effect. Or even to better numeric effect. It's just inherently cooler to always have the bigger number, no fuss no muss. There is 30 years of history and sense of scale behind those numbers.

        All of this would be one thing, if V5's developers were designing a brand new game. (I don't think a good thing, but it'd still be one thing.) But when it comes on the heel of 6+ traits getting nuked... the fan reaction of "so elders are weaker" was fairly predictable, even if it wasn't true. Paradox ignored the social aspect of these rules changes, and the fact they would foster a misconception among the player base that more attentive/devoted fans would have to correct.

        Just a bad decision on numerous levels. Should've kept 6+ traits. Way simpler.

        Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.

                    Here's the V5 wording if it helps:
                    Any vampire can call upon their
                    Blood to temporarily augment their Attributes, whether Physical,
                    Social, or Mental. When the character wills a Blood Surge, the player
                    can add a number of dice to a dice
                    pool incorporating an Attribute.
                    The number of dice a Blood Surge
                    adds depends on the character’s
                    Blood Potency; characters can only
                    use Blood Surge once per roll.

                    [...]

                    Blood Surge applies only to a
                    single roll of the dice.
                    The key bits being Physical, Mental OR Social, A dice pool incorporating AN Attribute, ONCE per roll and A SINGLE roll.

                    So it only applies to one roll using any one Attribute. That doesn't even mean it lasts for all Strength checks on a turn. That means it lasts for one Strength check on a single turn and that's it.

                    This is another one of those examples where the RAW are different to what has been claimed. It does make me wonder if everyone is playing house rules and just glosses over what's actually in the books? It happens!

                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                                    While true, combat doesn't work in the area of Strength/Dex/Stam the same either. Ergo that amount of expenditure isn't necessary anymore. Also, I'm pretty sure that your physical atttributes were limited to your Generation back then as well.

                                                    You couldn't physically pump your stats as a 8th generation vampire above 5. Helena would be limited to 9 in her Attributes.
                                                    The rule is you can pump up to 1 above your generational limit (to a maximum of 10) for one scene at a cost of 1 blood point per +1 bump. They can also pump any of the physical stats to as high as 10, but only for three turns after they stop spending blood on increasing their attributes.

                                                    So a 13th Gen with Strength 5 could burn 1 point to go to 6 for a scene. On the next four turns they could spend another to bump it to 7, 8, 9 and then 10, but unless they spend another blood point on pumping stats, that level will fade back to six after more three turns... and they've burned half their blood pool getting to 10 so they can only keep spending for five more turns if they started at full.

                                                    Helena thus could pump her stats to as high as 10... the limit of a mortal human dice pool and maintain that level for an entire scene... and could add +10 in a single turn due to her generational spending limit being so high.
