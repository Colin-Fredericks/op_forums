    Honestly, I don't think there's anything wrong with Christopher's concept because there's nothing inherently ridiculous about child vampires--they can be and usually are terrifying. Nicolai was one of the best characters of "classic" Chicago by Night and one of the most unsettling.

    But I did think the way he was presented in LA by Night was always meant to carry the whiff of the ridiculous. Like, "the mastermind of LA is not some Doctor Doom genius. Not in the slightest. He's more like the mall ninja kid waving around a internet ordered katana and talking about his latest obsession. Only he has the powers of a god."

        Berlin

        As CTPhipps notes the original Berlin by Night has come in for a great deal of criticism. It is widely considered to be one of the “so bad its good” books from the early run of White Wolf, much like Dirty Secrets. For every decent or even good idea, there is at least one WTF idea in each book. For example, that Cold War era Berlin would have two princes is a solid, and highly plausible, idea. That the Berlin zoo is housing vampiric animals is a WTF moment.

        In anycase, as CTPhipps writes, this chapter does a good job of updating Berlin into something playable, at least until the time of V5. And there is still plenty here to use in an Anarch game set in the city. Characters such as Ulfrike, Strohmann, and Gunther may all have survived, to make unlife interesting. And in my head Gunter is always pronounced Goonther and he acts like the Mike Myers character Dieter.

        This chapter does manage to feel a bit like a homage to Cold War era spy movies set in Berlin. An outsider (Berlin) comes into town, he cross the (location of) the Wall, gets involved in local power structures, deal with local women, there is a violent confrontation and even a car chase. There is a bit of The Spy Who Came in From the Cold to this chapter.


        Of course, this is Beckett we are talking about so perhaps it is closer to Top Secret.


        This chapter also does a solid job of depicting the local Kindred and the (then) current political situation, and interesting locations across the city. In the latter category, it is better than the chapters set in New York and London. The recovery of a copy of the Book of the Grave War gives it a position in terms of the time line, later in the narrative and before Beckett turns it over to Bindusara for repair and restoration (and Bindusara immediately sells it).

        Marie

        This character gives a dark patina to ever section in which she appears or is mentioned. BJD is hardly a comedy – aside from the joke CTPhipps and I make at Beckett’s expense – but it does manager to hit a lot of different tones. Horror, thriller, action, intrigue, and so forth. Except when Marie appears, because she gives everything at least a low revulsion.

        As WWII progressed, the Nazi high command increasingly brought in women to many positions otherwise occupied by men. This freed up the men to fight and participate in the war, and meant women worked to support the Nazi apparatus of state.

        Marie herself was probably a member of the SS-Helferinnenkorps (Women Helper Corps). Members were assigned as administrative staff and supply personnel, and served in command positions and as guards at women's concentration camps and participated in Nazi atrocities during the war.

        I choose to see her as Beckett’s opposite. He is a Noddist scholar, with the occasional good impulse and circle of friends despite himself. She likely destroys everything she touches, and never stops putting the flame to vampires, their history and anything remotely decent about vampire society. As Nazi NPCs go she is more legit than members of the high command or the Tzimisce SnM asshole, Doktor Totentanz.

        As an aside, she almost appeared in Hunger from Beyond, where her last name would have been Von Juntz.

                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                        Part of the issue of New York City is that I don't think it's a terribly interesting place Kindred politics wise. Which is weird because it is the place for most series in the world. Because the city is newly acquired from the Sabbat, there's no real ability for many Kindred to have the kind of lengthy personal connections to the city that vampires in Chicago have.
                        The writing of New York by Night came at a certain point in the publication history of Vampire: the Masquerade in which the notion of place was discarded. In the earlier books in the line there was a strong notion that travel between cities was dangerous, and that the Kindred population of a particular location was tied to that place. In part this was a literary device, to write about place through people, in a sense, a form of prosopography. So, the characters in, say, Chicago by Night are designed to reflect the history of the city. Tyler's line, for instance, is a chronological run through the stages of political protest in the city over its existence. Even the characters which didn't live their natural lives within the city often link to Chicago in thematic ways.

                        The problem with New York by Night is that by around the year 2000, the books being written for Vampire: the Masquerade envisage the game as being about undead jet-setters, fighting notionally important conflicts tied into the metaplot. There are many consequences of this, but one is that places become set dressing, backdrops to geopolitical struggles. Read New York by Night with this in mind is revealing, as it is a book in which the writer doesn't show that much interest in New York as a place.

                        (An even worse example of this in action can be seen in London by Night, where the writers are not just not interested in writing about the city, but also are distinctly uninterested in the period in which the book is set.)

                        This is the reason why, despite all its flaws, Berlin by Night feels, despite all its flaws, to be a more engaging book, one which is more distinctly part of the setting. As crass as the vampire Nazis are, they do say something about the history of the city. The dual, competing princes may seem contrived, but, on a metaphorical level, speak to the position of the old Kingdom of Prussia as a state feeling the pull of both Eastern and Western Europe. There is a lot which I dislike about Berlin by Night, but it feels like material I could work with should I chose to set a chronicle in the city. With New York (or worse, London or San Francisco), I suspect that I would be inclined to start from scratch.

                            I agree with this and it puts into words something I hadn't really been able to figure out until you state it. For me, the biggest flaw of New York City By Night is the fact that so much flavor went into things like Chicago By Night and its vampires reflecting mortal trends that it feels like the city is very arbitary. Given the fact New York City is the kind of place that has such an immense history and its citizens have their own famous connection to it, it felt like a massively wasted opportunity. The Tzimisce Antediluvian being the centerpiece of the city is also something that just isn't as interesting as the rest of New York City as a whole. Given New York City is such a pop culture centerpiece, it's also disappointing it's so lacking in flavor. Again, I don't think it was badly written but it's focus on Tzimisce and relative small size hurt the supplement IMHO.

                            London by Night is almost hilarious in its inaccuracies (I strongly recommend the RPG.net review of it) but the big thing about it is that it doesn't even apply any of its status in-setting to its write-up. It's a city full of Neonates and Ancilla when it should be one of the homes of Ancient powerful and terrifying vampires under Mithras' control. Europe was supposed to be a place where the Neonates were under the thumbs of brutal ancients who had divided up the territory among themselves while America was still something of a Kindred Wild West. Later books like NbN and LbN tried to drastically reduce the power level like V5 did with the Beckoning and Anarch revolts but did so without really justifying it. I also felt the characters didn't really resonate with the big personalities that made so many early characters memorable.

                            As for San Fransisco by Night, I felt that also came from the design philosophy of the city being just a set piece for the Kindred politics. The idea was creating a Kuei-Jin run police state where the vampires were running a resistance against it to "take back their city" as best I could tell. I'm hesitant to call out writers but that just doesn't feel like it fits at all with Kindred power politics, relationships, personal horror, or the city of SF itself. It also is, "oooo, those evil Kuei-Jin vs. us heroic Cainites!" - which, what? Given the city was one of the first ones to have any metaplot in V:TM and the setting of K:TE, it also felt like a waste. I always felt it would also be the perfect "iconic" city for Mage: The Ascension too.

                            I also feel like, for all of Berlin by Night's flaws, it certainly had PERSONALITY.
