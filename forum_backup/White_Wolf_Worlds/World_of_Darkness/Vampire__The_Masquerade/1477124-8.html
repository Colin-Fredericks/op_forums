                Regarding the Tzimisce and stereotypes:

                I don't necessarily think that I have too much to add on this front either, but a couple of points for consideration. I don't necessarily think that the Tzimisce themselves should be seen as a statement about Eastern Europe, but how Eastern Europe has been described and defined in relation to the Tzimisce is something that should be considered. It was pointed out earlier that portions of the real life USA do not entirely line up with their depiction in the WoD, but there is a difference between having particular differences or small divergences in history versus completely altering the reality in order to better fit a classic trope, a classic trope that was kind of racist even in its time. If modern Eastern Europe is still being presented as a quirky rural backwater in order to make it fit better with Tzimisce-as-Draculas, when in reality it is as industrialized and modernized as most other places, that is a problem in my opinion. I haven't read the "A World of Darkness" books closely, so I don't know how places like Romania are described, but I think it would be a problem if their reality was being completely ignored in order to make it better fit the preconceptions of outsiders, preconceptions that are based on a trope conceived of by outsiders which characterized the region as backwards and superstitious a hundred or more years ago.

                I also think it should be pointed out that "appeal to Dracula" is something that should be done with careful consideration. It's pretty commonly agreed among literary critics and scholars that Dracula the vampire resonated with Victorian audiences at least in part due to it paralleling stereotypes about foreign immigration as invasion, sexual violence, and STIs like syphilis. When you add in the fact that Tzimisce torture and body horror often carries sexualized aspects, there are some comparisons that merit reflection. I don't think these ideas dominate the entire character of the Clan, or that the Tzimisce concept is unsalvageable as a result, but I do think that they ought to be considered, and making efforts to expand the Clan beyond its association with Dracula is probably wise for that reason.

                Regarding Disciplines:

                I think it's a bit of a strawman to claim that combining Vicissitude with Protean is like wanting to combine Dominate and Presence, or Fortitude and Potence. Generally speaking, proponents are not arguing that it should be collapsed into Protean exclusively, but as an Amalgam with some other Discipline that can explain the distinctions between the two, while acknowledge that there is a considerable amount of overlap. The fact that Vicissitude can be used on other bodies, either to alter them or to transplant features from one specimen to another, is the main distinction. Protean is definitely altering flesh and bone when it adds claws to your hands or changes you into an animal, and the transformation to Zulo does not require the sculpting-by-hand required by earlier Vicissitude powers AFAIK. Presence and Dominate have significant differences in how they operate; one influences emotions, and thus can only affect thoughts and actions indirectly; the other directly manipulates actions, thoughts, and memories. There is some common ground between the two, but there are clear fundamental distinctions as well. And while increasing physical power via Potence could reasonably used to explain a greater resilience against physical damage, it is less obvious how that could reasonably explain the resistance to fire and sunlight provided by Fortitude.

                                                        Originally posted by Kharnov View Post

                                                        Something must be said here.

                                                        Categorizations aside, the Great Clans are not the core clans, and that's a part of the problem you see here.

                                                        Buy it or not, from a design perspective the game has seven core clans, no more. All others, without exception, are specialized extra options through and through. They're defined as clans because of their major roles in the setting, but although major those roles are still specialized from the POV of the authors.

                                                        That's why Vicissitude is a specialized gimmick. As are Obtenebration, Necromancy, Chimerstry, Quietus and Serpentis. Each is a very specialized gimmick in origin, meant to quickly and easily emphasize that clan's role in the setting as NPCs and frequently not designed for PC use.

                                                        My own thought on that, as already discussed in other threads, is that fusing Disciplines doesn't work for V20 and previous edition. The Discipline mechanics may demand an entire overhaul, as they did both in V5 and Requiem (very differently), but if you don't do it, then just getting used to their many similarities is the way to go. Otherwise you'll spend a lot of time and effort to make adjustments that may at best serve you for a couple powers while letting 50 other sets untouched and most people still not agree with your changes,

                                                        V5's take works. It isn't the best (because this is Requiem's by far), but it works just fine, it is just another way of doing it. The only problem is the ever hated limit of one power per level, that creates so much unnecessary restrictions.

                                                        Why cleaning-up powers doesn't work with Original Rules™? Because they're inflexible as designed, so no matter how much you see any other as similar, merging them simply doesn't work, you'll still end up with a power set that doesn't do what they're meant to do separately. Why V5's Vicissitude is a problem? Because beyond the whole point of Dominate-Amalgam not working, with the power limit it means that actually the Tzimisce don't fully have a third Discipline if they want to go Vicissitude. They have Protean and the slot to be used for making Protean also have Vicissitude effects. Now similarity is a problem as the same character is bound to have both powers, and is left with a dead Dominate slot effectively making them just two-Disciplines strong with a gimmicky extra.

Only later was it retconned that Dementation was their original Discipline, with Dominate Malkavians being (in lore) the result of a powerful ritual, "the Great Trick," that was meant to make them fit into the Camarilla better.

                                                            Necromancy is interesting as well, because I think there's some rationale for having it distinct from other kinds of Blood Magic, but you could just as easily imagine it as part of a "core" Blood Sorcery Discipline instead.

                                                            I was not aware that Amalgams counted towards the stupid 1-power-per-level restriction of V5, that certainly makes it even more annoying.
