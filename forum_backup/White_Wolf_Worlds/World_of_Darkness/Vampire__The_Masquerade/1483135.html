          What do you mean by crossovers? Do you mean PCs being from the different game lines? If so, it's generally been bad. I have a great core group of players now so it's not an issue of having power gamers or the mechanics being disruptive. It's about preserving the feel of how the setting should be interpreted by each type of creature. It's hard to do justice for each kind with the limited time an ST has to spend on each PC.

          Or do you mean the ST actively including elements of the various game lines in his setting for one of the games? If so, I've had good success here. It's something I actively do now. Since I don't have to showcase the world, just a selected element of it, I can do so in a way that adds to the setting's feel instead of being disruptive to it. Now, that's usually something more of a sandbox element than through active plot I am running. So it only happens if one of the PCs decides to involve herself with an aspect of the sandbox.

              All of them. Each splat has their own advantages and such. Even so, every splat can dip their toes into the territory of the other splats to some extent. Since vampire's are best at social/political manipulation, which is seen as the weakest aspect in a crossover, you can see the first issue. The only splat that doesn't beat the vampire is the wraith, which is basically out because they're stuck in the shadowlands.

              The best advantage of the common kindred is their blood itself. They can half all bashing damage, completely bind people who taste their blood, and increase their physical attributes all the way to 10 in short bursts. That said, every other splat can do all these too, or come pretty close to replicating them with their abilities. As such, vampires are often seen as weaker thanks to this fact.

              That said, almost none of the splats know that much about each other. Mages surprisingly know little about the other splats due to never interacting that often. Werewolves only see vampires are wyrm tainted, and often nothing of the others. Changelings actively avoid the boring other splats. You get the idea. Outside of a few individual members of each splat, nobody knows much, if anything, about the other splats really.

                  Cross Over or Kitchen Sink games are only as over or under powered as the game group want it to be.

                  A Wraith cross over just makes the Necromancy clans/bloodlines have more depth and story hooks. But it can have the side issue of increasing the necromancers power over other clans.

                  Adding Mage to Vampire usually breaks the game because of "instant sunlight" or the ever popular "turn the vampire back into a human" which tends to deflate the power of older vampires. Oddly enough I don't recall ever seeing a Mage sphere combination for increasing the power of a vampire.

                  Though if you want truely game breaking, do a cross over with Exalted since its original concept was the true prehistory setting of WoD. Caine would probably classify as an Abyssal Half Caste locked at Essence 3, which means most vampires are barely essence 1. Now add an Essence 10 exalt into the setting and watch everything go horribly wrong.

                              My reference was to the PC's playing Vampires, and NPC's as the other splats. Im mostly familiar with Vampire, Mage and Demon, with Werewolf less so. Thus far, I've found Vampires to be on relatively equal footing with other splats of similar build (Werewolf does start much stronger but doesn't scale well against Vampires, Mages scale from equal footing to "Jesus Christ, 12 Aggravated?" relatively quickly but are quite the glass cannon, and Demons only scale well with xp purchased Apocalyptic Forms).

                                          Never done a mixed PC group, but the other game lines sort of make a show sometimes. NPCs of other splats very rarely make a direct appearance, but their settings do. The power levels don't worry me much, because they're not going to be overshadowing the vampires, a VtM game is about the vampires. They're mostly there for flavour.

                                          Sometimes you see something weird, like your coeterie meets up at a fast food joint and you notice for the first time that there's a pretty noticable scent of human blood in the kitchen. "What the fuck is wrong with that guy's aura??" You may casually drink off some stranger in an alley and spend the rest of the evening wandering around fairy land. Sometimes werewolves go on a rampage at the company where your ghoul was working. You may observe a bunch of rats wandering around in a suit of human skin, or a death cult that appears to be worshipping somthing called a "Neverborn". The Elders usually call this "not our division," don't worry about it.

                                          Wraith predominates in influence for me, due to Necromancy. If you're playing Giovanni, expect the Underworld to at least be background noise even if you're not dealing with it yourself. Users of advanced Auspex, Dur-An-Ki and Koldunic sorcery can expect some degree spirits and Umbra shenanigans, though I've rarely had cause to bring them in. There are rare banes that can afflict vampires and become a problem for the community. Changelings have their love of political nonsense and their own dealings with the undead, particularly the unseelie who are drawn to vampire's "dark glamour" and the nobles lusting after their own lost immortality, so I can occasionally use them. They also have personal conflicts with the Ravnos that can crop up. Both Wraith and Changeling largely turn up as favour swapping. They're still niche and seldom happen though.

                                          Fera and mages are too rare, and typically hostile in the former's case, to really make an appearance. Also because I haven't actually played them and have concerns about potential power discrepancies. However kinfolk may make an appearance, and mages should be careful about the stuff they leave lying around. I haven't looked into Mummy, but I might for a Setite game. Demon I don't mind, but have never used. Hunter is an obvious one for crossover, though again I haven't used it. Recent events have me reconsidering that.

                                          Having vampires aware that the world is bigger than it seems and that there are other things going bump in the night, and frankly, they are just as bad if not worse than you, has worked great for me. Also they have a lot in common with those guys in a way. Blah blah, politics, blah blah, death, blah blah, apocalypse, blah blah, out of touch old people ordering me around.

                                              I'm the going to be mutant in the room that actually Loves crossover games.

                                              The best way to go about it from my perspective has always been to treat each splat like an assigned role. Werewolves are physical combat experts, Vampires are the "face" equiped for mental and social combat, changeling are specialty-expert and non-linear problem solvers, wraiths are the best at stealth, recon, and information gathering, and Mages are NPC ONLY if they show up.

                                              People don't need to stick to their role 100%(we're not playing d&d after all), but gravitating toward it and giving each role equal spotlight keeps players from being overshadowes. Playing a straight combat game with an ahroun get of fenris and a brujah on the same team will often leave the brujah feeling useless.

                                              You'll need to make a few quick fixes for various rules conflicts. For Example: when dealing with splats missing humanity and virtue scores various vampire powers need to be decided on an individual basis whether they're contested vs a subsyitute, are rolled against a difficulty instead, or Auto-succeed.
