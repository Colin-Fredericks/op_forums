    Paradox CEO says Bloodlines is a "Dead End".

    10-08-2024, 11:55 PM
    https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/pa...-someone-else/ - It's been nearly a decade since Paradox Interactive acquired White Wolf and the World of Darkness, and nearly as long since it started working with Hard Suit Labs based on its pitch for Vampire: The Masquerade – Bloodlines 2. And boy has it been a rough time.

    After four years working in secret, Hard Suit and Paradox announced Bloodlines 2 in 2019. We'd be playing it in 2020, the pair said. Then it was delayed. And delayed again. The 2020 target became 2021, and then it was delayed indefinitely, with Hard Suit given the boot.

    For a while, it seemed like cancellation was inevitable. And it did almost end up in the bin. "If we hadn't found The Chinese Room," deputy CEO Mattias Lilja tells me, "and seen what they'd done with the early work, [cancellation] would have been the next logical step, because we could not continue as we did."

    Lilja believes it's in a better place now—though it was recently struck with yet another delay, pushing it out of 2024 and into the first half of 2025. "It's been in development a very long time, but we are starting to see the game shape up to be something we can… we think it will be a World of Darkness experience."

    It's strangely tepid praise, but also refreshing. The reason for this interview was not the usual marketing-driven fare. No big announcements. No hype. Just a rather frank explanation about what's going on at Paradox, the reasons why it's been struggling recently, and what it's doing to change its fortunes. But even with that in mind, there seems to be a bit of nervousness about how Bloodlines 2 will ultimately be received. And it's not something that Paradox wants to go through again.

    "It is not in our strategic direction to make this kind of game," Lilja says. "So if Bloodlines 2, God willing, is successful, Bloodlines 3 [will be] done by someone else, on the license from us. I would say it's the sort of strategic way this would work. So it's still an outlier from what we're supposed to do, we don't know that stuff, so we should probably let other people do it."

    Paradox has its own small-scale experimental label, Paradox Arc, where it can try different things, but a big RPG doesn't remotely fit that. It would have to be something "very different", says Lilja, like a CRPG, but even that's a "big investment", and nowadays expectations are higher than ever thanks to Baldur's Gate 3. So "regardless of outcome," he says, Bloodlines is a "dead end".

    As for the future of Bloodlines 2, specifically: "We don't drop games. We make sure they work. We make sure they fulfil the promise that they have. The rest is very much up to the players." So you can expect the usual slate of updates and fixes, but DLC? I wouldn't hold your breath.

    "I think some studios do strategic investments, long term things, because they feel that the cost of not doing it is too high. But, I mean, I think it's fairly clear, at least to me, and I think to you, even in the best of cases, Bloodlines does not have a super long shelf life. That's not the way these games behave. You have an influx of players, there's a bit of word of mouth, and they have a high peak, and then they trail off. And it's not the type of gameplay that develops over time that much. So I think that's part of why these types of games are not really that attractive to us."

    Yet it was the persistence of Bloodlines' fans that inspired this sequel, 20 years after the original. Better supported RPGs, meanwhile, can keep going year after year, as evidenced by the incredibly long life of Bethesda RPGs. And then there's Baldur's Gate—a 26-year-old series that's just spawned its third game, which in turn has been one of Steam's most played games for over a year, despite the lack of DLC. Some RPGs have an incredibly long tail. But it is clear they aren't in Paradox's wheelhouse, and passing the licence to other caretakers might be the smart call here.
    The CEO saying that Bloodlines is a 'dead end' and writing off a hypothetical Bloodlines 3 isn't exactly instilling me with very good expectations for part 2. Honestly it sounds like the CEO doesn't have any interest in the WoD IP whatsoever. At this point, I'm kind of hoping they sell the IP to someone else who has more desire to work with it.

          Let's go over some of the highlights over the last few years:
          • Lamplighter's Leauge: Paradox had high hopes for the game and Harebrained Games are a good developer (they made those isometric Shadowrun games, for example). But months before the launch, thanks to a lack of support from Paradox 80% of the staff at the studio were let go. Then the game got released to middling reviews (mostly because of the bugs and lack of content) and then Paradox had to announce that it considered the game a failure. And it doesn't seem like there's any more content planned for it.
          • City Skylines 2: That game launched in a disastrous state. Just google it. A complete failure. The developers are still busy trying to crawl out of that hole and win back the goodwill of the players...
          • Star Trek Infinite: One of the more popular mods for their Sci-Fi-grand strategy game Stellaris revolves around Star Trek. And so Paradox thought: Hey, let's have someone develop an official version. And that's Star Trek Infinite except... the fan-mods are simply better. The game launches, disappoints (middling reviews, lack of interest, sales...) and six months after launch it's announced that Paradox will stop any support for the game. In other words, Paradox isn't paying the devs to patch the game or make DLC for it.
          • Life By You: It was pitched as a new Sims-type life-building game. They even created a studio for it called Tectone. Well, a month ago or so, Paradox announced it had decided to cancel the game. This game had been in development since 2019. Five years of development, a whole studio of developers and 19 million dollars in investment... all flushed down the toilet. Ah, yeah, they closed the studio Tectone as well on top of that.
          • Prison Architect 2: Despite previous delays, the game's release-date is now changed to "unknown". It seems like there's still a lot of work ahead for the developers. (source: "Paradox indefinitely delays Prison Architect 2, just like it did with Life By You before it was scrapped")

          Bloodlines 2 turning out to be a failure wouldn't be that surprising.

          Look at what I outlined above. This is what this means: Paradox isn't interested in paying The Chinese Room to patch the game and/or develop DLC for it. As the examples above show, Paradox has no problem whatsoever with abandoning games very quickly after release. Already musing about what Bloodlines 3 could be like is a very bad sign. I also feel really bad for the people at The Chinese Room who I imagine are working very hard right now to meet the new release date for Bloodlines 2 next year. Because as the examples above also show, you know what will happen to those people once the game gets released: They'll get fired. Because Paradox isn't interested in paying them to patch the game and/or develop DLC. And The Chinese Room studio is more focused on walking-simulator/first-person-horror-type-games like Still Wakes The Deep so they don't need developers who do RPG-action/stealth-stuff.

But if Paradox is led by an idiot who seems to have stuck his head in the sand and doesn't know where the wind is blowing... Well, I'm not surprised then that as far as publishing is concerned all you get from Paradox is a series of failures.

                              Not to be too forgiving here, but despite the tone we really haven't learned that much. Paradox was never planning on doing that much in-house, because they're not that big of a video game company. They were always planning on getting more studios with more experience in specific game styles to do the majority of the work for their brand; just like how even when they started a WW subsidiary way back when, they were talking about doing core development in house and then shopping the license out to other TTRPG makers to fill out the game lines. Saying that BL isn't Paradox's kinda of video game rings pretty hollow when Paradox up and bought a studio to add to their portfolio that does action cRPGs just to do the first iteration of BL 2.

                              Honestly, the frankness coming from such a high level c-suite level person seems to be something different from what's being said: Paradox has found out they really don't like being in the brand management business. It seems like they really misjudged the level of internal resources to develop and maintain the core brand identity to the point where they can make deals with existing studios to make X style of game while following Paradox's V5/WoD setting bible and style guides and then not be highly involved in the production of the game. Managing a brand that's going to feel cohesive across TTRPGs, IF games, action cRPG games, comics, and who knows what else is not easy; even if you're hiring other people to do the vast majority of the actual work to make the individual products happen.

                              The whole article feels like they're giving up and just planning to let studios to make whatever games they make as long as the pitch is good enough. While in general that's not a good idea because it leads to brand dilution and so on, it's a lot easier on their internal teams and at least in the short term could be profitable enough to make shareholders happen with the initial purchase before whatever Paradox does with the IPs after nobody really wants to make new games for them anymore.
