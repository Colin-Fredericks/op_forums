    At least on the Anarchs, that's largely their point. You're given their baselines in the book, and, unlike prior editions (or a bunch of media like Bloodlines), things like Baronies and setups with the Anarchs as 'Cam Lite' aren't what they are anymore. The Anarchs are a movement, not a hegemony, and also not organized in any way, so you have a huge diaspora of 'what it means to be an anarch'. The Anarch book for V5 illustrates this in its variety of articles and such.


    As an aside, re: core themes. Beyond the stated core themes of Vampire, and V5 specifically, a lot of people (especially people in this thread) also forget that this stuff isn't being written Just For Existing Players. They've stated before, both directly and indirectly, that the tabletop game exists to provide 'backbone' for story and such for their other media products. Anything being written for the TTRPG is also being looked at for how it'll look as 1. stuff to adapt/transition to things like TV and video games, and 2. how it'll look to people coming into the property through those things. And so going with a defined theme and tone and setup, including a defined antagonist group, makes that infinitely easier.

                Originally posted by elmerg View Post
                At least on the Anarchs, that's largely their point.
                Yes and no.

                To begin with, this isn't anything new. It is what the Anarchs were since day 1 of the game, all the way back to 1991. Not a block, but the multitude of people that didn't belong in the system, either by slipping through the cracks and not trying to go back, or by actively antagonizing it. Baronies are just a title never meant to mean much beyond 'the big boss in town isn't Cam'.

                And the problem is precisely that the Anarchs rarely got defined beyond that. They also were meant to be the protagonists since day 1, you live under Camarilla's control but you're not a part of it. The problem? As such, the Anarchs went undefined. The Camarilla was fleshed out, the Sabbat was fleshed out, and the Anarchs stayed a bunch of 'something'. The players went to play other things and the Anarchs laid more or less on the sideways. A lot of tables and LARPs ignored the Anarchs royally.

                It isn't by mere coincidence that players renewed interest in the Anarchs with V20. Giving actual flesh to the Movement was something badly needed. You don't need to make them a hegemony to do this, either, just create their context. Sample movements and political struggles, instead of an endless parade of sample boring kids with no idea of what they'll do with politics. And at the same time, not being a hegemony, somehow seizing power more than ever because the Camarilla suddenly can't stop the Anarchs if they get dumber.

                Originally posted by elmerg View Post
                Beyond the stated core themes of Vampire, and V5 specifically, a lot of people (especially people in this thread) also forget that this stuff isn't being written Just For Existing Players.
                My only conclusion is that you're not reading some posts, because despite a lot of people complaining about their personal situation, most posts are about things that apply pretty well to new players.

                Yes, the lore of VtM up to V20 is overwhelming and making some simplifications, especially on the core, helps a lot to make it welcoming to newcomers, which is a big deal.

                What on frigging Hell does that have anything to do with the Sabbat being playable? Do they get to be less antagonistic 'cause of that? They've have always been the default antagonists of the game, they getting fans never changed that not even in (most of) their fans minds. They're receiving a treatment in their own book that they should have received in the core, because THAT's the book to make a quick presentation of antagonists as just that, instead of saying your antagonists can't attend the game 'cause they're occupied fighting in Afghanistan (and somehow despite the extensive media coverage the Middle East receives nowadays no one ever noticed an open war among eldritch horrors there).

                From post #5 onward a lot of posts cover either how this idea of over simplification doesn't help with movies, doesn't help with videogames, doesn't help with player experience, doesn't help streamlining the themes of the game, and aren't even something new. RPGs have existed for almost 50 years now. Vampire for 30. Multimedia franchises have been a professional strategy in this market for about 15 years consistently. They're not treading unknown waters and are making rookie mistakes.

                    Originally posted by Ur-Than View Post

                    Funnily enough, I think one of the main but underlying problems of V5, the Anarchs being underwhelming and too close to the Camarilla in practice was explained well enough in a Requiem thread by monteparnas : the writers being from the US have a view were political factions are mostly unchanging and yet overlapse a lot. Democrats and Republicans are a lot closer as parties than similar divided political factions elsewhere in the western world.

                    Even if it's less true today, for a very long time, as an outsider, I saw very little difference between the two but there was some form of "tribalism" with people strongly associating to their party even if they represented perhaps only a fraction of it. But the opposition between such forces is largely irrelevant. It doesn't matter if you vote Democrat or Republican if they agree on 75% of their programs basically.

                    That's the Anarchs and Camarilla in a nutshell. The Anarchs don't seem to want to fundamentaly change Vampiric society. They want to tweak it, make it fairer for young Vamps, sure... but they do'nt fundamentaly oppose its tenets and rules. The Sabbat does and that's definitively what attracted me to them back in the day. If I wanted to play the ones saying "Fuck your system, I hate it, and I'll take you down with it..." well, it's Sabbat or bust basically.
                    I think it's never been Republicans versus Democrats because one side is absolutely in charge (The Camarilla) and the other side is, by definition, on the backfoot (The Anarchs). They're also living in a feudal not democratic society. Mind you, I've never accepted the view that Anarchs viewed themselves as part of the Camarilla. The Camarilla might consider themselves as having authority over the Anarchs but the whole essential argument to me has always been the Anarchs do not recognize the authority of the Prince or his court.

                    I've always felt that the best analog for them has been to emphasize the Gothic PUNK element and have thought the gameline was a supernatural version of Cyberpunk 2020. The Camarilla are the Corpos. They are rich, in charge, and a bunch of bastards. The Anarchs are the criminals, Edgerunners, and syndicates that live on the edge of society and out of the corpos control but with their own brutal society.

                                                            So...

                                                            On the actual topic of the thread:

                                                            I'm kinda frustrated that with 60 seconds, the guy couldn't have lingering on the write-up of one Path long enough for us to screen cap and transcribe it and see what the book is actually going to do with them in print.

                                                            Otherwise it's a decent looking book with VtM words in it, and my only real take away is continual vindication towards my negative criticisms of V5 core's layout decisions.

                                                            On the issue of V5 not just being written for old players:

                                                            V20 was the only VtM book ever written just for old players... and it still sold to new players... and revitalized the WoD for a whole new generation of gamers. VtM was a multimedia brand for most of the 90s and early 00s. VtM had prose (short, novella, stand alone novels, and multi-novel series), comic books, a CCG, boardgames and single deck card games, multiple video games, a TV show, and a WWE wrestler even licensed the name Gangrel for use as his schtick. VtM and the WoD spent most of the 90s as the biggest (though not that big) challenger to D&D as people's first RPG.

                                                            V5 doing things differently because it's meant to appeal to more people and more media presentations... needs to be unpacked and justified (or at least acknowledged not inherently a smart move on Paradox's behalf) because the original run of VtM was already good at that. Just repeating that V5 is meant to be the foundations of a bigger multimedia enterprise isn't enough. What are the changes actually doing that matter to that regard when the original managed it?

                                                            On the issue of the Sabbat not being playable for V5 because of cultural reactions:

                                                            If Paradox was worried about that, they'd scrap the RPG books completely and just go back to letting Onyx Path do whatever with them like they do with Exalted and the CofD. The introduction section of V5 is not shy about VtM being a game about horrible evil people. You can't make V5 family friendly by toning down the Sabbat.
