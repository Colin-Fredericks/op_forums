                Originally posted by Garygeneric View Post
                The whole Alamgam thing stems from wanting to homogenize Discipline content while trying to retain “clan flavor.” But it feels forced, like lore is being replaced by extraneous rules. Makes V:tM feel more theme park video game, less sandbox RPG.
                Frankly, I think they’d have been better served going the opposite direction and developed a unique discipline for each clan akin to how Dementation is a rather specific expression of Dominate with a bit of Auspex thrown in.

                Two-thirds of the work was already done; Assamite (Quietus), Setite (Serpentis), Ravnos (Chimerstry), Lasombra (Obtenebration), Giovanni/Hecata (Necromancy), Tzmisce (Vicissitude), Tremere (Thaumaturgy), Gangrel (Protean), Malkavian (Dementation)... leaving just Brujah, Nosferatu, Toreador and Ventrue.

                So develop, say, a clan-specific Brujah discipline that, say, replaces Potence with something that has more specialized applications of supernatural strength that perhaps overlap slightly with supernatural speed and presence... or a Ventrue discipline that is a Presence more focused on invoking obedience/loyalty reflecting Dominate and Fortitude (the influence is more resilient) leaking into it... or a Nosferatu discipline that’s basically Animalism+ focusing on empowering animal servants through the use of vitae.

                This would be even easier in V5 given its focus on specific powers over a more holistic discipline approach (ex. how Chimerstry starts with static images and higher tiers add additional senses, motion and permanence).

                If the devs had gone that approach they probably could have skipped amalgam powers entirely and made the identity of each clan stronger via the unique discipline each possesed in addition to the other general disciplines, banes and compulsions.

                                                    Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                                                    Still, the clans felt like they were trying to encapsulate -types of people- and clan-as-archtype seems more prominent than -clans as character classes... maybe they just did well, but I never came to the thought that they were actively trying to balance how many clans had what discipline.
                                                    Starting clan design from archetypes and keeping discipline parity in mind are not mutually exclusive design principles, like I suggested above. I think the common disciplines are simply too evenly spread out among the 13 clans in the original gameline for it to be a mere coincidence. It's very easy to end up with different results -- even some of the later official products that rejuggle the clan disciplines end up wildly imbalanced. For example, in the 20th Anniversary Edition of Vampire: The Dark Ages, six clans have Auspex in common, while only two clans share Celerity.
