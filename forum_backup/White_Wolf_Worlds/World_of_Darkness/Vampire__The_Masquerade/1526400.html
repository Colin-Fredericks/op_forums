              It is a question of how easily you can duplicate the clan flaw, and how effective the clan's culture is at identifying imposters. How easily one can develop one of the old "signature" Disciplines was another issue for a lot of chronicles (I always hated the idea that most of the Camarilla clans had Disciplines common to everyone, but everyone else had their own special Discipline that was supposed to be rare or unknown to others. That was a terrible design choice. Trying to universalize the Disciplines so this isn't the case is one of the rare few things about V5 that I like.)

              Some clan flaws are much harder to impersonate than others. It's hard to appear like a Nosferatu or not appear in a reflective surface like a Lasombra unless you are one. You could pick a Flaw that gave to you, but obviously that is not something you can normally cover because it's an artifact of the embrace. Likewise this can make those clans harder to infiltrate others. Marcus Vitel needed a bullshit magical device to do it. Nosferatu may be masters of Obfuscate, but all it takes is one person with better Auspex to expose the whole thing. A Gangrel with several animal features will be easy to detect.

              Some clans are much better at figuring out imposters. Malkavians are hard because of the Madness Network. Tremere because of the Pyramid (and possibly their Thaumaturgy). Ventrue because of how they keep their detailed lineages. Giovanni because they have extensive mortal ties. Setites are probably hard because members probably need to be able to spout out lots of indoctrination, though this has changed in later editions as writers tried to expand the clan far beyond what they were intended to be.

              It is not that these clans are impossible to infiltrate. There's just a lot more obstacles.

              Then there are clans that are easier to infiltrate because they are known for overly promiscuous embraces and are quite poor at any kind of way to determine who is really one of them. Brujah and Toreador are the easiest among the Camarilla clans. I'd imagine Ravnos would be too if you get rid of the Chimestry signature Discipline. Others I think would be more of a middle ground.

              Sabbat clans are hard simply because it is hard to infiltrate the Sabbat. But what about someone already in the Sabbat to infiltrate another Sabbat clan and anti-tribu? It could be fairly easy because 1) clans are less important in the Sabbat, 2) war parties can create lots of new members, 3) many packs can be isolated from others for a long time, and 4) pack member can pick up a lot of knowledge from other pack members. I'm not sure how useful it would be, but I imagine that while general members wouldn't have much interest, certain clans or anti-tribu might want to risk an infiltrator to gain an edge over an internal rival.

                  A slightly different angle regarding the Sabbat: Sabbat can be really good at faking being members of other Clans when they're operating in other Sect territory. Having some direct experience with this, even when it isn't strictly necessary, Sabbat scouting/infiltration/subversion packs can benefit by faking what Clans they're really members of just to sow misconceptions about themselves around should suspicion arise and their enemies start to figure out what's going on. The Sabbat having a much higher percentage of members who were Embraced without a lot of careful consideration mean you have a lot of Shovelheads that would in fact be better fits for Clans other than the one they happened to get stuck with; making the act easier to pull off as well.

                      Played several characters that were not the clan they claimed to be – all in LARPs, where you can get away with more shenanigans.

                      Played a Salubri Healer masquerading as a Malkavian. No one questioned the permanent bandana (or noticed the slightly projecting third eye prop underneath it). Some of the Malkavians convinced themselves that the character wasn’t a Malkavian eventually, but because he was politically influential in their favour, they just offered to teach him Dominate, rather than take any action to his detriment. Once he was finally exposed as a Salubri, one of the Malkavians led the local Tremere him – who successfully kidnapped him and tried to get him out of the locale – only for the Tremere to be taken out by the Malkavians Primogen’s sniper ghoul (another PC) as they headed out of town in a van. Obviously the Salubri had to disappear after that…

                      Played a Tremere masquerading as a Toreador in a city where the Tremere Prince had been taken out (as a setting primer) by the Primogen. My character was the former Prince’s childe, but didn’t last long as a PC because one of open Tremere PCs (who knew who my character was) arranged a meeting with my character, and was followed by almost every faction in the city. They chose to attack after my character had departed, killing the other Tremere. With my character exposed, there was no way back into the game for him. We did have the pleasure of a Toreador dinner party, where all the Toreador PCs went out for an in-character meal – but none of them were actually Toreador: one Tremere, one Daughter of Cacophony, one Lasombra antitribu, and one Setite.

                      Played a thin-blooded, 14th Generation Setite Warrior masquerading as a Ventrue (with a slightly better lineage provided by a Ventrue NPC, whom my PC was a pawn of – for a variety of political reasons). I expected to be exposed as weak immediately, and used as a lacky – but the other Ventrue players were inexperienced, and never asked for my character’s lineage. The character kept his head down, stayed loyal to the Ventrue patron (always putting Clan Ventrue's, and his patron's, interests first), and took advantage of every opportunity he could (save for becoming Prince, when that was on the table – because he didn’t want to be a harbinger of Gehenna) – eventually becoming the Warlord of Scotland (by which point it would be too politically embarrassing for the Ventrue patron to admit that he knew anything about the character’s true lineage).

                                  Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                  A slightly different angle regarding the Sabbat: Sabbat can be really good at faking being members of other Clans when they're operating in other Sect territory. Having some direct experience with this, even when it isn't strictly necessary, Sabbat scouting/infiltration/subversion packs can benefit by faking what Clans they're really members of just to sow misconceptions about themselves around should suspicion arise and their enemies start to figure out what's going on. The Sabbat having a much higher percentage of members who were Embraced without a lot of careful consideration mean you have a lot of Shovelheads that would in fact be better fits for Clans other than the one they happened to get stuck with; making the act easier to pull off as well.
                                  Another thing about the Sabbat: they're way more willing to teach Disciplines outside the Clan. Packs are often built on inter-Clan groupings, and Packs have both a pragmatic and emotional incentive to teach each other.

                                  Even the "snootier" ones, like the Lasombra and Tzimisce, have a vast group of younger, "lower class" members who don't fall into their Clan's culture. The Tzimisce might be typified by vile nobility, scions of ancient Carpathian traditions. But the most likely Fiend for a vampire to meet on a night-to-night basis is the shovelhead who barely knows, much less appreciates, that rich history. There's a reason many Tzimisce Elders see their Clan as going downhill in recent nights: the Sabbat is full of "wild seeds" that neither know nor care about the Clan's history.

                                  As such, they equally wouldn't care about keeping their Clan's unique Discipline a "trade secret". Why should they? Pass that good shit around, it's a party!

                                  Knowing this, the Sabbat is full of Packs who have the most "mongrel" portfolio of Discipline powers imaginable. Toreador having dots in Potence, Animalism, Dominate, and Chimestry, along with their in-Clan powers. Why? Eh, another Cainite was teaching - possibly in their Pack, maybe a visiting one they shared a Vaulderie with - and it seemed useful/fun.

                                  So few Cainites would think it strange if a supposed member of one Clan manifested powers linked to another. Indeed, the Sabbat would find it odd if a Cainite didn't seem to know anything outside their Clan. (Shovelheads excepted, since they can barely be counted on to know their own native tricks).
