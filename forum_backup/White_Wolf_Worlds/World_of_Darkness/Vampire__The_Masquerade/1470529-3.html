    Originally posted by Thoth View Post
    If you own the hospital or blood bank, the blood is already in your possession. There is nothing to hunt.
    You have no idea of how a hospital actually works, does you?

    Or any business by that matter, because thinking that you can dispose of every single asset of the business as if it was just your personal property is A) frequently illegal, B) always noticeable and C) the easiest way to end up without it for being a terrible manager.

    ​First, there's what Vamps Like Us said in post #43. Basically, your reasoning on how you can set up the entire system in your hospital to execute and cover up the embezzlement of blood is exactly what Herd does here. That's what I was trying to say this entire time: if you only have Resources, you have to describe this scheme, you have to dedicate time and rolls to the scheme, and at any point a new complication to your scheme may appear.

    Without Domain it is specially dangerous because other vampires have no reason to not interfere if they find out, unless again you step out of your way to take precautions, meet the interested parties to negotiate, put in general a lot of personal effort and political capital into making it work, still risking setbacks and deals with unreasonable parties.

    If you buy Herd, it directly represents your scheme and it is already well enough established that no further action is needed for it to be there and be reasonably safe. The ST may put it in the line as a story seed here and there, but generally you're entitled to take it for granted. If you buy Domain then likewise other vampires already recognize it and things are reasonably settled down. Unless some major political setback affects you, your authority over the hospital isn't questioned and the reasons for this may remain mostly as background instead of taking your play time and risk failure on dice rolls.

    Keeping your hospital as a safe personal space and blood source while just buying Resources will cost you a lot and be always at risk. It is a time and effort that most players would prefer to invest somewhere else in the game. The ST may give you some leeway depending on how it impacts or not the game, but you're not entitled to expect that. You should at least expect a roll from time to time to see if some problem comes up that wouldn't if it was properly a Background of that kind in your sheet.

    Not to say that while it is a poor attitude to mess with a player's traits frequently or without good reason on the ST's part, the PC actively putting their traits at risk for a greater gain is another matter entirely. By setting up such a scheme in your hospital without the backing of BGs meant for giving the benefits you're after, you're putting the company and your Resources at risk. A bad series of rolls or excessive abuse of this scheme could perfectly justify the PC ending up with mounting costs, frozen assets, and a series of other setbacks that could effectively harm their Resources rating directly. temporarily or even permanently.

    Because businesses, companies, etc. including hospitals are very complex things to manage and keep lucrative, and while schemes in them certainly exist, they do have risks and costs. While most rich people involved in schemes are probably off the hooks, that's because they do put the effort and capital to make it work, which would be represented in game either by dots in other BGs or by investing play time so to speak. And even then, every year there's a number of rich people all around the world suffering serious setbacks or even facing harsh consequences for it. They're not a majority, but they're there to show you that it isn't a guaranteed thing.

    The greatest reason to not be so secure in such an arrangement, though, is simply fairness with your players. Resources, Domain and Herd are all traits with equal costs, and are there to make for different character concepts. If you make one such character concept objectively stronger than the others by making it access the benefits of the others by default, even if just at high levels, then either your group will be extremely uniform, or there's a big chance that there will be some resentment from the players with the "weaker" concepts, and with reason. This is not about what money can buy, but about what the actual resources the players have, dots, can buy, and that they feel the end result of their expenditure as fair and fun.

                You have no idea of how a hospital actually works, does you?

                Okay so you are going with a personal attack stacked with a bunch of business-related platitudes that are great in theory but hold little to no relevance in real life business. If you are going with useless non-sense could you at least make it relevant?

                You seem to be operating under the concept that an Owner is the same thing as a manager. A common misconception for someone whose business experience is limited to managerial or lower positions. To be fair no one would expect a clerk to understand how the Owners conduct business on a large scale. Part of the reason they have business classes. As an IRL business owner I have to say that those classes are okay for theoretical knowledge, but very poor when it comes to practical application when it comes to business.

                A manager can make business decisions on the day to day activities of said business. Which workers are scheduled for what shifts, who shifts jobs on the fly if a problem arises, etc. But a Manager can't decide to close the business or sell off its assets. At most they can suggest to terminate a worker or resign their own postion.

                An Owner can do all of those things and more. If the business as served its purpose, you can close it and sell it off for profit. You could likewise expand it by investing more into the business or creating branch sites. You can change the direction of the business to suit current trends or choose to hold fast to a traditional business model.

                Owning a hospital is no different than owning a supermarket or running a factory. You own it, you have specialists in your employ, and you decide which direction the business goes in as far as research, development, or product development.

                You are imagining board meetings, committee hearings, public forums, or stock holders meetings. When you have a business model that is based off of single ownership, all of that goes away. Sure you might have manager grade personnel on site, but their function is to enforce you directives, rather than enforce the stock holders directives.

                Though out history there are many examples of very wealthy people owning newspapers, and despite those papers having all the journalists, the editors, and desk chiefs, the owner can say "we will not cover that subject" or this paper will support "this ideology" in its daily news coverage. Which is their right as the owner of the paper.

                Remember that we are talking about WoD here, which is supposed to be our world if only a bit darker, a bit more corrupt, and a bit harsher. So if it is happening IRL then that is a base line for the game setting, it can't by definition exceed it.

                        I wouldn't rule resources 5 as billions (unless we're talking weak currency here) Backgrounds 5+ are available in the WoD, even if they're not talked about much in vampire corebooks.

                        I see the resources background primarily as not what you own, but what you can do with it.



                        Very few vampires work for a living, they might busy themselves with work related projects, they might personally steal quite a bit, but they do these things on a foundation built and managed by others. Vampires either own stuff or they control people who own stuff, and they let their money work for them. Baring a few defects, any neonate who knows how to blood bond can easily join the ranks of the bourgeoisie If one of their proxies ends up in massive debt, it's very easy for a vampire to cut them loose.

                        This point is like... resources 1. Unless we're talking machineguns or cannons or equipping an entire posse the ammunition isn't expensive.


                        With Resources as a background, you can have a house and a mansion as well as customize them to the level you are able to afford to. I treat HAVEN as a background differently because if you buy that, you don't need to have the Resources and you have what I call, "permission to be a goddamned supervillain."
                        Haven shouldn't be a background, unless we're talking chantries with labs and occult libraries and other such facilities.
                        If you want something real cool it'd be like... Resources + Larceny.

                                                    Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                                                    IRL a LOT of blood that goes to a blood bank is wasted due to blood not having a great shelf life and supply/demand being inconsistent. It really probably isn't so hard for a few dozen bags to be written off each month, depending on the size of the bank.
                                                    This is true, and due to a lot of other reasons blood embezzlement is actually a real thing. There are even some people with any of the many conditions that can lead to hematophagy that manage to legally buy blood for consumption with some regularity.

                                                    What would be problems are:

                                                    A) Volume. A human hematophagus consumes a bag of blood a month at most, but a vampire is consuming at least one or two per day (depending on which of two conflicting information in the books you use), probably more to be safe, that's a lot for most banks, and not a small scheme in any case;

                                                    B) It's still not something you can assume you can do just because you own the place. Easy or hard, dangerous or safe, setting up the embezzlement scheme shouldn't be taken for granted if it isn't covered somewhere else, especially in a tightly regulated industry. While there is corruption and all that, the point isn't if it is hard, it never was;

                                                    C) Game concerns. At the end of the day, what's at the center of this is how the matter affects the game, and Resources is still just a BG. It must be balanced against the other BGs. Not for realism, not for mathematical balance, but simply because the players must feel their choices are valid and fair, not system traps.

                                                    Originally posted by Vamps Like Us View Post
                                                    I feel like older editions were better with money merits. Corporate CEO was a background I remember, Mansion was another. I'd have to pull up the old first edition pdf, but I remember there being a bunch of things like this to make resources five be more oomf.
                                                    Older editions gave a hard ceiling of how much you earn and how much you can get by liquidating your assets at each level of Resources, the problem is that this hard number does not translate well everywhere and much less age well. It was a bad idea from the beginning.

                                                    ​But as MyWifeIsScary said, this is covered in other books. To be precise, the best descriptions of several levels of Resources I've seen to date are in M20 (better than the Guide to Technocracy that introduced the idea of 6+ BG). Those guidelines are really good to have a better grasp about what you have at each point, although they need some time reading and digesting (they're good, but could have been better written).

                                                    It runs more or less like that: up to 5 Resources dictates primarily personal life, how much buying power you have to establish lifestyle and acquire things or amenities. So at 5 there's no personal possession you can't afford, be it a vehicle, housing, whatever.

                                                    At 6+ it starts to dictate how much you impact local economy. That's the level where you own key businesses and has enough money for your fortune to directly affect other people in a meaningful scale. A hospital probably wouldn't even be Resources 5 under M20 rules, although a small hospital you have little personal intervention on aside earning profit could be.

                                                    Under those rules, if you have enough money to threat half a hundred people with unemployment and then follow through without any impact on your Resources level because that's just that small a part of your operations, then by all means you have more than 5. And even then, mind you, those rules still don't let you just use your assets to freely emulate other BGs.

                                                        It's funny because maintaining money i.e., Resources is harder than the game makes it out to be. So while you can gain security from investigation by hiding your Resources in different areas than where your Story is taking place, nothing says those other places can't affect your Resources score on their own. Though this also means that causing widespread financial crashes could possibly be an ear mark of inter-kindred combat.

                                                        The sad thing is this debate is a lot like the old debates from Mage: the Ascension about how it didn't take much in the way of xp to get the right spheres of magic to be able to conjure nuclear weapons. The fun of an RPG is to be able to experiment with being able to do things you can't in real life. The problem is that not everyone thinks on the same scale. Tell one player that their character can conjure things and they will do little tricks to augment role play, tell another player the same thing and they will crush nations and redraw the map. I am the sort of ST that is fine with both approaches.
