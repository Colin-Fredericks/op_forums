                    Any reasonable discussion would conclude V20 does disciplines better (I mean you're all entitled to your opinions and all BUT THEY'RE WRONG UNLESS THEY'RE MINE FOOLS!!)

                    1-Disciplines are more natural because they are broader. What does V20 potence do? Adds strength die. Can I use this strength for damage? Yes. Can I use this strength to jump high? Yes. Can I use this strength to get past my enemy's defences? Yes! Can I use this strength to lift things? Yes! Can I use this strength to break through barricades? Yes! Climb well? Absolutely. V5 on the otherhand, we start with two choices; Jump Good, or more dangerous brawl damage... Very specific no? Reminds me of a perk tree for an arcadey RPG. We have some pretty extreme examples too. Celerity 3 for examble; rush your enemy, and only an enemy, to make an attack or rush in any direction but don't make an attack... I suppose the designers liked Mass effect. There are possibilities V5 presents that V20 doesn't, but V20 gives you more power per level, and a fair few V5 powers really stretch the definition of the Discipline.

                    (minor note; the pick'n mix approach of V5 also means I might pick progressions that will seem unnatural, for example I'll want to avoid picking up Dominate 1's "Forget" if I want to take Dominate 3's Forgetful mind to avoid redundancy, even though in a more natural system learning Forget should be a predecessor to Forgetful mind)

                    2- Discipline selection is just more streamlined.
                    In V20, I look at the list of clans, there's a brief summary of what their three disciplines do, and I can make a decision from that alone. There is no 'wrong' path here. If I get Dominate, I'll get every dominate power between 1 and 5 eventually, given enough XP.
                    In V5, I look at the list of clans, I look at the disciplines, which are varied. I then work through all the disciplines working out what the right path for disciplines are for my character, knowing that my selections will bar me from the other powers. And that's before we get into amalgams.

                    3- Brujah are Brujah and Wizards are Wizards.
                    In V5, Potence is as complicated as Presence and only slightly less complicated than Thaumaturgy (Sorry, "Blood Sorcery")
                    In V20, Physical disciplines are simple-as, and Thaumaturgy works best if you're prepared to research like an actual-wizard. The Advantages are manifold. First, somepeople just wanna play a Brujah, A mechanically light character; Potence and Celerity are one power with five levels, easy to memorize, meaning you only gotta worry about Presence. It's very satisfying. Let's say I get 5/5/5 in Brujah disciplines. In V20, I gotta keep 7 distinct powers in mind. In V5, I gotta keep 15 distinct powers in mind. Keep It Simple, Sir. conversely, If I'm playing a Tremere, well, I fully intend to geek out about having UNLIMITED POWER!!!! and a lengthly spell list. That's what playing a wizard is for. Imagine if DnD 6e said Fighters, Rogues and monks were too simple, but Bards, Wizards and Clerics were bullshit, so everyone has to play half-casters? Nobody wins there. Rangers are lame and Paladins are just shit clerics misusing a high charisma value.

                    4 Elder disciplines.
                    This one's somewhat more subjective, but look; individual powers above level 7 are rarely written well, but the concept of there being levels beyond 5 is fascinating. If I were a conniving vampire (Which I am not) I'd devour someone for the opportunity to develop transcendental power. That's what drives the Jyhad. If everyone caps out at 5, and blood potency is mostly just going to make you both really hungry and really picky, there's no reason to eat eachother (killing eachother's fair game but there's much less incentive for that) From a religious Sabbat/Assamite/Settite/Ravnos perspective, eating up's a reason to exist, it's a religious duty. From the Cam/Inconnu/generic elder perspective, it's a reason to get paranoid and supress the neonates hard, because you've got something they want and they'll only get it over your corpse. There's just a wonderful, sociological impact at play, and it's gone in V5.

                            Think this is one of the many areas where V5's intentions might have been good, but the execution is sorely lacking

                            As the CofD and some 20th books have demonstrated, there's a lot of interesting things you can do when you get off strict the 1-5 dots of powers VtM started with Disciplines. Lots of people have been complaining about Discipline bloat in VtM for decades. So V5 trying to adjust this wasn't wrong... just well.... the execution clearly has flaws and a lot of us feel those flaws don't outweigh the benefits.

                            For example, Wr20's dual Arcanoi tracks is a very interesting take on things. While still 1-5 rated powers, there's the common versions that pretty much any wraith can learn, and the Guild versions that requires (at least for modern wraiths) tutelage from someone that knows them; and you can learn both rather than having to pick. One of the major benefits of this is Wraith specific (1e/2e WtO had a weird thing where most of the Guilds were illegal entities but the Arcanoi were also linked to the Guilds, so how could you learn your Arcanoi without becoming a criminal?), but it could also be a model for how to address some of the concerns with V5's setup without completely tossing the benefits.

                            While it wouldn't resolve more specific debates about the utility of certain powers, I'd have been much more pleased if we had something like this:

                            A set of common Disciplines (basically the classic VtM ones, maybe elevate some of the others to this status). Variant Disciplines that recreate most rarer Disciplines as an additional set of powers ala Wr20 but not inherently limited to just two. So, for example Serpentis and Vicissitude could both be Variants of Protean.

                            Amalgams go back to being a separate thing like Combo Disciplines that don't count towards your max number of powers. Some of the really specialized Disciplines can really be reduced to a few of these rather than the bloated 1-5 dot versions in pre-V5, or some of the pretty bland single power Amalgams in V5.

                            Clans can extend favored XP bonuses to either a single Variant, or to buying Amalgams. This means our Gangrel that just favors Protean has greater flexibility in learning unique off-shoot powers, while our Tzimisce get the most out of studying the Vicissitude variant instead. This can help mitigate some of urges to pick rare Clan/Bloodlines because they get cool nifty unique powers, since you're explicitly trading one set of nifty powers for another. The choice is more of flexibility vs. specialization instead.

                            Not perfect mind you, but I just wrote it up right now. Just an idea of ways to play with the setup that could balance different desires for the game more successfully.

                                        Execution is still a huge aspect of this.


                                        Something I've lamented before, is that V5 didn't take more from Beast the Primordial mechanically when it was looking at the CofD for inspirations. Beast does, in fact, combine everything into Atavisms (basically physical powers, though plenty of esoteric things like being able to jump into one shadow and appear in another are in here), and Nightmares (more mental powers where you manipulate emotions, memories, and perceptions.... though you can still kill people with them).

                                        It works because neither are 1-5 powers. Both types of powers are just a big list of powers you can buy individually. How powerful they are is a mix of how powerful your character is, and how hungry your character is.

                                        I am extremely confident that you can take Beast the Primordial, take out all the dream-horror stuff, and instead of DIY monsters turn everything into vampires, and I could make a vampire game that might not be VtM or VtR, but would be still be a solid vampire game that would appeal to Vampire fans. Mostly because it's a matter of trimming things out rather than needing to add much.

                                        For a new edition of a game though, there's a case to be conservative with changes. You need to be sure that if you're going to make a pretty big change, the pay off of a better play experience for a wide range of fans. So for V5.5 or V6 or whatever, I wouldn't do my pared down Beast idea no matter how good of a vampire game I think it'd be. But that doesn't mean there shouldn't be some experimentation to see if there are better models than the original 1-5 Disciplines.

                                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                            I honestly have always felt that Disciplines should be more flexible like Mage magic. Sadly, V5 only does it halfway. If I were to revised V5 it would be to DOUBLE DOWN on this and add this change:

                                            1. You can buy as many powers for a level of Discipline or Combo Discipline as you want.
                                            I've done this in my group and found it to work out fantastic. I don't know why V5 banned taking multiple powers, but having multiple ones per level was one of the new edition's best ideas.

                                            I did find things to get slightly out of control at first. Everyone wanted to buy as many powers as they could, and usually higher-level ones.

                                            So I tweaked buying multiple powers at the same level to have a cumulative XP cost. I found that made things much more balanced, as it incentivizes filling out your powers at each level. I've also, as I mentioned earlier in this thread, waived or reduced Rouse costs for powers with a lower level than a character's Blood Potency. So players have been very happy to continue buying and using weaker powers.

                                            Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.
