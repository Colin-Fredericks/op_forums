            It doesn't matter whether or not a character has had a tragedy in its past. If being changed into a vampire and thrust into the cruel world of the kindred wasn't enough of a bad event, I'm sure that the character will suffer from some terrible event soon after play begins. Sometimes a character who has always lead a carefree or privileged life, who then encounters terrible hardships, can create a more dramatic story than a character who is hardened to misfortune. I recently watched a movie that I quite enjoyed as a child - Empire of the Sun - an autobiographic story about a wealthy spoiled British boy living in pre-WWII China who gets separated from his parents and ends up spending WWII in a Japanese internment camp. It was a very powerful movie, especially seeing the hardships of the camp through the eyes of a boy who had only previously known comfort and luxury.

            The most important thing about playing a character and dealing with bad circumstances is that you don't want to "no sell" the negative things that your character encounters. If the character suffers the loss of a loved one, show that character's sadness. If a character encounters one of their worse fears, role play that character's fear. When the character should suffer, use that suffering as a role playing aid to display the emotional depth of that character and the magnitude of the situation. What hardships the character may have faced before they became a PC is less important than how they deal with hardships that they are experiencing along side of their player.

                                    There's no "requirement" on having had bad things happen, and when I'm running a game I generally don't mandate players put certain things into their backstory (unless it's a particular theme for a campaign like, "Why are you all here together?") but that said I do ask players to include plot hooks for me and having bad things happen in a past allows for more hooks than having had a wonderful perfect past.                                             When you play vampire, it pays to be instigators. You should all have wants

                                            -I want control over this company
                                            -I want this place to be recognized as my domain
                                            -I want to host elysiums
                                            -I want a huge herd
                                            -I want this person on my side
                                            -I want to learn blood magic (and not get caught in a Tremere trap)
                                            -I want to avenge an injustice I felt as a mortal (The so called "list of kids who bullied you in highschool" is rather fun)
                                            -I want to fulfill my sire's wish of removing his rival.
                                            -I want to build up defences because my sire's rivals may come after me.

                                            You should try to work everything your ST throws at you into fulfilling your ambitions, and you should try to achieve them yourself when he isn't throwing anything pressing at you.
