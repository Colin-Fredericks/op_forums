    [V5] I dislike Touchstones as written and am considering a house rule

    06-06-2022, 04:54 PM
    I know that some of you also had their problems with the way Humanity, Convictions, and Touchstones work in V5, so I come to you with this issue in the hope of getting some advice.

    In general, I like the system well enough. (It's not as good as VtR 2e, but still...) My biggest problem is that Touchstones don't really bring an advantage to the table; actually, they are only liabilities for the PCs, since the damaging or the loss of Touchstones can cause Stains. In fact, they are only relevant because they are loosely tied to Convictions mechanically. So, neither do they bring a narrative advantage to the gameplay, nor do they give the PCs any mechanical benefits.

    (I also think that the Stain-reducing effect of Convictions isn't really a big draw, since it's just as likely that Convictions will force a PC to behave in a disadvantageous way. But this is neither here nor there.)

    As a consequence, I feel the optimal approach to character generation is to take the absolute minimum of Touchstones and thus Convictions... which is kinda sad, to be honest, since I like the general idea that Kindred have to be anchored to humanity in some way, that they need regular reminders of what it meant to be human.

    Anyway, since we haven't actually played V5 yet and are a bit cautious about installing too many house rules before testing the system, I was thinking about a minimally invasive house rule to solve my dissatisfaction with Touchstones:


    "If a PC has engaged with a Touchstone in a meaningful way, then the PC may add 2 dice to their Remorse roll at the end of the session." (This engagement doesn't mean that the PC and the Touchstone must interact, but the player should describe in some detail how the Touchstone helps the PC feel remorseful and more alive.)


    The idea is to actually use Touchstones as roleplaying opportunities for players that then result in a clear mechanical benefit. Think of Touchstones as a way for getting Remorse stunt dice (to borrow from Exalted for a second). What do you think about this house rule?

      I think the idea of Touchstones is interesting, but doesn't actually amount to much in the vast, vast majority of games. In the vast majority of games, the ST is trying to tell a story that is part fancy party mean girl socializing, part detective work, part mafia coverup, and part kicking ass with your cool vampire superpowers. He or she doesn't really have time to do much with Touchstones, and even the creative ones don't generally have the creativity to do much with the 12 touchstones of 4 players.

      Touchstones have, consequently, become one of those ideas that are almost irrelevant. People fill them out that part of the sheet, and then promptly forget all about it after getting engrossed in the mean girl socializing, detective work, mafia coverups, and fighting that characterize the vast majority of games.

      I don't know if a simple mechanical change would alter that dynamic, because it's a dynamic rooted in the fact that ST's are not professional writers, and players are not professional improve actors. There is no mechanic that's going to change that.

              If you're starting with CofD 2e style ones? Not really.

              In the CofD 2e Touchstones are, generally, positive things to have, even if like many external positive assets you can be harmed indirectly through them. The writing and rules treat Touchstones as things vampires want to have, and makes them worth fighting for. Purposefully wanting to off your own Touchstones makes no sense in a VtR 2e context.

              V5 is written much more from the perspective that Touchstones are liability that's only worth it if you struggle with the Chronicle Tenets. The corebook literal gives killing your own Touchstone because you're tried of the hassle as an example of the mechanics for how Touchstone damage/loss turns into Stains.

              ------------

              I think there's two things to do with Touchstones in V5:

              1) Detach Touchstones from specific Convictions, make Touchstones easier replace, and make them less restrictive on what they can be. Yes that's not one "thing" but it's part of a overall effort to make them easier to integrate into the story because it's easier to make ones that are relevant to the kind of story you want to play. Non-human Touchstones are esp. useful for creating relevant ones that don't get in the way of a lot of playstyles.

              2) Increase their role in Willpower damage recovery (they do have this in VtR 2e and V5, but it's frequently overlooked because it's not a huge bonus in V5). This is can easily be more useful to than the bonus to Remorse rolls (but that's not bad to have too), because V5 uses Willpower damage, a lot, in certain parts of the system (particularly social conflicts).

                  The irony is that Touchstones get far more use in Sabbat games than in Camarilla games. In a Sabbat games, Touchstones are these hugely valuable soft targets. Grab a Touchstone, add it to a vozhd, and you've caused Cammies to get sloppy militarily and likely made them frenzy in whatever location they first heard about it, and often caused them to move closer to becoming wights. And the Touchstones themselves aren't generally in on the masquerade, which greatly limits how well they can be defended. This makes them an excellent way to damage Cammy elders who are otherwise heavily protected. Slowly building Touchy the Touchstone-Vozhd can be the focus of months of satisfying gameplay.

                  But in a Cammy game?
                  ST-"The footsteps of the dreaded Vozhd shake the earth! Covering its flesh like huge boils or pimples are the heads of dozens of humans, each crying and their mouths screaming silently as they no longer have lungs. Your heart sinks into your stomach as you realize one of the heads is...BOB SMITH!"

                  Player-"Who?"

                  ST-"Bob Smith, the hairdresser you made one of your Touchstones"

                  Player- *gets a puzzled expression as they haven't looked at that part of their sheet since they made it four months ago* "OH! OH RIGHT! Yeah....okay, I'm like, 'oh no, poor Bob'"

                  Okay, I'm being a little hyperbolic, but not by much. There's such long stretches of the game being about fancy parties, detective work, and fighting, that long stretches go by with only minimal contact with their Touchstones, to the point that Touchstones are very much an afterthought. The game simply isn't about Touchstones, yet their function suggests it should be.

                              I think Touchstones are meant to be a narrative tool to help roleplayers who are new to the concept of Vampire: The Masquerade and incentivize human connections.

                              HOW I WOULD EXPLAIN TOUCHSTONES

                              "Your vampire does not have 100% only contact with his fellow vampires. Somewhere, somehow, you have at least one human being that has some emotional value to you. It can be your daughter, your mother, your girlfriend, or some guy that you hate SO MUCH you remember what it's like to be human. These connections are something that makes your vampire feel alive from. The Player, not the ST, should develop them and they should feel like someone that they are invested in roleplaying with. Even if it's just, 'My player character pays for Cindy's college because he's not a deadbeat dad even after death.' Conversely, the ST shouldn't make them a target right out the gate like Lois Lane to Superman. The best use of a Touchstone is not to kill them or threaten them but let them be used as props for the PC to act against in scenes that they want to play out."

                              I actually recommend the ST encourage the player to play both Touchstone AND vampire.

                                          I find that touchstones add a lot during character creation because it makes the player think about how the character would interact with other persons, and is created less into a void.

                                          How relevant they are in the story depends on what kind of game you run, if it’s constant troupe troubleshooting with 4-6 players, then they are on the back lines. If it is less about chasing a story and more about night to night existence, or just fewer players, I feel they get to the forefront. Not all of them, some are better written or simply fit better.

                                          These are also characters who may have life skills, and just like they can make unlife interesting for the Kindred, the reverse is also true. Probably somewhere between contacts and Allie’s, and you get them for free - albeit with a heavy cost if something goes wrong.

                                          A bonus on remorse rolls if spending time roleplaying with a Touchstone sounds like a good thing. But I would vary the bonus depending on how good the scene turns out, possibly and/or what kind of sacrifice the Kindred makes.

                                          I totally agree that using them as constant damsel in distress quickly removes any attachment and makes them a liability instead.

                                          I did play around with an idea for touchstones after reasing VTR, but for Revised. I wanted three types, a vulnerable one, easily harmed. This could be a person or a location that the vampire attached to. A more solid foundation, such as another Kindred, a style/subculture that was harder to affect. And finally something like a conviction, a city, a historic figure that was basically impossible to shift.
                                          The more exposed the touchstone was, the more powerful impact (don’t remember by what, lost notes) it had, allowing player to create their own risk and attachments.
                                          I think V5 handles changing Touchstones from a living person just fine. I see why they wanted it like that, but it is a very modular system by design and is in my opinion one of the easiest system to house rule in.
