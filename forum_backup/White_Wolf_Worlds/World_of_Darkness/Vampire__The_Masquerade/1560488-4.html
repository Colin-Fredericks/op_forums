                                        Originally posted by JagneStormskull View Post

                                        To be the nitpickiest, Lilith is mentioned in the Hebrew Bible (Isaiah, IIRC), although most translators translate the mention to her as "the night-owl," "owl-demon," or "night-demon," rather than keeping the Hebrew intact. And the Lilith being part of the Eden narrative is late ancient, not medieval.
                                        OK, since I need to break this out every once and awhile:

                                        I have four years of training in Biblical Hebrew. This is something that happens a lot when people that don't know Hebrew make claims about what's in the texts. "Lilith" as a proper noun never appears in the Hebrew Bible, the lines in Isaiah (34:13-15) is a list of animalistic plagues that befall Edom. In the Masoretic text lilit is presented in the singular, but in the Dead Sea Scrolls its in the plural (in line with the wild cats, and jackals presented in both versions). This context is why no serious scholars of the text consider this a reference to Lilith as a character, and thus the translation is rendered as either a nocturnal bird (to keep with everything else in the list being an animal), or a type of night demon (in keeping with the messy etymology of the root L-L-T in the early Semitic languages and a possible conflation with a type of Babylonian spirit with a similar phonetic structure). There's nothing in the base structure of the noun (esp. with the Dead Sea Scroll findings), it's context within the wider verse, or the context of the larger verses around it to indicate that the word is meant to be a proper noun name of a specific character "Lilith," and is instead meant to be part of a list of creatures that render Edom uninhabitable to human life. Whatever you want to translate lilit as, it has to make sense as a group of otherwise normal animals that have come to feast on the carrion and gore of the slaughter of the Edomites and their livestock.

                                        The Wisdom of Sirach - the book from antiquity - does not contain Lilith in any of the preserved traditions or manuscripts. Though the text is primarily preserved in Greek translation and the Greek translations of Isaiah didn't keep lilit either and that passage is kinda of a jumble of minor Greek supernatural creatures, and we only have fragments of Hebrew versions of the text. So it is, in theory, possible the original text in Hebrew did use L-L-T in some way, but we have nothing to indicate that's the case. The Alphabet of Ben Sirach is a medieval text that is attributed to the son of the guy the wrote the text from antiquity but there's not evidence of this being a true connection. That's where we see the early evidence of the Lilith of Eden.

                                        So, no, nitpick not accepted.
