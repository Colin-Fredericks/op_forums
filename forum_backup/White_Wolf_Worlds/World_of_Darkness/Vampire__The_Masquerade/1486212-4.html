                And it's not that the Inner Circle have no power; they do have power, just not absolute power that needn't answer to no one.                         Originally posted by The Traditions
                        First Tradition: The Masquerade
                        Thou shall not reveal thy true nature to those not of the Blood.
                        Doing such shall renounce thy claims of Blood.

                        Second Tradition: The Domain
                        Thy domain is thine own concern.
                        All others owe thee respect while in it.
                        None may challenge thy word while in thy domain.

                        Third Tradition: The Progeny
                        Thou shall only Sire another with the permission of thine Elder.
                        If thou createst another without thine Elder's leave, both thou and thy Progeny shall be slain.

                        Fourth Tradition: The Accounting
                        Those thou create are thine own children.
                        Until thy Progeny shall be Released, thou shall command them in all things.
                        Their sins are thine to endure.

                        Fifth Tradition: The Hospitality
                        Honor one another's domain.
                        When thou comest to a foreign city, thou shall present thyself to the one who ruleth there.
                        Without the word of acceptance, thou art nothing.

                        Sixth Tradition: The Destruction
                        Thou art forbidden to destroy another of thy kind.
                        The right of destruction belongeth only to thine Elder.
                        Only the Eldest among thee shall call the Blood Hunt.
                        Those are the basic laws of the Camarilla, the cornerstone upon which the sect's foundation lays, and the guiding body of laws that guides all of the Camarilla's policies, politics and behavior.

                        To blatantly disregard those is to breed instability and possible dissolution to the sect - and that's NOT something that the Inner Circle - the most cunning and apt members of the Camarilla, as well as the best connected ones - would willingly do.

                        Of importance for the present discussion is the Second and Fifth Traditions, that respectivelly claim:

                        "Second Tradition: The Domain
                        Thy domain is thine own concern.
                        All others owe thee respect while in it.
                        NONE MAY CHALLENGE THY WORD WHILE IN THY DOMAIN."

                        And the Fifth:

                        "Fifth Tradition: The Hospitality
                        Honor one another's domain.
                        When thou comest to a foreign city, thou shall present thyself to the one who ruleth there.
                        WITHOUT THE WORD OF ACCEPTANCE, THOU ART NOTHING."



                        "The Traditions form the legal backbone of Camarilla society, although in modern nights, the Prince fulfills the Elder role outlined in the traditions. Most vampiric culture springs from the Traditions – presentation is outlined in the Tradition of Hospitality, the Blood Hunt in the Tradition of Destruction and the parceling of land in the Tradition of Domain."
                        - From the White Wolf Wiki.

                        This means that any Camarilla vampire can VISIT any Camarilla city - as long as they fulfill the tradition of presentation - but they can only STAY with the permission of the owner of the place.

                        Guys, the Tradition of Domain is the second tradition! Down there bellow the Masquerade itself! It is the single most important tradition besides the masquerade!

                        And the letter of the fifth tradition, the tradition of hospitality is enfatic into reinforcing this: "Without the word of acceptance, thou art nothing."

                        The reason why cities became more cosmopolitan is political - not a thuggish imposition of Justicars. It comes from political agreements between Elders (Princes+Primogens) of different locations. And again, it's often better to make some concessions than facing some of the consequences, but again, no Kindred community (represented by their Primogen and/or Prince) are ever FORCED to accept foreigners, under any circumstances.

                        This means that ALL acts of "kindred immigration" are ALWAYS political acts.

                                                            Maybe. Although I disagree.

                                                            Ethics is a complex thing. And unless you consider the almost entirety of Humanity as monsters, we're not as better than a vampire would have to be than it seems at first. We cause a lot of impact on the planet and on other humans even when just attending to our basic needs. We are predators ourselves, just with the ability to elaborate complex belief and logistic systems to put on a facade of not needing death and suffering to survive and thrive. Yet we can be ethical about it. We can acknowledge our impact and do something to lessen it, we can question our needs and strive to better ourselves, we can find ways to detect and reduce the suffering we cause even if eliminating it isn't feasible.

                                                            Vampires are a blight upon the world because that's a game convention and a trope. And it is accepted because most people either find the trope appealing or just can't stand something that does to us what we do to others (including other human beings we just chose to pretend they don't exist as long as we don't specifically know about them). A vampire that is mindful of its own needs, but also the need to lessen the impact of their fulfillment, is just as ethical as a human can be.                                                             On the matter of following particular religions, it is a striking misunderstanding of non-Christian religions. A lot of the things Christians think to be common threads among religions are no such a thing. Islam in particular not only can have inhuman creatures that prey upon Humanity in some form among the Faithful. It does. There are spiritual beings in Islamic Faith that may be harmful to humans and still are every bit as Faithful as one can be, and in fact expressing the Faith is considered a protection because it shows the entity you're a fellow Muslim and protected under the law. Neither Islam nor Judaism have the notion of Damnation, eternal or otherwise, that Christianity has (and that came from reinterpretations on Plato, not from the Middle East). Almost no religion has it, actually.

                                                            The go-to pagan religion for Christians to show that others are just like them is "Norse Polytheism", because it's easy to show as having the same themes in some form. But you know why? Because we only have Christian texts on Norse Mythology. It was not a written religion, and most of our knowledge of it comes from the Eddas, two books written already two centuries within official complete Christianization of Iceland, based upon fragmentary stories passed down mostly by already Christian storytellers, and put together in a reconstruction effort by Christian authors. The first and most complete, by the way, by a poet, historian and politician whose main goals were the construction of a mythic origin for the Scandinavian royal families and the establishment of a book that would be very didactic on the characteristics of the hardest traditional poetic styles of Iceland.
