                      Resources in VtM is both very powerful and useless at the same time, with the deciding factor being how well both the Players and the ST understand how money actually works, rather than just copying what they have seen in movies or on social media. Resources functions a lot like poker chips in that even if you don't have the cards to win the hand, you can still either buy the pot or bluff by using your chips as psychological leverage. Alternatively, you could just consider Money to be Blue cards in a Magic the Gathering game.

                      But here is a big catch, the instant you go across a boarder, your Resources level changes with the exchange rate and access to banks. A person can live quite comfortably in say Thailand on about $1,000 a month. Over there that is Resources 3-4, but in the US making $10,000 a year is considered poverty level to the point that you barely pay any taxes, so it would abstractly round out to about Resources 0-1.

                      I think the real problem is that most people don't think about how their characters money works. When I play a high Resources character, I tend to flush out a lot of financial backstories and contingency plans. Meaning that if you have Resources 5, you own multiple businesses, each of which has its own perks, you own multiple havens, you have caches setup to deal with emergencies, you have isolated revenue streams so that if any one business gets targeted it won't affect the rest of your infrastructure. Now when you put that kind of thought into Resources, it is OP as hell, but if you are in the middle of wilderness with Lupines chasing you, a roll of hundreds is only good for tinder.

                      Though to be fair there was one game where the PC coterie used Resources to leverage a cease fire of sorts with some local shifters. They bought the bank which had the debts of all the local kinfolk and their mortgages. In exchange for forgiving the debts and pouring money into the community, the vampires were allowed to coexist in the area as long as they behaved. It also helped that if the vampires got killed off, legal clauses would mean the entire area would be rezoned and bulldozed.

                                  I agree with this. I think STs have problems with high Resources when they are not being creative enough. Or perhaps more fairly, I should say people are unfamiliar enough with finance to know what its limitations are. People have a tendency to think fairly one dimensionally. If a person surrounds himself with bodyguards, then it means I cannot run a plot to attempt to kill the character via X so that means I cannot run any plot. But instead that should just mean the antagonists have to address the issue from another angle. STs and Players often want to run "street level" style games, and in these cases having a Resources 5 PC can eliminate that style of play. But all that means is that the PC is not appropriate for the chronicle, not that there is nothing the ST can do to challenge the player. It's just that the style of game that is good for that kind of PC is often unattractive to all the other PCs, and it becomes hard to run the game. In these cases, the gaming group needs to have an OOC discussion on how best to handle this so everyone is having fun.

                                  And as you say, having lots of money means you are more noticeable and potentially might attract other enemies, or provide another opportunity for your enemies to hurt you in another way.

                                  I know current rules sets are much more nebulous, but originally Resources 5 may have made you a millionaire in net worth, but the actual extra cash to do something per month was something like $30k. in the nineties. That's still only around $40k nowadays. That's enough money to help your character grease some palms, buy a used car in cash to use as a getaway vehicle for the coterie, hire a lawyer to get a coterie mate out of jail, and other things. It is not something that allows you to ignore the world.

                                  At an extra $40k a month, that's not quite half a million dollars you have for the entire year if you spend it on personal protection. But all it does it get you into the world of Hollywood Celebrity bodyguard protection. It keeps out the riffraff, paparazzi, and stalkers from getting too close to you. It's not the kind of protection that allows you to prevent Assamites killing you or withstand a Sabbat siege. To simulate that level of protection, it's about the same as a Retainer and some Allies. And by having that kind of protection, you pretty much eliminate all the free cash that Resources provides.

                                  Resources 5 was never about Bruce Wayne and Tony Stark level wealth. It was that of a successful local businessman who now has passive wealth. Of course, if your ST allows you can have access to 6+ Backgrounds that will increase that and eventually get to those levels. But all that means is the nature of the game changes at that point.

                                          The thing about private security teams is, unless you're prepared to Dominate and/or Blood Bond them, they ironically represent a security risk as far as the Masquerade is concerned.

                                          They know your comings and goings. If you take them out on the town, do you just send them away while you're feeding? What if they accidently see? Disciplines, pay-offs, and Ghouling can mitigate the immediate threat of your bodyguards finding out you're a vampire. But they still know, and that means they could be compromised, one way or another. The more private security you have Ghouled, the bigger the drain on your own Vitae every month. You need to keep track of them all, and not neglect any of them. Ghouls are also...volatile, let's say, due to the influence of the Vitae in their system and their addiction.

                                          Obviously, older vampires deal with this as a matter of course. The benefits of having tons of Ghouled security outweigh the costs for them. But those ARE still issues, that younger Kindred (or other parties) can exploit.

                                          On another note, moving around with a team of bodyguards draws attention. If you've got a stable Herd or have other means of bringing vessels to you, great. But if not, everyone is going to know when the local rich VIP who only ever comes out at night is on the town. They'll remember if they go necking some customer at a nightclub, especially if they end up dead accidently. A Kindred's ability to be anonymous goes down proportional to the number of dudes in suits and ear-pieces follow them around.

                                          Keep in mind those bodyguards must also be minimally outfitted, unless you're going on a raid of some kind. You can't take your boys into a nightclub strapped with full military gear. (Unless you're in one of those states with open carry of assault weapons. In which case that advantage goes both ways. Be prepared for your enemies to walk around similarly armed). Point is, if your bodyguards only have concealed handguns, their utility isn't as good as it could be.

                                          A Haven with strong private security, unless it's out in the woods, is basically impossible to hide. Everyone will know a rich VIP lives there. It might make hunters think twice about attacking it, but it also means they know where you'll be, and can follow you elsewhere. You can't take an entire household's worth of mercenaries with you every time you want to go on adventures. And you couldn't leave your Haven undefended even if you could. Only part of your security detail goes with you, which means the full measure of your Resources doesn't translate to protection when you need to leave your house.
