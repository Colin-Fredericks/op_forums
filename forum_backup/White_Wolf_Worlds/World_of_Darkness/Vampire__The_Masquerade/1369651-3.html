        Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
        I did like Kindred of the East since it helped to alleviate the problems that arose from setting up Vampire as based in Christian mythology. "Oh, your Native American myths or African myths were just misunderstood monsters of Christian origin because Christian mythology is right and everyone else's mythology is wrong." Having something that was part of the Vampire setting, but that was firmly unrelated to anything resembling Christian mythology was really nice.
        There is a common claim in fandom that Vampire: the Masquerade is based upon a Judeo-Christian foundation myth which is portrayed, in the books, as inherently true. This is not actually the case; if one reads the books carefully, the "objective" history of vampires only goes back to the Antediluvians. The Second City, the second generation, and the existence of Caine only exist in vampire mythology, and there is nothing published to support their factual reality. These aspects of the background may exist in the game world ... or they may be the inventions of the third generation to hide the true origin of vampires.

        (This is, of course, entirely separate from books about other aspects of the World of Darkness, like Devil's Due, which are written with the inherent assumption that the Judeo-Christian origin myths are actually true. These are not generally very good books.)

        Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
        If they do decide to just get rid of the Wan Kuei and replace them with regular old run-of-the-mill vampires, I just really, really hope they don't get Kindred of the Ebony Kingdom treatment with the whole, "wink wink, nudge nudge, these vampires are just regular vampires in silly hats and you can see how they can all be traces back to the Antediluvians." Introduce completely different clans with completely different weakness and completely different abilities so we're not going, "Oh, I see which of the thirteen Clans this group was originally part of."
        The approach taken by Kindred of the Ebony Kingdom is arguably the least problematic and offensive way of dealing with African vampires. One significant real-world issue with how Africa is portrayed in popular media is the idea that the people of Africa are intrinsically different in some unspecified way from the rest of humanity. Yet, African history is tied incredibly closely to that of Europe and western Asia, with a huge amount of trade, conflict, and cultural interchange. It is important in writing about Africa and African history (and the history part is unavoidable when dealing with immortal vampires) to stress the continuity and commonalites between Africa and the surrounding world. Exoticism tends to appeal to people who like the idea of the "dark continent", a place of mystery and threatening otherness, though I'm not entirely sure that it's the best way to deal with Africa. Rather like some of the criticisms of Kindred of the East on this thread...
