                    Originally posted by blailton View Post
                    ​Spirit's Touch is only strong if you ST go full fluffy and ignore system rules. It need five successes before getting really useful information, with high difficulty if its not an object closely linked to the last owner.
                    You're mistaking overwhelmingly broken with useful. If Spirit's Touch could give all the good information by grabbing anything the subject touched, it would be downright broken.

                    It is on you to choose the object you're analyzing, so if you're trying it on random stuff it is indeed likely that you'll have consistently high dif and a bunch of useless information. But if you're careful with the object you want to use, then you're likely to have a reasonable or low dif and a context for whatever glimpse of information you get.

                    Now, what may be true is that not all STs know how to handle Spirit's Touch. It's RAW can be read in many ways, so the ST may choose to simply be stingy with it, always erring on the side of high dif and choosing the most shitty information possible. I do like the V5 version in which it lets the player choose the information beforehand, but the perfect rendition to me is VtR 2nd ed's, where each success lets the user ask a question from a list to the ST.

                    An ST that isn't a jerk, though, should know to measure the value of the information and err on the middle ground dif at least, 6 or 7.

                        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                        Actually I disagree that even the first levels of Auspex are less potent.

                        Information is a big advantage in any situation, and that's the first big thing Auspex gives you, the inherent versatility of information.

                        Even at level 1 your ability at catching clues, hearing things, etc. increases and that's immensely useful, always. At level 2 you get to read auras, and that is far more useful on a variety of situations than reading minds, as you can screen several people with ease, for many bits of relevant information.

                        The only power truly situational is the level 3 Spirit Touch, and even then it makes for a great way to get information through indirect sources.


                        Knowing is already an advantage, especially when you can use it both to fulfill desires or to threat impeding them.

                        On the Ministry, their thing is precisely less about knowing and more about getting things. They're brokers, they don't need to know what you want, you'll tell them yourself under the promise that they can deliver, no questions asked.


                        While I agree with Heavy Arms that CofD's take on it already addressed many of the issues (not only with Resources, by the way), As someone that works with economy and finances irl, the idea of breaking Resources that way does appeal to me. I'm not sure if it would be an interesting thing or not, but at least merits some attention.

                        I really like this view, because it made me realize an important point about some of the clans that seem like... they should be put away or get dunked, because they aren't dominating or martial. It kind of reminds me of CK3 too.

                        But to be simple let's just use the Toreador example (and yeah Celerity can be an excellent Discipline to evade the martial Clans), for they surround themselves by those who like or are allured by them (Presence), and maybe everybody wonders if it is just the social charms or the Presence that make the Toreador so hard to hate, even when they are little bastards.

                        Most importantly, Auspex... in a game where everyone has dirt, everyone, and if they don't have they have other weakness, retainers, touchstones.... To know this is to hold people in your hand. Will somebody try to attack your stuff if they know (or fear) that you know a good reason why the Prince or Sheriff should come knocking on their haven? Just the fear of the Auspex using Clans must be real, especially for the paranoid vamps.

                        Malks, Toreadors, Hecata, Tremere.... they seem so many times protected from the threats of other Clans, especially the first two (Hecata and Tremere have their own history, twists, diableries and etc., but not entirely negligible). They must have very specific dirt on people (not as much as Nosferatus of course), and if they don't they don't dig dirt, they are great bodyguards and threat assessment agents, and seekers too. All of this make them dear or untouchable in big webs of politics, which is more predominant or clear in the Camarilla.

                        And yeah, Auspex users can be great brokers and "cutters" (threatening or cutting off their desires as well, which I never thought about till now). Salubri are the Auspex users that got the less out of their discipline, given the situation of the Clan and its members, except if you believe "it was their plan all along".

                        Strange... When coincidence seems too convenient, I prefer to call it fate.
