    Why Shouldn't you Blood Bind / Dominate? [Revised / V20]

    06-11-2025, 09:14 PM
    Been running VtM for a few years now, and something that got asked a lot was questions like, "Why shouldn't I just try and blood bind the Prince?" or "I could Dominate the Sheriff..." Essentially using blood bonds, Dominate, and Presence to put every major player under your control. And outside of the Vampire Storytellers Handbook Revised explaining that Kindred find such a practice as lazy, there's not a lot of rational to explain why such practices are bad ideas. Or more specifically, it's not very well explained why these will fail.

    Yes, getting caught trying to Blood Bind the Prince, obviously is going to end very very bad for you if caught. But Presence is extremely subtle and can work on lower generation Kindred. Blood Binding, after three drinks, is near permanent. And Dominate has the capability to rewrite memories. I guess I'm just curious why more younger Kindred don't try these tactics, and what tools and reasons do the Kindred in Elysium have to prevent being victims to magical manipulation.

          The Blood Bond is extremely obvious. The Prince stops acting like himself and becomes completely obsessed with some neonate. Other vampires in the city will very quickly figure out what's going on. Maybe they'll kill the neonate, or they'll decide they don't need to obey the Prince, or maybe someone will move to overthrow the Prince, the Anarchs will start acting up. Whatever the case, a Prince who's obviously Blood Bound to a neonate won't retain the respect of other vampires in the city and won't maintain power for long.

          Dominate is fair game. There are likely a number of "puppet Princes" in various domains who are secretly under the control of one (or more) other vampires. The problem with Dominate is that it doesn't work very well (if at all) on vampires who are of lower generation, and it's likely that the Prince will be of lower generation than a neonate.

          Presence can be subtle, or it can be vulgar. As long as a vampire uses Presence subtly they can get away with it. But if a vampire starts tossing it around willy nilly, other vampires won't want to meet with him or her in person. They'll request to work through intermediaries like ghouls or such to pass on messages.

              I don't think there's any problem with PCs (or NPCs) trying to do any of those things. It's just the potential for failure should be high, and the penalty for failure will result in death, blood hunt, becoming a fugitive, or being subjected to a punishment like them being blood bound themselves to the person they attempted to target. (I'm making a distinction between a tactical use of powers like Dominate and Presence against a vampire versus long term or permanent control over an elder vampire). Elders just won't tolerate this.

              As for Elysium, most STs I know are very clear that ANY use of such powers against another vampire is violation of the rules of Elysium with all the punishment accorded to it.

              However, I think a problem for many STs is that while the NPCs being targeted should be very experienced at detecting such things and being able to avoid them, the STs themselves do not have a mastery of the rules, setting, and game mastering to reflect that. Players will be much more likely to pull one over on the ST than their PCs should be able to do so on certain NPCs. The ST is just too inexperienced to know how to handle the situation. (There's similar problems in other roleplaying games especially when PCs get to the point they can cast powerful magic spells which seldom get used. Many GMs just don't know what to do in response to a "game breaking" event because they don't have much experience with such play, and since there are almost always multiple players and only one GM, the players tend to outthink the GM.)

              A 300 year old elder is going to know someone is attempting to use Presence (even a "subtle" version of it) than a neonate would. Likewise for any successful attempts at Dominate. Why? Because they already would have experienced such things many times when they were younger. They already know the signs that they are being affected, or that someone they know is affected. Mechanically this may just be an excuse for the NPC to spend Willpower every time such things happen. In other situations it may mean that vampire's mentors, friends, or peers notice something is off and quickly figures out why. (Sometimes the ST will need to stack the deck if they think the PCs plans won't work, but if you do this let the players know why you are doing it and the limits you are enforcing OOC. Such times of making things fail automatically need to be restricted and the boundaries known in advance.)

              For the Blood Bond, it may be harder depending on how your play group handles it. I think as written, even the merest hint of some vampire's blood counts as one bond level. In practice, I found this means many stupid attempts by PCs to secretly blood bond others (whether mortals or vampires). In response, I just said that to actually get a true level of the bond, the amount of blood must be noticeable. You just can't blood bond someone without it being obvious.

              Another issue is that ambitious neonates (or whomever) trying to put all major players under their control will almost certainly be caught before they are successful, and they've just made enemies of EVERYONE in the domain. Not just the Kindred they were targeting. Everyone will know that they would lose in such a scenario and thus would want to retaliate against such Kindred as well. The perpetrators will have no friends.

              One option is that such plans will fail because more powerful Kindred have already done this, and neonates won't be successful because they won't be rolling against their targets but the stats of the Kindred really pulling the strings. For example, in the classic Chicago By Night setting, almost all the major players are in fact secretly controlled by either Helena or Menele. The moment one notices this young Kindred doing it (thus stealing their control over this valuable pawn), they are going to be quickly destroyed. Try to blood bond Tyler when she's already bound to Helena? That Kindred never wakes up from their day slumber because Helena's three thousand old ghoul burned down her haven the next day. Try to dominate that vampire who's already dominated by Menele? It fails because Menele's will just overpowers theirs, and the pawn still obeys Menele's much less noticeable power. Not all chronicles should have such powerful methusaleh vampires dictating things, but some level of layering the Jyhad is acceptable and not all vampires active in the city will be known by neonates.

                                  It's not a direct answer but I think this is an area where the VTES card game provides an excellent example of how vampiric influence and control can be represented. Excuse the explanation if you're already familiar with the game.

                                  In VTES the player can be thought of as representing a methuselah or other very stron behind the scenes power, play consists of recruiting/taking control of vampires by spending 'blood' and then using those vampires to foil the plans of the other players/methuselahs though various means. Vampires that can be recruited include Princes, Justicars, and other high ranks.

                                  One interesting point is that there are limited opportunities to suborn other players vamps, which I'm assuming is meant to reflect that such opportunites should similarly be limited in the rpg even though the players have more agency.

                                  As such I think it's a great game for STs to have a go at as it can provide good inspiration for their stories.

                                      I will add to what has already been said that a simple Empathy roll is all it takes to take a good guess at someone's intentions, no supernatural powers required. Most humans would not imagine Dominate, Presence or the Blood Bond, but for vampires these are normal facts of life by the time they are allowed to leave their Sire and be somewhat independent. Elders have greater experience, more reason to be suspicious, and a helathy (erm...) tendency to paranoia when it comes to these things.

                                      Similarly, Subterfuge rolls work to detect schemes, so depending on the circumstances, normal Attribute + Ability rolls can be enough to foil certain attempts, especially against elders with large dice pools which represent their greater experience.

                                      And I fully second what was said about even elder vampires being involved and pulling the strings, and things potentially failing because they need to be rolled against their bigger dice pools.

                                          So, something I was thinking about a bit more on this:

                                          In order to put yourself in the position of a younger vampires and why the common sense attitude of "don't try it," is so effective, it could be useful to think about our natural inclination to accept the "if something's too good to be true..." line of thinking.

                                          To an extent, the very fact that it seems like it could be so easy to just walk up and mind-control politically important vampires but vampires society isn't just a constant war of blood-bonds, Dominate, and Presence, would naturally ping our social instincts as there being some sort of unseen layer we're not aware of.... even if there wasn't really one. If we're presented with a situation where it seems both, "it's simple and almost anyone could do it," and, "almost nobody is even thinking about doing it," are true, our natural inclination is to assume the majority not trying it know something we don't and not trying is the better option (at least in the short term). Learning if they're right or just unwilling to buck a social norm will take time and investigation; likely with a lot of investment into local social structures for people to trust you with information instead of turning you in for obviously plotting to do the "bad" thing nobody thinks should be done.

                                          Even if all of that uncovers it's just a clever social rouse and you really could mind-whammy your way into local power without any significantly impressive powers of your own, you're then left with a dilemma of what getting that power would accomplish if you undermine the vary social order you're seeking to take the reins of. You, after all, don't want to be the victim of the next vampire that comes along with the same idea, so you need to protect yourself until you're strong enough to not be subjected to the same tactics you used.

                                              Disclaimer only my own perspective, ignore it if you disagree:
                                              Ignoring the logistics; there are tradeoffs to each of these abilities when making pawns.

                                              The Blood bond makes the character obsessive about you. You blood bonded the Prince. You turned potentially the most powerful person in the city into your stalker. Unlike when you do this to a mortal you are not the one in control of this relationship.

                                              Dominate strips personality away from the target. How much is up to the st. It's very good for guards. It's less good for major players if you want them to remain as such. You turned a leader into a mindless yes-man.

                                              Presence is like the blood bond except less predictable. How the target reacts to the emotions you force into him is up to his nature. Kindred are monstrous beasts prone to bouts of berserk frenzy. It's dangerous.

                                              - Ignoring the tradeoffs and no longer ignoring the logistics.

                                              It's noticeable. When you do it, and when others see the shift in the targets behaviour to anyone famimiar with the powers.

                                              These powers are much less effective against Kindred than mortals.

                                              Kindred punishments include things like hanging your flayed self upside down in Elysium for several weeks. Or months. That's the light sentence. Kindred justice is there to provide a reason not to. The punished will be an example to others.

                                              These people have security. The guards will shoot you. A lot. Stab you. Stake you. So on.

                                              These people have supporters, many who will be unknown. They will not like you coopting their ally, pawn, servant, or absolutely worst case their *friend*.

                                              - so how do you use these powers against other kindred?

                                              On occasion. When it's sufficently important. As a calculated risk. With a backup plan. And you do it to nudge things, not control them. Pulling off a presence against the prince at the right time, while he's distracted is the thumb on the scale to shift everything.

                                              Using it as a leash is what you do to kine.

                                              That's also the final reason. Your kindred are your peers. Don't treat them as cattle. Then they won't have to treat you like a mad dog. A lot of etiquette in all the sects revolve around not having kindred rip each other to pieces. Screwing with that is unwise.

                                              Not saying it can't or won't happen.

                                                      For Presence in particular is important to note that anyone can try to resist, anyone at all.

                                                      While that doesn't mean resisting is easy, it does mean that trying isn't something special, and isn't even stated as something you can only do if you know what kind of power it is.

                                                      Dealing with out-of-place emotions isn't completely strange for anyone, and Presence doesn't stop you from noticing that following on your emotions isn't in your best interest.

                                                      All that together points to a harsh truth: Presence is not a subtle power at all. Even "subtle" uses aren't really that subtle, specially considering how overpowering those artificial emotions are.

                                                      If you use Presence, other vampires will know. It isn't an IF, except in quite unique circumstances. Even if your victim doesn't resist, they know just what you did, and they will remember.
