    Ancient Setite metaplot thread

    05-01-2024, 10:55 AM
    Hello there,

    I'm opening this thread to welcome suggestions to retcon the Followers of Set's metaplot to explain the origins and deeds of the two bloodlines introduced in VDA20 and practically extinct by 1244/V20: Witches of Echidna (thought extinct by 1244 but resurfacing among the Anarchs in V20/V5) and Warriors of Glycon (who went extinct at an unspecified point in time between 1244 and V20).

    The lore about them is basically entirely found in VDA20 core manual and VDA20 Tome of Secrets, and it seems like the Witches of Echidna were well respected among the Mediterranean clans in the ancient world as priestesses, seers, spiritual or religious advisors, mystics and sorceresses. They gradually declined with the rise of Christianity first and the rise of the Tremere later.
    The Warriors of Glycon seem to have been the leaders and rulers of the clan until the priest bloodline got their own warriors and plotted to wrestle control of the clan away from them, which they succeeded at between 1244 and V20.
    The clan also has a much more international stock and presence in VDA20 than in previous editions: only the priests seem to have strong ties to Egypt (and more likely Hellenistic Egypt than previous eras): the Witches are mentioned as centered in Greece, but found more or less anywhere (even France and Germany and further North, or in Costantinople and surrounding areas), while the Warriors of Glycon are mentioned as being in North Africa, but Glycon hitorically was a deity originating from Macedonian snake-cults and a city in today's Turkey. Echidna, Typhon and Glycon all have strong historical and mythological links to Greece, Asia Minor and Macedonia, and this portion of Greek myths is often aptly compared to Mesopotamian myths around Tiamat and other snake-themed monsters or deities and early battles between gods and monsters or titans, as if the Greeks were influenced by these older myths early on in their religious history.

    This is, more or less, the data, and more can be found in the manuals. I'm eager to hear your ideas to flesh out more the early history of the clan and these bloodlines. I'm currently playing a French Witch of Echidna, after having played two Warriors of Glycon.

                      There has been so many retcons and changes to the Setites over the years that it's hard to reconcile everything. The Setites were established with some very specific lore that got in the way of viewing them as just another clan, so Revised made a lot of changes to them and V5 did even more. A lot of the uniqueness of the Setites were thus lost. So you'll need to decide what to do when trying to incorporate canon into your own ideas because canon can be contradictory. I tend to default to the earlier canon myself when these things come up.

                      If you go by the original description of the clan, it's important to note that around 900 BC, Set was defeated in Egypt, driven into exile, and his followers slaughtered. At that point, Set reviewed what had gone wrong and decided to make changes. The Followers of Set, as a clan with a culture and organization that we recognize today, is a result of that. In other words, the Setites were different prior to 900 BC. They were probably more like other clans, and the temple/cell based structure or corrupting things behind the scenes was adopted. It isn't clear whether all other Setites were destroyed by this time, though I think it's implied to be the case. It is also unclear whether all the other Setites were destroyed by Set's enemies, or whether Set "cleaned house" by eliminating any remaining Setites that he didn't find up to snuff. I imagine there could have been some Setites who survived the attacks by their enemies and were not purged by Set.

                      So if I was going to include any Setite bloodlines that existed during the Dark Ages and/or today, I would only have them be created after 900 BC. Another option would be to link one or both bloodlines to the previous generation of Setites before 900 BC. If so, that bloodline might be seen as heretical since Set did not want them to survive. They represent the previous mistakes that caused his earlier failure. That would explain why that bloodline(s) later went extinct "again" - the other Setites wiped them out since they are not part of Set's master plan. Obviously, using this option would create a very different dynamic for characters who are of this bloodline. It would also explain why this bloodline is now part of the Anarchs rather than aligned with the actual Clan.

                      Another thing I would probably do is trace any new characters of such bloodlines to a single survivor recently awakened from torpor. The bloodlines did seem to go extinct, but since all it takes is one survivor to create new ones, it's easy to come back. A single character in long term torpor who woke up is an easy explanation of what happened between then and now. Put your creative energy into making that single survivor interesting and how that character is viewed and interacted with by its relatively few descendants.

                      Personally, I have never liked the idea of sub-clans - those bloodlines that are members of the actual clan but had different in-clan Disciplines or different clan weaknesses. I just make them all mechanically the same. It's not like vampires cannot learn out-of-clan Disciplines. And I hold it as a rule that very often certain Flaws consistently show up in certain lineages, so I can even cover different weaknesses as well (but in addition to the classic clan weakness, not as a substitute for it). But I often retain the flavor text for specific lineages of vampires, or certain groups within it.

                                  So in my games I tend to use the idea of Typhon-Set. That is, Set was embraced by the vampire Typhon. Eventually Set diablarized Typhon. Typhon's soul and Set struggled for supremacy for thousands of years, and sometimes Typhon would end up in charge, sometimes Set. Depending on who was in charge at a specific point in time, they would create a new bloodline, or wipe out the followers of the other one. Eventually Set manages to become ascendent and wipes out the Witches, which were loyal to the Typhonic aspect.

                                  Then for the Witches of Echidna specifically, I have them as something of a Bahari cult. In mythology Echidna, the Mother of Monsters, was Typhon's wife. And there's a surprising amount of overlap between the mythology of Echidna and Lilith, especially in terms of giving birth to powerful monsters. Set claims to be the first vampire, but if you were to consider Typhon the first vampire, then he would be analogous to Caine and his wife Echidna would be analogous with Lilith. Maybe the Witches were a proto-Bahari cult, or maybe they were influenced by the Bahari. Either way in my games the Witches are closely allied with the Bahari, to the point that outsiders often can't tell the difference. Ultimately a handful of Witches managed to survive extermination by hiding among the Bahari.

                                  I tend to run the Witches niche as seers, cursers and monster makers. I very much have them play into the classical "wicked witch" archetype, pulling from stories like MacBeth or Beowulf (Grendel's Mother). Mechanically one of their unique tricks is a line of Thaumaturgy and Rituals that allow them to birth monsters or turn beings into monsters. They are closely allied with Bane spirits, and can manifest them across the Gauntlet or twist creatures into Fomori. Their most powerful Ritual allows them to give birth to a being that's essentially a Revenant/Fomori.
