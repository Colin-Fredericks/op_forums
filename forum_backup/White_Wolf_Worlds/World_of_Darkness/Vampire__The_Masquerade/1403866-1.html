    Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
    I believe lupines, in lupus form, are going to be very good with running you down. Low profile, fast, improved senses, supiriority to normal wolves in this form, and any gifts they might have; I wouldn't take that shot unless I had a full squad with me waiting in ambush. Shoot and leave is not a good option with lupines, especially considering their tracking capabilities. (That cross country combo sure looks good though).
    Yes and no.

    The low profile and fast movement only matters if you are shooting at them when they are active, a sniper by definition waits for the circumstances to be right before taking the shot. Meaning the lupine would be stationary, not on guard, and the shape doesn't matter given the precise nature of the shot and the small sizes of targets in training.

    As for improved senses, once again as long as you are down wind and a mile off, they won't smell or hear you. The first clue they will have that you exist is the targets head gaining a new hole. For a couple of minutes everyone is diving for and staying in cover. Next they try to figure out which way the sniper is shooting from, but since no further shots are forth coming, they will scamper from cover to cover out of realistic concern that they could be directly exposing themselves to follow up shots, this can eat up quite a lot of time and hampers their inherent movement benefits.

    How exactly would they track you? Vampires exceed human abilities and the world record for a human sniper shot is 3,871 yards or over two miles away. We have been discussing a shot at only one mile distance. For one mile between the sniper and the target there is nothing to track. At best they could guess that since they can't smell your scent or the gun powder on the wind even with gifts, they can only say you are not directly up wind, since you could be up wind but a mile off to the side. So the wolves would have to search in a grid pattern for almost a mile in every direction and only once they find your sniper blind will they begin to be able to track you, but by then you will have been gone for hours. Especially since the lupines will be searching while trying to keep in cover, which slows things to a crawl.

    As for the gifts themselves, Obfuscate does counter scent, hence why nosferatu can live in the sewers and not be noticed when invisible. Sure they might be able to find your scent and pick up the smell of gunpowder and gun oil, but that will just lead them along your exit path to where ever your get away vehicle or extraction point is, but that doesn't help since as previously stated they are a few hours too late. This becomes even more problematic if the sniper was trained in escape and evasion techniques like running through water or spreading a box of chili powder all over their sniper hide to spoil the scent and mess with the lupines noses.

    To be fair though I will admit that this style of fighting is not for every game, but in truth when you have to fight, you fight to win, not to fight fair.

            MyWifeIsScary My first question to you would be - what is the purpose of Quietus to you? As a designer. Mechanically and narratively, what is Quietus meant to be?

            There are two obvious answers, assuming we don't want to redo Quietus too much. The first is that Quietus is the assassination discipline. The second is that Quietus is a blood manipulation discipline.

            For an assassination discipline, we are keeping Silence of Death and keep designing in this area. An off the top of my head sketch:
            1. Blessing of Elil, Lore of the Clans Vizier version of Silence of Death - you project a sphere of silence but can choose to exclude things from it. It's magical silence so objects that left the sphere are still silent (such as bullets).
            2. Ishtar's Touch. Bite a target (without taking any of their blood), spend a blood point, roll Stamina + Intimidation. For the rest of the scene you gain +Successes in dice to your mundane social checks against the target. This is your "Solid Snake holds the target at gunpoint and questions them for intel" power.
            3. Utu's Lock. Rub some blood on an object, the next person to touch it takes damage. Now by level 3 we have a solid "assassin" package that lets a chargen PC be an enjoyable Tactical Espionage Action protagonist.
            4. Nergal's Call. Aka Dagon's Call, but with an assyrian name. Untraceable remote attack. Fine as is.
            5. Ashur's Road. Spend a blood point to transform into a stream of blood for a turn, along with all your equipment. This can be done to pass under a door, or as a defence action in combat to avoid an attack.

            For a blood manipulation discipline. I don't have cool names, you'd have to figure out your own. I would also use this to replace the Tremere Path of Blood, because it's kinda not great:
            1. Haemomancy. Touch blood, roll Willpower, learn one fact per success related to the target's health, physical condition or character.
            2. Scarlet Shepherd. Vampire learns the direction and general distance of blood sources sympathetically connected to a sample. Vampire themselves is connected to their herd, their ghouls and any vitae they have spilled recently.
            3. Theft of Vitae. Theft of Vitae.
            4. Haemothurgy. Spend a blood point to control blood in unnatural ways, such as moving it or forming combat tentacles (like Arms of Abyss). The blood needs to be already present, so if it's not - you'll have to bleed yourself some more.
            5. Puppetmaster. Make a roll, control target's actions for a turn per success. The target doesn't know they are being controlled, but unless you are performing very minor actions they will realize it's not just some accidental hand twitching. This can be used to force vampires to activate disciplines they know (and this can be subtle, such as activating Dominate on a sentence that vampire is saying of free will). This is very spooky to other vampires and cause for great distrust..

                It's been a while since I discussed this, but my interpretation of Disciplines in general is that they aren't immutable laws of supernatural reality, that only work because that's the only way they could ever work.

                Disciplines are a set of powers discovered and refined by their inventors - usually by the first three Generations - meant for particular purposes. Their inventors wanted something out of the powers, and the sets of them we encounter are the ones that got used so often as to become ingrained in the Blood itself.

                (Obviously, I'm talking V20 and earlier here. With V5, this goes right out the window.)

                This is why, say, Serpentis is a Discipline built around becoming more snakey. Set really liked snakes, and so most of the early Serpentis powers are designed to facilitate this. However, Set also wanted to be worshiped as a god, which is why later powers began picking up abilities that facilitate looking like a god, or having storm powers. It's a toolset that formed for Set's purposes, and changed as the needs of Setites changed. (This is why in DAV20, you had Serpentis 5 being used to produce monsters, and Serpentis 4 created a more dramatic "war form". Meanwhile, by modern day, Serpentis 4 merely turned you into a big snake (because it's more subtle than a person with a snake lower half or a typhonic beast), and Serpentis 5 made what was once a Ritual into a standard power known even by non-blood sorcerers).


                For Quietus, I'm much more inclined to go with "Quietus is the assassination Discipline", rather than "Quietus is the blood manipulation Discipline". If it's a constructed powerset, then its theme would need to be less fundamental, and more about what Haqim (or his Warrior followers) needed it for. In this case, Assassination. That its most basic power lets you sneak better has nothing to do with whether it's blood-related, and everything to do with "sound canceling" just being really useful for an assassin to have. To the point that it was developed in the same skillset as other assassination powers, even when Assamites had access to Obfuscate, a more dedicated "stealth" Discipline. It was THAT important to have in the "Quietus package".

                Hell, most vampires would appreciate being able to move silently.


                To reiterate, this is just my interpretation of how Disciplines work. If your vision for what they are is different, we may never see eye to eye on this.

                    Originally posted by Draconis View Post
                    Random musings:

                    So, my biggest issues with Quietus have always been that the first power has no thematic connection to the others (as mentioned), and that the powers feel…redundant. Turn your blood into poison, turn your blood into better poison, turn your blood into more versatile poison…

                    Some other clan-specific disciplines have the same issues (like Vicissitude, which is three powers that all do the same thing to different extents, and two unrelated Protean powers). And I'm not sure it's something that can be entirely fixed within V20's paradigm. Maybe a good model would be Chimerstry, which does a good job of making the enhancements feel meaningful, imo: each one feels like a significant upgrade over Ignis Fatuus in and of itself, but comes with a significant cost that means you won't want to use it every time. But &quot;poisonous blood&quot; is a very narrow space to work within.

                    Maybe borrow some ideas from the Tremere's Path of Blood? &quot;Manipulate properties of vitae&quot; is a larger thematic space to explore than &quot;your blood is poison&quot;. Take some of the elder abilities, like &quot;inflict the drawbacks of high Generation on someone&quot;, and move them down into the easily-accessible 1-5 progression; mix them with variants on some of the Tremere abilities, like stealing vitae from a distance, which could be an enhancement to Dagon's Call. And after all, the Banu Haqim were following the Path of Blood for thousands of years before the Tremere showed up. (That last one is a joke, if it wasn't clear.)
                    I rewrote most of the base Disciplines for an online v20 game set in Vegas that I plan on running soon, and this is basically the exact route I took with Quietus.

                    Here are my 1-5. Some of them are adjusted elder-level or v20DA powers, some of them were lifted from other homebrews that I found online, and some were my own creation. P.S: this may seem a little OP to some of you, but the vast majority of Disciplines in my game have trended towards being buffed rather than nerfed, so it is fairly balanced in comparison:

                    Level 1 - Scent of Vitae
                    Eons ago, the Banu Haqim served as both the judges and executioners of the earliest Cainite societies, and even the Clan’s neonates were capable of hunting down anyone who dared to break the Laws of Caine. At its most basic levels, Quietus allows a vampire to identify the source of Cainite vitae by scent, gleaning information about the bleeder’s Clan, age, and Generation. This power also allows the user to determine if a person is a Cainite, ghoul, or mortal.
                    System: The user spends a blood point and for the remainder of the scene, she may identify the source of any amount of blood by scent by making a Perception + Awareness roll. This can be used on a pool of blood to identify if any Cainites contributed to it, on a stranger to determine if that person is a ghoul, mortal, or Cainite, and on a Cainite’s blood to determine their age, Generation, and Clan.
                    Note: this power does not increase the user’s sense of smell, per se, it simply allows her to identify the unique scent of Cainite vitae. Any blood samples or targets must be close enough to sniff, and the user cannot track someone like a bloodhound without the use of Protean.

                    None/Botch: The power fails
                    1 Success: The user can determine if a small blood sample is composed of vampiric vitae, in part or in whole.
                    2 Successes: The user can determine if a person is a mortal, ghoul, or Cainite with a sniff, and will know if she has smelled that person’s blood before.
                    3 Successes: The user can determine the age of a Cainite whose blood is sampled.
                    4 Successes: The user can determine the Clan of a Cainite whose blood is sampled.
                    5+ Successes: The user can determine the Generation of the Cainite whose blood is sampled, and possibly other information as determined by the number of successes and the Storyteller.


                    Level 2 - Mark of Guilt
                    Using this power, the vampire marks a target both with her blood and with the judgment of Caine. This can take the form of a bloody handprint from an enemy combatant, or a subtle smear from the midst of a crowd. For as long as the victim is marked with the user’s blood, he is unable to spend blood points, as his vitae is continually being used up in the form of a grisly blood-sweat.
                    Originally used by the Banu Haqim of antiquity when performing interrogations and dealing with Cainite prisoners; the loss of blood and rising hunger of the target, while useful as a bargaining chip, was mostly secondary to the benefit of blocking the use of vampiric Disciplines. In the modern nights, a number of other useful applications have arisen. For example: ensuring that a business deal is performed without the use of any mind-altering Disciplines, or humiliating a social rival in the city’s Elysium by making them ruin their outfit while tempting Frenzy.
                    System: The user first spends a blood point and applies it to her target through physical contact, which can require a Dexterity + Brawl roll, like a normal attack. For the remainder of the scene, or until the target removes the mark, he will sweat out his blood at a rate equal to his own Generational maximum, or 3 blood points per turn if used on a mortal. This counts as a vampire’s blood spent for that turn, effectively locking the target out of their blood-activated Disciplines, vampiric healing abilities, and Attribute augmentation. This power lasts until the target is able to remove the mark, which can be done with a single action. If used outside of combat, the victim must succeed at a Perception + Awareness roll (difficulty 7) to notice the mark.
                    Blood lost through this process is considered dead and inert, providing no sustenance to a vampire. It could, however, inspire frenzy in hungry Cainites. While this power isn’t necessarily a Masquerade breach in and of itself, its effects are quite dramatic and will likely cause a panic in an area filled with mortals. Obviously, if used on a mortal, Mark of Guilt can be fatal, depending on how long they are affected.

                    Level 3 - Dagon’s Call
                    With the slightest scratch delivering a dab of blood to mingle with that of the target’s, Dagon’s Call twists a victim’s vitae against him. The target’s eyes swell and take a deep burgundy hue, muscles seize and veins bulge beneath the skin, and thin trickles of vibrant scarlet bleed into tiny rivers from all openings in the anatomy. This nightmarish affliction persists until the target collapses in agony or death throes, leaving no trace of the attacker’s presence.
                    System: In order to use this power, the character’s blood must first be ingested or enter the target’s bloodstream, usually through drinking (even from a ghoul belonging to the character) or being pierced by a bloodied weapon. The character waits a minimum of one hour for her blood to be fully absorbed, entering the circulatory system to securely grip the victim’s internal organs.
                    After a moment’s concentration, the vampire bursts her target’s blood vessels, internally constricting the target’s whole body by flooding it with ruptured hemoglobin that strangles him from within. To activate this power, the player spends one Willpower point and rolls Perception + Awareness (difficulty equal up to the Storyteller, depending on the vampire’s current proximity to the target or the amount of time elapsed since she touched the target, whichever is greater). Failure on this roll means that the Willpower point is wasted, and the target must be exposed to the user’s blood again.
                    The player and target make contested Stamina rolls (difficulty of each roll equal to the opponent’s Willpower). The target suffers levels of unsoakable lethal damage equal to the number of successes on the player’s Willpower roll minus successes on the target’s Willpower roll. To continue rending her opponent from within, the player may spend additional Willpower each turn after the first, accompanied by further contested Stamina rolls, until she is defeated on a roll. A botched roll results in the vampire suffering an amount of lethal damage equal to her failures on the roll.

                    Level 4 - Baal’s Caress
                    The ultimate use of blood as a weapon (short of diablerie itself), Baal’s Caress allows a Kindred to transmute her blood into a virulent ichor that destroys any living or undead flesh it touches. In nights of yore, when Assamites led the charges of Saracen legions, the Assassins were often seen licking their blades, slicing open their tongues and lubricating their weapons with this foul secretion. Baal’s Caress may be used to augment any bladed weapon; everything from poisoned knives and swords to tainted fingernails and claws has been reported. Additionally, the Cainite may spit their corrosive blood at distant targets or vomit up a torrent of corrosive bile upon their victims
                    System: Baal’s Caress does not increase the damage done by a given weapon, but that weapon inflicts aggravated damage rather than whatever kind of damage it would normally deal. No roll is necessary to activate this power, but the vampire must coat their weapon in a number of blood points up to their Generational maximum. This may be done over multiple turns, adding the blood points to the weapon’s total each time. One blood point is consumed per hit. For example, if a Cainite poisons his knife with 3 blood points and strikes his opponent, one blood point’s worth of lubrication disappears, leaving enough for two more attacks. For this reason, many vampires choose to coat their weapons with a significant quantity of blood before they enter combat. If the vampire misses, no tainted blood is consumed.
                    A vampire may also bite her tongue or lip to spit a single point of envenomed blood at a lone target, or regurgitate up to her maximum amount of blood point expenditure per turn to hit multiple targets. The Cainite may spit or vomit her envenomed vitae up to three feet (one meter) for each dot of Strength and Potence she possesses. Spitting at a lone target requires a successful Dexterity + Athletics roll (difficulty 6) to hit, and vomit may be sprayed to hit multiple targets at the Storyteller’s discretion. Spewed blood contacting the victim’s skin causes two levels of Aggravated damage per blood point spent. If the blood is ingested, this damage is unsoakable, even with Fortitude.

                    Level 5 - Thinning of the Blood
                    Much as its title suggests, this level of mastery of Quietus allows its user to temporarily raise the Generation of a Cainite by focusing her will and touching her target’s skin. The target immediately feels the power of the Blood of Caine slip away and is effectively reduced to the status of a thin-blooded vampire. More effort is required to activate his Disciplines, his vitae cannot sustain others, and his will can be more easily crushed by vampires who possess more powerful blood.
                    Of course, this power can be useful in combat, as the target must spend far more blood to achieve formerly simple vampiric tasks, but it also prevents a vampire from sustaining his ghouls or siring childer. While the effects of this power do wear off after a number of nights, intelligently timed or repeated applications can wreak havoc on a well-established vampire’s sphere of influence. Previously enthralled subjects may rebel, desert, or even hunt their former masters during the day as their blood bonds fade to reveal resentment and hatred.
                    System: The user must spend a Willpower point, make a Willpower roll (at a difficulty equal to the target’s Temporary Willpower,) and touch her target’s skin. After contact, all of the target’s blood point costs are doubled (e.g., using blood-related Disciplines or healing damage,) although he still only loses one blood upon rising in the evening. Furthermore, he is unable to create or maintain a blood bond, cannot sire new vampires, and is treated as a 14th Generation vampire for any Generation-dependent effects.
                    These effects last a number of nights equal to double the number of successes that the user scored in the initial activation of this power. No successes means that the effect only lasts until sunrise. On a botch, the user is affected instead, also lasting until sunrise.

                        Here are my 1-5. Some of them are adjusted elder-level or v20DA powers, some of them were lifted from other homebrews that I found online, and some were my own creation. P.S: this may seem a little OP to some of you, but the vast majority of Disciplines in my game have trended towards being buffed rather than nerfed, so it is fairly balanced in comparison:
                        Level 1 - Scent of Vitae
                        Level 2 - Mark of Guilt
                        Level 3 - Dagon’s Call

                        I like. Potent, yes, but a good source of ideas, even if one wants to tweak the exact mechanics. I like Mark of Guilt and Thinning of the Blood, particularly.

                        Inspired by Mark of Guilt, a couple of ideas;

                        A) Sanguine Exchange - You touch your enemy and draw forth a number of blood points immediately that are transmuted and can be used to activate disciplines, temporarily raise your physical attributes, or heal injury (even if such a use would normally require a separate action). Any not immediately used are not lost by the target, so it's in your best interest if you wish to weaken someone more fully to burn as much blood as you can activating Celerity, healing wounds and enhancing all of your physical attributes as high as you can. You can draw forth 1 BP / success on the roll, and this number is not limited to the amount of blood you can spend per turn based on your Generation (and, indeed, you may also have spent blood normally up to your /turn limit, before using this ability). Because the blood is immediately transmuted and 'burned,' and not absorbed into your body, there is no risk of Blood Bond, or suffering a similar risk (such as from a bloodborne contagion) from this power's use.

                        B) Bound by Scarlet - You link your vitae to another with a touch, for weal or woe. You can spend your own vitae to heal their wounds, or enhance their physical attributes, or activate Disciplines such as Celerity, so long as they remain within ten paces. The effect lasts for the scene, unless you choose to end it earlier. You can also interfere with their own use of their Vitae, if they are a vampire. You spend 1 Blood point per turn you wish to 'block' them, and they have to spend 1 Willpower to access their blood for the turn, or else be blocked entirely from spending blood.
