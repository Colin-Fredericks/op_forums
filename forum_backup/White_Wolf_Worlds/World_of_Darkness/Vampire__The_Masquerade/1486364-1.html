                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                        Depends on how you define Independents. Are they Clan based Independents, Autarkis who move from city to city like Beckett, or people who just want to keep their head down?
                        I mean kindred who want their community (usually meaning the city) to be outside the sects, regardless of how it's run. I think that's what Heavy Arms is describing. This could include:
                        • Medieval princes, and their successors, who rejected the Camarilla when it was created.
                        • Medieval princes, and their successors, who rejected the Ashirra when it was created. I note these guys separately, because it would have started earlier and Playing the Sabbat implied that Tzimisce in Anatolia and North Africa did this.
                        • Other princes who rejected any sect when they reached their part of the world, usually in Africa and Asia.
                        • Kindred of clans traditionally excluded from the sects, who control their own domain, usually Giovanni or Ministry.
                        • Kindred who settled in places beyond the grasp of sect authorities and maintained local autonomy (e.g. the Pioneers and I think some places in 2nd Edition/Revised South America).
                        • Kindred who overthrew sect authorities in their own domain and don't keep affiliation to any global sect. This includes:
                          • Anarchs who distrust organisation at larger scales.
                          • Kindred who approve of Camarilla (or even Sabbat) traditions, but not oversight from justicars or archons (or cardinals etc.)
                          • Princes who want to be sovereigns and subjects who think that benefits them for some reason.
                        • Kindred who are so isolated by circumstance rather than choice that the sect to which they nominally belong can't stretch far enough to intervene there and doesn't know what they're doing.
                        • Imitations of VtR cities. I've been trying to work out the San Francisco Mission and Swansea's traditionally Longinian, clan divided cloister, but they don't make much sense with VtM's history.

                                        Despite that in his specific case he wasn't expected to succeed, it would be just a bonus if he did, LaCroix probably represents well the main Camarilla tactic against Anarchs: politics, not arms. This will always be their default, war is a last resort. Instead, carve out a niche in the city where you can put someone to act as Prince and let them sway public opinion with time. Maybe you can bribe a local to come to your side and act this part.

                                        As long as you have the resources and skill to provide more safety and stability than the local powers, the city will fall in line in time. You are immortal, no need to rush things. If you can't surpass your enemies politically, then even trying to conquer this city is probably a bad move anyway, going to hurt more than it's worth.

                                        The Camarilla would only go to open war if the city is too strategically important in a global scale, or too belligerent for the usual strategy and there are Camarilla vampires with personal interest in taking power there by force for their own sake. That may happen if you have a nearby Camarilla city that's too crowded and a number of ambitious vampires frustrated with low prospects at local power.

                                        But those would be rare situations. Most of their wars are either defensive or preemptive against targets that really will attack if left unchecked until they amass enough resources. Even such wars are hard enough to organize in the Cam. And I doubt they have enough Archons at any given time to have one in every big Camarilla city. How many Archons does a Justicar controls regularly? Most of the time I doubt their total number reaches 100 world wide, while the number of cities with more than a million inhabitants exceeds 500, so the Cam could have easily up to 200 under them.

                                                Originally posted by Gimmisky View Post

                                                All sects have those. The Anarchs just seem to rely on plot armor much more than the Camarilla and the Sabbat.
                                                What I mean in that post is that the Anarchs could rely less on plot armor because they have some very impressive members to justify how they handled certain things.

                                                One of the problems though, is that the books tend to present Elder Anarchs as disillusioned. They recognize that if they exercise they personal power they have, the end up becoming that which they hate (Smiling Jack and Nines both say as much in Bloodlines); they're not in it to kick out the old kings and take crowns for themselves. They want a different way of doing things.

                                                That's why this thread was titled as a setting change, not just a discussion of what's already in the books. All the higher powered Anarch NPCs in the books would make more sense because they'd have more purpose being part of a movement creating change, than as Elders moping about how they don't become Baron because that's just another name for Prince.
