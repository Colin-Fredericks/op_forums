                                Theres a few products on Storytellers Vault ( Analects, Ghost Families, Wicker Wars etc.) that do a much better job of setting up Hungry Ghost history and background than the corebook does and they are well worth picking up as alternatives if you haven't already completely reworked things to remove all the " Anime and Shaw Brothers " that passes for such in the corebook.

                                I know I've already gone over this a dozen times ( perhaps more?) with Mr Phipps but looking at post #263 literally nothing I've ever posted has made any impression at all.

                                Obviously there are also hundreds of Cainites throughout Asia itself and have been for thousands of years spreading through South East Asia( Vietnam/Laos/Cambodia/Malaysia/Thailand/Indonesia/Phillipines) first with the Hindu kingdoms ( mostly Ravnos and some others) and empires out from India then with the Muslim expansion ( Most Cainite Clans) at later dates and then with the Colonial Powers. Before this there were undoubtedly scattered individual Gangrel and Nosferatu. In northern asia ( Mongolia/Korea/Japan) the Anda, Nagaraja, Nosferatu and Niktuku have existed since ancient times, many more came with western ventures and fleeing Baba Yaga both in ancient times and more recently. South Asia ( Bangladesh/Sri Lanka/India/Nepal/Pakistan) has always been a melting pot of Cainite and Hungry Ghost societies. In fact the only place in Asia absolutely without Cainites is Central China simply because it is the centre of Quincunx power. But as said I've already posted this dozens of times....

                                I could mention the numerous Indonesian, Indigenous, Pinoy and other groups I've run campaigns for who were glad to play beings from their own or neighbouring folklore because they were kind of over the Hollywood Viral Vampire that Cainites represent.....not that they didn't play them as well they were just considered a little silly next to homegrown horrors. And yes we made a lot of fun of the KotE corebook and its overwhelming US filtered Sinoism but theres an awesome game in there.

                                Of course over time we arrived at making Hungry Ghosts a world wide phenomenon in slow retreat from the ever spreading horde of Cainites spreading out exponentially from Africa some 20,000 years ago to the Levant then the Middle East, South Asia and Europe and then to the rest of the world. And had some great campaigns dealing with NA, Polynesian and Australian Hungry Ghosts.

                                            Yep. The common defense for just splattering Cainites everywhere with no context throughout the world is that the "Cain myth was always unreliable"; as if we didn't have multiple VTM supplement books, the Gehenna Chronicles, and D:TF all but confirming Caine as the 1st Vampire™ already. The Judeo-"Christian" (I put that in parentheses because lately actual Jewish people nowadays dispute this framework) is the norm surrounding the Kindred, has been since 1st Edition so you can't scramble from that. They literally have Generation, in which makes you weaker with each new Embrace with how far removed you are from Caine. Don't get me WRONG, I love oWoD, but one of the things right CofD/VTR did was decoupling a central plotpoint character from Vampires; and NOT making them explicitly center around a biblical myth only.

                                            To remove/retcon the Wan Kuei is to assert that the belief systems and/or religions that definitely predated Judaism, Christianity, and Islam (in chronological order of emergence) were wrong or the aforementioned three is the right way. The Wan Kuei weren't perfect at ALL, but atleast White Wolf tried; even as feeble as it was in not trying to lamcast this orientalist facet many critiques of KotE see in it.

                                            You say that KotE isn't an accurate depiction of the morality of the population centers they're involved? Fair. But atleast they are something that is atleast half-way accurate or atleast DOES reflect the beliefs of the populations they're involved with. Abrahamic religions however before colonialism definitely weren't native reflections of their morality at all.

                                            What KotE really would need is the current maintainers/writers looking at the inaccuracies of the past and merely just sprucing it up (and this can certainly be done with extreme advances to the internet not really available in the 90s, so there can't be any excuses), as to not remove the Wan Kuei considering how important they are to oWoD. The only answer for just killing off the Wan Kuei unceremoniously would just be the laziness we see in the overall "5th" modern modus operandi.

                                                Culturally, I think the biggest disconnect between 'the west' and 'the east' is that the west modernized on its own, changing as it pleased shedding off outdated values for the sake of progress, while "the east" modernized at gunpoint (often literally) and has largely tried to balance the ideas of their traditions and modernity. This is obviously a gross simplification, but it's a good chunk of the reason why western authors get all star-eyed looking at these well preserved "spiritual" traditions, while very similar traditions in europe and western asia are consigned to history and largely forgotten about. Some regions of europe are particularly disconnected from tradition and thus some feel like they're missing out, becoming greatly enamored with the idea of eastern balance between modernity and tradition. I think it's rather natural honestly, even if I have grown to detest the idea.

                                                Just talking about Hungry ghosts (HG) in general (not in v5, but in the future) I think there absolutely is room for them, the issue is that there are issues, and they can be fixed.



                                                The part where they war with Kindred for no particular reason... Mostly stems from racism, but there are aspects to this that I think should stay, I mean, Kindred are nasty, and it's very likely that, were the kindred to become dominant, they would try to assert authority over the Cathayans and likely start destroying newly born HG "In the name of the Masquerade" or something like that; HG might win battles, but Cainites win wars. It's not entirely weird for HG to pre-emptively fight kindred influence... though traveling to california for it is clearly clownish stupidity and the result of writers being fools. The biggest issue from the player's perspective is that the HG supposedly mean you can't do a Camarilla game in Asia... which is unrealistic when you consider... Colonialism. Hell, Hong-Kong had 3000 inhabitants split among a dozen villages before the British took it; No doubt it'd be a hotbed for kindred. The British, French, Portuguese.. very few places escaped Colonial rule and the few that did were highly influenced by the europeans, probably losing a port-city to the Kindred as a result.

                                                I'd push the more militant HG inland/Outside the cities where the kindred don't go. The more moderate HG would work great as characters to work with/against in an Asian Camarilla court. The big problem with everything I've said is that it still kinda excludes someone from playing Vampireark Ages: Pyeongyang... unless maybe you flirt with mongols or something.

                                                I don't think there's an especially huge problem with the HG not matching up precisely to local legends (Kindred don't match up either, and there's enough movies to cast some doubt that they match any particular movie)
                                                The pretentious Dharma/Ki stuff... honestly, from reading a fair few translations of cultivation novels, it fits. It fits really well. Only thing it's missing is the part where they all learn to fly upon reaching a certain level and lines such as "You dare!" and "You are unworthy of my blade/You made me draw my sword"
