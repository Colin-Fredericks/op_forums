                            Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
                            A necromancy Path centered around 'resurrecting' knowledge could be a cool concept
                            Ok, so I went ahead and gave this a try because it seemed like a fun idea. I also wanted to emphasize how the predatory nature of a vampire can combine with a scholar archetype and have a Path reflect that, creating something for an insatiable glutton for knowledge. I tried to balance the 'noble' concept of resurrecting knowledge with destruction and egoism.
                            The numbers for rolls are obviously not tested and done by gut feeling.



                            Dust Path

                            Unusual for Necromancers, the practitioners of this Path do not primarily deal with Wraiths, but instead have learned to conjure and condense motes of memory from the Shadowlands. Only those fragments of remembrance that have been swallowed by Oblivion are truly lost and beyond the grasp of the Necromancer.


                            • Gather the Mnesia
                            The Necromancer reaches briefly into the Shadowlands, gathers a pinch of fine, gray dust and pulls it into the Skinlands. This substance is known as Mnesia and is an important catalyst in all further powers of the Path.
                            Mnesia is what is left of souls, memories, and plasm – the sediment of the underworld, ground down by eons but not yet consumed by Oblivion. When inhaled, ingested, or rubbed into the skin, it acts like a potent memory enhancing drug with a mild hallucinogenic effect. It is quite addictive to kine, and even kindred need to be careful with its use.

                            System: The player spends a blood point and rolls Wits + Occult against the local Shroud rating. A successful roll nets one dose of Mnesia. When consumed or applied as described above, it lowers the difficulty of mental feats related to memory by 3. This includes recalling suppressed or supernaturally erased memories.


                            •• Whole in Death
                            The Necromancer rubs one dose of Mnesia onto a heavily damaged object or corpse, and then ceremonially destroys it completely. This creates a Relic of the object which is summoned into the Skinlands to take the place of the original, in pristine condition. This power is mostly used to restore old books, artifacts, relief inscriptions and the like for study. In the case of a corpse, a gestalt body made of memory remnants is created, which can be questioned.
                            However, Relics do not last long in the Skinlands and after a while will fade into the underworld. Whatever remains of the destroyed original crumbles to dust in seconds after that.

                            System: The player rolls Dexterity + Occult against a difficulty of 7. A success means the object or corpse is restored for one scene, or longer for extra successes. It is possible to extend this duration with repeated use of the power and application of Mnesia when the Relic begins to fade.
                            Note that corpses reanimated in this manner do not summon a Wraith, but may attract its attention. The gestalt body contains the memories and personality of the living person up to the moment of death, and depending on circumstances may be hostile or uncooperative.


                            ••• Thirst of Coeus
                            While feeding from a vessel under the influence of Mnesia, the Necromancer can draw out and remove secrets, knowledge, or memories from the victim together with their blood or vitae.

                            System: The player must specify the memory they intend to drain. The base difficulty of the Manipulation + Occult roll is 10, but is lowered by one for each previously consumed blood point. The player must decide at which point during the feeding process they want to execute the power. A single success is sufficient to drain the desired memory, but extra successes can increase details or scope. The victim may try to resist with a difficulty 8 Willpower roll, soaking successes.
                            Characters with the necessary level in the Vitreous Path may use this power on Wraiths after blowing the Mnesia on their Corpus.


                            •••• Deny the Maw
                            This power allows the Necromancer to capture the soul escaping from a dying body. They sprinkle a dose of Mnesia over the victim, literally pull the soul out, and then warp it into the shape of a physical object.
                            Most commonly, the shape of a book is chosen, but some prefer musical instruments or another type of object that can ‘speak’. That is because an item created this way retains the memories of the captured soul, which are reflected in its function. For example, the content of a soul-bound book will contain passages of the victim’s life, texts about their passions, or unrealized ideas. The content may even change over time, or rotate through topics. An instrument will whisper these things when played, while scenes may appear as faded visions on the surface of water in a soul-bound bowl. The power also works on weakened Wraiths, but in order to succeed requires possession of a Fetter item.
                            The binding is permanent and irreversible. Any Wraith seeing such an object would identify it as a soulforged item and rightfully wonder how it got on the wrong side of the Shroud.

                            System: The player spends a point of Willpower and then rolls a Willpower test against 8. A success means the soul was captured and the player chooses what kind of object they would like to forge from the Corpus of the soul. When attempting the use of this power on a Wraith, the Willpower roll is contested and the character must possess at least one Fetter item of the Wraith.


                            ••••• Gnostic Chains
                            After tossing a dose of Mnesia in the air before them, temporary Fetters bind nearby Wraiths to the Necromancer, creating conduits through which the accumulated knowledge and skills of the ghosts can be channeled. This power can also be used as a means of information gathering, as the bound Wraiths become mere extensions of the Necromancer’s mind. Fast thinking casters have employed this as a lighting rod mechanic to lessen the impact of a mental attack on them, diverting the brunt of the effect onto the ghosts. While bound, the Wraiths become catatonic and all their senses are shut off. If they are attacked, the link to the caster severs.

                            System: The player spends a Willpower point and rolls Stamina + Occult against the local Shroud rating. For each success one Wraith is bound to the Necromancer, increasing a Skill, Talent, or Knowledge rating of the player’s choice by one, for one scene. Abilities raised by this power can not exceed the maximum rating set by the generation of the caster, but the same Ability can be raised multiple times.
                            Additionally, the caster has access to the memories of the bound Wraiths and can search them for useful information. For example, whether they have seen a specific person or witnessed an event in the area. The caster can sacrifice the link to one of the bound Wraiths to divert a single mental attack. If choosing to do so, they must remove one of the temporary Ability increases.



                            Rituals / Mnesia Alchemy

                            Rituals associated with the Dust Path are alchemical procedures that require an Intelligence + Alchemy roll (difficulty 3 + level of the procedure) to perform, as well as the necessary equipment.


                            Plasmic Guide (Level One)
                            Mixing a point of vitae, ectoplasm, Mnesia, and then incubating it for a month in a hermetically sealed jar, the Necromancer creates a small Plasmic homunculus. It follows them in the Shadowlands and is sensitive to caches of lost knowledge, to which it will draw attention (“Hey! Listen!”). It may also alert the caster in case of imminent threats on the far side of the Shroud, or perform simple scouting tasks there. It is otherwise very weak and a single successful attack will destroy it.


                            Fickle Memories (Level Two)
                            In an hour long process, the Necromancer crafts an artificial memory which they then imbue together with a bit of their vitae into a dose of Mnesia, which is then calcified. If blown onto a victim, the false memory will be implanted in the target’s mind.


                            The Blood Remembers (Level Three)
                            Ingesting a distilled and putrefied mixture of vitae or blood and Mnesia, the alchemist receives potent visions of events in the life of the donor, or the donor’s bloodline. Extra successes mean the visions go further back in the line of the donor’s ancestral line, kindred or kine. This elixir is mostly used to uncover secrets of the bloodlines, and can be greatly enhanced if laced with water from the River of Death, granting the Necromancer control over the visions.


                            Pasiphae Extract (Level Four)
                            The Necromancer must capture a Wraith and trap it in a container or immobile form. They then put it into the athanor, burning away the Psyche until only the calcified Shadow remains. This is then crushed and mixed with Mnesia, dissolved in water, filtered, and finally distilled. The result is one fluid ounce of pitch black liquid, that if ingested, induces acute dementia in the victim. The effect starts slow but is incremental, and unless the warped Shadow is exorcised, utterly complete.


                            From Beyond the Lethe (Level Five)
                            The alchemist mixes five doses of Mnesia, bone meal, and scrapings of a type of lichen that grows on tombstones with blood until they have made a dough. This must be left to rest and rise before it is baked into a small loaf of blood bread. When ingested, the consciousness is projected into the Tempest.
                            This is an extremely dangerous endeavor, as the Tempest presents a very real danger to the projection. The journey is therefore only made in a last ditch effort to hunt down fragments of a precious secret before they tumble into Oblivion.
                            The effect lasts one day, during which time the body of the Necromancer appears to be in Torpor.

                                                Ars Notoria

                                                Practitioners of this Path employ a technique called Method of Loci to improve their memories and general mental capacities. They build a mental space, which represents a vault of knowledge – a mental library – which they can enter through meditation. With each advancement on the Path, they expand this memory palace and add more utility.
                                                This technique is much older than its name, and a staple in many magic traditions. As a result, the Ars Notoria can be found in variants throughout all schools of sorcery. Even in cultures without written language, the symbol of the book can be replaced with mnemonic devices to achieve the same effect. Modern versions tend to replace rows of bookshelves with a data center and terminals, but the exact expression depends on the preferences of the practitioner.

                                                In game terms, the levels of this Path double as ranks in a combined but somewhat limited, specialized version of the Demesne and Library Backgrounds. These benefits are accessible without blood cost, but require a moment of focus to enter the mental space:

                                                • The time needed for theoretical research is dramatically reduced. Without restraints of the physical body (or physical laws), things move much faster inside the mind. This can be exploited to use the mental library as an efficient space for research and study.

                                                • Having access to memories in a well organized structure results in a type of Eidetic Memory. The Sorcerer is able to recall minute details and examine memories at leisure. One specialized application of this is speed-reading, where having caught a brief glimpse of a page is sufficient to recall and study its contents.

                                                • Many practitioners employ tulpas, semi-autonomous thoughtforms that are part of the Sorcerer’s subconsciousness. These can act as librarians and scribes, performing menial tasks of organization and cataloging, while the attention of the Sorcerer is otherwise occupied.

                                                System: Characters can usually enter their mental library within a single turn and without a roll, if undisturbed. Depending on distractions, a successful Perception + Path Rating roll (difficulty 5 and up) may be necessary. While inside, they are not completely deprived of their sensory connection to the outside world, but may suffer increased difficulty to their Alertness rolls.

                                                There is no hard rule on the perceived time dilation the Sorcerer experiences, and it should be treated narratively by the Storyteller. If tulpas are employed, the Sorcerer is able to maintain one per Path Rating – they should be treated more as automatons than conscious agents. They are just symbols for subconscious tasks of categorization, mental sorting, or ‘ambient’ thoughts on various topics the Sorcerer performs and do not require active attention.

                                                Like any other Demesne, the library is a space that can be invaded by mental attacks or certain powers. The consequences of an enemy plundering the well organized memories of the character should be obvious.


                                                Powers


                                                • Librarium
                                                The Sorcerer puts their accumulated knowledge to use, mentally collecting references for a task and using them to improve their performance.

                                                System: The player may allocate temporary bonus dots equal to their Path Rating to Abilities of their choice, as long as they have the necessary sources to draw on in their mental library. The number of successes on the roll to activate the power is the number of turns these dots remain.


                                                •• Tablinum
                                                The Sorcerer is able to transfer the contents of any medium into their mind by touch, or fill a blank medium with contents from their mind. While this is an effective way to quickly transfer information, it can also be used to create convincing forgeries. The medium itself does not change, and during the transfer, an onlooker may notice dim streams of letters flowing along the arm of the Sorcerer.

                                                System: The transfer time for the contents of an average book is one turn, but may be longer for large volumes of data. The number of successes on the roll to activate the power can be used to determine the volume of data transferred per turn, with about one Gigabyte of information per success. If used to create a forgery, the successes increase the difficulty to identify it as such. The Sorcerer must be able to touch the medium ‘as an item’: putting their hand on the case of a desktop computer will not work, but a cased external drive would.


                                                ••• Scriptorium
                                                Through immersion and observation, the caster is quickly able to adapt to new and foreign concepts by cross-referencing them with their own accumulated knowledge. After spending enough time with something new, they can add it to their library, and learn it at an astounding rate.
                                                This power can be used to quickly pick up new languages, foreign etiquette, new rituals, an obscure branch of mathematics or anything else the Sorcerer did not previously know before.

                                                System: After at least one scene of witnessing whatever new knowledge the character wants to acquire, the player may deduct the successes on the roll to activate the power from the XP cost of a corresponding character trait (to a minimum of 1) and immediately purchase it. If the player does not have sufficient XP, they may choose to ‘sell’ permanent points of Willpower. In case it needs to be said, this power obviously can not pick up traits that are incompatible with the nature of the caster, such as the Gifts of a werewolf or awakened Magick.


                                                •••• Laboratorium
                                                The Sorcerer thoughtcrafts a space for experimentation and creation of new knowledge. A mental study or laboratory has many advantages over its mundane counterpart: it is always accessible, any necessary tools can be created by a thought, and the very laws of the realm obey the will of the researcher. While the experiments may not be repeatable in the physical world, the Sorcerers have found a way to circumvent this problem. This power allows them to transfer physical objects between their mental laboratory and the outside. Aside from providing a fantastic creative space, it is also a useful cache for equipment.

                                                There are, however, some limitations: First, objects with more mass or larger than the caster can not be transferred. Second, no self-aware beings can be transported. Reportedly, there have been successful summonings of ephemeral entities inside a mental laboratory, but most researchers wisely stick to lab mice. Third, anything brought to the outside must consist of physical material – in other words, built from materials first brought inside. And finally, any object brought outside with too outlandish properties will be subject to Paradox and destroyed.

                                                System: The number of successes on the roll to activate this power determines the total mass of objects that can be transferred. The exact nature and results of the experiments depends on the nature of research and what the Sorcerer’s goal is and should be handled separately. The Sorcerer can work in the laboratory while physically sleeping.

                                                Example: Hassam al-Zaher, who researches the properties of the Shroud, wants to test a theory that the emotions of mortals affect the local Shroud strength. He transports canvas, painting colors, and some alchemical components into his mental laboratory. Hassam can create painting utensils and the equipment to alchemically process the colors by will, and crafts some paintings that supernaturally evoke different emotions in a viewer, but also record variations in the Shroud strength on the back of the canvas. Once finished, he can transport the paintings to the outside.


                                                ••••• Stellarium
                                                By this point, the Demesne has become strong enough to connect with the Astral Umbra, allowing the Sorcerer to project into and explore the very realm of ideas.

                                                System: A successful roll to activate this power allows the character to exit their mental library and fully project into the Astral Umbra, usually the Vulgate. The physical body appears to be in torpor, and loses all sensual connection to the physical world.
