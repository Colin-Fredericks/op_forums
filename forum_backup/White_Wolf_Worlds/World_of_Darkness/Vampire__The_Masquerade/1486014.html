          It's supposed to include at least half of the core book's "Tremere" rituals and paths.

          But if we want to talk about story, it's the weakest of the three largest traditions (Tremere Hermetic thaumaturgy,FoS Ahku) for both mechanism and logistics.

          Tremere Heremetic Thaumaturgy:
          Scope: Hermetic theory is an all encompassing, moderate, idea: All religions are part true, so put aside your differences, come together and let's do magic. If it exists, magic for it also exists. If some other magical practice can do it, we can do it to. 10/10
          Mechanisms: Tremere do really well in standardizing their practice and their members are relatively uniform which is really good for teaching new people. 10/10
          Structure: The Tremere's entire clan structure revolves around supporting thaumaturges. There's a little brainwashing and blood bonding going on, and anything involving vampires and big egos is going to have a little dysfunction. But honestly? I think this is the best system for vampires. 9/10

          Average: 9.6, will invariably monopolize the magic of the world.

          Settite Akhu
          Scope: Everything needs to be put in the framework of Set. You are limited to mimicking what you think the God/His allies can do. This would give the FoS a 6/10 but fortunately the FoS are really very liberal with what Set is and who his allies are, so I'd give it a 7.
          Mechanism: Getting really, really into roleplaying an egyptian priest is the way to work the magic; it's fun. The LARP might cut into your ritual time, but nothings so gratifying as to work the magic over the god you geek out to. 7/10
          Organization: Everyone's bound together by a love of Set. Practicing and sharing the magic with believers is proof of your love of Set: You're in a cult, and that's actually a good thing for you. However FOS don't get together in excellent structures like the Tremere to effectively divide labour, so more of their time is spent on cult micromanaging and politicking, which is probably why magic is an out-of-clan for them. 7/10

          Average: 7/10

          Assamite Dur-An-Ki
          Scope: Like hermeticism, Dur-An-Ki, also encompases everything, but unlike hermetics, Assamite sorcers do not "meet in the middle". Dur-an-Ki is too broad. It contains both dirty witches making covenants with spirits and elegant scholars drawing power from the stars: these people aren't likely to get along and see the opposite extreme's practices as bad for their own. I give it a 5/10.
          Mechanism: there's no standardization and not much in the way of shared beliefs. Also most books have such mages require drugs or pain or some other extreme state. 3/10.
          Structure: No standardization, being dependent/hostage to the other castes, not really having local Assamite sorcerers who can spend time to train neonates means an increased reliance on embracing the rare few who already practice magic, further limiting numbers and muddling the standardization 3/10.

          Average: 3.6. They're failures who rely on natural talent and who are pigeonholed into an undesirable system by an unforgiving caste system. But magic in the WoD isn't about talent, it's about knowledge. There might be some exceptional individuals among their ranks that can go toe to toe with some of the best Tremere has to offer, but the Tremere has more of those.

                      Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                      It's supposed to include at least half of the core book's "Tremere" rituals and paths.

                      But if we want to talk about story, it's the weakest of the three largest traditions (Tremere Hermetic thaumaturgy,FoS Ahku) for both mechanism and logistics.
                      I politely disagree with the width of the difference and many details of the conclusion, but not with the general gist of it.

                      My own perception of the matter, taking only the lore:
                      Scope: Tremere Hermeticism is all encompassing, but exists under a rigid and exceedingly complex theoretical framework. While it's useful for the final result, breaking an idea and reassembling it as Thaumaturgy is a lengthy process that shouldn't have a seamless result. Akhu has a solid basis that makes developing new magic much more a matter of intuition and inspiration. Dur-An-Ki has an idea of accepting other magical practices at face value that makes incorporating things from the outside basically plug-and-play.
                      Mechanisms: Disagree completely here. The standardization is a good thing, but Tremere Hermeticism has a high threshold to start doing stuff. Getting the theory is important, understanding why a ritual works is important. Once you get to it it's easy to get how the stuff your peers do work and study them, but until you figure it out you're on tough waters. Akhu is time and resource consuming, but the fact it relies on religion, again, makes it very intuitive for the practitioner. Dur-An-Ki is a middle ground where you do need to study a whole new thing each time, but you don't need to understand it in a deeper level, the fact that it work is good enough, and itt does make for an easier time learning whatever the student already had some understanding of, as they don't have to unlearn and learn again under a new magical paradigm.
                      Structure: Here I mostly agree. The Tremere simply has a far better clan structure to support sorcerers. Even if local Tremere aren't the greatest practitioners (the clan does need people doing other things), they have a Chantry with access to their extensive support network to help new sorcerers, and standardization goes a long way into making the material accessible across the board. But I wouldn't so completely dismiss the Banu Haqim here, the sorcerers usually have contact with each other and some gathering places, and while it isn't easy for them to share magic, it is to find tutors in the specifics you want, something that isn't always easy on the Tremere side because specific knowledge is something they're encouraged to hoard and dispute over, while the Haqim Sorcerers don't have much to lose spreading any particular magic in the clan.

                      With this, I completely agree that the Tremere as a clan are certainly superior, but I wouldn't dismiss the Sorcerers as failures at all. The ultimate advantage of the Tremere is actually something the game doesn't even have a lot of rules on, rituals with multiple participants. Every time on the lore the Tremere outdone everyone else, it was this kind of ritual, which they can organize and execute in a scale no one else can compete with.

                      But from a mechanics perspective, that is what matters here, I'd say at least in V20 the Sorcerers actually have the strongest magic, not the weakest. Because of two small rules only they have: the possibility for the ST to add Paths to their list of options just 'cause, and the option to learn anything from other magical Disciplines at +50% cost. With this, while their standard options are limited, they're solid, they have an exclusive Path, and they have potential access to anything else even if at extra cost, something the Tremere can't do. That means a Banu Haqim can potentially learn anything under Necromancy, Thaumaturgy and Koldunic in the core book alone, and even anything the ST introduces in the game by any means for any Blood Magic. If you add Rites of the Blood, then they get even more powerful with access to Kalif, as difficulty reduction is one hell of a rare resource in VtM.

                      Personally I don't think the rules as they stand now are very good to demonstrate any of the lore differences, and I'm strongly against rules that make any choice for the players objectively inferior, so I tend to be a bit more lenient with access to Paths the player wants. And given that the rules do not require or even suggest to be too strict on which Path pertains to which Discipline, I don't think there should be a lot of practical differences in power as they are.

                              It's a bit outside of rules and lore of the game, but I collect grimoires as a hobby and have a few from the middle east, the classics from Egypt, and multiple hermetic ones. If I were to make an analogy, I would liken the differences to mathematics: the basic language and rules are the same, but the application varies on what you are doing. If Hermeticism is like calculus, middle eastern magic is like algebra.
                              Formalization isn't something that's exclusive to hermetic magic either, it's more dependent on time than discipline; old spells rely more on theurgic invocations, new spells more on ritual, symbolism, correspondences and established conventions. Eventually all traditions start to borrow heavily from each other when the world started to get smaller.

                              Bottom line, if the Assamites and Setites didn't stop developing their magic (and why would they), the scope should be pretty even between them and the Tremere. If something doesn't fit your paradigm, you can always develop an analog that does. I think it's also important to consider how much an occult library can mitigate some of the structural differences- and Mesopotamia and Egypt definitely had a head-start with written records. I would still give the Tremere the overall edge, simply because of the superior internal organization.

                                                          Honestly, I think you're understating how Paradigm can be a problem even if we don't get into mage territory:
                                                          Let me give a bad but easy-to-understand example.
