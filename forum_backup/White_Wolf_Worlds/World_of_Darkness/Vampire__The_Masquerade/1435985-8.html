And they don't go to bed with a Disney villain song and dance about how evilly evil they are.

                No, they believe they're right, and what they do is morally justified. Either in the name of making necessary sacrifices for progress, to solve for some utilitarian equation, to rid of the world of perceived evils, or simply to get rid of one group of people or another who "don't have the right to exist". They do the shit they do, believing the ends justify the means, and the ends are so utopian, moral, or otherwise desirable that any means to achieve them are necessary and proper.

                As a hypothetical, let's say you're some poor unfortunate Vietnam draftee. You have no idea why you're fucking there; you're told you're out to stop Communists or what the fuck ever, but the reality on the ground is this is such a malaria- and dysentery-infested shithole so far removed from the developed world nobody in the world except the Vietnamese ought to give a fuck about how the Vietnamese are governed.                 And you've seen the consequences as your buddies get sick, hurt and shipped out, and die off one by one to the local diseases, booby traps, mines, grenades, and ambushes of an enemy you're never given clear orders to fight in pitched battles or to secure and hold strategically-important ground. No, you're just shipped out on an endless Groundhog Day-esque cycle of patrols and S&D missions while the casualties, rancor, and rage at your overall circumstances pile up.                                     Yeah, there's absolutely no reason to go further into real-life atrocities to continue the discussion. I said what I did for what I believed was necessary context and nothing more -- that those who engineer and execute atrocities don't do it because they understand or perceive it to be evil. Rather, they do it because they perceive it to be right, or at least justified; or, as Arendt made the case in Eichmann in Jerusalem, by those who think nothing of it at all. And in some cases, as an outlash part and parcel of cyclical violence.

                                    To a certain extent, this mirrors itself well in WoD's morality system, as the mechanic represents a slippery, entropic, slope towards casual inhumanity. Low-Humanity vampires rarely envision or rationalize themselves objectively as creatures of unrepentant embrace of "evil"; that's the purview of select Paths, that only do so by way of contrast to Humanity, and even then in the context of some intellectual framework which rationalizes evil as necessary means to desired ends. Even the Sabbat's twisted moralities cannot exist separate from Humanity nor outside the context of Humanity; an antithesis cannot exist without a thesis.

                                    How is this relevant to whatever the Second Inquisition is, whatever it does, and what its ends may be? Because they believe what they're doing is right, and that the ends justify the means. Spoiler alert, this doesn't end with the eradication of inhumane vampires, a restoration of some arbitrary bureaucratic status quo, and a farewell to arms.
