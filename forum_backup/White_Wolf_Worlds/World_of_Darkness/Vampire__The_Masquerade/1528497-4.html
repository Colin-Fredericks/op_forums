                            I can say I'm not against camp in my VtM games.

                            Hell, I literally wrote a luchador Nosferatu into this chronicle for no other reason than if a scene starts getting excessively boring or stupid, I can interrupt it by blasting reggaeton, and have him show up, dropkick someone or deliver a chair shot before running off. Maybe start a good old fashioned feud with a PC or something stupid. Or, send his famulus (a pigeon which, naturally has a tiny little luchador mask matching his own) to crap on someone, or his three-dot Retainer who's a ghouled mini-estrella. He's even an Osiris, and his Herd are ring girls.

                            Lucha libre is awesome, Nosferatu are awesome. Luchador Nosferatu is awesome squared. Especially since he's the Prince's herald, he delivers every one of her proclamations or edicts as he would a wrestling promo.

                            But the setting is deliberately designed as post-Gehenna, and it drives home how vampires are really scraping the bottom of the barrel to have anything approaching a functioning society for a guy this expressly stupid to be Herald. And this particular Nosferatu has a really depressing backstory, about how he played rudo to his mortal brother's tecnico, and their big break was this grand and elaborately planned storyline where they were going to switch roles...then he was Embraced by an elder who didn't understand kayfabe, for him to get his "revenge". Now, he gets to play rudo...forever.

                            Camp is fun, but I'm all about satire and substance. "Trenchcoats and katanas" provides the former, but not necessarily the latter, and for that it just comes off as terribly edgelord to me.

                                                        I rarely wade into this part of the forum these days, but I'll ramble a little.

                                                        In my view, it's quite possible for V5 to have (or have had) the best of both worlds - entry level, accessible books with little to no metaplot (as James mentions, books like Diablerie: Mexico, Alien Hunger, etc. - and we have them in the form of Trails of Ash and Bone and the Players Guide - which I still think needs an apostrophe), and metaplot heavy, world expanding books (as CT mentions, Beckett's Jyhad Diary, though I'd put it at the extreme end of that wedge - and we have them in the form of Cults of the Blood Gods and Forbidden Religions). I anticipate we'll keep making books of both types at Onyx Path, albeit not for Vampire or the World of Darkness, specifically.

                                                        One approach does not eliminate the other. But designers and fans alike can become tribal and see one thing as ruining, or harming the other, and so they pivot to "this is my way and your way doesn't work for me, brother." It's perfectly normal for any artist to want to leave their own mark or steer a property under their control in a way that appeals to them, and they think the audience will appreciate. Some new fans will jump on the changes as a good thing, some old fans will jump off because they see the changes as a bad thing. As we see with a lot of long lasting fictional worlds (DC with the New 52; Doctor Who's various regenerations under different showrunners; Mortal Kombat's recent reboot, just to name a few), it's impossible to launch a new "edition" or introduce a metaplot shake-up without pissing some people off. But I very much believe it's possible to make one edition connect to another coherently and satisfy audiences from both camps (not 100% of the audience, ever, but some), even if some of that audience avoids picking up this book in favour of that book. Being able to say "this book isn't for me but I'm looking forward to the next one" is a much healthier mindset than "this book killed the line and my enthusiasm."

                                                        The fault, dear Brutus, is not in our stars, but in our selves. We shouldn't see every book as something we should be beholden to, no more than a fan of Doom Patrol in the New 52 should be expected to buy Green Arrow just because he crosses over with them in one issue. And that's okay. If you like V5 just based on the core book and can run a thousand plots from it alone, that's just as fine as wanting to buy every book because you want to keep up with the adventures of Lucita. I'd argue that for the Storyteller who can do that, V5 has been a tremendous success.

                                                        I see metaplot as a hook to keep people engaged and interested, but not one that's going to work for everyone, which means I try to ensure every book has some metaplot and some material for people who aren't interested in that kind of thing. I can see from this thread that there are real fans of metaplot and people who don't give a damn about Theo Bell et al. For all that there are some people saying "I don't want another book of Thaumaturgy rituals" or whatever, it's books of powers and player options that always sell the best across the entire RPG industry, including Vampire. I'm sure Renegade's newest Blood Sigils book has done very well. So these books need to exist to cater to the fans who want them, just as books with metaplot content need to exist for other fans. And I think material to satisfy both can exist in the same book each time. You can probably tell from all the books I developed for V5 that that was my design philosophy.

                                                        And you can by all means disagree as to whether it worked.

                                                        This thread shows there are a lot of different, clashing views as to "what would have been better for V5?" Everyone has an opinion. For me, I developed the game I'd want to run or play, barring instances where I was obliged to add this thing or that thing at the behest of the license owner. In none of those cases did it ruin Vampire for me. You pick and choose the bits you want to use and keep the rest of the toys in their box.

                                                        As an aside, as the co-developer of Beckett's Jyhad Diary, and a frequent writer and developer for the first few years of V5, I refute the common complaint that we didn't continue the plots in BJD. We were never going to do that with every single plot, because the plots introduced in that book are there for players to explore and resolve in their chronicles. The fundamental complaint about Vampire of old is that Lucita and Anatole, or whichever NPC was flavour of the month, would move the plot along regardless of the players' actions. I still think this is a silly complaint, as a decent Storyteller would insert the protagonists into pivotal roles instead of using the stand-in NPCs. But despite my view, I'm aware of the complaint that "this chronicle just lets me sit back and watch as NPCs complete the story" (then do something!), so it would have made no sense for Beckett to write a sequel resolving every single plot hook of the hundreds introduced in BJD. I made links to at least a handful of plots in BJD (and other V20 and earlier books) in every V5 book I worked on, and that ended when I stopped working on V5, but that just means the plot is there for you to explore. If, for example, we had the plot of Enoch waking up from BJD be immediately resolved in V5, what would be the point of introducing it in BJD? Those plots are open for a reason, and part of the reason I enjoyed V20 so much was it enabled me to expand metaplot without furthering it. BJD is a tool. Storytellers and players who see metaplot as a limitation to play ("the Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting says Thay is now overrun with undead so we can't play our games set there") would probably be better off not engaging with it.

                                                        If you're a fan of old metaplot and you want to see it in V5, it's a valid criticism to say "I want to see more of this." It's less valid to say "It's not there" because it is, at least in most of the books. It may not be the metaplot you in particular want to see, but who really was going to continue the story of the Blood Brother psycho surgeon Dr. Varona?

                                                        As another aside, I'm no big advocate for The Fall of London. I agree it's one of the weakest V5 books, for a myriad of reasons not relating to the quality of the writers. I remember sitting on the balcony at the Modiphius offices, the summer heat scorching the top of my head (back then I had hair, but it was thinning), and discussing the plot they wanted in that book. They wanted a time-hopping, D&D, Curse of Strahd style campaign. What came out isn't exactly that. But I find the idea that you could discount it due to Mithras's presence and plotting a little shallow. He's a key power player and antagonist and your characters are operating in his shadow, but that's one of the key elements of Vampire - powerful vampires push you around, so you can try to cut them off at the knees. Now, if your coterie decides to serve and save Mithras, good job bootlicker! You've helped the Powers That Be. If your coterie undermines him, that's even better. There's room for stories like that, and not everyone needs to like them. I'd argue The Fall of London would have appealed to a wider audience if it hadn't had the whole "you wake after year of torpor" start, but I'll stop beating around the weeds on this one. Point is, there are plenty of good reasons to dislike that chronicle, but I'd argue the presence of a controlling methuselah is pretty typical for Vampire.

                                                        Do I think V5 would have been better if it had removed all methuselahs and elders as it mooted doing in the core book? Absolutely not. Because it only mooted it, and in the same book (and Camarilla, and Chicago, etc.), had contemporary quotes from vampires such as Lucinde and Talley, who are hardly young fledglings. The point of the Beckoning is for Storytellers to pick and choose the elders they want to keep, and dispose of the rest. The reason Menele was removed but Helena was not, in Chicago, was because we needed to show that the Beckoning affects vampires differently, and some devise methods of staving it off. Nerissa was added at the request of Paradox, as I recall, and we were advised to keep several well known elder figures (Critias among them) to create that connectivity between editions of Vampire. Lucita and Vykos are later mentioned in the Sabbat book as still being around, so the Beckoning is, like BJD, a tool. Storytellers should use it to craft the World of Darkness they want. Wholesale removal of every one of these interesting characters would make Vampire a tonally different game.

                                                        Anyway, work calls. Just wanted to dump my rambling thoughts out here. Have a good one!
