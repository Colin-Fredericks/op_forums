    How do vampires maintain security while hunting?

    12-15-2021, 11:28 AM
    It's something I thought about when playing Coteries of New York. Your mentor routinely hunts out in the open with just one ghoul. If someone wanted to assassinate her, it doesn't seem like it would have been particularly difficult. How do elder vampires protect themselves when out hunting? Do most of them keep an army of heavily armed ghouls around at all times when out seducing in clubs, breaking into the homes of sleepers, and mugging alleyway bums? For the supposedly "social" class of monsters, it sure does seem like much of their existence is highly impractical to do as anything but a loner.

          With the size of cities compared to vampire population, keeping a low profile while hunting isn't exceptionally hard. As long as you keep your enemies from learning your feeding habits, you shouldn't have that much of a problem. Even then this is one of the reasons to have feeding grounds strictly defined and enforced. An elder probably do not hunt over an entire borough, but will want to know who exactly, if anyone among the Damned, is even stepping there.

          That aside, power in all its forms is used to keep protection. You want people to know that messing with you isn't without consequence, whatever this consequence might be. This is one reason for the Camarilla to be real on its enforcement of the Traditions: laws that work bring safety, and the first priority of an elder isn't to kill opponents or even Thin Bloods, but to not die. The Sabbat keeps the Vaulderie and the Packs for similar reasons, without the protection of the law, always have at reach someone you can thrust enough to not kill you, and preferably kill anyone who tries.

          Bringing your food to you is the ultimate prize. It doesn't matter if one of your heavens is a VIP club or if your thugs kidnap beggars and bring them for you, safeguarding a place and have your victims coming is one of the safest options. Having the blood brought to you at a different place from where it was extracted is even safer, and most elders won't let taste stay in the way of safety on common nights. Any Tremere that knows rituals to keep blood fresh and tasty in a bottle is in for making a lot of money.

              First, the grand strategy: Don't make enemies, or at least serious enemies. A petty rivalry might be fun, so long as both parties know the rules, but if someone doesn't know the rules? Axe em. Target ambitious bastards that make you worry, but do it secretly; if you succeed, they're dead or they learn their lesson, if you fail, you shouldn't be a suspect. You can do a lot by building good relationships with everyone, though of course sometimes getting too friendly with Vampire A might upset Vampire B.

              A stable government that keeps some semblance of law and order will help you live longer. Your politics should always favour stability.

              Second, the Tactics.

              There's a hundred ways to hunt and each clan is generally going to approach feeding differently. The Tremere and Ventrue control their environments. The Nosferatu and Malkavians don't let people know they were ever there. The Brujah and Toreador are really the interesting ones. The Toreador need to be selective and they've got the tools to be selective, Auspex helps you find problems, celerity helps you avoid problems, and Presence helps deter problems: they benefit from large crowds that prevent violence helps.. The Brujah are... well, if you can kill the Brujah in the first round, GG, if you fail to kill the Brujah in the first round, GG.

                  When you get down to it, if somebody puts it in their head and takes the time for it, most vampires can be assassinated, hunt or no hunt. A daytime raid with enough determination behind it can end the vast majority of vampires. The balance of power within a city is something like mutually assured destruction: an outsider may not know where the havens of everybody are, but the longtime residents of cities are intimately familiar with where they each lair. Few vampires are protected sufficiently from this: a major Tremere chantry might survive if warded enough, and some havens may be obscured by elder levels of Obfuscate.

                  While you cannot really prevent an assassination attempt on you from succeeding, you can definitely deter an easy attempt from having a chance. If it would take a dozen trained ghouls to take you down in the daytime, for example, that's too big an operation and would cause serious enough ripples to bring an Archon to the city. But if two ghouls could take you down, everybody is going to be tempted if they see any gain in it. So a few booby traps and a few armed ghouls in each haven typically keeps the peace.

                  A similar logic operates in the case of hunting. Celerity, Obfuscate, Protean, Vicissitude, Serpentis, Chimerstry, and Fortitude are all great for getting away from an ambush; Auspex helps you see it coming. If you're weak enough to get gibbed by a few armed ghouls or neonates, people will be tempted to inconvenience you during feeding. If everybody knows you have a fighting chance or could somehow get away, they would have to put together experienced Kindred to whack you, and the Prince will notice. If you sufficiently annoy somebody with connections, they can contract an Assamite onto you and you have practically no chance; but that's why everybody's polite to each other's faces in Elysium, always.

                  Ultimately, there's a tradeoff between convenience and safety. You could have your ghouls bring you meals like a lot of paranoid elders do, but then you grow out of touch with the world and with your Humanity. It's also horrible: imagine the part of the lockdown where you sat at home all day and had delivery, but have it span a few centuries. Also note that in vampiric power, charisma and courage count for a lot. If you're too chicken to step out of your haven and stretch your legs, other vampires won't see you as a leader, and others will come to dominate city affairs. Only elders with powers like Auspex 6, Dominate 8 etc. can really afford to direct events from a distance (Tzimisce, Lasombra. Ventrue elders).

                      Many aspects of V:tM falls apart when you focus in on logistics or logic. Between the ability to have a live in Herd, online banking, the Internet, and video conferences.... there really isn't a reason for a vampire to leave their havens, which in today's world can easily be a converted decommissioned nuclear missile silo or similar military grade secure installation.

                      The notion of vampire society is more of a game mechanic on par with having a party meeting in a tavern or inn. It helps create role play situations rather than give an accurate simulation of creatures with the abilities and proclivities we know and love.

                      As for the idea of hunting, in truth it should really be a feeding method used by traveling vampires rather than established locals. Any Clan/Bloodline with Animalism, Dominate, or Presence should really have a Herd background. Clans with Obfuscate would be the main ones to "hunt" in the modern era. But once again if we are talking about Elders, they would travel with a herd in tow rather than hunting.

                      At the same time, while it is logical to say that assassinating a target while they are hunting, the reality of it is that you probably won't know when a target will go hunting, and more importantly where they will hunt. At best you can follow a target to their hunting grounds and hopefully be able to sneak up on them, but you won't be able to set up a proper ambush. Any vampire worth their fangs will have four or five hunting areas and switch between them at random, usually with a travel route that allows them to spot any uninvited guests.

                                              I disagree that there's no reason to have Domain or a Herd greater than 2. There's a very good reason to have both: Redundancy.

                                              No matter how secure a vampire appears, setbacks can snowball into catastrophic problems. You don't want to be the Elder who relies on a pool of vessels, only for half of them to be taken out of commission, beyond the vampire's reach, tainted by disease, or actively targeted.

                                              A vampire keeps more Herd so they can soak losses without significant disruption of their regular feeding. That same vampire seeks to retain control of a Domain, so they always have the option of doing an old fashioned hunt in territory they control. Just having that option is worth its weight in gold.

                                              Especially if, despite the Elder's best efforts, they end up making enemies that are willing to escalate matters. We can talk all day about the best defense against being targeted is to not make enemies in the first place. Reality is rarely so accommodating, and the Jyhad even less so.

                                              We must also remember that Elders, like all people, are not perfectly rational beings that only ever make the most efficient decisions. A vampire will seek to acquire more Herd, because the caveman part of the brain (to whatever degree that remains in the dead corpus that they call a body) still hews to that most simple adage: more food = better.

                                              By that same token, no matter the level of sophistication at play in the Elder's mind, the Beast is gratified by having territory it can call its own. Seeking to acquire and expand Domain is, often, felt to be a good unto itself. The ability to say "this place is MINE, stay out!" is all the reason an Elder needs to do so. And when a city has been carved up between Kindred power players, the only way to get more Domain is to shave it off a rival.

                                              Hence the Jyhad.

                                                      While redundancy is a good strategy, the scope of it is what I am disagreeing with.

                                                      Why have a larger herd, which must be maintained by the way, when you can own hospitals and blood banks. Alternatively, you don't have to own those places, you can just have a really high-grade medical coverage plan that lets you get "transfusions" anywhere in the country. Unless you are running a compound where the Herd never leave, each additional herd member is a potential security hazard. Just having more of them doesn't solve a problem or prevent one, it just adds to security hassles.

                                                      Even if you do go the hole private herd in my basement route, have you considered the logistics of housing, feeding, and upkeep on that many people? The average american home only has about 3 days supplies in it. Without a lot of planning and investment there is no way to survive a siege with a large herd for any meaningful length of time.

                                                      Domain is the same scenario, why claim a domain that you have to defend. That basically is putting up a huge neon sign saying "find this elder here". Since most Elders tend to have lower Generations than most of the locals, that is just endless conflict waiting to happen for nothing more than bragging rights to an eating area. Of course just become you are an Elder and claim Domain, it doesn't mean a younger upstart Prince won't just declare immanent domain and seize it or declare it a public hunting area.

There are still plenty of cool games to be played with them, but they won't interact in the same way with the world as younger vampires.
