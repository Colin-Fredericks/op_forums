    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    As much as I don't like to put it in such terms, Ragged Robin is right. If you like that feel and don't want to include the whole convoluted setting, Requiem covers you far better than any Masquerade edition will.

    This is basically the state of things. At some point 2nd edition had too much of a metaplot, and Revised definitely went too far. At some point the lore of the game was locked into that. I personally totally agree with you, and I discuss Masquerade more out of nostalgia than anything. For playing, I actually switched almost completely on Vampire to Requiem.
    My feelings on Requiem are complicated, but I don't want to get into the weeds of it here. Long story short, the (early) setting of Masquerade is simply a better fit for my personal tastes, and it has nothing to do with nostalgia -- I didn't crack open a single World of Darkness book until the original game line had already come to an end in 2004.

    I can't help but feel like "Just go play Requiem!" is a non sequitur response to what I was talking about. I wasn't disagreeing with the basic concepts of the original setting, but only voicing my disagreement with the direction the game line has taken since 1st and early 2nd Edition, as was (accurately, in my view) detailed by Black Fox in post #37. I have little interest in Vampire: The Sect Wars, and I reject the idea that this is all that Masquerade has ever been good for.

    When Mark Rein-Hagen himself says that metaplot is stupid, I feel like I'm justified in that position. This is not an appeal to authority -- you don't have to care about what Rein-Hagen thinks one way or the other. I'm simply pointing out that one of the chief creators of the franchise does not consider metaplot a strength of the game, so it's not far-fetched to suggest that Vampire: The Masquerade is more than just its metaplot (which really only became a thing in the Revised era, as far as I can tell).

                Nah really the more I think about it the more I agree that METAPLOT IS DUMB. Like any metaplot. Good metaplot, bad metaplot. Wouldn't matter if Tremere got his perfect comeuppance or if the Anarchs unanimously became lilth worshippers and then used the power of friendship to strap the antideluvians on ICBMs heading for the sun. I don't mind the self-contained struggles of the vampire population in ST Petersburg because I can happily ignore it and nobody's going to tell me my game is wrong over an enjoyable read, but reads become significantly less enjoyable when suddenly they undermine the storyteller and players and new players come to your table expecting Y when you're playing a very incompatible X.

                From an OoC perspective, It's just shit. I don't give a fuck about Hardstardt or Rudi or other SNPCs. VTM works so well because it gives you a toolbox and so much room for making your own city. You have the groundwork for an ecosystem but freedom to make your own political model. Every metaplot development is, at absolute best, is another tool for the toolbox but more likely, at worst, you're going to have another kid dicking around in your sandpit, taking away your toys and smashing your finely made castles. The only good metaplot development in modern nights has been Ur-Shulgi waking up, because it provides more tools than it takes away and initially isn't so obtrusive (No Assamites came to your city, carry on, business as usual)

                From an in character perspective, it's just shit. We're playing with a slow-to-change immortal society that, before 1900, had a few upsets at the beginning of the early modern period, had the Tremere pop up a thousand years ago, and... otherwise mirrored mortal trends and didn't really make much noise. But now Vampires Twist and turn like a twisty turny thing and are lead by the stars of a soap opera. Yeah sure, "Final nights" and all that, but evangelicals have been claiming the end is nigh forever now and all they need to do is just keep pushing the date forwards.

                                    I can't help but feel like "Just go play Requiem!" is a non sequitur response to what I was talking about.
                                    And that's why I don't like to put in those terms, but I couldn't help it.

                                    My point is, the metaplot dominates VtM now and have been for a long time. I like the first V20 books precisely because they're more agnostic on this and I didn't wanted BJD on principle. But it was inevitable.

                                    Today, and for a long time already, the metaplot is the main mark of VtM to go forward, and so it will be the way to go forward. You can stay playing your own thing with the 1st ed setting as basis and only adding whatever you like from other books, but the other books will just keep reminding you that you're playing your own thing and that they're not meant to support your setting.

                                    It isn't right. It is horrible. And now it is even worse, since they give support for even less styles of play. But it is what it is, we can't expect things to change now. Many buyers fully expect metaplot development. Most players want the books to delve into elements that take from that 1st ed feel. Masquerade won't go back.

                                    Now, I can't help with your feelings about Requiem, esp if I don't know them, but that was sincerely what worked for me. I found there many things I treasured in VtM in 1st edition and that were dumped to make room for the metaplot. And Requiem didn't dumped them, even when more elements are added they are easy to ignore or tweak to my needs without directly contradicting the setting. It was a genuine suggestion that worked for some people.

Making versions of the same setting in different time periods may work. But metaplot itself is stupid.
