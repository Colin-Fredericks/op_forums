    You are tasked as the architect of a great vampire settlement.

    05-31-2023, 07:02 AM
    Think a bunker or a hidden city in the middle of nowhere.

    Possible clients include:
    -Someone who believes Gehenna or some other apocalyptic event is nigh.
    -someone with extreme isolationist tendencies or who wants to escape the Jyhad.
    -A former king who misses being a king (bonus points if you can put in a castle) or someone overly enthusiastic about their interpretation of the second city.
    -A billionaire narcissist with delusions of granduer (I'm not sure about the possibilities when utilizing awakened magicks but mundane-wise living on Mars is too much of a stretch, but if Vampires went to the moon would their sleep cycles be moon time? That might be more convenient)

    -A Ventrue has very specific but-endangered tastes and will go to extreme measures to protect his herd. A malkavian with compulsively extreme protectiveness over her herd wants a safe space.
    -A Tzmisce wants a controlled environment for his experiments ,
    - An infernalist or some kind of cultist who wants to do this to please his demon/god master

    Client concerns:
    -It needs to be built with secrecy, but a lot of manpower will be needed for the build: the place needs to be large with many facilities so nobody gets bored or overly suicidal and the human population needs to be large enough to prevent inbreeding. Keeping such a large project secret will be a massive undertaking.
    -The human population need to be well fed with a variety of food, and this needs to be done secretly.
    -The humans can't escape to tell the world.

    Where do you build it, how do you build it, what measures do you take to keep the people in, what Disciplines or other powers do you need to maintain control? What kind of hooks could you put in that draw the players into finding the settlement?

          If you google "billionaire bunkers" you will get plenty of resources (including schematics) for the real life industry centered around catering to very rich doomsday preppers; there should be enough overlap for a starting point. This includes questions on how to handle a large number of slaves that cater to the needs of their master. Yes, people professionally discuss questions wether electric collars are a good way to ensure the loyalty of the plebs enshrined with their tyrant.

          Anyways, it should provide a basic infrastructure. Few actual locations are public of course, but since most of the bunkers are repurposed military structures they tend to be in isolated areas like deserts or steppes. Large plains also have the benefit of making aerial supply possible, initially at least, and provide a natural border that prevents escape. Using or expanding an already established structure also helps with secrecy. I would say that eliminating information about a location is easier than trying to build a large structure without anyone noticing (in the satellite era). Regardless of whether you build in Antarctica or on Mars, some eye in the sky is going to mark it down.
          Actually, while a logistic nightmare, moving the operation into a different realm might be the safest bet. Enoch manged to go on for a pretty long while. Lorewise there is also Alamut for an isolated enclave, but the Assamite Clanbook wastes little space on the specifics of the involved logistics iirc.

          The biggest problem for doomsday bunkers is that no-one has managed to create a sustainable isolated biosphere yet. Surface agriculture is both a risk of exposure and to security, so going full underground would be the clearly preferred option- luckily, blood magic (or straight up Magick) provides ways to cheat away the problem of how to feed the mortal Herd. Still neither easy nor inexpensive, but at least possible. If you go with the Enoch option, you may be able to minimize the required effort, depending on the rules of the realm. The Bahari somehow found a way to support a mortal populace in the underworld, of all places. If figure the middle umbra might be more accommodating.

          I guess the question about specific Disciplines (magic always helps) and possible hooks hinges on the location and type, but in general, any "Lost City" style hooks would do. Picking up a paper trail, rumors, etc.

          Edit: If you're interested, the Earthdawn RPG has this very scenario as an essential back-story element; One city, Parlainth, decided to nope out of dimension instead of bunkering down. It did not end well, but has some very entertaining setting novels as result.

              Use vicissitude and build myself a house in the middle of nowhere, alone, using the clients' bodies as the materials.

              So then, crazy and probably unworkable ideas:

              Location: As close to the poles as possible, possibly on them. Antarctica looks nice. While this means that half the year the vampire populace will be non-functioning, for the other half of the year you have a lot of activity time. It also provides isolation and adorable pet ghoul penguins. The only downside is that you won't be readily available half the year to keep an eye on things.
              There's plenty of empty territory to go around, so rather than a shared settlement, everyone can take over or establish their own research base or ice castle or something. What you do there is your own business. Except for the infernalist, he sounds like trouble and we should all throw him in the volcano for our own good.
              If it must be a joint location, I believe there are ice caves that look quite pleasant and wouldn't turn up on google maps.

              Learning fortitude and protean may be necessary for all inhabitants who wish to avoid frostbite and navigate their surroundings. Thaumaturgy will be invaluable. Maybe levinbolt, technomancy and conjuring can substitute for manufacturing and provide electricity, security and other modern conveniences. A combination of lure of flames and neptune's might could be used for reshaping ice into structures, perhaps? Dominate, chimestry and presence can be employed to keep the inhabitants, kindred and kine - happy, well behaved and occupied. If you can find a way to manipulate the nearby volcanoes (maybe with the way of fire or something) you might be able to get geothermal heating.

              The lack of civilisation means that, in case of Gehenna, there are unlikely to be any ancients in the area, nor will the area be of much interest to rivals.
              The defenses will include ghouled orcas on patrol around the continent because making the already sadistic murder dolphins high on the essence of murder sounds darkly hilarious.

              Food:
              The massive downside here is keeping the kine alive, it is definitely not suitable for the human species and bunkers may be hard to maintain, as well as the food requirements. Ghouling them would require vitae. Revenants may be hardier (and longer lived to boot), but may be hard to get enough of them to keep a stable population for feeding. Perhaps enough uses of vicissitude combining traits of the cold-resistant local fauna with humans could do something to stop the humans dying from frostbite?

              And no amount of ghouling will help with the fact that the herds need a food supply. Can plants be ghouled? Two dots in the green path might help, since we would not be at the mercy of time to get edible crops. Speed the Season's Passing, being magic, possibly does away with the requirement for fertile soil. Grab a potato, magic it, get about ten potatoes. Repeat. Job done. (Honestly, the usurpers have been sleeping on the untapped potential of agricultural rituals.)
              Unless you find a way to make them cold resistant, vampires may need to hunt and fish for their herds themselves. Or pay a gangrel to do it. Or train animal ghouls to do it.

              How much blood do whales have, for feeding purposes?

              Antarctica does get tourists and military and researchers who come and go, if some of them go missing now and then and happen to end up in the herds, well, it's a harsh environment. You can also feed on the research bases rather than an established herd from time to time, and if they say anything they'll make terrible witnesses considering scientists stationed at these places tend to go loopy anyway. And kine that escape the herd will die on the ice long before they find anyone to tell.

              Edit: Alternative feeding method: take a thinblood (who cannot form bonds), fleshcraft it so that it has no senses or limbs and is nothing more than a fleshy sack with a hole you can pour blood into. Pour animal blood in, bleed vitae out. Serve to the local population.
              Keep a thinblood larder.
