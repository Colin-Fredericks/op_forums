        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

        Gotcha. That puts things into a better perspective. I think Matthew Dawkins shares the view as in his Inconnu thread he talks about how weird it is for Caine to be taken at face value when Cainites older than Judaism are a fact.
        Judaism didn’t just appear out of a vacuum and the Torah was written down from a transmitted oral tradition that pre-dated it by at least centuries (based on structure the oldest section of the Torah is believed to be the wording of the song the Israelites sang after they escaped Egypt - “I will sing to the Lord who is gloriously triumphant. Horse and chariot He has cast into the sea.”).

        Similarly, the Flood story seems to be based on a shared oral tradition related to the 100+ meter rise in global sea levels at the end of the last Ice Age c. 14,000 years ago (not coincidentally this is the same time frame as the supposed destruction of Atlantis and the inundation of the inhabited Dogerland and Sundaland regions).

        The track of humanity in the form of expulsion from the Garden to the east, then Caine’s exile further east yet and a return to the Levant region after the Flood also tracks the migration pattern of early humans out of Africa to the Middle East then to Asia with a post Ice Age migration back west by the ancestors of the present natives in the Levant.

        The point is that the notion that the biblical stories recorded in the Torah didn’t just spring up and though aspects of it are wrapped up in the specific religious interpretations of the time it was written down, there is enough of the early stories that aligns with prehistoric migration patterns to believe that the broadest strokes are actually recounting real history as filtered through oral tradition.

        Thus, while the events involving Cain might not have happened exactly as written in Genesis (the story is being related by those claiming Seth as their ancestor), but it could be a recounting of a prehistoric conflict that drove “Cain” (an individual or group associated with farming) out of the region after “Abel” (a subset of the people “Adam” associated with herding) was destroyed and the rise of “Seth” from the people “Adam” to replace those losses.

        In a World of Darkness context this outcast or outcast group could be the first vampire(s) and thus, it makes as much sense as any to call the first of that group “Caine” in the same way we refer to our earliest identified common female ancestor as “Mitochondrial Eve.”

        In other words, even if their name wasn’t actually Caine (and they could have been a woman), a term for the First Vampire would be needed to discuss the concept and, at least in Western civilization, that name for them may as well be “Caine.”

                        Werewolf was its own theology altogether. Mage was about Consensual reality altogether too, and Changeling was similar to Mage in this regard aswell; humans and/or strong concentrated thought-lulls in general formed reality through collective thought for the most part. Wraith since the get-go was always nebulous in a good way and kept things open. Then by KotE White Wolf was getting together and was making its own original IC cosmology onto itself, taking some elements, but not making real world religions or "one central truth" to the game. Then Hunter: Reckoning came along. It had heavy Christian themes on the surface level, but in the same breadth was heavily connected to KotE through a few major characters in the lore of that game via the Messengers.

                        Then D:tF came and sledged all of that like I already discussed in many other threads in the past before. And yes, D:tF is very "pro-Christian"; they literally made certain through Lucifer that Christianity was made by him from the shadows to fight against the Earthbound; who for some reason all have inhabited "key objects" in certain places and certain times, to ostensibly become the puppet-masters of every single non-Abrahamic religion. The D:tF books say that what Lucifer made-up for the books in oWoD-verse Christianity were basically recountings of the Fallens' war against the Host, with a few modifications. So basically from an IC-meta standpoint, Christianity is truest religion in the franchise apparently; because remember, the Fallen were apparently the "creators of the entirety of the oWoD universe":

                        (despite the fact they know nothing of the Fera (ESPECIALLY about knowing nothing of the Mokolè, the oldest splat that were presumably active during the Triassic Era), haven't heard of any of the ephemeral Umbras (Middle & High Umbrae specifically), they don't know anything about any Mage factions, nothing about knowing the Yama Kings who also apparently worked for god, but were never sent to the "Abyss", etc.).

                        D:tF is an example of what NOT to do when regards to honing only one explicit narrative. Same point carrying over to why its nonsensical to just full implant Cainite vampires wholesale and just retcon out the Wan Kuei, which by itself would hemorrhage a huge amount of oWoD connected cosmology and lore. It's also not as if there ISN'T any Cainite Vampires in Asia to begin with, there is.
