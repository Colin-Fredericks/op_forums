                                        For reference, I really know my stuff with Vampire combat.

                                        Short Answer: 8th Generation with 100 years experience as Vampire (equivalent of an Ancilla).

                                        Long Answer: 8th Generation allows 3 blood to be spent per turn (for healing and pumping blood) and that helps a lot. And if the Vampire (or the player) knows how to fight well with the game mechanics, taking out a Werewolf should be doable without silver. With silver, it's easier but we aren't considering disciplines yet. The Werewolf doesn't have to clinch/hold in order to bite for aggravated because he has a snout like a dog. And he already has Crinos claws. Knowing this, we should give the Werewolf respect and recognize he is a combat machine from the get-go.

                                        The most important things against a Werewolf are either silver or real combat experience. Powers are useful but if you can't dodge or you aren't wearing armor, you're probably dead.

                                        If you have 3 in any of the physical disciplines, that's a start. Celerity is the best against this foe, but if you're holding silver, a lot of Potence could do him in if you're willing to take a hit or two of aggravated and risk death. Fortitude will only be useful if you have high levels of it against a Werewolf since 99% of his attacks will be aggravated.

                                        Not including level 5 powers below

                                        Obfuscate is going to be a boon if you can sneak up on him. Level 4 might allow you to disappear or be harder to hit.
                                        Serpentis will give you aggravated soak and a powerful mesmerize ability so you can knife him with your silver dagger without him resisting.
                                        Obtenebration will give you more attacks and more strength, and possibly reduce his stamina which will be INVALUABLE, as well as blinding him.
                                        Protean will give you aggravated damage.
                                        Presence 2 can reduce their dice pools.
                                        Dominate 1 & 2 could stop him in his tracks IF you make eye contact in combat.
                                        Auspex can enhance your Perception checks to notice things but not a lot else unless you want to read his mind (which is usually not helpful in combat)
                                        Dementation can essentially make his decisions a little stupider in combat because he'll be freaking out from phantom sensory input.
                                        Chimerstry could be incredibly useful for imaginative players. Illusions work wonders.
                                        Vicissitude will only be helpful if you have already prepared.
                                        Thaumaturgy is a great weapon if you use it right (each Path differs greatly in usefulness)
                                        Quietus is deadly. Just straight up deadly. I would happily take this over many of the others.


                                        Almost any level 5 power will give you a great advantage. But... if he has some great Werewolf Gifts, it's back to square one and you're on even footing again as an experienced 8th Generation.

                                        If you have an older Vampire with worse generation, then the advantages grow with Disciplines and Abilities but they drop off with pumping blood and healing. So you will want better physical stats to start off with since you can't pump blood as fast or heal as fast. If you are a terrible generation as a Vampire but you have combat experience, it really pays off to roll 8-10 dice when attacking because you're easily able to target the eyes and cause blindness. That will make them retreat but they just heal and come back. You need to chop off a leg. You need to blind him. If you have good stats, chop off his god damn leg. Don't just hit him, you're good enough to aim for a particular body part. So take it out. Keep him from running away. Blind him. Blast his teeth out with your shotgun. Remove his ability to act.

                                        If you are of a strong generation with less experience, you need to really tussle it out physically as your blood pumping is your greatest weapon. You have gotta grapple with him so he can't bite you. Then you gotta bite him.

                                        All of this changes if you have silver because that becomes your main line of attack. Tremere can levitate silver powder from a pouch and toss it into the Werewolfs fur, clothes, etc. That kind of messes them up. It can shackle their abilities.

                                        Vampires live longest when they know how to avoid combat so your best bet is to not fight if you have the option because Werewolves are capable opponents from the start. You never fight a capable opponent. You always have someone else do it. Next suggestion is to bring lots of help WITH SILVER if you do fight them. And third, if you're alone, either be extremely adept with brawling (he's gonna get close no matter what), or have a Discipline/Weapon that can stop him in his tracks or hinder his ability to hit you.

                                        Go for the legs. Go for the eyes. Be good at grappling. Use silver. BE GOOD AT DODGING.

                                            One advantage Vampires also have if you use the Crossover rules from older editions, is that according to these rules you usually need a power of the same level or higher to counter another power.

                                            Which means to see for example a Vampire with Obfuscate 5, a Werewolf would need a Level 5 Gift, and most Werewolfs don't have the rank necessary to get level 5 Gifts, while every Vampire can learn level 5 Disciplines.


                                            If we really just look at the Generation as factor, and mostly ignore disciplines and gifts, the vampire needs probably to be at least 6 Gen or lower, at this point he can pump his attributes to levels similar of what a Werewolf gets from his shapes, which should give him and advantage if he also raises his skills above 5. But even then he probably still need a little bit Celerity, armor and silver weapon, to level the playing field. It depends of course also on the edition, since the rules for multi attacks always change. But it would probably still a tough fight.
                                            If you take disciplines into consideration, a Vampire has potentially access to some discipline powers, that could him let take out a most Werewolf quite easily.
