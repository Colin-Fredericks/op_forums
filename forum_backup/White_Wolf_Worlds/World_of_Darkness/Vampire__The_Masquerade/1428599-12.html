    Originally posted by Black Fox View Post

    I hear you.
    ​
    I've said before that I don't like metaplot as the reason for publishing sourcebooks because it forces people to play in one particular chronicle style. I prefer an approach where sourcebooks are toolkits that allow people to play the game they wanted. I think one of the beauties of the original V1/V2 sourcebooks is that they did that. The concepts of the Camarilla and Sabbat (and other concepts) allowed people to roleplay a wide variety of vampire types.

    Whatever its merits, it seems V5 does not. And its a weakness.

    And I say this as someone who has never liked Necroscope style Vampires and eliminate Vicissitude entirely in the chronicles I ST around 20 years ago, and thus changed the Tzimisce into something I suspect is very much like V5's version. But that doesn't mean I don't understand that others do like the Discipline and the Clan's portrayal for decades, and they don't like how the game no longer supports that.

    The game's setting is breaking as a result of these metaplots. Each time they introduce retroactive continuity, and there are just too many retroactive continuity changes required to make it work. The setting is increasingly being damaged. It would not surprise me in some future V6 or V7 that the next group of developers decide the original seven clans of the Camarilla should rejoin, the Tzimisce (and other clans) be restored to what they were, and a “back to the basics” attempt is made to restore the setting; or that the merry-go-round approach to sects continues and we get yet another selection of clans of the Camarilla or even more bizarre changes.

    I think a better approach is to define the "core" elements of the Vampire setting, and realize there are now (at least) 3 different versions of it. Those different versions are simply examples of what one can do with the core setting, but shouldn't define them. Players, of course, have had even more variations in their home chronicles. In my opinion, it would be better of the game company to divide their published material into a core book dealing with mechanics and the core setting, and then a variety of approved "metaplot/edition/variations" sourcebooks that explore the different ways to approach it. Each of those visions can still be supported - especially now in a world with PDFs and print on demand versions don't require game stores to physically stock everything. Canon Elements of Vampire: the Masquerade (that is present in all incarnations; this is what V20 attempted to provide at least in its corebook)
    • 13 clans divided into the Camarilla, Sabbat, and Independents with individuals also belonging to Anarchs and other minor sects
    • Camarilla as the dominant faction
    • Jyhad / Manipulation by Methusalehs
    • ​Antedeluvians are a mystery
    Elements of the first era of Metaplot (Greenberg – V1/V2)
    • Multi-layered Jyhad – Anarchs vs Prince, Prince vs Primogen, Prince vs Camarilla, some Anarchs against the Camarilla as an institution, Camarilla vs Sabbat, True Hand versus False Hand, Gehenna Cults; elder Vampires remaining in contact with past friends in rival sects
    • Lots of unknown secrets – True Brujah, fate of the Cappadocians, nature of the Salubri
    • Vicissitude as an Umbral Disease
    • Lots of Bloodlines with mysterious origins
    • Sympathetic vampires struggling with their condition a common element (perhaps too much)
    • Encouragement of crossover with the rest of the WoD
    • Often prone to gonzo elements at the highest level
    Elements of the second era of Metaplot (Achilli - Rev)
    • Sect politics most important element
    • Sect conflicts along the lines of a military campaign
    • Gehenna imminent so there are major changes to the setting to reflect that countdown
    • Golconda is a lie or at least a disappointing reality
    • Stronger emphasis that deviating from Humanity feeds the beast combined with belief vampires can't really follow Humanity to any large degree over time
    • The events that happen in the metaplot essentially destroys the Masquerade, but the setting refuses to admit it
    • Vicissitude now just an ordinary Discipline
    • Clans have many sub-clans with separate mechanics
    • Elimination of the elements of previous metaplots that conflict with the new style
    • No crossover with the rest of the WoD
    Elements of the third era of Metaplot (V5) / I admit this is my impression from hearsay as I've never read any V5 material
    • Worldwide Wrestling/Soap Opera script-based approach to vampire politics - Clans change sect; clans split up; bloodlines join up to become one clan
    • Very event driven in the sense that changes to the core setting are done just previous to Players playing in the game; these aren't events players participate in
    • More humane vampires an option again, but generally most vampires depicted as evil
    • Streamlining of mechanics while allowing more options, and these mechanical changes impact the setting accordingly
    • Nostalgia for certain characters and ideas found in earlier editions that show again in more profound ways
    • Elimination of the elements of previous metaplots that conflict with the new style
    I'm sure others would articulate a different set of bullet points, but I think there is general agreement that these are very different styles of play, and that each subsequent version applied retroactive continuity changes to eliminate certain things in the previous edition and to set up unique things in their version.

    These are really three separate incarnations of the game's core setting. They're not really continuations of the prior version even though they make reference to many of the same characters and concepts. It's like the old Earth-1, Earth-2, Earth-3 and so on of DC's Pre-Crisis superhero comics. Each one has its own version of "Superman" (or whatever character) with its own lore and continuity, but they are not the same character.

    While I have my preferences, I have to admit that I'd probably have fun playing in any of these kind of chronicles once. But I find it distasteful that I'm told this is the way the setting will be going forward permanently (until, of course, another version comes around that invalidates this version).

    Of course, my own version played at my tables has elements from all of these as well as many things uniquely derived just from me. And the same goes for many other home chronicles of other players. They're compatible with the "core" setting - just not these metaplot separate interpretations.

    What I am looking for our tools that allow me to run the kind of vampire chronicle I want, as well as the occasional look at how others run their games so I can be inspired and steal new ideas. I don't want to be told to radically change my chronicle in order to accommodate the new metaplot setting - no matter how cool or interesting I may find it as a whole or as parts. I don't like the idea that new material seems to invalidate my own ideas.

    Game companies love metaplot because it's an easy way to get the fanbase to buy new sourcebooks that provides the revenue treadmill. However, I think that's an illusion. Because what ultimately happens is that fans of the game's previous incarnations simply stop supporting the game line at all and retreat back to their already purchased books. So after the initial rush of sold material, the company now has a much smaller fanbase buying materials than it did before.
    This broadly reflects my view, too. I liked how M20 presented conflicting metaplots as Future Fates, for example, giving us more, rather than less, choice. V5 definitely gives us less choice than V20.

    I like the Dragons as Old Clan Tzimisce. But it is reductive to make that the whole experience, rather than allowing for the breadth that was there before. Dracula is fine, and all, but Vykos is the signature Tzimisce character and should, therefore, be represented as a more viable choice than they are presently. Vykos was always more central to the game than Dracula.

    Discipline-wise, I'd give traditional Tzimisce:

    Animalism
    Auspex
    Blood Sorcery (optional swap: Protean)

    The Old Clan would swap Blood Sorcery for Dominate. Now you have all the different options again.

    But the more I engage with V5 as it is, the more I keep thinking I should finish my homebrew 'classic' VTM ruleset. V20 was very close to where the ideal VTM Classic would be, but still needed some fixes that only started to be hinted at late in the V20 line (e.g., V20DA's simplified combat).

                                                        That's just not what the game is about, though. Never has been. There's no gothic horror in unflappably accepting "oh I'm a bloodthirsty murderous monster, sweet."
