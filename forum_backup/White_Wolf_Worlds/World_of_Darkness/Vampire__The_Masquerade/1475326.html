    How would the other supernatural's react to a vampire Path of Karma (sadhana)

    02-23-2022, 12:54 PM
    I absolutely love path of karma. I made a post on how this was the most insane blood sorcery path on reddit too, but I want to hear your opinions.

    Here's a site on that details the abilities of this path> https://mowcadah.proboards.com/threa...ana-path-karma

    The comments I did get on the post are pretty informative as well> https://www.reddit.com/r/WhiteWolfRP...path_of_karma/

    This path allows you to view some ones past life, their current life's past, and their future. At higher levels the sadhu (Sadhana practitioner) can see his past life, they can even make someone relive their past life and use knowledge from their past lives. At the last level the sadhu in question can manipulate their own and someone else's next reincarnation.

    This could change the face of the supernatural society as we know it.

    Just think about it!!!!!!! :O

    How do you think other splats would react to this power?

    Would the euthanos see this as perverting the cycle? Would the other traditions see this as dangerous weapon? What about the technocracy, would they try to destroy it, or try to learn it? Would the kraft's even care? Reincarnation seems to be something extremely hard to learn if it doesn't align with your beliefs.

    Would the fera see this as the wyrm upping its game? Would the more scholarly try to understand and learn this power?

    How would the sabbat react? The cam? The anarchs? Would the Followers of set see this as a way to bring back set? Vampire society as a whole?

    Fae?

    How would this knowledge affect the WOD as a whole?


    I'm not grilling you, this is just to get you thinking

    Tell me what you think.

      Okay so here are my thought meta wise:
      • The path is redundant. My issue with Revised, and older editions, is the massive number of disciplines and paths that are the same thing just slightly reworded. This path is effectively a highly specialized Auspex mixed with elements of Dementation. Both of them can already see a target's past/future in different ways, so this power is already out classed by two common disciplines.
      • The path is useless in a lot of narrative ways. Reincarnation doesn't come up a lot in World of Darkness and is a highly debated religious topic in and out of game. For example, what does picking a person's next incarnation actually do? Both in and out of game, that could literally be the most epic/horrific thing to do, or just do literally nothing. It's highly situational and will never see a true resolution in a standard game of VTM, narratively or mechanically.
      • Trying to tell the future in TTRPG sessions is very difficult, and it's this whole ability's thing. You'll get completely different results.
      With all that out of the way, how would other supernaturals react? Well, here's a quick list:
      • Fera. Sadhana, as a discipline line, is very weird. It's a blood sorcery that actively challenges religious ideas and philosophy of the region, but that's not inherently wyrm-like. That said, the fact that vampires practice it would immediately make the western Fera look upon it as a twisted practice and seek to rid the world of it.
      • Mages. Mages would not care. The Avatar is linked to the soul, so any incarnation of the mage would have the possibility to awaken. Not only that, but mages with the right knowledge of the spheres could do the same thing.
      • Wraiths. This path means nothing to them. Many would take it as a sign that there's hope of escaping their hell hole of a world but are not benefited from it at all. It could arguably give them some hints of how to transcend, but overall unlikely.
      • Fey. Vampires smell benign, and this specifically smells benign as all hell. Don't approach.

                                      About the Mage side of reincarnation. While the particulars (what is the soul, if the identity it's the same, etc...) are up to debate, Mage has a long story and many rules regarding to some level of reincarnation. In particular, even if the "soul" (whatever that is, does it even exist or are wraiths merely reflections of a past being cast onto the Umbra?) doesn't reincarnate, the Avatar most certainly does.

                                      Now, how much the Avatar it's the soul, part of the soul, or a wholy alien entity ... it's up to debate. Actually, Mage doesn't talk much about the soul (and when it does, it's often the Avatar). A few pointers:

                                      *There's a Background that let's you access your "past lives" knowledge, the knowledge of the previous owners of your Avatar (aptly called Past Lives) and raise or use skills related to them.

                                      *As said, Avatars always chose new hosts. This is particularly dramatic with Nephandi, whose Inverted Avatars unavoidably corrput the new host into a new Nephandi.

                                      *The Gilgul rite it's the most dire punishment of mage society, it "kills" the avatar ending the capability of the user to do magick, and supposedly, stop "Avatar reincarnation" shenanigans like Nephandi Avatars corrupting other people. However, Lore has one counter example: Heylel Teomim's bizarre Avatar was Gilguled for the crime of betraying the First Cabal yet in the Horizon Wars someone appeared who supposedly had inherited Heyel's Avatar and legacy (then again, Heye's Avatar and persona were very special. I wouldn't say categorically that it doesn't work.. At the very least, it does seem to delay that reincarnation enough for mages to think it does work)

                                      *One of the reason given for the "himalayan wars" to be so bloody was the past lives. As the Wiky says: "The dead on both sides reincarnated, returning as newly-Awakened children and youths still bearing old grudges". It's noteworthy that the two factions involved believe in reincarnation, so that may have affected tings. And the event it's vague enough to suspect how much of that was the belief, or actual magic being casted. Even if it was just nature, one could still say that's something Avatars can do and not that reincarnation it's a thing .

                                      As for how they would "react". For some it would be as natural as breathing that this would be feasible, as per the himalayan wars. For others it would be alien and thus would rather offer different explanations of why the power works (The soul?, NAH, it's just your run of the mill manipulation of time and causality).

                                      Most of the Path it's just Time 2. The level 5th it's when it becomes interesting for Mages, since one would need high levels of Entropy, Time, Spirit and perhaps Life and Prime, to replicate it (and Paradox, a lot of that). The limitation of the target being a "mortal" it's a bit annoying because most splats don't count as "mortal" in the vampire sense of the word (it tends to mean "human" rather than just "can die" - it almost certainly means human in this particular power albeit an ST could rule otherwise), but still it's very powerful.

                                      As for how to kill it: Mages have been known to kill (if not permanently at least in a very lasting way) immortal things that have unusual souls and "reincarnate" with perfect memory and all their powers in their own regenerated bodies (read, Mummies which can be perma-killed with Spirit 5/Prime 5). So I would imagine that stopping reincarnation wouldn't be impossible (and may be easier, even), but since it's a rather esoterical skill most likely it wouldn't even be tried (and most mages wouldn't know how to do it, even if the had the rigth Spheres in the first place).

                                      From the Vampire side. What about Diablerie? Diablerie eats the soul. If that work, it's almost laugably easy. It may even be tried w/o knowing that the vampire has this power.

                                      However, maybe, I'm not 100% sure but perhaps this power could work anyway in spite of your soul, if it has a Buddhist way of understanding the thing. I don't know a lot about the subject, but I think Buddhists don't believe that you have an "inmortal" shiny ego that moves from body to body. So, their concept of reincarnation could be unrelated to the soul. I don't know enough about the religion to say that's the case, but I wanted to toss it out anyway.

                                              Most other splats already deal with reincarnation or have well established deals with their own afterlife. This path doesn't really adds a lot to them.

                                              Fera and Werewolves: I wouldn't say they're dumb, that's a really narrow view on things, and is you think they're simple you know nothing at all about them. IIRC their spirits usually go to a known afterlife at their Ancestral Lands in the Umbra, and they have hard evidence on this because contacting ancestors is common enough to be a Background, also available through rites and Gifts. Yet they may reincarnate under a Totem's intervention, and even under some rare and specific Gifts, so while a similar power isn't common for them, it is far from a challenge on their beliefs;

                                              Mages: Due to the Avatar, most Mages already believe in reincarnation anyways, with the details varying a lot between factions. And the thing is, since they already have an explanation for what they see with the Avatars, they'll simply see this power as an interference in whatever they already believe. And they're Mages, interfering with things is kind of their thing. Dealing with souls to this degree is... covered in different aspects by some Spheres like Spirit, Mind, Time, Life, Prime and Entropy. Different combinations can give a Mage the means to cover the general idea in different ways;

                                              Changelings: They ALL factually reincarnate. The Changeling Way is an undefined ritual that literally any Fae can enact to become an immortal, reincarnating mix of Fairy and Human soul, and they all underwent it. Remembering past lives is a Background for them (for Mages too, btw), recovering the name of their original incarnation and its history is literally their Coming of Age ritual. This power do not means much to them. They also know already how to end Changeling souls definitely and are unlikely to even care about ending a vampire's soul for good, this is just not their problem;

                                              Mummies: Reincarnation is their whole shtick. While for Changelings it is a fact of life without much effect on game outside Past Lives, for Mummies it is their main mechanic during play. Since they're being empowered that way by a hugely powerful ritual it is unlikely that such a power would even affect them, but if it did it would just be a minor problem for them, as the worse it can do is to kill them again in their next life, then they just come back again;

                                              Demons: Not even death itself is a thing for them, much less reincarnation. They're already pure spirits that possess people and can jump from body to body if they need to. Being sent to Hell isn't death for them either, and they can be easily summoned back to Earth. While there are ways to deter them (not particularly hard in comparison to other splats, either), the concept of death itself simply does not apply and reincarnation isn't necessary, as they can always come back again and again unless they're destroyed as souls, which doesn't seems to be something this ritual can undo;

                                              Vampires: Yes, even the very ones must be spoken about. While being able to reincarnate would certainly be a blessing for many a vampire, they're immortal undead. Dying and reincarnating still means dying first, and losing the entire potency of your blood in the process. You'll come back after some time, without much memories, as a living baby. What use is it for a vampire? What kind of great advantage does it grant? It is certainly good to have such a back-up plan for worst case scenario, but it barely scraps things for worst case scenario. The biggest advantage is for vampires that actually want to go back to being mortals, as now you can offer them release from undeath. It is the game where thee impact is the greatest, but is still limited.

                                              About the Week of Nightmares: The spirit side of the nukes (it was at least three of them) didn't do much because it wasn't meant to do much anyway. There were lots of other things in the area, Werewolves, Mages and other things, and none of the survivors of the blast itself were affected in a weird way by the Spirit factor. What the Spirit factor did was to help with the actual spirits in the area (that was a royal mess), and maybe affect some supernatural powers in effect. The nukes were meant to work because they're nukes.

                                              It didn't helped with the clouds, either, although the Technocracy does believe it did. The clouds were gone because the Kuei-Jin that summoned them perceived that Ravnos was stunned enough by the nukes and that the Mages had the solar mirrors already in place (how the fuck she knew, I have no idea), so she decided to dismiss the whole effect. The whole affair is somewhat confuse, but it is described in the Time of Thin Blood book with a reasonable amount of details, most such theories come from people that didn't read it first hand (that book is from 1999, a lot of current players weren't even born back then. I've reread my copy recently precisely to discuss about the WoN).
