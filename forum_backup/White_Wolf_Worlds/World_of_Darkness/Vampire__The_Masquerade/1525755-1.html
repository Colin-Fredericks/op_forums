    Originally posted by Ulisses View Post

    Good Lord, Vampire The Requiem 2th is the best vampire tabletop game made, my real dream? Take Rosa Bailey and Matthew Dawnkins em give them total criative control to create Vampire The Masquerade 6° edition (with base rules of VtR 2th adaptation to Masquerade scenario)

    Forget V5

    This is the perfect world for all fandom of Vampire rpg.
    The problem with importing the logic of Requiem into Masquerade is that Masquerade is not simply a vampire (TT)RPG: it is a cultural artefact that has reached a certain level of aesthetic and thematic stability in the three decades since the release of the game's first edition; to put it simply, Masquerade does not share its "vibe" with Requiem or (to my knowledge) any other vampire RPG on the market. It's just not possible to 'update' Masquerade without confronting and - this is important - working within the established conceits of the setting.

    I'm basically content to resign myself to pre-2015 editions of the game, but: in order for me to invest my resources in any future edition of Masquerade, it must be willing to engage with the world and style of the entire V:tM line beyond the particular tastes of singular players who had the luck to find themselves at the writer's desk; it must seek to expand player options instead of limiting them, in awareness of the different playing styles preferred by separate sections of the community; and do away with excessive nostalgia for the oldest editions of the game at the expense of the rich and diverse setting it has developed since '91.

                    I cannot agree with this logic, honestly: the classic VtM system badly needed a rules overhaul, if it was to be relaunched - otherwise, a 5th edition would have really been just a soulless money grab. V20 already gave us compiled and streamlined "classic" rules, without significant modifications: but between V1 and V5 stood 17 years of evolution of game design.

                    The NWOD / COFD lines have developed their own identity in time, but both their rules and their themes are rooted in a re-imagination of the original WOD. To adopt their best practices and ideas, after years of testing, is just sound game design. That was nonetheless supplemented by many other original innovations.

                    Bridging this divide with an inter-operable system and a setting which was more flexible and would allow porting of good ideas was a DnD5e-like operation, which I always felt was a good idea: the problem is, they hoped to close edition wars between OWod and CofD, but instead another edition war between Wod5e and Wod20 flared, and I feel this ultimately contributed to killing the CofD. After 19 years and 2 iterations, Chronicles lines had basically published everything a toolbox setting could support, they would have needed a 3rd edition. That however, would have split both the fanbase and marketing budgets way too much.

                            I don't trust the current leadership at Paradox, the current in-house WOD-team or Renegade to really steer this ship to success but if I alone were in charge, what I would wish for would be this:

                            For V5:
                            • Setting-books. Chicago by Night (and the other affiliated books plus Fall of London) are the only setting-books we've got so far. One of the hallmarks of the Renegade-material has been a very generalist approach to the setting. And whenever things get specific you get fleeting glimpses of worldbuilding that are often awkward, baffling or just straight-up nonsense. It would be nice if a Renegade-supplement actually delved into portraying a specific city/region and trying to do some genuine worldbuilding.
                            • Clan-lore. Some have a good amount of lore in V5 but others have barely any lore in V5. Doesn't matter if it contradicts old lore, just actually have lore for how to play Clans and portray them in a game. The current approach isn't giving freedom to players, it's just laziness. The concepts are way too specific to hand them to people at the people with these half-assed presentations. Of course, this means re-treading similar paths to previous editions but V5 wasn't written with a distinct enough vision to not do that. (And both H5 and W5 have suffered in the clumsy and poorly thought-out attempts to divorce those two splats from previous editions.)
                            Beyond that for WOD5E:
                            • It's time to just admit that WOD5E has failed. There was a moment to make WOD5E happen and it was around the time of the release of V5. That moment was squandered due to scandal and it all has gone downhill since then. Especially since the videogame-side was similarly cursed.
                            • Instead of re-building some sort of mega-brand with broad appeal, it's time to just meet the fanbase where it is. CofD 3rd edition, WOD 30th Anniversary, WOD6E... all of it will be barely profitable but hey It's something... Clinging to the idea of making WOD5E happen is just a road to nowhere at this point.

                                                            It IS just a soulless money grab.
                                                            I am willing to die on that hill
                                                            You know why the Chicago by Night 5th Edition and its supplements or anything basically Onyx Path Publishing made was a HUGE success?
                                                            Kickstarter​
                                                            Becasue everything they made was made for the gamers, gave them what they want for their money, not showed everything and the kitchen sink Paradox decided the players want down their throath - or at least tried - and failed misreably
                                                            But tried to grab as much cash as inhumanly possible nontheless and still not willing to admit their mistakes and pumping out 5th edition didasters after disasters

                                                            RPG companies at the end are still companies, and have to make money. The IP acquisition was motivated of course by the idea that they would make a "Cyberpunk 2077" move with Bloodlines 2, but the Paradox CEO of the time was also a long-time gamer of WOD, and personally invested in the setting.
                                                            ​


                                                            The treadmill of the 90s too could be called a money grab, and it gave us a very deep setting. It wasn't soulless.

                                                            The Wod20 editions could be called a money grab based on nostalgia as well. But they were works of love, gave us updated setting and rules, and restarted the classic Wod. They weren't soulless.

                                                            ​
                                                            As for the gamer argument ... Martin Ericsson is a gamer, Jason Carl is a gamer, Outstar is a gamer, they all are gamers. It is also frequently forgotten that Matthew Dawkins and OPP freelancers worked a lot on V5, and that Matthew was Creative Lead Designer for a while and its basically firsty brand ambassador, as the Gentleman Gamer.

                                                            And yet, Wod5e took their risks in changing rules and metaplot. Had it been soulless, it would have looked much different and more conservative. Wod5e was realized by gamers and long time fans, helped by experienced game designers and historical authors and freelancers. Had it been a soulless money grab showing everything down the troath of old time fans, they wouldn't have started the Storytellers Vault.

                                                            That said, editorially speaking, Wod5e is not a disaster: it has re-entered icv2 charts various times, coming in the top5, and it has wide international distribution agreements. It's also doing fairly well on Drivethrurpg. Kickstarter by Onyx Paths realized amazing products, whose reprint as physical editions has powerfully propelled Wod5e in the icv2 charts again, last Fall: but had not been for Paradox deals and Renegade distribution, they would just have stayed wonderful books acquired by ... 1000 or so Kickstarter backers, and later on an equivalent number of people on Drivethrurpg.

                                                            To get my italian V20 distribution back again, another publishing house had to publish V5 ... and this propelled back interest in the franchise and moved the other publisher to print more V20 books.

                                                            Nowadays, the WOD is widely available in a huge number of countries, languages and publishing formats, and also supports extensive fan modding. This, by itself, it's awesome.

                                                            Unfortunately, Wod5e has failed in many of its original ambitions (uniting fandom, having a Cyberpunk 2077 moment, having a Dnd 5e big recognition...), and even though commercially speaking is doing well, it's just a sidenote on Paradox balance sheets.

                                                            This, I think, is sad, missed chance: for a brief moment we had important designers, committed fans, long-time stewards of the franchise, and an international distribution network with huge transmedia ambitions working together to bring the Wod to its formery glory. It didn't work as intended.
