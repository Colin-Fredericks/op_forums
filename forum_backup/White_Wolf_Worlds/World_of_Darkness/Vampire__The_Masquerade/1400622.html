                      My answer would be that vampires and garou both hold long grudges.

                      The Tzimisce are immortal, hand down traditions, and view themselves as the rightful and absolute overlords of their lands. They would have no patience for competition and would carry grudges for centuries if not millennia once slighted. Just ask the Tremere or the Ventrue.

                      The Shadow Lords are a tribe of garou, who are tradition by nature with strong oral histories and able to literally access their ancestors. Simultaneously they see themselves as divine champions on a religious crusade against creatures of corruption, including vampires, and the eventual pact with Kupala by Tzimisce fiends would not help matters.

                      Given their natures it's almost inevitable there would have been a conflict. From then on it's centuries of back and forth guerrilla warfare. Tzimisce pledge to uphold the honor of their lineage and destroy Shadow Lords. Shadow Lords swear to avenge their ancestors and destroy packs of Tzimisce. The battle continues nigh on eternally, with the occasional pragmatic armistice to break things up.

                      Hey they might both be ruthless, vicious, and willing to hold a grudge until they're last moment on earth; but some times you've just got other priorities.

                      Like peasant uprisings. Or famines. Or Mongols.

                                      I would think the general depiction of the war is actually one of prolonged, but sporadic conflicts that flare up from time to time, but with long periods of peace in between. During those flare ups, the Shadow Lords slaughter the vampire neonates, the vampire elders hide, and the Shadow Lords proclaim victory and go home, and the surviving vampire elders get even more powerful.

                                      For the Shadow Lords, victory is killing lots and lots of vampires. And they probably do kill a lot of neonates and some ancilla. For the vampire elders, victory is accomplishing whatever end goal they want regardless of the destruction of all those inferior neonates and ancillae. So both sides declare victory a lot. Since there is no objective perspective, it's hard to say who truly "won".

                                      I think every now and then, the Shadow Lords gain a decisive victory in that they actually kill some elder vampire. Likewise there are some conflicts where the casualties among the Shadow Lords are too excessive for the number of vampires they kill.

                                      Overall, who is ahead depends on what you want the current status quo to be compared to what it was. Since the Tzimisce really got hurt bad during the Anarch Revolt, and many members fled to the New World, the Shadow Lords have probably marginally come out on top for reasons independent of their own actions.

                                                      Originally posted by Rock113 View Post
                                                      It is, well, very strange that one side didn't do something really badass to crush down the other...
                                                      Somehow I wrote a huge post but the power went out and now I'm kind of pissed off, so I'll keep it brief...

                                                      I think that their war was more akin to a "turf war" than a real war. They intensively inhabited the same area, but that's it.
                                                      Lords had the forests and peaks, Tzimisce had cities, castles and sometime peaks too.
                                                      But the area they inhabited wasn't some secluded place or an isle, was basically the gate that separate central europe from eastern europe.

                                                      This means that Tzimisce had other issues. They were meant to be the "royal" Clan in their land (the average Ventrue can be a noble, Tzimisce in DA e before thought themselves a step higher) and to keep the power on the kine they really couldn't devote their time to spar with the wolves. Stay in the cities, maybe let the humans build more cities and then relocate to the new one.
                                                      Let some lesser-breed Kindred (like Nosferatu or Gangrel) or those crazy Kolduns roam the forests, they'll keep the wolves busy and away from the cities and with some luck will get killed as well.
                                                      But the imperative is to control the cities.

                                                      And the Lords have other issues too. Thanks to Kupala and other beings, the spiritual landscape of Dacia/Transylvania is nothing to scoff at. They can't just devote all their times to exterminate bloodsuckers. And why they should? It's a well-known fact that two or more vampires in the same room will try to off each other after some time, unless they find some bigger enemy that unites them. So if the Lords starts an all-out war, the vampires will fight side-to-side instead than trying to kill each other... (exactly what happened in the Chicago War)... and the Lords are more thoughtful than that.
                                                      They pick their battles.
                                                      Kill the vampires that roam the wild areas or those that encroach your sept's interests. Let the other think they are safe in the cities, this will keep all of them together and they will keep killing each other.

                                                      Of course this also mean that can exist some unspoken agreements like "we stay here, you stay there and when we pass each other we both pretend to look the other way", and this is also why the Lords got their reputation of deceitful untrustworthy backstabbers. "After all" the other Garou reason "the leeches are just behind the next hill, why they don't assault them night after night?"
                                                      Because probably they and the Tzimisce will end up both destroyed by the conflict and some other group (the Silver Fang and Ventrue, for example) will just march in the lands taking their due and silencing the last surviving members that dare to speak aloud.

                                                      But even without going with the full-strategic approach they were just scared of each other.
                                                      Tzims know Lupines are strong, hunt in packs and have weird powers the Tzimisce couldn't understand or replicate. And those that replicated them sometime would end from the "weird" to the "completely nuts" (how else can you describe a Tzimisce that lives in secluded areas, at direct contact with... earth? Water? Wind? Whatever she's studying? Kolduns were strong and useful if they survived, but not every Tzim would go for that, not even for those powers).
                                                      If you want to kill the a whole Sept you must march an army of reventants, Vohzd and Slachtza into the forest. You know that most of them will not return and you must hope they are enough to kill every werewolf in that cave you luckily found. And that's because you, the Elder or the Voivode, will certainly not march with them toward certain doom. Of course, you may send some Childer, the expendable ones.
                                                      Then what? Your forces will be severely depleted, and you'll be weakened toward other vampires and voivodes.
                                                      And that doesn't even take into account that you may not have killed all the werewolves, and that may call other wolves from outside to make an example of the weakened you.

                                                      On the other side, the Lord know that the older vampires are so strong that you need whole packs to go toe-to-toe with one, that they have whole broods of younger, weaker, leeches near them and that they have weird flesh-shaping power they can't understand and that maybe only the Wyrm can replicate. They may have some spies (mainly Kinfolk) trying to spy on them, but generally steer away from cities.
                                                      If you want to kill the Voivode, you must assault his castle, where all those horrors dwell. And even if you kill all the horrors, all the weird humans that doesn't scream running away but try to stab you with a spear, all those lesser leeches that somehow seems increasingly armed with silver the more older they become, you still need to go after the Big Bad. How many will survive such assault?
                                                      Then what? Your forces will be severely depleted, and your sept (and maybe the other ones) will be so weakened that Wyrm minions (the real ones, not the unknowing ones) will find much easier to make Caerns fall one after the other.
                                                      And that doesn't even take into account that you may not have killed all the leeches, and that the surviving ones may follow your surviving warriors, find the caerns or (even worse) your Kinfolk, your family and exterminate, maim or enslave them. This means that in one hundred year, that vampire will still be around, and there will not be any new Lord taking your place, while you died in that glorious assault or some years after that trying to defend your Caern from some Fomori.


                                                      So, they were just scared of each other.
                                                      Not because the other was more scary than them, but because the other side was strong enough to fatally cripple them and make them a target for more problematic foes.

                                                      The relationship between Tzimisce and Shadow Lords is just that:
                                                      "You stay on your side of the fence, while I'll stay on mine... and try to move the fence the moment you look away. And in the meantime I'll kill all your friends that pass near my home."
                                                      "Gladly, I'll do the same".
