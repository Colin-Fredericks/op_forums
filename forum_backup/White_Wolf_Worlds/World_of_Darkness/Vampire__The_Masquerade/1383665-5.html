                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                So... back to armor...

                A few house rules ideas were presented, it might be nice if some people with significant play experience with the game could give some feedback on them even if they aren't interested in using them? Is there some rule missed that specifically interacts messily with them?

                Mine was based on the idea that armor is rated 0/2/4/6 in V5. Do any of the supplement have armor rated as an odd number? Over six in a fashion that might imply a Dexterity penalty scheme? Etc.
                No current V5 supplements have any additional armor or weapon mechanics. Everything abides by the core rulebook's material. So there's nothing that gives those odd numbered ratings in an official capacity.

                Some thoughts based on V5's system balance, working from the existing systems and roll methodology:
                * Armor should abide by the rule of '1 minimum damage' in some way. There are very few things that will allow damage to round to 0 (Firewalker) or that negate all damage together (a level 5 Fortitude power). Armor also needs to figure out how to not be redundant with Fortitude (downgrading and reducing), or be overpowered with them stacking.
                * Penalizing only Dexterity will not have the effect that it did in Revised/V20, as Dexterity is much more rarely used in conflict pools (the default use of Dex in conflict only in one-handed melee weapons and raw dodging, which isn't a pool used in every conflict).
                * Weapon damage scales in the manner it does because it's a roll-off system, so damage in and of itself has a scale based on the capabilities of the user, outside of the automatic successes on damage/. On averages, every 2 dots/dice = 1 successes. So if armor negates the damage bonus of weapons, you end up with the characters' raw skill being more important in damage and that will probably skew aspects of the system.
                * The system averages swing as you get more and more dice in a pool (this is why elders get scary with both passive and active dice adds).

                Just running some average numbers on just making armor equal damage reduction, armor would negate the bonuses of weapons plus some, leaving skill and supernatural capabilities as really the 'edge'. Potence would be even more useful than it is now. Fortitude's physical damage mitigation becomes overpowered if it stacks 1 for 1 with Armor damage reduction (someone with Armor 6 and Fortitude 3 is subtracting 9 damage min 1 from all incoming attacks), so you're going to be relying a lot on fishing for critical success. Cutting the amount of damage reduction would perhaps make it more balanced/viable, but basic stacking is going to end up with some levels of functional invincibility in a straight on fight.

                                    Well, it’s not an objective statement because, subjectively, I find the system entirely intuitive. Body armour converts a certain amount of aggravated damage into superficial damage. That is pretty intuitive to me. It’s an opinion, which is valid, because all these qualitative statements about intuition are subjective not objective.

                                    Vampires don’t gain any extra protection from armour against swords, guns, bombs and hammers because none of these things inflict aggravated damage against them. This is because they are vampires - magical undead beings. It’s not a question of being 'too cool for armour’ - which is a strawman argument because nobody suggested this - but a practical aspect of the functional use of armour largely being redundant for them.

                                    There is an argument, however, that vampires could gain some protection from armour against stakes (and arrows), insofar that these things can immobilize a vampire with called shots. It could be ruled (as a house rule) that strong armour might mitigate against this effect. You could also make an argument that body armour could, in theory at least, mitigate against the type of aggravated damage that does affect vampires - like werewolf claws or the like. In these cases, I would rule, that aggravated damage could be reduced to superficial damage in the same way that armour helps mortals against bullets, etc. It probably would have to come down to a house ruling on the matter, but it wouldn’t take a lot of effort or difficulty to do so.

                                    For me, though, the whole debate here is overstated because armour is not something that normal mortals wear most of the time, either in the real world or in the stories I tell, and vampires are generally trying blend in with them in a typical, city based game. Vampires have inbuilt advantages that normal mortals don’t have but mortals (and Thin Bloods) could mitigate against their disadvantages with equipment like body armour, or advanced weapons and tactics of various sorts. This is how I expect The Second Inquisition to work, and is essentially what the rules are trying to be presented as.

                                            Confounding your arguments is not the same thing as being confused, by the way. For a vampire, any damage that is superficial can be instantly repaired - at a rate determined by their Blood Potency - by rousing the blood. Impairment from superficial damage only occurs when the Health levels run out, and the damage suffered is halved, so the risk of disembowlement or a missing arm is somewhat overstated too. It’s superficial, in fact.

                                            So, if a mortal gets shot with a shotgun for, say 8 points of aggravated damage, they are certainly incapacitated and most likely dead. If they wear heavy body armour that converts 6 points to superficial damage, they will probably survive at least, with maybe a bit of aggravated wounds and maybe impairment depending on their Health levels (based on Stamina). They would need medical attention and time to heal those extra Aggravated wounds, in any case.

                                            If a vampire took the same shot, without armour, they would only take 4 points of superficial damage and most likely no impairments unless they were especially low on Health levels. They could then Rouse the blood and instantly heal at least one of those wounds, depending on Blood Potency, and continue to do so each round if they wished.

                                            So, in all, Vampires still have the better deal by far, and armour in this circumstance is largely redundant for them. This is because they are fictional, magical beings. I am certain that a player who knows about such things would appreciate that point.
