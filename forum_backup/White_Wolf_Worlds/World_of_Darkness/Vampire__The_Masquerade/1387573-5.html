            Clanbook: Ventrue (in ultima parte)

            And finishing up.

            The Operative
            This one is a little odd. In one sense, he's a little like the previous military character, just involving spooks and the intelligence community instead of military as a focus for a more physical action oriented Ventrue. This isn't really a bad idea. He worked himself up through the ranks of government surveillance and usually illegal work through a mix of competence and lack of morals, with the hope of eventually becoming one of the upper echelons of the Deep State, the secret masters who control the government regardless of trivial things like elections or laws. And he got his wish, only to find out that the ranks he was joining were the Ventrue and the Camarilla. Now he continues on as before, only with the minor problem that being a Ventrue means that a lot of other vampires automatically suspect him of being up to something. The character is Physical primary, and for some reason has his Dexterity maxed out to five, leaving him three Strength and two Stamina. This doesn't seem bad, just maybe a little odd or imbalanced. Likewise, all of his Social dots are dumped into Manipulation, leaving him with ones in Charisma and Appearance, which against feels odd and out of wack. Mental is more normal with a 3/2/3 split. He's got three dots each in Investigation and Subterfuge, and twos in Alertness, Brawl, Intimidation, Firearms, Stealth, Linguistics, and Politics. I find it rather odd that's he's only got a single dot in Security, and none at all in Computers. You'd think those would be vaguely important for someone who has specifically mentioned as having done electronic surveillance and black bag work. Disciplines are three in Dominate and one in Fortitude. I think this is another case where those seven points would be better served to other ends. I'd probably have gone with 2/1 or 1/2, leaving "brainwashing mastermind" to be something to work up to. He's got three dots in Contacts, two each in Influence and Resources, and one in Generation. This means he has more Influence than any other character in the book (anyone else with it only has the background at one dot), which feels wrong to me. Humanity is six and Willpower is seven. Overall, I like this concept and description, but I feel the stats need to be rethought and reworked a bit.

            The Union Boss
            I suspect that this is, in someways, meant to be an adaption of Jimmy Hoffa. He's one of those old school trade union leaders from around the 60s or 70s, probably from a working class background (though it doesn't specify) who ended up making deals with less reputable types and eventually learned that some of them weren't human. He was going to testify to the government and the FBI-SAD only to end up embraced because he was too useful to kill but too dangerous to let be. However, his sire has recently decided that unions aren't such a useful power base anymore, and so has cut him loose. I find this a rather interesting origin for a Ventrue. Also interesting is that I could easily see this character interacting or even allying with certain types of Brujah or Anarchs. He's Social primary, with four each in Charisma and Manipulation. Abilities are mostly twos, including Brawl, Dodge, Empathy, Intimidation, Leadership, Melee, Bureaucracy, Finance, and Investigation, rounded out with a few ones. This results in a very well rounded character who'll be able to function in several different scenarios and chronicles. He's got one dot in each Discipline plus one extra in Dominate. This is probably another one where those seven points could've been better spent elsewhere. Backgrounds are spread evenly between Contacts, Fame, Resources, and Generation. I think he really should have a couple of dots in Influence. Humanity is eight and Willpower seven. Overall, I like this character (and again one of those I like better than I remember liking it 25 years ago), though I think he could benefit from a few point adjustments.

            The Young Turk
            I think this character is maybe supposed to be a woman, but I honestly couldn't tell. The image is extremely androgynous and the write up is gender neutral. Something of a prodigy, they managed to master the fields of politics, business, and academia, but only in pursuit of the greater goal of the power that comes from behind the scenes of those arenas. At this point, they ended up hindering the manipulations of an elder. This probably would've spelled certain doom had not an ambitious ancilla decided to embrace them before that happened. Now they continue on as before, being highly aggressive and ambitious in the pursuit of power and influence, but being careful not to make unnecessary enemies on the way up (after all, you never know when various neonates of other clans might be useful in the future). In some ways, this is almost a similar idea to the first character, the Anarch Wannabe, just with the self confidence dialed up to 11 and no lingering parental independence issues. The image also makes an impression, but I'm not sure what kind. It really and truly looks like someone reinterpreted the leather-daddy biker from the Village People as a super-androgyne rather than a bear. Not that I find it bad. Hell, I wish I'd seen someone cosplay is at a convention. The character would certainly fit in at the Succubus Club. Anyway, they are Mental primary, with a 3/4/3 split, and Social secondary, with 3/2/3. I feel Manipulation might be more useful to this character than Appearance, but I'm kind of on the fence about it. They have three dots each in Computer, Finance, and Investigation (making them the most tech savvy character in the book), and twos in Bureaucracy, Etiquette, Drive, Firearms, and Subterfuge. This is rounded out with a number of ones, mostly in Talents. Disciplines are one dot each. Backgrounds are three in Resources, two each in Allies and Contacts, and one in Mentor. Humanity and Willpower are both seven. I think this is a pretty good character, if maybe a little vague in focus. Weirdly, I kind of want to try taking this one and the Anarch Wannabe and combining them into a single character that has the best of both. But, otherwise, this is a fairly solid entry.

            So, that's our Ventrue. Overall, I think this was a pretty good showing. My personal favorites ended up being the Lover of Unlife, the Anarch Wannabe, and, strangely, the Union Boss. But really, all of them are pretty solid conceptually. I think the recurring issue is certain places where traits are slightly out of wack, and where freebies spent on an extra Discipline dot could've gone to much better use with Abilities or Backgrounds. But those are generally easy fixes.
            Weirdly, I really can't recall any ideas I had for Ventrue character templates 25 years ago. In hindsight, I am a little surprised at a lack of any sort of Old World Nobleman type. And maybe a vampire/Noddist historian.
            In terms of representation, we have only two women - the Corporate Executive and Lover of Unlife - and one undetermined. One, the Councilor, is black. Everyone else appears to be white.
            I think the Ventrue's overall theme was best summed up by the revised Clanbook with the ancient Roman concept of Dignitas. The most literal translation is "worth", and it's the origin of the modern term dignity, but it encompasses the idea of rank, position, and authority as well as worthiness and honor. It perfectly sums up the clan's attitude about themselves and their role in Kindred society. Members of the clan are expected to uphold that ideal, and I think all of these characters do a pretty good job of that.

            So that is the last of the original seven Camarilla clans. Next, I'll be taking a brief interlude to look at the two Caitiff templates from World of Darkness: Outcasts. After that, I'm debating between actually looking at the premade sheets in the 2nd ed Players Guide and the templates in Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand. (Though I'll be perfectly honest, I don't really like either set.) Then, depending on logistics, either the first of the final six clan books or (if I actually have it) Alien Hunger.

                World of Darkness: Outcasts (Caitiff)

                Released in 1995, Outcasts was a book providing information on Vampire's clanless Caitiff, Werewolf's tribeless Ronin, and Mage's ... Hollow Ones. (Yes, instead of the completely factionless Orphans, for some reason. Also, it didn't cover anything about Wraith's mishmash Beggers' Legion, and Changeling really didn't have anything comparable.) Basically, it was about characters that didn't really fit in to their game's socio-political structure (at least, as far as the first two groups went) and how they may, sometimes, interact with each other. And it gave two premade character templates for each group.
                The entire book was written by James Moore. He was a fairly prolific writer for the WoD back then. I think he may have created the Samedi, but I'm not sure. He certainly liked using them a lot in his tie-in fiction. I can't really tell who did the artwork for the two Caitiff characters. A different artist was used for each game line's pair, and these two don't have a signature or initials the way the Werewolf or Mage ones do. And, quite honestly, the Caitiff images are some of the worst in the entire book. By and large, there's a lot of good artwork in this book, including a lot of the actual chapter about Caitiff. However, a few of the images in that chapter are sub-par to kind of bad, and these two character images in the back of the book are as bad as the worst of those. This is unfortunate, especially given how the two werewolves have this nice light greyscale pencil art kind of look, and the mages are these fairly striking and stylized heavy ink images that were very common in that game's 1st edition, making them feel very much a part of that game. In contrast, the two vampires are these sort of muddy and crude things making too much use of black ink and thick details lines. They just look ugly. I rather wish Joshua Timbrook had done these, but I don't think he was working with WWGS anymore at this time (for reasons I'll probably get into when I get to the Setite book).

                The Questioner
                This character, drawing on certain period fiction, always "wanted to believe", growing up with a strong interest in New Age mysticism, parapsychology, UFO lore, and the like. Rather than become an FBI agent, she became a journalist, using her job as an excuse to follow her true passion of running down clues about the existence of the supernatural. I somehow suspect she ended up doing a lot of work for tabloids. And true to form, one of her leads ended up embracing her. And then just leaving her on her own. Having little or no clue about Kindred society, she continues on as before, hoping to meet others like herself and learn as much as she can about this newly unveiled shadow world. Overall, I think this is a pretty good idea for a character, especially a Caitiff who is probably going to be interacting more with other Outcasts and various Twilight People (psychics, sorcerers, etc.) than vampires. She's Mental primary, with four dots each in Perception and Intelligence. Weirdly, she's Physical primary, with three dots in Strength and Dexterity. This leaves all her Social traits at two, which feels strange for a character based around the idea of going out and trying to interact with and even interview other supernatural creatures or witnesses. She's got three dots each in Investigation, Law, Occult, Firearms, and Security. Investigation and Occult I can understand. Maybe even Security. I'm not sure why she has Law that high and especially unclear on why Firearms is that big of a priority. She doesn't even have a gun listed among her equipment. She's also got two dots each in Politics, Intimidation, Streetwise, and Subterfuge. I have the same question about Politics as I do Law, and her write up and roleplaying hints don't really convey the idea of someone who tries to intimidate people. (The first thing it says is "be friendly.") This feels like a character that should have some ability in Empathy and Computer, two traits she has no dots in. Likewise, she's only got a single dot each in Stealth and Science, which seem more fitting with the back story. Disciplines are one each in Potence, Thaumaturgy, and Presence. Potence I certainly get, as it's almost instinctive on some levels, though I wonder if Fortitude might work better. Thaumaturgy is also certainly in keeping with the character's background. Presence feel a little random. I'd think Auspex or Obfuscate would be more useful to a reporter. Backgrounds are four dots in Contacts and one in Retainer. Humanity and Willpower are both eight. As I said, I really like this character's concept and initial write up. I think the character sheet is kind of a disjointed mess though. I have this suspicion, based on what I remember of the fiction James Moore wrote back in the day, that he had a much more high-combat/heavy gunfire image of the WoD than some of the games, especially Vampire, tended to actually be going for. Not that there's anything wrong with that overall, but if a reporter needs to have the same level of Firearms as a typical soldier or SWAT officer, it feels off. So, I think if the character sheet was redone and the points shuffled around, this would be a really good character for a street level Vampire or mixed group chronicle.

                The Angry Victim
                I'm not sure how to feel about this character. Basically, he's been picked on, bullied and made to feel like crap his entire life, and is convinced that all his failings in life are everyone else's fault. And now he's been turned into a vampire, is very high on his newly discovered sense of power, and out to get revenge on anyone who's ever done him wrong, including the kid that gave him a bloody nose in the fifth grade. While I can somewhat empathize with the character - God only knows what crazy ideas I'd have had if becoming a vampire or werewolf at the age of 19 or 20 - I can't help but feel that this character is here more to be laughed at than to be taken seriously or sympathized with. His attributes are ... exactly the same as the previous character. This must be some sort of editing error, as I'm not sure I see this guy as being that perceptive. Almost all of this abilities are twos, save a four in Stealth. I don't think that quite fits either. He doesn't sound like he's going for the lurking stalker archetype, especially as he doesn't have Obfuscate as a Discipline. Those twos include Acting (?), Alertness, Brawl, Dodge, Streetwise, Etiquette (?), Firearms, and Bureaucracy. I find it weird that he doesn't have two dots in Melee as well, given his revenge fixation. He actually doesn't have all of his ability points. He's got thirteen Talent dots, nine Skill dots but one of them is that forth dot in Stealth breaking the rules, and only four dots in Knowledge. And he's only spent eight of his freebie points. Disciplines are one in Auspex and two in Potence, which seem fitting. Backgrounds include three dots each in Generation and Contacts (again, no real context for this in the write up; he feels like a loner and even has that as his Nature), and two in "Wealth". Humanity is seven and Willpower is eight. Damn. I feel like there's an idea of a potentially interesting character here, but it needs to be developed a little more in the writing. This is the sort of background that could be turned into an interesting coterie mate or associate of the Nosferatu Equalizer. Unfortunately, it falls a little flat, and the character sheet is a total mess. I'm disappointed on several levels.

                So, those were, besides the Sabbat's Pander Anarchist, our only examples of Caitiff PCs for a long long time. One is pretty good, the other not so much. One is female, one is male. (Curiously, this is the only female of the six in the book; both of the Werewolf and Mage ones are male.) The art makes it kind of hard to judge ethnicity. The Questioner could be interpreted as being Latina or possibly black or mixed race if you wanted.
                One of the characters I remember really enjoying in the original Hunters Hunted was the wealthy old man turned Caitiff recluse who now financed human vampire hunters out of a sense of revenge. I rather wish he'd been mentioned again among the "famous" Caitiff. But I really would've enjoyed seeing a Caitiff with a bunch of Resources, Influence and the like. Also, given how much the chapter spent on the idea of Caitiff as originators of their own new and unique Disciplines, I'm surprised we don't have one of those. The Gangrel Clanbook spent some time discussing vampires who, effectively being quasi-caitiff from a social standpoint and their lack of knowledge about Kindred society and truths, believe all the stuff from legends and movies (including weaknesses to garlic and crosses, dressing weird, and so forth). I rather wish that'd been touched on here as well, as a stereotypical Count Dracula or Elvira character would've been fun.
                The Caitiff don't really have much of a theme. But if one were to try to find one, I think I might make a case of it being the idea of failure. They represent a failure of their sire, of the power of Vitae, of Kindred society, and perhaps even of the world itself as it spins toward Gehenna and the Final Nights. Neither character really plays in to that so much, but I wouldn't really expect them to.

                Next will be Clanbook: Assamite. After that, I'm probably going to have to go look at Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand, as certain things about it are probably going to tie in to explaining certain things about Setite and why the final four clanbooks are so different from the previous ones in a number of ways.

                        Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand (part one)

                        I'm not a big fan of this book, for a number of reasons. It was initially advertised and solicited as being about the Black Hand, the Sabbat's elite warrior society, only to be a bait and switch that instead was about a secret conspiracy/Gehenna cult of elders presented as ultimate puppet masters behind both the Sabbat and Camarilla and safeguarding humanity from alien parasites. (Something similar happened around the same time with the Legend of the 5 Rings RPG, when a book solicited as being about merchants and trade ended up being about a secret organization of organized crime ninja or something. Maybe this was the trend in game design that year.)
                        Also, at some point around this period, something happened internally at WWGS that ultimately led to Vampire Developer Andrew Greenberg and Werewolf Developer Bill Bridges leaving the company. Apparently Steven Brown, the writer of this book, as well as both Sabbat books (the PG and the STH) also stopped working for WWGS, and it would appear that artist Joshua Timbrook did as well. Exactly what caused this is anyone's guess.
                        Anyway, this book was written by Steven Brown. The character template art is by Lief Jones. I don't dislike Jones's work. I think he makes good use of lines and detail. However, everything he does has this somewhat distorted look to it (like a fisheye lens effect) that I tend to find a little off-putting. All of the templates in this book seem to have overly large heads. The characters in this book are all made using the elder creation rules from the earlier book Elysium. I'm honestly a bit surprised that Elysium didn't have any templates in it. I would've appreciated seeing some who weren't part of this cult, especially ones like Tremere or regular Brujah who are barred from this super special secret club house.

                        The Antiquarian (Gangrel)
                        I have to give credit to this for being atypical for the clan, even if in some ways it is an extension of the Explorer from their clanbook. But if that one is Indiana Jones, this is more his rival Belloq, an Old World type in a nice casual suit. Unfortunately, this character is from the The Rom, which is a bit of a strike against it. But at least it only plays on some of the early material's complete lack of understanding of how vampires would be viewed by Roma culture rather than a bunch of the really awful stuff from WoD: Gypsies. Anyway, the character developed a passion for history, especially Kindred history and the relics relating to it. Eventually someone decided he would fit in with the cult and recruited him. Apparently, his "love for nature" makes him good at surviving expeditions, because "Gangrel = nature" or something. He seems fairly normal for an elder vampire, perhaps too normal. He's Mental primary, with both Intelligence and Stamina at five, and Perception and Wits at four. The rest are threes, save Appearance, which is two. Investigation is maxed out at five dots. The rest of his abilities only go up to three at the highest, including Linguistics, Occult, Politics, and ... Computer? This guy feels like he'd be too old school to have actually noticed computers by 1994. I can picture him having a desk that still includes an adding machine and ink blotter. He only has a single dot in Survival, which seems out of step with he character description. Likewise no dots in anything such as History, Archaeology, Kindred Lore or the like to reflect his interests. Disciplines are mostly three dots, including the clan ones and Auspex. Plus a single dot in Obfuscate. Backgrounds include Allies, Contacts, Resources and Retainers at five each, plus three in Herd, two in Age, and one in Generation. Humanity is five and Willpower is eight. Overall, I generally like this one. He fits the Gangrel fairly well (and I think in some ways he may be part of the inspiration for Beckett), and his interests seem like something an elder would occupy his existence with. I think some of the traits need to be adjusted, but other than that, it's pretty good.

                        The Corporate Conspirator (Lasombra antitribu)
                        Basically your standard stereotypical elder. From a poor immigrant community who became a servant and later Childe of a vampire madam. He was a member of the Camarilla and Sabbat at the same time, playing them against each other for his own interests until he was caught by a member of the cult and brought into it. (Good thing he wasn't from one of those clans they don't like.) Now he just continues as before, pursuing his own interests while convincing most of the cult that it's for their overall benefit. So, your typical cookie cutter elder. (Oddly, he doesn't seem to be more than 100 to 150 years old, making him more an ancilla than an elder.) He's Social primary, with a 4/5/4 spread, and a 3/3/4 for Mental. Like our last character, he's got one skill maxed out to five, in this case Finance, and the rest three or less, including threes in Acting, Alertness, Etiquette, Bureaucracy, Investigation, and Politics. I'm a little surprised that his Law is only one. Disciplines are just his clan ones at threes and four. Backgrounds include Contacts and Resources at five, Influence at four, Allies and Retainers at three, Generation at two and Age at one. Humanity is five and Willpower eight. This is overall an OK character. He's just kind of generic and nothing really stands out about him.

                        That's all for this one. Next will more Black Hand elders.

                                Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand (part two)

                                Continuing our collection of elders.

                                The Coup Leader (Nosferatu antitribue)
                                As odd as some aspects of Jones's artwork is, I will give credit here in that we have a Nosferatu who, although his face is heavy shadowed and hard to make out, isn't as distorted as most of those in the clanbook. If he's ugly, I get the sense that it's a restrained kind of ugly. I also like his suit. (I guess I'm just a sucker for a well dressed Nosferatu.) Anyway, our character was a simple English cobbler until turned into cannon fodder for the Sabbat. He survived, but after another attack where he was used as cannon fodder, he decided he'd had enough and left the Sabbat for the New World. He didn't really care much for what he saw of the Camarilla or Anarchs either, and ended up spending most of his time just derailing everyone else's grand plans and plots. Eventually he came to the attention of the cult and was recruited. Only now he wants to turn his efforts against the Black Hand as well. Apparently he really loves mortals and wants to defend the innocent from wicked vampires. While I don't object to this too much, it does kind of reflect one of my issues with the entire book overall, about how the Black Hand frequently seem to be set up as the WoD's secret heroes in a game that's meant to have protagonists and antagonists rather than heroes and villains. And as a general rule, I kind of expect my elder vampires to have grown apart from mortals on some level. (I believe this is part of the reasoning for why elder characters default to only five in Humanity.) But if you spend the points on extra humanity (five freebies per dot, IIRC), then it would fit. But there is a small problem here. This is the only character in this book who doesn't have Humanity. Instead, he's on a Path of Enlightenment, and in this case a new one from this book, The Path of the Scorched Heart. This path is designed to repress and do away with one's emotions, reflecting the True Brujah who created it. And while it has this whole thing about differentiating between good and evil, only feeding on "evil" mortals, and working for the greater good, it doesn't seem to match all that well with the character description. And, for a character whose concept is "coup leader", he really doesn't seem to lead any coups. As far as stats go, he's Social primary, with five dots in Manipulation and four dots in Charisma and ... Appearance. Did I mention that this is a Nosferatu? Strength and Dexterity are also four, while Stamina, Perception and Wits are at three. Abilities include four dots each in Acting, Intimidation, Firearms, Melee, and Politics. He's also got threes in Brawl, Dodge, Alertness, Leadership, and Subterfuge. Disciplines see Obfuscate maxed out at five dots, with three in Animalism, two in Potence, and one each in Fortitude and Presence. Backgrounds include threes in Age, Military Force, and Retainers, and twos in Allies, Contacts, and Generation. His Path rating is five, and Willpower is nine. Generally speaking, this isn't a bad idea for a character. I kind of like the idea of an elder who has just developed this dislike for all the factions and actively works against all of them. But he seems to lack a certain ruthlessness or edge that I expect from my elder vampires. And the character sheet does raise a few questions. I think I'm going to have to consider this a really interesting idea that just misfired.

                                The Dotting Mentor (Nagaraja)
                                So, this character was a poor Cajun who discovered his true talent for violence and killing after being drafted. (I guess he was drafted by the Confederacy during the Civil War, unless maybe it was WW1?) Apparently she just liked to kill and destroy things, and he turned on her when she killed a school bus full of high school students. A cult member happened along at just that time and helped destroy her, and then recruited him into the cult. Once again, this kind of sets off my "we're the good guys!" sense. Apparently, now he's like a super supportive and somewhat over protective godfather (or maybe helicopter parent) type for those he mentors and trains. I actually find that to be a kind of nifty idea for an elder character, and potentially fun to play. However, I'm not sure it - or this character in general - really represents the Nagaraja. They're described as preferring scholarly or mystical types who study death rather than people who cause it. I also kind of had the impression that they didn't really exist outside of the cult, so that one going rogue was a super rare occurrence among an already super rare bloodline. I kind of feel like this concept would work better with a Ventrue, Toreador, or Malkavian. Anyway, he's Physical primary, with five in Strength and fours in Dexterity and Stamina, as well as Perception and Wits. Abilities include four in Firearms, and threes in Brawl, Dodge, Etiquette, Melee, Security, Stealth and Thanatology. So, basically, this is a KillNinja with good manners. (He has two dots in Music, which makes me imagine he's a Zydeco KillNinja.) Disciplines include four dots each in Special Sauce #1 and Special Sauce #2, ie Necromancy and Nihilistics. Also two in Auspex and one each in Potence and Celerity. Backgrounds include four in Resources, three in Generation, two in Contacts, and one each in Age, Allies, and Retainer. He actually hasn't spent all of his Background points (or the bulk of his freebies either). Humanity and Willpower are both six. I have mixed feelings about this character. Like I said, I really enjoy the basic concept, although I feel it might work better with a different clan. On the other hand, from a pure trait perspective, this is a KillNinja, which also doesn't seem to fit what the Nagaraja are presented as being. If you were doing a Nagaraja clanbook that included multiple character templates and wanted to do one that showed them as warrior rather than scholar, I'd be all for it, but as the only one in this book that introduces them, I'm not sure that's the best idea. I fear this one is, probably not a misfire, but something that just missed the target. Which is a shame, as I think Helicopter Sire is a really fun idea for an elder.

                                The Former Chatterling (Toreador)
                                So, Chatterlings are basically children who are kidnapped and raised by the cult, indoctrinated with their ideology and trained to be servants and ghouls, with the eye toward potential embrace at some point. Effectively "more Black Hand than Black Hand", you'd expect. The book describes them as the cult's most insane and ruthless members. This character is ... none of those things. Kidnapped and raised by the cult, trained and eventually embraced, yes. Then he was presented to his city's Prince as a child of one of the Primogen (also a cult member), and acts like a normal Camarilla vampire and upholds the status quo. Although for some weird reason - never made clear - is that he pretends to be a Ventrue. And that's about it, really, as far as his duties to the cult go. Meanwhile, he sees all these other "normal" vampires and has very vaguely defined second thoughts about his upbringing and loyalty. Exactly how and why isn't mentioned. He's Physical primary, with a 4/5/4 spread, and four dots in Charisma. Manipulation and Appearance are three, as are Intelligence and Wits. He has four dots in Music, I guess because he's a Toreador. He has threes in Acting, Melee, Security, Stealth, and Occult. This is rounded out with twos in Alertness, Brawl, Dodge, Etiquette, Firearms, Repair, and Investigation, and ones in Empathy, Intimidation, Subterfuge, Drive, Survival, Linguistics, Medicine, and Science. These are not exactly the skills I'd pick for a deep cover operative. If anything, it feels like some of them were picked by throwing darts at a board. This is a character who really needs things like Subterfuge and Politics. Disciplines are Auspex at four, Celerity and Presence at three, and Fortitude at one. Backgrounds include four dots in Mentor, two in Generation, and one each in Allies, Contacts, Retainer, and Secret Identity. Again, he hasn't spent all his points. Humanity is five and Willpower seven. Given how they are raised, you would expect the entire point of Chatterlings is to totally indoctrinate them into the inhuman mindset of one of the Paths of Enlightenment, but apparently not here. Overall, this character just kind of feels like it was phoned in. The concept and write up seems to have an idea - or possibly two different ones; the Chatterling now questioning his upbringing and the vampire who masquerades as other vampires - but doesn't really take the time or effort to develop it. That character sheet feels disorganized and perhaps incomplete. I does not appear to have spent more than a third of the 50 freebie points it should have. (And looking back, I see that this appears to be the case for all of the characters, unless there's something in Elysium's elder rules I missed.) I am forced to judge this as a failure.

                                And that's all for this entry. Next will be three more of our elder cultists, followed by the final two and final thoughts about them. Then a return to clanbooks.

                                            I'm not a big fan of this book, for a number of reasons.

I didn't think it was hamfisted like certain developments in Revised because the game was only showing me what was potentially out there as opposed to "Sorry, now the Gangrel have left the Camarilla."

                                            The Greenberg era Vampire was excellent in showcasing how damn complex everything was. You go from a simple Anarch vs Camarilla perspective. Then you realize the Camarilla is divided between Prince and the Primogen. And then that the Princes actually defy the actual Camarilla organization in terms of not wanting Justicars and Archons to usurp their authority. Then you get the Sabbat vs Camarilla conflict. Because, you know... that's so much less silly than the Chatterlings and Temporis.

                                                I think in hindsight, I rather wish that second edition Vampire had made a somewhat stronger effort to differentiate between your more localized/street-level/personal chronicles about neonates and younger ancilla and what Kindred existence is about for them and what they focus on vs more grand scale/political/strategic war of ages type stuff involving older ancilla and elder characters who no longer really interact with the mortal world in anything but a kind of abstract way.                                                 I think the game line as a whole really could've benefited from having early (like 93 or 94) releases that focused on how a Prince's court and all the city offices like Sheriff and whatnot worked and especially how core-book level PCs might interact with it and even became part of it, and a similar one for Elysiums that focused more on neonates and ancilla, saving the rules and methods of elders for their own stand alone book. Likewise some sort of book about just what goes into picking, developing and protecting a Haven (probably half havens and the other half extending that mindset to the larger scale of PCs establishing and maintaining their own domains), one about exactly what constitutes a city's Rack and how they work in a setting/chronicle and what PCs do there, a book about all the ins and outs of the sub-genre of vampires being famous celebrities and just how to handle the Fame background in terms of the Masquerade, a book about how vampires interact with human religions and some of their own cults, a book about money and just how vampires make it and hide it, and one about what the hell vampires do for regular fun and entertainment.                                                 Then for elders, a book about making elder characters, one about upper level political games and plots, a collection of weird elder conspiracies (like the Black Hand, the Inconnu, and, say, the Ordo Dracul or something) that elder PCs can be part of, a book about planning and running chronicles that span decades and centuries, and maybe even a book about Methuselahs were you don't even play them as regular characters but instead like nation states similar to TSR's Birthright setting or PC games like Civilization.

                                                        Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand (part three)

                                                        Once more into the breach.

                                                        The Globe Trotting Assassin (Malkavian)
                                                        This is another KillNinja. But I will give it credit for being a kind of interesting one. I do like the character image. Weirdly, he reminds me of former Vampire writer and Exalted developer Geoff Grabowski, were he a little thinner and his head about 50% too big for his body. He's a Malkavian, and I have to give a lot of credit here for him not being in anyway wacky or Fishmalky in any way. Instead, he's one of those types who seems relatively normal, at least until you get to know him and realize he's subtly mad in the way of certain fanatics and obsessives. In life, he was a piano prodigy. His sire embraced him for that reason at the age of 17 and then effectively kept him as a slave prisoner performing just for the sire. He absolutely hated his sire, and as methodically as planning out a recital, planned out his destruction. Now he believes that all vampires must be destroyed as they are evil. But the Black Hand found him and convinced him - mostly - that they were the good guys and now use him as a weapon to destroy "the most evil of vampires who were evil." So now he walks the earth killing "evil" vampires, and apparently practicing the piano as much as he does martial arts. He's Physical primary and Mental secondary, as most KillNinja are, and naturally has the five in Dexterity. Intelligence - the least necessary for KillNinja - is a two so the other two traits can be fours. He's got a four in Firearms and threes in Brawl, Dodge, Melee, Stealth, Security, Linguistics, and Music. All the KillNinja abilities, plus piano. (I kind of wish that four had been in Music rather than Firearms. It feels like it would've fit the character better.) Every other is at one, save Athletics, Alertness, and Investigation at two. I think he has the most Disciplines of any character, with four in Obfuscate, threes in Auspex and Dominate, twos in Celerity and Quietus (of course), and ones in Potence and Fortitude. Backgrounds are just one in Age and three in Generation. (Apparently, these cost four background - and freebie - points per dot, which has been throwing off my counts on all the previous characters.) Humanity is six and Willpower seven. I don't dislike this character. In general, he's not a bad concept, especially for a Malkavian. But it again features the "the Black Hand are the secret heroes and good guys of the WoD" thing that kind of irks me. I kind of wish the music angle had been played up a little more, and perhaps that there had been hints that sometimes his definition of what qualified as "evil" was entirely random and obtuse.

                                                        The Government Leader (Ventrue)
                                                        I think the name here might be a little misleading. Our man is a long time political operative, being a spymaster back in the days of King James I. In his role, he discovered the existence of vampires and was embraced into the Camarilla. And at some point, he discovered the existence of the Black Hand and was inducted into their ranks. These nights, he's deeply interwoven with the government (his quote makes it sound like he's in the US, but I honestly find him more interesting as some sort of secret master within the British government), using his influence within various government agencies to cover up the existence of Kindred, investigate other supernatural creatures and events, and gather intelligence for the cult (and I presume himself and the Camarilla at the same time). However, he's more than a bit paranoid, and believes that there may be even more secret conspiracies behind or beyond the Black Hand (which is an interesting idea that I wish this book had explored more). The character image is ... a little odd. He appears to be in the middle of wiping dog crap off his shoe or something. I'm also sort of amused that he bears more than a little resemblance to George H. W. Bush (though perhaps by way of Pruneface from Dick Tracy). His Perception, Charisma, and Manipulation are all maxed out at five each, as are Bureaucracy and Investigation. He's also got fours in Acting, Subterfuge, Security and Stealth. Weirdly, Politics is only three, while Leadership, Law and Linguistics are only one each. His Disciplines are all the Ventrue ones, including four dots in Dominate, plus two dots in Auspex. I'm a little surprised he doesn't have any Obfuscate. (Or Thaumaturgy, given it mentions he used to be a member of the Rosicrucians.) Backgrounds include five dots of Contacts, three of Resources and Status, and two of Age, Generation, and Secret Identity. I admit to being confused as to why this character has no Influence at all. If any character would have that at five, you'd think it would be this guy. Humanity is five and Willpower nine. Overall, I think this is not a bad character. I do like the effort put into the back story, which I think helps elevate this from "typical elder" to a more interesting character. I think it would benefit from a better name, and maybe some alterations to a few traits, but I like this one more than I thought I would going in to it.

                                                        The Jyhad Fanatic (Assamite antitribu)
                                                        #@%& you, book. Seriously. ::sigh:: I was never really wild about the term "Jyhad", even back then. It always struck me as a little weird that a stylized spelling of a term from an 8th century Middle Eastern religion would be the word various elders is Europe and the Americas used to refer to the eternal struggle between themselves and various secret masters. Surely there could've been some Latin or Greek word that imparted the same idea. (And then ten years later, that term suddenly takes on a totally new significance in the modern world that makes its use even more problematic.) I'm not really even sure where to start with this one. Should I mention that the write up specifically mentions him being willing to blow up the World Trade Center? This was in poor taste even in 1994, a year after the parking garage bombing, but in hindsight after 9/11, it looks even worse. He's the cookie cutter Assamite of 2nd ed, raised by the assassins just like his dad, and embraced into the Black Hand. (Specifically the Eastern branch of the Black Hand, unlike all of our earlier ones who are Westerners, just to further make this an alien "other".) At some point, he encountered an ancient Methuselah who made him her tool and a tool of the Antediluvians, and he took this as some sort of religious revelation. Now he's even more devoted to the Antediluvians and super excited for the coming of Gehenna. As far as Stats go, he's a KillNinja. His Physicals are all four dots (and are Primary, meaning he's missing a dot). And like the Malkavian, his Mental is 4/2/4, because KillNinjas don't need Intelligence. Brawl, Dodge, Melee, and Firearms are all four dots, of course. Alertness, Athletics, Stealth, Survival, Security, Investigation, and Occult are all three. There's a random single dot in Music. Maybe the Malkavian gives music lessons to other KillNinja. Celerity and Quietus are at four, Obfuscate at three, and Auspex and Fortitude at two. Backgrounds are Age and Generation at three each, cuz KillNinja don't need no friends, connections, or money. Or, you know, a Secret Identity or anything. Humanity is five and Willpower ten. I have no idea why this character isn't on the Path of Blood or something similar. The character image is of essentially an almost stereotypical violently crazy looking North African/Arabic/South Asian man, with guns. While I can appreciate the need for a character that reflects the cult's weird reverence for the original Clan Founders/Antediluvians, and that weirdly enough the Assamites may be one of the ones best suited for this role, I just actively dislike this character on so many levels. It's like a perfect storm of stereotypes and bad ideas from the time, some of which look even worse in hindsight. I wouldn't go as far as to call this character racist, but I could certainly see where some people might. It's just bad all around.
