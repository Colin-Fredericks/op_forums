    The O

    I’ve decided Agata Starek looks and sounds a great deal like Outstar. It’s in her eyes, the merriness, the capacity of homicidal violence…

    Anyway, in this section Starek shows up and harasses a supposed friend who is dwelling in Milwaukee. Louis (the Anarch Starek is visiting) and Starek herself have both terminally offended a group of vampires called the Oswobodziciele. The O themselves are opposed to blood bonding people, which Louis did to some of them. Starek is all about punching up against bosses and forms of power, which suggests she and the O should have common ground. But they also take exception to people using them as target practice, which Starek apparently did sometimes before the story.

    Louis shows Starek a member of the O, his former servant, he has recaptured and is keeping chained up in his basement. She decides to embrace the fellow just because.

    Several things stand out about the story. For one, unlike most sections in the book, this one is presented as a traditional bit of fiction, with a third person omniscient point of view. For another, the neighborhood in which Louis dwells is oddly normal – humans feel safe enough for a girl to ride up the street after dark. Even so, Louis is an utter monster. He murdered the family of his former ghoul, and keeps prisoners chained to a wall in his basement (albeit with SnM gear because Louis is bad at planning). Starek herself compares the setup to that of a serial killer. Further, the Anarchs are just another set of tyrants to the people the enslave with the blood bond.

            The Ministry of Love

            The Anarchs have always been at war with East Asia. The Anarchs have no law, only crimes, says Lynskey. Nothing is illegal; social pressure is used to exert control, in place of law.

            (clears throat)

            Right. So this section is a transcript of surveillance the SI is performing on vampires of the Ministry of Love, first a pair discussing the name, an induction which goes wrong (or right, depending on your point of view), and then the ministry vampires determining they had been bugged and the bug was in a bit of decorative statuary. The SI provides analysis of the Minsitry, but like their action the SI are a day late and a dollar short.

            The section has some humor to it and perhaps even some irony. The vampiric Ministry of Love is not as powerful or frightening as the Ministry of Love in 1984, but I think the rebranded Settites were named for the group in Orwell’s book.

            Unfortunately the section is too brief to really provide much insight into how the Ministry of Love operates, except when they are barely ahead of the SI.

                                        I think that the Setites are fundamentally running headfirst into the fundamentals of their concept. The Clan was based on the John Milinus version of Set worship that, itself, was a rough bastadization of Robert E. Howard's Conan the Barbarian. It was originally just supposed to be a cult of evil, corruption, and darkness for player characters to go, "Yeah, I'm a vampire and evil but not THAT evil."

                                        Which got people wondering why we needed the Setites and Baali both. Now, I loved the way that the Followers of Set were expanded upon by adding a Paradise Lost variant to it. "Set is Lucifer and Lucifer is waging war against an unjust system by the Aeons to free us all." However, it's still pretty one note.

                                        I like the fact the Setites are having a doctrinal schism since as a fan of religious history in general, you aren't doing it right if people aren't fighting over doctrine. The Church of Set being a bunch of fundamentalists who feel that the other pagans have wandered too much off the, "We are a cult of EVIL, Set dammit!" feels appropriate. It also gives Ministry PCs a lot more options to construct their characters and some enemies off the bat.

                                        I admit, I love Noddism and the Church of Caine but I do think they stole a bit of the Setites schtick.

He wanted to create Nieztschean Superman but the majority of Sabbat were the equivalent of Alt-Right trolls as well as thugs. It was a really effective roleplaying experience about the death of idealism as well as the realization that utopian dreams often fail.
