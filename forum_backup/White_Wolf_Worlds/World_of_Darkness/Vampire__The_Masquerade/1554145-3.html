        This is where I potentially bring up a controverisal element of my dislike generally for characters that are "these are shock value characters that exist for you to murder them." Which is something there's only a few of them in the game and OPP really disliked (I'm guessing) because they always get either killed off or heavily changed.

        I'm not going to judge that decision but also give up some thought.Also, how the idea of adding "shock value" elements ruins characters that might otherwise be a useful NPC. Shock can be good but...rarely is.

        Velya the Vivisectionist has a concept. What with fusing the Wight Little Girl to his back and the two of them existing as one. It takes an 800 year old Sabbat scholar and founder and just makes them so damned WEIRD that the only thing to do with them is put them down. It also highlights the Sabbat is full of gross nuts.

        Jacob Schumpeter is a Ventrue who can only feed on battered women. It's "justified" in so much as it actually makes sense for his character as a small insignificant Lifetime movie villain that is his concept. You CAN use Jacob intelligently and he is a "cheap heat" villain for a game (with the caveat that killing him will result in them having killed one of the Prince's brood)

        But he's an uninteresting monster too and it's notable that he got Second Inquisitioned while Bobby Weatherbottom ended up putting his girlfriend into a coma and feeding her blood constantly so he can feed on her (I had her as a Thin Blood so she could be an eternally regenerating blood bag). Bobby is objectively worse than Jacob in many ways but the shock value feels consistent while the other is, "bad man."

        Then there's good characters ruined by shock value. Horatio Ballard being a pedophile Ventrue sort of drowns out all elements of his character even if it's only him feeding on children of his family versus anything worse (I'm not getting into that). That's solely so the players can justify killing him and making him the villain in any fight for Prince.

        Then there's Jan Pieterszoon.

        Yeah, we know why he died at Prague. But also we have no idea how his feeding restriction related to his past and probably any attempt to explore that would only made it worse. It was something I played with in my rejected Theo Bell novel pitch but I couldn't thread that needle and ditched it.

                                        I think the Least Favorite List needs an acknowledgement of "Real Life Historical Persona is a Vampire."

                                        Measured from best to worst.

                                        Al Capone is a love him or hate him character because we know what happened to Al Capone and there's no mysterious disappearance that really allows him to live again any more than Elvis.
                                        He's also inherently silly (Al Capone is a gangster in charge of Chicago's underworld!). The thing is that if you don't MIND a little silly, he's not the worst person you could pick for a vampire if you were a Ventrue. Inherently, he's kind of a litmus test for how much you are willing to take your World of Darkness to cheese.

                                        (And inherently, the WOD is a 90s comic book like Spawn or Batman: Knightfall or the Crow--acknowledging this makes it better not worse)

                                        Lous Pasteur is a weird choice for a vampire. However, I've used him despite this and actually quite like the idea he is the kind of guy who would be able to crack vampirism scientifically. Not out of any vast magical knowledge but that he was the guy who knew what we needed to do with milk.

                                        Then there's Rasputin that is a bad joke that was never really funny in the first place. I actually enjoyed the Alan Rickman biography on the guy so seeing him used in Anastasia, The Kingsmen, Doctor Who, and Hellboy doesn't really feel all that tasteful. The guy may or may not have believed his own religious heresy but the poor bastard was murdered by people who got away with it and he's not really that fun to make fun of.

                                        Saint Germaine basically is one that just is kind of boring to adapt because he's been adapted many times already into vampire fiction. Hell, even in Assassins Creed V, though the time period was all wrong for that one. If you're going to adapt a guy to becoming a vampire then a guy with associations with the supernatural is probably a good idea. John Dee gets a pass for this as well.

                                        Erich Weiss: I don't like this guy for the simple fact that I actually think he'd make a much better version of the Arcanum or Technocracy. Many people wrote pulpy stories about him as an occult detective but he doesn't really work as a Tremere, let alone an Anarch Tremere.

                                        The Final Reich: I'm not sure what needs to be said here but weirdly, Himmelr isn't the worst so much as Goring. Putting aside the incredibly poor taste of it, actually, no let's not put aside the poor taste. It's all in poor taste but Goring is just a WEIRD choice.
