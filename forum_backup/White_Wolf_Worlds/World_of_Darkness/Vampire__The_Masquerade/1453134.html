    Gangrel survival tactics in rural areas

    06-22-2021, 06:33 AM
    How do Gangrel, especially young Gangrel who can't yet turn into bats, survive in rural areas for years, even decades at a time? Is it just because werewolf numbers and vigilance are overrated? Do they stick strictly to roads and truckstops, never actually venturing into the forests? That is where the hitchhikers and truckdrivers they presumably eat are, but seems rather dull for creatures who enjoy the wilderness. Are Gangrel effectively banned from the forests they love until they can fly and view werewolf territory from on high?

                  I find the insistence on nigh-total intersplat ignorance to be monotonous and rather unrealistic if these creatures are meant to have been bumping elbows for literal centuries. Experienced/properly mentored Gangrel should probably know to look for certain signs suggestive of entering the territory of a Garou pack or proximity to a cairn---ritual mounds, glyphs slashed on rocks and trees, etc. If they see the signs suggesting Here Be Lupines, they simply give it a wide berth. There are not nearly enough Werwolves in the world to protect every acre of wilderness, and even the theoretical territories of large Septs must include large swathes unmonitored from time to time.

                  Nor do I think it especially dangerous for the Outlanders to maintain a subsistence and the Masquerade in a rural area, although it is inevitably tedious and lonely compared to the unlife of the Gilded Cage. Barns and sheds, esp. those left derelict, can make decent resstops with a ghouled hellhound or other famulus to ward people away by day. Farmhouses in the Americas are generally well isolated and provide a night's easy feeding, whereas in the Old World, country villages are likely to have long-established "understandings" with local nightgaunts.

                          In fact, this might be the sort of thing the Gangrel bargain for in Primogen meetings. An ancient forest full of sacred sites is useless to the Gangrel, but it's equally useless if the area gets completely developed and urbanized. But an ancient forest that gets chopped down and desecrated and then replaced with pines that will be chopped down in turn periodically is very useful to them.

                          That might be worth, say, the Gangrel Primogen ordering his/her progeny to lend military aid to that Ventrue who has no desire to risk himself or his progeny fighting Sabbat or Anarchs. Cancel your plans to expand the city into that forest, but make sure it gets chopped down and replanted, and I'll put three Gangrel ancillae on the case of killing your enemies for you.

                                  There's a variety of answers depending on the specifics about Lupines you decide on for your chronicles. The answer in everyone's chronicles will be different based on what specifics they choose for their chronicle. I think there are several things to keep in mind.

                                  1) While there are areas of extreme danger for any vampire (likely any place near a kinfolk community, a caern, or a particular protectorate defended by a specific pack), these are not omnipresent in the setting. An ST needs to make decisions about what areas of definitely off limits, what areas represent a heightened danger, what areas are of routine danger (for non-urban), and which areas are probably just as safe as the large urban metros. The Gangrel clan probably has significant knowledge of the boundaries of these areas (simply by the long experience of their members who know when Gangrel had disappeared and are assumed dead) and shares them informally with new members. These boundaries likely adjust over time, but slow enough that the bulk of Gangrel are protected at any one time until they figure out the new map. The VtM's game claims of the outlands being extremely dangerous because of Lupines has some basis in fact, but is probably more due to great uncertainty and a lack of knowledge, not that it is actually instant death every time you leave the city.

                                  2) The Gangrel advantage of not detecting as Wyrm with lower levels of Humanity is probably a big advantage as a lazy way to search for vampires is probably just by using that Gift. This likely helps fledgling Gangrel a lot since most newly made vampires are likely to be of higher Humanity anyway and be extremely cautious when it comes to Masquerade (and it's not like most vampires have to be taught the importance of the concept even if they don't yet know the Traditions).

                                  3) Gangrel being constantly on the move works to their advantage. Lupines are territorial creatures and likely prone not to keep pursuing a lead once a possible or detected threat seems to have left. A Gangrel doing something suspicious in one small town is likely gone the next day or by the next week. There's likely not a lot of time for even local Lupines to become aware there is a vampire in the area before they are gone. Most aren't looking to lay down roots, build influence, or have a permanent haven. In contrast, your Ventrue or Toreador will do just that, and a long enough time gets them discovered and killed.

                                  4) While Lupines in general hate vampires, there have always been significant exceptions as certain tribes, camps, factions, or individuals, have some kind of dealings with certain individuals. It's a business arrangement, not friendship, and such deals have to be kept on the download. But there's probably enough such Lupine "friends" to give enough hints and warnings to individuals that keeps them away from the greatest areas of danger as they travel. (In exchange, those vampires do something in exchange for their "friends.") Newly made Gangrel can't take advantage of this, but long lived Gangrel could very well have long standing associations with certain Lupines that have been made over their long lives and even passed down to childer or coterie mates. And some of this knowledge will spread throughout the clan per point 1 above.

                                  5) The actual wilderness is very big and very isolated. This link has a map that shows the 47% of the US that has no one living in that area at all. And lots of area outside that has extremely low population density. Now while these are the areas the Garou are most likely to be, they're still going to be concentrated in only certain areas. A lot of wilderness isn't near a caern or kinfolk settlement. As long as the Gangrel is willing to feed on animals, they could likely survive for a long time. How many caerns does even a state like Wyoming have? Much of the state is probably "open" in regards to the Gangrel who have the powers best suited to survive there and are less likely to be randomly detected as Wyrm. If they act smart, they should be OK even though Wyoming is likely a stronghold of the Lupines relatively. The continental US and Europe is not like Canada or Alaska that still has a lot of wolves, and therefore a greater number of Lupines based on that population instead of the human population. In fact, simply avoiding areas that are known to have wolves probably eliminates a lot of danger for Gangrel combined with some other knowledge.

                                  6) Certain lineages of Gangrel likely have other allies that will protect them. For example, in the first Sabbat books, they made a big deal that many nomadic "Country Gangrel" were of American Indian stock (likely because the setting history says it was the Sabbat who first came to the New World, and this is pre-revised Code of Milan and Purchase Pact that saw the Sabbat change). I always imagined that most of these Indian Gangrel were the old followers of the Path of Harmony. So in some of these Indian communities, there may be a heritage of "strange people" or "spirits" (or whatever euphemism is used for these Gangrel) visiting them so they have safe spots they can always come back to as they travel back and forth.

                                  Newly embraced, inexperienced Gangrel likely survive through a combination of luck, and their sires not dumping them off in an area known to be hostile until the time comes when they find them again and teach them the official ways. Then they pick up enough knowledge that probably keeps them safe a significant amount of time.

                                  The other clans lack much of the informal knowledge the Gangrel keep so they frequently stumble into situations that kill them because they have no idea that small town inbetween Cleveland and Cincinnati is known to be deadly (because it's a kinfolk community that is well guarded by Lupines), but it's such a convenient spot for a vampire to "lay low" when fleeing one city for the next, that they keep getting killed. So the Kindred of Ohio think the state is a death trap. But the Gangrel just know - stay away from that county. It's dangerous. Go off on the sideroads to the east, and you'll be fine as long as you don't start trouble or act stupid.

                                  ​There's probably some other things you can stick in there, but it's probably a good set of principles for an ST to begin with.

                                          In most modern nights games, I play lupines as more a memetic threat and convenient bogeyman used by most to explain the unexplained -- or as a cover story for goings-on a city's elders would prefer not be known. Vampire traveling between cities gets merced by...

                                          ...a group of hunters? Lupines did it!
                                          ...a rival or enemy? Lupines did it!
                                          ...the sherrif? Lupines did it!
                                          ...Anarchs or neonates/ancillae out for a diablerie? Lupines did it!
                                          ...a Sabbat pack? Lupines did it!
                                          ...their own inability to feed, or find shelter from daylight outside the city? Lupines did it!

                                          Or maybe a vampire just ran off to...

                                          ...defect from their clan? Lupines did it!
                                          ...defect to another sect? Lupines did it!
                                          ...avoid a blood hunt? Lupines did it!
                                          ...avoid a vengeful elder or sire? Lupines did it!
                                          ...hide they learned too much? Lupines did it!

                                          The latter category being a common cause for the former, of course.

                                          For my games it's the quintessential "dog ate my homework" of the vampire world, the subtext for those who've been around a few decades being a wink-and-nod "it's best for everyone if nobody investigates this, if you know what I'm saying". It's not exactly falsifiable under most circumstances, and lupines are enough of a meme to make sure only the stupid and ambitious (read, those best eliminated from the "gene pool" at earliest opportunity) go sniffing around where they shouldn't. But at the same time, practically every Kindred has "a buddy of a buddy" who tangled with a lupine once...or at least thought they did...and miraculously lived to tell the tale lupines are not to be fucked with.

                                          Not that elders with Dominate, Protean, Obfuscate, Vicissitude and a creative bent, Chimerstry, or in a pinch well-trained ghouls in convincing costumes, would have a vested interest in faking a lupine attack every once in a while, and letting their victims escape to perpetuate the tale.

                                          As far as actual dealings with lupines...I tend to play it by ear, depending on what clans and character archetypes are being played, and what themes I'm prioritizing in a given game. I've played them as monster movie lupines, lycanthropy and all, at times, and others I've gone so far as to name them by tribe although I stay dodgy on what it is they do or why they exist. The one thing I never do is cross over WtA crunch with VtM crunch.

                                              Even ignoring the absurd size of the USA and instead focusing on more developed lands like western europe, there's still just tons of unused land. Unless a vampire goes wondering into a caern, a lupine isn't going to discern that a person in the distance is a vampire. Lupines don't spend time fanning out searching for undead that've gone for a midnight stroll, if it's not the Caern then it's a waste of resources. Furthermore, Lupines like pure wilds, while Gangrel are looking for something more rural/the outskirts of town/a village.

                                              1- Lupines are mortal, and though they heal at fast rates, that doesn't put oxygen into their muscles. A vampire can presumably continue to sprint all night, so while a Gangrel without 4 Protean or crazy physicals is likely to be run down within a hundred metres, the gangrel will win any long distance chase.
                                              2- Fortitude would help the vampire run through spiked bushes without getting caught by the thorns.
                                              3- Bodies of water provide a great escape option for the vampire, who doesn't need to worry about drowning or cold. Shallow water may give lupines an advantage however, as crinos gives them an easier time wading through the water.
                                              4- silver is relatively cheap and you can carry a lot of it on you.

                                                      To add to everyone else's points, I contend that the "meme" of Lupines hiding just beyond the city limits, waiting to slaughter any vampire that ventures out, is a bit of "Old Vampire Wisdom".

                                                      Which is to say, it's how Elders interpreted the world back when they unlived during pre-modern times, and that they continue to teach their Childer, regardless of how applicable it is. Same with a lot of what baby boomers tell their kids, about employment or the affordability of housing. They cannot internalize that conditions would have changed so drastically from their (un)lived experiences.

                                                      In medieval times, the number of mortals and "developed" land was far fewer, and the wilderness and Lupines much more abundant. It made sense that if a vampire wanders too far into the forest, they'd get ganked by a random encounter of wolf men. Nor did a medieval Kindred have much recourse when it came to crossing those gulfs of wilderness quickly or easily.

                                                      Nowadays, there's way fewer Lupines, way more "developed countryside" (farms, ranches, parks, etc.), and far more expedient ways of getting from city to city. Heck, driving a car alleviates a vast majority of dangers to a vampire on the road, because they can cross hundreds of miles in a single night AND use the trunk of their car as impromptu shelter if needed. At least when it comes to Lupines, a car moves too fast to even be noticed.

                                                      But the meme persists, because that's just what every Kindred has been told by their sires and reinforced through peer consensus.

                                                      Heck, Gangrel likely work to perpetuate this perception. Insisting that the land outside the cities is still dangerous AF and full of furry terrors. They do this for two reasons. One, to discourage other vampires from building influence in more rural areas, allowing the Gangrel to keep it all for themselves. And two, so when a Gangrel is being hired to act as guide or courier or smuggler or whatever, they can extract greater Boons, by leaning on the idea that the non-urban areas are a death trap and only they, the Gangrel, know how to navigate it. It makes Gangrel indispensable to ignorant and paranoid Kindred.
