                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                            But in a more specific reaction, I think that V5 definitely is a more mature game due to a vareity of factors. . The Thin Bloods also get analyzed as a concept of people who are closer to humans than vampires and may have advantages that could allow them to replace humanity while also leaving them vulnerable to the oppressive system they are found in.

                            Like MAGE: REVISED the game is very much about reframing the setting to be less about occult mystery and BIG SCARY MONSTERS than more about living in the "real world" as well as interacting with mortals on a regular basis. The existence of Touchstones is a rough one but make sure the player characters are FORCED on some level to not be totally divorced from human society but have at least one mortal friend to remind them that they can't just totally interact with vampires.

                            The depiction of the world is also much better with an international global view of things like the Ashirra, South America, Mexico, and other nations with a nod toward V:TM's massive global fanbase versus its original super-America focus. We also get more acknowledgement of the changes to modern society with the rise of the Global Surveilance State using the Second Inquisition as well as the changes necessary to maintain any form of Masquerade.

                            The renewed focus on feeding also encourages player characters to think about the people they use for blood as feeding rather than just resource management as well as the increased chance of frenzy as well as taking innocent lives. That's in addition to incorporating so many real life issues into THE ANARCH and THE CAMARILLA. I feel the game has also gotten back to being the dark progressive horror game of 1st Edition.

                            It's a bit more vulgar but I feel like its renewed its focus on personal horror and taking place in the "real world." Mind you, a large part of this is due to Matthew Dawkin's great writing and the Chronicles involved. RUSTED VEINS is probably one of my all-time favorite adventures and is focused on a drug deal gone bad and human trafficking. I also was a huge fan of THE SACRIFICE which is fundamentally a story about how the Sabbat has failed and how butt hurt this made Malenkov. Its resolved not by shooting up the place but by appealing to Malenkov emotionally and playing politics with Sierra.

                            So yes, much darker and more mature game. Like 20%.

                            I still loved the previous version(s), obviously but it's my 2nd favorite edition.

                            CULTS OF THE BLOOD GODS is perhaps the best written example of the darker and more mature game that it's become but CHICAGO BY NIGHT 5E is also wonderfully human in its dark and tragic stories.
                            The WoD and VtM in particular were always about those themes. Always. There is no addition of 'mature' in this edition. It is nicely updated to the modern changes, yes, but it makes the nightly world rather smooth to blend in without any immersion. The world in VtM is just our world. If I were interested in re-thinking the current challenges of the international society and humankind as a whole I would've just opened my newsfeed, it's dark enough to start pondering on where I'm going to get food, money and support to the ones I care for today, tomorrow, in a month or a few. It all feels like transitioning the challenges of personal consciousness in the modern trying times into a roleplaying game. I don't find anything less mature in previous editions attempting to add supernatural, surreal feel to this existential horror of everyday(night) (un)life V5 is pushing down my throat as my sole trouble as a nighttime predator. Old ed's all allowed - and encouraged - all styles of RP including both vampions and personal night-to-night moral struggle with oneself, and the best games were those when you, your party and ST managed to combine the best of both worlds. Personal horror mixed with the global. Pangs of consciousness paired with sect duties. Internal conflict gripping you in the middle of a supernatural one. It allowed the lovecraftian fear of the unknown to be reflected and projected upon oneself to dig deeper than ever. The rules and the lack of coherent global storyline (the newsfeed of WoD, so to speak) of V5 do not allow room for that.

                            If it was aiming at the global audience it's a weak argument. I am a part of said global audience and I feel like this edition is the most American-centric, forcing a certain viewpoint onto me where previous edition did not. It is the most rigid and agenda-pushing edition in regards to the mindset it promotes. Even though I support said mindset for the most part, I certainly do not encourage this stuff to be pushed in-game. And I am not alone in that feeling among the 'global community' you mentioned. Rather personal stuff to mention, but it was in no small part the WoD, Vampire, books I read and games I played and even tried creating myself which pushed the boundaries, opened the world to me, so to speak, made me ask the others and myself the most difficult questions. Now, the V5 tried to re-iterate the same lesson. I'm not going to whine about hearing it again especially when the younger generation of players probably could need it and would find the same enlightenment I did back in my starting WoD years, but hell, why so straightforward? Where's the finesse? And the good old ways to counter that ideological leaning, the noblebright with the grimdark, progressive with conservative? Precise, carefully placed cynicism from books of old got traded for edgy internet culture-heavy jokes. Is that mature too?

                            From one hand, it tries to attract new players and make sure they are not overwhelmed by tons of old lore. That's awesome, I bet it succeeded with it and I congratulate the authors and the publisher if that's so. From the other, it tries a little too hard at that.
                            Sometimes it all feels like an attempt from the grown-up creators of the game to remind their jaded, old guard fans about the initial idea behind the whole story. It's a bit heavy handed at that, you know. And there's certainly nothing especially more mature or dark in tone or mood of it than there is in previous iterations of the game; it's more zoomed in to personal issues neglecting the global ones. Saying it's more mature in any way is a matter of personal preferences.

                                Cults of the Blood Gods pretty much gives you the tools you need to make the necromancer of your nightmares. No all of the powers, from older versions, are not included in this one book. But more will be released, in time.

                                The introduction of "organized religion" pretty much brings back "paths" for everyone who doesn't want to play with human morality. The different cults featured in the book, give you lists of convictions for them, and lets you adapt your play style to whatever it is you are looking for. With the inclusion of the "How to Build Your Own Cult" section, whatever path you miss from previous editions can be remade in V5.

                                The re-introduction of bloodlines brings many familiar faces back to the table, and shows me(at least) that the Sabbat is still going to be a force to be feared. Do you have to play Giovanni as heartless soul devouring monsters, no. But the option is still there. Want to play a homicidal Nagaraja....Go right ahead. Is Lamia what you are looking for? They're here too. Just because they are called Hecata, DOES NOT mean that the individual bloodlines have stopped ANY of their goals or ambitions.

                                The Lasombra are a part of the Anarch movement... Yeah, ok? Is it really that inconceivable; that the Lasombra would sacrifice a few of their own in order to extend their reach? Let's not forget that the Lasombra are still pulling the strings for the worlds most powerful mortal religion...

                                The Ministry, sacrificed a few of their number as well. Why? So that they could join the Camarilla? Not very likely. So that they could destroy it, from the inside out. Now that makes more sense.

                                If you want to take everything for face value, it does first appear that the power structures have fallen. But in the dark of the night, the truth will reveal itself. The Sabbat are still here, just not making as much noise. The Camarilla is still here, and crushing the dreams of fledglings nightly. The Anarchs think they are getting ahead, but just may be in for one hell of a rude awakening...

                                The hunger dice (Hated by some/Loved by others) give the beast a driving voice in the World of Darkness. All Kindred feel a hunger unlike anything any of us has ever experienced. The simple phrase that we halfheartedly use of "I'm Starving", for example. How many of us can honestly say that we have went for more than a couple days without food? Most of us can't go an entire day. The average Adult can survive for about a week without food, provided they still have access to water. Dehydration can claim you much, much faster. Kindred cannot eat food. They cannot drink water. Every night of their existence, is an agonizing reminder of that. The only thing that they can do, to at least numb the hunger, is to drink blood. The blood does not actually remove the hunger they feel. It just masks it, for a short time. And the more they force their blood(rouse checks and such) the quicker the hunger returns to them. Just imagine what it would feel like to not eat for a couple of weeks, what might you do to get that pain to go away? Even if only for a night. Then imagine to not eat for a year...

                                This world can be as dark as you, the Storyteller, want it to be. The same as every other version of Vampire. Yeah the Meta Plot changes with every edition. So, just use the parts you like, and discard the rest. The lines on a road help you get where you're going, but in no way prevent you from going where you want.

                                                            If anything, I would argue that V5 is even bleaker. It really emphasizes that you are, at best, a handful of rungs up the ladder in Cainite society. Elders, whether in terms of generation or raw age, are off the table as character options, leaving them as powerful, inhuman patrons and enemies whom your character can never realistically hope to match in terms of raw power. Your best hope of bringing an Elder down is a combination of cleverness, surprise, and alliances that can vanish at the drop of a hat.

                                                            The Sabbat may not hold sway over hundreds of square miles anymore, but they are either right under your nose in cells waiting to snatch you when nobody is around, or as a horde in a distant land that is becoming ever more monstrous and dangerous, a ticking time bomb that nobody is really ready for.

                                                            The Second Inquisition might be protecting humans from vampires, but they aren't discriminating based on age or humanity. Doesn't matter if you were Embraced last night and have no idea what's going on- they will torture you within an inch of your unlife, and then turn you to ash. Honestly, the best you can hope for is to turn traitor and help them, so that you'll be the last to go.

                                                            NobleBright? Nope. This is Gehenna stretched out over the length of an edition's lifespan.
