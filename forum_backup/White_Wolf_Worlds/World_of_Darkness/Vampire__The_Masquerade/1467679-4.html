        Originally posted by Amethyst View Post
        I have two sort of Abrahamic gods in my WoD. One is a truly ancient pangender being of pure light known as the One or the Absolute. Along with Gaia the mother goddess, the One created the earth and possibly the whole universe.

        The second god, a much younger definitely male god, is known as El, the Demiurge or simply God. He created the Garden of Eden, cursed Cain(e) and went to war against the Fallen.
        There's good support for this in, for example, Revelations of the Dark Mother and Mage.

        I don’t know enough about Asian faiths to include them, but I think Buddhism believes in a creator similar to my conception of the One.
        Hey, Buddhist here! Buddhism does not believe in the One. There is no creator god in Buddhism, and that was and remains a big point of difference between Buddhism and Brahmanical faith in India at the time. Buddhism is usually considered agnostic (though there are devas in some sects and some models of the cosmos, which are not really the same as gods in the Western sense). I hope that helps!

        Anyway, I've offered this fix before, but I'll put it out there again.

        The easy way to fix the Wan Kuei is to make them a worldwide phenomenon. Western Hungry Dead are like Frank Cotton from Hellraiser. They probably escaped from any one of the Western Umbral worlds where sinners wind up. Or they have some other reason for clawing their way through the Shroud. They differ from Risen in a number of ways--mostly that Risen start as wraiths who come back to resolve unfinished business; Western Hungry Dead start off as a divided soul (the Shadow and Psyche are in separate places) that first escapes some awful place, and then reunites both parts to cross the Shroud.

        Meanwhile, Cainites in Asia exist and have their own domains, politics, and so on, perhaps with some sort of treaty with the local Wan Kuei. But the Wan Kuei are more numerous in Asia because a) they are strict about Cainite population control and b) the Eastern Wan Kuei stem from a curse laid down at Mount Meru, which is allegedly, for them, somewhere in Nepal or Tibet.

        The Eastern Wan Kuei are the descendents (genealogically and/or spiritually) of the original Wan Xian who were cast out, and so tend to cluster around the countries closest to the Himalayas.

        The Western Hungry Dead tend to be lone wolves and arise independently. Most are mistaken for wights and zombies, and thus end up being put down. Maybe they have their own curse based on the history of the Imbued and/or Inquisitors. But there are myths of the Western "Wan Xian" re-appearing through history, so perhaps they weren't dealt with in quite the same way and were allowed to come back from time to time in a curse-free form.

        If you wanted to be really inclusive (and V5 does seem to want to streamline things), you could even make all non-Cainite undead use the same base template with modifications, to allow them to be distinct in terms of their "aims" and their banes. So mummies would be Hungry Dead who have a "Dharma" of Balance instead, must go through a cycle of death and rebirth, get to learn mummy magic, and aim to remain on the earth indefinitely, rather than aiming to ascend. They don't take damage from sunlight, as that would be a unique bane for the Wan Kuei, but they do run the risk of losing their memories with each rebirth. Risen, then, would be the Hungry Dead on the "Dharma" of Passion, who get access to Arcanoi, have a more adversarial Shadow, and lead very short-lived existences because it's much easier for them to Transcend or fall go Oblivion. Etc, etc.

        Obviously, you'd need non-specific terminology for things like Dharma, Chi, etc. So these might be Way, Essence, etc. Hungry Dead could have a variety of regional names too.
