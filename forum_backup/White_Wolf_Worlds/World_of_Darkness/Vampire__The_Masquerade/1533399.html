    How would you rewrite the original Giovanni clan description?

    03-30-2024, 08:50 PM
    The Giovanni were introduced in the first edition Players Guide which was then reprinted verbatim in the second edition Vampire Players Guide. It had all the basics of the clan as we know it, but things were left purposefully vague. Likely because the history of Clan Giovanni was to be revealed slowly in the subsequent Giovanni Chronicles and other sourcebooks.

    Eventually we got the name of their earlier clan, the Cappadocians; the history of the Cappadocian clan in broad strokes in their Dark Ages clanbook; and confirmation that a whole lot of Cappadocians had actually survived the Giovanni purge to the extend their claims of wiping out the other members of their clan was just ridiculous.

    I think the "mystery" behind the Giovanni were very much a PLAYER mystery, in terms of what we as players would learn in the course of several years of real life gaming releases, and much less a CHARACTER mystery in terms of what vampires probably knew or at least perspective from centuries of knowledge. So basically, how would you rewrite the original Giovanni clan description in order to accurately describe the clan as it should have been known back in 1991.

    Which is that the Giovanni family is dominant since Augustus is recognized as the clan's eldest, but that all of these surviving Cappadocians - the Samedi, the Lamia, various Cappadocians that survived the purge, and possibly ancient and elder Cappadocians long thought dead but could possibly be stirring? Obviously you can't refer to FUTURE events (future in the sense of occurring post 1991). Likewise there can still be some mysteries left to reveal, but no more than any other clan has. So explicit mentions of things like the Harbingers waking up is forbidden, though such mysteries can be hinted at to give inspiration to the STs. Those canonical events in whatever editions may or may not happen. It's just what the status quo should have been reveled back in 1991.

    So it's basically describing what is now called the Hecata except 1) no Nagaraja because they aren't actually part of that clan, and 2) the Giovanni are still seen as in head of it. The idea is to provide enough so that a player wanting to create a "Giovanni" clan character in 1991 can do so regardless if they are Giovanni or one of their allied families like the Dunsirn, be Samedi, a surviving lineage from Lord Camden or anyone else, and so on.

    So no more than around 800-1000 words for the whole shebang - general description, nickname, appearance, haven, backgrounds, character creation, disciplines, weaknesses, etc. One requirement is that all these bloodlines should be treated mechanically the same - they all have the same weaknesses and disciplines instead of the variety they originally had. Customization of specific lineages can always be done through appropriate Flaws IMO.

    How would you do it? I've tried writing it myself, but always overshoot the word limit by a lot. I just try to include too much detail and haven't yet figured out a way to make things succinct but interesting.

    Hoping people will be interested and that we can see some really inspiring examples.

          Alright, I’m taking a shot at it. This was a little bit rushed, with more time i would probably change a few things. I think if i were making the Giovanni from scratch, I would have selected a name that sounds more like a surname from Medieval Venice rather than the Italian version of John. That is why I have an asterisk with Giovanni. Maybe Rosellini? Anyway, i am keeping it as Giovanni now for the purposes of this exercise. I went a little bit over 1000 words, but I’m still in the neighborhood.




          Clan Hecata

          The Hecata have had a long history as a Clan of scholars and mystics, studying the mysteries of death, not only physical death and the vampiric condition, but also the spirits of the dead. These necromancers did more than study death, they looked for purpose in unlife and members of the Hecata were prominent in cults and religious movements, using their connections to the departed to unlock long forgotten mysteries. Traditionally more arcane and intellectual than political, the Clan had a long history of neutrality in the world of kindred schemes and conflicts. This would begin change in the Middle Ages with the rise of what would become the Hecata’s most prominent faction.

          At some point in the Middle Ages, a family of Venetian Necromancers was embraced into Clan Hecata. At the time, this mortal family was already centuries old and were masters of Necromancy to rival even the elders of Clan Hecata. The Giovanni* family, with their powerful necromantic magic, financial acumen, and fierce loyalty would soon come to dominate the Hecata Clan. While the more fractured and independent Hecata pursued their mystical arts largely independently, the Giovanni family worked together to advance their already considerable knowledge, used their mystic arts in combination with their vampiric abilities to accumulate wealth and power, and embraced only within their own mortal family.

          Within several centuries, the Giovanni family was by far the most powerful faction within the Hecata, so much so that other factions tried to resist their rise to power and were defeated by the still young and relatively tiny necromancer family. The nascent Camarilla, wary of the Giovanni’s rapid rise in power and fearful that this group of relative neonates might join the Anarchs, sought to curtail their ascent. After a relatively brief conflict, the Giovanni agreed to remain neutral in all kindred affairs, an agreement known as the Promise of 1528. Since that time, the Giovanni have grown in wealth and temporal power, but have stayed true to their promise of staying out of vampiric politics. It is rumored that this promise has an expiration date, that it was intended to last 500 years and that in the early decades of the next millennium (its 1991 now) the Giovanni will be free to take a more active hand in vampiric affairs.

          For centuries the Giovanni have embraced only from their own family, with young Giovanni mortals trained from childhood to become necromancers and eventually, if they prove themselves, vampires. The Giovanni are known not only to keep their vampiric blood in the family, but have long practiced incest as well. As time passed, the family has become more inbred, more perverse and dark, further removed from humanity - if they were ever that human to begin with. Rumors abound about the twisted upbringing Giovanni youth are subjected to and the dark rituals they use to commune with or control the dead. Perhaps more than any other group, the Giovanni are secretive, mysterious, and insular. The family has at its disposal both immense mystical and financial power, as its long policy of neutrality has allowed them to amass both in relative peace. It isn’t uncommon for members of the Giovanni family to occasionally make deals with other kindred - loans, revealing information known only to the dead, business deals, selling secrets. Almost without exception, the Giovanni honor their contracts and agreements, though their sinister reputation makes many kindred deal with them reluctantly.

          In modern nights, when most kindred think of the Hecata, they think of the Giovanni family. Other factions and individuals are scattered across the world, sometimes even powerful elders, but in influence and notoriety they pale in comparison to the Giovanni family, small as they may be.

          There are several other factions within the Hecata that are somewhat well known. One are the Cappadocians, perhaps the largest group within the Clan even including the Giovanni. The Cappadocians are known as religious scholars and mystics, largely apolitical who keep to themselves and, despite their relatively high numbers and greater age, maintain a low profile. The Lamia are another well known group, a mostly female cult dedicated to Lilith, some say that that they served the Clan founder in ancient days. Now the Lamia are largely extinct. The Samedi are hideously ugly, so close to death itself that their appearance is that of a fetid corpse. There are numerous other tiny groups of Hecata around the world, some well established, other merely rumor, though none can compare to the Giovanni in influence or notoriety.

          Nicknames: Necromancers

          Appearance: Members of the Giovanni family all come from from a single Venetian family, and are almost always caucasians with dark hair and brown eyes. The Giovanni also tend to have a distinct appearance, a certain similarity in features that arose from inbreeding over the generations. Some also have physical deformities as a result. The Giovanni tend to dress in conservative and formal styles, expensive suits or elegant dresses. Other Hecata vampires are far more diverse in appearance, both ethnically and in terms of attire. The Samedi, all having the appearance of rotten corpses, usually care little for style, save for clothing that may hide their hideousness.

          Haven: The Giovanni family maintain several opulent estates around the world, usually if there is a Giovanni presence in a city, there will be such an estate. When only one or two Giovanni are in a location, they usually have a mansion or luxury building in which to sleep and they often have other properties - graveyards, abandoned hospitals, or other such locations to practice unwholesome rituals. Cappadcoians favor much more humble dwelling where they can maintain their privacy, though they too prefer a location where they have access to the dead.

          Background: Members of Giovanni are all homeschooled, taught about the supernatural from a young age and trained in the dark arts as well as receiving an education on finance, politics, and other practical subjects. Most spent years as ghouls before being embraced. Cappadocians come from more scholarly or religious backgrounds - professors, priests, philosophers, doctors.

          Character Creation: in most cases mental attributes are primary, Giovanni nearly always have high resources unless some misfortune has befallen them.

          Clan Disciplines: Auspex, Fortitude, Necromancy. Different Hecata factions tend to favor different out of Clan disciplines - Obfuscate for the Samedi being the most common along with Dominate for the Giovanni.

          Weaknesses: being closer to death than other vampires, the kiss of the Hecata drains the life from victims as well as the blood, inflicting 2 levels of damage for each blood point drained.

          Organization: the Giovanni are strictly organized, ruled by the patriarch of the mortal family, now an elder vampire, often with vampires obeying the commands of their sire who is also a mortal mother or father. Other factions within the Clan have little in the way of organization.

                              Personally, I don't know if the original write-up for the Giovanni is that bad. Not for its own quality, but in a meta sense.

                              It's easy to cite "player-facing mysteries" as a reason to have them gain more definition over time as the playerbase becomes more familiar with the Clan. But to a certain extent, "player-facing mysteries" are also mysteries to Kindred themselves, in-universe.

                              Never forget that the World of Darkness is "The World of Darkness" because it's a world of conspiracies. There are a million things hiding in the shadows. Whole societies that keep their existence, much less their influence, a secret from most people. As well as secret from each other, both between splats and within them. Clan Tremere doesn't want knowledge of them and their operations getting out to either random dudes on the street, the Order of Hermes, the Sabbat, or their fellow Camarilla. Operational Security demands that every faction in the WoD keep their cards close to their chests. They aren't like the players and storytellers, who can crack open whatever book has been published and have all the Deep Lore spelled out for them.

                              Everything else being equal, one must always assume ignorance on the part of the people (un)living in the WoD, as a default position. You must instead justify why characters know anything at all.

                              Even as a practical matter, any given player (at least back when original WoD was going) was likely to only know what was relevant to the character they were playing. A player running a Ventrue PC would know what was in the VtM core book, and what was in the Ventrue Clan Book (whichever was available at the time). Obviously, with the Internet, all bets were off. But at least notionally, the Ventrue PC couldn't be expected to know the information found in the Malkavian or Tremere or Giovanni Clan Books. Much less in books that ranged farther afield, like the Giovanni Chronicles series. It's one of those things players needed to roleplay, their character's ignorance of deeper secrets.

                              For this reason, one must ask what is reasonable for a common Kindred on the street to know about the Venetian corpse-f***ers. Or rather, what's reasonable for a Neonate to know about them.

                              I would argue, for most PCs existing in the Camarilla, Anarchs, and Sabbat, the answer is "not a whole lot".

                              Even setting aside that information on anything more than a few centuries old is gated by a bunch of miserly Elders who see Lore as coin and have massive trust issues, Clan Giovanni is particularly out of the wheelhouse of most Kindred. They're an independent Clan that deliberately excuse themselves from the politics of the sects. They're insular and Embrace only within their mortal family (or families), so they keep a fairly tight lid on who can go blabbing all their secrets. They're functionally a crime family, so they definitely don't advertise what they do. And they're just creepy (both for the Necromancy thing, and for the Necrophilia and Incest things), so folks who know about them are reluctant to seek them out unless they have good reason.

                              So there's very good reason to suspect the nuances of Clan Giovanni aren't common knowledge among the Neonate age bracket (by far the largest proportion of the Kindred population).

                              Like, do the various branch families deserve mention in a hypothetical updated write-up? Probably not. Most Kindred may not realize they're Giovanni if they don't have the Giovanni name. What's a "Dunsirn"? Never heard of them.

                              Is the fact Clan Giovanni were an outgrowth of Clan Cappadocian, that rose up and slew their forebears, common knowledge? Maybe not. Most Neonates are concerned enough with just navigating the Jyhad as it exists presently. When every scrap of information comes with a price tag in Boons, is a Neonate willing to take on more debt for what is effectively trivia? As far as most Kindred know, the Cappadocians are of interest only to historians. Assuming a character would even think to ask if the Clan operating tonight was not the same as has been operating since time immemorial. Most other Clans readily boast of their ancient history, even if they don't share what that history is to just anybody. Why would a Neonate think Clan Giovanni are any different? The Giovanni a Kindred is likely to meet certainly wants to appear like the scion of an ancient and established Clan, so they're not going to volunteer the information that they're only a few centuries old.

                              The only ones likely to tell their fledglings there was a Clan BEFORE Clan Giovanni are 1) the Sabbat (who may describe them as either folks who continue to serve their Antedeluvian, or as folks who successfully killed theirs), and 2) Elders who want to badmouth the Clan as usurpers. Anything more concrete, and you'll have to start digging yourself, pay the toll in Boons, or have a Mentor knowledgeable enough and willing to spill (which the player must pay for at Char-gen).

                              Is it common knowledge that all those Bloodlines (Samedi, Nagaraja, Harbingers of Skulls) have a connection to Clan Giovanni common knowledge? Flat no. Kindred can certainly speculate, of course. It's not a big stretch to guess at a connection. But even the books themselves have always been very vague and wishy-washy over whether anyone but the Harbingers were definitively connected by blood. (And, in the case of the Nagaraja, the books were clear no relationship exists). If even the text demurs on establishing the truth, it can be assumed it's not commonly understood in-universe.

                              In summation, just because WE, in the Real World, know all this stuff through our collective readings of the text, doesn't mean it's common knowledge. Therefore, it doesn't make a great deal of sense to inject all that information into a write-up meant to represent what a Neonate PC is likely to know.
