                        Vamps in requiem are not squishy when put against humans but they still suffer from being the first game of the line, the other supernaturals are therefore more powerful although not to the exent of WOD where they are outclassed by practically everyone.

                        Yes, in WoD vampires suffer a lot from having a "ceiling" in the form of generation, so while theoretically some vampires are among the most powerful creatures in the world, I mean in the form of Methuselah and Antediluvian, the average player character is by far the weakest. Even then, Methuselah suffer from a set of "not so well-developed" defensive powers, to use a light term. Especially since Fortitude is by far the worst physical discipline vampires have access to. So, as I say, even Ur-Shulgi could be beaten by a pack of "nobodies" werewolves, just because the system doesn't cover up the said power of such a being has in the metaplot. IMO, it could be fixed with some simple adjustments, like allowing aggravated damage to be soaked with both Fortitude and Stamina and giving Fortitude some decent powers. It's even funny because RAW Fortitude is so bad, that Protean offers much better defensive powers, hell even Thaumaturgy is better for defense, with rituals that give the character extra soak dice (a level 1 btw, that could naturally be "upgraded" to a better and higher level version) or allow characters to soak fire damage, etc.
                        I always make such adjustments, because I'm used to ST crossovers and as I love to dwell in other night folks lore, such as mage and werewolf, and lately changeling, I know that adjustments are very necessary to have a "healthy" game.

                            They're far less squishy in Requiem 2nd, even without Disciplines. To begin with, they're far harder to cause even Lethal to, much less Aggravated. Things like actually seeing well in the darkness, being able to track and identify blood better than a hound, and unleash your predatory aura as a weapon are nice, too, as innate abilities. The better defined resistance Disciplines are just icing on the cake.

                            Either way, you can port the pieces little by little, and the similarities between the systems means that evenn without reading it entirely you can get the gist of most things. I think the biggest difference in the specific regard of Fortitude/Resilience is that Requiem gives Stamina as extra Health Levels instead of having a Soaking pool, but also makes avoiding the attacks in the first a default thing, so most of the time the attacker won't have a full attack pool and may even have a very tiny one, without need to declare an action for defense.

                                        Another thing that requiem vamps have over masquerade is that they can learn most of the disciplines without a teacher and without having to taste blood, the clan specific disciplines are only 5 (protean, dominate, majesty, auspex and nightmare) and are the exception but resilience, celerity, vigor (requiem version of potency), animalism and obfuscate are available to everyone, this allows for much better customization compared to masquerade. A disadvantage they have over masquerade is that, once in torpor, any damage can do them in easily meaning that stomping on a torpored vamp is enough to do it in, this means that being torpored even against humans, is a death sentence while in masquerade (v20 and revised) it takes aggravated or decapitation to permanently kill a vamp.
