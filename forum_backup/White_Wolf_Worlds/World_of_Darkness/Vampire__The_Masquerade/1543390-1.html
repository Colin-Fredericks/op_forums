    More from Rock Paper Shotgun: "Bloodlines 2 is more "spiritual successor" than sequel to "a competently good game by 2004 standards", say Paradox"

    Vampire: The Masquerade - Bloodlines 2 won't be an "open sim" like the 2004 original game, according to Paradox Interactive. Now in development at The Chinese Room, it'll be an action-RPG with a relatively linear story set in the World Of Darkness universe. This obviously plays to The Chinese Room's strengths - they're better known for melancholy or horrifying strolls through broken spaces than the Dishonorable massaging of intricate systems. But it also reflects Paradox's view that the original Bloodlines has been "mythologised" a bit: people love the memory of it more than the reality, and there are aspects of the 2004 game, according to Paradox's deputy chief executive officer Mattias Lilja, that simply "wouldn't fly today".
    (highlights from me)

    So there you go... Now it's official: It will be more like a Chinese Room style of game (almost linear, story-driven experience) with a little bit of action thrown in.

    But I gotta wonder: When they hired Chinese Room to do this, I can't imagine they hadn't set out to do this very limited story-game with a bit of action from the start. I assume it's that type of game where you get dialogue-choices towards the end which decide what type of ending you're gonna get - but outside of that, the player's actions have no impact on the story. This also fits the talents of the Chinese Room. That's the sort of games they're famous for.

    And also this bit from later in the article:

    If Paradox have learned anything from the past few years of delays and cancellations, it's that making or publishing games outside their traditional grand strategy dominions is a risky business. During our chat, Lilja spoke at length about the need to refocus and make smaller bets on projects in future. With regard to Bloodlines 2, we shouldn't expect another action-RPG from Paradox in the near future, though Paradox do expect the game to at least break even.
    (highlights by me)

    So it seems the future for the WOD IP will be smaller games. But also: They hope it breaks even. I mean, what can you even say to that... A glowing endorsement it certainly is not.

    Here's my biggest fear: It's gonna be a very short game - but the price won't reflect that.

                                    The whole idea of downplaying BL 1 as being "mythologized" seems to imply a severe lack of understanding behind how cult classic media becomes what it is. Cult classics are usually commercial failures (or at least under performers) that pick up audiences over time because they have something that people love even with all the other options out there. Whatever that is, that thing that's "mythologized," is what's actually keeping people playing/watching/modding the game two decades later. Nobody is expecting a sequel to be the original again with a fresh coat of paint, but a successful sequel needs to understand what the people that still love the original loved about it, and figure out how to bring that into a new story.

                                    We all know there are aspects of BL 1 that didn't age well even with all the fan fixes. That's why fans want a new game. But people that weren't even born when BL 1 came out wouldn't play the game now if the core experience itself was the problem.

                                                "We are happy to share that over the coming weeks we will reveal the four clans playable at launch. The first will be revealed next week.
