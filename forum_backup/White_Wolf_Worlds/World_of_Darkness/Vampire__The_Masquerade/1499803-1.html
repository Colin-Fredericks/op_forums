    Originally posted by Muad'Dib View Post

    This seem to be an overestimation of the weight or difficulty of the responsibilities, duties, and challanges that this Tremere Vampire might face and have to deal with. I think he should get some definite help and/or honest advice in regard to many undertakings, projects, and matters he decides to be involved with or becomes entagled in. Vampiresses and Vampires from Clan Tremere certainly have the culture and also as well the intelectual and mental instruction, schooling, and/or preparations needed to assure this kind of aid and support.

    I would also say that the Tremere Hierarchy (and possibly also other groups consisting of Tremere Vampires, Tremere Ghouls, and maybe also Ducheski Revenants - like Spellbinders from House Goratrix, Anarch Tremere Kindred, or Warlocks from House Carna) also certainly has rules, laws, and logistics which are each organized with at least some considerable focus towards aiding individuals / characters / fellows / persons in the role of commanders, generals, and also of course leaders.

    And it seems to me you are have limited experience with the convoluted ups and downs, back-&-forth and office politics that are at the heart, for good and for ill, of being in a managerial position in business or public service. Adding immortal ravening undead with a variety of magical powers to the equation can only further complicate matters.

    Yes, he will have all kinds of allies, lieutenants, contacts, pupils, pawns and underlings - those are essential to making things possible in the first place, no denying that, but they are people who may have multiple layers of interests, loyalties, passions, obsessions and so on. Departmental miscommunication, passive-agressiveness, obstrusiveness, distraction, personal rivalries in the ranks are some of the all too common headaches someone playing the role of undead boss might deal with in a nightly basis in a well-defined hierarchic chain, that without adressing people in the rungs below that might be actively sabotaging one's leadership for all kinds of reasons.

    Increase the scale involved - from city/domain to state, to national and then multinational - and well, i can see taking in that Quaesitor role as something of a sabbatical leave even.

        Anyway, back to the initial subject of Pontifex Johannes Dee and stuff to do with him, well....

        - I dare say that being Pontifex of the British Isles can be trouble aplenty. Just think about it, being not the regent of the London or just any other one chantry but having authority - and consequently responsibility - for all chantries in England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland and maybe Ireland proper too, with possibly one other pontifex acting as "midle management" per country.

        So Dee has to deal with keeping tabs and coordinating 3-5 pontifexes under him, dozens of regents across the isles and all kinds of internal rivalries, intrigues and external rivals/threats of varying scales these multiple chantries might have gotten deals or trouble with across the ages.

        Oh, yeah, let's not forget Dee was embraced in the early 1600s, what while quite respectable in matters of age, leaves room for some of those regents and pontifexes to be less favored but much, much older kindred with all kinds of weird ties, secret loyalties, obsessions and agendas to complicate one's unlife and watch out for.

        And we are are not even going into the matter of the commonwealth and how they interact(ed) with his sphere of influence and all kinds of friendships, rivalries or enmities that might crisscross the connections past and present. In retrospect i really ask myself about Dee's relations with both Councillor Wyncham and Regent Thrace.


        - Dr. Dee's connection to the appointment of Dylan Bruce into the Red List and past role as Quaesitor can be of major relevance too - for starters, because it shows how much clout the man possesses within the halls of the Ivory Tower in the first place. Second, the fact he's a major multinational pontifex kind of makes him probably the closest thing to a de facto leader of the Quaesitore outside of the Justicars themselves during the late 80s and most of the 90s. So, while theoretically below them, being the top Warlock in the field means Dee was basically the blood magician equivalent of Karsh within the premisses of the Camarilla.

        Plus, his personal interest in Dylan is on itself a ready made hook, in that it gives us excuse to involve Dee, any other quaesitors, alastors or archons he might have seniority over (plus all kind of allies, contacts, pupils or pawns) into Dylan's many shenanigans as presented in both Dread Names and BJD. Just how much does Dee know or not about Dylan, Gotsdam, Tiamat - or the Book of Grave War (that first appears in Diablerie: Britain, might be said), for instance?


        Anyway, those are a few ramblings that come to mind after skimming back & forth on the things referenced previously.

            There's a quite simple answer actually, imho: accountability. Being a specialized archon means his position theoretically has less power but also comes with less strings attached and supporters' interests/pressures for a kindred to juggle while doing its own thing.

            Think about it, being a Justicar would put him in a very visible position with a whole variety of tradition-related responsibilities for princes and primogen all across the world to badger him about. Meanwhile, while as a mere senior quaesitor, Dee gets far less pressure to deal with - for a similar or potentially greater amount of influence through playing the role of advisor to not only the justicar he's technically serving but all of them.

            Besides, the greater part of his equals are all (lower-ranked) clanmates plus all his work load is about beating other blood magicians to steal their stuff, how is that not better than being Justicar?!
