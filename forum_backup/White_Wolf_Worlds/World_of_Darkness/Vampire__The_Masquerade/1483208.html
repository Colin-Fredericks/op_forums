    Could lower generation dhampir-parents happen, hypothetically?

    06-06-2022, 07:41 AM
    This is just purely speculation. Dhampirs are fine as a concept, but their being a freak occurance of the blood thinning is something I'm fine with. I'm also fine with "baby half-vampires for everyone" being VtR's terriory, as I like having the settings distinct.

    Nevertheless, some things make me wonder if they'd make a "normal" vampire capable of siring a biological child.
    Thinbloods died like everyone else, their embrace wasn't any different. Their blood is so thin it's a miracle it reanimated them at all, so logically they should be more corpse like, not less. But they're not. Which has lead me to the conclusion that thinbloods are only more alive because the blood isn't strong enough to fully apply the curse and repress that life. Thus this potential exists in all vampires, but the lower generations have the full curse repressing it. There are ways to regain biological function/alleviate the curse, so, hypothetically, could these also permit siring a child?

    Blush of life; As of canon, unlikely. I assume that the blush doesn't stop the erasure or suppression or whatever of DNA, regardless of restoring sexual function. Perhaps having humanity of 8 - 10, a state that allows the use and maintenance of the blush effortlessly and at will, would strengthen it enough to make it possible, but idk.

    Domino of Life: Possibly. Depending on which version, either restores one biological system per ritual or all systems online. You could use it to restore DNA and reproductive capability.

    Corpse in the Monster, Gift of Life: Probably could, I think. It essentially allows limited daywalking, shuts down the beast and allows the user to "gain many traits of an ordinary human" and "experience and enjoy many of the fine things about human life" until until next sundown. Of course the extent of the effect is left up to interpretation.

    Golconda would probably do the trick too, but good luck with that.

    I also wonder if having lower generation would result in more potent half-vampires. The offspring of the 16th and 15th generation can only take a single dot in any dsicipline. If a 13th generation could do it, would their kid get a two maximum?

    Prone to hyperbole

          I allow it in my games, but let me be clear that we're stepping into House Rule Territory. A regular vampire can create a dhampire but it's incredibly rare and not something that can happen by accident. It requires the vampire to use various types of blood magic rituals and a lot of blood. It's an exhausting, time consuming process so even among the rare vampires who could perform something like that, very few of them do so. Really,for the most part it's not really worth it. Vampires have other options, like making an actual Childe or a ghoul and some clans have revenants. The two clans that create dhampires the most often (though again it's still pretty rare)are the Giovanni and the Setites.

          Among the Giovanni, the clan is a family and it was only natural that some of the members would seek to discover a way to continue their lineage even after death. Using the Corpse of the Monster: Gift of Life is a starting point, but also requires a number of further necromatic rituals. Due to the nature of Gift of Life, which only lasts for a single day, females aren't able to get pregnant and give birth in this manner, but males are capable of impregnating a living woman. The embryo carries the curse of death and the mortal mother often suffers heavily from anemia during pregnancy, and many pregnancies end early in miscarriages or in the death of the mother. Half-vampire children created in this manner have a natural affinity for the Underworld and are able to peer through the Shroud and can even reach across it. Unlike other dhampires who are limited to one dot in Disciplines, the children created by this method are also able to train a single Necromantic Path to 5 dots.

          These dhampire don't really fit into the Giovanni hierarchy. First off, they're considered lower than "real" vampires. At the same time though, they can never be turned into a real vampire, whereas any mortal, even a ghoul, could potentially be given the Gift and would immediately be ranked higher in the Clan than the dhampire. The Rosselini's see them as rivals and the dhampire are rare enough in the clan that they have no real support network. Further on several occasions, dhampires have flagrantly betrayed the Family. This has given them all a poor reputation, which has resulted in the Family treating them all with heightened suspicion and less trust and that only encourages further rebellion. In the modern era the Giovanni frown on making dhampires. It's not illegal or punishable but it's seen a waste of resources when there are better, safer and more trustworthy options.

          With the Setites, the Witches of Echidna most frequently gave birth to dhampires. The Setites claim (and it's it's entirely plausible), that they were ones who first created the rituals for giving birth of half-vampire children thousands of years ago. The Witches of Echidna are believed to have gone extinct nearly a thousand years ago, but their magical knowledge and rituals passed into the hands of the rest of the Clan.

          Children born as a result of Echidna ritual tend to have inhuman characteristics (using Gangrel animal features for inspiration). Mechanically, they're uniquely capable of learning Disciplines above one dot, but doing so lowers their maximum Humanity/Path rating, makes it harder for them to resist frenzy and makes their inhuman characteristics more prominent. Those who end up delving too deeply into their vampiric heritage (putting too many dots above 1 into Disciplines) end up turning into truly monstrous inhuman wights locked into permanent Frenzy. The Witches were able to control them via Animalism. Children are either encouraged to push past their limits so that they can be turned into shocktroops or are specifically discouraged from doing so in order to prevent them from going insane depending on their mother's goals.

          The ritual requires that the vampire in question be female, or at least have a womb and undergo pregnancy until the child can be born. As the pregnancy progresses the vampire has to "spend" more blood points per night. Notably this ritual can be performed on another willing vampire, allowing her to get pregnant and carry a half-vampire monster-child to term. The Setites have occasionally used access to the Ritual as a bargaining chip for convincing a vampire to join them.

          Beyond that there are apocryphal stories about Tzimisci who have managed to attach the still-living reproductive organs of other people onto their own bodies and sired or given birth to children that way, but if true that would be pretty horrific. The Tremere undoubtedly also have the mystic capability to create rituals to give birth to half-vampires, but it's not something that they do with any frequency. Any Tremere that chose to sire or bare a dhampire would be doing it strictly on their own for their own purposes.

          None of the other clans that practice blood magic (Assamites, Lasombra, etc) are known to have any rituals for giving birth to half-vampires. It's possible some member pioneered a method at some point, but that magical knowledge hasn't spread to the rest of the clan.

                          A different way to look at it is what is the ambient magic level at.

                          According to the cosmology of WoD, the modern era is defined by the loss of magic in general from the world. This is the reason why the Tremere made the jump to vampirism in the first place. If the era system used in both Exalted and Kindred of the East is to believed, then the 6th age will progressively gain magic until the cycle returns back to 1st age levels. As the ambient magic/essence levels increase, the greater the chance for more powerful undead to sire offspring in some way.

                          This then ties back to the old notion that Cainites are incredibly weak descendants from Abyssal Exalts. Caine was probably an Abyssal Half Caste, capped at essence three.

                          So it is less about special mechanics, and more about waiting around for another 20,000 years or so for the wheel of ages to cycle back to fertile essence levels. Though the idea of having to wait for some great cosmological event to allow a vampire lord to have an offspring is a bit old hat at this point.

                                  In strict terms - only thinbloods produce dhampir. As Anubis suggested this enters homebrew territory.

                                  In less strict terms... I think there's ample justification narratively for lower-generation vampires producing dhampir. One way (already hinted) was some kind of magical ritual or procedures (similar to how Revenants are created.) Requiem had a mechanism for Dhampir creation that went this route that might be adapted to such an approach. Alternately there are some spells (like some of the Mortis path like Gift of Life) that could suppress or temporarily reverse the vampiric condition - that might also permit birthing a Dhampir (this kinda makes me think of Talaq from 'A world of Darkness' as Mummy the Resurrection mentions that Talaq was restored 'to a semblance' of life, suggesting he's not fully mortal. Perhaps an artifically created thin blood.) It would also be similar to Yang-enriched Wan Kuei creating Dhampyr (an altered undead state)

                                  Another possibility is narratively that sometimes flaws or weaknesses in the curse manifest (often reflected as flaws) but there have been other cases (like Dunlop from Giovanni chronicles III - a 6th edition Gangrel whose sire had 'corrupt' blood that resulted in Dunlop continuing to age - about a year for every decade that passes. Time of Thin Blood also had a Thin Blood flaw that allowed them to age (1 year for every 2 decades) which was similar, and that suggests Dunlop (and probably his Sire) was a thin blood. And both Time of Thin Blood and Beckett's Jyhad Diary leave some wiggle room for the possibility of Thin Blood to occur at lower generations (Albeit far less frequently) reinforcing that possibility.

                                  Taken to it's logical conclusion you could argue that a 'low generation' birthed Dhampir is just a very weak thin blood that isn't ageless (just a slowed lifespan) - 5th edition seems to lean into 'duskborn' being a whole category of human/vampire hybrids)

                                  Anyhow, what this does for a Dhampir is open to interpretation depending on how you want to go with history vs popular media. Treating low-generation Dhampir as analogous to Ghouls of low generation vampires (same chart) or borrowing the Dhampyr rules from KOTE leans more towards mainstream fiction (I'll also note its not unprecedented. Evidently early edition Revenant as per MET and the Tal'mahe'rae supplement not only aged slower but may have had higher levels in disciplines. Since a Dhampir is a Revenant 'bred down' from a Vampire rather than 'bred up' from a Ghoul - as per Time of Thin Blood - they could be argued to have that similar potential.
                                  I believe the Storyteller Vault 'Accursed Heirs' had their own take on Dhampir abilities and that might be worth looking at.

                                  A more traditional vampire's 'powers' leaned more into the realm of being naturally gifted spellcasters and learning all kinds of magical effects to help them in vampire hunting. You could reflect this as an affinity for hedge magic, or you could also treat them as an inceptor that develops powers in the same way Blood Magic paths are developed (And with the same limitations as blood magic - needing to spend blood points to activate Paths.) Time of Thin Blood actually has a bunch of rules and suggestions for how you 'develop' new disciplines and the guidelines for statting them out that might be useful here.

                                  Yet a third take is to lean into Requiem and Kindred of the East's view that dhampir/Dhampyr are creatures of fate and probability given their unnatural and improbable birth. That means you could stress their magical power as a form of probability manipulation (some game systmes like Demon the Fallen's Storyteller companion or HtR's rulebook treat mage coincidental magic as 'luck' in much the same way Half-damned Dhampyr's Joss powers did.)

                                  In that same vein you might consider that some vampire (and thin blood especially) flaws apply to evolved Dhampir as well (some vulnerability to sunlight, or garlic, or holy symbols perhaps in order to reflect the fact that the curse is stronger in them.)

                                      As an exceedingly rare occurrence I see no problem with this notion. The only problem is defining what would be the "defining spark", the particular circumstance that make fertility a possibility for the vampire. My take on it would be as such:

                                      First you define the "base state" of the vampire: how old, Humanity or Path Rating, and if they have the Thin Blood Flaw. Becoming fertile is "easier" for a vampire still in their natural life span, specially if they would be still in fertile age if still alive. Even more in the first night after the Embrace, as they would still be fresh corpses. After a time when you would be dead anyway I would plateau it, so it is equally harder for a 100 yo or a 10,000 yo. A high Humanity rating helps too, but I would actually accept any high Path as long as having the child isn't a sin for the Path in some way. And the Thin Blood Flaw always help a lot at any period in time.

                                      That said, I do think the current Thin Bloods are something special, as the appearance of Dhampirs becoming somewhat less rare is an interesting plot point for me.

                                      Second I would consider what is the "aid" for it. Is it just the Blush of Life? Only under the most favorable circumstances you would have a chance. Is it a ritual like Gift of Life, that let you recover some semblance to the living? That's a little better, but the lost DNA still means you need pretty good circumstances. Domino of Life can specifically be used to recover fertility, rituals to that effect would be the most likely to work with less requirements. Whatever the means, the hard part is to maintain it for the first leg of the project, which is becoming fertile, generating viable gametes and impregnating or being impregnated. It would need at least a week and up to two months, even for males.

                                      But what would really matter is that "defining spark": why should the universe let this happen? Why this pregnancy is going to happen? "True love" between the wannabe parents would be a strong trigger, not so much because it's a magical sentiment, but because simple unadulterated lust and affection are imprinted with Life. Ok, it is a magical sentiment, then, but not in the more puritan sense, a fleeting romance counts as long as it has lust and affection grounded in the here and now. Another strong spark would be a real, intimate desire to have a child, not for a further plan, but for the child itself, for being a parent. Again, it doesn't have to be a healthy, realistic notion of what that means and entails, as long as the desire is for parenting itself. Other triggers may be religious or supernatural in nature, even an outside intervention, but without then you would need to really work hard on the circumstances to get a child on dumb luck.

                                      My take on the result, though, wouldn't be any stronger than "normal" Dhampirs. The half-vampire is its own thing, a state between the Curse and a normal human, born and living despite the touch of the Blood, not empowered by it, and I don't personally think that giving more potential based on the parent's Generation fits the concept. I'm not personally against having ways for Dhampir to achieve higher Discipline levels, but I wouldn't tie those to Generation that way.
