                        RPGs are for stepping out of your shoes and into someone else's for a play session so you can unplug from your life and explore alternatives with relatively no consequences. But this also means that any given RPG means vastly different things to each player.

                        VtM to a teenager usually embodies those feelings of angst, isolation, raging inner feelings, all consuming/obsessive love, and the various high school drama that goes along with that mountain of emotional & psychological baggage. Horror at this point in life is raging all over the place, but not understanding the collateral damage you cause to those around you.

                        VtM to a young adult (mid 20s to early 30s) is probably a way to get back to a time when they didn't have the responsibilities that they endure now. The PCs have career paths that are fun, they party every night, they travel and go on adventures. Basically, everything a 9 to 5 eternal wage slave dreams of, will never get, and can only labor under massive amounts of financial debt. The horror of this point of life is that the wage slave existence is eternal, you will never be rested, you will never have a better life, and your chances at finding a mate are dwindling by the year.

                        VtM for a mature adult (mid 30s to 40s) can go in one of two ways. The first is that the player is trying to experience something from an earlier time in their life. Be it the carefree days of being a teenager or being in their 20s before their bodies started breaking down on them. The second path is that the player is wanting to explore variations on a theme, in that they have made it in life and as such understand what is important and what isn't, but they want to see how that understanding of the world changes with different skill sets and goals. The horror of this stage is one of "what's next?" in that if you have survived and managed to escape the rat race, what else is there in life. You have been through enough things that what others see as important; you see as a fad. What others claim is a disability; you merely see as a weakness they need to learn to overcome. But the worst of all is that in the end; you just don't care.

                        IMHO this is one of the main reasons Elder Vampires are so poorly written in general. When you become a vampire, you are now ageless, immune to disease, have magical powers, and are separated from mortal society enough to fulfil any base desire on a nightly basis. Once you have secured an income source, a high security haven or two, and a safe source of blood, you have nothing left to set up. Everything else after that point is merely something you do for amusement or in the cases of more enlightened individuals, to improve oneself. Instead, Elders are written and used as plot points rather than mature fleshed out characters.

                        Just because a game claims to be one thing or another, doesn't mean that the individual playing it would perceive the situation as such. If a Lawful Good Paladin in D&D can slaughter their way through non-believers and have a good nights sleep, then a Vampire who simply accepts their unlife and lets go of trivial things, can be just as happy. Then again there just are those individuals who psychologically are comfortable in harsher situations than others. What some consider Horror, they would consider a normal day.

                        In conclusion I have run vampire games that ranged from goofy comedy to players needing to do some IRL thinking because they went to very dark places, and everything in between. But as long as the group had a good time, that's all that mattered.

                                There are several reasons why suicide is a heavy topic, and usually conversations about vampires and suicide aren't very constructive in the middle of arguments if "they would" or, worse, "they should".

                                But yeah, the bottom line to it is that it isn't a normal action in any sense. It isn't strength and it isn't weakness, it isn't courage, it isn't cowardice. It is too easy, both for those who are sick and think about it and for those who don't, to imagine or say shit about it. The reality of it is messy, painful and desperate in a level most people cannot grasp even when in its actual grasp.

                                Vampires possibly commit suicide. If you feel confident to deal with this topic with your group, go for it. If you're not sure, or worse, if you have a personal opinion on it and wants to show your headcanon on suicide to people you're not sure about... JUST DON'T. And it isn't necessarily a big cause of deaths among them, either, no matter how bleak you think their existence is.

                                Yet, the point of the thread is that their existence isn't necessarily bleak. Because the very reasons why we think it would be may not be there to begin with. We can form deep connections to people we knew just a few years ago, and cope without a problem with losing old connections as time goes on and they become sweet memories. I've lost families, friends and pets through my life, yet I keep making friends, commemorating births and adopting puppies that I know will grow old and die.

                                It isn't hard to think of a character that savors life and contributes to society just as anyone could being a mortal, once we takes in the proposed subversion of the premise and stop thinking this character in a Punk-Gothic context (even if still with a Punk-Gothic aesthetic). Actually, with a bit more effort we can have such a character even in the middle of a more "regular" chronicle, as a foil to the setting itself, something not completely absent in 1st edition, by the way.

                                                    "...And together through ages of the world we have fought the long defeat."
                                                    J.R.R. Tolkien, the Lord of the Rings.

                                                    This is, in my humble opinion, the phrase that best describes Tolkien's work. Ultimately, it all comes to dust; even Middle Earth will one day be over, and this concept that can only be called as Entropy is present in the entire Tolkien's legendarium, with each passing age being less magical, less fantastic than the previous one - the first age is the age of the great evil of Morgoth, but also the great heroism of humans and elves and dwarves, and the great beauty of the Silmarills. The second age is a bit lesser version of the first, with the great powers of Morgoth gone and so too the wonder of the Silmarills, but marks the ascension of the grand kingdom of Numenor, the ascension of Sauron as new Dark Lord and the rings of power. The third age is a lesser version of the second one, with Sauron weaker and jewel that was Numenor destroyed, and Valinor forever out of the world. But, it marks the creation of Gondor and Arnor and the coming of the Istari (the five mages, among which were Saruman, Gandalf and Radagast). And finally, by the forth age, the elves are gone, there are no more dark lords, and gone too is all the wonders of the world. And Middle Earth goes, slowly, diminishing itself.

                                                    And despite that, you fight; you keep fighting the long, inevitable defeat. No matter what, it will all come to an end. And yet, this is a noble fight, exactly because it's impossible. It's a call to fight for good not because it's an achievable goal, but rather because it is the right thing to do.

                                                    I did play once a 4th gen methuselah that reached Golconda. My vampire characters are ALWAYS hopeful and inherently good - I at least try to make them that way. Not because of humanity or any such. And no, not because I think that the vampiric condition (in my view) is pleasurable or desirable. No, on the contrary, I always find it abominable. But rather because I always choose to fight the "long defeat". No, my character will not achieve victory, or peace. Yes, he will lose in the end, either suffering a horrible death or losing himself to the beast. And yet, my characters will ALWAYS cling to hope - even thou it's a fools hope. Because the "long defeat" is a battle that is worth fighting, even thou the defeat is inevitable.

                                                    So, althought I see VTM as a very dark game, my games paradoxically have this shine of light that never fades, no matter what.

                                                    So, althought in my opinion the vampires from VTM have nothing going for them except their inevitable doom, hope and good are always present, even in the darkest places.

                                                    I mean, I played I freaking 4th gen Setite, son of the dark "god" himself, that rejected him in his face and went on to search for Golconda for freaking sake! - and that character basically became a monk, just imagine that, one of the most powerful vampires in the world and a powerful setite sorcerour, living as a beggar in the middle of the woods, drinking blood from animals alone and self declared "not in the Jihad" - which I literally was; and I could ALWAYS manage to enter ANY domains unmolested, because I always had some valuable wisdom for others with which I could "buy my way in". That character became a vampiric legend, sometimes refered as highly as Saulot himself, so yes, I do play VtM in a positive way. But I still think that cainites are beyond salvation - they are utterly doomed.

                                                    But, exactly because they are doomed is that their fight is so much more noble, at least when they try to do the right thing despite that.
