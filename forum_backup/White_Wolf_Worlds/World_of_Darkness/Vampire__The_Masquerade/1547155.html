    For instance what my Malkavian would tell a neonate about he knows about Vykos.

    "If a 3rd of the stories about this Tzismce is true, then we are in a world of trouble and you better pray that you have a quick way to end yourself.       My gaming group and I are almost wholly uninterested in any of the signature characters so we don't use them or mention them in our chronicles. However, there are undoubtedly vampires famous/well known/notorious to the greater vampire community around the world (as opposed to well known to local vampires who dwells in the same domain).

      For the most part, these are probably characters currently active now as opposed to figures that are simply historically important. Vampires are much more likely to know something about one of the current Justicars than someone who did something important during the Anarch Revolt, but whose activities nowadays are not so noteworthy.

      To be honest, I believe someone like Vykos would probably be mostly unheard of outside the Sabbat. Despite everything written about him as a "signature character", the character is actually not that important. Within the Sabbat he would have the reputation as a respected Noddist scholar and cruel torturer, but he has practically no accomplishments to his name. He's an inconsequential figure of the Anarch Revolt. Patricia of Bolingbroke, Lugoj, and Gratiano might be known for their deeds, but not Vykos. If he played any real role in fashioning the sect in its early days, we've not been given any details on what he did so it's safe to assume he didn't. Vestgeir and Vasantasena are known for their contributions. Vykos isn't. As a Sabbat who still dwells in the Balkans, he might even be totally unknown to the vast majority of North American Sabbat who would have no knowledge and little interest in the scholarly libraries he maintains there. He'd be known only to other serious scholars within the Sabbat, by members of its top hierarchy, and the relatively few Sabbat who actually survive in eastern Europe. Outside the Sabbat, his name wouldn't be known at all except elders who might remember interaction with him when they were younger, have some personal history in Constantinople, or are one of the people in charge of fighting the Sabbat and have some internal knowledge of that sect.

      In general, I think we players know far too much of the setting compared to actual characters in the setting, and we tend to make lots of assumptions about how much our characters would actually know. That's why as ST I like to create my own mysteries and characters while keeping the broad overview of the setting and its history intact (what would be known if one only had the corebook). That way well read players don't have any advantage over ones who are not, and things are kept more mysterious.

          Usually, the average character knows next to nothing about the "signature characters" in the game.

          Without social media, most modern people wouldn't know who lives down the block or possibly even next door. Vampires are a true minority population which doesn't have a lot of communication between its various strongholds, let alone between clans or between elders and neonates.

          VtM has this weird almost "double think" game design where the vampires are simultaneously a highly integrated society that governs most aspects of unlife and every city practically being its own independent city state that has no real interaction with other cities or princes.

          Pretty much the entire modern world runs off computers, yet in the modern day I would guess that most youngsters have barely any idea who Steve Jobs or Bill Gates are or how they pretty much built everything we use on a daily basis. Signature Characters would be like asking a Gen Z or Gen Alpha what they know about Steve Wozniak or Alexander Turing. This becomes even more extreme when you consider the time involved with some of these characters, Turing only takes us back to WWII in the 20th century. Given their ages it would be like expecting a Gen Z or Gen Alpha to know about the Antikythera mechanism and how it relates to modern computers.

                          Honestly, i'd have more of a problem defining who might a character of mine per se be of late than what its hypothetical knowledge or familiarity with any signature characters might be, it has been more than a few years since i last played.

                          But i think.... yeah, Melchiah, the legate of Cain, an individual of most dubious lineage embraced in the middle ages, and that claims to have been ressurected by Caine in the '90s after meeting final death in Berlin during the Thirty Years' War (most everybody agrees he just entered torpor and was woken by the Beckoning of the "False Caine" incident in the city).

                          He is part of a noddist society - they refuse the label "Gehenna cult" for a number of reasons - that formed around the "False Caine", believe him to be very true (in a manner of speaking) and know him to be very much still active and around. Melchiah in particular is part of a cabal of "oracles", kindred with extensive Auspex and occult knowledge dedicated to keeping track of any possible sightings of the first kindred and passing information along to other worshippers who might desire to follow its tracks in pilgrimage or seek to meet their guide once more.

                          ​A particularly wild but extensive series of rumours about Caine appearing as a taxi driver and possibly colaborating to the fall of prince Lacroix and the Camarilla presence in Los Angeles led Melchiah & his coterie of brethren-bodyguards-templars to that city and a journey where they would at least partly track down Beckett's footsteps in BJD​, if not outright collaborate/compete with the notorious noddist troublemaker extraordinaire in many steps of his quests, bumping with many (though far from all) of the book's dramatis personae & events along the way.

                          He has developed an intense frenemies/rivalry dynamic with Beckett, a decidedly more cordial & respectful one with Hesha Ruhadze, led​ Aisling Sturbridge from considerable distrust of the clanless to paroxyms of fear & paranoia (to the point of currently believing he's either a Salubri or direct descendant of Lazarus in disguise) through a series of misunderstandings, managed to drive Vykos to frenzy with, on Theo Bell's words, "sheer tactical weaponized offensive ignorance genius" of the elder Tzimisce's past (and something of a noodle incident of the game that Beckett and Bell both enjoyed retelling a little too much).

                          He would also later on as the chronicle advanced reveal previous familiarity with Dr. Mortius, Lucita and, to a much greater and surprising degree, with Fatima Al-Faqadi, Victoria Ash - and Carna, of whom he was once lover and companion in her time as prince of Marseille, according to Fatima. She has also dropped occasional hints Melvin (Melchiah's "pre-rebirth" name) ​is not in his first search for the Book of Grave War, might in fact be one of the first Panders, served for a time in the Fire Court, once belonged to a now-dead clan and, for some reason she doesn't really know, had some massive bad blood on the part of Tegyrius (but might relate somehow to Melchiah's impressive breadth of knowledge on setite, ravnos & cappadocian beliefs and occult lore or however he obtained it), who would probably like even less of learning of his closeness to Victoria.​

                              My character is a former Sabbat City Gangrel masquerading as a Caitiff in a Camarilla city. His un-life began violently as a shovelhead, clawing his way to survival during the chaos of his forced embrace. Now, he serves as a Hound for the local Sheriff, a role he performs out of necessity. The Sheriff, the closest thing he has to an ally, wields the truth of his Sabbat past like a blade at his throat. Disillusioned and mistrustful, he exists in a perpetual state of paranoia. No one in the city truly trusts him, and he doesn’t blame them. Yet, despite this, he continues to play his part, investigating and eliminating threats at the Sheriff’s command. Deep down, he knows he’s just a disposable pawn in the Sheriff’s bid for power. His loyalty is not born of respect or friendship but of necessity and fear. He knows enough to tread carefully when it comes to signature figures in the world of Kindred politics. Tales of the Nosferatu’s secretive network make him uneasy, knowing they might already know his secret. His knowledge isn’t deep, but it’s pragmatic. At the end of the night, he’s just trying to stay one step ahead, whether it’s of the Sheriff, the Camarilla, or the truth.
