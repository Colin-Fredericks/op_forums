    The problem with the puritans (in my opinion of course), is that they're almost all horrible at creative endeavors, and daring to be further bold, just terrible at understanding what made Classic World of Darkness so interesting. I have more of an admittedly neophyte perspective to preface, as I'm only "older" GEN Z and wasn't around and kicking when the classic era was still in full motion.

    The ability of choice of how to run and interact with the world, is a staple of what an RPG should be. Though that's not to imply that mitigation of that aspect, when trying to incur a certain atmosphere or game logic in concert with metaplot - i.e. generations with Masquerade, in contrast with the fluctuating potency of Requiem; where Masquerade is about power lying in the eldest, wherein the young most plot to subsume (diablerize) such elders if they wish to truly ascend to the highest point of Kindred society, Requiem is simply about just waiting long enough for you to get that power and nothing else - said mitigation is only really good when it's done carefully and emplaced at near-perfect places of design ("near," because chasing perfection shouldn't be the point).

    The puritanical branch of White Wolf Writers in vivid contrast, whether old or new, think they've actually achieved orchestrated pure artistry by emphasizing the "human" or "street level" (the latter playstyle I've come to abhor because how it was used to break Masquerade, then the other games) and, in the same breadth, by mitigating global scale, outsized epic plots, and supernatural grand strategy; that they have kept and/or "saved" World of Darkness.. when all they have done is completely, and inequivalently, destroyed it.

    In an effort to be "grounded" (when ironically most of their implementations have been completely nonsensical in the greater scheme of oWoD itself) they have essentially become an extreme reverse of the so-called "trenchcoat munchkin katana" style that they have so despised; YA-tier yuppie posers, who only bare to have the most limited perception of media outside of YA, and think every other genre is "silly," when in actuality most of YA is undetailed and surface level trash (which WoD has unfortunately aped due to the horrible writing and lack of care with this edition).

    This is very evident with how the non-VTM X5 titles were dealt with. Whilst V5 had a lot of lorebreaking retcons, they could remain retcons as such because the writers at least somewhat played VTM or some iterations based on it (Requiem) somewhat knew what they were doing; but it's very apparent that they did not understand Reckoning or Apocalypse, at all. From the Imbued being removed from their literal main game to Werewolf being a complete erasure-baring reboot (with the absolute worst cardinal abolishment in Werewolf being the de-emphasized connection with the Umbra.. in a game outside of the Werewolves, is primarily about spirits), you can tell what they remove and what they keep follow a pattern (in overall relation to what they perceived as "grounded" and how they excise specific aspects too "high-octane gonzo" or "too grognard" (term-of-phase many along the puritanical prescribe to fans of Classic World of Darkness))

    For example, to circle back to Masquerade, the Tremere and the Lasombra:

    The Tremere were targeted for removal by the puritanicals due to many factors;

    1) they kept the Camarilla too strong. It has become obvious these revised-era puritanicals favored the Anarchs over the former, so they fabricated that contrived SI aerial missile assault on the canonically teleporting blood magically cloaked Vienna Chantry, wherein subsequently the major Clan flaw of Tremere of loyalty to the Seven became non-existent and now Anarch Tremere are (un)surprisingly now possible! Not only Anarch Tremere. How convenient and completely grounded, and of no way of an artificial contrivance of logic!

    2) while I never really found the Tremere interesting, it's well apparent that many of the non-puritanicals of VTM (I'll henceforth prescribe them as the "gonzo-radicalists") liked the Tremere because of their extended granular powerset in the form of Thaumaturgy. So many Paths and Rituals to them, it was honestly a beautiful sight; a problem for the puritcanicals, however, because such a thing was of course "badwrongfun." But on another route of circulation, the puritanicals liked the IDEA of Thaumaturgy and/or (now generically parlanced as) "Blood Sorcery." The solution? What I mentioned they already did in the first point. Two birds with one stone (in the overall tangent scheme of "streamline" game design philosophy); remove a "problematic" "overcomplexitied" perceived "munchkin-like" Discipline and co-opt / mutate a power to be "defanged" so to speak, to be more palpable to the arbitrariness of the puritans (and their small unimaginative audience).

    Such as thing was not exclusive to the Tremere, with Tzimisce being burdened by this with the removal of Vicissitude and Serpentis with the Followers of Set (now Abrahamicized to the so-called "Ministry": which on another brocade, being that of a means to de-emphasize their connection with Egypt / North Africa from the game. You can tell that these writers adhered VERY heavily to the particular piece of advice of only "writing what you know" instead of having the bravery to research and implement respectfully. But no, just metaphorically Christianize than put in effort to what the former actual good writers, with limited research abilities of the 90's, at least tried to do). Also not to forget about Necromancy and the erasure of its Paths, along with its absolute hamfistery of so-called "amalgamation" with Obtenebration...

    And speaking of Obtenebration, now the Lasombra:

        OK, I think this has gotten pretty far from helping to clarify the impact of hiring specific old school WW creatives to work on WoD5. We might be "on topic" for the broad forum, but this isn't a thread for all of this. It's also getting from beyond some minor snide jabs (though to be clear, largely at things done over 25 years ago), into a whole essay about design mentalities in TTRPG creators/developers/etc.

        First, a bit of a pet peeve: lets not call them puritans. In English at least, a puritan is a member of one of the Puritan movements of Protestant Christianity. While the adjective puritanical does come from deriding people for acting like the Puritans, the noun didn't acquire the same dual use. If people are really desperate for a label why don't we use one that actually communicates the problem. The attitude that got Justin so unpopular back in 1999 was that he's a prescriptivist. That he, he thinks there are "correct" and "incorrect" ways to play a TTRPG internal to the game (as opposed to violations of social norms like cheating your dice rolls, mistreating other players, etc. which are incorrect ways to play any game). It's also a term that doesn't run afoul of what happens when the creator of a game feels that way about it. None of us here might give a shit about how Mark RH feels is the proper way to play VtM, but it's hard to consider him puritanical for having a very narrow idea of what that is since he created it in it's "pure" original form before other people tampered with it into something else (that was, after all kinda the whole point of Puritanism: to reform Christianity to its "proper" "pure" form, not to just recreate the "mistakes" of the Catholics but with the King in charge instead of the Pope). Prescriptive thinking is still a perfectly appropriate label for his comments on the matter since we get to play the game however we want and no prescriptivist can do more than fume about it while we go have fun.

        And lets face it, prescriptivism is way more common in TTRPG creatives than most of us as fans would like to admit. It's an inevitable side effect of having a creative vision, and the drive to actualize it into a game. People make games to be played a specific way, because that's what they're designing and what we're paying them to do. I've listened to and read enough Steve Jackson to know that "universal" RPG makers are just as subject to this as people that make highly bespoke indie games. What pisses us of isn't the idea that the people that made the games designed them with specific game play loops in mind (we actually love that, and I'd argue that W5 and H5 suffer heavily from Justin not really caring as much about that and just making Paradox happy with core books before he moved on to the next job he had lined up, rather than too much prescriptivist mentality), but the badwrongfun push back when people are having fun playing the game differently. A designer without a solid concept of an "ideal" version of their game and how it should be played is on that's going to make an incoherent mess because that ideal is what you need to judge the rest of the product on.

        So, to get back to Paradox: this is basically Paradox's problem with the TTRPG books. They hire loudly vocal prescriptivists because they assume that confidence and history with the product means vision, drive, and so on that consumers will follow (and they really don't care if those are old gamers coming back or new ones, a sale is a sale, and whether we're talking Bloodlines 2 or Hunter the Reckoning, the labels are always less important than having a qualify product that people actually want). They're not thinking "hey, when was the last time Mark made... anything... for a TTRPG that really spoke to younger and newer players?" or "Why didn't the CofD start really get a strong identity as its own game with its own dedicated fans instead feeling like the late Revised WW authors rebooting the WoD until after guys like Justin left?" and probably most importantly I'm unconvinced anyone at the company ever really asked, "Is Martin making VtM for Martin, or for the widest audience so VtM can reclaim a spot within the cultural discussion?" The point isn't ad hominem put downs of people because of a specific design mentality, but thinking about what the right design mentality is for what the company wants out of all this; a popular enough IP to license for lots of profit.

                    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                    Prescriptive thinking is still a perfectly appropriate label for his comments on the matter since we get to play the game however we want and no prescriptivist can do more than fume about it while we go have fun.
                    Here's the thing. As it turns out, they can. "No one is coming to take away your books" and "no one is kicking down the door to tell you how to play" are the usual statements put forth in response to what, sure, let's call it prescriptivism then, gets called out for in its impact. But they elide over the actual problems, and also why this kind of thing can generate such ill will when inserted in places it previously didn't really exist.

                    When we pay for ttrpg books, we are in part paying for a support service because we don't have the time/inclination/juice to just create our own game, rules, what have you, and especially in an increasing age of gaming groups forming via online grab bags of strangers structure, an actual published game is the easiest medium to organize them towards doing ttrpg stuffs, as it were. It's the most viably direct route to the fun/catharsis/Aristotle's theories about how to safely experience emotions you can't otherwise, or whatever all else someone gets out of doing the ttrpg thing.

                    If the game ceases to support playing a certain way or indeed goes so far as to make playing a certain way such a punitive hill to climb that you might as well not even bother, then the simple result is that the game gets less use out of any way besides what it encourages. For example, if Giovanni Chronicles 4 gives you dead zero nothing to help you play or run in accordance with the first three parts of the series but you still wanted to do that, you either now have to, as a GM, pull an entire adventure out of your ass, or, as a player, try to convince your GM and gaming group to pull an entire adventure out of their ass. Past a certain point of devs narrowing the crap out of a gameline from how you were using it, you're throwing away time and money on a game that not only doesn't want you to be playing it, it's taking, for instance in V5 especially, dedicated measures to ensure that vibe. Past a certain point, you have to wonder what the hell are you doing here, you don't belong here, you don't belong here (and the devs have definitely been vigorously suggesting you are totally a creep, you are indeed a weirdo). Past a certain point of lack of support, a game will mostly be a means to form groups for the way it is supported.

                    And the thing of it is, being real, VtM successfully sustained 4 entire editions with no really deep prescriptivism beyond "this is a game about playing Vampires in a darkly themed lore rich setting based off the modern world, in which there are a variety of ways to engage with the experience of that setting". Yeah, revised hated your guts for that being the case if that was what you were into, that range of play, but as myself and others noted, it could only sometimes try to actively get in your way about that. And yet lots of people enjoyed having tangible support for lots of ways to play make believe about vampires, or 4 entire editions of VtM would not have existed to have let them do that.

                    To be topical about Paradox and the state of vampire the masquerade, V5 went all in on prescriptivism by contrast, completely indulging its devs to go hog wild with prescriptivism. And while you can search my posts for me repeatedly noting that V5 has certainly found a fanbase for doing so for the past 7ish years, we have in the end come to the flaw of Justin Achilli style prescriptivism, or MRH's, or whoever. The game ran out of ideas. The game used up all the runway its narrow little approach gave it, and is now doing something that I can certainly note anecdotally is giving a number of V5 fans fits, and more objectively point to various staff and dev statements in the past for sheer sharp contrast with the now.

                    As I've said, it's lately putting out books that fly wildly in the face of its years of repeated mission statements, open derisions, promotional campaigns that made.. choices... and even in book material. The game is becoming disjointed and contradictory on itself as we get books about high scale plot, about romance and fucking, about playing older vampires, books that are nothing but grab bags of powers, books working in bloodlines that are now getting significant enough treatment to show up with dedicated named sections in promoted previews as if to say "no no, this book is still usable for you, Salubri players!" (which is ironic and hilarious for this being some of the most attention in modern era games the Salubri have received outside of Lore of the Clans in V20), books that make nonsense out of the Second Inquisition because ItV is popular again. It doesn't come off as the game having range this time so much as the game getting desperate and almost random in the face of the identity it tried to ground itself in, successfully or not. And that's because of, even in comparison to revised, how hard it tried for so long to do the opposite. Again, 7ish year old gameline. The people who came in new in 2018? Y'all getting older, soon you will be as decrepit as the rest of us! *makes eeerie aged finger waggling gestures, then has to go take tylenol arthritis for my fingers hurting from connective tissue disorder*

                    By contrast, 1st edition VtM almost immediately branched out in all kinds of ways, right after the corebook really, and 3 more editions kept that more open approach going, even when they so clearly and desperately didn't want to.

                    Some prescriptivism is a good idea, sure. Some measure of identity, setting, vibe, what have you. But taken too far? (And if you feel that Achilli hasn't been part of doing so, don't know what to tell you there) It's just the serpent that eats its own tail, and it's difficult to look at V5 and not see it running the hell out of tail. Maybe the new devs will figure out some way to refresh it (though any other edition of vampire after this many years would have definitely gotten the ol 'new edition time!' button pushed), maybe they won't, but they definitely have their work cut out for them.

                                    What can instead be said with concrete relevance about Pendragon is this: Pendragon is an rpg from the 80s about playing knights in Arthurian Britain. It is either famously narrow in its take on this, or infamously narrow, depending on if you like this game or not. It certainly does exactly what it tells you it is going to do, and rigorously mechanically reinforces that (as long as you ignore some of the source material it claims to us- *stops self* That's a different discussion).                                     The thing about Pendragon is that it is fundamentally allergic to wider ideas, approaches, narratives. It is 6 editions deep now into basically recycling and mandating the same notions, over and over, with little variance. The bells and whistles change a bit, the system gets more refined-ish, but really, that's mostly it. There was one edition in there that was more experimental, but also thus very polarizing in the game's fanbase and in many corners viewed as a mistake, to put it gently. The art improves I personally think? Though even that is a controversial thing to say in some corners.

                                    Pendragon recycles its adventures across editions (the biggest adventure release it made for its newest edition is a moderately updated for 6th adventure from 1st edition, as an example), with not a lot of variance. Pendragon at this point is, outside of when it was in its heyday and they could put out more adventures, basically the corebook, the Great Pendragon Campaign, and some supplements and a few other adventures for flavour. Repeat across several editions. The biggest innovation of 6e is that they decided to split the corebook into multiple books, and are going to do the same with the GPC. And they did a starter set I suppose, because everyone is doing one of those these days.

                                    If someone wants to tell me that Pendragon innovates just fine I'm going to respond that Pendragon's biggest recent deal was a kickstarter for republishing their first edition core/boxed set thing and that part of their promotional campaign was to proclaim, directly "you can't improve on perfection". I wondered if this meant they were going to refund all the people who bought the 6e corebooks they just released because everything after the 1e core was a lesser effort? But apparently not. It did fine for a kickstarter for let's say an at this point 5th tier rpg? Not an indie, but certainly nowhere near wider acclaim (200k or so)

It certainly has a very, very loyal fanbase that has supported it through multiple editions, mostly. But it is a niche ttrpg game, and ttrpgs are already themselves, niche. It lost its heyday much faster than the WoD did honestly, and is at this point, if we want to be honest, a few steps above an indie game. People in the ttrpg space as it currently stands will, even if they don't know much about it, probably have some idea what you're talking about if you mention Vampire the Masquerade, or Shadowrun, or what have you. The odds that they've heard of Pendragon are well lower.

                                    And I need to clarify that for various people, this is a feature, not a bug. They hold up Pendragon in a way that people will refer to an obscure musician as "your favourite musician's favourite musician." It's better that Pendragon is a smaller game than to have compromised its artistic integrity or some such thing.

This game embraces being calcified).

For the WoD to be Pendragon. An extremely specific thing, that enforces the "best" way to be that thing, narrowing its focus entirely that way. Wanting to be Pendragon is unavoidably a statement that you would in fact be fine with a smaller audience as long as you got to reinforce the true best way of gaming. In fact that might even be preferable, almost moral. I mean the 6e corebook has been out for over a year and it hasn't moved past Gold on drivethru. And that's, y'know, not unsuccessful? But that's the core rules, of the newest edition. Regardless, when you bring in those writers and give them even something resembling free reign, that is what you are going to get, and that's what we got. I mean Ericsson was more about winning fan arguments on the internet, but Achilli and Rein Hagen have for some time lamented that Vampire isn't the personal horror equivalent of Pendragon.
