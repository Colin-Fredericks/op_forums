                            Originally posted by Lysander View Post
                            How would you improve on making Ur-Shurgi more interesting and giving him something akin to a personality?
                            First of, embrace the flaws instead of pretend they're not there or aren't flaws. Ur-Shulgi was embraced too young and in the midst of a huge mess. Baali or not, being emotionally stunted makes perfect sense for him and I run from there, so he's not alien because he's old, he's just a broken child with the emotional intelligence of a too traumatized and never very self-aware pubescent or prepubescent child.

                            That can make wonders by just re-contextualizing everything: he's utterly devoted to Haqim because he's a father figure. Ur-Shulgi is permanently stuck in the Concrete Operational stage of development, almost incapable of complex abstractions. He legitimately believe that following the law is important, but the notion of spirit vs letter of the law is beyond him. He has more resistance to frustration than a toddler, but less than a teenager, and can barely understand what he's feeling. He's impatient, with little long-term thought, so he didn't went forth as soon as he got up because he's that good, but because he's reckless and don't know better.

                            None of those things need to make him less intelligent, but contextualize his actions and puts this intelligence in perspective, he is smart to an extreme, but he has glaring awareness gaps that are hard for an adult to make sense of. Which sometimes just plays in his favor anyways, because makes him unpredictable, but in the end it isn't a strength and won't work always.

                            I personally thing as a personality that's enough. The rest is to make this apparent for the reader and play its consequences, so not all of his actions seem just pure badassery, but also quirks and some miscalculations that arise from his failings. Have him make decisions that are reflexes of who he is, his mental strengths and limitations, what is important to him and what isn't, and so on.
