    Running the Free States - 7 - San Francisco and the Vampire Club

    04-18-2025, 09:21 PM
    Sam Francisco could play a possible important role in an Anarch Free State chronicle. However, it's important to note that canon is not consistent.

    In an earlier post referencing San Francisco, I stated:

    Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
    San Francisco never got its own city book despite a lot of things being established about it. A lot of early WoD fiction was set place in San Francisco, so we do get a lot of information if you bother to read these novels and short stories. More importantly we get a write up of "the Vampire Club" in A World of Darkness, 1e. Despite San Francisco being Camarilla, it is intended as a kind of "Casablanca" where all kinds of vampires can meet and intrigue with the Vampire Club being analogous to Rick's Cafe Americain. Very unusually, it mentions that it was the site of the 1946 Conclave that resulted in the “Palatine Treaty” which gave the domain of San Francisco almost unheard of autonomy. The domain of San Francisco was called the Palatinate of San Francisco, and its Primogen the Palatine Council.

    In historical terms, "palatines" were usually lower lords who nevertheless assumed some of the functions that normally the crown itself would have. In this case, presumably the Prince of San Francisco might be invested with powers normally delegated to the Justicars. Whether that is actually the case is unknown.

    It's not clear to me what exactly that may mean, but I am leaning to the idea that the Justicars and Archons leave it alone even though the domain is governed by Camarilla standards. Presumably there are a lot more details in the "Treaty" that would proscribe when the Justicars might intervene. Presumably this is to justify its status as "Casablanca" in its "Vichy, but Neutral" role. Prince Vannevar Thomas himself is probably more of a Major Strasser type with Sebastian Melmoth more like Captain Renault and Tex R Cainan as Rick Blaine.

    The Anarchs do seem to view San Francisco and Prince Vannevar Thomas as a threat, but presumably its nebulous status suits both the Anarchs and Justicar Petrodon.
    This is even referenced in the Anarch Cookbook which states:

    Originally posted by Anarch Cookbook, page 41
    Free States cover the northwestern coast of Mexico, through San Diego and Los Angeles, to the neutral Palatinate of San Francisco.
    However, this is not the case in the Los Angeles By Night sourcebook. San Francisco is not stated to be any kind of "palatinate" and is just treated as the closest Camarilla bastion that threatens LA.

    This thread is to be talk about the various canonical San Francisco in the World of Darkness, and how it can be used in a chronicle that concentrates on the Anarch Free States.

      Let's start with some basic information on the domain of San Francisco as it existed in the early nineties. It was mostly detailed in a series of fictional short stories and novels. I think there was an intent to publish a San Francisco By Night sourcevook, but this did not happen in the early editions. Only in the Revised Era after Kindred of the East corebook was published was such a sourcebook printed, and it clearly was not along the lines of what the early game envisioned.

      Although intended as some kind of "neutral ground" between Anarchs and the Camarilla, the domain itself was governed as any other typical city. It had a prince and primogen council.

      Vannevar Thomas was the Ventrue Prince of San Francisco. The primogen consisted of:
      • Tomaine (Brujah)
      • Alicia McGreb (Gangrel)
      • Bloody Mary (Malkavian)
      • The Dowager (Nosferatu)
      • Allanyan Serata (Toreador)
      • Honerius (Tremere)
      • Nickoli (Ventrue)

      So domestically, it was the typical Camarilla city. However, it was supposed to feel like a neutral domain inbetween Anarch and Camarilla. How can we achieve this in practice?

      In the movie Casablanca, those who resisted the Nazis and wanted freedom would go to the city, but be trapped there unless they could receive "letters of transit". The authorities were not hostile to people there, but collaborated with the Nazis. This is not mirrored well in the setting. If people want to get the Free States, they can. They have no reason to go to San Francisco. Instead, it is a bit inverse. Someone from a Camarilla domain who wants to visit an anarch should visit San Francisco instead and not the Free States. Why?

      Because the lack of any kind of law and order in Los Angeles means that visiting Kindred could get killed. It just takes one jackass, and you are dead. Instead, you should visit San Francisco where your safety is guaranteed. Your anarch childe/sire/friend/old ally/etc. from Los Angeles can then meet you in the Vampire Club where their safety too is guaranteed.

      In the LABN sourcebook, Prince Vannevar Thomas is just one more scheming Camarilla Prince who wants to overthrow the Free States. In earlier sourcebooks, he is the Palatine Prince of an independent domain - backed by the Justicars unlike Siegfried's domain in Vancouver - who will guarantee the safety of Los Angeles Anarchs who visit there (assuming at least they do not create trouble in the Bay Area).

      Obviously some sort of compromise between these two versions can exist. The question is which version provides the most opportunities for the ST when running an Anarch Free State chronicle.

              It is easy to say that San Francisco should be some kind of city of intrigue where anarchs and elders can meet and plot. However, I think it becomes complicated when trying to explain to PCs why the anarchs just don't attack and take San Francisco. That would eliminate any potential threat after all, and the benefits of places like the Vampire Club seems nebulous.

              One is to emphasize the power of the elders in San Francisco and the support the Kindred there have for them. They did after all defeat the initial anarch attempt to take the city back in 1945. Of course, the city of San Francisco is now vastly different in 1994 than it was in 1945. In many ways I think people would assume San Francisco should be more amenable or welcoming to the anarchs since the sixties. Of course, Kindred there may contrast that with how the Free States are actually governed.

              Non-Brujah might very much fear the Brujah clan, and we can make it that San Francisco is not a very good place for the Brujah. Other clans make sure of that. Yeah, local Brujah vampires may get dissatisfied, but instead of staying in the city and causing trouble, they might just go down to Los Angeles anyway. The other clans strengthen their hold over the city. Not only are the Ventrue, Toreador, and Tremere committed to the status quo, so are the Gangrel, Nosferatu, and Malkavian.

              Another thing is that the actual governance of the Free States don't attract many Kindred who might otherwise be sympathetic to the anarchs. It's all well and good to shout about freedom. It's another thing to see no personal safety, lots of Kindred dying, open cannibalism, and gang warfare. They are close enough to the Free States to have a lot of good information. Anarchs they once befriended are now long destroyed, and they don't particularly like how the experiment has turned out.

              So the LA Anarchs just don't see San Francisco as a tempting target. They don't have local allies that could help them. The elders there are powerful. Attempts to take the city would bring Petrodon and his archons to the city immediately. To take the city would take a great effort.

              Now because the Free States are very much a "do whatever you want", there's nothing preventing some charismatic anarch from convincing others to do it anyway. Nobody is going to stop them. However, I think in practice, there isn't an anarch leader who could convince a critical mass of anarchs to do so. Of course, MacNeil could do it, but we can easily say that he personally sees the arrangements of the 1946 Conclave to be beneficial and honors it. He's not going to stop any other anarch from leading an attack, but he won't participate himself. The other major anarch leaders probably have their own reasons to do the same. So that leaves only lesser stature anarch leaders who would want to attack it.

              I imagine some do, and that there have been various low scale anarch attacks in the fifty or so years between 1945-1994. It's just a quick way to get anarchs killed. Vannevar Thomas and the primogen don't particularly like that, but they don't hold it against MacNeil and the others. If they did, they might risk him leading the next attack. Better to just deal with the troublemakers as they come and maintain the status quo.

              Letting these foolhardy anarchs kill themselves strangely doesn't seem to diminish MacNeil's standing. Why? It's because the other anarchs know MacNeil is doing things to help the anarch council. He leads the Anarch Council and provides help to anarchs in other domains. A general needs to make strategic and tactical decisions. If he and the council decided it's better for the movement to leave San Francisco alone and support efforts elsewhere, it's something other anarchs can accept or tolerate even if they disagree with. Thankfully we do have lots of canon examples where the anarchs do receive help and even successes at places. I imagine to the vast majority of anarchs, "MacNeil knows best" and the obvious support of other anarchs like Salvador Garcia is enough that most don't question the decision not to invade. And for those anarchs who do complain, the problem for them is simple. If they care so much, why not do it themselves? Those who do, get killed. Those who don't look like blowhards and discredit themselves. In either case, they cause no more problems for MacNeil.

                          One thing to consider is that San Francisco was almost certainly the fallback position/refuge for Kindred who escaped the Second Anarch Revolt - in other words some of the toughest, luckiest and most resourceful vampires on the Left Coast, all of them possessed of a powerful disinclination to roll over as the Anarchs roll in, with a well-regarded, well-entrenched and successful Prince to rally the defences.


                          Another is that, like West Berlin, the city of San Francisco is strategically and politically-valuable to the Camarilla to a degree that suggests they would further reinforce local defences to the point where seizing the city would be a protracted and presumably painful process (No matter how certain an eventual Anarch victory may be).

                          If nothing else, San Francisco tying down a significant portion of Free State forces whilst the Camarilla (or worse, the Sabbat) hit the weakened Anarch Frontier would be a potentially-nasty strategic situation for the Staters (Especially if Camarilla relicts or infiltrators in the Free States made some trouble).


                          (I almost forgot to suggest that having an outpost of the Ivory Tower still standing tall in Anarch territory would have been - and may continue to be - valuable to Camarilla propagandists, a rare victory in the ongoing humiliations of the Second Anarch Revolt).


                          Another point to consider is that Anarch Idealogues (Especially native Californians made since the Summer of Love) might be entirely convinced that the counterculture traditions in San Francisco and the Bay Area, coupled to the Essential Rightness of the Anarch Cause, means that it’s impossible for the Camarilla to hold out in San Francisco over the long haul - and therefore shout down or knock down anyone who calls for Direct Action, so that the Free States can finally present the world a Triumph of the Anarch Ideology (Not just win another punch-up with teeth), representing the Sect achieving it’s full maturity.

                          Also, the ‘Counterculture’ Anarchs might be deeply reluctant to turn their old stamping grounds into a war zone.


                          Finally, it seems reasonable to suggest that the Free States have more to gain by holding San Francisco a hostage to fortune than by swallowing up yet another city - it’s easy to imagine the current quid pro quo which leaves a single Camarilla city near the heart of the Free States being arrived at after concessions and compromises that probably explain the ‘Palatine’ status of the local Prince & Primogen: by allowing these individuals unprecedented power to negotiate with their neighbours, the Inner Circle of the Camarilla also allow them to take any blame that might attach to said compromises and concessions - allowing the Ivory Tower itself to stand tall and retain the option of disavowing any of the Palatinate concessions at discretion).

                          Meanwhile, the Free States leadership have a nice, vulnerable Camarilla sore spot to poke when they want to send a message without risking their assets in Enemy Territory (and hopefully extract more concessions or score points in the ongoing Cold War situation).

                                                      As I explained earlier, there's some non-alignment with earlier references to San Francisco and the Free States to what is in LABN. The references to San Francisco there treat it like any other Camarilla domain. Still, more civilized anarchs might visit the Vampire Club. There's various reasons why they might.

                                                      First, the club has an "open door" policy. Any Kindred "in relatively good standing" is welcome. I'd take that to mean not an obvious killer or cannibal to other Kindred. Whatever the definition, it has to extend to most anarchs. I'm not sure how that is different from any kind of Elysium, but A World of Darkness 1e was written very early, and at this time "elysium" as a concept might just have been a local thing associated with Lodin in Chicago. Now it'd just be easier to say it's just a recognized Elysium. However, beyond that, I'd emphasize the welcoming nature of its owner, Sebastian Melmoth, who sincerely just wants people to enjoy themselves, including Caitiffs.

                                                      Second, its general manager, Tex R. Cainen, was sired by an LA anarch (back in 1916 who was destroyed in 1921) and was somehow involved in the 1944 uprising against Don Sebastian. Whether or not he counts as an actual anarch, he certainly has some amount of street cred with MacNeil, Garcia, Fortier, and others. Any visiting anarchs from LA are probably told to see him and not cause trouble. The ST could probably also some backstory about he and Melmoth participated in the 1946 Conclave held at the Vampire Club and helped negotiate the "Palatine Treaty" that gave it "unheard of autonomy" and seemed to make peace with the local anarchs. Like a lot of things, I wish we had more details on what exactly this did and how this kept the peace, but whatever the ST decides, it can give greater weight to Tex's reputation among the anarchs.

                                                      Third, I'd probably make some high sect status anarchs regular attendees just to give it some bona fides. I wouldn't make it any of the Kindred on the Anarch Council or the major gang leaders. Whatever the major promises or reputation, there's too much risk for them to attend someplace they do not control themselves. Not saying they never have shown up or never would, but it'd best to make any appearances rare and surprising. Nor anyone too disruptive like Smiling Jack or someone on the Red List like Germaine.

                                                      However, we do have good candidates. Crispus Attucks is one. He's a frequent traveler who has made a lot of contacts. He's a noteworthy historical figure that some Kindred might want to preserve simply because. And despite his physical power, he knows how to behave. He certainly has the anarch status that would cause many to respect a place he shows up at. Amethyst might make an interesting attendee. While important locally, she doesn't have a reputation outside LA. She travels frequently, and as a Caitiff she has both Melmoth's sympathies and would provide proof that he treats the clanless well. She has good social skills and due to her prior history of being a consort to the Prince of Baltimore, she would get along well there. Since I don't have Louis Fortier showing up himself, one of his brides would be good. I'd pick Elena Gutierrez as she's his agent to the outside world including other vampires. Someone like Therese Voerman would also make sense.

                                                      One or more of the Brujah Idealists could also attend, but their status would only be important to other Brujah who respected their clan prestige, not as members of the anarchs. Likewise I might have the Vignes couple show up on occasion if an event was particular to their tastes, but despite any local respect the LA anarchs might have for him, I'd doubt he has any real pull with them. But the attendance of more local anarchs would make the Vampire Club more attractive.

                                                      I'll probably add a few more to this list of characters I created for my chronicle. Of course, if I want to use this as a setting element, I need to find a way to include the PCs on this list as well. I'll probably figure out a good "quest giver" who would ask the PCs to travel there to conduct business on her behalf.
