                    In addition to things like a Kevlar vest vs. a stab vest regarding how they'll perform there's a wider issue.

                    A lot of people defending the V5 rules seem to be operating from the stance that modern armor works by "transforming" "lethal" attacks into non-lethal attacks. That an attack with a gun against bullet resistant armor changes something about the bullet so that it causes bruises instead of holes.

                    The problem is that this is accomplishing by having the armor absorb as much of the force of the impact as possible, and thus transferring as little of the force to the armor wearer as possible.

                    If you get shot while wearing Kevlar and get a cracked rib instead of a bullet passing through you, it's because all the energy necessary to push a bullet through your body was imparted into your vest instead of you.

                    That's where the V5 method falls down. This is actually something that hasn't changed in thousands of years. The technology has gotten better. Hard vs. soft vs. hybrid armors have come in and out of fashion in reaction to weapon technology improving. But the job of all armor is to absorb the energy instead of letting it through. The reason a war hammer striking plate mail causes a concussion instead of a lethal skull fracture is the same reason a 9mm pistol shot into Kevlar causes a massive bruise instead of a punctured liver: the armors absorb the damage so the target doesn't have to absorb as much of it.

                    There's no logical reason why a bullet that hits a Kevlar vest worn by a vampire does the same amount of damage as to a vampire that doesn't wear armor, because the Kevlar vest wearing vampire literally has less damaging energy for their body to absorb.

                    Or to put this a different way, if you accept that a ,22 pistol does less damage to vampires than a .357 magnum pistol, as the V5 rules say they do, you cannot adequately explain why without showing the V5 armor rules don't make sense

                                                        Well, the simplest without redoing any numbers would be something like:

                                                        If an attack would already do all superficial damage, armor reduces the total damage take by half its rating before the superficial damage itself is halved. So if your vampire is wearing Kevlar, and is shot for 6 superficial damage, it's reduced to 4 (Kevlar has an armor rating of 4, divide by 2 to get 2, and 6 - 2 = 4), and then halved because it's superficial damage to 2 final damage recorded.

                                                        It's a bit clunky, but the armor ratings are too high to reduce their full value, or reduce after normal superficial halving.

                                                        Something that should work on top of that without having to change the math (so, again, something that might be added into a supplement since it doesn't mean just starting over completely):

                                                        All armors have three ratings General/Firearms/Special. General applies to hand-to-hand combat, thrown non-explosive weapons, bow based weapons and the like. Firearms applies to guns and some other modern weapons if those weapons specify. Special is for protection against non-standard damaging effects such as a fire-fighter's gear helping them fend off fire, or a gas mask protecting you from pepper spray (bit of a guess as chemical weapons aren't really covered). If the total of all three is greater than 6, and for each 6 rating, the armor imparts a cumulative -1 penalty to Dexterity rolls.

                                                            My personal tweak to the V5 armor rules:
                                                            Change the text on page 304 to:

                                                            Armor
                                                            Each point of armor changes 1 point of Aggravated damage from puncturing or bladed weapons (per damage roll) to Superficial damage, which is then halved as usual. If the armor value exeeds the amount of sustained superficial damage, reduce the final amount of damage by 1 to a minimum of 0. While this protection is in general more useful to mortals and thin-bloods, as vampires already consider those types of damage Superficial, even vampires profit from being prepared.


                                                            Wearing heavier armor just hightens the chance to get that extra damage reduction of -1 and doesn't take all the spotlight from Toughness (Fortitude 2).
