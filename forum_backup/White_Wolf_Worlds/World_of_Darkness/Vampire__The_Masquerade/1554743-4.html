            Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

            The "western" vampire isn't any legend to begin with, just a pop-culture made thing, so I'm not overly worried about it in fiction.

            But most importantly, that's about the game and making it serviceable for the players.

            When you make culturally-appropriate variations elsewhere, you're not serving the players from that place, you're serving the western players with a "cultural experience" (that may be more or less stereotyped). It runs the risk of alienating the actual people that live in those places and want, you know, to play the game.

            In VtM the best version of this we ever had (and still with plenty of problems) was the Ashirra: they're a distinct political entity, but it has the same vampires and clans, it just adapts them to life in the Middle East. Even then, it's important to have a presence of the other sects in there, and also a presence of the Ashirra in the "west", because players want options, especially classic options.

            Other "localizations" of Vampire, but above all KotE, run into this problem hard. Making them as culturally significant as possible meant changing several basic assumptions of the game and all but barring local players from having a classic VtM game with canon material. They had to be a foreigner's vision of their culture instead of, you know, whatever they wanted. The Drowned Legacies are maybe the least bad, although they're still pretty bad and their lore brings this all over again.

            So, that doesn't mean you shouldn't have cultural representation, local political movements and more types of creatures. It means you shouldn't make those things as mutually exclusive.


            Closer to that: make more options, not less.

            As long as the presence of Kuei-Jin don't block Asia from having regular Kindred and their sects, it wouldn't be a big problem. Let each be its own thing.

            In fact, it would also be interesting to have non-Asian Kuei-Jin, showing that the phenomenon isn't just a cultural tokenism, but a real thing in the WoD that happens all around.
            ^THIS

            If you tasked me with making KotE work in modern Vampire, I'd retcon it to flip the "invasion" dynamic. Instead of Wan Kuei invading the American west coast recently (cringe, "Yellow Peril" garbage), you make the Camarilla invade Asia a century or more ago.

            You know, to coincide with European Colonialism.

            Western vampires marched into China, Japan, Korea, etc. alongside the armies of Britain, America, etc. Displacing Wan Kuei and defiling sacred spaces. Exploiting the fact that Wan Kuei are limited to however many souls claw their way up from Hell, while Kindred can Embrace without limit.

            Their victory wouldn't (and shouldn't) be complete. A tenuous ceasefire is reached, while both sides entrench themselves. Maybe having to keep their heads down in China during Mao's cultural revolution, where any whiff of superstition is ruthlessly stamped out by the state. Wars in Korea and Vietnam either throw nighttime warfare into disarray, or provide new avenues for settling old scores. Suffice to say, there's lots of interesting ways you could use the history of East Asia to spin 100+ years of coexistence (or lack thereof) between "native" and "transplant" vampires.

            What you'd have is modern nights where players could easily run EITHER kind of splat in the same locales. (Indeed, run both in the same coterie, if desired). Portray the two as equally viable, and equally valid.

            You wouldn't have a setting where an entire, rather large chunk of the planet is barred from a major splat for seemingly no reason!? It's unrealistic (Wan Kuei were capable of perfectly preventing ANY Kindred foothold in all those large, populous counties, DESPITE enduring European Colonialism, war, disease, cultural upheaval, etc?). And it's a waste of what could be a VERY interesting state of affairs.

                                    My fellow posters have a point, vis a vis Kindred who migrated into East Asia far earlier than European Colonialism. (And also that Europeans were influencing Asian for longer than 100 years. This is why I said "100+". I couldn't recall the actual timeline, so I had to set a baseline to start from.)

                                    Indeed, I imagine that, when the Camarilla (and to a lesser extent the Anarchs and Sabbat) really started making inroads in Asia, coinciding with European Imperialist forces, the native Cainites of the region almost certainly identified more with the Wan Kuei than their "Western cousins". Despite that they're different supernatural creatures.

                                    (Much like how, say, in India, the Christian enclaves in Kerala established in the early centuries CE probably identified culturally more with their Hindu neighbors, than with the Portuguese Catholics that arrived in the 1600s).

                                    Speaking of India, actually, one must consider how the Wan Kuei rivalry with Clan Ravnos would work. My immediate thought is that it's informed considerably more by rivalry between cultures and empires. The Wan Kuei of the Middle Kingdom don't appreciate Indian Cainites trying to muscle into their territory, for the same reason the mortals of the Middle Kingdom wouldn't appreciate armies from the subcontinent trying to conquer their lands. Once again, the problem is Imperialism.

                                    Cainites native to China and other areas, meanwhile, are broadly tolerated (to the degree that blood-sucking monsters, of any origin, "tolerate" anyone) because they assimilated into their local culture and power structures. Or else carved out their own power structures, and have resisted full assimilation long enough that their political positions are entrenched, with treaties and pacts and whatnot. With only the most ancient Wan Kuei remembering a time when this wasn't the case, and even that entrenched Cainite population were "bastard offcomers".

                                    The Camarilla would occupy a distinctly more antagonistic force, because they came in with all the White Imperialists, kicking in their doors and demanding everything. Looting, disobeying local laws and norms, usurping local sovereignty, defiling sacred sites, and disrupting the flow of Chi. Things native Cainites know not to do, both to avoid starting fights and for their own practical benefit.

                                    From a Chronicle standpoint, this creates interesting dynamics for not only local politics in games set in East Asia, but in the options available to players. They could play as Wan Kuei pulled from the native population. As Western Kindred (pick a sect), either Embraced from transplanted Europeans or from the native population. They could be Cainites Embraced by the native Cainite population, inheriting their uniquely local flavor of the base VtM Clans/Bloodlines. (And could be either native or Western people Embraced into it). They could even play as Europeans who died, got sent a Hell(s), and clawed their way back up as Wan Kuei.

                                                            In my homebrew games, I had Hecate as believed to be the first vampire. She and the Eriynes desired worship of their own and stole souls from Hades that they gave immortality and amborsia before setting up their own kingdom in Atlantis. The gods, being the gods, were furious and cursed the first vampires by deluging their city and then each laying a curse upon one of them.

                                                            The Book of Hecate, which my players had found, said not only were there more than 13 founders but that the various Clan Founders decreed that they would attempt to appease the gods by being like the gods who had cursed them. The exceptions being the Helios cursed Typhon who turned to the worship of the Scourge of the Gods in hopes of getting revenge and the Vestian cursed Echidnans who vowed they would unleash monsters onto mankind for the rest of time.

                                                            In the game, Beckett pointed out that a lot of Noddist ideas were similar to the ones in the Book of Hecate and he wondered if the Jewish version was actually a modification of the Greek legends than the reverse once the Hellenistic conquest happened.

                                                            Brujah: Athenans/Aresians (two bloodlines)
                                                            Banu Haqim: Eriynes (for the three castes)
                                                            Cappadocian: Hadeans
                                                            Followers of Set: Typhons
                                                            Gangrel: Artemin
                                                            Lasombra: Nyxians
                                                            Malkavian: Maenads
                                                            Nosferatu: Hephaestus
                                                            Ravnos: Hermes
                                                            Salubri: Apollyn
                                                            Toreador: Aphros
                                                            Tzimisce: Echidna
                                                            Ventrue: Zeusian

                                                            I had the implication be for my campaigns that there were actually a lot of these, "similar to the Book of Nod but different" texts lying around Kindred society rather than just the Book of Nod being the one. Of course, in my games, I also had the Book of Nod allegedly written by Cappadocias as he was the Antediluvian who had converted to Christianity and was also insane, framing everything that he supposedly remembered happening through the framework of Abrahmic religion. There was also allegedly a second Book of Nod that was written on the interior of Saulot's tomb which was roughly the same stories that Cappadocias shared but different names which the Tremere wrote down.

                                                            This is the only "proof" that the Noddist version is more true than any others. But talk to local vampires and virtually every culture has their own version of the First Vampire, Flood, 13 assholes, and betrayal.
