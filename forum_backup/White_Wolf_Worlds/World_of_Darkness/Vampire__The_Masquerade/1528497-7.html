                I wonder if abandoning the trappings of a TTRPG entirely would've been the best course for a V:tM revival after V20. It's been a long, long time since the game's target demographic has been "people who play TTRPGs" and not "fans of V:tM"; if these fans are primarily interested in the game for its metaplot, reviving the V:tM label for novels/comics/video games/what-have-you set within the cWoD universe might've just turned out to be a safe investment. Expanding V5 beyond the TTRPG medium seems to be a priority for Paradox, anyway.

                Barring that, I think literally anything that extricates V:tM from V:tR would've been better for the label than what they ended up doing. I don't think reviving V:tM was worth the canning of V:tR, not least when the result is some strange mixture of the two; sometimes it might be better for the dead to stay that way.

                    Originally posted by gamiem View Post
                    It's been a long, long time since the game's target demographic has been "people who play TTRPGs" and not "fans of V:tM"
                    While there were people who were traditional roleplaying gamers who gravitated to VtM when it was first released, the game was famous for attracting a large number of non-gamers. VtM was the first RPG for lots of people who were attracted to it simply because they liked Anne Rice novels, vampires in general, were interested in the Goth subculture or similar, or that it didn't seem to be about combat. Lots of people who never would have played D&D and similar games became interested in VtM. I noticed lots of people involved in drama or theatre began playing. I think these people from non-gaming backgrounds were easily half the game base, if not more. It's a big reason why White Wolf grew so quickly in the nineties. They were tapping into a market that TSR and other gaming companies were not doing.

                    Certainly TTRPGS were not the sole means of attracting an audience. They were really the first company that pushed Live Action as a medium, and that attracted lots of people to it. They pushed early into media with books and the TV show. And I know the Bloodlines videogame also attracted a fair number of people who did not play RPGs earlier. Not everything worked as expected, but they certainly had lots of success.

                    I think a tabletop version of the game/setting is pretty much essential, but supporting other forms of the setting is a smart idea and not exclusive to a tabletop game.

                        I think metaplot in general ruins RPGs. But I would be fine with a compromise where the WoD picked ONE location which would be a "living campaign" setting that gets updated over time for those people who really, really, really like RPGs as a form of passive, serial entertainment, and want to see how different writers periodically update the local setting and its NPCs. (Since VtM has a very worldwide fanbase now, I'd also be OK with each major demographic getting its own city to play with - one for North America, one for Europe, and one for Latin America (maybe others too) where local game writers/creators could develop. Obviously there's a lot of talent interested in the game in each of those locations that could provide a lot of local flavor where people located elsewhere would miss.

                        You could pick a city and design it in a way that you could justify characters from all sects, visitors from the more minor clans, bloodlines, and groups. That way all sorts of stories could be told. You could even try it as a "shared world" style anthology series like the old Thieves' World or Wild Cards where the editor kept continuity and consistency (or at least tried to) between the different authors.

                        Just don't do that to the greater setting as a whole. A single vampire domain (or Garou protectorate, or whatever) can remain isolated. Some STs would ignore it entirely and create their own chronicle, ignoring canon. Others would incorporate it into their chronicle, but not base their games there (but might have the PCs visit that city at some point). Some might set their chronicles only there. Those who really want to follow characters in the VtM setting like its a comic book or serial fiction could have their kick, but it wouldn't alienate others.

                        One could argue this is what Chicago By Night is becoming or could be, but I think it's the wrong city for it. CbN was not designed to be such a setting, and lots of players used that first established setting to do their own games, all of which went into their own direction. What would work best is a brand new city designated from the very beginning as a living campaign domain so that everyone buying the sourcebook knew what they were going to get, and thus not be disappointed when it got updated.

                                                    I'd made it 70% the same.

                                                    But remove the Beckoning (terrible idea to remove the conceptual 'The Man' to take down) and kept the elders and ancients more prominent in the game setting.
                                                    As someone else said, the characters need something to 'punch up' instead of 'punch down'.

                                                    Also I find a few renamings incredibly boring (The Ministry especially, I would just renamed them Setites and that's it).

                                                    The Sabbat could be reduced to a faction that went somewhat in hiding or somewhat more cultish instead of a controlling big domains faction of the revised era.
                                                    I don't have too much complaints about it
                                                    But it shouldn't have been removed as a player option.

                                                    The Anarchs I have no complaints they were pushed more on the forefront like in 1st edition, but they should have worked in making them more interesting. Subcultures, subfactions, player options. A la revised Guide to the Sabbat.

                                                    It's good that the Clans don't have precise sect separations.
                                                    All clans in all sects is good

                                                    Some stuff and changes I feel are improvements (the new Toreador weakness, the Tremere being split in different houses)

                                                    I dislike the basically removal of the bloodlines, a few were fan favourites

                                                    I don't dislike the push of the thin-bloods. It's one more player option

                                                    The resonance of the blood still sounds like an uneccesary complication to me, but it can stay as an option

                                                    So to sum it up, expand and not reduce.

                                                    I could have been in favor of a corebook that reduces the options but without negating them and allowing their inclusion in supplements.
