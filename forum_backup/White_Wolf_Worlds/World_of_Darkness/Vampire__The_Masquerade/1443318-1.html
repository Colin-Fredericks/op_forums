            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

            Oddly, I do think Humanity loss is much-much harder in V5 because the stains system give you a lot more leeway versus a dropping ten point system.
            They're both dropping 10 point systems with a set of "bad stuff" that prompts drops, and then dice rolls to see what happens.

            V5 is "nicer" on Humanity loss when it comes to the "mundane" evils of violating Chronicle tenets, where 1 Stain is significant (and can be offset with a Conviction completely) while 2 Stains is supposed to be particularly nasty violation, and earning 3+ just from violating tenets gets hard to imagine what you'd even do. It's much harsher on vampire specific stuff; esp. Touchstones. If your vampire is caught in a bad situation (and V5 actively encourages STs to set these up) you character can get slammed with 3-6 Stains from a single scene thanks to vampire specific Stains, possibly putting you in impairment, Aggravated Willpower Damage, and increased Frenzy risk for the whole session unless you willingly drop Humanity to end the effect early (and of course, risk having it happen again).

            So, I go back to what I said. It's not about one being "objectively" better than the other. They do things differently. V20's rules are more codified and less subjective, which may be frustrating or fun depending on your group. But there's also pretty much no reason a V20 vampire is going to drop below Humanity 4 unless they're actively trying to be an evil scumbag. In V5, there's no safety net where small things stop being a risk to your Humanity. A V5 vampire that has 1 Stain at the end of every session because of some seemingly small violation of tenets will, eventually, fall to the Beast. At Humaniy 6 or 7 it's a very unlucky roll, and it might be infrequent enough that you could save up the XP to recover if you don't spend it on other stuff. But once the drop starts (or more likely after a bad session where they picked up more than 1 Stain) each drop gets more and more likely for the same thing.

            Again, not saying one does things in a specifically better way, but they're very different ways. Claiming that V5 makes Humanity loss harder to happen is basically leaving out a massive caveat of, "if your ST doesn't want it to happen, because if they want it to happen, they can make it happen very easily."

                        How are the Disciplines and Humanity simpler in v20 may I ask? That seems to be the opposite from my perception.
                        To try to answer, obviously not MWiS:

                        V20 Disciplines are not really mechanically simpler on an individual power level. They're simpler because there's only one power per dot by default. You don't have to pick between options, and plan your picks to make sure you don't lock yourself out of powers you might want,

                        Humanity is more of a shift in what's simple. V20's Humanity mechanics are very straightforward: "if you commit a violation, roll your dice, then apply results." You don't have to pick Tenets, Convictions, Touchstones, and so on. You don't amass points that can result in penalties that you need to keep track of. What counts as a violation is more complicated because there's a large chart with a lot of room for interpretation (esp. for anything that's not on it). There's also a lot of fiddly bits that are adjacent to Humanity in how the Virtues work.

Because ‘Humanity' can be built and used in a more bespoke way, the need for lots of alternative Paths becomes slightly redundant - although maybe you could have individuals rename ‘Humanity’ to something else.

                                                            One thing I do like about V20 and prior is difficulties. In V20, 4 dice means you're competent, in v5, without house rules, 6 dice means you're competent, 4 dice will fail you most normal tasks without willpower expenditure. The classic vampire really enables diverse skills while the V5 method really pigeonholes you.

                                                            It's been said above, but V20's disciplines are better for a number of reasons.
                                                            First, simplicity. V5 presents us with too much choice; not only do you need to pick your Discipline but you've got to pick your powers within that power, and you have to pick carefuly because you can't have everything and you have to actively plan out a career path and choose which powers you will never have. Also you need some other disciplines for combinations so you can't get X3 without first getting Y2. That's a headache. V20 might give us 20 disciplines instead of 7, but you can compartmentalize easier; you know you'll never get obtenebration so you can comfortably shut it out of your mind, nor will you get Chimestry anytime soon. When you get Auspex 1, you're not excluding yourself from any auspex power, you can get every 1-5 auspex power simply by going from level 1-5.
                                                            Second, most common disiplines are in a very logical order. The extreme may be the physical disciplines like Potence, where level 1 gives you +1 strength and level 2 gives you +2 strength, but there's also a good deal of logic in the advancement of, say, Dominate, where you have a word they must obey, a sentence they must obey, memory alteration, complete brainwashing, and finaly you deprive them of control over their own bodies and take their action for them through possession. This makes Disciplines much easier to remember and makes them more like a vampiric extention of power rather than just a bunch of powers put together for a game.
                                                            Third; If you want a simple fun vampire and don't want to think too much, a Brujah gives you very simple mechanics; Potence and Celerity are very very simple, meaning you only need to remember what things do for Presence, but If you want to go nerd hard into some complexity, you can pick up a Tremere and have lots of different paths and rituals. V5... well, A Brujah is only slightly less complicated than a Tremere, because Potence and Celerity are now as complicated as any other discipline and Sorcery is as complicated as any other discipline but also has the benefit of rituals.
