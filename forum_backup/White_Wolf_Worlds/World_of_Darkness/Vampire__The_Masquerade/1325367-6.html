    OK, first off, is there are real point here, or are you just trying to turn this into a nitpick off as it if actually undermines the point I made that started all this?

    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    I disagree.
    Name one thing she actually did that wouldn't have made her a glorified Keeper of Elysium if her club and the blood bank were in someone else's domain instead of being in a place where none of the vampires care enough to take it over?

    It's always been about the duality of the horror versus the awesomeness of the power.
    The authorial intent has always been the horror of power (this goes back to that whole "did you just put in an element, or did you actually use it," thing). That that the audience chooses to engage with it in different modes is a somewhat different conversation about how authorial intent and audience interpretation should be weighed in critical analysis.

    There's a reason Requiem and V5 both moved to put some mechanical roadblocks to have the games work well as pure power fantasy as written.

    And Bloodlines has many-many moral choices.
    How many of them actually matter besides keeping your Humanity meter up without spending XP on it?

    You don't need to act consistently moral, just moral enough times to make sure your Humanity doesn't drop for the times your moral choices actually drop it (which is probably less than it should if we were actually trying to make a point about morality in the game).

    I've never dropped below Humanity 5 in Bloodlines... despite all the murder, theft, and just generally not being a good person my characters have done. You actively have to try to put your Humanity at risk in the game.

    And beyond that there's so few choices with consequences. One of the first things you can do in the game is shake down a rich guy for cash and vendor trash... and nothing happens to you if you do it. And that's how most of the game works. It's pretty simple why that's the case: avoiding dead ends in the game.

                                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                There's limitations of the medium.
                                There are limits. Bloodlines was a few miles away on the 'safe' side of things. It in no way pushed the limits of the medium at the time.

                                First off, Tung makes it clear that Santa Monica being a turd is an in-character impression at least he has. So this isn't just an impression created by any technological issues, but something made a case for in the script itself.

                                Second, nothing with the technology of the time stopped the game from having some non-interactive named vampire NPCs milling around. If they wanted Santa Monica to look more populated they could have. You don't have to flood the game or anything, but there's lots of little touches you can do. A vampire at the club besides Therese/Jeanette. A Nos in the sewers later in the plot once the Nose don't feel as threatened by the Sabbat. A vampire stalking the pier that mutters something about being bored watching the Thin-Bloods for a reason to do something to them once the pier area is open. Have Jack, Therese/Jeanette, and Tung mention vampires (you can hack Therese's and Jeanette's computers which would have been a perfect time to add some random vampires to the setting with minimal work) that you never actually see because they don't matter to the story.

                                Those are the exact tricks TES series uses to make their cities seem more populated than they really are. Seyda Neen, the starting town in Morrowind (which came out before Bloodlines) has over 25 named characters around. Most of them are set dressing, but it makes the tiny little place look populated.

                                Bloodlines didn't need the breadth of Morrowind, but Morrowind more than adequately shows that any appeal to limitations is nonsense.
