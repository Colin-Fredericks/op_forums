        Originally posted by Resplendent Fire View Post

        The reason can be "We decided we want more clans." It's fiction, it's made up. It's not a real thing, nor does it describe a real thing. Of course any such decision would be contextualized, but it looks like they're going to call the Salubri a clan anyway, so we're already past the triskadeka event horizon.

        The succession of the Tremere over the Salubri isn't because there were 13 clans, it's because the Tremere wanted the power of having a third generation founder.

        It doesn't erode the lore. There are a lot of things in V5 that I personally feel erodes the game but I do not believe any of it particularly erodes the lore. One of the things about a fictional setting is that we're always going be learning new things about the setting, we're going to learn things that recontextualize what we already know, and we're going to learn things that may contradict what we knew before. This has been the case for sure since first edition, and since 13 is just a number, it's fine if there are 14 or 15 or 16 or 12. The Book of Nod itself, like all accounts of Cainite history, isn't bound to be historically factual within the game itself.
        Like any work of fiction, symbolism in text makes a difference, and that is what you get with the number 13. Fact is, it is the reason why the creators deliberately went up to 13 Clans in the first place. It’s not like *I* chose the number of Clans being 13 and made it canon - it was the creators. Whatever they saw as important as a symbol in the game hasn’t really changed with V5 - which also explicitly refers to 13 Clans. Personally, I just think it loses symbolism and a particular atmosphere - that the writers themselves established - to hand wave the symbolism away in a cavalier fashion.

        The difference between a bloodline and a clan is like the difference between a planet and a dwarf planet. It may not be arbitrary, but it's a semantic distinction based on features deemed to make something a planet or a dwarf planet, and such definitions could change in the future.

        First edition Vampire said there might only be nine antediluvians or as many as 27, so the idea that "there were always 13 antediluvians" isn't even accurate.
        Well, it's a change of subject, but the precise reason why Pluto was finally declassified from a ‘Planet' to a 'Dwarf Planet' was that it couldn’t guarantee a clear orbit around the Sun. It wasn’t just a semantic distinction, but in any case, it’s not really a precise analogy to compare with 13 Clans. Fact is, ’13’ will remain a mystical number for all sorts of cultural reasons whether or not Vampire chooses to have 13 Clans. If Vampire ceases to have 13 Clans it will just mean the game is no longer able to tap into whatever symbolism is associated with the number 13 - which I think is a shame for aesthetic reasons.

        What first edition did, regarding Antediluvians, was allude to writings in the ‘biblical’ text of the Book of Nod that mentioned '13 Tribes', and then created a mystery that implied there could be 13 Antediluvians or possibly more, as children of Caine. That mystery fueled much of the backstory and meta plot of the game over the years. By just making 14 Clans, and essentially making it clear that 13 is no longer a significant number (and by extension, making the writings in the Book of Nod redundant) it reduces the mystery in the game. Thereby affecting the atmosphere.
