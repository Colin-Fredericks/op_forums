            Knives.

            That faction, which venerates a vision of
            Haqim as a blood god who desires the extermination
            of all other Kindred, has ruled the Clan of Silence
            for centuries, presenting an image of Clan Assamite
            as a family of assassins obsessed with diablerie.

            spent under a blood-curse inflicted on them by

            when the Tremere Clan placed all Assamites under
            a newer and more potent curse with the Treaty of
            Tyre. Denied the chance for regular diablerie, the
            Web of Knives turned to contract assassination in
            exchange for vitae, leaving the Viziers and the Sorcerers
            to their own devices.

            This state of affairs continued until a seer predicted
            the rise of Ur-Shulgi, a Methuselah of unimaginable
            power who sought to lead a culling of
            the Assamites. Galvanized into action, Tegyrius, the
            leader of the Viziers, and the Amr fashioned a conspiracy
            of Viziers, Sorcerers, and moderate Warriors
            wishing to defect from the main Clan in the event
            of Ur-Shulgi’s rise.
            This makes Al-Ashrad to be the mind behind it while Tegyrius is more of the spokesmen. It's notable both of these guys are 2000+ year old vampires who don't appear to suffer the Beckoning. The Vermillion Wedding is kind of fascinating because it depends on a massively powerful Methuselah being married to a reasonably powerful Elder. You have to wonder if Victoria Ash thought this through or not.

            Magic by Other Means: The Tremere Inner
            Circle is livid at the prospect of a bloodline as magically
            puissant as the Assamite Sorcerers gaining
            admittance to the Camarilla and threatening their
            occult monopoly. Local chantries do whatever they
            can to make Assamite refugees feel unwelcome while
            Princes who disdain the Tremere welcome the newcomers
            as a magical counterweight. Kindred of any
            Clans not gifted at sorcery get caught in the middle.
            So we really have the canonical answer here that the Pyramid doesn't want the Assamites joining but they can't stop it and plenty of anti-Tremere Princes are willing to open their doors.

                                                    Originally posted by Father Enoch View Post
                                                    Wouldn't that make Al-Ashrad a Baali, then?
                                                    It's weird. The cut text from Cb:Assamite Revised that said ur-Shulgi was Baali is here: https://whitewolf.fandom.com/wiki/Un...samite_Revised

                                                    It suggests ur-Shulgi was Baali, but Embraced by Haqim, and was later either re-Embraced as Assamite or had the Baali essence torn from him to make him a proper Assamite again.

                                                    So he both is and isn't Baali, and his childe might be either Baali or Assamite (al-Ashrad doesn't have the trademark Assamite dark skin which is suspicious...).

                                                    (This has turned into a tangent on one of my favourite parts of canon, so I apologise. Stop reading if you're not interested in Baali lore and lots of conjecture...)

                                                    Essentially, the full story is this: Haqim took a shepherd boy with a soul destined to become corrupted (i.e., he likely had an inverted avatar, making him a Widderslainte), threw him into the Well of Sacrifice at the site of the First Tribe's city (Chorazin, probably), making him into a Baali in the same way Saulot created the bloodline in the first place.

                                                    The boy arose as a leader around which all the Baali rallied. This made them a unified force, so they were easier to take down. Haqim arrives at the end of the war and basically turns the tide.

                                                    After the Baali were defeated, Haqim tore the 'demon' from him (you could interpret this in multiple ways; since Embraced mages lose their Avatars, it seems unlikely it was that, though).

                                                    Then Haqim told everyone he was forced to Embrace the 'innocent' boy to save him on the battlefield, hiding his true origin and his own role in the history of the Baali. Though this could be literal -- meaning perhaps he re-Embraced him into clan Assamite, and therefore used some form of the Baali rite of apostasy.

                                                    But Sarah Schneier, the IC author of the email outlining this info, emphatically says, "It is not Baali, and it is not possessed, and we do not think it is not working with or for anything infernal, but it is a case where the whole is greater than the sum of its parts, and it is not one of Haqim's Children any more, not really. It is something more, and it is something less." Which muddies things a bit (I assume the double negative is an error, meaning he isn't working with the infernal), but still seems to back up the theory that he isn't Baali anymore (or at least, isn't totally Baali).

                                                    Notably, the story of the shepherd boy parallels that of the slave boy who is the first Methuselah to claim the title of Shaitan, as described in several older sourcebooks detailing the origins of the Baali (and later repeated in the Revised Storyteller's Handbook).

                                                    Both ur-Shulgi and the slave boy are linked to a time in which 'the skies rained fire and wept blood' (slave boy) or 'the stones spoke and the skies cried a blood rain' (ur-Shulgi). Shaitan/the slave boy is said to have rallied the Baali together, using the title given to their mythical Antediluvian founder, and this title was later used by Nergal and Azaneal to similar ends. This matches ur-Shulgi's origin story closely.

                                                    This may reconcile two of the contradictory origin stories of the Baali, where Shaitan was said to have founded the bloodline in their first appearance (the DA Companion) but later on, the bloodline was founded by three separate vampires (in Cb:Baali), all sired by Saulot (or Cappadocian, or Tzimisce, or all three of them at separate times using the same well...).

                                                    It's a clever way to make all the stories mesh, while adding some suitable apocalyptic menace. And it makes the Baali a creeping threat to all the clans -- they've wormed their way into the history of every clan in one way or another, which also ties into the original history of them, where they recruited apostates from all the 13 clans to found the bloodline.
