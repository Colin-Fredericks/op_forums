    Sabbat as nuanced player characters vs. murder hobo NPCs

    06-08-2024, 01:15 PM
    There's a problem with the Sabbat that is basically summarized by me as "The Doctor Doom Paradox" which is like the Magneto Paradox but they've more or less settled on him. The Doctor Doom Paradox is that every ten years or so, someone decides to either redeem Doctor Doom. He starts acting like a hero or doing heroic things that highlights that maybe he's not as bad as all that. The problem being that Doctor Doom is one of the most popular and well-liked villains of all of the setting so then someone else comes out with something that has Doctor Doom do something absolutely horrible that resets it to the status quo.

    Fans like the Sabbat being expanded on as being a much more nuanced 3-dimensional antagonist by 2nd Edition until many people were playing them more than the Camarilla.

    But the Sabbat exist to be the murder hobos who get shot in the face as their role in the game. They exist because there's a long history of vampires who are just murderous thugs and idiot hell raisers like NEAR DARK and plenty of other stories where vampires are just complete monsters. Part of the conception of the Sabbat was the idea that they're dominated by the Beast and this is a Bad ThingTM. They never "stopped" being guys who played football with human heads (and that's soccer not American football, you damn Yanks) but they got the nicest shade put on them by the Paths of Enlightenment--which were themselves just meant to be in-universe the "Path of whatever the Regent wants you to do." You need the contrast to the Camarilla and Anarchs of vampires who have just lost everything and become utter monsters.

    However, a major allure of V:TM in the first place was playing "the Bad GuyTM." It was an appeal in the 90s when it first started that you wouldn't be a Lawful Good wandering murderer ala D&D or Call of Cthulhu investigator bound to get eaten. No, you would be a monster struggling wiht their humanity and while some of you would be Angel or Hannibal King, a lot of you would still fall prey to the Beast. So the whole argument the Sabbat should be off-limits doesn't really hold water. It's the same reason playing a Giovanni or Setite is viable even though both of them are every bit as vile as the Sword of Caine.

    I think it really boils down to whether you think the Paths/Roads are viable or not. Justin Achilli made them utter nonsense in Revised and basically, most Paths are just vamprires brainwashing themselves into thinking they're being enlightened when it's just a temporary bandaid against the Beast. That most Sabbat are just Low Humanity. This is a direct contrast against the Dark Ages, though, where Roads are as common as Humanity (and Humanity is just another Road) and a perfectly valid means of Kindred expression. They also had redeeming qualities so no Road was just, "I'm an evil bastard. Get with it." Even the Road of the Devil was like, "God sucks, let's overthrow him for the good of all."

    So what do you think? The Sabbat could use some nuance but I also feel like they really should be a failed alternative to the Anarchs. Once you succumb to the Beast, you really can't come back from it.

          It feels really quite strange to me that the designated antagonist Sect would include more playable factions and templates than the Sect that's supposed to be the focus of the line. I can't exactly fault any player for preferring the schizophrenic smorgasbord that is the Sabbat when the game itself appears to say you must align yourself with the Sect if you want to pursue any plotline or character archetype more exotic than doing Dracula re-enactments of The Godfather. I'm not saying this is a good thing—some of the Sabbat's internal diversity seems frankly incomprehensible to me. Why would the Lilith-worshipping Bahari align themselves with the Sword of Caine? What is stopping a Kiasyd from joining the Camarilla? Why on Earth is the Camarilla suddenly intent on persecuting Noddists?

          There's not much I like about V5, but I think they were right to distance themselves from the very concept of "Sect politics" and shatter the Sabbat into more than one Cult (cf. the Church of Caine). I truly believe there's no need for a designated antagonist faction in V:tM—playing the antagonist is the central appeal of the game, as you say—but I do think it's always good to have a rich selection of conspiracies-within-conspiracies to sow paranoia among the players. I still wish V5 committed to the version being a reboot from the very beginning, though. I don't think there was any way to return spice to the two primary factions while maintaining continuity with previous versions of the game; too much had already been drained out of the Camarilla and the Anarchs for any deconstruction of the Sabbat to not come across as a personal attack on those players who prefer the Sword of Caine.

              My main problem with a murder hobo Sabbat is that I can't suspend my disbelief enough to see it working as a social construct like a sect. A bunch of almost-Wights can not function as an efficient group, and frankly even the more nuanced and revised version of it stretches the band-aids of Vinculum and Paths to their limits.
              This has led to the Sabbat's eeeevilness becoming much toned down in my games, because a) some of it is so stupid it is self-destructive and b) it doesn't need it to be a counterpart to the Camarilla.

              I honestly prefer to have them as a dogmatic but different group vis-a-vis the Anarchs and Camarilla, together with the independents they form something of a Mario, Luigi, Wario, and Waluigi constellation that works well as a closed system. So in summary I like the Sabbat as 'the other bad guys' rather than 'the bad bad guys'.

                  The original Sabbat had a lot more variety to them. Revised did its best to nuke any kind of diversity within the Sabbat so that everyone was now essentially a member of the Black Hand.

                  I believe the original purpose of the Sabbat in the setting was to provide a way to portray pre-Anne Rice vampires: the kind of vampires that show up, turn whomever they bite into more vampires, worship Satan (at least in the Hammer movies), and be villainous as opposed to the more nuanced portrayal of vampires in the Camarilla and Anarchs. However, the initial Sabbat sourcebooks provided a lot of diversity within that framework. Here are some examples

                  First, there were large numbers of Country Gangrel who were on the Path of Harmony, and they seemed to be one of those aspects of the Sabbat that existed pre-revised Code of Milan before the Sabbat became more centralized.

                  Second, the Loyalist movement seemed to have a lot of Cainites who objected to the more monstrous Sabbat. I think that is why the character of Dominique was included in Children of the Inquisition. She was there to show there was a powerful movement within the Sabbat that objected. Yet now she's the forgotten character of CotI even though everyone else became a well known NPC.

                  Third, many nomad packs - heavily influenced by the Country Gangrel and Loyalists - had a wide variety of vampire types. They were often on their own, and therefore didn't participate that often in the more gruesome ritae like founded covens often did.

                  Fourth, there were groups who were not portrayed as that much different from their Camarilla counterparts. Yeah, the Malkavian and Toreador antitribu were darker, but the Brujah antitribu did not seem all that different. The Ventrue antitribu were mostly hard core loyalists and seemed to be the "conscience" of the sect. and the Nosferatu antitribu were basically the same as Nosferatu except they were hedging their bets. They would even met the other Nosferatu under truce and exchange information!

                  Fifth, we would later learn that much of the Black Hand's leadership, the supposed sword of caine, was in fact part of an enemy sect who were appalled at how the Sabbat treated humanity and working to destroy the sect from within.

                  Sixth, all of this was possible because of the central tension of a sect based on absolute freedom versus the need for the sect to survive against its enemies. The murder hobo aspect was certainly a big part of the sect, but it was not intended to be the only part of the sect. If not for the metaplot of Revised, this would have been kept in future sourcebooks. But since it made impossible how Justin Achilli wanted the metaplot to be, he just ignored everything that got in his way and included a lot of "yeah, those guys are all gone" comments in Revised's Guide to the Sabbat.

                  = = =
                  The major flaw of many Paths of Enlightenment is that the writers forgot the purpose of any Path (including the Path of Humanity) is to BIND the Beast so that it does not overwhelm the vampire and hijack control. Which means that any successful, well developed Path must somehow control the Beast and therefore deny its ability to take over. That meant telling the Beast "no" whenever it was hungry, afraid, or indulged too much. That could be based on simply coming up with reason to say "no" (based on Conviction or Self-Control), or allowing it some measure of satiety (Instinct) so that it was appeased and would not try to take over. Too many Paths of Enlightenment, while providing some measure of internal consistency, did not seem based around denying the Beast which is why they were derided as "Paths of Doing Anything I Want". Fundamentally, if the Path was not somehow constraining/denying the Beast, it could not be a successful Path as eventually the vampire could fall to the Beast and become a wight.

                  While some Paths were developed well enough in that aspect, others were not. So it's not surprising future writers and many players forgot what was supposed to be the purpose of any Path.

                  I think one thing that might have been interesting in the setting is to have Sabbat remember various "failed Paths" that didn't work as intended, and therefore abandoned as mistakes. And perhaps certain NPCs working to refine some of these failed Paths into a coherent, systemic philosophy that could do what the Sabbat needed them to do which is prevent vampires from becoming wights. You could even have in Sabbat chronicles the idea that sometimes entire packs become wights because they didn't take their Path seriously, and rest of the local sect needs to wipe them out. It should be an ongoing problem in the sect.

                  If you have a Sabbat chronicle where the failure of members to adequately take their Paths seriously lead to problems with wights, then there definitely is a role for more "humane", nuanced PCs and NPCs instead of them seeming like anomalies. "Say what you will about the Path of Harmony, the Country Gangrel don't have a lot of wights amongst them."

                                                          Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                                                          ^This. The Anarchs definitely suffered from being the awkward middle child of the main sects. The Camarilla had all the power and structure, and the Sabbat were given all the freedom. The Anarchs existed somewhere in the middle, which made them stand out less. If players wanted to operate in Kindred politics, they stuck with the Camarilla. Whereas if they wanted to do whatever they wanted, they went for the Sabbat (which did everything the Anarchs claimed to do, but better).
                                                          I would argue something different. I've known lots of players who loved playing Sabbat. The thing that attracted them was not "freedom" or "to do whatever they wanted". It was the distinctive element of Sabbat culture and organization. They loved the idea of Paths of Enlightenment. They liked the various ritae. They liked the distinction between nomads packs and founded coven. They liked the intrigues among the Bishops and Archbishops. They liked the tension between the ducti and priests. They liked that monomacy and diablerie were acceptable. They liked the idea of the Caitiff joining together as the Panders and forcing the clans to take them seriously. They liked the mythology of defeating the antedeluvians and Noddist lore. They liked the internal tension created by the Sabbat Inquisition and the Black Hand within the sect. Certainly some preferred playing Loyalists and leaned into the Sabbat's freedom line, but even those players knew the sect overall was clamping down on that. What they liked was reacting against that structure while still participating in it.

                                                          In other words, the Sabbat provided a strong framework for PCs to roleplay in. The unique institutions and culture of the Sabbat inspired their creativity despite being a constraint on what they could ultimately do because it provided lots of tools they could use to customize their characters and chronicles. People who liked the Camarilla likewise liked the Camarilla because of its unique institutions and culture. Both sects provided lots of tools to players to craft interesting characters and insert them into fun situations.

                                                          What made the Anarchs less appealing was not that they existed "somewhere in the middle". It was they lacked any kind of interesting framework or organization that made them stand out. The Anarchs, in contrast to the Camarilla and Sabbat, seemed bland. There wasn't enough hooks and distinctive elements that could cause players to easily create distinctive characters. Everyone just seemed like a generic thug. The Anarchs, as a group, lacked the kind of tools that both the Camarilla and Sabbat provided. All the Anarchs were, were vampires who opposed the Prince the most, and that was about it. Other than that, there wasn't much that was distinctive about them.

                                                          Now very early in the game, it didn't seem like that. Chicago By Night provided us several different groups of Anarchs that seemed to indicate there were ideological, political, or personality divides among the Anarchs. The Brujah clanbook and other sourcebooks mentioned an Anarch Council that seemed to be running the revolution. The Anarch Cookbook provided some key personalities and political testaments. This built up a lot of enthusiasm and expectation that the upcoming sourcebook Los Angeles By Night was going to be a big sourcebook with lots of interesting lore. Instead, what people actually got was disappointing to a lot of players. It sucked a lot of energy out of players for Anarchs, and I remember at the time seeing players who had played mostly Anarchs previously just no longer be interested in the sect and changing their play styles. Because as the Player Guides and clanbooks came out, the culture and offices of the Camarilla made it seem more fun to play in.

                                                          I think if Los Angeles By Night or some other sourcebook had provided more structure, factions, and ideas about the Anarchs, then they would have kept a lot of their initial popularity. The Anarchs simply didn't have the cool toys or tools that the other groups did.

                                                          I do think it was possible to have provided the Anarchs with those kind of tools. I brainstormed some of them back in this thread, starting around tab 5. Create some large scale political factions among the Anarchs (and their internal rivalries and feuds). Make it clear the Anarchs are not limited to those, and many of those factions are internally split, but having them would help players create new characters. Provide some real detail on the Anarch Council and how it has actual loyal operatives in other domains. Many Anarchs may not be affiliated with it, but making it a Comintern like structure that does provide some help and guidance, a network of support, and various assets is another good tool for players and STs. Also provide the obvious counterpart to that - describe how the Anarchs are riddled with informers, double agents, cowards, and how the Camarilla in general and Justicar Petrodon in particular undermine and combat them. Describe their relations with the Sabbat, particularly between them and Sabbat Loyalists and what they have in common and what divides them. Are they just enemies, do some Sabbat Loyalists actually prefer the Anarchs to their recently centralizing sect, is their Sabbat brainwashing too much for them to do so? All of these would provide lots of tools that could cause players to latch onto the Anarchs.

                                                          But the Anarchs never got any cool toys that only they got to play with.
