        Originally posted by Sergeant Brother View Post
        So, I love Camarilla Lasombra, but I do agree that defection plot had some issues.

        In large part, because it isn’t cool. The Kasombra came to the Camarilla on their knees, humbling themselves. That isn’t their style. That isn’t cool.

        That is why I would rather the Lasombra gain enough power to demand full status within the Camarilla.
        In "The Sacrifice", I gave the Lasombra an angle to pursue on the subject by saying that the deal they made with Kevin Jackson was very much a "Please don't throw me in the briar patch" sort of thing. They targeted Kevin Jackson because he's anxious to make history and an ancilla so he didn't have the age to spot the trap they were laying but he also was powerful enough in Chicago that he would be able to set the stage for the defections across the rest of America (where the Camarilla, once in the Camarilla, would spread).

        What was the trap? One that Critias spotted but decided to let past?

        Well, Sierra Van Buress dropped the hints and guided Kevin Jackson to demand that the Lasombra present a Lasombra Elder in exchange for every defector. Something so outrageous that it would be assumed the Lasombra would reject out of hand. Agreeing to it would make it seem like the Lasombra was insane and crippling their own clan.

        In truth, it was a calculated bit of theater that came in five parts.

        1:] The Lasombra made this deal with the assumption they would need to purge the Sabbat traditionalists and loyalists that would not be willing to defect to the Camarilla. The Lasombra were not going to leave a bunch of enemies behind them. Hence they were going to be doing a purge anyway and the ashes could be used to buy their members passage.

        2:] The Lasombra are quite happy to cheat the Camarilla while the Tremere are at their lowest and the Banu Haqim lack the inherent hatred the Sabbat possess. The Lasombra present a pouch of ashes from an 'elder' that they kept forty bags from, staked members of other clans, false identities for the dead, and lies.

        3:] Plenty of Princes didn't care about the deal Kevin Jackson made if they received private grauitities. Not every Prince cares about the Sabbat being weakened if they can make their own arrangements. The Amis Noctis cleaned out the Sabbat's coffers (barring the Grimaldi) and can give great tribute.

        4:] The Lasombra are spreading out or plan to. One or two Lasombra in most major cities is a lot easier to work with than Princes who are worried about an invasion. The Lasombra may not have access to cellphones or the internet...but their ghouls do as do couriers. Their efforts are coordinated with the agents having the goals of making friends and building power bases each city.

        5:] The Lasombra's final issue is that plenty of the Lasombra just stayed behind when the Sabbat left their cities. Sometimes with amiable ex-Sabbat willing to pass themselves off as Anarchs or Caitiff. Mexico City, Montreal, and other cities have Lasombra welcoming the Camarilla in--its hard to argue with their presence then.

        It was an extremely well-done plan and while it had quite a few hiccups because it turned out the Amis Noctis underestimated how many Lasombra marked for destruction would have their OWN plans to betray their fellows and jump ship (plus take advantage of the opportunity for settling old scores--treachery is the way of the Sith), it overall worked extremely well in moving a few hundred Lasombra and crippling a lot of the Sabbat counteroffensive against the "traitors."

        Obviously, this isn't canon.

                    Originally posted by Lysander View Post
                    The Gangrel leaving the Camarilla was a good idea in my book and I'm surprised that they stayed as long they did.
                    The main reason they stayed is that they don't have clan-wide organization of any kind, they're fiercely independent individuals as far as clan policy goes.

                    And the second side of this is that the Camarilla wasn't so much a group as it was an idea, a way to organize society, and a set of laws meant for every Kindred.

                    So for most of the setting's history being Camarilla isn't just default in the sense that it is expected for most groups (actually the default has always been the Anarchs sooner or later in the chronicle), but in the very real sense that being Camarilla isn't an actual effort, leaving it is. Because leaving it means deliberately choosing to ignore it's rule and laws.

                    Even most Anarchs weren't necessarily non-Camarilla if they were just against the current Prince or something like that. If you so much as accept that the city has a Prince or Primogen and the Six Traditions, you're Camarilla, that's it. And from their part, the Camarilla doesn't accept members, it assumes it's universal among vampires. While it does have a few global or regional institutions, it was more an idea than an organization.

                    Justicars are one of those few institutions, and almost the only one that affects nightly life, and they're a political appointment. So if one Justicar leaves the position... you just appoint a new one, as long as there's a single vampire in the clan who accepts the role, it can be done.

                    For the Gangrel being Camarilla was just a matter of other people saying they're in and they having no reason to argue the point, plus there being enough clan members who actually gave a fuck to become Justicars, Primogen and stuff. Leaving was actually a very involved effort.

                                That has a lot to do with my personal take that the myths are far more important by themselves than the reality behind. Myths matter because people act on them, they learns from them, they take them as literal gospel. They also change them for various reasons, and those changes carry on and produce their own consequences. In this sense, the notion of the myths being real actually gets in my way in my chronicles than really help in any way, as it creates a kind of pressure to define "The Truth™" that just messes with my game.

                                So I prefer the Kindred myths to be seriously misguided. They have a kernel of truth, but as a direct telling of what happened they missed the point completely. When this truth, or part of it, is revealed for any reason, it looks nothing like what the myths say. Also because myths are generally the result of millennia of retellings and conflations, so even the actual people told about in them can't easily recognize the story or how it came to be.

                                Just imagine a chronicle where the players are vampire archaeologists hunting down artifacts and texts and whatnot about an Ante, learning a complex tangle of contradicting stories and secrets, only to find out in the end their true patron was the Ante itself all along trying to piece together how the fuck their name came to be associated to a tale with so little resemblance to anything that ever happened.

                                Also I tend not to have the Antes in torpor if they're at all relevant to a game. They're mostly awaken and aware and doing whatever they want. Which once you have such incredible powers... isn't really much, because why bother?
