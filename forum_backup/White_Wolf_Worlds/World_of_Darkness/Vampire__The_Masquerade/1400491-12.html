                                        I think you could view the Sabbat as being vampires with more of a 'physical' emphasis - more warrior caste the way the Camarilla are the filthy politicians (more 'mental?') and the Anarchs are the workers (more 'social'- I dont know if I'm making communist puns here) In that context you've had the Sabbat 'going to war' and that's going to have consequences. CtPhipp's Crusades analogy has them bringing back (plundered) knowledge, but there can be other dimensions to it. Think of something akin to Vietnam - returning from wars, having to struggle with 're-integrating', suffering from the vampiric equivalent of PTSD, etc. Some may still be determined to wage that war, or may see new threats (ie the Second Inquisition) cropping up and then go to war
                                        against THAT new threat. You could also have a faction of the Sabbat interested in maintaining elements of the Black Hand ideology ('guarding the herd's of humanity from predation. because they see those herds as their property, for example.)

                                        An 'army' is going to need more than just the tip of the spear though. You have the officers and leadership. You have your provosts/Military police (a reimagining of the Inquisition?) you could even have your own diplomats and workers, and suchlike. The Antes may or may not still be a threat but Sabbat who 'return' from the wars can find plenty of things to face or do. They're likely to find the Camarilla and Anarchs muscling in on territory they abandoned when they went to war, so they'll have to start carving out their own niches again to reclaim territory and resources. Or perhaps some of the Sabbat are sent 'back' from the War to start establishing new bases from which supplies (and manpower) can be routed into the wars (recruiting grounds, logistics, etc.)

                                        Diablerie for this imagining of the Sabbat is likely to be a tool of intelligence and self-improvement. Learning to use the enemy's own weapons against them basically (with all the benefits and dangers that implies.)

                                        I wouldn't call it strictly 'good' or 'bad' but it depends on how its used and justified. Heck, maybe you have the concept of diablerie starting to break previous definitions (thee original material has always been kind of murky I felt... 'True' Diablerie always required consuming not just the vitae but soul of the victim. And yet there are plenty of cases where sucking the blood without consuming the soul still confers some measure of power including lowered generation. It's just a more imperfect process.) So 'Blood' Diablerie might be treated differently from 'Soul' Diablerie.
                                        There's also variations found like in thautmaturgy (the rite of the bitter rose) which opens the possibility of new ways to utilize Diablerie as part of a larger war (maybe the Sabbat develop their own synthesis of traditional thaumaturgy and thin blooded alchemy? I dunno.) Diablerie of the Soul may still be considered something to be restricted (there ARE downsides and dangers, like the soul of the victim taking over his attacker) but Blood diablerie might be more of a gray area where rules can be more safely bent and justified.
