            AA and AAA are also meaningless industry jargon at this point. Remember when they started to try to hype up AAAA games as a thing?

Games that embrace what their core experience really is are going to do better than games that try to make a $100 million game for $50 million, instead of making a great $50 million project.

                            We also see employees in the games industry laid off even if the game they worked on did really well. Even if the publisher is making record profits.

                            Because to these businesses, making more money than ever is the right time to fire all their staff and hire new people at a fraction of the pay, to consolidate those gains.

                            Doesn't matter if this gets rid of the very people who made that profit possible. It doesn't matter if it means losing hard-won institutional knowledge, and forces developers to train new employees from scratch. It doesn't matter that the games they just made are now harder to maintain in the future, because the people who knew how it worked were just fired.

                            If they aren't making the most amount of money in that moment, the corporations could not care less.
