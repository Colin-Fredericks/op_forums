    Your noddist fan theories

    04-24-2014, 07:56 PM
    I thought we could share some of our own noddist speculations, and ideas that are not explicitly supported by the books. Here are some of mine. May contradict each other:

    The third Baali was Rayzeel. And that was why Saulot commanded his childer "Do not embrace love"

    Ahab going over to the Baali was what he was embraced for by Saulot, to cover for him and Rayzeel in the creation of the Baali. Both those Baali creation stories are true.

    Shaitan from Chaos Factor was Rayzeel. They were both singers at the start. Rayzeel stole Saulots heart singing in the dark before he knew what the pits she was singing to was for.

    Brujah was the first Antediluvian to fake his death. He and Troile together ambushed Ventrue, and Troile diablerized Ventrue. This happened sometime between 1256 and 1250 BC. (Youngest known 4th gen Ventrue/Oldest known 4th gen brujah). Not originally my idea.

    Troile was originally a Torador, and have in fact been confused with Arikel in several genalogies.

    The original Ashur was Saulot. Cappadocious was his first childe, and the bloodline he spawned before he went east. Cappadocious studies lead him to the first diablerie, and the decay his line suffers is the punishment.

    Set was sired a Gangrel of the 4th generation. He diablerized his way to third. The Tlactique are descended from his oldest get, before his diablerie.

    Typhon was Brujah. Osiris description of his emotional deadening matches the Trujah clan flaws and the powers match Potence, Presence and Temporis.

    Set was infuencing a family of Italian necromancers for centuries in the guise of Dis Pater. When he was corporally disadvantaged around year 1, he took up the guise permanently. The Giovannia made strides in Necromancy that would so impress the Cappadocians because they had heavy help from the lands of the dead.

    Osiris physically destroyed Set around the brith of Christ. He had motive, means, opportunity and was speciffically without alibi.

    While Augustus Giovanni believes the True Vessel contains the remaining blood of Cappadocious and the people who participared in the ritual generally believe it to contain the mortal blood of Augustus, only the traitor knew that it contains the remnant of the blood used to embrace Augustus -that of the Lasombra Antediluvian.



    As theories, I believe there are few things contradicting them, and lets us face it, established facts in Kindred history have plenty of stuff contradicting them. There are also litte supporting them, but that is what specualtion is for

    What are some of yours?

                  I usually count all named Antediluvians from official sources as "real" in the sense that they exist or have existed, because I feel that the world is more interesting with a higher number of strange creatures. I have created a fair bit of history for some of the Antediluvians without Clans of their own, and have even let my players meet some of these obscure ancients at times. In my version of the World of Darkness, Ashur and Saulot are definitely distinct characters, and both Arikel and Ishtar exist as separate Antediluvians. Some of the weird Disciplines "invented" by strange Bloodlines are actually far older than the Bloodlines themselves, having been the signature Disciplines of Antediluvians who did not survive long enough to form Clans. The Disciplines were then either rediscovered by Bloodline founders or kept in play by other ancient Cainites who then created these new Bloodlines for different reasons. Even Daimoinon is such a Discipline in my setting - the Baali were given the Discipline by Saulot when he created them, but it was originally the signature Discipline of one of Saulot's fellow Antediluvians. I have made up a lot of stuff about Malakai in particular.

I could see that the Tlacique are inheritors of Set's Clan before his change towards what we currently see as being Setites. For the record, I've wondered about just WHAT Set's progeny were like before... I'm a little annoyed at myself for not seeing that possibility before this.

                                                  Here are a couple of my own oddities:

                                                  Set's Wife: The tales of how Set warred with his brother, Osiris, also includes their sister Isis, but their sister Nephthys is completely missing. What if that's by design rather than accident? In the actual, Real Life™ mythology, Nephthys sometimes appears in a 'was also there' capacity, and that gave me ideas. I thought, what if perhaps she had been marginalised by Set, who then took over parts of her portfolio? Nephthys was the original goddess of the night, goddess of dark dreams and hopes, but Set stole those aspects and ditched his sister and wife. So I put her in the tomb of Al-Mahri under the ghost city of Enoch, had her also go by the name Erishkegal, and once she was released, she started going after Set's worst childer.

                                                  Caine's Sister:* Yeah, [i]Constantinople by Night[/b] has a group of vampires who venerate Calomena as Caine's sister, but they come across as daft neonates who don't really know much. However, there are apocryphal Bible books (notably the Book of Jubilees and the Books of Adam and Eve, again in Real Life™) that suggest the first mortals were born with twin sisters. Cain's sister is named Awan or Luluwa, and so that he wouldn't need to marry her, Adam and Eve chose a second set of twins. Abel was to marry Awan, but Cain didn't want to lose her; it's suggested THAT's why the first murder occurred. In my own apocryphal Book of Nod, when Caine flees into the wilderness, he goes with his twin sister, and out of desperation of hunger and thirst, they drink each other's blood. Luluwa became the first ghoul, but Caine was so disgusted with what he did that he sent her far from his sight. Being his sister, his lover, his thrall, she could not stay away, of course. She may still be out there, likely immortal (being Caine's ghoul and all), and her motives may not align with Caine's...

                                                  The Crone: She's pretty much as presented in the Book of Nod, but she survived Caine's stake and the sun, and is also Eickos from the 'Fair is Foul' scenario of Gehenna. However, her raison d'être is a desire to actually die. The thought that dark magics would make her happy out in the land of Nod, but that was not true. She thought that the blood of Caine would make her happy, but that also was not true. After the purifying light of the sun failed to destroy her, she sought other methods, but none worked. Taking the history of Eickos from Gehenna into account, the Crone's motive for joining with Lilith's Apostates stemmed from a hope that Lilith would bring an end to an impossible existence. Whether the Crone be Adam's second wife as mentioned in Revelations of the Dark Mother or as the Almighty's feminine counterpart, who can say? Her motivations hinge on a hope that Gehenna or whatever apocalypse comes will end her own life.
