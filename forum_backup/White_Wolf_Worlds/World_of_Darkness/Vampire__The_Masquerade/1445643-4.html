        Originally posted by Shakanaka View Post
        The obvious solution in this matter would be IMPROVING on the unwritten regions and think up something interesting. VTM isn't just about Sects alone, its about Vampires. Bring relevance instead of de-revalancing things or potential things.

        In the V5 length of things anyway, there is no real exact summation of where the metaplot is going anyway. The Camarilla now more than ever is very weak; Anarchs are ascendant because much of the Camarilla has been crippled with all the Elders of the latter leaving for the Gehenna War; and the Sabbat is just entirely absconded to the Middle-East (and the reasons for this unilateral breakage across the board is not something I agree with narratively). Nobody is having any global presence now more than ever. Instead of a Sect having a presence in another unwritten to give it "flavor", it's just best to actually fill unique stuff and lore about it on its own.
        And like I mentioned, Vampire the Masquerade is a game focused on the Camarilla (the organization that created the Masquerade) with some focus on the Sabbat and Anarchs in their role of foils and antagonists to the Camarilla. Vampire products unrelated to the Camarilla tended to be fairly niche. The majority of games are going to involve the Camarilla because that's the default way in which the game is set up. So people are going to be less interested in material that doesn't involve the Camarilla in some way because it's material that probably won't end up in their campaign. It's worth noting Kindred of the Ebony Kingdom was very much a unique, one-off product. We never saw anything like it before or after.

        I would like to see those other places finally get detailed, but it's mainly a feeling of "better late than never" (which, when the game line officially ended back in 2004, was where we were left - with many locations never getting detailed). I simply think it would have been better for the game to, early on, make the Camarilla a global organization and focus more on describing places outside of Europe and North America, rather than making those places mystery boxes and then waiting 30+ years to finally open them up. That just ended up rather pointlessly "othering" those regions and making them feel completely tertiary and unimportant to the gameline.

        As note with V5 I am a bit worried that the Middle East will end up being another mystery box, since it seems likely that the new WW will be hesitant to fully explore the Beckoning and the Gehenna War that occurred (and is still occurring) there since any exploration of that part of the world will necessarily need to also closely look at how those events played out as well as the aftermath. And I'm just not sure that they're all that eager to explore those things any time soon.

                The Anarchs and the Sabbat were always related to the Camarilla due to their roles as foils for the Camarilla. In fact with the Anarchs, to a large degree the entire point of that faction is a rejection of the Camarilla. You can't discuss the Anarchs without talking about the Camarilla, and vice versa. Likewise you can't talk about the Sabbat without talking about how and why they rejected the Camarilla. So any book dealing with the Sabbat or the Anarchs always ended up coming back to the Camarilla because of how central it was to the formation of both of those groups.

                But, even with the Sabbat as you yourself mentioned earlier, we got relatively little detail on Sabbat territories that were located far away from Camarilla territory like those further down into South America. That's because the default play assumption was that players would be playing Camarilla vampires in North America or maybe in Europe, and have little reason to go to such places, so far outside of their territory. The game has always heavily focused on the Camarilla at the expense of pretty much everything else.

                So we have places like India, and Africa, and Southeast Asia in which you have vampires, but since they don't really interact with the Camarilla those vampires and locations were never heavily detailed in the books because there was little expectation that players were going to be traveling there, or running games there. The only exception was Africa, which got 1 book, though in comparison to the 100 books written for VtM that was not a whole bunch.

And I would have much rather seen those places explored in the books than left as unopened mystery boxes.
