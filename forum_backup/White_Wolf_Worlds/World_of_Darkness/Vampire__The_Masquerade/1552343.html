    What attracted you to VTM?

    07-14-2025, 12:19 AM
    I got intrigued by a series of Mick Farren novels that focused on a coven of undead led by a thousand year old vampire by the Victor Renquist and how they reside along side humans and influence people or organizations when they need to. This series made me interested if this was ever done in a rpg game and thus I discovered VTM.

    So how did you discover the game?

                                  I remember when I saw Vampire: The Masquerade (first edition) in a local game shop back in '91. I flipped through it and oh my! Did I HATE it! It looked like they'd made un-dead monsters into D&D classes, the 'sooper powerz' (Disciplines and blood magic) looked simplistic, and the art did nothing for me... Fast forward a year-or-so, and a mate lent me his Second Edition book. I had terrible insomnia and decided that I'd read through it to find EXACTLY why it was bollocks.

                                  Um, needless to say that once I actually READ it, I fell in love. I spent all night poring over that book, and then the next day I did the same, and then I started writing notes, and well. All this time since I've been building my own world (of Darkness) for myself and — when they can attend — my friends, using that baseline that I loved.

                                  What I found most intriguing was the deep history element. I'm not a huge fan of the Book of Nod as THE HISTORY OF THE KINDRED. Rather, I'm fascintaed that there is scholarship into the mytho-historic origins of the Damned. The mechanic of Generation and the implication that it all started somewhere in the unknowable past — something that even 'immortal' monsters cannot know — lit a fire in me. The idea of the Book of Nod as a discrete artefact grew into it being more a collection of somewhat-related pieces, and some of the Damned see it as all nonsense whilst others believe it to be (no quotation marks) canon. That is, no two Kindred or Cainites or what-have-you can agree on their origins, and that fact makes their war of the ages so, so personal AND far away at the same time. The clans and bloodlines grow out of this set of disagreements, and those lines in turn force the drawing of other lines. For the trench coated neonate with her naff katana on the rain-soaked streets of wherever, the Jyhad is always part of the night she struggles through without any real understanding of why.

                                  A lot of the above has grown in my head over the past decades, mind you. I mean, it's all there in the very first book (which I do not own and adore), and it finds its way through all of the editions. It's filtered differently in Revised versus V5, but it still exists. The long arm of history-based-on-memory, it is a core precept that I found irresistible. It's a big part of why I have a Beckett action figure on my desk and own the Book of Nod Artefact Edition and my players know the name 'Bindusara' as blood-sucking scholar of the supernatural rather than an emperor in Indian history. There are MANY other points in the game that I find wonderful, but I believe this is what first made me excited.

                                  The TL;DR version: First attracted by spite. Eh... But it got better?
