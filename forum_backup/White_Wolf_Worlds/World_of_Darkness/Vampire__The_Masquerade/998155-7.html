                                            Originally posted by Vertical8 View Post
                                            Am I the only one who can't make heads or tails of the "By Oath of Fealty" section on page 78? I've read it a few times and can't wrap my head around how the mechanics are supposed to work.

                                            I didn't write that section (I did knights) and it is, honestly a bit mangled, but I can summarize it for you:

                                            1) The liege gives the vassal Background dots that can be combined with the vassal's for an Anchor background, in exchange for twice as many dots from the vassal for their own use.
                                            2) The liege's dots are worth half as much (rounded up) when pooled for Anchor Background purposes.
                                            3) The pooled vassal/liege Background cannot exceed the liege's rating.
                                            4) But the liege can provide the same Background dots multiple times -- as many as their rating -- without using up the Background.
                                            5) Terms may be set that replace a certain number of Background dots granted by vassals.

                                            The systems for knights are based on this last point. For instance, instead of providing 6 dots to a liege, a veteran knight (or someone who everyone thinks is one) only needs to provide 3 + the reputation/service's value of 3 dots. For Cainites, being a vampire is a benefit of this kind on its own. For example, a vampire willing to shed blood in service is considered to provide a value equivalent to 4 dots.

                                                    Originally posted by Aahz View Post
                                                    Can you make an example showing what everybody is providing an can use in the end?
                                                    Again, this is not my section, but:

                                                    Sir John of Dover, a veteran knight (2 dots of reputation for terms) offers Resources 2, Retainers 2 and Influence 2 to Arnott, Duke of Kent -- that's 6 dots of Backgrounds and 2 equivalent points in terms. The Duke offers Resources 4 in return, of which 2 dots is added to Sir John's total.

                                                    Sir John now accesses Resources 4.

                                                    The Duke accesses Resources 6, pools Sir John's Backgrounds with his own or can otherwise call on them, and Sir John himself must provide 40 days of military service a year for the Duke.

                                                    Now if they were treating each other as social equals they would pool their Backgrounds normally instead, but the advantage here is really that Arnott can build this relationship with multiple vassals without having his Resources Background used up by these commitments.

                                                    As I said, I didn't write this section, but one thing I'd note totally unofficially is that even though a liege can use the same Anchor to support multiple vassals, those vassals' contributions in return should not stack, or else the liege's Backgrounds will really get out of hand. I'd say the liege gets the best pool out of all contributing vassals instead. If you want to get really technical you might say that each additional vassal that would have increased the value instead provides a -1 difficulty break to rolls using that Background in conditions where it would make sense.

                                                    Another question that needs answering is pooling of the non-Anchor Backgrounds -- does it work both ways, so the vassal can gain from them when pooled with the liege's? I'd say the answer is "yes," and that this half-rating grant of the liege's rating is consistent throughout.

                                                    For example, Arnott may possess Retainers 2 and Influence 3, and pooled with Sir John's offerings, this increases to 4 and 5, respectively, but Sir John only accesses 3 (his + half the Duke's rating) and 4 (same, rounded up). This makes vassalage a bit of a better deal.
