    Ministry in the Camarilla, Banu Haqim in the Anarchs

    06-07-2020, 05:08 AM
    I’ve Long since thought that there should be more Clans in the Camarilla. My one exception was always the Assamites, or the Banu Haqim as they are now called. Through out the ages they dedicated themselves to killing and diablerizing other vampires, even having to be hunted nearly to extinction by the Camarilla and cursed by the Tremere. If it Clan should be forever barred entry from the Camarilla, it should be them. In the books it’s heavily suggested that they blew up the meeting with the Ministry and even aided in the attack on the Tremere Chantry, suggesting that the Camarilla leadership are complete imbeciles when it comes to their dealings with the Banu Haqim.

    The Camarilla and the Setites, now the Ministry, have never had any major struggles with each other, aside from the rivalries that all Clans have. They are sneaky and corrupt, which is what the Camarilla is really all about. The a ministry can use their position with the Camarilla to better control politics and corrupt the masses. Meanwhile, within the Anarchs the Banu Haqim can combat vampiric excesses as they often have and get a few more chances to kill at their discretion.

    Even from a Discipline perspective, things are more balanced with the Ministry in the Camarilla. Now the Anarchs have two Protean Clans and the Camarilla has two Blood Sorcery Clans.

    Changing how this turns out would take only a minor tweak to the canon events - the Camarilla discovers the Banu Haqim bombing plans (successful or not) and slap themselves in the face for ever considering associating with the Banu Haqim and welcome the Ministry in. I’m not sure how important this reversal would be to the setting, it’s only just happened, but I like it more than how the events played out.

      Since Revised at least WW made a strong effort to present the Assamites as something different from the stereotype of blood crazed diablerists. In V5 ONE THIRD of the clan has joined the Camarilla, those who wish to keep their attachment to humanity (and the religious faiths they followed in life). These are NOT the blood crazed assassins you talk about (and btw, the Assamites were never "hunted nearly to extinction" by the Camarilla...) and the matter of them joining the Camarilla is well detailed both in BJD and in the V5 products. Given the story as presented it makes sense these schismatics were accepted into the Camarilla (some ofthe Tremere tried to stop that from happening because they felt threatened by other blood mages, but some others, like Meerlinda pushed to have them join, while the other clans understood the advantage of having a second clan capable of Blood Sorcery, diminishing the Tremere power, and then the Tremere splintering happened and they suddenly lacked the political clout to stop the "Assamite merger"). More importantly from the political point of view having the "humanist" Assamites join allowed the Camarilla to further their alliance with the Ashirra. The Assamites had always been the dominant clan in the Ashirra (folowed by the Lasombra... note how the Lasombra joined next?) and them joining meant a much easier deal for concluding a functioning alliance with the oldest functioning vampire sect of the world.

      The Setites had actually petitioned the Camarilla for joining but were rejected. Considering how they are the ideological foil of the Ventrue it makes sense, it makes even more sense if you consider how the Camarilla only wanted a new clan between them and the Assamites and choose the latter because of all the political benefits they brought to the sect (or to the majority of the Camarilla clans at least).

                                                          I have a weird thought.

                                                          Hardestadt was a... arguably, the... primary leader of the Camarilla, and a noted conservative who felt things in the sect were fine as they were. The Ashirra are to be ignored. The Anarchs are vile, but ultimately unimportant. The Camarilla rules over every vampire in the world, whether they know it or not. Antedeluvians are down the memory hole.

                                                          There were other elders who disagreed with these positions. Most elders disagreed with Hardestadt on one or two points, a few with all of them.

                                                          "Hardestadt" was actually Hardestadt the Younger who was impersonating Hardestadt the Elder.

                                                          Sooner or later, someone somewhere was bound to figure out that Hardestadt the Younger seized control of the sect. Those in a position to discover this were also in a position to not want to embarrass the sect by revealing it. That said, something would have to be done. This could not stand.

                                                          Hardestadt was assassinated by Theo Bell.

                                                          The Brujah were promptly removed from the Camarilla, in a divorce of mutual loathing.

                                                          Theo Bell is not put on the Red List.

                                                          Theo Bell is said to be an emissary between the Camarilla and Anarchs, despite his manifest inappropriateness for the role.

                                                          The Banu Haqim (assassins whose discipline spread includes Obfuscate abilities which allow them to hide their identity among witnesses) are accepted as a full clan within the Camarilla.

                                                          If a group of elders wanted the False Hardestadt destroyed, what would they have had to pay the Banu Haqim to carry out the contract?
