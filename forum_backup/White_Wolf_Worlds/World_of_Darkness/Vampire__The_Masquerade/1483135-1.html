            Originally posted by Prometheas View Post

            Okay this has been bothering me for a while, but don't essence 3 exalted only have an average of 20-30 dice before artifacts?
            Yes but no, as "average Exalted" isn't really a thing. If dedicated to a given profession, activity or way of life, Exalted are typically specced to kill, with a reasonable expectation to have 10 or more dice before Charms (up to double or so after as an effective rating - Solars roll huge amounts of dice, others have different avenues like dice to success swaps or smaller number of auto-successes). If not, it might not even be a Trait or Traits that they have particularly high unless they're Attribute-based Exalted, in which case they will typically have a high minimum because of the demands of being what they are.

            But there are other difficulties that denizens of the WoD might have in fighting some Exalted. Fire and light-conjuring Solar Charms against vampires, for example. Blazing Solar Bolt would be the end of most Setites, and depending on the edition, a Solar might be able to make melee strikes over a much wider area than that with their "sword pressure". One introduced in the 2e supplement Glories of the Most High, World-Scarring Glory, could take out several city blocks. Ironically, though, Lunars have to wait a lot longer to turn into city-crushing kaijuu than Mokole, who can do that out of the gate in chargen.

                    Dice pools are almost completely independent of Essence until you get into Essence 6+, at least for first and second edition characters, as the game lazily decided "Essence 6+ means that you can get Traits at 6+ when they normally capped out at 5, not including Virtues". At this point, specialist dice pools are about Essence x 2, plus three additional dice worth of specialties for a narrower range of rolls - which, in a very cheesy manner, often ended up as "my favorite weapon type + 3". There were oblique hints of "cap-breaker" effects for Attributes and Abilities in 1e, and 2e kind of added some, but they mostly facilitate getting higher Ability and Essence Charms at a lower rating for said Traits, at least for Abyssals and Solars, so as to not break the math further.

                                Originally posted by Prometheas View Post

                                What about with charms like exigencies/masteries/etc? I'm not very learned about exalted, but I've heard that exalt have a number of ways to boost their dicepools that I thought were keyed off essence?
                                The Exigency is the 3e process by which the Unconquered Sun lets lesser deities create Exalted, known as Exigents. It doesn't have much to do with this conversation, other than that some of those Exalted might get really weird.

                                As for dice pools depending on Essence, well, that's some vocabulary confusion. Essence, the Trait, is a measure of spiritual cultivation. Essence also refers to the cosmic building block of the setting, but when Exalted are using it they're spending motes, a definite quantity like Blood Points, Rage, Gnosis, or Chi, to do something, and the dice-adding Charms generally add one die per mote spent (three motes dedicated to get one die or bonus dot to something for an entire scene, for the "booster" Charms that last longer), and how many you can bank at any given time also depends on Essence, the Trait. There's also a Charm that can expand that capacity (ten motes per purchase), with a caveat for accessing or filling the pool; these were first added in 1e Abyssals, who had what were originally terms Bloodthirst motes. Guess how they had to recharge that pool.

                                (It's entirely possible for Celestial Exalted like Solars or Abyssals to walk around with pools that are 40 or 50 motes total at the low end. The expansion Charms can take this over 100 even for those who haven't gotten Essence 6+ yet.)

                                                        One of the problems regarding balance stems more from a single fact that affects mostly PCs, that is that unlike in Vampire, mage players and werewolves aren't the " lamest and least powerful creature among their kind who's barely superior to the average human". So, it's a simple problem that has indeed a greater importance than it may seem at first glance and I've known several players who didn't feel like playing vampire for this exact reason. Vampire PCs are expected to start the game way too "lame" for their kind, as one friend once put it to me "You can't even FEEL like a TRUE vampire with 3 dots in disciplines and everybody around you and their mother is more powerful than you" and I'd say he's not really wrong. The perception is that the PCs in vampire are so vulnerable and weak that it's really hard not to feel damned while you can't see the sun anymore and still you can barely overcome a normal human in overal strength. Just imagine if Mage PC's started with only 2-3 dots in spheres and/or you had mostly only access to perception powers, it sure would feel different, but instead Mages start with 6 dots in spheres, they're probably not that impressive for their kind, but still they can definitely FEEL like real mages, the same thing is true for Werewolves, to start they begin with the powers associated to werewolves from legends, (I'd say even much more so, as werewolves in WoD are clearly superior to the general werewolf from legends, as WoD is the only universe I know where werewolves are the "best fighters in the world") they can change form at will, etc, so they don't really depend on Gifts, Gifts for werewolves are just pluses, now imagine if the only feature starting werewolves PCs had was being vulnerable to silver and then they'd be closer to what the average PC feels when starting a vampire game lol

                                                        Unfortunately, vampire core books didn't change the starting points in later editions and didn't offer much alternative, like expressing in the books that you could make PCs start with more than 3 dots in disciplines, etc. I know many people who dare not attempt things that aren't mentioned in the books, especially inexperienced STs. So, the inexperienced ST is going to bring these discrepant views of these beings into one world and certainly vampire PCs are going to be the least interesting/powerful by far. That's why I usually advise people wanting crossovers to make it so with elder's rules, this way you avoid making your vampire players simple support actors in the story. Of course, one could argue that if you make an overly social game, that's going to make the vampires shrine much more than the others, but it's usually very simple to turn a social situation/scene into a combat one with a few gestures, so to speak lol
