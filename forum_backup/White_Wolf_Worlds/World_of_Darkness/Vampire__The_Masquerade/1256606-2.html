        Originally posted by KarlB View Post
        I'm perfectly fine with vampires not having any special bonus to see in the dark by default. There's two level 1 disciplines that help with that already. In fact, if you look at vampire fiction as a whole, the most recurring power that vampires have is super strength and not all vampires in this game have Potence either.
        It's true that most cinematic vampires seem to start with 1 dot of Potence, Celerity, Fortitude and Auspex, and then it's a crap shoot whether or not they will learn other stuff (like Dominate or Protean).

        That might be an interesting variation on the game, for every starting vampire to have a dot in those 4 Disciplines, and then 1 or 2 dots to put into Clan Disciplines. Clan Disciplines and Out of Clan Disciplines would remain the same, so that a Toreador would have to pay OOC costs to raise Potence or Fortitude to 2. Like vampires from Blade or Buffy or Anne Rice novels or whatever, even a 'newbie' vampire would gain some strength, speed and resilience (and enhanced senses). Starting vampires would be a bit tougher than currently, but that's fine, they'd still be complete pushovers compared to Garou, and the 'lore' generally portrays *all* vampires as at least somewhat stronger/faster/tougher than the average human.

                            Originally posted by Monalfie View Post
                            This feels really pedantic. At the end of the day, Kindred are mystical creatures. What they achieve through the blood is, at the very least, achieved through that. If someone wanted to ascribe an aspect to that, seems fine.
                            They may be mystical, in some ways, but they are still the more mundane of the supernatural denizens of the World of Darkness.
                            But my point is more on "a vampire without Disciplines is still more or less a human", they can mostly do what humans do (unlike werewolves, for example, you take Gifts out of a Garou and you still have a Garou). Disciplines are what give them the supernatural edge they have.

                            Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                            SalamanDer
                            Except, as noted above, humans aren't blind in anything but the most absolute darkness. And we have other senses besides sight as it is (we're just very visually oriented and take training to make better use of our other senses actively). If a vampire's blood alone can be channeled to let them perform superhuman feats of strength (and do so without the physical changes necessary to comport with physics), the idea that they can't increase their sight especially (as blood flow and muscle control are really important to sight) doesn't really hold a lot of water. You don't need to shapeshift to see better.

                            VtM vampires also have plenty of innate powers that buck they idea that they're just "undead humans." Humans don't have the ability to staunch wounds reflexively, retractable fangs, or wound closing saliva for other examples.

                            VtR 2e is, again, a great example here. Vampires in VtR 2e don't get "Darkvision," just reduced penalties from darkness for general sight based perception. Except for a key thematic thing: blood. When it comes to blood (and heartbeats) VtR 2e vampires get huge perception boosts. This also means the classic "vampire can feel the blood in your veins and fresh vampires can get disoriented by all the blood pumping around them" trope actually works. VtR 2e's Auspex and Protean work just fine without specific "see in the dark" powers. Protean (as linked above as well) in particular lets you shapeshift to far more potent sensory improvements than seeing in the dark (such as bat level echolocation, or a snake's infrared vision, etc.) with are useful for more than explaining how vampires hunted for human blood without Protean 1 and before modern cities in VtM.

                            Though Blood Surge written more openly does at least partially address the OP even if didn't see it that way.
                            That's what I get for oversemplificating.

                            Human's are not blind in darkness, and that's a thing. This doesn't mean they can see well in the darkness. And this doesn't mean even that Auspex 1 and Protean 1 are equivalent. One let you see better, so it can lessen the malus in poor lighting, the other let you see in ambients were the human eye could not distinguish almost anything like they are in broad light. There's a large difference.
                            Now, if we're speaking of Heightened Senses (Sight) is a matter, if we talk about Eyes of the Beast is a whole another matter, and I think here we are mistaking the two effects. Because the example you're making of is from the Auspex area, but "see in the dark" (original question of the topic) is domain of Protean 1.
                            So you don't need to shapeshift to see better. Totally true. But you need to shapeshift to see in the darkest night like it's all well illuminated.

                            Moving on, as I mentioned above, Cainites are still extremely humans. While anyone can detail the structural difference with a human (they also burn like crisps), they are not able to do any of the astonishing feats usually associated to vampires. Yes, they sprout fangs, they can patch themselves up, close with saliva wounds they inflicted biting their prey (I don't think they can randomly close wounds like some wannabe miracle-worker) and can boost their body almost as they had a huge adrenaline surge. But any of this can give them the supernatural edge they have?
                            I think no. Without counting the obvious phisical differences, their actions without Disciplines, are all in the domain of a human. These are the Vampires of Masquerade (and, by the way, this is why the "withering" is so fearsome, without Disciplines even the fearsome Elders and Methusealk get kicked down to "human level").
                            And, I repeat, I was stating it like this in response to the question of the topic.

                            I don't know how the Vampires of Requiem are, but the ones of Masquerade are just humans knocked out of the mundane world with very little natural abilities. Beside those, they have the Disciplines, and the Disciplines are the bulk of a vampire's power. And they get the Beast too, as a wrapping to the whole package. Giving more supernatural skill to the vampires would make them less "masquerade". As a I see it, that's the core concept.
