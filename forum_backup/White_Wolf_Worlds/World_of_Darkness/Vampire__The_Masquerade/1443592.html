    The official V5 vs. V20 thread

    04-12-2021, 10:14 AM
    The edition wars are over and got very personal. However, it's been a much better and more polite place these past few months. As such, I think it'll be okay to make a thread for officially debating the merits of V5 vs. V20.

    1. Be polite: Everyone here is a fan of Vampire: The Masquerade after all.

    2. Respect there's no obvious answer: Some people like different things.

    3. Have Fun: This is a debate that's meant to be about your love of the game, not frustrated with other poster's opinion or specific details.

    4. Remember this is an OPP forum: This is kind of an off-beat mentioning but it should be noted that Onyx Path Publishing created V20 and yet also has done extensive numbers of supplements for V5. They have dogs on both sides of this fight and this is their forum, so, hey.

    5. You're welcome to like and dislike elements of both: There's things I love about V5 and things I hate. Same for V20.

    6. Try not to threadcrap: If you flat out don't like one. Focus on the positives of the one you do. Focus criticism specifically versus saying, "V20/V5 sucks."

    So with these rules in place, feel free to say what you think both sides did better and why you like or dislike things.

                  V20 vs. V5

                  I think that the biggest advantage of V5 over V20 is the fact that I'm a huge metaplot fan while V20 was designed to be metaplot agnostic. All of the characters were effectively frozen at the end of Revised and I had all of the Revised books so it didn't bring me much necessity to get V20. There's some good supplements that I enjoyed picking up toward the end like the revised Black Hand, Anarchs Unbound, and the Red List but I feel like it was limited by the fact that it was "frozen" without any way to move forward.

                  V5 may not have always made popular changes but it made changes and moved the setting into the 21st century.

                  V5 vs. OPP V5

                  One of the things that needs to be acknowledged is the fact that I think there's multiple versions of V5 and this is a reboot that has also undergone "course correction." I feel like the main book of V5 had a lot of very provocative changes to the setting that caused people to react badly. The Elders being Beckoned, the Sabbat going off to fight the Gehenna War, the lack of detail on certain clans, and so on. However, I think that Chicago by Night went a long way to correcting a lot of these flaws. Both it and Cults of the Blood Gods went a long way to providing flavor to the setting that was missing as well as dialing back the more overt changes. This made a much more playable and interesting V:TM and follows up on a lot of the metaplot elements of Beckett's Jyhad Diary.

                  Chicago by Night Fixes

                  * Nope, not all the Elders went to the Middle East.
                  * Nope, not every Elder feels the Beckoning.
                  * This is how the Anarchs work as a separate sect in the same city as the Camarilla.
                  * The Elders lie about banning cell phones. It's just no social media and that's so stupid that only Anarchs would do it.
                  * The Sabbat are still around enough to have the Lasombra defection matter. They're not all in the Middle East.
                  * Here's the Lasombra so you can USE the Sabbat if you want.
                  * The Second Inquisition is dangerous and has killed some vampires but is more interested than monitoring vampires than total extermination. It's also very divided.

                                      Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                      Basically, because I believe it is ANY mortal that the Vampire actually cares enough about to think about something other than BloodTM as their primary resource. If you care about your daughter, father, brother, lover, or slave then they are a Touchstone.
                                      While in theory they could be Touchstones (that's how VtR 2e does things), mechanically they aren't inherently Touchstones that matter to the Stains/Humanity system. For a Touchstone to matter mechanically, they have to represent a Conviction in the eyes of the vampire. Deep emotional connections to humans, by itself, doesn't do anything in V5.

                                      It seems like a lot of gamers think it must be someone they're nightmarishly obsessed with or in love with.
                                      It's certainly hyperbole that's gotten worse in critical-of-V5 circles, but V5 didn't do itself any favors here. If you don't want your Touchstones slapping you with more Stains than the Conviction linked to them actually saves you from, you do have to really carefully manage your Touchstones; which translated to in-game actions is very hard to avoid looking like an obsessive and abusive thing.

                                      You risk gaining two Stains any time a V5 Touchstone does something that is significantly out of line with your idealized version of them in your head. So your choices are: not have Touchstones (getting rid of them is a pain, but once they're gone they're gone) but recovering from Aggravated WP damage is harder, rack up Stains as people get on with their lives without living just to be a symbol for you, or use your influence (mundane or supernatural) to force them into the role you need them to function as.

                                      So you can toss yourself to the Beast, or you can be an obsessive creep that controls people's lives to lock them into a static image of what you think they should be.

                                      It's not that this is an inherently bad conflict for a game to explore, but a lot of V20 and V5 fans alike really don't like how it's forced into the system the way it is. It feels, to me, that Touchstones are one of the aspects of V5 that's very quickly approaching "so house ruled/Rule Zeroed/Golden Ruled/etc. that most players don't even realize they're playing a modified version of the rules," status. It's sort of like the assumption that if you're playing a d20 game, 1s are botches and 20s are crits. A lot of people assume this about d20 games and never bother checking to see if the rules are actually written that way because it's just gotten ingrained in people's minds that's how it works.

                                      ------------------------

                                      That all said, I'm largely in agreement with archderd. There really isn't a "V20 vs. V5 debate." What people have really been arguing is "change to V5 or stick with earlier books" and V20 is just the most recent of the earlier books making it the most played of the pre-V5 editions at the moment.

                                      I don't know anyone that considers V20 to be some massive step up from Revised. It's great for compiling a lot of late 2e/Revied material into a much smaller package than all those supplements, and has a few nice changes here and there (and some inexplicable ones), but it's the nostalgia omnibus edition it was always meant to be. If polled a many "sticking with V20 over V5" folks as possible with the question of, "would you like a new edition of VtM that advances the metaplot into the 21st century, and involves a major rules overhaul to improve on all the issues of classic VtM?" I'm confident you'd easily get 90% or more responding enthusiastically with "Yes!"

                                      When Onyx Path announced what they were calling VtM 4th Edition (not seeing 20th as a proper edition), the vast majority of the response was excitement over the potential for what a real change up was going to look like. All the acrimony on the "sticking with V20 side," is that V5 didn't live up to what we wanted to see out of that. Hence we didn't change over to V5.

                                      --------------------

                                      I don't want to beat a dead horse, but I also think one of the biggest issues is how rushed V5 was by nWW because they got it stuck in their heads that they had to get V5 out for GenCon that year. It's painfully obvious V5 needed more development; and perhaps a lead that wasn't as stubborn as Martin (it felt like a lot of the designers lower on the ladder were more open to some of the feedback during the public playtests, but a lot of that feedback was ignored by the time V5 was put out, and it's hard not to see Martin as the guy that made that decision) who clearly had a few ideas he wanted in the game no matter what.

                                      CT's point about V5 vs. OPP V5 illustrates this a lot to me because V5 has a lot of potential in my eyes, but constantly shoots itself in the foot. Almost everything listed in what makes V5 Chicago and Cult of the Blood Gods such improvements to V5 are things nWW got direct feedback about during the playtest in exactly those directions. It wasn't stuff "fixed" in hindsight, it was stuff that needed to get fixed because the wrong people were in-charge of V5 (not that it had to be Onyx Path, since plenty of us fans spotted the same things).

                                      V5 is as frustrating and divisive as it is, partially because to a lot of us it was so close to being what we wanted when Eddy and Rich announced their idea for a new edition, and so many of the spots that are deal breakers are things that we've been highlighting since the first public alpha.
