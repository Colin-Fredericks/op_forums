        Originally posted by Damian May View Post
        Slightly off topic but I've always wondered why folks see VTM:B as so integral to VtM that they want tabletop tie ins and all the rest? I mean I was playing VtM for over a decade before VtM:B came out so it had literally no effect on me except ' a neat tie in CRPG that my computer would die if it tried to run.....also so buggy as to be almost unplayable'.

        Just curious as it seems to be somewhat integral to some of the louder voices heres idea of what VtM is whereas for me it was the equivalent of a VtM keychain in regards to the actual game.
        Probably not the right person to talk about this, since I personally haven't even played the first Bloodlines game, but:
        1. Bloodlines was many people's first exposure to VtM. That it was what initially hooked people into the setting, and prompted them to look further by picking up the TRPG.
        2. Bloodlines being the only VtM content that had people going. Game was released the same year that the WoD ended. And since this was a time where no one knew that a V20 was ever going to be a thing, and the initial controversies and backlash against Vampire the Requiem, Bloodlines was all people had.
        3. The simple merit that, in the time period it was released (and by extension now) video games received more coverage then TRPGs in the geek-sphere. That to the mainstream geek crowd, this was the most they were ever going to know about it. Same with Shadowrun, how many not immediately in the TRPG community only know it for the SNES game, or more recently, the Shadowrun Returns trilogy of games.
