      Eh, I'd argue probably not because:

      1. The majority of diablerists were going to be members of one Warring Sect for another. During wartime, you can kill whoever you want and no one will care.

      2. The thing about Gangrel on the Road are going to be that they're not going to run into one another nearly as often as Kindred that are congregated in one specific area like Elysium or not. I mean, Yellowstone is pretty huge even for Kindred stalking the campsites.

      3. Gangrel are generally more in touch with their Beast than most Kindred. If only by 20% or so. If they're going to diablerize someone, it's going to be very deliberate versus Kindred who lose control and sink their fangs into a guy.

      4. Gangrel are also good at taking the measure of other Kindred. The Big Dog will not eat the Little Dog and the Little Dog is not stupid.

      5. Depending on the Prince, Blood Hunts are already guilt-free power offers. It may destroy your social cred to eat Annabelle once Kevin Jackson gets sick of her and piss off all her friends but it's legal.

                                                          I think an issue that comes into play is how easy is it supposed to be to know how to diablerize someone in your chronicles?

                                                          We, as the readers of sourcebooks written to play a game, have a wealth of knowledge about diablerie from a game mechanics perspective, but I have doubts as to how well known both the practice and the practical details of doing it are known IC, at least outside the Sabbat and Assamites. Like many things, we have a different perspective in early Vampire than we do in later editions when it became much more common.

                                                          One aspect of diablerie is that you continue to diablerize the vampire after that vampire's vitae has run out. You just don't drain the vampire of her blood points, but proceed to health levels as the vampire drains the "life force" and then finally consumes the "soul" of the vampire.

                                                          So I think it's important to realize there are two related, but distinct terms of diablerie. One is just "a vampire who has drunk all the vitae of another vampire" and the second is "a vampire who has performed the actual complete act of draining another vampire's vitae, life force and consumed the Fire Within."

                                                          I am sure there are many vampires who know the first definition, but not the details of the second. Even if they are determined cannibals to drain the vitae of other vampires, they won't ever truly succeed in diablerie. They'll just put the other vampire into torpor.

                                                          Furthermore, even those who learn the real steps that need to be done may stop well short of actual diablerie. You need to make Strength checks at Difficulty 9. As readers who have read out of character what needs to be done, we can have our characters act accordingly. How many 9s have I rolled? Is it enough to eliminate all their Health Levels? If not, I will continue to such even though I am now on my tenth extended roll on Strength and may need to do a dozen more to get all the nines I need. But that is applying out of character knowledge to a scene. What would happen if the ST rolled all those dice for you? When would you stop? What if you didn't know the goal was to eliminate Health Levels, and was just told to "keep going" after all the vitae was drained? In practice, a lot of vampires, especially those doing it for the first time, will give up long before they complete diablerie and get any benefits. Or perhaps they did succeed, but on a vampire of inferior generation and therefore gained no power from it.

                                                          I wouldn't be surprised if there is a large number of vampires (at least outside the Sabbat and Assamites) who believe diablerie giving you power is a myth like Golconda. They've "done" it (improperly) and know they've gotten nothing out of it. It tastes delicious, but it doesn't do squat for you. Those rumors have to be out there just as much as the good rumors. I imagine most of the successful Blood Hunts of the Camarilla end long before the victim is truly diablerized.

                                                          I remember one detail in the original Chicago By Night quite well. And that was how Alan Sovereign had heard a rumor that killing one's sire and drinking his blood makes a vampire more powerful. There's a lot of implied assumptions in that statement. Sovereign is a fairly influential and knowledgeable vampire albeit a neonate. He's one of the prince's brood and the main lieutenant of Horatio Ballard. He has a Secrets score of B- (he pretty much knows everything concerning vampires in Chicago except the politics within the Primogen and the struggle between Helena and Menele. Like a lot of character traits in CbN, not everything makes sense and there are lots of vampires that know more than Sovereign, but he's clearly in the middle of the listed NPCs.)

                                                          He's an example of a clearly well informed vampire. Despite that he has 1) never previously been exposed to the concept of diablerie before, 2) only recently heard about it as a rumor, and 3) the rumor is wrong (and vague) on details. I think a lot of vampires, especially non-elders, should be like that. And the elders who do know, why the hell would they ever explain that to anyone?

                                                          So I think there is a big difference between the first definition, which many vampires will know as drinking the blood of a vampire, and the actual secret definition (that it gives you real benefits and has a special procedure you must follow to do so).

                                                          Given the social stigma of such cannibalism and the effects it has on one's aura (though that may only happen after true diablerie, but I think it would be interesting to have such auras be seen after failed attempts at it), there may be much less diablerie done outside the Sabbat then one may thinks.

                                                          Characters in the setting don't have an official rulebook and setting guide like we do. As such, I think STs can have a wide range of options of how well known the details of the diablerie concept are in the general vampire population.
