    I've felt there was enough Antitribu for players to play Lasombra in prior editions.

    I don't like the Camarilla getting the Sabbat's religiosity. It could happen, certainly; Sabbat defectors would push for that kind of thing, but It feels like the writers are struggling between wanting to be 'relevant' and projecting their own political experiences onto vampires where it isn't entirely appropriate: The Anarchs are morally grey, the Camarilla as "The Man" are black, and the Sabbat are gone because "we didn't need something blacker than black or it'd make our point silly as the Camarilla would look like the moderates again... hmm" or something to that effect.

    If the metaplot doesn't have the Sabbat come back in a grand, glorious crusade and the 'gehenna war' was made to be part of some -actually good- plan, it will forever keep much of the fanbase playing v20 (but then there's.. let's not get into that)


    I dunno, I think the Lasombra are willing to play the long game
    Lasombra don't lose to ventrue because they're an inferior clan with delusions of grandeur (a lot of them do have delusions of grandeur, but so do Ventrue) The Lasombra lose because they aren't nearly as well organized. They're probably the worst organized clan in the Camarilla, even if we re-include the Brujah (because it's absurd they'd leave) virtually every lasombra wants to be the boss (there's a hilarious flaw in LotB to represent the extremes of that) So I doubt they'd all agree to a master-plan, especially considering the questionable departure from the Sabbat which was surely haphazard at best.



    If I were writing reasons to make the Lasombra more Camarilla friendly, rather than absolutely fu.... I will be using V20 as a jumping off point:
    -A few high profile elders and (possibly with a methuselah or two) build up their strengths, encourage faction conflicts, and slaughter their probable opposition to declare a number of high profile cities Camarilla domains. To bolster their cities and add legitimacy to their claims they invite other Camarilla vampires over (A few elders for stability, but also many neonates and thinbloods, struggling with their own cities, come over; they'll be eager meatshields when the counter attack comes)
    -The original Lasombra Antitrubu, with news of these new cities taking potential Sabbat attention away from them, start caching in their favours to embrace.
    -Gratioano... does something mildly interesting.

                                                    I appreciate that V5 really shook up the status quo, but naturally, I don't like all of the specifics about it. I loved the Sabbat and Sabbat campaigns, and that I can't run such campaigns any longer is something that really puts me off V5. (well, okay, I -can-, but the game certainly doesn't help me). In particular, I think the Sabbat had a much more interesting political structure and more compelling internal conflicts than the Anarchs and the Camarilla (see my thread "Why the Sabbat is more interesting than the Camarilla" for more on this), and my greatest fear about V5 is that it may have squandered much of what made the Sabbat so fascinating.

                                                    I'm not too fond of the Lasombra joining the Camarilla in V5. My main issue is however not on a worldbuilding level, but a purely thematic one - The Lasombra are simply too similar to the Ventrue. They were always, to my mind, a darker version of Ventrue, with very, -very- similar themes. However, that's not to say that the Lasombra necessary had to be in the Sabbat, as Sabbat Ventrue were also a compelling faction, and the story of how they split from the Camarilla Ventrue is very great. But Lasombra and classic Camarilla Ventrue in the same faction feels...boring and bland. I think the Tremere, having the same classy feel as the Ventrue yet being in many ways different, were a better thematic fit as the main rivals of the Ventrue within the Camarilla, and by crippling the Tremere, V5 really damaged this intresting conflict. Had the Lasombra joined the Anarchs instead, I would have had much less issue with it. They could easily have become the Ventrue of the Anarch movement. It would suit both their concept and their lore. Having them stay in the Sabbat would have been fine too.

                                                    However, with roleplaying games, one can always take inspiration from ideas and make something else out of them, so perhaps ironically, my first V5 chronicle actually features the Lasombra joining the Camarilla. But I did it in a different way. I 've written about it in other threads already, but the short form of it is that Lasombra elders don't feel The Beckoning (presumably because they did indeed destroy their antediluvian long ago). With the Sabbat in chaos for many reasons, most of the Amici Noctis lost faith in the Sabbat. In my chronicle the Amici Noctis is a very small group (as I wanted to feature them in scenes in a practical way), and the only one who disagreed was cardinal Menuven. He (they/it) remained in the Sabbat and eventually became its leader in the New World, but the Amici Noctis threw him out and embarked on their new plan.They had decided to try and worm their way into the Camarilla, for they hoped that their many unbeckoned elders would eventually (in a hundred years or so) gain them a high position in that sect. Of course, this plan hinges completely on them managing to hide their beckoning immunity, so the Amici Noctis had to murder tons of noncompliant or untrustworthy Lasombra, but the complicated situation in Mexico City (Sabbat civil war, Gehenna War, Camarilla siege, etc) gave them a good excuse. One of the Amici Noctis, Eleiser de Polanco, led a fraction of Lasombra in Mexico City in defecting to the Camarilla, and handed the Camarilla tons of Sabbat secrets - and scores of dead Lasombra, especially followers of the Path of Night. Naturally, the Camarilla weren't about to trust a traitor, but the situation on the ground in Mexico City was very, very complicated, and de Polanco made them an offer they couldn't refuse. So, controversially, Fiorenza di Savona of clan Ventrue made a deal with the devil - de Polanco would be allowed to rule as Camarilla prince of Mexico City, with her as his seneschal, but the Lasombra would -not- get a justicar, and Polanco had to consent to the Lasombra primogen being a known Camarilla-loyalist from a long Lasombra antitribu lineage. De Polanco also had to purge all known path of Night followers and overt Abyss mysticists from the Lasombras ranks, but that wasn't a big hindrance - most of those had left for the Gehenna War or escaped with vice regent Menuven to Acapulco.

                                                    TLDR; in my chronicle, the Lasombra did join the Camarilla, but as a very long-term power play, rather than from desperation. The Camarilla accepted this with reluctance, but it happened thanks to a unilateral action on a local level in Mexico City, that was only approved by the Inner Circle after the fact, because they couldn't say no to being handed Mexico City on a plate. And some really metal Lasombra like Menuven remains staunch Sabbat loyalists.

                                                        I think the Lasombra joining the Camarilla is one of the less good ideas in V5. My main issue is however not on a worldbuilding level, but a purely thematic one - The Lasombra are simply too similar to the Ventrue. They were always, to my mind, a darker version of Ventrue, with very, -very- similar themes. I think the Tremere, having the same classy feel as the Ventrue yet being in many ways different, were a better thematic fit as the main rivals of the Ventrue within the Camarilla, and by crippling the Tremere, V5 really damaged this conflict. Had the Lasombra joined the Anarchs instead, I would have had much less issue with it. They could easily have become the Ventrue of the Anarch movement. It would suit both their concept and their lore.
                                                        Ultimately, I feel differently on this.

                                                        My thoughts being:

                                                        1. The Ventrue and Lasombra make excellent rivals in the sect and there's now a Shadow Lords vs. Silver Fangs power struggle going on between them. The Lasombra offer an alternative to the stodgy old power structure that previously existed.

                                                        2. The Ventrue have never had anything but glowing support from the Tremere because the former have needed the latter and vice versa. The Tremere don't WANT Princes and Praxises (though they won't turn them down if they fall into their lap) but need stability that the Ventrue provide in spades. Their current crappy situation shows the Ventrue are vulnerable without their "Court Wizards" but also that the Ventrue did jack for the Tremere when they needed them.

                                                        In other words, the Tremere as Ventrue rivals is a thing I've never done so that may explain why the Lasombra fill a void in my world where they don't in yours.

                                                        3. The Lasombra really were wasted in the Sabbat where they were just the Ventrue of the sect. The Tzimisce served the same role as the Tremere to the Ventrue and it was really an unnecessary duplication. It doesn't help the Sabbat don't have any of the culture and politicking that the Camarilla does. No need to be subtle in the Sabbat if you can just challenge a rival to monomancy like you're Klingons.

                                                        4. I feel like the Ventrue don't have nearly the same amount of flavor or panache as the Lasombra. They're kind of bland compared to the Lasombra.
