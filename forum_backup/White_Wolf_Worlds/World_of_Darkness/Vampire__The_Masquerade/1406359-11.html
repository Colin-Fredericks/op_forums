                        Originally posted by False Epiphany View Post
                        I remember in CTPhipps' Let's Read of 1e Chicago how one poster said "a lot of the vampires feel like ordinary people who just happen to have a blood-based diet." Characters like Son, Jacob Schumpter, and Gordon Keaton served to illustrate the blackest depths of Kindred monstrosity, but there was a pretty big spectrum.

                        Here it feels like everyone has been shoved squarely into Son territory.
                        The game originally was supposed to be about personal horror - an ordinary person struggling with their new vampiric condition. As such, there were plenty of examples of that kind of character in Chicago By Night, and to a lesser extent other early edition material. However, these kinds of vampires were increasingly removed from play for various reasons.

                        First, there was a confusion by some players that personal horror meant whining, pseudo-angst, and life avoidance, and that created a backlash against those kind of characters since they irritated a lot of people.

                        Second, the game seemed to go overboard on vampires that didn't actually deal with the condition of being a vampire. They seemed more like characters in a Chris Claremont X-Men story, so again there was a backlash against that. The combined backlash of these first two elements meant the game began emphasizing truly horrific characters and any kind of vampire who struggled against her condition was dropped or shown to be a fraud.

                        Third, as the setting became more developed, the game switched from being one based on personal experience and exploring the setting, to an increasingly militarized confrontation between Camarilla and Sabbat (or other factions). And there really isn't any room for vampires fighting against their condition in that metaplot scenario. Who cares about the personal struggles of Hannibal King, Nick Knight, or Louis when the Sabbat is burning down your haven and you have to lead the task force against their laboratory spewing out Vozhd.

                        I think the prevalence of Humanity 10 vampires and those who've achieved Golconda went too far in first and early second edition, but the counter-reaction went too far as well. I think high humanity vampires, though rare and mostly limited to inexperienced neonates with some notable exceptions, do have a place in the game. The game just needs to acknowledge the difficult path ahead of them and present them dealing with the appropriate challenges of that lifestyle.

                        I've never seen the Anarchs as a morally superior force to the Camarilla, but certainly there is a role for high humanity vampires in some of their ranks.

                                In my experience, the more external politics you insert into a chronicle, the less time there is to focus on the personal struggles of being a vampire. That isn't to say one is better than other (I usually prefer a mix of both). Like action, mystery, or romance, they're simply different subgenres the game can encompass. A given chronicle is likely to focus more on some subgenres than others, as is the gameline as a whole under different creative leads. This can also, unfortunately, alienate some gamers when they feel like developers are pushing their preferred themes and subgenres as the "one true way to play"--see Revised's focus on inter-sect warfare and V5's on elder-less street-level play.

                                As a sourcebook, Anarchs is most useful when it has content that GMs running different styles of games can mine. I think it does a pretty good job there. There is a balance of metaplot-central characters like Tyler or Theo Bell with with loners who are off being vampires without much concern for larger Kindred society.

                                        Beast on a Leash

                                        Never spank a vampire.

                                        Chinasa Adeyemi interviews petplay enthusiasts Janet and William Stoughton In this third of a series on vampires in sub cultures. As with the two previous entires it reminds us vampires are horrible people, the worst kind of fan, and ruin everything they touch.

                                        Specifically petplay is a kind of sexual play, a variation on a theme when compared to BDSM. The Stoughton’s are involved with mortals in this, but despite their own supposed best intentions they remain vampires and so are evil and horribly dangerous to everyone around them. The Stoughtons cannot help but violate some basic tenants of sexual play of any kind, in that they are lying to and not acting on good faith with the human participants.

                                        The humans involved do not know they are dealing with vampires when they start discipline, bondage, punishment and similar activities with the Stoughtons. This gets humans killed. The vampires treat this as both a minor inconvenience and little more than a social faux pas.

                                        More so than the last two section this one is a pitch dark comedy, almost to the point of kink shaming. That nice suburban couple with the lovely house and well-kept yard are (1) into some pretty kinky stuff, (2) vampires, (3) and sometimes murder their doms because of poor planning and bad communication. Honestly, the section feels like it is (to use the British slang) taking the Mickey out of too tame BDSM stories and fantasies involving vampires – here it is asserted that will get you killed.

                                        This section, like the last two, offers plot possibilities but mostly for a group of mortals dealing with the damage vampire create. There are few reason for other vampires to get involved, except possibly to cover up a Masquerade breach created by the Stoughtons.

                                            Vegas Nights


                                            So this entry is about some Rando who goes to Vegas for an interesting weekend and gets exactly that as part of an encounter at a vampire owned and run club (and possibly casino – it’s a little unclear).

                                            Specifically, Rando runs into the Voerman sisters, Jeanette and Therese. The Voerman sister appeared in the original Bloodlines game. The catch is there is (depending on your point of view) there is only one Voerman, and she suffers from multiple personalities. One personality is the straight-laced Therese, the other being the more daring and gregarious Jeanette.

                                            While not expressly stated, it is almost certain Jeanette uses some disciplines to lure in and subdue Rando, as he states within hours of meeting her he is asking her to marry him. She agrees – and they actually have a Vegas wedding – and then she embraces him. Later Therese is utterly exasperated by all of this and gives Rando a job as a means of finding something to do with the man. Rando talks about getting a promotion, no longer having to work as a barman, and how to use dating apps to lure in prey.

                                            The Voerman sisters have apparently moved from LA to Vegas, and opened a club, again named The Asylum, like the club they had in Bloodlines. What prompted the move is never stated, and probably not even very important. I find it amusing that Rando and Jeanette apparently got married when he was in an altered mental state, which is one of those stories about things that happen in that town. What happens in Vegas, stays in Vegas.

                                            This section is interesting, and even amusing. The Asylum acts like a hotspot for Anarchs in Vegas, and probably an important one for across the US. Vegas has more or less landed in the hands of the Anarchs after the Second Inquisition moved through, destroying the Camarilla. That is an interesting tidbit of information. Also, the entire section reads like the background of PC who used merits to purchase an interesting sire. That is not a bad thing, just an observation. And the section provides more plot hooks than the last few.

                                                    BDSM, Mountain Climbing, Metal, and Redundancy

                                                    Honestly, I think the BDSM part of the book is the absolute worst part of the book and it's not because it's offensive but it's the fact that its very redundant as well as a bit of a non-sequitor. We've already had three parts of the book related to Anarchs preying on the hobbyists of topics related to their preferred topic in life. We had the heavy metal girl and the mountain climber so now we're getting a BDSM couple that murders people. It's a little weird too because there's nothing related to being an "Anarch" that really says, "My hobbies define me." This strikes me as something that relates to the fact that they did this entire book without any OOC information. As such, they seem like they wandered a bit off-topic and just went for, "Tell a horrifying story about a vampire."

                                                    I feel like they could have thrown in some stories about Camarilla oppression, religious cults, or something else that would have given a better perspective on the Anarch Movement. One thing I feel like this book lacks is the world-weary "survivor" story that I feel like Anarchs would have. You know something like, "My name is Carol. I was Embraced by a Toreador who liked my dancing and then abandoned me after he found out that I just did it for the money. Then my family was murdered by the Prince after I stayed at a friend's house and he couldn't find me. He murdered her too. I didn't ask to be a vampire but it didn't matter because of their stupid laws. The Anarchs may not be that much better but I'll someday kill Tara, the Judas Prince."

                                                    Honestly, the book doesn't seem to have much focus on the Anarchs' WHYS hating the Camarilla or why at all. I find that very weird.

                                                    Humanity

                                                    I understand why they chose to go the route they did with the fact virtually every Anarch in the book is a scumbag. Revised was the first of the Editions that really embraced that vampires are BAD and that had its benefits to the line. Justin Achilli, the new Justicar of our line, loved making it clear both the Camarilla and Sabbat were equally bad. The Anarchs lacking edge and being the "Sabbat but whinier" was a fan perception that was propagated by the fact that they didn't seem ruthless enough to seize cities or Mass Embrace or otherwise "whatever it took" to take down Camarilla cities.

                                                    I feel they're overcompensating here to make the Anarchs edgey and have gone a bit far in making them discount Sabbat, ironically what they weren't before.

                                                    The Voerman Sisters

                                                    I think the use of Bloodlines was a really smart move by V5's developers and Jason Carl in particular. The LA By Night web show on Geek and Sundry/White Wolf's Twitch channel took place in the video game version of LA versus the Anarch Free States established by Los Angeles by Night. I know they're the same setting but they had a lot of changes between the two with the show leaning much heavier on the video game.

                                                    Having the Voermans move to Las Vegas is interesting and probably fits LA by Night where Vannevar Thomas declared a Blood Hunt on her last season. It also
                                                    is a place that fits both of them as Jeanette is the perfect party girl and Therese is the excellent cold-blooded business woman behind such. I should note that Las Vegas was also a Giovanni city. Like Boston, it was a city that wasn't supposed to be under control of the Giovanni due to the Treaty that keeps them out of the Jyhad. However, it was under de facto control of the Giovanni. Here, it seems that they were overthrown and it left a power vacuum that the Anarchs moved in.

                                                    The thing is that Therese/Jeanette are 6th generation vampires so they could actually be BARON of Las Vegas rather than just a immigrant to the city. It also means that the poor schmuck that was Embraced on a whim, blood bound, and then put behind the bar is a 7th generation vampire. I also like that she's just recruiting a bunch of employees for her club. I also think club instead of casino because there's more than just the Strip.

                                                    A reminder that the Asylum is also a Loresheet now. It's a franchise that Therese runs from Vegas with vampire employees. Same for the Succubus Club.
