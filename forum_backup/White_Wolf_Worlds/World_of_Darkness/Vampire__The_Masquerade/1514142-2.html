                Yeah, I don't really buy the idea that Paradox just can't walk and chew bubble gum at the same time. Especially when one is just "communicate the state of development". While not without work involved, drafting a public statement about an ongoing process, it's orders of magnitude more manageable than making a video game. Especially if, as stated, they have or could have people whose JOB is to interface with the public. What are you paying PR for if not to do that work for you?

                Either something's wrong with PR itself, or there's something wrong with the game. Either way, it doesn't imply the ship is sailing well.

                Moreover, whatever problems might be going on behind closed doors, NOT talking about them is arguably worse (that is, unless what's going on is really, criminally bad). We can imagine worse than the reality. And not talking about the state of the game means you're not really exercising control of the situation.


                (To use an example: back in 2015, when Games Workshop was shuttering Warhammer Fantasy Battles with a bullet and rebooting it with Age of Sigmar, the company at the time had ZERO online presence. No blog, no newsletter, no social media. In 2015. The only information they put out, after they destroyed WHFB's setting with an apocalypse, was ONE ad in White Dwarf, a print magazine, saying that Age of Sigmar was coming. And then total radio silence. For SIX MONTHS. GW basically let a fanbase that was already mad about the End Times stew in their own rage for half a year. Talking to each other online and in game stores, with no input or intervention from the company. Even if AoS's initial release build wasn't hot trash, the negative reception from fans was all but inevitable. It was so bad that not only did AoS require a complete overhaul just to make it playable, let alone good, it got GW's management fired due to how badly they messed things up. That almost never happens, bad bosses taking the fall for their mistakes. THAT is what happens when you refuse to communicate with your customers.)
