    Shaitan being the actual Huitzilopochti was right there from The Chaos Factor. In fact, his position as the Aztec god was fairly central to the whole plot.

    Since, I came into the WoD through Mage in the 90’s and it wasn’t until the 2010’s that I finally started exploring Vampire as part of someone’s crossover game, I’ve always run my games as if The Chaos Factor’s take on Shaitan/the Baali was THE canon for the group and thanks to his infernal investments on top of his formidable vampiric powers and age, Shaitan is actually closer in power to an Antediluvian than any extant 4th Gen.

    Throw in the BJD option where Enoch reveals the Antediluvians aren’t actually as powerful as they pretend to be and you get a possible Gehenna setup where Shaitan may actually be as strong or stronger than the genuine Antediluvians and might even be the taint-bringing Father of Darkness whose power comes from the nether realms.

    As to The Children; my personal take in relation to some of my headcanon about all generations’ blood slowly thickening over millennia is that the Caine from which generations are counted isn’t even the first vampire, that there could have been dozens of generations between him and the actual “Blood Caine” (basically the vampire equivalent of Mitochondrial Eve... and which also explains the Laibon and Drowned Legacies... they’re descended from “Blood Caine” but not from Biblical Caine.

    Anyway, that means there are unknown additional generations of vampires that may predate Biblical Caine and are so old that even trying to raise them from their torpor is futile, but they’re also so massively old that False Slumber (Auspex 9) and various other elder disciplines allowed them to influence the world through their “dreams” and their astral selves and possibly forgotten discipline powers would have be mistaken for demonic entities throughout history.
    Can you remember which chapter of BJD had this revelation from Enoch? I don't recall it off the top of my head (though I read it several years ago).

                    Yeah, it was deliberately left as just one option... another was that this coffin sealed up many thousands of years ago contained only ashes that, if studied with certain magic would be identified as belonging to a thin-blooded vampire; which would confirm the idea that the blood potency of each generation thickens with time (though measured in centuries to millennia) and at one point Caine and the Antediluvians were as thin-blooded as the 16th Generations of today.

                    Which, on its own has some interesting implications in the V20/Revised canon where some of the thinbloods are “Inceptors”... vampires who created their own disciplines... something previously believed exclusive only to the Antediluvians.

                    Except, if the Antediluvians were once thinblooded themselves and that was the point in time when they were creating their new disciplines... then there’s a link. No one’s been able to do much with disciplines because the general prohibitions against thinblooded progeny kept the blood too thick (one might say “too static”) to have the flexibility needed to incept new disciplines.

                    And it could have literally been a long con by the Antediluvians to keep anyone else from being able to incept new abilities that could challenge them in ways their current stasis would prevent them from being able to adapt to.

                    If you’re running with cyclical Gehennas it could even be the blood getting too thin and new disciplines being incepted that causes the Antediluvians to stir because they need to wipe out the potential threat.

                    Take it one step further with the thickening blood and the option that the Antediluvians are NOT as powerful as they pretend to be suggesting that there could be a “max blood potency” for vampires that they’d all eventually reach if they live long enough and it’s entirely possible that some Antediluvians meet final death in each Gehenna cycle only to have a younger inceptor replace them (and by the time the next Gehenna cycle rolls around in a few thousand years their blood has now reached “max potency”).

                    Basically, Generation only matters for those who haven’t yet reached the thickness of blood where max potency caps things. Caine, the “Second” and “Third” Generations are all said to be equally strong. But if the “Second” was actually the 22nd Generation (making what we call the 12th Generation actually the 32nd) and there was still a 12th Generation Vampire around they’d both be equal in power since they’re both at max potency...

                    ... and from the point of view of a “5th/25th Gen” sired 5000 years after that... would they even be able to tell the difference? They’re all Blood Gods powerful beyond comprehension and so in stories the “active” ones when things finally get written down are labeled as the 3rd Generation while those dead or in permanent torpor are labeled the 2nd Generation and their distant progenitor as Caine/the First Generation (which also fits a lot of human psychology in general... we really like the number three and dividing into thirds for some reason and it turns up in myths and stories a lot more than you’d expect for a bilaterally symmetrical species with five digits on each of their four limbs).

                    But it also provides a really fun answer for what the Children are, and also potentially some of the other elder evils out there. In Mage the Malfeans worshipped by the Nephandi were said to be the dreams of the sleeping Neverborn, but if you wanted to play with it, the Neverborn themselves might be the psychic manifestations of these elder vampires slumbering eternally beneath the earth.

                    Side-bar: in one massive Gehenna/Apocalypse/Armageddon crossover I once ran I mashed the eldest Nephandi Lords, Antediluvians and Maeljin Incarna into being different faces of the same entities. This would essentially be a variation on that thought.

                                I'm currently reading Ray Jenkins' "Clanbook: Baali" (available at Storytellers' Vault), and have been struck by how neatly the author ties together lore from several game editions into one tidy package. It's very clever.

                                Jenkins frames the Children of the Outer Dark as entities that lived in the Void before Creation, which definitely lends itself to a reading as them being Neverborn Malfeans if you want them to be something that's known to exist in other game lines.

                                The author takes care to repeatedly emphasize that the Children are definitely not demons from Hell, and that the First Tribe did not worship demons. They worshipped the Children, and their dealings with demons were for power to either wake the Children or keep them asleep. In the modern nights, the more conservative Molochim stick to the old ways, while the clan's demon worshipping bad apples are descendants of Nergal or the Other who have wandered off the path through centuries of bad leadership, careless siring, and shoddy scholarship.

                                In this model, and in a chronicle that uses concepts from Demon: the Fallen, the Baali would have had plenty of contact with the Earthbound over the eons, but only because the Baali were summoning and exploiting the Earthbound for power to either wake or keep asleep the true objects of their worship.
