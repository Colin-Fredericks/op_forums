                                                Speaking as an ardent fan and defender of a big and messy crossover World of Darkness, I don't think that needs to operate as a default assumption. It's probably important to discuss with your play-group, find out what expectations people have for the game and such, but what option is best is ultimately a product of that conversation. Obviously there's no good to come from trying to force a WoD crossover from a Storyteller that is uninterested or unable to tell that kind of story. And a group of new players who were drawn in very specifically by (insert game-line) may not be best served by a game that forces them to deal with the Rashomon-style mess of bringing the full complexity of *every* game-line into the narrative.

                                                Originally posted by Submariner93 View Post
                                                Thank you all for the insightful answers. I see werewolves as a toolkit: a supernatural antagonist, a boogeyman, a dip into nature and animism, etc... so everything you have written can be useful. I never thought about the fact that maybe it's ok for the character (and for the player) to not understand at all what is going on, or try to understand gifts, auspices and spirit world stuff (unless explicitly being important for a character). Maybe in that case another vampire NPC could be helpful in giving some filtered explanations.

                                                I will definitely check the hunter manual and Crimson Thaw for inspiration.
                                                ​

                                                Especially if the role you envision for the Garou is that of "the boogeyman," having most of what's actually going on not be comprehensible to the characters (or players) is not only acceptable, but a good thing. You shouldn't be looking for the chance to use it to gank someone, but you can add some genuine horror by having them dealing with a threat that does not follow rules that they can understand.

                                                Using vampire NPCs for filtered explanations is definitely a good idea. And it's important to remember that, from the perspective of a crossover-style World of Darkness, even those filtered perspectives are going to be immensely limited outside of quite exceptional circumstances. Many previous versions of WoD games had a special Knowledge stat called "Lore" that characters could acquire, with different versions for the various types of supernatural beings, and being able to know pretty much *anything* about the specific details of others splats was predominantly limited to N/PCs with that specific Trait. And even then, you'd have to have 4 or 5 dots to come close to the amount of knowledge that the typical player gets just out of reading one of the core rulebooks.

                                                (I realized writing this paragraph that I don't think you've specified an edition. Lore still exists in Mage 20, but I can't recall if it comes up in the other 20A lines. And other editions are mostly outside my knowledge.)

                                                Even a Werewolf character probably shouldn't know everything that you can find in just the core rulebook for WtA, so your average Vampire is not going to have a clue about how any of the Garou's various abilities work. That's the realm of hardcore specialists and Methuselahs, who can be expected to have learned more simply by virtue of the quantity of time they have available to them.
