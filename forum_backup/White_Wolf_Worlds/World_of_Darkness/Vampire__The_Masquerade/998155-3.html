                    A note on Warhammers, from a historical perspective.

                    They were originally designed as a counter-weapon to medieval armors, with the purpose to deal extreme concussive damage that didn't need to penetrate the armor to deal effective focused damage, while at the same time of course being quite deadly to non-armored enemies. Originally they had no spike, only the hammer part, and usually had a long shaft for effective swings, and in some regards could be considered pole weapons, especially the models used against mounted opponents that were almost halberd-size sometimes. Spikes were quickly added however to this effective weapon to make it even more versatile and deadly.

                    Most fantasy setting games (DnD we're looking at you) always got the Warhammer-part confused with Mauls, a weapon that didn't appear until 14th century or so. So that's that. Ironically old school rpg's like Rolemaster actually got the Warhammer defined more correctly as more of a spike weapon than a club weapon. But the rpg legacy has Warhammers all confused, usually calling what actually is a regular Warhammer for War Mattock instead.

                    When inconcistencies like this appear in rpg books, I correct them myself. It's not hard to do. Especially not in a storytellign setting where everything is supposed to be more of guidelines in how to create dramatic conflict and scenery and not tactically correct combat. For that I recommend stuff like Phoenix Command. Hahaha. Have fun with that.
