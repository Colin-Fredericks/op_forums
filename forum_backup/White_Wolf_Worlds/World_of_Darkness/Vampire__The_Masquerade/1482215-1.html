            Maybe. Look at any MMO (RPG or FPS or etc.) that's under Microsoft's umbrella: horrible development cycles, hated initial releases with years worth of fixing to do, but once the game is out Microsoft stops fucking with the game's dedicated teams and the games actually tend to improve significantly. This is because Microsoft has this really screwed up policy around how many long term employees their studios can have, and thus the development team are full of short term people that come in, do what they can, leave, and someone else gets hired and loses a huge amount of their time before being the next to go just figuring out what the short term before them was doing. Once the needs of the big starting development cycle is over, and a smaller more dedicated team of long term people can handle the vast majority of needs, that policy stops getting in the way and the games get better.

            I saw one video that made a strong case that Microsoft has basically tossed ~$50M worth of potential profits in a fire because of this policy killing the hype around initial releases, and forcing games to spend years getting to a point where people like them enough to start being profitable. Unlike CCP or even Paradox, it's entirely possible that Microsoft is such a big company that it doesn't actually give a shit because it looks like such a small blip in their quarterly reports, but from a perspective of the fans of those games that didn't have a say that Microsoft bought out the company that makes them, it's a gigantic pain in the ass to have to weight 4-7 years before the games actually are actually good and slowly start to bring in new players to be worth playing (since MMOs need people to be fun).
