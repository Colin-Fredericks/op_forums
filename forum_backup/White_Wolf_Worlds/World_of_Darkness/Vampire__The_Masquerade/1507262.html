    Have you ever had your "villain" win scenario?

    04-03-2023, 12:29 AM
    I'm asking honestly here, I know most people write that the PCs should always come on top, but when they do stupid things like handing crucial information/artifacts to the villain "just for kicks", I think there should be consequences, and I'm trying to experience in a "Villain win scenario, you helped him, now deal with it", but I wanted to know how many of you have actually the experience of this kind of situation. I know many storytellers are afraid of trying this (especially inexperienced ones) because it would likely make the players unhappy, but I'm of the opinion that consistency comes first, so if you helped the "villain" get what he wanted, now you have to deal with the consequences.

    Please share your thoughts.

                  It's perfectly fine for the antagonist NPCs to "win" if the PCs screw up. In my experience, when the PCs know they screwed up, they are disappointed if there are not consequences. As long as the NPC winning is "fair".

                  The challenge for the ST is running with this and creating the next challenge that allows the PCs to respond in a way that could give them a win. That does not mean they reverse their defeat as if it did not happen. But that they have a chance to still challenge the NPC in a way that makes sense given the new status quo.

                              The tricky part of wanting a villain to win is you have to be sure what exactly you want from the win.

                              Do you want the Villain to challenge the PCs, do you want the Villain to punish the PCs, or do you want the villain to just be a banal evil that makes the PCs good by comparison?

                              The BBEG can pull a xanatos gambit, but not everyone is up for playing against that sort of an opponent. A lot of people play games to decompress, rather than amp up the mental exertion required to play against a xanatos gambit, let alone construct their own. Alternatively, not everyone can wrap their mind around that kind of planning, so to them it just comes off as ST said the bad guy wins.

                              A better way would be a hybrid story where the BBEG has bigger plans, but the Coterie only interacts with the street level plans. So from the PCs point of view they might get some blackmail material on one person, steal some stuff from an estate, and vandalize a local nature area. Each of these actions is a separate storyline, and they are always paid for their efforts, it is only later on that it is revealed that the nature area was a shifter holy site which held warding in place, the random estate items were valuable artifacts to be trade to the Tremere, and the black mail allowed the villain to manipulate some mortal elections which in turn game him unparralled political power in the city. This way the BBEG wins, the PCs just don't percieve him as the villian until the end, and even then they might want to continue working for them.

                              As for what happened in one of my games when a PC group "lost".... The coterie was supposed to track a hunter who was using a local gang to soften up cainite targets. They managed to get some intel about a warehouse the gang was using. When they showed up, no one was around. Just a couple dozen empty wooden crates. The only thing inside was packing material that reeked of cosmoline and gun oil. The Players theorized that since the crates had cyriilic markings on them the gang must be moving drugs from eastern Europe. So, they planned to go over to the gangs main hide out and rip off the drug shipment, thinking it would be easy since they would only have a few pistols and maybe a shotgun to fight with.

                              Well, the entirely mortal gang decided that uninvited guests was the perfect opportunity to try out their military grade weapons shipment from the Soviet Union. Between the entire gang wielding AK-47s with tracer ammo and the fact the PC somehow missed all the hints and thought that knocking down the front door was the best way to go, all but two members of the coterie were in torpor or dead by the end of the encounter. The mangled pair who managed to escape crawled back to their communal haven and opened an ice chest which had been delivered to their door with markings from a local blood bank. The hunter had a phosphorus grenade under the blood packs, when they players lifted the blood pack up, the safety bar sprung off and it went off in their faces. Needless to say, the game was done at that point.

                              The villain doesn't need to go full xanatos to be a force of vexation and suffering for the players. Sometimes the players are their own worst enemy, the NPCs just reap the benefits.

                                                          Mr-Two decades of WoD experience, you probably should phrase your quandaries a little differently if you aren't so new to the game. Moving past that:

                                                          Can the villainous NPC make any attempt to reconcile with the players? Reward them generously for their contribution? Publicly commend their efforts in helping him and thus setting in stone their position in the eyes of other vampires? Or god-forbid, move them with reason?

                                                          If not, maybe you should encourage them to cut their losses and run, perhaps come back decades later when they're ready.


                                                          If you don't want Villain to just end your players should conflict be garanteed, there's a few things you can do
                                                          -the villain swoops up the player's backgrounds and alliances​ in the hopes that they won't do anything stupid until they see the error of their ways. Low-key hostage taking is always fun.
                                                          -Before the villain tries to kill them in one fell sweep all he makes an error in calculation or a third party interferes to create a level playing field or allowing them enough time to flee.
                                                          -Non lethal punishment. Maybe the big bad has a soft spot and thinks the players will come round to his way of thinking... after a few rounds of torture. Hell the big bad could even play the roll of the saviour.
