        Originally posted by Draconis View Post

        It shows up in one or two of the Mage books at least, explaining OOC that turning a vampire mortal again requires rolling more successes than Y##H (which is why it can't be done). That's the sort of thing I'm talking about wrt confirmation of the myth.
        There’s a Vampire book that gives you several options to turn a vampire mortal again. It’s called the Red Sign. Some options involve mages but not all of them.

        The point of the “can you roll more successes than God?” Rethorical question is simply that stuff like turning a vampire back to human should never be achieved through mechanical solutions alone. You don’t just solve that kind of problems by rolling dices. You build chronicles to find ways to do so.
        That may range from rediscovering an ancient and powerful ritual that still works in the current age, to track down Moses Maimonides through the planes (and possibly travel back in time as well) and convince him to perform that kind of magic on you...

            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

            Well, Gehenna.

            But speaking as a huge fan of Noddism, I support 100% Caine being the true origin of vampire. Why should it be different than other gamelines?

            Edit:

            What do I think of the Tzimisce

            I admit, my fanboyism of the Tzimisce affects me when discussing them. If I had to choose to get rid of a clan and I had a choice between the Ventrue or the Tzimisce, I would 100% choose to get rid of the Ventrue. The Tzimisce do everything the Ventrue do but better. They are the embodiment of rulership, nobility, and absolute dominion over their holdings. As much as I love the Anarchs, I don't think the Tzimisce could ever join them. Every Tzimisce is a leader and born to rule over those surrounding them. I can't imagine them ever submitting to the egalitarian principles the Anarchs nominally support.

            The Lasombra ain't got shit on them.

            I figure the Tzimisce just let them THINK they were leaders of the Sabbat.

            I think that the idea of eliminating the Venture in favor of the Tzimisce (and maybe the Lasombra) is actually kind of cool. When they came out with V:tR, they did away with the Lasombra and Tzimisce, perhaps with the implication that those concepts should fit into the new Ventrue Clan. Though that just makes everything a bit too generic. It V:tM is to be streamlined, maybe it would be cooler to get rid of the Ventrue in exchange for two leadership Clans, the Tzimisce and the Lasombra. Tzimisce to represent old fashioned leadership with a strict sense of honor, a tie to the land, and so on; while the Lasombra are the ruthless social darwinists who have no honor or adherence to any strict hierarchy, anyone who earns it has a place on top. Having those two archetypes actually lessons the need for a generic leadership Clan.

                                    I disagree that the Ventrue are boring or banal. But they do represent a different style of rule from the Lasombra and the Tzimisces (they really should have kept the -s on the end of that). Of the three, the Tzimisce are the one without a natural inclination towards a social discipline. Being less prone to inspire or awe their subjects, or to simply dominate their minds, they’ve gravitated towards other forms of motivation.

                                    Its actually why I don’t think they should have Dominate as a clan discipline, though I’ve always thought Protean made sense.

                                    Interestingly, what “Old Clan” means has shifted in different editions. In 2e they didn’t have Vicissitude and were involved with the Tal’mahe’ra. In 3e (and presumably 4e) they had Vicissitude because all Tzimisce did, and the Old Clan were just the ones that never joined the Sabbat. I expect 5e will follow that model, minus the unique discipline. There’s a line in Chicago by Night that implies any Camarilla Tzimisce would be considered Old Clan.

                                                        Vicissitude is the most interesting discipline in the game, bar Thaumaturgy if you start looking up the theory. Offering the elderly their youths back, provided they agree to a life of servitude. Giving a rapist a sex change, providing collector's items for exotic aquariums, falsifying marks of infernalism... You can do some wonderfully weird things with Vicissitude. Vicissitude, like dominate, is good, but in the hands of a creative player, it's godly fun. It's also super interesting in that there's a moral argument for it with some thinking it's just an evil-alien-societal menace and another progressive line of thought.

                                                        Vicissitude is a discipline you can build a character around. Aside from sorcerers and a few lasombra that go all-in on the abyss weirdness, most clan specifics are highly specialised abilities that are useful but not core to a character; An Assamite doesn't need Quietus to poison folk, a Ravnos doesn't need chimestry to decieve, A Gangrel doesn't really need potence to survive the woods, it just makes it easier. A vampire surgeon can't do anything to another vampire, and they'd need centuries to be able to do the things they'd be able to do with vicissitude after a decade. Vicissitude lets anyone be an artist, a transhumanist... Like, sure, mundane skills are super helpful and at times necessary, but Vicissitude is like photoshop in a world where doctors can only fingerpaint (What an Ironic analogy)

                                                        Tzmisce are weird doctors that forgo regular equipment for a jar of dirt. That to me is very much more interesting than Ventrue B
