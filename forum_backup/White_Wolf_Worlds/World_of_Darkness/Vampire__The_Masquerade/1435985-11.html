                Which way to play True Faith is one of those “toolbox” elements that can shape a campaign. For my own campaigns, I distinguish between Faith (basically super willpower) and True Faith (divine miracle-working).

                The former requires no particular level of Humanity, but is also limited in effect; it can force back vampires and resist their effects. The latter can also perform healing, blessing objects, exorcisms and wards against supernatural evil, but requires high Humanity and associated Virtues be maintained.

                Basically, those with Faith are the fanatics; those with True Faith are the saints. The former are common in the Society of Leopold, the latter are almost unheard of within its modern ranks (which is why my own campaign has the separate Order of St. Michael as the good-aligned counterpoint to SoL/SI).

                            Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                            Thing is, God in the old testament is a monumental cunt more often than not. Flawed, often downright vile gods are a feature of most big mythologies. Do you really need to be a nice person to have faith in a not-nice god?
                            In terms of VtM lore, and presuming the recounting in The Book of Nod is accurate, I think a lot depends on how much of the cursing was God and how much was Caine suffering a VERY botched Awakening due to Lilith’s little blood ceremony.

                            Because I think there’s very strong evidence from the description that the angelic curses weren’t... the angels were, in fact, manifestations of his own guilt by his Avatar in a botched Seeking (and might have even gone marauder... turning the curses levied in his Seeking into physical manifestations that could be passed to others). Vampirism became his paradigm and this was established so early that it remains a mythic thread to this day. This would also be why he could invent new disciplines and lay curses on his progeny... the first vampire isn’t even really a vampire, but an immortal (possibly Marauder) Mage.

                            If so, then, in accordance with the Wormwood ending in Gehenna, God only gave Caine immortality so he would have as much time as he needed to repent his sin. It was Caine who chose to undergo a dangerous magickal ritual to obtain power that made him a vampire and Caine who chose to inflict it upon others and those so inflicted chose to compound the sins by inflicting it on even more.

                            God can’t take away free will and still be a just God (and in V5 he has not unleashed the Flood 2.0 to wash away the vampires yet), but He could give the power of True Faith to mankind to help them resist the vampires.

                            Something worth remembering too; at least from the Catholic perspective on biblical accuracy; is that while the books of the Bible are inerrant on matters of Faith, the events prior to Babylonian Exile are as much allegory as fact and were written by people who existed in the world and were products of their time and place.

                            Thus, they have to be read in the context of those times and, by the standards of Mesopotamia c. 550 BC (when the majority of the Old Testament was assembled from fragments and oral traditions), the God presented within was pretty much as loving and merciful as people of that society could understand a divine being to be... certainly in contrast to the Babylonian creation myths which cited the origin of humanity as arising from the blood of demons and thus were only worthy of being slaves to the gods (and, by no coincidence, the Babylonian priest-king who was their representative on Earth).

                            How much of the Bible stories you want to be literal and how much you want to be allegory for God’s relationship with mankind in the fictional VtM universe is going to vary from Storyteller to Storyteller. More recent official material seems to lean more allegorical as the question of if Caine was really the first vampire or if the vampires even all share the same common origin is left more open, but I figure that’s as much about leaving wiggle room for individual campaigns than a specific retcon.

                            Regardless, the answer on the source and power of Faith is one that can be left to the individual campaign.
