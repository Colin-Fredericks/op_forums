            Originally posted by Vamps Like Us View Post
            For example, a character who sees a lit cigarette in a nightclub, or a screened-in fireplace in an ally’s home, might grow uneasy, but is unlikely to succumb to the Red Fear.

            You could take this as the target number being low so most likely the vampire would not fail his roll. It does not say he wont, only that is the more unlikely outcome.
            It's an example; the dispositive language in the excerpt is "innocuous source", "directly under the character's control", and "induce Rotschreck". A source of fire being directly under a character's control is self-explanatory, but the definition of innocuous is "harmless or inoffensive"; in other words, fire is only going to induce Rotschreck if it could potentially harm the vampire. That's an entirely different phenomenon than resisting Rotschreck; it is induced regardless, it is up to the vampire to resist it once induced.

            In other words, to even call for a check to resist Rotschreck in the first place, the fire must be in a position or context to cause potential harm to the vampire. A burning torch or candle secure in a sconce is not in a position or context in which it could cause potential harm; it's not moving, it's not flaring, it's just quietly burning shedding light and heat. Vampires will certainly be conscious of it and likely stay away due to risk and psychological aversion, but it will not induce Rotschreck. Only if it is taken and wielded by someone hostile to the vampire, would it induce Rotschreck and force a resistance check.

            Storytellers should always ask themselves "does this fire pose a threat to the character?" before calling for the Rotschreck test. If no, it cannot induce Rotschreck, and therefore calling for the roll is inappropriate. Instead, the storyteller should narrate the scene with a mind towards highlighting discomfort, and mere discomfort does not provoke Frenzy any of type, whether that's hunger, fury, or fear.

            Not to put too fine a point on this, but we're not making your average player or average clan roll Frenzy checks any time a Harpy opens their mouth or upon sight of an enemy or rival, on the first and every subsequent combat round, blood pool/hunger dips below optimal, seeing a sunrise on a television screen, or at the mere sight of a crucifix. Because not every stimulus that might be broadly construed as a possible Frenzy trigger, is guaranteed to trigger Frenzy. Fire is absolutely no different than any other, just by merit of being fire.
