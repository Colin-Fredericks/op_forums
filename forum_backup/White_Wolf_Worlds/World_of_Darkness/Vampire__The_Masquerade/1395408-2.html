                ^This.

                I disagree with the idea that happy people don't become adventurers. A person's capacity for desire - greed, ambition, altruism, glory-seeking, etc. - is unquenchable, even if they otherwise have nothing particularly bad going for them. They want MORE, and one way or another have determined that adventuring is the way to satisfy this ache.

                Obviously, both the circumstances that led to the Embrace and the new vampiric state establish a baseline of tragedy that all Kindred must reckon with, the life leading up to it and the unlife following the "transition period" might be light on suffering, as it were. The character gets involved with the plot - or, as MyWifeIsScary alluded to, MAKES the plot - because they think they have something to gain. Even if that something is intangible (glory, "the greater good", fun), or illusory ("taking my rightful place as monarch of the Martian principalities", said the Malkavian, without a hint of irony or embarrassment).

                If this were D&D, let's say, it would be much the same. The Fighter wants to establish a kingdom by his own hand. The Cleric wants to rid the countryside of infernal influence and establish a temple to their god's majesty. The Wizard wants to built a wizard's tower and stock it with ancient tomes of forgotten lore, rescuing the knowledge from obscurity. The Rogue really, really likes works of art for purely aesthetic (or prestige) reasons, and is willing to steal them.

                All that changes with Kindred PCs is the exact nature of they "projects". The Ventrue sees Kindred society as another ladder to climb. The Tremere has all the time in the world to study magic - an intellectual good unto itself - and that eternity isn't nearly enough to learn all they want to (which is everything). The Gangrel controls a number of cattle farms to feed them, but thinks they could always use a bigger business (and a bigger share of the local beef market). The Malkavian, obsessive compulsive, thinks economic and social inequality is bullshit (because it applies "the rules" unevenly between groups and social classes, and that's unacceptable), and is going to take everything over so they can MAKE it all make sense, despite the system not really being a detriment to them personally.

                Magestuck: The Ascension

                    Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                    I disagree with the idea that happy people don't become adventurers. A person's capacity for desire - greed, ambition, altruism, glory-seeking, etc. - is unquenchable, even if they otherwise have nothing particularly bad going for them. They want MORE, and one way or another have determined that adventuring is the way to satisfy this ache.
                    If a person was happy and content in their life, they by definition wouldn't be desiring more or different things. Humans have a nasty habit of subconsciously wanting all kinds of things while never realizing it consciously. This is part of the reason why they stay in jobs they hate, maintain relationships that are toxic, or keep bad habits. Vampirism tends to remove some of the primary motivators to adventure. The fear of getting old and missing out on life is no longer applicable, as long as you have access to blood and sunlight shelter there is no end date to existence.

                    VtM might weave tragedy into every characters story, it also caters to a psychology pattern that is quite the opposite of the classical "adventurer" mindset. Vampires are supposed to stay in their cities due to real or imagined danger of lupines. So from the beginning they don't go far afield as a base line. Then the fact that they can't move about during daylight makes the characters less likely to travel due to time constraints and means they rarely stray far from their haven, let alone their city. Sure there is the random mobile Brujah biker gang or group of Gangrel wandering about, but the majority of vampires tend to stay put.

                            Yes and no.

                            If you are playing in a game with grossly over inflated vampire population numbers, then yes you have near constant game of thrones power plays and body counts. Alternatively if you are playing with the suggested ratios, then a city the size of San Francisco would have like six kindred total at any given time. Such a small population basically means there is no Camarilla, Sabbat, or any other organized power structure. It would just be a single coterie running the town or a few vampires who have their own niches in society and just quietly co-exist.

                            VtM has always has this strange fluff dichotomy that gives the impression that every vampire is a cursed loner, doomed to suffer through an eternal night in crushing isolation, while some how simultaneously being just one vampire amongst hundreds that are churned out by the elders for their insane plots. I get that VtM capitalizes on teenage angst and turbulent emotions, but after a certain point you have to pick a world state.

                            The real reason is of course creating fertile grounds for storytelling, which means the end of the world is always nigh, the power structure is always unstable, the given elder is always teetering on the edge of insanity, the enemies are always laying siege to the gates, there is always the threat of being replaced or being seen as a threat, etc. This goes back to the original post about there must be tragedy in a characters backstory, either the characters history or their situation has to act as a story catalyst to promote the game play.

                                            It's not just "safe and hidden" it is the entire world population of vampires, which includes those that are awake, in torpor, newly made, elder vampires, and oh yeah.... the entire Kuei-Jin population of the world.

                                            Here is a quote from the Vampire Storytellers Handbook, 3rd Edition WW2304 published back in 2000.

                                            “How many vampires are there, anyway?

                                            From a strictly Cainite point of view, probably around 40,000. The Middle Kingdom has roughly 20,000 Kuei-jin, for a grand total of approximately 60,000 – one for every 100,000 people on the planet.
                                            Of those Kindred, the clans make up the enormous majority of the population, leaving the rare bloodlines, fallen clans and other, less-quantifiable vampires at about 2,000 vampires in total. Cities aren't likely to have more than one or two Harbingers of Skulls or Gargoyles, if they have any at all. In fact, most cities don't have any Kindred other than those of the clans – and that's precisely how the clans like it. It is, after all, better to deal with the devil you know.…”
                                            So there is a stated max population for vampires, and it is lower than most people think due to the Kuei-jin numbers messing with the math. But more importantly once you start allocating the population by clan and bloodline, and then finally adjust for how many vampires are inactive (torpor, trapped in other dimensions, etc.) The number of active vampires in any given city starts plummeting real fast.

                                            I mean depending on how you look at it the torpor/inactive percentage goes from 20% to 50% of the vampire population. This in turn makes having 200 cainite vampires in a city about 1/200th of the global cainite population, with a 50% inactive ratio that becomes a 1/100th portion.

                                            In short when you boost up the numbers in one section of the world, large swaths of the rest of the world technically wouldn't have any vampire population.
