    I work from the principle that 90% of what the clans "know" about the Antediluvians is imprecise or patently false.

    So much so that the idea that one clan = one antediluvian might on itself be an incorrect assumption and little more than self-serving propaganda to separate themselves from the bloodlines. The Antediluvians transcend clan lines, some have no childer or grandchilder, while others have spawned several clans through the ages under a variety of names and guises.

    In fact some bloodlines might be remnants of fallen clans and some of the known clans we know "just" ascended bloodlines.
    "Gehenna/the Culling" is a once in eternity event, anyone older than 2500 years claiming to belong to one of the contemporary clans is probably either mispresenting things or outright lying through their teeth.
    Specially that bastard Mithras. The guy is a goddamn vampire passing himself as a flipping sun god. For millenia, come on people. Mithras lies, Mithras always lies....

        I must agree with the assertions that Cainite lore is mostly uncertain. Just as bloodlines have differences, and 13 Clans were spawned by 3 individuals (or a bit more than that), none of the common knowledge myths are verifiable. And one thing I always ask myself, is what the hell do the pre-Christian Kindred believe? And why for so many of them was convenient to let people get this hebraic myth into the mix of vampire lore (of course, with the rise of Christianity in the Mediterranean, this might've been too big to hold).
        What would've been the other genesis stories? Requiem gives some other that are fun to play with, and I would've liked to Masquerade to at least show here and there some vamp groups with different ideas. I mean, if you don't meet a Methuseleah of 2000 years, what prevents you from thinking that vampires were created from more later events?

        So, I also very much prefer the more parable and biblical (or mythological) approach to Antediluvians. Those stories that show a bit of them, but not much, and leave much to interpretation; almost as if we tackled these stories as "how the Clan of that Ante wished to be seen, and what identity they wished to forge", just like when Nations start forming and they create founding myths and fish-out and polish heroes of their history.
        To me, getting deep into Nodism in-universe (also here in the real world lol) is like a loophole of never ending conspiracy, false testimony, unreliable and biased narrators (at best), and lots of erroneous or fake information. Besides, no vampire will remember or want to remember all that it occurred so many hundred of years earlier, nor they at the time were recording it for this purpose (mostly). And then there is the power to just tell everything you want, who will deny it? Mithras sure does this as it has been said here.

        In the end, I don't think it possible to talk Antediluvians without some heavy tones of mythological and religious. The Ravnos and Setites founding myths are great examples, although they clash too heavily with Nodist myth to just integrate them, and I've always found it weird how Nodism just... kind of.... dodges talking about some of these (less European and Middle-Eastern) Antes much, but still confirms they are true Antediluvians and authentic Clans. It just feels weird. I would imagine the Book of Nod would either reinvent their origins, say those guys faked their own myths, or that it would go the other route; saying they are usurpers, fake Clans.... or it could even go with "Caine (or 2nd gen) saw merit to these outlanders and invited these foreign Clans to their society".

        Strange... When coincidence seems too convenient, I prefer to call it fate.

        -Blood Omen: Legacy of Kain d=
