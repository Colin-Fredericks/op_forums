    I agree with all of this and will add that I've lived in Eastern Europe and toured the region as well. I'm not going to speak for Eastern Europe, except to say that the depictions in most WoD books are bad. Transylvania is a developed industrial and (mostly) agricultural region, and is multi-ethnic. Saying it is backwards, and home to monsters is a bit like saying the most productive and bucolic parts of the American Midwest are home to monsters and backwards.
    Part of this was just unfamiliarity with that part of the world. And when most of the setting material was written in the 1990s, the region was mostly known for its transition from Communism to a functioning market economy. Twenty years after that, those countries are all very different. (Originally as written, the writers tried to do a good job at describing the actual world as such as then back determine how vampires managed to create that. If the game had continued with it, they would have reevaluated how changes in the real world during the nineties would have determined what vampires did. But they didn't. Metaplot became its own thing. And therefore the economic boom of the 1990s didn't mean the Sabbat had somehow lost control and the Camarilla retook major portions lost to them in decades past, but the Sabbat expanding during this time. So as the metaplot diverged from reality, the difference between the two worlds (the real and the fictional WoD) diverged as well.) And part of this is just old gothic tropes of Uberwald which are now well over a hundred years out of date, but it forms the basis of much popular fiction about vampires, werewolves, and such.

    At the same time, the official World of Darkness version of the Midwest is home to monsters and backwards. There's supposed to be less modern and more gothic architecture in Chicago and other cities, things like the Chicago Union Stock Yards which closed in 1971 were still open in the early nineties when Chicago By Night came out and are presumably still there in WoD Chicago. Vampires and ghouls run underground slavery rings throughout the region. The Obertus control an entire village somewhere in Nebraska or Kansas. PENTEX is dumping pollution like it's still 1950 ignoring the vast amount of environmental laws and improvement that have actually happened in the US and other parts of the world. Fomori serial killers rampage everywhere. Sabbat perform Palla Grandes in the middle of Detroit. And so on. In other words, the WoD American Midwest isn't anything like the actual Midwest either.

    Before complaining about racism and prejudice, people need to keep this in mind.

                                        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                        Yes. It was racism, and Communist-related xenophobia. No beating around the bushes. Tzimisce depiction was born out of racism and Eastern Europe got a heavily racist depiction.
                                        I'm sorry, but this is complete nonsense.

                                        Americans did not think of Eastern Europeans as being another race, or held even more mild ethnic prejudices. This wasn't the 1890s or 1920s when immigrants from Eastern Europe were unfamiliar. Many Americans by then were of Eastern European extraction and completely assimilated, and Mark Rein*Hagen himself was a huge fan of Vaclav Havel as can be seen by many of his dedications. By the time the WoD came out, the Cold War was over. Americans were incredibly sympathetic to the people who lived in former Communist countries and wanted to see them succeed. We didn't see people from Poland, Romania, Russia, or elsewhere as bizarre aliens we didn't understand. Americans didn't see Poles, Russians, Romanians, Hungarians, etc. as the enemy during the Cold War. We saw Communism as the enemy. We knew of the rebellions against Communism in 1956 Hungary, 1968 Czechoslovakia, and Solidarity era Poland. We knew about Russian dissidents like Sakharov and Solzhenitsyn. We were incredibly sympathetic to the people of those countries. We distinguished between the people who lived under Communism and the ideology itself. Since there were many examples of the same ethnic groups living under two different systems, this was easy to do (North Korea/South Korea, West Germany/East Germany, China/Taiwan). After the fall of European Communism, we certainly didn't see them as strange outsiders.

                                        Furthermore, there is absolutely nothing about the Tzimisce that was connected to Communism or even the people of Eastern Europe itself. It was either vague Dracula stuff, or strange Necroscope stuff. There was nothing that associated it with stereotypes about Communism or any stereotype of Eastern European ethnicity. Americans were eagerly putting the Cold War behind us because we were greatly relieved that it was over. As a college student at the time, I know we embraced many students at our universities from the former Soviet Bloc at this time, and were very interested in sharing stories and overcoming any lingering divides as a result of the Iron Curtain.

                                        If anything, the clan most heavily associated with the former Eastern Bloc was the Brujah, not the Tzimisce. The Brujah were the revolutionaries which metaphorically tied to the Communists. The Brujah was one of the original clans presented in the corebook and best known to players. We knew that the Soviet Union was supposed to have been ruled by a Brujah Council. And the Brujah Justicar had a very Slavic sounding name, Carlak. The Tzimisce clanbook, in contrast, was many years off. And the Sabbat, while present in Europe, was mainly a North American phenomenon except for a few select cities and regions in Europe. Europe was instead the stronghold of the Camarilla.

                                        You can think of the depiction of the Tzimisce as awful, in poor taste, ignorant, and ridiculous, and that's fine. I am not a big fan of the clan itself primarily because I never liked the Vicissitude Discipline, and too much of the clan's characteristics is based on its signature Discipline. That lends to a stereotype. But you shouldn't be ascribing bad writing to the motivations of racism.

                                                    (Caveat: Haven't read any of 5th ed, unlikely to ever change that.)

                                                    I've always rather enjoyed the Tzimisce. While the sort of David Cronenberg/Clive Barker style body-horror aspects of Vicissitude are probably a bit overplayed, I still find that aspect effective in moderation. Likewise the potential it gives for exploring things like trans-humanism/trans-vampirism, gender fluidity, body-modification subcultures, and some of the stranger corners of pre-modern natural philosophy and more modern biological and anatomical sciences.
                                                    Beyond that, I do also enjoy the "Dracula thing" and all it entails, with Carpathian/Romanian history, the rather distinctive German/Slavic cultural divide that used to exist in that region, medieval warlords, and the like. Also the real world Vlad the Impaler and his curious legacy as historic tyrant, national hero, and arguably one of the most death metal rulers of the early Renaissance period. Equally so the literary and cinematic Dracula, from Stoker's original story with its haunted castle and black magic, up to the over the top Hammer films, and beyond.
                                                    Then there's the angle of mysticism and "witchcraft" (ie Eastern European pagan sorcery and folk magic), which plays well with folk horror tropes. Tied into this is the Orthodox monasteries of the old Byzantine empire and whatever heresies or "Things Man Should Not Know" they are hiding.
                                                    Overall, I've always found a lot of interesting material to work with over the years.
