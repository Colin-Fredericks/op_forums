        I think it's mostly Paradox's fault that the comparison to Bloodlines 1 is being made. It isn't just the name, it's part of what Paradox wants people to think about. When Bloodlines 2 was originally announced they even had brought on people who had worked on BL1 after all. And even with the Chinese Room going into a very different direction, you still have the cosmetic pack called "Santa Monica Memories" where it says

        Vampire: The Masquerade - Bloodlines 2 - Santa Monica Memories lets you relive the nostalgia of the original Bloodlines. To decorate your haven, this cosmetic pack features iconic items, including a Stop Sign, Ankaran Sarcophagus, and Voerman Portrait. Additionally, with Santa Monica Memories, your haven will be adorned with a clan neon sign each time you complete the game as a new clan.
        So it isn't like the marketing doesn't want people to think about Bloodlines 1.

        But at the end of the day, it's not like anybody was forcing Paradox to release a sequel to Bloodlines. Obviously, with BL1 having a cult-following there were quite a lot of people wishing for a sequel. But still, it was a choice by Paradox to deal with the expectations and demands that come with finally announcing that anticipated sequel. Additionally, the way this had turned into this lengthy and troubled odyssey was wholly unrelated to it being called Bloodlines 2.

        It also doesn't help that I've played Hollow Knight: Silksong and Hades 2 this year. These are two great games and they're great examples of sequels done right. Both games took everything from their predecessor and just gave you even more. These are sequels done the right way. Of course, there are a lot of differences between these games. But if we're just sticking with the idea of a sequel: When you're putting a game in front of me called Bloodlines 2, the game better be able to make the case of why it's called that. And having played two games this year that are well-made examples that this can be done leads to the Bloodlines-name become a drawback for this game.

                                                As an aside: Dragon Age: Inquisitor (aka the third game) is fairly well liked in the DA fandom despite being a pretty different game from the rest too (Dragon Age doesn't really have a core identity at this point so that's a big problem); the biggest problem for Inquisitor is that skipping DAII is rough on making sense of things, and II is probably going to remain the worst game in the franchise even after Veilguard failed.

                                                I bring this up because Dragon Age was, as mentioned, mismanaged as a brand. Each game tried someone different and thus the franchise got mechanically incoherent even if Thedas as a setting got interesting as it got more and more explored and flushed out. If you like an Elder Scrolls game, the drastic odds are that you're not there for the specific mechanics that get tweaked, but the larger mechanical coherence of the game-play loop. There is a game play style to the series that's consistent from Morrowind to Skyrim, that Dragon Age never established.

                                                That is, on that side of the reviews, I think the issue at hand. There's a line between tweaking things between installments, and changing them to the point where players feel like it isn't a "true" sequel anymore. Players don't like buying the same exact game time after time... even if they overwhelming shell out for the same game over a radically different game when push comes to shove (see any sports video game franchise). Hitting that balance between some upgrades, some tweaks, some stuff to make the game feel fresh and worth paying again, vs. just being a glorified carbon copy of something you already have is an ever narrowing goal for video game designers working on major franchises to hit. Bethesda was really good at it for awhile (we'll see with recent high level changes in the industry if that stays true), while Bioware has a stronger reputation for being hit or miss on this.

                                                BL2 is a drastic shift of game play style from BL1, on top of things like the value of the game regardless of it's title, that's clearly not doing it favors with the early review crowd.
