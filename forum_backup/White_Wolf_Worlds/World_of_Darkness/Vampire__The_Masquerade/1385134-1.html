        V5

        One area I've experimented with for Elders and Methuselahs in V5 is the idea of the Withering and statlessness. Basically, if you attend the Gehenna War and answer the Beckoning then you are going to keep your 6+ stats or Elder Disciplines. The Call of the Antediluvians is such that answering it promises you to keep your level of power if you survive their judgement and join with the battle against their enemies.

        If you DON'T answer the Beckoning then you are going to slowly start losing your Disciplines over 5 and gradually become as weak as an Ancilla or at most an 8th level Elder of significant power. The only way around this status is regular diablerie either of Thin Bloods or stronger vampires. Just drinking vampire blood can forestall the Beckoning but not the power loss.

        This has had an enormous affect on those Beckoned vampires who have stayed behind because even if its weak enough for them to resist, the loss of their upper tier powers has caused the Camarilla to lose battles they would have otherwise curbstomped the Second Inqusition or Anarchs with.

        Some Elders have ALSO returned from the Gehenna War with few memories of what happened there but full powers. Tyler, for example, has returned from Carthage with no memory of events but far stronger powers. An encounter with her that goes violent will be summarized as, "You lose."

            Still brainstorming but a few things that came to me as I was reading over the thread...

            •Virtues
            -Conscious: as it currently stands mechanically (at least V20 and prior), I'd say that 'Justification' might make a bit more sense to describe its primary function. However on the other hand, I can see the argument that succeeding on conscious is about still feeling bad for doing something wrong, so I suppose it depends on how you handle the virtue, because I've definitely seen it run as essential 'Justification' at times.

            •Morality
            -I think more of a point could be made about how torturous, radically altering, and difficult adapting to a Path of Enlightenment from Humanity should be. For all vampires really, but especially for the Sabbat where priests should be constantly struggling to keep their charges from crumbling into insanity or becoming wights from the strain of their existence.

            -Masquerade; another morality point is something of a philosophical one. Whether consciously or subconsciously, a character's morality is their mental self-image that they construct to keep at bay the reality of monstrosity. That doesn't mean morality is a condemnation though. I think that stories of the desperate emulation of humanity in the face of existential and perhaps inevitable degeneration are beautiful. This is why one of my core philosophical themes for VtM is the idea of 'Becoming the Mask'.

            •Comedy
            -Bloody food; I don't know where this headcanon started but for a chronicle using comedic elements I would definitely consider implementing it. The idea is that vampires can eat food thoroughly mixed with blood. Ridiculous? Yes. But darkly so and sometimes that's the vibe you want.

            •Characters
            -Washington D.C. has steadily seen more and more Romanesque architectural design over the past half century, even more then its famous original monuments. Wonder why...

                        For V20:

                        • I seconded eliminating extra attacks. I'm toying with the idea of a house rule that would make Celerity like Potence and just give auto-successes for a blood point, or extra movement. For Werewolves, they could spend a point of rage for auto successes to Dex and/or Str, like V20 Celerity or Potence.

                        • At least make soaking automatic instead of a roll - there are some quick combat rules in Dark Ages V20 that can be adapted pretty easily.

                        • I stole the V5 hybrid Blood Potency/Generation rules for my V20 game. It seems to be working out ok.

                        • I also grabbed the Predator Styles from V5, but as part of the character concept, not character creation. I think it's a helpful idea to think about how a new vampire might actually be feeding, especially for new players.

                        • I also like the idea of V5 Resonances or something like them as bonuses for drinking supernatural critter blood, or maybe for highly dramatic mortals.

                        For V5, I haven't played but have been thinking of ways to tinker with the system. Here are some ideas:

                        • Limit Messy Criticals to actions fueled by blood, rather than just any action

                        • Armor when not applied to Aggravated damage reduces superficial damage by half it's value; so armor does something for superficial damage

                        • Allow non-human touchstones. Maybe a merit to skip them entirely if a player really doesn't want one

                        • Drinking supernatural blood slakes all hunger - this is kinda implied in the Methuselah's Thirst merit, but not spelled out anywhere that I could find

                        • Allowing multiple powers for Disciplines at levels that Blood Potency gives a re-roll to the rouse checks for.

                        • Consider high/low tiers for the Chronicle Tenants. If taken to the absurd, vampires could be sent into Wassail from things like snitching too much or failing to support true love if certain tenants are used.

                        • Consider something else than automatic stains for Embracing or Blood Bonding. That seems like a port over of the Requiem willpower hits for embracing or creating ghouls, which as I understand are meant to limit mass embraces or ghoul abuse. With the freeform Chronicle Tenants, having these automatic stains doesn't seem to fit well the the idea of open tenants, and may even clash with some tenants, depending. I feel like temporary Blood Potency hits (minus the Bane penalties and such) could work.

                        • This might be too supers with fangs, but I miss vampires with higher than human stats, at least attributes. Maybe give some sort of Blood Potency bonus to attribute rolls?

                        More of V5 wish list than rule changes:

                        • More advanced combat rules would be nice, something like the WoD Armory books from CoD

                        • More powers for Disciplines that do not require rouse checks - I have a personal preference for passive abilities or unlimited use, and I like how in V20 most of the disciplines allow for this style of play

                                                            So I've been thinking about vampiric history some, because my brain likes to think about how the qualities and traits of those earliest vampires established trends and history that paved the way for the vampiric setting we have today in the World of Darkness. So, looking towards the near beginning, most especially in Egypt, Mesopotamia, China, and the Indus-River Valley; here's some things that came to me...

                                                            VAMPIRES OF THE BRONZE AGE

                                                            •Blood Divinity
                                                            -You are the children of gods. Any Vampire of this period is like the legendary Gilgamesh yet even greater. Not only are you the child of a divinity, you yourself are now an eternal deity, like your progenitor even if somewhat weaker. You may hear your sires whisper of the half-remembered tales of One God that haunted The First, He Who Sired Us All; yet those stories are fragments and shadows after millennia of death and deluge.

                                                            The vampires of these nights, who I treat as most likely being a majority range of the 4th-7th generation with the 5th and 6th being predominant, I divide into three categories.

                                                            --High Gods: These are the ancients. Those who rule in the dark cities of the underworld or in the heavenly realms above, far from us. They are unspeably powerful and their whims and wills are what shall shape all of the world that is to come. Their presence defined empires and entire lands. These are the third and fourth generations, the highest progenitor gods that we all must worship, though one worships their own grand-father most highly, as is right and just.

                                                            --Temple Gods: These are the beings that stalk the night and shape the world with their steps. They claim great city-states as their domains and they are housed within the temples of those cities. They are revered as gods upon the earth, sat beside-above-or upon the very throne of the city. Their temples are wetted with the whole cities sacrifices and in turn the temple god guarantees the cities prosperity in accordance to its own will and power. These gods rival and battle the temple gods of other cities with only the common ties of a great progenitor being respected and even then rarely. When these gods are righteous and powerful, their wills create empires and they become one step below the high gods. Yet when they fail their cities fall to ruin and they are dragged from their temples to be trophies or sacrifices to the temple gods of the victors. These are largely the fifth and sixth generations.

                                                            --Shrine Gods: These gods are weaker then those ancient ones that made them yet stronger then any man. They dwell alone within villages afield or as priests and lesser gods of those temple gods that command their home cities. Families and households often cherish their shrine gods, many of whom are revered ancestors. They are given sacrifices to keep them strong and so they might represent their patrons interests to the courts of the gods. These young gods are largely the sixth and seventh generations, as well as the eigth in lands outside the Middle East where the first high gods dwell.

                                                            These times saw humanity rising from the early ages of copper and stone to create some of the first great examples of what latter people's would call civilization and vampires were at the heart of it. Kings were defined by connection to the gods and religion was at the core of all life. So in this great and early time, the vampires could truly live as gods and why shouldn't they? Were they not immortal and filled with the divine blood of the high gods? How could they be anything but divine?

                                                            Yet all things must end and so would this era. Many of these civilizations would fall in the Bronze Age Collapse and other analogous transition periods and so vampires are left to ask why.

                                                            Was it merely the sudden shift of history due to natural factors? Or, as the Noddists posit, was the fiery collapses of these civilizations the punishment of The One for the hubris of vampire's claiming to be gods. Later years would see fewer examples of such activity, yet it was still prominent in the west into late antiquity and arguably still exists to some degree or another in the shadows of the modern nights.

                                                            Yet time has passed on and for better or for worst, the nights of the Blood Divinities have come to their end, leaving only memories and dust in their wake...
