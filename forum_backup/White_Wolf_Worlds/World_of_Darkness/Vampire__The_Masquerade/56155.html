    Need Help Conceptualizing the Followers of Set

    01-23-2014, 11:32 AM
    I've got no idea what to do with these guys!

    They're named after an Egyptian god, but they worship their own weird force of darkness and destruction. This somehow gives them snake powers because of a vague connection to this Egyptian god (despite Set having nothing to do with snakes), and then they have a sort of vague tie to corruption and temptation via drug peddling. It just feels like this grab bag of thematic elements that don't mesh well at all.

    I'm almost tempted to look towards the Baali for covering the same ground in a much cleaner way. You have the religious zeal, you have the temptation and corruption, and you have a Discipline that makes a fair amount more sense.

    Can you help me out here? I don't want to have to ignore one of the core clans!

    Remi. she/her. game designer.

          While I entirely agree that in many respects Baali and Setites have very similar thematics and ideas, and the themes of the Setites can be somewhat confused in many of the books because there was either not enough room for detail or not all of the writers quite understood what they were writing. In my experience I've found the following:

          Baali work much better as an antagnoist in a chronicle, especially with their massive infernalist influences and overtones, their actions and schemes can range from the small term mayhem to the grandoise infernal design. Even the most dispirate of characters will be able to work together to fight off the Baali, they are the similar but slightly 'outside' antagnoist that all the factions can hate, especially in a Dark Ages chronicle. Consider that the primary clans that continue to work against the Baali are Assamites, Salubri and Setites.

          Setites on the other hand I've found work much better when written as potential allies, because they exist outside of the political structures of the western courts they have every reason to offer their services to various factions in exchange for protection. This means that they have to prove themselves exceedingly useful to be kept around despite their strange ways and unseemly reputation. I've found then that Setites work best when used as the competition for clans such as the Nosferatu and Tremere, in part because of their own networks of informants and their own mystical capabilities which are usually outside that understood by western cainites. You can even have the Setites at first demand much smaller boons in recompense, lull the characters in believing that they've resisted their attempts at corruption and manipulation by making them obvious and playing to the sterotypes, all the while the Setites are actually playing a much more subtle game of corruption. They have centuries to wait afterall, and they'd rather have more allies and eventual converts than blow it on something crass. The drugs, prostiution and other inequity is just the very basic stuff that the Setites can be doing, they can also be providing information, criminal services outside the hands of the usual elders of the city, mystical assistance, social assistance, anything you can think of.
          The Setites then become strong allies of the down on their luck or unconnected characters, reguardless of what tier the players exist on they can provide an edge that their enemies do not neccesarily have. All the while they are being led very slowly through the revelations, so slowly that the characters will never notice it until its too late.

              A few things to mention

              Major thing: The FoS all have a connecting Ethos. Namely, the FoS believe the gods and similar beings constrain people and vampires, keeping them as their slaves for worship. The FoS have kind of a faux-Gnostic approach of believing they need to break down the societal web which constrains the true essence of beings; the idea that some god can tell you what you can and can't do. And many see the many rules of society as a similar web of shoulds cast over mankind and Vampirekind. At their best, the Setites wish to liberate others from this slavery and web. It's how the Aeons or gods stay on top and keep humans weak and vampires under control.

              But they can't be mistaken for good guys any more than other Vampires can. They tend to do it by breaking people, bringing them to rock bottom and rebuilding them in their image. The debasement, in theory, is to aggressively force a reexamination of worldview. This also tends to put people in their debt. But many never reach that point. These talents for debasement often let a Setite pursue their goals by controlling other Cainites and trying to break down the Camarilla or Sabbat.

              Setites also tend to control many ancient sites, and have access to troves of ancient lore. They are in many ways guardians of secrets of the past, and in that vein they learned to negotiate with Cainites who want what they have.

              The Baali lack a coherant ethos. Some are demon worshipers, others follow "the hive" while still others seek to facilitate the return of the Children...or prevent it. Some Baali actively strive to keep the Children asleep and prevent the end of the world. Those who are demon worshipers are pretty easy, pop culture gives us a thousand uses for them. The Hive followers are strange, and make for a more truly alien encounter then any Setite or the other sorts of Baali. And those seeking to hasten the destruction of the world via the Children are also truly mad, often trying to unearth one's resting place.

              This means the Baali and Setites want some different things. Infernal Baali want souls for their master. Setites tend to be disinterested in souls. Either the PCs are pawns or the PCs are worthy of being freed from the Aeons, one or the other. Baali on either side of the conflict with the Children or those of the hive tend to be less interested in corrupting PCs, though the hive ones may want to add to the swarm. This is not to say a Baali trying to awaken one of the children or a Baali trying to keep them asleep have no use for the PCs, but it is less driving. While somewhat similar, the two lines have clear differences.

              In modern day especially, too, the FoS vastly outnumber the Baali. The Baali are a Bloodline, and one in decline, so their numbers are a fraction of the FoS...you could probably fit them all in one apartment. The Followers of Set would fill a complex, possibly more. This is slightly less of a spread in a Victorian Age game though there is still a large outnumbering, and in Dark Ages their numbers may be close, with Setites only having slightly more.

              Their magic, again not a direct theme, is pretty different. The Setites practice Akhu, an Egyption sorta Necromantic magic, while the Baali if anything tend to practice Dark Thaumaturgy.

              Does this help?

                  Joined just to post on this

                  You're absolutely right on the Baali, as they are a much more straightforward group. They're evil demon worshippers interested in souls, demonology and generally bad things. As tempters for the sake of corruption, as eviler-than-thou antagonists and as Demon-themed monsters they do much better than Setites. It's true what the Baroness says about their motives being rather heterogeneous, but simply put, they're much more straightforward.

                  The Setites, indeed, are a rather confused bunch in-canon. Clearly, the writers themselves didn't know exactly where to go with them, or had vastly different opinions on it, and as a result the Setites' background, motives and role are much more vague. That's not at all a bad thing, as you can basically do with them whatever you want. They're an open invitation to be creative with a clan As such, they have a few narrative advantages:

                  1) Mystery:Remember that you, or at least the players, don't have to know everything either. The Setites have one thematic going for them vis-à-vis the Baali, and that's their emphasis on knowledge, manipulation of history and secrecy. All the irritating Egyptian clichés might in the end just be an efficient act just to cover what's going on underneath. The only thing you're bound to keep as a certainty is the snake-fixation, as their discipline is based on that.

                  2) AncientKnowledge: Sure, the Baali also dwell in ancient lore, as so many clans do, but the Setites are old. One text mentions them being much older than Caine. I really liked that thought, but little has been done with it afterwards. The Setites and their master are easily linked with ancient astronaut- and chariot of the gods- theories, and even with Cthulhu-type stories if that's your thing. If you want an explanation for Serpentis, read David Icke's mad (but real!) theories on reptilian monsters with strange powers visiting earth for millennia before humanity, from other dimensions, and their offspring posing as humans. This might look far fetched, even for a vampire game, but the longer you think about it, the better it fits. Take for example the location: Setites are canonically 'big' in Egypt, India, and originally in Latin America... three regions abundantly mentioned in alien conspiracy theories, with pyramids and other impressive structures claimed to be constructed with unknown technology and with serpent like creatures appearing as gods.

                  3) The Necessary Evil: Setites are considered Evil in most cases, but they're not so bad that they basically have a killed-on-sight guarantee. You mentioned replacing them with Baali, but a known organization consisting of these straightforward monsters would be much more difficult to explain, as no neighboring kindred organization would allow it to exist at all. In fact, a lot of canon tells about how Kindred groups unite against all odds every single time a Baali organization rises to the surface, and I believe this to be belief worthy. The Baali cannot be reasoned with, and the only proper way to deal with them is utter destruction. They're true fanatics, as you said. The Setites might have motives that are as bad, but at least on the surface they're willing to deal with others. Whatever they're hiding, openly they look more like ultra-orthodox believers in some weird ancient religion than in extremists trying to bring as much evil in the world as possible.

                  The previous posts are very old already, so I don't know if it's appreciated that I still answer this, but this is a theme that one can find very little on, while it's actually a good question, so I took the shot

                  Knuddel!

                                                  DREAD THREAD NECROMANCY STRIKES AGAIN! I made an account just so I could give my two pence on this.

                                                  I love everything about the Setites (down to the snake motif) except for the whole Egyptian shtick. Not only was it poorly implemented, but for a game whose primary mythos is based on Abrahamic religions, it sticks out awkwardly like a sore thumb. On the other hand, I love the Satanic aesthetics of the Baali but I don't care for their "let's end the world" nihilism nor their "let's kill babies to prevent the end of the world." So, what I do is combine them into a single clan, which I name The Serpents of Eden. They keep their snake theming, their gnostic philosophy, their utilizing sin to guide others to enlightenment, the whole "breaking the chains of God," but tie the main mythos to Satan/Lucifer and the Fall of Man. They definitely have splinter groups like (you guessed it) Egyptian Serpents who view Satan through the mask of Set, Nordic Serpents who worship Loki, and then the bastard children (the Baali) who don't want enlightenment but want destruction. In the modern nights, they are also commonly associated with The Fallen, seeing them as infernal divinity in human flesh (which is somewhat true).

                                                  All of that said, I think V5 did a very nice job reconceptualizing the Setites into the Ministry, making it a lot more free-form and less dependent on Egyptian mythos, basically doing what I mentioned above but without forcing ties to any one mythos.
