    Why did gangrels leave the Camarilla ? I mean, really ?

    Hi everyone,

    There's a lot of things I don't understand about the reason(s) why the gangrels leaved the Camarilla. I assume I don't have all the right informations.
    With the few things I know about this, the situations sounds like this :
    Xaviar : "I met a really strange and god-like powerful thing ! It surely was a mathuselah !"
    Camarilla : "Lol. Mathuselahs don't exist."
    Xaviar : "Alright then ! I am sure about this, and you hypocrites made me risk my life on it ! Me and my fellows leave this hypocrital organisation !"

    And that's it. I want to make my players live this separation, and I have many questions about it.


    Why shouldn't the Camarilla believe in mathusela's existence ? I mean, they are myths, but vampires are myths. Why old vampires with god-like powers should be this unthinkable ? Do they lie about it ? If they do, why ?

    I can understand why Xaviar is bored with Camarilla. But why all the clan leaved it ? I really don't get that.

    Thank you for your lights, kindreds !

              On Monday, 26 July 1999, Xaviar addressed a Gangrel Gather in Clinton County, New York to fight a "Toreador" who had fleshcrafting abilities. All the Gangrel fought this neonate named Leopold in Adirondack State Park, but he had the Eye of Hazimel (artifact that allowed him to use Chimerstry 9) and was used mostly as a catalyst for the Eldest's (Tzimisce Antediluvian resting beneath New York) powers of Vicissitude and Koldunic Sorcery. This decimated the Gangrel with very few survivors.

              In truth, Leopold, who himself believed to be the childe of Victoria Ash, was Gary Pennington, a Tremere antitribu, childe of Nickolai (who orchestrated the death of Petrodon), but he was heavily dominated and didn't know that himself.

              Xaviar went to the Camarilla council that was dealing with the Sabbat attack on the US East Coast and wanted to gather everyone in the Camarilla and fight this "antediluvian", but they scoffed at his demands. In anger, he left and took everyone who was follow him. He went from city to city to speak with Gangrel of note to follow him out of the sect. Many agreed, a few notable stayed like Karsh.

                                  Canonically, Xavier thought he met an Antediluvian. He didn't, actually, but this was sufficient impetus for all Gangrel to leave the Camarilla.

                                  This is, imo, kind of unsatisfying, and doesn't make a lot of sense: sure Xavier is a cool guy, but that many Gangrel abandoning their sect because he thought he saw a spooky thing?

                                  So I tend to run it differently. What follows is not canon unless you decide you want it to be at your table, but it's what makes the most sense to me. It's also told from a very definitely Anarch perspective, since when this story came up in game, it was being used as a recruitment tool by an Anarch. But I'm a big fan of unreliable narrators and multiple versions of every story, so here goes:

                                  See, the Camarilla had never really liked the Gangrel. You don't need wolves and savage beasts in your ivory tower. They made great guards and soldiers, especially with Protean's combat potential, but they were always a Low Clan and treated as inferior by the "blue-blooded" Ventrue and "refined" Torries. They stuck around mostly for the protections the Cam offered, because it was better to have a safety net than to be entirely alone.

                                  When Xavier went from city to city calling for his clanmates to join him, the influential bit wasn't "I met an Antediluvian and Hardestadt doesn't believe me"—it was "look, the Camarilla just sent hundreds of Gangrel to their Final Deaths to protect their own interests; if you abandon the Cam and join me, you won't be going off on your own, you'll have your whole clan watching your back, and that's more than the Camarilla will ever offer you". And that was pretty tempting compared to what the Cam was apparently selling ("we might sometimes pretend not to think you're inferior…to your face at least…as long as you do everything we say and lay down your unlife on command to protect us").

                                  But that…really doesn't look great for the Cam. The Gangrel aren't the only Low Clan who's gotten the short end of the protection deal. If the Gangrel left, what stops the Brujah from doing the same? And the Nos? And the Malks? The whole Ivory Tower system is great for the 1% at the top, but can't function without a 99% who are willing to spend their unlives supporting that 1%. So they spun the story differently. Instead of "we mistreated the Gangrel until they finally had enough of it and fought back", it became "this one delusional Gangrel thought he met an Antediluvian—he didn't, btw—and when we asked him to present any shred of evidence to support this, he went off to go be feral in the woods or something instead". That's the version most Camarilla neonates have heard, and the Elders are banking on them never looking at it closely enough to notice that the details don't line up.

                                                      I think, we as players/readers have too much knowledge of "facts" surrounding the game. It was always heavily implied (down to the Gehenna book) that "all the myths are real". That was compounded by the fact that a lot of ancient power players (aka Methuselahs) were still active in the setting, thus theoretically having witnessed events first hand. That was one of the things that Requiem tried to turn into unreliable narration through the "fog of ages".

                                                      But Masquerade never made the attempt to remove knowledge from the game. Quite the opposite, with games lines such a Dark Ages, and chronicles like the Giovanni Chronicles, the myths surrounding the Tremere and Giovanni were verified.

                                                      Even Carthage isn't much of a myth. Except that the presence of Baali puts a different spin on this supposed "utopia".

                                                      It is one thing to use myths and legends as a validation of power (Gangrel, Toreador, Ventrue, Nosferatu), but another thing to be confronted that those validations do actually exist.

                                                      Consider the in-game angle: Lineage back to a mythical antediluvian is a justification for the right to rule. Boasting that one's antediluvian sat at the Gathers also paints a certain picture of the clan. Using unseen monsters to keep your childer in line is also a valid reason to tell a myth (fear the monster that is more monstrous than us), lastly Carthage is big propaganda for the Brujah to rally around. No matter if it is true or not.

                                                      The "failing" of the older Vampire material was to treat this myths as real and true, thereby dispelling their mythological nature and getting the players to accept them as real, because the sourcebooks backed them up.

                                                      Now, we are left today with the conundrum to turn accepted truths of the setting back into the myths they should be. That makes it hard to wrap our heads around certain events in the setting.

                                                      Compared with an average Neonate on the streets, we are Noddist scholars on par with Vykos and Beckett. Just to put things into perspective. We as readers have a library of Noddist lore to draw on that would take them centuries to gather.
