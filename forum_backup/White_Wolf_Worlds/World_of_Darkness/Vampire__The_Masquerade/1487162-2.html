    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    How many murders are you allowed before its too many?

    As a Storyteller, I think vampirism should be thought of as, "Aside from that, Mrs. Lincoln, how was the play?"

    Vampirism should be something that is normally incredibly good and you would be extremely happy right up until you run into things that are life-alteringly horrifying. The Beast and the Hunger are things that are intertwined and increase your chance of being a murderer (accidentally or otherwise) 10,000%.

    I mean, how many times would you accidentally kill someone you feed on or attack them and break their necks during a frenzy before you've decided being a vampire was a bad deal?

    Assuming you are, for the most part, a normally empathic and feeling person.
    Playing devils advocate for a moment, while I see what you are getting at, it feels like it is missing a core truth about existence outside of a contrived society. Namely that death is part of life and that either killing to feed or killing to keep from being eaten is natural. It is a very first world morality centric view to presume that such a natural law doesn't exist anymore or has somehow been left behind, or potentially even more dangerous, the idea that we have evolved beyond it.

    There are three responses to this existential truth:
    1. Denial - Basically hear no evil, see no evil, speak no evil. Label anything that makes one uncomfortable as evil. Ignore the possibility of this reality affecting you and then freak out or break down when life hits you with it.

    2. Obsession - This one truth becomes the only truth. Blood for the Blood God! Milk for the Khorne-flakes, etc. Every desire and bad behavior is validated by concepts like "The strong eat, the weak are meat", "Survival of the Fittest", and the classic "kill before being killed". Rather than being aware, the focus is laughing at the collateral damage.

    3. Acceptance - There will always be violence, strife, winners and losers, the eater and the eaten. The only thing you can do is be ready to fight when required, capable of running when prudent, and always considering what any given course of action or inaction gets you as a result. Can you kill a threat to you or your interests, sure. But at the same time you are aware of the diminishing returns on carving a bloody swath through all who stand in your way.

    VtM tries very hard to force the discussion to only be about Denial or Obsession, but then tries to sweep Acceptance under the rug like an uninvited party guest. This is born out by how the various morality paths are handled and how Golconda is not exactly mechanically functional.

    I mean I have never heard of games where people ran an entire coterie who were all post Golconda. I have heard many variations of "played a tzimisce who skinned a baby with a cheese grater and used its still living body as a fleshlight", but practically no games where the vampires are at peace with what they are. Admittedly the instant you focus on acceptance, that can fall into a "Vampions" style setup very easily. But if the antics of the superheroes in "The Boys" are anything to go by, then Vampires can easily be "heroes" without losing that dark edge.

        Which gets to a core truth about Vampire: The Masquerade really.

        1. Are vampires natural? There's a very good argument that vampires are not in any way natural but the embodiment of unnatural. Or is the denial that tortures you? Those who try to live like lions or bears as predators may be on to something or they may simply be taking a nose dive into the Beast because vampires are a supernatural curse that divorces you from the world as it should be.

        2. Does "natural" matter in the slightest? Shatterpoint by Matthew Stover, oddly, was a pretty good adaptation of Nieztsche to the Jedi of all people. Basically highlighting that "civilization" is about smacking down the natural and that good is a wholly unnatural construct. However, that's what makes it right.

        The best conversations about V:TM that I've had about morality weren't "weepy vampire feels bad about himself" but discussing the fact that you are a being that is probably going to be a plague on the rest of mankind and a negative net-bad no matter what you do. HOWEVER, that doesn't mean you don't want to live or that you're obligated to destroy yourself or other vampires. It just means you learn to live with the fact you're the bad guy and maybe do as little evil as you can along the way.

        I think these are some of the best roleplaying opportunities for gamers.

        Like, "Yes, I'm doing the selfish and wrong thing but that doesn't mean I have to be as evil as possible either."

        And you can have the alternative answers:

        * If you can't be "good" then be a different kind of monsters and seek a Path or Road.

        * Maybe being an animal can still teach you to be better.

        * Maybe you are evil but maybe you can still be a net good by being an enemy to worse people.

                                        I think that's exactly his point, actually, that you're missing.

                                        A Punk approach is easy to apply to a historical game because the players are modern people with modern sensibilities. But the characters are not. A character with a Punk ethos is actually an anachronism. Criticism to the social rules of the time did existed, but it had completely distinct context, connotation, values.

                                        So that's still the same problem: the setting is predicated upon a set of values and view of reality that aren't as intrinsic to the human condition as the setting itself claims to be the case. DAV and Victorian Age are Punk because they claim those modern values to be objective natural reality and, as such, the values under which such characters should live and/or be judged by the players.

                                        To be honest I would go even further, because I think that even giving those values a broad "modern" stamp may be a problem if you end up thinking those values are the modern values. They are a set of values that are modern, a modern take on a particular mindset common in some current populations. But even in modern times it is hard to objectively state the values predicated by the game as a universal phenomena or even as the standard conclusion of Ethics, Philosophy or anything else. Actually they're very crude outside the confines of genre.

                                        More importantly here, for a work of fiction predicated upon this Punk-Gothic mix of genre and tropes, the standard values and mood of the game are perfectly valid, no problem. But under the proposal of analyzing a premise subversion it is utterly foolish to be attached to them except in the sense of maybe saying you wouldn't be personally able to go with this subversion. The whole point is that many players run characters that simply and truthfully enjoy being vampires, and whether those characters are moral or not under any particular set of values, the fact remains that those characters themselves are not predicated upon the setting's values.

                                        No one is evaluating the values and ethos of the game and if they work within the premise. That's not the point. The point is to subvert the very premise, and as such the values and ethos upon which it is predicated.
