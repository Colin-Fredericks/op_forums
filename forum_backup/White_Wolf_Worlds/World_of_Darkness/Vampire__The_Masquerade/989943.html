    Fixing the madness that is Way of Spirit

    10-19-2016, 01:13 PM
    So I'm running a game that has been going since 900A.D. and as a result, I have a Koldun in the group. They are a veteran VTM player and usually quite reasonable about mechanical and ST mandates but in the period where someone else ran the game picked up Way Of Spirit and refuse to discard this monstrous gamebreaker of a power. The only compromise I can get them to accept is more limits to the power, and they need to be well thought out.

    (Onyx Path, I love y'all, but you really dropped the ball with not fixing this infamous mess of a path. Koldunism in general tbh. I will NEVER buy the 'this power is rare' argument for something being hideously OP.)

    My current thinking is that, first, it can only see through obfuscate if the levels match and the Koldun passes an Auspex vs Obfuscate roll. That aside I am planning to limit the dice pool of the powers used at range to that of the WoS level (max 5). What are people's thoughts, and how would you fix this power?

                  First of all, remove the whole "see through obfuscate" thing, or at least require auspex to be used through Way of Spirit to detect obfuscated vampires. Also, the koldun shouldn't be able to use more dots in auspex than he/she has in Way of Spirit.
                  Second, tell the player that it will take time for a koldun to acclimate him/herself to a new area. Depending on how fastpaced your game is i'd say a month/year/decade/century for each dot to become available. (So for example, a koldun has 4 dots, but can only use 2 dots because they have only spent 2 decades in that area).
                  Also, you could nerf the range on it.

                  lastly, it is your prerogative to change or disallow any power in your setting. If the players can't respect that then you've got other problems than an op Discipline.

                              Maybe the question is: in which way is it overpowered in your game?
                              Doesn't use of Way of the Spirit require concentration? I mean "the koldun sees, hears, smells and even tastes everything at once" doesn't really sound like something anyone would be able to handle constantly without some serious concentration.

                              I'd see it kinda like Auspex 1 only "supercharged". You don't normally run around with heightened senses all night, every night because it would be overload and there is so much going on that could distract you or dazzle you in some way (sudden loud noises and whatnot).

                              I think I'd play a bit more on that its supposed to be a supernatural expansion of the Kolduns senses. As a simple analogy: you sure can listen to every conversation going on in a room with 15 other people but if you concentrate on one conversation enough to actually make sense of it, you'll probably miss most of what is said in the other conversations.
                              As translated to the way of the spirit: If you're busy tracking that trespassing Nosferatu in the northwest and playing games with him by throwing other Koldunic powers in his way your awareness of what happens in the southeast will probably be limited.

                              It might be written differently. I once made a Koldun character for a LARP Vampire game and as we had to adapt the disciplin to the live rules that were in effect I did a lot of talking with the gamemaster about Koldunism. Had I learned Way of Spirit (it was a starting character so still quite limited in what ways to learn), he would have required me to concentrate and do nothing else while using it.

                                  Other than the options listed already a couple others come to mind.
                                  • Allow it to pierce Obfuscate as per the sidebar on pg 142 regarding 'Seeing the Unseen' (meaning the Koldun needs a higher WoS rating and must roll Perception + Awareness or equal rating becomes contested roll) or allow Auspex to be used through it like a Koldunic Path for that purpose as mentioned before
                                  • Require a Perception + Appropriate Ability roll to pick out important details, while the Koldun may perceive everything that doesnt mean they can properly filter it
                                  • Require additional blood be spent to activate Koldunic Paths through WoS based on the dots needed to 'see' the target (ex. if the target is roughly a mile away the Koldun would need WoS 4 to perceive them, meaning an additional 4 blood points must be spent to activate another Koldunic power in addition to any other costs)

                                      (Onyx Path, I love y'all, but you really dropped the ball with not fixing this infamous mess of a path. Koldunism in general tbh. I will NEVER buy the 'this power is rare' argument for something being hideously OP.)
                                      I agree that noting rarity in a power does nothing to rein it in, and is counter-intuitive. In universe, if a power is invented that is super-twinky it will be recognized as such and spread quickly. If a writer wants to limit a power, they need to do so inside the power itself.

                                      As for your original dilemma, that is one of the downsides of handing over a game to substitute ST. You surrendered power over game-play for a while, and now you should just accept the results and move forward from there.

                                      If you have spoken to the player and he is firmly loving the Way of the Spirit, then meet him on his own terms. The thing about vampire is that there is always, always a bigger kid on the block. One thing I usually let my players do is more or less choose what abilities are in play. If someone buys firearms, there will be shooting. If someone buys investigation, there will be crime scenes to examine. In this instance, if he wants to play in a world where players make deals with chthonic eldritch abominations to gain twisted, dark powers beyond the ken of mortal man... well, how does that normally turn out in fiction? And what powers are available when there is no throttle on discipline purchases?

                                      Just turn off your own distaste for powergamers and join in the fun.

                                      For example, the Old Clan Tzimisce are notorious for rivalries within their own clan. Have a more powerful npc come knocking, with similarly overpowered kraina. It could even be a friendly npc who only wants to the pc to "help administer" extensive holdings. Any tool gets boring when used for actual work.

                                      Or, send another enemy calling. The possibilities are pretty wide, actually. Garou might not like the pc trucking with wyrm spirits. The Tremere might show up to find a mystic site for their newest chantry.

                                      Oh, and remember that seeing all is not always a good thing. I am not aware of anything in Way of the Spirit that serves as what Hero System mechanics would call a "Flash Defense".

                                      You know the Mytherceria and Ogham powers that create a glyph that attacks anyone who looks upon it? They can draw that anywhere in the pc's range and it might effect him.

                                      Disciplines that work through sensory overload should also convey. Chimerstry used to create a flashbang grenade effect would effect him anywhere in the domain. Sure, he may detect it as fake, but it's still really, really loud.

                                      Think of what you could do with Melpominee.

                                      For that matter, what happens if someone using Way of the Spirit views, with all senses, someone flashing their fangs, growling and using Presence? Does it convey through Way of the Spirit? How about Presence 5?

                                      Pretty much any line of sight (or range of hearing, etc.) power used in his domain just gets funneled to the pc.

                                      Bottom line, never just take away something a player really wants and has paid points for. Talk to them first, and if they want it, let him have it. Then really let him have it.

                                              In the case of someone actually participating in a chronicle, the opinion of your Storyteller/what you can persuade them of is a lot more important than the opinions offered in a 4-year-old necro-topic. But broadly speaking, I think most of the people who have posted in this topic agree that:

                                              a) simply allowing one level of Way of Spirit to automatically pierce any level of Obfuscate is silly-broken,

                                              or

                                              b) the vague wording of the rules for WoS being able to "see through" Obfuscate leaves plenty of room for Storytellers to add requirements or adjustments without explicitly breaking RAW.

                                              Do you need help persuading your Storyteller of a particular version? My personal preference involves two things. First, one needs a level of Way of Spirit equal to or higher than the level of Obfuscate to "see through" it (equal levels likely mean Resisted Rolls of some sort). Second, using Way of Spirit's "see everything" powers can be extremely disorienting and require considerable concentration, lots of rolls to find specific things, pick out particular details, etc. Even if the Koldun has high enough Way of Spirit to see through your Obfuscate, they might still mess up the particular roll required to detect you (Obfuscate might even add to the Difficulty a bit).
