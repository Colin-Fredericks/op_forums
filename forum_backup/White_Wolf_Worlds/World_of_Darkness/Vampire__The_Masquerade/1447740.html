    Lowkey awesome Vampire cities

    05-06-2021, 08:56 PM
    Which, by Vampire canon underdeveloped and/or underappreciated, cities do you feel it would be awesome to set a chronicle in? A non-negotiable criterion must be that it has never had a "By Night" book. Preferably, it shouldn't have played a big part in the metaplot at all (so, Vienna and Venice would be out for example). Bonus points if it has an awesome opera house, because one of my players wants to play a phantom of the opera nosferatu.

    For me, Russia in general immediately comes to mind, due to it's fascinating history and extreme contrast between wealth and decay, beauty and ugliness. Russia is already like the West run through a World of Darkness filter. You almost wouldn't have to exaggerate.

    Las Vegas is an obvious choice as well, for a plethora of reasons, but I've already run a chronicle set there.

    Asia, of course, has a myriad of options, but please refrain from them in this thread, I feel strongly that V5 as currently written can only adequately portray Vampires in a more or less western context, plus I love Kindred of the East but currently don't know what to do with it.

    Please make a case for your suggestions by explaining what makes them awesome, and how vampires would fit into it.

              I feel like it is important to know the city. Not saying it is required, but it gives a better feel for how things...feel I guess. If you have driven the streets you know what traffic is like, how the buildings look, the types of stores and restaurants, how the food is made, how the people feel about things, etc... Even just a nice vacation would be helpful I think. TV shows set in an area can be helpful if they are actually filmed there and are not just a hollywood stereo type. It's why it was so advantageous to be from Chicago's south side, having been to Gen Con all those years in Milwaukee, Etc... the game really felt like it was meant for us.

              Anywhere the government turns a blind eye to violence, something like Portland would be ideal, and there are plenty of choices. I lived by Seattle and local news did a much better job of showing how great Seattle could be for vampires, especially Anarch and Sabbat games, than you might think given how upscale and expensive Seattle can be, if I never lived there I probably wouldn't even think of it.

              I really recommend thinking about somewhere you are familiar with over just random city that has a big population.

                  I've been living in Lithuania for the past year (and a bit before that, I seem to move around a lot) . Baltic state, anexed by the soviets from WWII. I don't live in the capital, though I've visited, and there are still "old towns" in the centre of the cities here. We don't have the high-rise blocks of russia, 12 story buildings are the limit and rather rare, with most going for 9 or 5 (5 stories was popular because you didn't need to build elevators for them) but we're also, statistically speaking, heavily populated by depressed, suicidal alcoholics, and the population is aging because anyone young wants to leave.

                  And as for the rest of your post, I'm reminded of a game I ran set in Atlanta. For most of my life till shortly after that point I've lived in places that are almost completely racially homogenous or at least well integrated. It was difficult to get my head around a setting where there wasn't a default assumption for race, there wasn't a different naming convention for each race, and yet race was important enough that I need to include it in the description whenever a crime occured.

                  A decent vid on soviet city planning

                          ROME: The decadence and glory of a lost empire with many vampirical influence. An important capital in Europe, an state by itself, with the Vatican and the hunters threatening every misstep. I can imagine the tunnels of the Cloaca Máxima plenty of elders, and the mafias of Suburra fighting for the turf. It would be the first catholic Camarilla city, also, distancing the religion with the beastility of the Sabbat.

                          DAMASCUS: The Gehenna war, a cultural crossroad in middle east, an awesome melting pot where the kurds, the sunnies and the christian have been living in peace for centuries, but now are facing a ten years civil war. With elders buried in the sands, the Ashirra, the Camarilla, the Baali and the Sabbat, Damascus in the definitive battlefield of ideologies and sects in the 5th edition.

                          VENECIA: The power base of the Giovanni, a political player for centuries that orchestrated the fall of Constantinople, the origin of the trading and capitalism in the west. Venecia combines the romanticism of the courts and the meetings of the Camarilla with the gothic menace of the things under the water, the premascine. Cloak, daggers and conspiracies in the elysium.

                                  My vote would be Prague. It has an interesting history, fantastic architecture, and a world class opera house (the Estates Theatre). There's a lot you can do to develop it as a setting for Vampire. Another city with an Old World feel and tremendous history would be Budapest with its Hungarian State Opera House. I believe both have been mentioned occasionally in various supplements (like Transylvania By Night), but are essentially undeveloped.

                                  If you want something completely different, there is Sydney, Australia with its iconic opera house.

                                  If you are looking for something in the US, you can use Dallas/Fort Worth. It's a huge metro area in the US that has been neglected in game. Bass Hall in Fort Worth is a newer opera house, but it looks wonderful.

                                              I'm going to second Portland, OR as an excellent city that has been oddly untouched in 30 years of Vampire (or even White Wolf as a whole). It's an interesting city in many respects---a unique city council form of government (with a long history of corruption---at one point there was an IRL scheme by Labor Unions to literally seize control of Oregon's government), zoning laws keeping it dense and compact despite its population and the largest amount of parkland in any city of its size (Forest Park, which is over 8 miles long, is hard by the city). And of course the iconic Mount Hood looms over the skyline like an American Fuji. Memphis, TN is a similarly sized city with an interesting local culture with its famous music scene and iconic skyline including the famous pyramid.
