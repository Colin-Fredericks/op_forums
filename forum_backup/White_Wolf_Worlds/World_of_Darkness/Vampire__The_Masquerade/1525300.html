    Bows in VDA20

    11-10-2023, 05:34 AM
    Hello everyone!

    I was looking over the rules for bows in VDA20 and... is it just me or are they really not that useful for vampires?

    They do 2 lethal damage (4 with longbows, but they seem to mean the British longbow type specifically, rather than the common longbows available since prehistory) with no option to customise the bow to the archer's strength. Most of the damage would therefore be expected to come in game from the archer's extra successes on the Dexterity + Archery roll, with the advantage that occasionally one may shoot the first arrow ambushing, negating dodge, and that dodge maneuvers are difficulty 8 unless one activates Celerity. One may also use multiple actions (with rising difficulties to hit) to shoot one arrow per Archery dot per turn, but usually that means difficulties 7, 8 and 9 (and no attack can be made at difficulty 10 with multiple actions). A mastercraft bow (Tome of Secrets) makes that 6, 7, 8 and 9.

    In short: it just does not seem to put out enough damage to even cause one level of damage on many rolls, factoring dodge and soaking in. Of course, there is a distance advantage, but that's about it. I get the impression that most targets worth shooting wouldn't even be bothered by the arrows. Staking the heart is definitely not an option, with difficulty 9 to hit and a requirement of 3+ damage inflicted.
    Let's assume I have Dexterity 5, Archery 5 and Celerity 5 and am specialised in this roll. 15 dice at diff.9 with specialisation averages 3 successes. Let's assume the victim has been ambushed and does not dodge. I roll 5 dice for damage (2 of the bow + 3 of the successes, and I am using the rule that you roll all successes to hit rather than only those beyond the first) at 40% chances of success: average is 2 successes. The impaling has already failed and the victim only needs 5 dice to soak to suffer no damage at all on average. I I have Dexterity, Archery and Celerity all at 5, it's fair to imagine that a worthy target could have Stamina, Fortitude and Armor at 5, too, but would still be perfectly safe with much less.

    Unless one has Quietus Cruscitus to envenom the arrows or Celerity 7 Flower of Death to dramatically increase successes to hit, archery seems largely useless to vampires, except for circumstances where other things would do a better job anyway.

    Am I missing something?

          Bows vs vampires really comes down to the idea of volley firing a bunch of arrows without metal heads (to count as stakes) and hope that one out of the few dozen shots raining down will be a heart shot. But this requires a large group of archers, enough open terrain to pull this off, and the vampire to stick around once the first volley has landed.

          More realistically if you are doing an ambush scenario a crossbow firing a bolt would be the better option since you can constantly aim and wait for the optimal heart shot, whereas with a bow, especially the heavier ones, holding a drawn bow for any length of time is quite tiring.

          Using fire arrows to remotely light a prepared trap of oil and hay bales to cause the vampire to freak out or become trapped in a building is the safer option rather than trying to use the bow and arrow to directly do damage.

          As for rules for upgrading bows to deal with higher strength values, take a look at the smallest versions of Ballistae which would simulate what a potence invested vampire could manually wield. While a mortal can't see much at night to aim, a Vampire who has Potence and either Auspex/Protean is essentially a very mobile siege weapon. A scorpion ballista would be about right for this sort of thing.

          In one V: DA game, a Tremere had an NPC coterie of Gargoyles under his command. Properly kitted out they could decimate entire fortifications and slaughter small armies. They naturally have potence which means they could use such upgraded bows/crossbows, Flight allowed them to be mobile and attack from multiple vectors, just add some flaming oil flasks to cause some chaos on the ground. Whoever survived the nightly raids eventually would be hunted down and taken out by simply landing on them.

              Yeah, we seem to agree. I didn't mention battles or hunting because they were already obviously not relevant to most vampires most of the time. So I focused on combat or assassination or similar things, where it's still ultimately pointless to spend dots in Archery.

              As for Setites, depending on the edition, the serpent tongue does remove or mitigate darkness penalties. Unfortunately it does not explain why or how. If one likes snakes and animals in general, the reason is clear: snakes smell through their tongue and some also have heat-sensing (though I'm not sure that's tongue-related), and their tongue-empowered smell is so powerful and accurate that they can accurately bite from a distance in the dark. So a detail-loving ST might describe through smell the info the vampire gets in the dark, but including precise location, which is something we as humans are not used to get much through smell. It does, however, also open the door for discussions between players and STs over what the vampire is actually aware of and what not, as the text in VDA20, for example, only says that penalties in the dark do not apply if the tongue is out.
              Part of the darkness issue depends on how the ST describes environments and handles sources of light, too.

              Also, thrown weapons make sense both narratively for vampires with a fighting background - many types of soldier carried some kind of thrown weapon to soften enemy ranks before impact, and you can still throw weapons in a skirmish, let alone assassination attempts - and rules-wise - Strength + X damage is competitive with hand-to-hand weapons and can inflict damage and also be augmented by Potence or blood expenditure. The real issue is bows. They're ok between humans (who may be too poor to afford heavy armor and do not soam lethal damage), but I would have warrior clans and bloodlines quickly teach their childers that archery is not a great skill among Cainites.

                  Snake tongues do not play any part in thermosensing; that's a property isolated to certain snakes that have noticeable pits in their face, including pit vipers and pythons, mostly, and these sense organs hook up to the same main nerves that go into the face and eyes. Colubrids like the so-called Egyptian "asp" (actually a form of true cobra) would not possess this capability.
