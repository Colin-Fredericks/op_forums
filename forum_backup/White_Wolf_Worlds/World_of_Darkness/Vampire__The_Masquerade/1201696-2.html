        Here's one I thought up for grassland-dwelling Koldun, with the North American prairie in mind but this could be exported to any similar ecoregion.

        Key attribute: Perception

        1 - The prairie moves and sways with the wind, whispering and singing as the air moves through the grass. Upon activating this power, the Koldun enters a state of communion and unity with the grass; the grass sways and moves around them, making the Koldun harder to see or hear, and carries whispers of those around, making the Koldun more aware of their surroundings. For the duration of the scene, the Koldun gains one die to all Stealth and Perception rolls, including to use later levels of this path.

        2 - Sod is the foundation of the prairie, and with this level of mastery the Koldun learns how to animate the dense, powerful roots within to grasp and strangle their targets. The Koldun nominates one target, and rolls to activate; upon success, so long as the declared target remains in the Koldun's sight, roots spring forth to clinch the target. The roots' Strength is equal to the Koldun's path rating, they use the Koldun's Brawl rating, and they remain animate for one turn per success on the activation roll. Mortals slain by this power are automatically drug beneath the soil to be consumed by the plants.

        3 - Wind blows strong on the prairie, and at this level the Koldun learns how to manipulate it as well. Upon activation, dry and dusty winds surround the Koldun, lasting for one scene. Dust blurs the Koldun's silhouette and winds buffet projectiles, increasing the difficulty of ranged attack rolls against the Koldun by two. In close combat, the dust irritates eyes and lungs, increasing the difficulty of all attack and defense rolls by two. Just as the case with Pietrosu's Hospitality, the Koldun is immune to the effect of dust and wind while this power is in effect.

        4 - Prairie fires as a vital part of the ecosystem, revitalizing the land with nutrients, and allowing new plants to grow and be fed upon. At this level of mastery, the Koldun gains control over fire. Stories exist of unwary Tremere who would attempt use of Lure of the Flames against prairie Koldun, to meet Final Death as control of their own flames was wrenched from their minds. For each success on the activation roll, the Koldun can increase the duration of their control (one turn per success) or the intensity of the flames (+1 difficulty to soak and resist Rotshreck for each success) of fires that already exist; the Koldun cannot create fire by using this power alone. The Koldun must spend their turn concentrating to control the flames; for every turn spent controlling them, the fires spread or recede 10 yards/meters in the direction of their choice. Smoke created by controlled fires obscures vision and confounds ranged attack rolls as normal.

        5 - The pinnacle of this path, the Koldun gains mastery of the herbivorous animals that graze on the plains -- and even a way to circumvent Final Death itself. The Koldun feeds an herbivorous animal no smaller than a cow a point of their blood, and activates this power. If successful, the Koldun invests a dot of Willpower into that ruminant; from that point forward, the Koldun may always know where this animal is and its health, and by concentrating perceive through it. The Koldun may use powers through that animal as if they were possessing it. So long as the animal lives this power remains in effect, but the Koldun may prematurely end the power (and retrieve their Willpower dot) by ritually slaughtering it; if the animal dies for any other reason, the Koldun's Willpower dot is permanently lost.

        If the Koldun should happen to meet Final Death while this power is in effect, the enchanted animal becomes pregnant (even if male). If the animal survives its pregnancy, the Koldun is reborn in their true form, clawing their way out of and feeding from the animal in a frenzy, killing them in a horrific shower of gore which costs the Koldun their permanent Willpower dot.

            Kraina of the Succulent Karoo

            A relatively small region on the West coast of South Africa, Great Namaqualand is situated North and South of the Orange River, with the mouth emptying out into the sea. Its close proximity to Great Karoo means its biome is identified heavily with that area. Namaqualand and adjoining coastal areas are called the "Succulent Karoo", due to the great number of succulent plants in residence. This is due to the semi-arid climate, which renders major forms of agriculture a non-option (though Ostrich farming is quite popular). Succulent plants and flowers seem like the only thing that can grow, the latter of which makes Namaqualand a popular tourist spot.

            Kolduns, however, have unlived in the area long enough to know the darker sides of this region. Diamond mines are huge in Namaqualand; a great deal of conflict was fought on these lands over what are colloqueally called "blood diamonds". Yet more blood soaked into the land from 1904 to 1907, when the German Empire cut its tooth on mass slaughter with the Herero and Namaqua Genocide. An attempt at racial extermination that killed roughly 80% of the native population; what remains of the original culture is preserved by surviving Nama and Khoisan tribespeople.

            Resident Kolduns maintain that the killing was encouraged by German Ventrue, who sought to claim the land - and diamonds - for themselves. That local Naglopers and other African Kindred were targeted during all the conflict was an obvious fringe benefit for them. If the Ventrue wanted diamonds, of course, the Naglopers could give them gladly. Namaqualand Koldun had been in communion with the spirits of buried diamonds for centuries, and had long ago incorporated the hard gem's power into their own sorcery. The lands of the Succulent Karoo are a place where life mixed with death, and the Koldun in residence perfected that dichotomy.


            * Bloom The Daisies Of Spring: The rich floral make-up of Namaqualand makes for a major tourist attraction. The coming of spring induces the blooming of vast fields of mesmerizing flowers, mostly daisies. These captivating extensions of life - beautiful, but impermanent - make for good tools in magic.

            This power allows the Koldun to cause a small patch of flowers to erupt from the ground around her feet. Their vibrant colors and rich aroma entrance targets, keeping them drowsy and captivated. While users of Animalism don't need this power to pacify individuals, it can work wonders on whole groups of people. Technically, the power can be made to work with existing flowers as well; the Koldun can make flowers native to Namaqualand to exhibit these effects, even if wrapped in a bundle (so long as the flowers aren't wilted).


            ** Succulent Reserve: Agriculture really was what allowed Kindred society to flourish, by allowing mortals to have a more stable food source. In areas - like Namaqualand - where agriculture is unreliable, the ability to store blood for long periods of time is paramount. Thankfully, Namaqualand is replete with plants adapted to a hot, dry environment, namely by storing quantities of moisture in their succulent flesh. Koldun had many centuries to study these plants, learn their secrets, and even make them work for the Koldun.

            This power allows the Koldun to feed points of her own blood to a succulent plant - like aloe, cacti, ice plants/carpet weeds, and Stonecrop - and have them store the Vitae long term. The green flesh takes on a scarlet hue, and this fleshy plant matter can be consumed or pierced by vampires or ghouls for the vitae (be wary of Blood Bond, though). Technically, this turns the plants into ghouls themselves, but their succulent nature makes them absorb the blood points very slowly. Such a plant only burns through a single blood point a year, making them hardy containers for storing Vitae long-term. Namaqualand is often dotted with patches of hidden red plants, which act as the cache for one Koldun or another.


            *** Diamond Fist: Known as one of the hardest materials in the world, whole sections of Africa are dominated by diamond mining companies. Namaqualand plays host to more than a few company-owned mining towns. What the executives of these companies are becoming more readily aware of is that Kolduns of the Succulent Karoo have been inviting the spirits of diamonds into their bodies for far longer than these modern conglomerates have been mining them.

            The Koldun coats small portions of their flesh with diamonds. This is usually enough for the knuckles of their fists or the claws on their fingers, but many Koldun combine this power with Vicissitude to create more exotic natural weapons. This increases the damage done by +1, and makes the damage type Aggravated. Hardened by such a dense mineral, their natural weapons can pierce or cut through all lesser materials, even stone or metal. There's a reason certain industrial processes use diamond drills and the like.


            **** Succulent Forest Of Thorns: Every cactus has its thorn. Life in the arid semi-deserts of Namaqualand is harsh. Plants must not only adapt to hold water, but develop weapons to protect it from thirsty animals. In a further development of their ability to make plants bloom, the Koldun turns the battlefield into a thorny nightmare.

            The Koldun summons a forest of large cactus plants to fill the immediate area. Not only do they cause damage when they rush to life all around enemies, but they are thick and sturdy enough to inhibit movement. The character must make Strength checks to move around, and they take additional health points of damage all the while. It's death by a thousand cuts with this power. Naturally, the Koldun herself is immune to both the thorns and the difficult terrain.


            ***** The Orange River's Blessing: Also known more simply as "the Diamond Body", this power extends the benefits of Diamond Fist. Instead of coating the character in diamonds, though, it instead temporarily changes the Koldun's entire body into solid gem. There is, however, a requirement: in addition to paying 3 blood points, the character must fully immerse themselves in water, after which they leave the water as a diamond. As the river Orange washes diamonds out to sea, so too must the benefits of its blessing come from water.

            This power gives the Koldun 3 points of armor, and further halves the damage taken from all sources. This includes sunlight. However, while the character is being struck by sunlight, they may opt to spend a Willpower point and make a Dexterity + Science roll, to collect the light and bend it like a prism into a concentrated laser. This laser is considered Direct Sunlight for the purposes of soak, but is concentrated enough that even mortals will take Lethal damage from it.

            There is one final caveat to the "Diamond Body". Like all diamonds, they are NOT truly unbreakable. Diamonds are actually brittle, their molecular structure making them hard in certain directions, but prone to dramatic fracture in others. If the Koldun is struck in a certain spot (their heart), they will take full damage, which may disrupt the form entirely.

                For my principal Vampire character I jury-rigged two krainas to use should the Storyteller prefer their individualized approach, though I'd be fine with just using the equivalent Koldunic Sorcery paths from the corebook if they didn't want to deal with the disparate systems.

                For his primary kraina, I started with the Way of Earth as a base, eliminated a redundancy, and branched off some of the powers to fit his less mountainous Moldavian homeland.

                Moldavian Kraina (Attribute: Stamina)

                • Grasping Soil (as per Way of Earth •)
                •• Trovant Living Stone: A successful activation roll grants the koldun two dots of armor with no mobility penalty for the rest of the scene. He may also use the Block maneuver against meleeweapon attacks unarmed.
                ••• Muma Pădurii's Clutching Hands (as per Dance of the Vines, The Green Path •••)
                •••• Root of Vitality (as per Way of Earth ••••)
                ••••• Crumbling of the World Pillars: The koldun spends a Willpower point (in addition to the usual cost and activation roll), collapsing the ground in an area within line of sight into a sinkhole.Everything in the affected area falls, taking 10 dice of lethal damage (unless able to clear the edge of the sinkhole with a Jumping roll). Additional damage may occur due to falling debris. The area and depth are determined by the number of successes rolled.
                Successes Area/Depth
                1 success 15 yard/meter radius, 30 yard/meter depth
                2 successes 30 yard/meter radius, 60 yard/meter depth
                3 successes 60 yard/meter radius, 120 yard/meter depth
                4 successes 120 yard/meter radius, 250 yard/meter depth
                5 successes 250 yard/meter radius, 500 yard/meter depth
                His secondary kraina is based on the Way of Fire to reflect the seismically active nature of this region in southern Moldavia. (It has various methane-spewing mud volcanoes, Romania's only volcanic crater lake, eternal flames erupting from the ground, etc.)

                Vrancea Kraina (Attribute: Charisma)

                • Fiery Courage (as per Way of Fire•)
                •• Combust (as per Way of Fire••)
                ••• Timsos' Fetid Breath: A successful activation roll fills an enclosed space (up to the size of a small house) with poisonous vapors for two minutes per success. Living beings immediately drop to the Injured health level and suffer an additional level of bashing damage for each turn of energetic action they attempt unless they succeed on a Stamina + Athletics roll (difficulty 6). Once a mortal loses consciousness from this effect, she can survive as many minutes as her Stamina rating before suffocating to death.
                •••• Living Fires of Lopătari: A successful activation roll disperses the koldun's form into a cloud of blazing natural gas up to three meters in radius and a meter in height, for a duration of two turns per success. Anyone caught in the flames suffers two levels of aggravated damage per turn (soak difficulty 7). The koldun is immune to physical force and fire damage for the duration, though sunlight and magic still affect him as usual.

                I haven't settled on the capstone power yet. Volcanic Blast from the Way of Fire might be appropriate since a possibly still-active volcano, Ciomadul, is nearby. Or I might expand on the fourth dot to have some sort of wider natural gas explosion that ignites flammable structures and sparks forest fires.

                        Kraina Of The Home Soil

                        ...
                        I think rank 2 shouldn't refill the Koldun's blood pool. Sort of begs the question of why even bother going out to feed? I think a more workable solution would be to have the rest give them a number of "phantom" blood points the Koldun can use for activating Disciplines, raising Attributes, and healing damage, but which doesn't "actually exist", therefore making them unavailable for sustaining ghouls or performing certain sorcery rites that require shedding blood. Nor can they be used to wake up in the evening, meaning a certain amount of actual feeding is needed, though higher level powers can provide this through the soil, obviously. Moreover, the "phantom blood points" only last for the rest of the night, and requires this power to be re-used the following evening.

                        Other than that, though, I like this Kraina a lot.

                        An idea I had a while ago - not for a VtM vampire but one for DnD - is an old vampire lord who, after centuries of ruling his nation, retired to the land itself. And because the people were already accustomed to coming together and paying their lord's blood tax, they continue to do it centuries later. Every so often - probably during the Winter Solstice, when the night is longest - the common folk would get together and give a little of their blood to a bowl, which would then be poured into a hole dug into the ground. Them giving the old king his due. Regardless of what the political or religious situation was in the country at the time, commoners would know not to tempt him, when a brief bit of superstition each year would keep the Old King sated and pacified. (My idea was that this vampire would then be an Undying patron for Warlocks, who would adopt many vampiric practices to appease their master. One mission they would go on would be to travel to one of these towns and make sure the reigning government or church structure didn't stop the people from making their yearly bloodletting. Lest the Old King grow discontent and send wolves and dark spirits to remind the people who, at the end of the day, is the actual authority in the land.)

                        So, yeah, I like this Kraina, because it makes that kind of thing more plausible in a VtM game. The oldest Methuselahs lay underground, with the most conservative local mortals continuing to pay the blood tax. That way, their undead master doesn't rise up and make their discontent known.

                            Kraina Of The Home Soil (Revised)

                            Key Attribute: Stamina


                            • Fat Of The Land

                            The Koldun is as much ruling lord as some sort of dependent slave to their Home Soil. The beginnings of this path enforce that idea by allowing the Koldun to draw upon the spiritual wealth of her land, taking strength and vitality from that which is hers.

                            System: The Koldun, after sleeping buried (via Protean or even a simple shovel) in the raw soil of her homeland/source of their Home Soil for a full day, gains a number of Blood Points equal to the successes on the casting roll, though points gained may not exceed the character's Generational maximum. These points are usable only for the following night, before evaporating from the Koldun's system, the borrowed vitality returning to the earth.


                            •• Homeland's Help

                            Building on her connection to her Home Soil, a Koldun may now find succor and peace in the earth of their homeland. The comforts of mortality may have been lost, leaving a raging Beast in their place, but the Koldun may find a balm to her spirit in using this power. Some say the earth is a mother, and Koldun who employ this abillty may well agree.

                            System: The Koldun, if interred in her native soil, can refill her Temporary Willpower after an hour of silent meditation and rest. Ironically, this power costs a point of Willpower, making this a strategically useful power rather than one for dire emergency. The feeling of being cradled by the earth has been found to soothe the Koldun's Beast, as well. For a number of nights equal to successes on the casting roll, all rolls to resist Frenzy are at -2 difficulty for the Koldun, the peace bestowed by the comfort of her homeland calming the ferocity of her spirit. This power can only be used once per lunar month; premature attempts only end in failure.


                            ••• The Master Below

                            The Home Soil of a Tzimisce holds the deepest of spiritual connections to the vampire, and a Koldun using this power can take full advantage of this to bolster her own connection to vitality and the safety of the earth itself. The Koldun is The Land, and from beneath it she rules.

                            System: A buried Koldun using this power can be fed blood by drinking it directly from what is spilled over her place of rest. Spilled blood is mystically funneled directly into the Koldun's system, bypassing any potential obstacles or contaminants in the soil between her and the red offering above. The soil itself becomes an extension of the Koldun, even bending any flora within arm's reach ( a radius of 1-3 meters) to its ends, able to will its vitae into the vegetable matter of these plants in order to make and maintain ghouls and blood bonds through them; often causing subtle but unnatural changes to these plants in the process. In addition to this, the buried Koldun does not suffer from the normal penalties for operating during the day and may use Dominate, Auspex and any mental Disciplines requiring eye contact as though the Koldun's vitae-infused foliage/grave were her eyes.


                            •••• The Black Soil Feasts

                            Developed in the far reaches of the past, when swords of iron or even bronze still spilled blood on the soil, this power allows the Koldun to ensure that spilled blood goes where it belongs: to the master of the Domain, of course.

                            System: Whether through combat, accident, butchery or murder, blood spilled on any earth considered to be part of the Koldun's Domain is transferred to her mystically; due to the elemental nature of this transfer, it negates any risk of poisoning or possible Blood Bonds, in the case of spilled Kindred Blood. The Koldun gains a separate, phantom "Blood Pool" to record any gains (which cap at double the Blood Pool of the Koldun, making low Generation Koldun with this power a terror to behold), and can be stored in this immaterial "Pool" of life energy indefinitely, until needed by the Koldun. These "Blood Points" may only be used to increase Physical Attributes, power Disciplines, heal injuries and fuel Blood Sorcery. The vampire must still feed to replenish her normal Blood Pool (lest she starve or succumb to Hunger Frenzy) and use her standard vitae to make and maintain blood bonds and Ghouls. This power is passive and considered always on when the Koldun is within the bounds of their Domain. The Koldun need not be interred to reap the benefits of this power.


                            ••••• Kupati

                            At the pinnacle of mastery in this Kraina, the dynamic of soil and blood comes full circle, the Koldun becoming able to imbue their Domain with her own vital essence. The Koldun achieves true mastery of this Kraina through personal sacrifice to the land, her spilled blood mingling with the soil to strengthen the Domain.

                            System: The Koldun drains five Blood Points from her own veins onto the bare earth of her Domain, mystically feeding her essence to the land. For a number of weeks equal to successes on the casting roll, the flora and fauna of the Koldun's Domain thrive almost beyond belief. Crops yield more bountiful harvests, fields of flowers bloom more richly than before, and trees grow faster (sometimes almost a meter a week, if some reports are true). Local animal life grows more healthy, sometimes even breeding more successfully. For the duration of this mystical enhancement, the Koldun may employ Animalism at -3 difficulty on any animal naturally residing in the area. The land drinks of the Koldun for this vitality, however, and the blood of a vampire can be a left-handed gift. Certain anomalies have been known to occur on occasion; an increase of thorny plants in the area, paths becoming impassable, overgrown woodlands causing people to become lost, or even local fauna showing an unnatural cunning and becoming more prone to aggression.

                                The Columbia-Lacamas Kraina

                                Attribute: Perception


                                • Steelhead’s Wisdom

                                The Koldun may peer into a body of water and view events that have transpired on, in, or around it from the water’s perspective. Some older practitioners of this art claim that the vampire communes with the spirits of the waters when using this power; younger Kindred scoff at such claims.

                                System: The number of successes rolled determines how far into the past the character can look.

                                Successes Result

                                1 success
                                One week

                                2 successes
                                One month

                                3 successes
                                One year

                                4 successes
                                Ten years

                                5 successes
                                One century

                                Every Success beyond 5 allows the Koldun to see one century farther, which can make this quite the potent tool for a curious sorcerer. The Storyteller may require a Perception + Occult roll for the character to discern very small details in the transmitted images. This power can only be used on natually occcuring fresh water; brackish, salt or tap water simply won't do.

                                •• Lacamas Cage

                                The Koldun can command a sufficiently large quantity of water to animate itself and imprison a subject. This power requires a significant amount of fresh water to be fully effective, although even a few gallons can be used to shape solid bonds of animated water.

                                System: The user spends a blood point and rolls activation as normal. The number of successes scored on the roll is the number of successes the victim must score on a Strength roll (difficulty 8; Potence can add to this roll) to break free. A Koldun can maintain only one Cage per dot of Perception at a time, although the caster is free to invoke multiple uses of this power upon separate victims and may dissolve these liquid prisons at will. If a sufficient quantity of water (at least a bathtub’s worth) is not present, the difficulty of the roll to activate this power is raised by one.

                                ••• Lacamas' Maw

                                Channeling the nature of the deep sinkholes (called potholes by the locals) associated with Lacamas lakes' attendant waterfalls, a Koldun may use this power to drag a victim into a bubbling, aqueous pit in the earth. She need only gesture and the soil beneath her victim opens like the maw of a great leviathan. This power may ensnare any victim who stands upon the earth within 100 feet/30 meters of the Koldun.

                                System: Every success on the activation roll leaves the victim immobile for one turn. However, a victim of this power can attempt to break free using a Strength + Survival roll (difficulty 8), and doing so requires five successes. As the earth continues to shift and bubble while the power remains active, this roll must be made as a single (though repeatable) attempt rather than an extended test. In addition, beings trapped in the swirling, crushing Maw suffer one level of lethal damage each turn. Beings capable of soaking this damage may do so, but at difficulty 7. At the end of the power’s duration, the earth yawns once more to release the victim in a great expulsion of water.

                                •••• The Columbia Unleashed

                                When the rains come in the Pacific Northwest, the Columbia River leaps its banks and whole freeways are swept away, along with any person, living or dead, unwise enough not to seek higher ground. The Koldun wielding this power carries with her the surging might of the Columbia wherever she goes.

                                System: The Koldun releases her blood into running water - a river or stream is most common, though a rivulet or even just runoff from thawing snow is enough — and rolls to activate this power. On a success, the blood bolsters the flow, causing it to swell and surge forward, a flash flood given magical life. Anyone, as well as most anything not secured to the ground, caught in its path will be swept away towards the point of lowest elevation at a rate of a hundred yards a turn, and must soak five dice of bashing damage per turn. Any subject somehow prevented from being carried downstream — wedged against a wall or other immoveable object, for instance — takes twice the normal amount of damage. Additionally, mortals must make a swimming roll (difficulty 8) each turn to avoid swallowing great mouthfuls of water; failure inflicts one additional level of lethal damage which cannot be soaked. These rushing waters last for a number of turns equal to successes rolled to activate the power, or twice that number if the source of the flooding is any Pacific Northwest river.

                                ••••• Tempest of the Evergreen

                                The Pacific Northwest is known for rain and cloudy skies. Its storms can be devastating, and the local Koldun haven't ignored that fact. By invoking their blood and applying the full force of their will, a Koldun may take control of the night sky itself and make it their weapon. Churning gray clouds blot out stars and moon, unleashing spiraling gusts and a driving downpour of freezing rain. Lightning arcs overhead, with each thunderous flash casting jagged shadows across the land.

                                System: After a successful activation roll and the expenditure of a blood point, a Koldun may conjure a terrible storm. It takes six hours for the clouds to gather and thicken overhead, minus one hour for every success rolled. If this results in a duration of less than one hour, the clouds blossom outward from directly overhead like a black canopy, filling the sky in mere minutes. Once the clouds form, the rain begins to fall in torrents and the lightning begins. The storm persists at full strength for one hour per success rolled. It gradually dissipates over the hour following that. During the peak of its fury, the storm may cause flooding and certainly chills any exposed mortal to the bone (1 die of unsoakable bashing damage every five minutes of full exposure). Lightning strikes regularly, far more than a usual storm. Indeed, for a cost of one Willpower point, a Koldun may direct lightning at a foe using her Perception + Occult (difficulty 6). A successful strike inflicts 10 dice of lethal damage (which can be soaked normally). Only one such attack may be made each turn, with a botch directing the full fury of the storm at an unintended target (such as the Koldun herself).

                                    The Cascade Arc Kraina

                                    Attribute: Manipulation



                                    • Exhalation of Louwala-Clough

                                    This power is the foundation upon which the rest of the Kraina is built, growing from gentle entreaties to powerful summonings of the power roiling beneath the ground. With this power, the vampire may summon a smoke-filled rain of ash that can blind and confuse foes. To do so, she chants a short paean to the mountain, known by most as St. Helens, and spills one point of blood upon the earth (or sprays it from her bleeding mouth onto the ground around her, as some Koldun of the region choose to).

                                    System: Within moments of successful casting, the unnatural smoke and ash blankets the earth and falls from above in heavy drifts, filling a radius of ten yards per success. Each minute that passes reduces the power’s radius by one yard, leaving all living beings in the area coughing and choking (giving a -2 to Stamina based rolls except Soak), for the duration or until they vacate the area. Vampires and other unliving beings, lacking the need to breathe, are immune to this specific effect. The combination of ash and smoke reduces Perception based dice pools by 3 for all during the duration, except the Koldun using this power. Beings reduced to zero Perception by this power must use the rules for fighting blind if they should enter combat.

                                    •• Cascadian Vent

                                    The subduction zone of the Cascade Volcanic Arc is known for its abundant reserves of methane, among other things. Using this power, the Koldun may call forth the foul gases underground to hamper and damage her foes.

                                    System: The Koldun expends blood, at least one point’s worth but as much as she wants, and rolls to activate this power. Once per turn, for a number of turns equal to the successes she rolled, to a limit of however much blood was spent, she may cause the gasses beneath the ground to erupt upward, forming a tiny volcanic cone. These gases are noxious in the extreme; anyone caught within a ten-foot radius of the cone is subject to uncontrollable coughing (for mortals, -3 to all Dexterity or Stamina based rolls, except Soak), irritated eyes (-1 for all Perception rolls involving sight) and severe nausea (roll Self-Control once per scene, difficulty 7, to prevent vomiting). Worse yet, the bubbling gas is highly flammable; any nearby flame will ignite the gas in a terrible explosion, inflicting five dice of lethal damage (aggravated to vampires) to all those within range of the cone. If this power is activated within the Cascade Arc, each blood point spent counts for double.

                                    ••• Klickitat's Blazing Cloak

                                    Mount Adams, known alternately as Pahto or Klickitat, is a sleeping giant of a volcano. It stands now, waiting and standing vigil, preparing to unleash its fury one day. With this power, Klickitat may be temporarily coaxed awake and tasked being a sorcerer's personal defender. The Koldun raises her hand and the earth splits, spraying a wall of glowing magma 10 feet/3 meters high. Normally, this wall forms a 10-foot/3-meter radius circle around the vampire, although the sorcerer can raise other shapes with practice and skill.

                                    System: The circle of magma summoned with this power has a lifespan of two turns per success rolled. If the Koldun wishes to release the magma along a shape other than a protective circle, increase the base difficulty by one. Characters cannot approach a wall of molten rock without a Courage roll (difficulty 8), and even then, the close blistering heat inflicts a level of aggravated damage. Actual contact with the lava increases the damage to three levels and raises the soak difficulty to 9, assuming any sort of soak is possible. The Koldun takes no damage from her proximity to the summoned magma (although contact with it still damages her as normal). When this power is activated within fifty miles of the Cascade Volcanic Arc, the Koldun treats all difficulties to summon and control the magma as 2 lower than normal.

                                    •••• Rumbling of Cascadia
                                    The Cascadia Subduction Zone, known for its natural gas reserves, is also known for its violent tectonic activity. Though seldom reported in modern times, the Subduction Zone has the potential for some of the most devastating forms of earthquake. This power takes that potential and puts it in the hand of the Koldun. Calling forth the wrath of the igneous earth itself, the Koldun may smite her foes with the full destructive power of an earthquake.

                                    System: This power requires a Willpower point in addition to the usual cost and activation roll. The Koldun expresses her ire, either vocally or by physical gesture, and her anger flows through the soil to any target in her line of sight. The quake erupts outward from that point, inflicting 10 dice of lethal damage on everything and everyone in the area of effect. Most wooden structures collapse entirely and even buildings of concrete and steel may grow cracked and pitted with superficial damage from the shaking earth. This tremor lasts one turn per success and may affect areas whose size is determined by the number of successes rolled, though the Koldun may choose not to exert the full power of the total successes rolled. After all, what Koldun would lay waste to a place that they can later conquer for themselves?

                                    ••••• Cascadian Flow
                                    At the final mastery of the Cascadian Arc Kraina, a Koldun commands magma, burning ash and volatile gases to explode from the ground in a huge pyroclastic surge, like a geyser. The molten rock and ash spew across a wide swath before crashing to earth and flowing in all directions. Anything in the path of the surging, burning earth scorches, melts, bursts into flames or vaporizes within moments. The caster may direct the Flow to erupt anywhere in her line of sight.

                                    System: This power costs one Willpower point in addition to a blood point. For every success on the activation roll, the initial pyroclastic flow lasts one turn. The rivers of burning liquid rock and flowing ash then spread themselves over the area sluggishly for twice this duration before suddenly cooling and hardening, the ash settling to coat anything unfortunate enough to have been caught in its fiery path.. Even if an object survives the heat, it now lies trapped beneath quickly cooling rock and acidic ash. Water coming into contact with the ash becomes undrinkable and slightly caustic (two levels of Bashing damage per pint/litre consumed, diff. 7 to Soak if drunk. If gotten in the eyes, mortals spend 10 - Stamina turns blinded). Anything that makes physical contact with the Flow (including the Koldun) suffers a minimum of three levels of aggravated damage. For objects that don’t have health levels, the Storyteller must decide how many turns they last before melting or erupting into their own inferno. A botch on this power calls forth the pyroclastic surge under an unintended target, possibly the Koldun herself. This power costs no blood to activate while within sight of the peaks of the Cascade Volcanic Arc; any Koldun wielding this power here has surely spilled enough blood to appease the spirits of the fiery mountains.
