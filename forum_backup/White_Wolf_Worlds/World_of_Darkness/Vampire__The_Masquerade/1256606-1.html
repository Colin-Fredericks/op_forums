    Originally posted by Sergeant Brother View Post
    Giving vampires night vision is one of my older house rules. I replace Protean level 1 with a different power.
    That's a neat idea.

    Poaching an idea from Trinity's shapeshifting Aptitude, Biokinesis, an alternate level 1 Protean power could allow the vampire to make minor changes to their body structure to specialize towards one type of activity, say increasing dice pools or effective Dexterity for swimming, climbing or running, so that a Gangrel might seem unnaturally good at getting around in the wild (able to run faster than her foes, or climb seemingly inaccessible cliffs, or swim like a fish).

    Thickening one's hide to give temporary extra soak would seem like a neat idea, for a clan that didn't already have Fortitude.

    Tying it into a pre-existing Vampire mechanic, the ability to spend blood to boost Strength, Dexterity or Stamina, could be another tack, and I'm fond of the idea of mechanics that tie together like that, and synergize. So a vampire with this alternate protean 1 might get an extra point to an Attribute raised, or the boost might last an additional turn (or, for non-combat uses, like boosting Dex and then running a long distance, or boosting Stamina to endure an inhospitable climate, or boosting Strength before building a barn or digging a tunnel, allow the 'boost' to last an entire scene!).

Oddly, one particular thing that climbing using Protean 2 has often reminded me of is how Gargoyles (of Disney fame) scaled solid stone by clawing into it. It's not exactly subtle, but I don't think it's meant to be. Protean, as a Discipline, is rarely subtle.

                                                In V5 you essentially can do this, as a Blood Surge can apply to a dice pool that includes any Attribute which implies that Kindred can enhance any of their core stats with Vitae now - and since all the examples I see for perception based activities seem to use Wits + Awareness/Insight/Investigation/etc I would see it as being viable to Blood Surge to get a bonus to a perception based task. So, it is kind of like this in V5:

                                                Blood Surge: Rouse Check, Bonus Dice based on Blood Potency, does not negate "darkness" issues (though may offset penalty)
                                                Auspex: No Rouse, Bonus Dice based on Auspex, prone to sensory overload, does not negate "darkness" issues (though may offset penalty)
                                                Protean: No Rouse, No Bonus Dice, gain Intimidation Bonus, negates penalties from "darkness" including supernatural

                                                So, really, Blood Surge and Auspex are a compensation for a handicap, while Protean is altering the eyes to remove a handicap entirely. At least that is the V5 approach to it. Previous Editions I think are pretty similar, though enhancing Attributes with Blood was relegated to the Physical category so you would lose that off the options list and only have Disciplines to rely on. Long story short, WW basically did give all Kindred "see in the dark" in V5, it just comes with a risk of Hunger unless you have a dedicated power for it.

                                                    SalamanDer
                                                    Except, as noted above, humans aren't blind in anything but the most absolute darkness. And we have other senses besides sight as it is (we're just very visually oriented and take training to make better use of our other senses actively). If a vampire's blood alone can be channeled to let them perform superhuman feats of strength (and do so without the physical changes necessary to comport with physics), the idea that they can't increase their sight especially (as blood flow and muscle control are really important to sight) doesn't really hold a lot of water. You don't need to shapeshift to see better.

                                                    VtM vampires also have plenty of innate powers that buck they idea that they're just "undead humans." Humans don't have the ability to staunch wounds reflexively, retractable fangs, or wound closing saliva for other examples.

                                                    VtR 2e is, again, a great example here. Vampires in VtR 2e don't get "Darkvision," just reduced penalties from darkness for general sight based perception. Except for a key thematic thing: blood. When it comes to blood (and heartbeats) VtR 2e vampires get huge perception boosts. This also means the classic "vampire can feel the blood in your veins and fresh vampires can get disoriented by all the blood pumping around them" trope actually works. VtR 2e's Auspex and Protean work just fine without specific "see in the dark" powers. Protean (as linked above as well) in particular lets you shapeshift to far more potent sensory improvements than seeing in the dark (such as bat level echolocation, or a snake's infrared vision, etc.) with are useful for more than explaining how vampires hunted for human blood without Protean 1 and before modern cities in VtM.

                                                    Though Blood Surge written more openly does at least partially address the OP even if didn't see it that way.
