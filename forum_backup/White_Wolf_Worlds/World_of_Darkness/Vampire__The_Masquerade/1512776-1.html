                My opinion...

                Vicissitude 6 is the kind of power only an elder can achieve. I find acceptable that it allows to graft living parts to an undead vampire.
                There's to say that vampire - with few exceptions like Samedi or specific flaws - are not rotting corpse. They're "trapped in stasis", but they don't rot and the simple fact that a vampire metabolic functions can be briefly reactivated strongly suggest the vampiric body is not dead tissue.
                According to Beckett's Diary there is even some minimal cellular replication, and with thin bloods being able to occasionally reproduce I think that vampires can be firmly placed on a whole different category from Risen and zombies.

                Living parts grafted to a vampire body would almost certainly be "ghouled", sustained and even regenerated by the power of Vitae. But ghouls are still fundamentally living, so those parts would still be supernaturally living tissue.


                - - -

                I would love to know more about the opposite experiment, the Wolves in Sheep's clothing route from Winter's Teeth. What would happen to a ghoul who replace most of his body with vampiric parts?

                                            In South Slavic folklore, a vampire was believed to pass through several distinct stages in its development. The first 40 days were considered decisive for the making of a vampire; it started out as an invisible shadow and then gradually gained strength from the lifeblood of the living, forming a (typically invisible) jelly-like, boneless mass, and eventually building up a human-like body nearly identical to the one the person had had in life. This development allowed the creature to ultimately leave its grave and begin a new life as a human. The vampire, who was usually male, was also sexually active and could have children, either with his widow or a new wife. These could become vampires themselves, but could also have a special ability to see and kill vampires, allowing them to become vampire hunters.
                                            The bodies of vrykolakas have the same distinctive characteristics as the bodies of vampires in Balkan folklore. They do not decay; instead, they swell and may even attain a "drumlike" form, being very large, have a ruddy complexion, and are, according to one account, "fresh and gorged with new blood".​ in Greek folklore, the vrykolakas are described more as cannibals than bloodsuckers with a taste in particular for human livers​

                                                        No. Graft Life to Life fully incorporates the new part into the recipient's body. If the recipient is alive, it's a living part incorporated into a living body; if the recipient is undead, it becomes an undead part incorporated into an undead body. Hence the comparative ease of attaching limbs to vampiric bodies: there's no pesky "compatibility" concerns.

                                                        Either way, it's a real "going around your ass to get to your elbow" way to do things for a Tzimisce, if for some reason they want to knock around in a mortal body for a while. Especially an elder. It's easier and more efficient to just possess a szlachta or vozhd via Animalism, or any mortal via Dominate (it's a common discipline, popular among elder Tzimisce, and even in-clan for the old farts).
