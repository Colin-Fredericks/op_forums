    Well the thing is that it is NOT one large organized group. It is a umbrella term for a bunch of vampire-hunting organizations and hunters sharing resources and supporting one another. The Kindred don't know this, for the most part, because they don't know anything about the Second Inquisition. That's partially why they haven't been able to crush it and the fact that its successes are being propped up by other Kindred trying to use it.

    OPERATION: ANTIGEN for example is a alliance of the Arcanum, SAS, Knights of Saint George, and London Police Department with the secret support of Mithras' human cultists. It doesn't have any influence outside of the UK but it doesn't NEED to because taking down 50% of the vampires of London is pretty fucking huge numbers-wise in vampiredom by itself.

                        I've never considered SI to be an organization, but a group of people who know about the existence of vampires and are trying to deal with them. These people are strewn willy-nilly across the world in varying positions of power and influence. Some of the are cabinet undersecretaries, some are special forces operators, some are captains in the Army, that sort of thing. They wield enough influence to be able to bring some force to bear from time to time, under the guise of the War on Terror, etc. They try to share information with one another as best they can.

                        Sometimes, when someone gets busted as a spy for a foreign government, it's actually an SI operative caught for passing intel to an SI colleague. Sometimes, those training accidents are SI people plugging a leak. So, it's not a world-spanning shadow government or even an agency. In my interpretation, most SI don't know more than two or three others. Except maybe those who know the most and I'd have them be someone like a major in the Air Force - too easy to make them a politician. Easier to hide as a mid-level officer.

                        But that's just me.

                                                            First, the thing about compromised links is that’s why you try to establish more than one; so you can cross-check intel from multiple sources and if a channel is proved compromised you shut it down. This isn’t even super-secret sources and methods here; this is the sort of stuff that Tom Clancy was able to research from public documents and put into novels.

                                                            Second, a serious question then about the strike on Vienna you call terrorist action; at what point is it not a terrorist action, but government policy?

                                                            President Obama ordered drone strikes on targets all over the world including on US citizens without trial. Outside of the far right, that wasn’t considered terrorist action even though there was plenty of collateral damage in those attacks. So at least by the definitions of the US government and media there IS a level of authority where such actions are generally considered legitimate and not terror strikes.

                                                            But what about when the President of Iran orders an assassination of a foreign target and there’s collateral damage? Terror or legitimate and why or why not?

                                                            How about when the head of the CIA appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate orders a drone strike on a target because of national security concerns? How about when a subordinate designated by the CIA head orders it?

                                                            Because the SI material released so far (mostly in the V5 Camarilla book) doesn’t indicate these are isolated cells; they mention interagency (and international) cooperation and approved black ops budgets in the billions. The Tremere Chantry in Vienna was hit by a joint SOCOM (United States Special Operations Command) task force... meaning approval for the strike came from at least the level of the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff if not the President of the United States.

                                                            Which is why my question before is important... because to label the strike against Chantry in Vienna terrorism, then you also have to label things like the raid that took out Osama bin Laden (where the US entered a foreign country without permission, stormed the compound and killed a woman and one of bin Laden’s kids in the crossfire) as terrorist actions too. Is that a position you’re comfortable with?

                                                            Because let’s be honest here... vampires may be the protagonists of the game, but they are villain protagonists and theTremere leadership in Vienna was soaked with centuries of innocent blood. They absolutely deserved to be wiped out with as much extreme prejudice as delivered to any terrorist cell.

                                                            How many vampires out there would NOT be considered a serial killer by any reasonable legal standard?

                                                            Are the SI really terrorists for (with at least some level of government sanction) targeting the members of a criminal conspiracy organized to protect a whole group of serial killers from the consequences of their crimes?

                                                            I don’t have a good answer for where the line is and what nuances matter in the decisions. Where the line falls for your campaign is something each GM needs to answer for themselves.

                                                            Personally, I intend to run them as heroic antagonists because the majority of vampires in my campaign city are complete monsters.
