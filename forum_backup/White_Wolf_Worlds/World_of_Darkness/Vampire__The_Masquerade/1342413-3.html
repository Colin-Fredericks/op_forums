    Quite a number of other antideluvians could have felled Ventrue. 10 fortitude isn't invincibility in the face of someone with 10 potence (It probably looks like an ordinary fist fight, really) and Ventrue are not famed for their detection/escape abilities. Do note that if we don't use ventrue's hypothetical 10 fort as "plot device" and just use abilities as listed and scale large weapons accordingly, Antideluvians would be seriously threatened even the smaller modern cannons, nevermind the stuff you'd find on tanks (which are in turn dwarfed by the guns people used to put on ships)

    (I hold to the belief that the weak of nightmares was all chimistry)
    Now, while that might not necessarily be correct, it's likely that neonates can experiment with their own levels of potence/fortitude and extrapolate "if 5 fortitude's like this, 10 fortitude aught to be twice as strong, right? That's not too scary"


    RE: The Cam denying Antes.
    The Cam is smart. Adamantely denying that something exists creates suspicions, reasoning that something's harmless creates reassurance. The Cam denies "antideluvians" and doesn't like the term because it has connotations. It's like using a racist slur around anti-racists. People'll think you're dangerous. At best, you're showing anti-establishment tendancies, at worst, you're sharing heresy and treason. In a conspiratorial society like vampires where cults are a threat and everyone needs to support the bottom line or else things'll go to shit, that's unacceptable.

                                    Just to double down.
                                    A lot of vampire's dumber decisions are due to real life, or one's own table bleeding into the setting as a whole. There's this cognitive dissonance between vampires and the people who play them. If we forget about today and just talk the 90's/early 2000's, without going too into it/revealing how much i'm talking out my ass, your average vampire player is young, liberal, middle income, and really digs anti-establismhent stuff. Your average vampire is old, rich, conservative, and really wants stability.

                                    This is why the game started something like 90% of vampires are Camarilla, and most of the few anarchs are just Camarilla with a few different ideas . But of course it played and half or so of the players became anarchs so the writing shifted. Then by revised the cam isn't such a global entity, and even if we just talk about the united states, they've got, like, 65% of the vampire pop (How I got this seemingly arbitrary number: Population of united states, takeaway california, florida and nevada, rounded) and in V5 the Cam is like... well it's basically all gone.

                                    The gangrel and there cool-wolf look attracted the most anti-establishment players. And of course at that point people are asking "Why are the Gangrel in the Cam if they're all edgy narcissistic loners" alongside "why aren't the anarchs more popular if the cam are always presented so dysfunctional?" (because of course the prince must be the antagonist; a stable city would be boring)

                                    But y'know, just because you want something to happen doesn't mean it's a good idea. Anyone who's familiar with game design will know that the player needs fun challenges, or else they'll try to work out how to "beat" the game in the most easy (boring) way possible. It's a trap to give the players everything they want. Making the Anarchs more successful is the trap, and giving the Gangrel to the anarchs is another trap (as well as immensely unsatisfying to those who've played loyal cam gangrel)

                                            I don't think that's what happened. I can count on the fingers of one hand the number of times I've even witnessed an Anarch game. It's Camarilla by a large margin, then Sabbat, with Anarchs dead last, and not even a competitive last place.

                                            In my experience, most STs seem to like the Cammies because the structure gives them a game that is easy to control. A Camarilla game lends itself readily to a World of Warcraft structure, where there are NPCs with exclamation points on their heads and they send you on quests. It's laws also allow STs to reign in the worst impulses of players, since the ST can always declare that the Prince has declared no one is to use explosives even in self-defense, because the attention such use brings is a threat to the masquerade. The control mechanisms of the Camarilla become tools of the ST's control.

                                            I think the reason they decided to focus on Anarchs is they really wanted to double-down on the "personal horror" angle. They wanted something like the TV/movie stories where someone becomes a monster and they mostly deal with things in almost complete isolation. Their concerns are not being caught by their neighbors and the police. They worry mostly about their monstrous urges giving them away, rather than any larger "sect business".

                                            They want to completely move away from the World of Warcraft with dice game model. They want something more like a serious version of the Santa Clarita Diet. Just you and a few others, all of you extremely weak, trying to resist killing people, and scrambling desperately to hide evidence when you do kill.

                                                        See, to me, Gangrel are mostly the types that only nominally join sects, at most joining the sect of "what I wanted to do anyway". There are few gangrel who truly align with the ideals of the Camarilla/Anarchs/Sabbat, but there are also very few who are going to go out of their way to piss off the sects. Individuals? especially ones that try to infringe on the Gangrel's interests? Sure. Gangrel are much like Nos; Comfortable on the periphery, they advance and retreat on their own, not with the sect. A hard stance like "we're out of the Camarilla" doesn't suit their modus operandi at all. To top it off, the Gangrel aren't like the Settites or Giovanni, they're naturally not adept at making arrangements as a neutral party, infact they have many disadvantages. Gangrels don't build up so much, they don't connect so much, and are largely only useful as mercenaries. The Gangrel outside of the Cam are at a massive negotiating disadvantage, because what prince wants his city flooded with difficult to control mercenaries? When they were in the Cam, there was a degree of confidence in both parties. Now that they're out, nobody has reassurance, unless someone gets in with the blood-bond...
