    (V5) The Gehenna

    02-05-2024, 11:47 PM
    You see, Vampire - The Masquerade seems like it's really the TTRPG made for me. This underlying idea that there are things out there in which commonners don't fully comprehend - nor should they - just constantly scratches THAT itch for me. Together with that comes that massive dark cloud in the distance, slowly creeping towards the watcher, as the wind blows stronger and the air gets colder. For the common men and women of the world, that could very well be vampires, werewolves and - God forbid - the Wraiths. Now, for the vampires, that's certainly Gehenna.

    You see, something about this paranoid fever of impending doom that the concept of Gehenna brings just scratches THAT itch for me, but unfortunately, I really don't like the way White Wolf handled it in their past materials - from what I know, of course. You see, I don't like the idea that Tzimisce is basically New York's sewers and Ennoia literally becoming one with planet Earth, but I really love this idea that there is a "World of Darkness" and a "World of Darker Darkness", and that there are things even the Garou should dread to face. With that in mind, the main plot behind my campaign is the lurking paranoia that the signs of Gehenna may cause in the World of Darkness. Lovecraftian much!

    Gladly, I know just how to build that (I'd say it's my specialty making mindfucks out of ideas) and how to engage the players on discovering more and more about this broader scope of things, involving Garous and Wraiths (which have an immense interconnected importance to my story). My problem is in handling Gehenna itself, if it ever comes to happen. One of my favorite things in Vampire - The Masquerade (and even more so in Requiem) is the low-fantasy aspect of all of it, how it's supernatural and mysterious, but still grounded on a palpable reality. I like to think that the fantasy one come to expect from things like D&D, for example, is the wonders and subsequent terror of looking up in the night sky and seeing the infinitude of the vast space; VtM is the wonders and subsequent terror of looking down the abyss and find something slowly creeping back to you from it.

    Without rambling on even more about my lovecraftian ideas, I'd really like Gehenna to happen in my campaign, without actually blowing up the Earth, but giving her a taste of what an actual World War III could cause the planet. How could I "stare down the abyss" but keep it in the low, palpable, fantasy that I've described?

          Originally posted by NickNickeo View Post
          Without rambling on even more about my lovecraftian ideas, I'd really like Gehenna to happen in my campaign, without actually blowing up the Earth, but giving her a taste of what an actual World War III could cause the planet. How could I "stare down the abyss" but keep it in the low, palpable, fantasy that I've described?
          What you may want is some kind of event foretold in vampire "prophecy" that is something that must happen in order for Gehenna to occur. Thus you have the foretelling of prophecy happening, but not needing it to result in the End Times. It's simply a sign that Gehenna will in fact be happening sometime in the future. A necessary stop along the way. Perhaps its something stated in your chronicle's version of the Book of Nod (for ideas you may want to consult the Gehenna cults in the Elysium sourcebook), or in some kind of recognized semi-authority apocrypha like a text addendum to the Book of Nod (which may not be widely accepted by Noddist scholars) or a result of "visionaries" like a Malkavian prophet or other figure.

          I think using some kind of Gehenna cult as an active force in your chronicle might do the trick for you. It allows you to introduce some members of the cult early in your chronicle and be relatively normal, but the cult can later be used to explain strange events to come or recruit the PCs as allies or cat's paws in its intrigues. In terms of the actual event used, that is really up to you. Although I think having some slumbering Methusaleh rise at the end of your chronicle and demonstrating its vast powers before the chronicle ends (possibly, but not necessarily, by the PCs destroying the threat) is one option.

              A low-key Gehenna might just be The Withering, or The Beckoning, or something like that. A creeping dread that things are *wrong* and aren't working as they're supposed to, rather than a big crunch that ends the world.

              You could also go super individual with it, and have Caine visit Kindred one by one to judge them. If they fail, they just disappear. He doesn't do it in any order that makes sense, so it could happen to any vampire at any time.

              If you want to build up to it, have the coterie investigate strange vampiric disappearances around the world. Perhaps each one is connected by a symbol or telltale sign that was left behind.

              Only once the characters start to realise that they might be next do you reveal that it's Caine -- and then the panic sets in!

                                          I think what you need to implement your Gehenna, here, is to answer a simpler question: what you want to change in the setting for the rest of your game?

                                          Making the Gehenna happen without ending the overall street level tone isn't as hard as it seems. You just have to define what scar it will leave in the world as the Kindred now it.

                                          You can draw elements from Requiem, for example. It may be VII, a murderous group killing Kindred without rime or reason, but absolutely unstoppable. It may be the actual Disciplines changing, everyone noticing but completely clueless about how or why.

                                          One book I really recommend for you isn't even WoD, but D&D 3.5: Elder Evils. This is one of the best books I've ever read about making apocalyptic plots, and the main ideas and mechanics are easy to translate to any system.
