    But to make a slightly longer argument:

    1. V:TM and specifically V5 is a horror power fantasy of the kind very common to the Nineties and very similar to the "Dark Age of Comics." You are both simultaneously as monster, sexy, horrifying and alluring. Spawn, the Darkness, Chastity, Lady Death, the Midnight Sons, and Blade are pretty much the Kindred (pun intended) of V:TM. Being a vampire is something meant to be freeing from the social restrictions of morality while simultaneously introducing you into different forms of oppression. Being black, gay, a woman, or other minority means little as a vampire but it also is something that you are still surrounded by and can affect.

    2. They "backed down" on the Chechnya issue for reasons that I think they should be given a pass on. Mark Rein Hagen stated that the people involved were actually threatened and the distributors of Russian White Wolf were put on national television. There was also fear of going after people there. It got a lot of attention and raised money for saving lives but replacing the content was probanly good to protect real lives. It didn't help a lot of Western fans did not understand what the point was trying to make.

    3. "Woke" as a verb means that a person is aware of systematic prejudice and institutionalized disparities so V:TM has always been "woke." In fact, the irony is that Werewolf: The Apocalypse has always been the MOST woke as it is a game that is from top to bottom about corporate greed, indigineous issues, and the cruelties of traditionalist religious power structures. In V:TM, it's always been "The Elders on top, neonates on the bottom, humans as their prey."

    4. Part of what made CBN 1st Edition so great was that having been a player since grade school of Dungeons and Dragons, the game introduced me to a collection of vampires that were a mix of multiple races (black people in books! GASP! Gay people too!) as well as orientations that I previously hadn't seen. Its baked into the core DNA of the game that this is meant to be more representative of a diverse population than games typically were of its era.

    I feel V5 tackles grittier subject matter and remembers this because 2nd and 3rd got too wrapped up in the apocalypse and sect warfare. Vampires vs. human traffickers is just as compelling as vampires vs. Sabbat (though I tend to prefer vampires vs. human traffickers who work for the Sabbat).

    5. Part of what makes Carna entertaining is the assumption that she is correct about the fact that she is an oppressed minority within the Tremere AS WELL as a serial killer. We haven't seen her write-up but part of what makes Carna and Rudi interesting are they may be substantively right but they're also fucking awful people. One of my favorite characters from CBN1st Edition was Dickie Fulcher and while a lot of people focused on his name being the same as a comedian's, I was always fascinated that he's a die-hard supporter of Maldavis and a mass murdering serial killer.

    6. Another area that I think handles the issue well is one we've disagreed with repeatedly and that's how to handle Followers of Set orthodoxy. Yes, Keminitri is only against Set but Set is the cult leader and the Followers of Set are a cult (specifically one cult and dozens of sub-cult that it franchises). Set apparently Embraced multiple beautiful redheaded women, made them nearly omnipotent 4th geenration Demigods, and then BRAINWASHED THE SHIT out of them with his free love murder cult. Their sex, drugs, and rock and roll cult stems from them downward to oppress more women and men in its ranks.

    I don't think you should make the Followers of Set a bunch of incels but I actually think THE CHILLING ADVENTURES OF SABRINA makes a good example of them. Women are encouraged to be sexy, seductresses, and told they're "free" while simultaneously men and the orthodox cult atke advantage of that. Its not the main defining element of the cult but is certainly a way to use it.

    7. Sexism in the Tremere is something that Ailsing Sturbridge dealt with, Carna, Erichtho, and multiple other female Tremere that mentioned it in their backstories. Merlinda may be one of the Seven but apparently she doesn't care. Also, the people the Tremere recruit from for the past 1000 years are generally not the same groups as other Clans that have a more even female-to-male ratio. The sexism of society means that the average Tremere recruited from scholarly and ocuclt backgrounds are going to preclude women until the 20th century. No wonder the Clan has a reputation for sexism.

                                                Anarchs, Thaumaturgy, Blood Sorcery, and the Tremere

                                                Honestly, my opinion is that Thaumaturgy is way overplayed and I don't have an especial fondness for House Tremere. I feel like it was frequently treated as the "one discipline to rule them all" and it was my hope that V5 would work to expand the flexibility of other Disciplines so that people would want to get Protean every bit as much as Blood Sorcery. Unfortunately, the Combo Discipline limitations and lack of Elder DIsciplines severely limits this and I'm disappointed vampire powers remain so limited. I'm glad they've simplified Blood Sorcery and limited Paths to rituals so that it's not quite such a broken OP collection.

                                                Nevertheless, I admit that the Tremere aren't my OTP. I do have MANY players who absolutely love them, though, and are utterly and completely obsessed with playing them. I don't get it, probably because I'm Chaotic Neutral in real life and thus the idea of playing in a rigidly controlled Freemasons-esque secret society doesn't quite appeal to me. My love of Clans can basically go like this:

                                                1. Malkavians
                                                2. Brujah
                                                3. Followers of Set
                                                4. Tzimisce (Dark Ages)
                                                5. Lasombra
                                                6. Duskborn/Caitiff
                                                7. Toreador
                                                8. Ventrue (love to hate em)
                                                9. Tremere
                                                10. Gangrel
                                                11. Nosferatu
                                                12. Giovanni
                                                13. Assamites
                                                14. Ravnos

                                                One of the things that are consistent in my players' love of the Tremere is that they very much like playing up the controlling nature of the Tremere. House Carna liberating members from the Pyramid's heriarchy and the sudden death of the Council of Seven. Whether or not you approve of these events, they're interesting Tremere-based events that are worth exploring in-game because they're related to their concepts. Like, killing Aunt May is a big deal in Spider-Man and terrible for him personally, but it's certainly a storyline.

                                                The "Rebellion Tremere" has always been a concept I've been interested in as it's an environment of near total control of thought, actions, and activity versus the relative freedom of other clans.

                                                I think Carna opens up new roleplaying opportunities and storylines for Tremere, which means it should be supported.
