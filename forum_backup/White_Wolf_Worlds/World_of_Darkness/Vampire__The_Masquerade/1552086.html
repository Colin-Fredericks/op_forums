              We did a "try to cram as many end of the world scenarios together and see what works".

              So, mixed Ascension, Apocalypse and Gehenna. A bit of the Kuei Jin ending and something for Demon and Mummy.

              Long story short, everyone got what they wanted (thanks to mage ascension for humans, and a mix of the werewolf endings), but the process was traumatic (gehenna, mummies, etc).

              Nearly all of us had a character in a major splat (Mage, Vampire, Werewolf), except a few of us that got Major and some minor, or my case that I got only Mage (late joiner to the game).

              We overlapped scenarios, so when the Tzimisce rose in New York, some Werewolves were there to punch it in the mouth(s). When Lasombra blanketed the earth, a cabal of mages summoned enough sunlight to kill it (and no one but us knew what killed it), Mikaboshi (briefly) became the Demon Emperor, Mummies were throwing a spanner in the works; Mummies ended up causing the Wall to strenghten and fucking up the Kuei Jin but saving the world from the 1000 hells as a side effect; etc.

              I think it was the best thing we ever played and was all over the place because we (players) though we were getting the ALL bad endings, but somehow our ST got us through all of it.

                              I've never used any of the Antediluvians in my chronicles and don't plan to do so. My main concern is how terrifying are we supposed to hold Gehenna if they (even only some of them) are up and about being active in recent times? In order to preserve the mystery and fear that should surround them, they must be unknown. This also means that any 4th generation childer who were not sired in very ancient times, either have their generation reduced to 5th generation or are 4th generation only because of diablerie.

                              Unfortunately, even canon creates problems for this as we have at least two Antediluvians being active enough that they sired new childer in the Middle Ages and at least one being active and known even as late as the formation of the Camarilla. So I've had to change things from canon in my own chronicles.

                              This is how I handle them:

                              The Second City was Babel. After Babel fell, the Antediluvians went to their own different cities in Mesopotamia where they became their legendary god-king founders.

                              The Antediluvians have more or less been in torpor since 4000-3000 BC. For the most part, all the stories told about them are before this time. There are still some exceptions, and each time there was activity, something important happened.

                              Lasombra did wake up some time after this to destroy ALL of his original 4th generation childer and embrace Montano sometime before the Santorini explosion. However, he went back into torpor after he successfully identified his only loyal childe, sired him, educated him, and released him (Montano). I haven't yet decided if he instructed Montano to bring Gratiano to him for some kind of cold embrace, or if he instructed Montano to instead embrace Gratiano himself. In either case, while Montano did not want to do this he obeyed his sire.

                              Saulot was briefly active during the time of Classical Rome as he embraced his last childe during the time of Caligula. Some time prior to that is when he discovered and realized Golconda and spread it to some of his childer.

                              Cappadocious woke from torpor several times in antiquity, each time corresponding with a great plague. He converted to Christianity during the Antonine plague, and he performed the Feast of Folly during the plague of Justinian. He did not actively sire Augustus, but informed his childe Japheth in dreams to bring Augustus to his torpored body and perform a cold embrace. The manner in which Augustus diablerized Cappadocious is totally different than what is in the Giovanni Chronicles.

                              Typhon was active at times in the history of Ancient Egypt, but most of the activity attributed to him at this time was actually his 4th generation childe Set, brother of Osiris. He was active around 900 BC when his forces were mostly annihilated by Horus and the Silent Striders and driven out of Egypt. He destroyed most (but not all) of his surviving clan then and established a new way the clan would organize itself to prevent such a defeat from occurring again.

                              Tzimisce briefly woke to defend himself during the Anarch Revolt when he slew his attackers, but went back to torpor soon thereafter.

                              Torpor is simply the best survival strategy for the Antediluvians, and it is a necessary phase to both build their powers and conduct the Jyhad on a psychic level. More often than not, when an Antediluvian does waken from torpor, it is an outside event that forces it on them. When it isn't, it's because it is a necessary part of their plan in the Jyhad. In so far they are "active" today, it is not physically active but psychically active despite being in torpor.

                                      It seems sensible to retain Antediluvians as the Sleeping Giants of the setting, rather than actual NPCs - though some part of me loves the notion that when the Antediluvians ARE active one notices them only with the benefit of hindsight (Since vampires are, after all, shadowy predators who thrive by operating Sub Rosa so it makes sense that any vampire able to live so terrifyingly long are going to be past and present masters of “Run Silent, Run Deep”).


                                      As for Gehenna, I’ve gone back and forth on this, but for the moment I tend to side with the notion that Gehenna is mostly the end phase of the vicious cycle that defines vampire existence to this very night - a long game that produces reigning champions and survivors rather than winners or losers.

                                      Whether this will always be the case, however, should remain an Interesting Question.


                                      When it comes to the Garou, however, The Apocalypse is Played For Keeps: a genuine Ragnarok, to gives the long painful struggle a point and a Big Finish.
