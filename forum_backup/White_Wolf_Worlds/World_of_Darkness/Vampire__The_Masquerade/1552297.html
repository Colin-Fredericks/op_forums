                      I don't think anyone expects vampires to sparkle outside Twilight, nor do I think it bad that they do there. I actually like Twilight's worldbuilding proposal, as badly written as it is.

                      What I personally think is the most common misconception, while not terribly wrong, is the idea that Vampire (or any horror setting for that matter) is Grim Dark. Gothic Punk isn't Grim Dark. Hope is an integral part of the game. It was downplayed with passing editions to broaden the options for STs, but the idea of darkness being unavoidable actually runs counter both Gothic and Punk literature.

                      But isn't a terrible misconception. If that's what floats your boat, go for it, and in the end this darker take (if not necessarily all the way to Grim Dark) is more popular (hence the changes through editions).

                                              Something I recently clocked in vtm/wod writers struggle with intent of the work often relying on lazy slogans or reductive cliches.                                                   There's also the difference between how people played the game vs. what the game actually said. I think it's fair to say that V5 is intended in many ways a return to how some people (maybe even a lot of people that did so) played 1e and early 2e; even if it wasn't always strictly in-line with what the books themselves presented (esp. supplements). This certainly applies to authors as well (since any franchise as old as VtM has current authors that weren't playing the game when it first came out), but it's a bit different than just falling into non-statement traps.

                                                  This is generally the ethos of the OSR style of games: use modern game design techniques to create games that lean into how early D&D was played over directly iterating on the games via editions to try to dial in author and player communication. There's definitely a lot of good to be had in trying to refine a game from this perspective rather than the more traditional revision-over-time style that games generally have to do to last long enough to start with.

                                                          I mean, there is a canon lore, because canon just means the lore presented officially in the books and/or as recognized by the property owners (that is, Paradox can decanonize old official canon if they desire to). I feel like people can forget that canon doesn't inherently mean consistent or non-contradictory. One of the perceived advantages of unreliable narrators is that they can be wrong so you can adjust the lore-as-consumed by changed the character speaking (though many WoD contributors over the years have expressed serious concerns with how well that plays out given how many readers assume that the unreliable narrators are still communicating what the authors intend to be the official "truth" of the setting). There is canon lore that X NPC said various things on various topics, because their thoughts are presented officially in the texts. How accurately the NPCs are communicated the intent of the authors can be debated by fans, and altered by future writers.

                                                          I'm not sure it's a good idea to dwell too much on divisions within the community, but I don't think it's as clear cut as you're saying. I'm in my third decade of playing WoD games (so I'm pretty sure I get to count as an "old school fan") and I vastly prefer the CofD approach of a looser canonical history of the setting (though there is a lot more canon lore and history than is sometimes acknowledged) over the older approach the original WW took with the WoD. V5, esp. compared to H5 and W5, was written with far more desire to honor the old canon of the game without being strictly beholden to it.

                                                          I also don't think most old school fans that dislike WoD5 are somehow wrong about where WoD5 is headed... where it's headed is largely why they don't like it. WoD5 is not what I want, and I'm extremely sure moving closer to what I want is not going to happen, so I'm not a WoD5 fan. Simple as that.
