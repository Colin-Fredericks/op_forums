    Honestly, if the Week of Madness was used to explain why the Sabbat descended into that state of, well, utter madness, I'd prefer it. It's basically ONE Antediluvian who woke up and the cold hard truth is that the Sword of Caine wouldn't have been to stop him. And Ravnos is probably not the most outwardly destructive of the lot. It should have been enough to send shivers down the whole sect, and combined with the Gehenna war going on, it'd be terrible for the Sabbat.

    I could see a schism following it, with the Lasombra going "Okay, we certainly didn't kill our own Ante, we're ditching this shit, we will al be destroyed if we go toe to toe with them" and the Tzimisce fitting against the most extreme elements, who are mostly just monstrous from the start and feel that they need to become even more in order to have some hope of defeating the Antediluvians.

    So, a three way schism : Lasombra going toward the Camarilla for, well, protection; Tzimisce going anarchs because they fucking hate Tremere and want to create a new vampiric society anyway while the maddest of the Sabbat remain and get madder by the night.

    Individual anti-tribes would join either of the splinters, and it could be interesting to have a whole bunch of former Sabbats joining the Anarchs and going "we were always the real Anarchs anyway" on the rest.

                                                            Originally posted by Manfr View Post
                                                            those staying in the Sabbat are the hardcore, ISIS-like or whitesuprematist-like terrorists, with whom you will want to talk only at safe distance and after a sound deprogramming.
                                                            First, phrasings like "Ultimately, Sabbat vampires don’t have a choice in many of the actions they take. In giving themselves to the Beast, they’ve set aside that central riddle of the Kindred condition." don't give the impression that the authors think a deprogramming is possible.

                                                            Second, if they did, then being able to play Sabbat members, especially one's with still some shreds of Humanity, would be a far better representation of how those things work and a recognition that people in such a state are still people.

                                                            Like how people sometimes leave Scientology, QAnon and ISIS, after a long time of suffering while their twisted beliefs conflict with their actual best interests, and would also respect the feelings of their loved ones that desperately want to get them back.

                                                            The Sabbat isn't a RL terrorist group, but fiction is a means for us to understand and explore reality. By making the Sabbat unplayable and the existence of conflicted members impossible they're barring an important avenue for exploration and a tacit statement about their world view. Given the context and proportions, the former is far more problematic than the later, since it is just a game, but the later is still problematic nonetheless and something that really ticks me off.

                                                            It is too easy to write-off extremists as non-human and don't think further about it. And that's exactly what they did.

                                                            Originally posted by Manfr View Post
                                                            I think that in V5 you can run a range of very interesting and varied post-Sabbat games, all grounded in both the previous lore on the Sect and in the lore from the new edition.
