      Stereotypes are stereotypes, but the clans have been around for thousands and thousands of years and different clans have gotten a reputation for different things. And in general, the reputation is more accurate than not. The Nosferatu as a whole tend to be pretty good spies. The Ventrue has a whole tend to be decent leaders. The Malkavians can often bring insights that other clans can't.

      Of course you can absolutely have Ventrue who are complete fuckups at leadership roles, or Nosferatu who couldn't sneak their way through an empty room. But for the most part, more often than not, a member of a clan will be at least somewhat decent at the thing the clan is known for. Not necessarily the best, not necessarily better than members of other clans at the thing, but at least a bit above average.

      So if you need help breaking into a building, you'd ask a Nos for assistance. If you need help in manipulating a group of mortals you'd turn towards a Toreador. And even if the one vampire you turn towards couldn't help you, they very likely know someone in their clan who could.

          How do I roleplay the Clans 1#: Ventrue?

          The Ventrue

          Generally, the Ventrue of the Camarilla are people that struggle with the fact that they're viewed as a necessary evil in Kindred society. They're mostly embraced from the privileged elites of society and are used to everything going their way. Furthermore, their powers are tailor made for assembling a power base of wealth as well as influence. There is almost never a poor Ventrue unless they are a fantastic moron or the subject of enemy action. However, the Ventrue struggle to deal with the fact that they are the leaders by default rather than acclaim.

          In the simplest terms, the Ventrue can't compel the majority of vampires to obey them very well. The Brujah are resistant to any form of orders, the Nosferatu have their own hierarchy, the Gangrel don't CARE about leadership, the Toreador are the real masters of the game, the Tremere have their own hierarchy x11 (and don't respect anyone who can't pull a rabbit out of a hat), and the Malkavians? Well, there you go. The power of the Ventrue means they can compel mortals to obey them through force or majesty but Kindred are much more resistant. So Ventrue inevitably end up using money, violence, and tradition to try to assert their specialness over all other Clans.

          The Ventrue believe themselves to be the natural rulers of vampiredom and heirs to Caine but this is not an attitude that the rest of Cainite society takes all that seriously. Generally, the best they can get is, "Ventrue rule is the worst form of government except for all the others." Someone has to bribe the mortal photographers and erase memories after all. For a Clan culture and blood that demands respect and obedience, a huge number of Ventrue quietly seethe at the fact other vampires don't automatically defer to them in all things. A few Ventrue decide they must thus PROVE themselves to be the best possible leaders while others lash out in petty displays of vulgar excess or tyranny to vent their frustration.

          Vampire society couldn't function without the Ventrue doing the grunt work of protecting the Masquerade and accumulating mortal influence but its hard to be an aristocracy when the peasants can punch through walls and ALSO compel you to love them.

              How do I roleplay the Clans 2#: Toreador

              If you can read someone's mind and control someone's emotions then there is absolutely nothing preventing you from making them dangle on a puppet string. The Ventrue can compel you to do whatever they want and make you believe they are the best person for the job. The Toreador have you think it was your own idea and they're a bunch of harmless socialites. The Toreador are the true masters of the Camarilla and it exists in a very real way because of their efforts. Not because they want to protect the Masquerade, though that is obviously there, but because the Toreador wanted to create a vampire society that they could be the mean girls and popular boys of. Elysium, the politicing, the status, and intrigue? All of the inherent backstabbing and social climbing inherent to the Camarilla is the product of Toreador maneuvering. So much so that no one usualy questions why it even exists.

              Why were the Brujah kicked out? Because the Toreador decided that they were no longer any fun and constantly ruining their fun. Why is the Camarilla now a much more exclusive old boys (and girls) club? Because the Toreador got stick of the tenor of the place being lowered. Thin Bloods and Caitiff can trace massive amounts of their persecution to the Degenerates' utterly pointless classicism designed to make an in-group versus an out group.

              The Toreador are the grandmasters of social combat and their best argument for being even better at it than the Ventrue is the fact that they are routinely dismissed as compared to the Tremere or Ventrue as the beating heart of the Camarilla. Even Anarchs think they're a bunch of fashionistas and snobs rather than propagandists or schemers. Their pretensions of artistry and patrons of beauty fall before the fact they are much-much better at stirring up shit. It's entirely possible THEY created the Ventrue's superiority/inferiority complex.

              They need to feel alive because so much of their power is about making fake emotions and knowing how to manipulate people.

              Ironically, the Toreador's greatest weakness is the fact that they can't turn this off. Being able to know the true emotions and thoughts of everyone they know with Auspex means that virtually every Toreador eventually becomes the heartless manipulative sociopath vampires are always accused of being. They can't stop backstabbing, one-upping, undermining, and playing the Game even when they want to have genuine alliances. Its an addiction and a source of major instability in every Camarilla society.

              No domain can be stable if Annabelle's shoes were maroon at a red-shoe party. If they can't find drama, they will make it.

                      I accept the existence of the clan stereotypes by viewing them as cultures that have been passed down from sire to impressionable new childe for generations.

                      The one clan that I really relish tweaking, though, is the Nosferatu. They may have put in a lot of work in recent decades to market themselves as information brokers in order to carve out a space for themselves in the prestation economy, but their real culture as a clan is one of doomsday preppers.

                      There's a passage about the Guruhi in the Ebony Kingdom book, about a peacefully negotiated end to hostilities within the clan, that certainly lends itself to a reading in which the Nictuku gave up hunting the rest of Absimiliard's children *centuries ago.*

                      This is too funny for me to not hold it dear as cherished headcanon. It keeps me warm on cold nights. A storyline that was otherwise never going to be resolved is tied up in a neat bow. One of the biggest secrets in the European and American World of Darkness is completely unguessed at by the great sewer detectives, *and it's about their own clan.* Every time the Sewer Rats lose communication with their kin in another city, it's probably another supernatural competitor for the same turf - Black Spiral Dancers or Bone Gnawers, Sluagh or Redcaps, the Tzimisce under Manhattan - the great detectives never get to the bottom of it because they assign blame to a menace that only exists in their heads.

                      If Baba Yaga's really dead, the werewolves did it, but you'll never convince the paranoid Nosferatu of that.

                                  Clan Thoughts #3: Tremere

                                  I tend to view the Tremere as individuals who are almost comically enslaved to their own reputations.

                                  This could be way off but my general view about the Tremere, especially Pre-V5 but still after, is the fact that they stuck playing the role of the power-hungry evil sorcerers. The average Tremere is Embraced from a University grad student rather than people at the end of their lives and would probably spend the next century of their life researching without much complaint. Letf to their own devices, the Tremere are some of the most boring individuals you might ever meet in the World of Darkness.

                                  However, the nature of the Tremere is the fact that they are constantly required to justify their existence in the Camarilla even centuries later. The Tremere are constantly trotted out for every single problem imaginable ranging from, "Who murdered Dave?", "What is Steve's Clan? Is he really Ventrue?", "Is this house really haunted?" The Tremere also stereotyped as being ambitious power-hungry psychopaths so they are always the first people that a would-be usurper is approached whenever there's a coup planned. Many Tremere actually resent the titles of Seneschal and Primogen that are forced onto them as "rewards" for being there to do their wizardry. The Tremere play the games of prestation like all Kindred but have an embarrassment of riches they often have little use for.

                                  Tremere do not care whether the other Clans respect them. They only care if the other Clans get in their way. Many Tremere Regents think of themselves as the janitors of their city, cleaning up after incompetent Princes and Harpies because the only way they can do their research in peace is in a stable non-Sabbat, non-Hunter filled cities. This means preserving the Masquerade, managing business interests to keep themselves funded, and trying to avoid all-out wars they will be caught up in. Power to the Tremere is the raw magic of their Disciplines and mortal or even Kindred power will almost always be a dull substitute.

                                  "You don't understand power, Grand Admiral Thrawn. Conquering worlds you'll never even visit isn't power. Neither is destroying ships and people and rebellions you haven't looked at face-to-face." ―Joruus C'baoth

                                  "Power is power," -- Cersei Lannister

                                  Which is ironic because there is a place that the Tremere toss aside all of their traditional apathy to Kindred politics and boredom to become Toreador gerbils on coffee. Tremere hate other blood mages, particularly other Tremere. There is seething resentment and ambition hiding behind the eyes of virtually every Apprentice that bringing another Warlock into your home is taking your life into your own hands.

                                  It's ironic for a Clan that used to be ritually blood bonded to itself but every Tremere plots against each other. They jockey for power, prestige, and access to the forbidden tomes. They undermine each other, sabotage one another, seduce, and even plot murders when they're not readying for duels to the final death. All of it for the glory of the Clan, of course, and the fact House Carna and House Tremere are feuding doesn't mean they won't team up against outsiders. In the end, other vampires aren't really "real."

                                  The Tremere hate the Banu Haqim less because of the fact the latter have tried to kill them for millennia and more because the later have Blood Sorcery. The same for the Tzimisce. A Caitiff who knows Blood Sorcery is targeted because he's a valid target in the game but the intensity of the hatred is not that different from their own in-clan rivals. A secret is only valuable the fewer people who know it after all and a Tremere measures themselves against other magicians.

                                      I think that I run the Clans pretty close-to-book. My chronicles are V20, with frequent bits of "deep lore" taken from the Revised-era Clanbooks, and bits and pieces taken from V5's additions to the Metaplot. Having run VTM now for a little over two years, trends have certainly emerged in the way I tend to play NPCs of certain clans, emphasizing certain aspects.

                                      Brujah tend to be intense and passionate, but often surprisingly well-controlled. Brujah who make it to Ancilla status tend to be ones with Self Control of 4 or better. They aren't always dedicated to a higher cause or philosophy but they do tend to be social, joining groups and accumulating followers. You rarely see a Brujah on their own, they almost always have gangmates and mortal followers (cultivated via Presence rather than Dominate, these Allies and Herd tend to be quite outspoken and willful). They are one of the most common clans by number, especially in the lower age range.

                                      Toreador are flashy, scintillating, bitchy and have a definite tendency to be some of my cruelest characters. They often dress outrageously, costumed to attract attention. Their culture strongly emphasizes the art of the put-down. Many of them are purely social creatures who rely on the rules of the Camarilla to protect them, but the occasional "wareador" will be among the deadliest Kindred in my settings. They are also an extremely common Clan, filing up Kindred gatherings and dominating any social scene.

                                      Malkavians come in two varieties. I haven't much used standard Malkavians, with the Dominate variety being much more common in my Northwest by Night chronicle due to the city's Malkavian Primogen having a very large brood. Dominate Malkavians tend to function much like the stereotypical Ventrue, building power structures around themselves (in the Primogen's case, a powerful regional crime syndicate). There's a little bit of envy there---the Malkavians see how power, influence and respect comes so easily tlo the Ventrue, and they are weighed down by their derangements and negative stereotypes among the Kindred. Their derangements are rarely as obvious as the Dementation-using side of the Clan and tend towards deformation of personality---rage, paranoia, envy magnified to maniacal proportions. As a result the clan tends to stand apart and operate much like a family--often squabbling and fractious, but ready to support one another against the Kindred who fear and despise then.

                                      Most of the Assamite NPCs I've used are of the Vizier variety and I emphasize the Clan's learned, cultured aspects. Poetry, history, philosophy and faith are deeply woven into their clan culture. Playing the Assamite Primogen is exhausting because I work hard to weave poetry from Rumi or Omar Khayyam into much of her dialogue, which requires reading and preparation beforehand.

                                      With Ventrue, (as you might guess, one of my favorite Clans) I emphasize the collegial aspect of their clan's culture. They act as one, almost always presenting a united front when dealing with other clans in the city. This compensates for the fact that they are one of the fewest clans by number--only the worthy[ will be selected for embrace, and pass through the long trials of the agogwe. Necessarily they are few in number, but their vast resources, competence and cooperative culture more than makes up for this. Beneath that front however, their ambition drives them to dark deeds and endless plotting. I dig the corporate aspect of the modern clan and they mainly subside in the worlds of high finance, technology or business and politics, with a few outliers rooted in the city's underworld. They are snobbish and competitive but also brokers and deal-makers. Their pride is both a source of strength---they are in some ways the most "selfless" clan--and their downfall.

                                      Gangrel are mysterious. They come and go through the borders of the city at will, prowling where other Kindred fear to tread and inhabiting the desolate and liminal areas of the city. Other Kindred know to give their claws a wide berth. They always seem to be outsiders and loners, for few can guess how deeply bound together the Gangrel are by their oral legends and traditions. Getting to know a Gangrel NPC over an extended period will show how deeply bound these "loners" are by obligations to their Clan. The few who try to join the Camarilla tend to be treated rather badly, used as fodder and scarcely allowed to gain status by Kindred who misunderstand and quietly fear them.

                                              Clans are natural self-fulfilling stereotypes.

                                              IMO most common Disciplines are learnt via hunting behaviours. Presence is usually gained by charming your fangs into someone, Potence is gained by holding them down and so on and so forth. Or if you want to say the chicken comes first, Someone good with Dominate will have an easier time manipulating someone into being fed from, and anyone is going to use the methods easiest for them. In any case, Someone with a lot of levels in Potence will be more likely to use physical force to feed, and can't be described as a gentle person. Disciplines offer a Positive feedback loop: Once one strategy for feeding becomes easy, the more you can rely on it. The more you use it, the easier it gets.

                                              You add in the weaknesses and boom, it becomes very easy to become the archetype. The clan culture follows. Most clans have a culture that very naturally follows their disciplines and weakness. They in turn become very likely to embrace good fits. Deviance isn't impossible, but you're likely to become a Caitiff as a result.

                                                          Ventrue

                                                          Embrace: The Ventrue look for people that can carry out clan ambitions. They look for competence and drive, and they are more likely than most to interfere on an individual member's choice of Childe, so not following their style is a dangerous prospect. That way we can expect that most of them were at least good prospects in life, at least as far as it could be guessed.

                                                          It doesn't means every Ventrue was rich or successful in life, or that just being rich places you in their radar. Life is a bitch and the real world isn't as meritocratic as some people think, if it will ever be, and most Ventrue know it, so sometimes a jewel can be find among the downtrodden, and a great number of the upper echelons of mortal society can't really make it as Ventrue.

                                                          But the trend is still there, of course. It is easier to develop and show your talent when life already blessed you, while poverty may kill it too soon. The new rich tend to be higher profile in general society than members of rich traditional families, which make them a bit hard to remove and Embrace. And of course, knowing exceptions exist don't eliminate bias completely. In the end, the majority of the Ventrue was "well born" in life and is used to be on the top of the ladder.

                                                          Structure: probably the second most structured clan in the Camarilla, the Ventrue are accompanied by their peers from afar from Embrace to Final Death. They are selected, prepared, trained and indoctrinated. It isn't too much of a thorough indoctrination, though, more geared towards efficient service than anything, but is still intense. After that they're still watching, offering help and demands for every member.

                                                          That makes little for informing behavior directly, but weeds out most of the problematic newcomers and continually creates prestation within the clan. A Ventrue Vampire isn't a stereotype, but exists in continual service to the Ventrue Institution, with varying levels of actual belief in, agreement with or compliance to this institution.

                                                          Weakness: certainly the clan Weakness creates a pressure no Ventrue can ignore. On one hand, the variety of it means they'll have to develop far different strategies to form and keep their herds and feeding grounds. On the other though, all of them must develop the skills to put those strategies to work, so all of them must be pragmatic and organized, with a good long term vision and people skills.

                                                          Or, at least, be able to delegate this to someone competent. Delegation is an important ability in itself, so yeah, that work, too.

                                                          Disciplines: it's easy to attribute success to the combination of Dominate and Presence, and in a certain way this is true, but here is the thing: those are hard to use Disciplines. Not hard on their use, but hard on their long term outcomes. Few powers are so likely to backfire royally on the user if applied without care. Dominated people are pliable but relatively obvious, and it only gets worse if they're Dominated with frequency. Presence creates drive, but little direct control over the targets' actions.

                                                          Given the Ventrue's necessities and ambitions, they are more powerful for what they must learn to use their powers sparingly than by just using them. They learn what makes people tick, without the benefit of just see it. They learn how to select targets and influence without pressure. They can completely overcome someone's will, true. But what a single person means to them? If you brainwash a police captain all you get is an early retired police captain. Controlling the police department takes a lot more of cunning.

                                                          Fortitude helps with the two other things they will need, survival and lots of patience. As masters behind so many people, even without imposing their will, the Ventrue are the obvious targets. As the people that will be present at high profile meetings, they're on the hot-spots of violence. As the charismatic generals in battles literal and metaphorical, they are the ones to take the shot. As leaders that must keep morale, they must take the shot and shrug it off. And as master planners trying to reach beyond their direct powers, they must be used to spend most of their time just waiting.

                                                          Culture: the clan is deeply informed by the upper classes and by high administration. Once it meant the Senatus, then it meant the military, now it means big investors. One way or another, everyone watches everyone else's achievements and have to meet expectations.

                                                          A big difference from the Ventrue to some aristocratic groups is their take on personal value, though. Your value is defined by your work to the clan. Your pedigree may play a role, but ultimately they are perfectly willing to throw a prince under the bus if they do little for the greater whole, and protect a peasant with true ability to achieve.

                                                          In the end no matter how you define aristocracy, the clan remember the time when it meant nothing and knows the tides will change anew. And this is something to be taught at the agogwe, so it doesn't matter how much you believe your blood, live and dead, matters, you know it won't save you if you mess up.

                                                          Work to the greatness of the clan. Keep the stability of Kindred society. Organize people, this is more important than ruling them. Let fools be fools, but make them reach the goals. Stay focused. Be a part of the whole and reap the benefits we can achieve together.

                                                          Characters: Many Ventrue came from structured backgrounds and went through a structured learning process after an embrace into a structured clan. They may have wildly different views of the entire thing, but they're well adapted to work within it.

                                                          It is easy then to miss their actual variety. Those people are very used to presenting an unified front even when it is far from it or unnecessary. Most of them are also used to hierarchy, and to act from near the top of it, especially if they were rich in life, so acting accordingly is like second nature to them. It doesn't mean they're necessarily tyrants, or even that they're as arrogant as they seem, but they likely seem the part for people unused to this sort of ambient.

                                                          That way, Ventrue tend to follow through with the system despite their actual opinions about it. Some willingly choose to remain in lesser positions because it fits their personal preferences for one reason or another. Some game the system for personal gain, other do so to help the downtrodden, but go unseen for not speaking overtly against it. They are just used to the idea that bashing it doesn't work. Some will try to build their own power base to act against it, which obviously mean that even when they act against the clan, they look like the clan.
