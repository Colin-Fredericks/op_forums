                            Originally posted by False Epiphany View Post
                            I mean, I do. V5's mechanics have... a lot of issues (though some pretty great ideas), and Masquerade's are now 30 years old, so I've just played with Requiem rules. Heavily house ruled, the point of now being its own game system.
                            V5 has driven me right into the arms of VTR and... I'm happy about it. I love the VTM setting, but there are so many things I've now pinched from VTR (aforementioned Kindred Senses, the rewrites of Protean and Auspex, etc).

                            This is too complex for my tastes. I introduced Hunger and Rouse checks into my game, but I modeled bigger blood pools by removing Rouse costs from (most) Disciplines with a lower rating than the vampire's Blood Potency.
                            That's a really simple, elegant fix. One of my pet peeves about rouses and Blood Potency is that the table is way more complicated than it needs to be, with re-rolls, blood surge, variable heal costs, etc. All those things were simply achievable with blood points before.

                            Having simple rules is, IMO, better. E.g., if blood surge equalled Blood Potency, too, and it lasted for one scene in one Attribute, that might be better. Or maybe you allocate dice across Attributes equal to your BP for the scene, so you can have +1 Strength, +2 Dexterity and +1 Stamina if you have BP 4.

                            To me, that's preferable to making blood surge basically a slightly better version of WP expenditure. (Also, am I alone in thinking Netchuch's name for this, the Quickening, is better than blood surge? I also prefer VTR's Devotions as a term than V5's Amalgams, which sound like dental work. But that's another tangent...)

                            Re: Compulsions, which some people have had a problem with but which others like but not as written, I wondered whether you could make Frenzies a bit different, so that you only roll once (rather than making it an extended test), with the following outcomes: when you score 5+ successes on your roll, you avoid Frenzy; if you pass with 1-4 successes, you gain a Compulsion (perhaps with an onset delayed by one turn per success, if you're generous); if you fail, you Frenzy; if you botch, you Frenzy and lose a dot in a Virtue or develop a Derangement.

                            (You might need to tweak the number of successes based on how many dice people are rolling. With V20 Virtues, it might be better to set the bar at 3+ successes, since you'll be rolling 1-5 dice. Maybe add Humanity/3 like in V5 to push those dice pools up a bit or give a bonus for vampires who are fully fed.)

                            That way, Compulsions are the default, but only when you'd normally test for Frenzy. You trade losing complete control for acting more like a vampire, as mediated by clan and concept.

                            If you botch when subject to a Compulsion, only then would you get a Bestial Failure; if you score 5+ successes, only then would you get a Messy Critical. These are less common as a result, and therefore more meaningful. You could reward the player by letting them resolve the Compulsions when they BF or MC, or by allowing them to regain WP by turning over control to the Beast for these brief moments.

                            That gives you the seasoning, as it were, from V5, without so much loss of control. For more danger, you can simply add Bestial Failures and Messy Crits as outcomes when you botch or crit when hungry, but not in any other state. This way, it's a dial you can turn up or down.

                            Boom! Simple. It works with blood points or rouses. It loses Hunger Dice. It gives everyone more chance to get the tone right for their tables.

                            We use VTR Beats anyway, so the simple fix for us is letting PCs convert any botch or crit to a Bestial Failure or Messy Critical for a Beat, in addition to the options above. Making those options a choice with a reward makes things more appealing than making them all stick.

                            Finally (and this isn't something I'm interested in as much but others might be), if you want more dynamic blood points, I'd do the following: for every health level you inflict upon a human while feeding (you can normally inflict three per turn; humans only have seven HLs, as usual), roll two dice; or take 10 points if you are willing to drain someone dry instead. For each success, you gain one blood point. On average, that means a human gives you seven blood points and you can leave them on the cusp of life or death, but they give you 10 if you don't care about leaving them totally dry.

                            Each time you spend blood, instead roll two dice per blood point. Each die that fails, you lose a blood point. Each die that botches also triggers a Compulsion. This way, you can't quite gauge how much blood you'll spend at a given time but you lose all the re-rolls, variable heal costs and variable blood surge on the Blood Potency table. If you want things a bit more reliable, have all humans provide a fixed number blood points (up to 10 as usual) and just randomise the expenditure.

                            In this way, you can take a lot of the new stuff from V5 while still using the core V20 chassis.

                                                    Requiem 2e vampires are stronger than Masquerade vampires in many ways, honestly.

                                                    Elder Disciplines may not be a thing, but 1-5 Disciplines all got huge boosts, especially at the low levels. Dominate 1 lets you command people to do whatever you can cram into four words and rewrite their memories of the current scene. Dominate 2 turns someone into your slave for all night with one roll (and a weighted dice pool that favors the attacker). Dominate 4 lets you rewrite an entire lifetime's worth of a victim's memories, even inventing families who never existed. Auspex 1-2 let you look into people's heads with explicitly no way for them to tell someone is reading their mind. Many of the 6-7 Masquerade powers got folded into 4-5 Requiem 2e powers.

                                                    The increased power level of those Disciplines is okay in Requiem's setting, which in many ways is less political. Or at least more dynamic. The War of Ages between elders and neonates isn't really a thing and power shakeups are more common in cities. But in a Masquerade game, I found the 2e Disciplines to be much too powerful. And by "powerful" I mean, they tended to cut through layers of intrigue and social interaction, and made players devote more time and thought than I liked towards how to counter adversaries' Disciplines. My Ventrue player cheered when I nerfed Dominate. (I found V5's caveat that Dominate can't be used for interrogation purposes to be incredibly wise.)

                                                    Requiem 2e has a lot of cool ideas. I love how all vampires can see in the dark by default. (They are nocturnal, the Beast doesn't play well with fire, and electricity is a comparatively modern invention.) Many Disciplines with "meh" powers got seriously beefed up. But they're designed for a different setting than Masquerade and need tweaking when they're used in it.

                                                        Originally posted by adambeyoncelowe View Post

I changed them to only work against mortals or vampires whose blood the character has recently tasted, and found that to reduce "everyone could be reading my mind" paranoia levels.

                                                        One of the biggest things I like about 2e VtR Disciplines is how many of them no longer take dice rolls. You just say "this cool thing happens," no fuss no muss. I've increasingly trended away from requiring dice rolls for powers that don't directly harm or influence other characters.

                                                        Having simple rules is, IMO, better. E.g., if blood surge equalled Blood Potency, too, and it lasted for one scene in one Attribute, that might be better. Or maybe you allocate dice across Attributes equal to your BP for the scene, so you can have +1 Strength, +2 Dexterity and +1 Stamina if you have BP 4.

                                                        Scene-long physical boosts is basically paying blood to use Potence, IMO. I liked the way Requiem did it with spending a Vitae giving you a +2 bonus on a single Physical roll (IIRC?), no messy generational caps or diminishing bonuses past X threshold. Just spend the blood, get a +2 bonus. Boom.

                                                        Conceptually, I like Physical Intensity/Blood Surge/whatever as a brief, desperate surge of blood-fueled strength. It can let any vampire pull off incredible physical feats, if they really want to, but it's not something they can use all the time like Potence.

                                                        [qupte]I also prefer VTR's Devotions as a term than V5's Amalgams, which sound like dental work. But that's another tangent...)
                                                        Me too. I just use Devotions as the name for individual powers within a larger Discipline.

                                                        This was another design idea of V5's that I really, really liked, as an aside. Multiple powers per Discipline level. I can't believe earlier editions didn't think of this.

                                                        The big thing I've changed is not restricting players to only one power per Discipline level: they can take as many they like, though every power beyond the first at the same Discipline level costs extra XP. Players have loved it. It's also allowed me to open up many high-level Disciplines at lower levels by inventing scaled-down versions. For instance, Beast Shape works just fine at Protean 1 if you limit it to a non-flying, non-dangerous animal for only a scene. My player with Protean 2 loves being able to turn into a cat.

                                                        Re: Compulsions, which some people have had a problem with but which others like but not as written, I wondered whether you could make Frenzies a bit different, so that you only roll once (rather than making it an extended test), with the following outcomes: when you score 5+ successes on your roll, you avoid Frenzy; if you pass with 1-4 successes, you gain a Compulsion (perhaps with an onset delayed by one turn per success, if you're generous); if you fail, you Frenzy; if you botch, you Frenzy and lose a dot in a Virtue or develop a Derangement.
                                                        I like this idea. Messy criticals, bestial failures, and hunger dice were a prolix and non-intuitive part of the rules, but bestial compulsions were cool. Easiest way to insert them into a game is to just attach them to other dice rolls as a consequence for not rolling high enough. Don't even have to be just frenzy rolls.

                                                            Something I think VtR 2e did well (esp. since it redeemed one of the worst rules in VtR 1e) relates to the idea of having something between VtM 1e-20th's "nothing or frenzy" and V5's "here's a half a dozen ways the Beast might screw up your character's plans," approaches: Lashing Out.

                                                            When VtR 2e vampires go Beast to Beast, the loser effectively gets smacked with a Compulsion in V5 terms (though there are just three universal ones instead of Clan ones and all that). It has that "Beast gets under your skin and you do not so smart things without a full blown Frenzy" part that the earlier games lack, but isn't as agency ignoring as V5's mechanics tend to be. It's never an accident, because there's always at least one vampires that is purposefully making it happen.

                                                            It's easy to picture how street level politics are full of Lashing Out as vampires push each other around for dominance and control, but polite high political vampire society considers such antics beneath them (not that they aren't all ready to do it at the drop of a hat if cornered or need to put a youngster in their place without drawn out conflict) and uncouth to maintain the appearances of civil vampire society.
