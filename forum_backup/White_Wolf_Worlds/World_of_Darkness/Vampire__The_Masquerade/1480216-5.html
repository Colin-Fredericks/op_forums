    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    Actually, I don't think [Tzimisce] as a Lovecraftian Great One was a bad thing by itself. I surely agree that sometimes it was heavy-handed and there should be more mystery around it, but giving a sample form to the horror the Ante is supposed to be made it far more palpable, and makes a lot of sense as vampiric legend.

    It is actually the lack of such a form of horrific apotheosis that seems frustrating for most Antes. We're told that they're out to eat their progeny and end the world, but just by being... older? While their In-Clan Discipline spreads gave us something to imagine, there's actually very little to picture how the world ending under [Ventrue], Arikel or Ennoya looks like. That was only barely addressed in Gehenna itself, but that's not just about what form they'll effectively take in the end, it is about what imagery stories about the end conjure for them, this we have nothing on those and many other Antes.

    Sutekh is a powerful sorcerer and cult leader about whom we have many and varied stories that help picture what his raise could look like, if just to explore the legends in a game. [Tzimisce] has a powerful image as this eldritch abomination, somewhat compensating for the lack of variety. What the others have? Even the Malkavian Network is too abstract and vague a threat, and [Ravnos] just a heavy metal week.

    I understand your point, and you raised an interesting observation. My main problem with the Tzimisce "Lovecraftian Great One" thing is that it makes it look like "he/she/it" is much more than the others, while it shouldn't be so IMO. But, as I said, you raised an interesting question that is the fact that we lack "imagery" for most of the "Antes" (although, to be fair, the fear of the "Antes" come much more from their sheer power and their will to destroy/subjugate the world than from their looks, as in some cases nobody would even know who or where the Ante is and that is fearsome as well).

    In terms of imagery, I like the Lasombra one even more than the Tzimisce one. I mean, a creature who merged with the Abyss and can be anything anywhere in the world and could just dominate the whole world like a dark lord or cast it into eternal darkness is also very interesting, but both are basically on the same level. Nosferatu is also one that strikes fear, btw for the opposite reason than Tzimisce. I think it's also very fearsome (possibly even more so) when we don't know and can't track where the enemy is. Some, I think, come off as "too soft" or "too glamorous/sexy", but mostly because of the stereotypes associated with those clans, like Toreador or to some extent even Ventrue. Both clans very influenced by Anne Rice and the imagery associated with them (especially Toreador) doesn't usually strike fear as the others mentioned (I mean ,certainly Lestat is powerful and all, but he's so sexy I wouldn't mind much if he's a "bad guy" to me lol).

    All in all, you certainly came up with a good observation, perhaps it would have been better if we had more imagery of the Antediluvians, but as I said, we could see this through different angles.

            Count me in as a fan of the Dark Ages Tzimisce, but not a fan of the modern Clan.

It turned out that neither of them really was the kind of natural force they imagine themselves to be.

[Lasombra] died of starvation from exploring the Abyss, leaving a desperate but powerful Ghoul, and Gratiano don't want to reveal that the clan feared the Wizard of Oz for centuries.                     For whatever the truth about them is worth, these are Antes you can put in front of your PCs with their full power and knowledge without fear of the chronicle getting out of hand. They can provide help or antagonize, can't be even approached in sheer power, even by Methuselahs, yet they won't just "win" by showing up.

                                                            I wonder if how applying the concept of Charm Modes from Exalted: Essence could work here. The idea there is that certain (all?) Universal Charms, which all Exalt types have access too, have alternate and/or augmenting effects depending on the user’s Exalt type.

                                                            So where base Animalism only affects mundane animals one at a time in an immediate and physical way, Tzimisce Animalism can target humans (making it pseudo-Dominate), Gangrel Animalism gets the ‘inner Beast’ and frenzy manipulating powers (invoking the Berserker legends, tying them back to Odin and co.), Nosferatu Animalism targets entire swarms at once (imagine hordes of rats, cats, roaches etc.),
