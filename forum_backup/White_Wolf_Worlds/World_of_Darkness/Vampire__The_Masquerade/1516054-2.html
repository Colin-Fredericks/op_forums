                One aspect which I think influences why neonates don't get fabolously successful very quick is how predatory Elders are. If you run the Elders as conservative model citizens then, yes, many tactics to give neonates fast power and riches would work.

                But if you run Elders, as I've come to understand how they operate "in universe", then there's two very possible outcomes for a neonate reaching success. Only possibly mitigated by having sufficient contacts and such in the influential (aka Kindred Elders) local population.

                1. An Elder sweeps in and takes the stuff from the neonate. That's predatory behavior if I ever saw one and vampires are supposed to be pretty predatory.

                2. One or more Elders might think that this young neonate could, in their paranoid minds, become a problem or a threat due to talent and such. Thus they kill the neonate.

                I think that many GMs play the Elders, for gameplay reasons, to be essentially benevolent, understanding and respecful towards younger Kindred. Yes, some neonates can absoluely fight back if the Elders would screw with them. These neonates get targeted for elimination early in their unlives before they can gain the proper means to fight back against the Elders.

                Thus my understanding is that alot of neonates who has considerble talent simply have to play worse than they can in order to not stand out and get targeted by the older, less human, Kindred.

                                Players just generally don't want to do that sort of thing. Some players want to be rich, so they buy high backgrounds on character-creation, and then live out the fantasy of holding lavish parties where they sip blood from wineglasses and have catty conversations with other party-goers. No need for a lot of criminal activity in that case. You see a lot of Toreador mains doing this.

                                Others want to live out the fantasy of being a savage monster. They want to sleep earthmelded and pounce on people in alleyways. Money actually gets in the way of that fantasy, making living the way you want to fantasize living into a sort of plot-hole. You see a lot of Gangrel mains doing this. Doing things like taking the No Haven flaw and absolutely never buying it off.

                                There's really not much call for doing this sort of thing. Character creation allows you to bypass it, and those who don't bypass it generally don't want it in the first place.
