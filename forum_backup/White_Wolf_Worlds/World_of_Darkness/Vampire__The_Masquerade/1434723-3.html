                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                            Because that's a very common thing among religious sects.
                            I loved the character idea, but I want just to add some nuance here.

                            While religious schisms are a very common thing, and reigning in other sects too, the actual handling of schisms varies greatly from religion to religion. Most religions don't have the same amount of weight put in the idea of canon as Christianity has, and the Setites aren't an exception in this regard (although not necessarily much similar to most other religions, either). Schisms may happen over a lot of different grounds, whose relevance is unequal among religions, and the options on how to handle schisms and schismatics depend a lot.

                            Christianity has dealt with schisms in many different ways through history. Usually the most important thing for the Catholic Church isn't your stance on canon in general as much as your acceptance of clerical hierarchy. Heresies had to be pretty wild to receive some actual backlash until the Reformation. The Roman/Orthodox schism was all about the role of the Bishop of Rome, and they're technically all Catholics instead of an actual heresy for each other (it's complicated).

                            Muslims have another set of complications. The Quran doesn't define any form of clergy and it is against an organizing religious body. There is no "church" as Christians understand it, even by other names, in the Islam. Mosques exist as places for the faithful to congregate, but the administration and maintenance of the Mosque is a lay job. Instead, it places the job of guiding the faithful, wherever it's needed, directly in the hands of scholars and jurists. A Mosque's Imam is an academic whose expertise is recognized by the community, and the many, many branches of Islam are born out of academic controversies (and political shenanigans, and there's more to it, but that's the basic gist of it).

                            Buddhism is extremely decentralized in itself, while it may be extremely organized and centralized in certain countries or on certain branches of the religion. While there's a lot of divisions and an "almost-schism" between the two bigger branches, its emphasis on the community basically made religious conflicts not a thing simply because it doesn't stop the monks from engaging with their communities and their conflicts even against fellow monks. In fact, sometimes you had monasteries at actual bloody war against one another, without ceasing to recognize the other faction as as much Buddhists as themselves. The existence of cross-monastery hierarchy depends a lot more on politics than religious doctrine, usually by the local government's intervention, except in the more esoteric branches.

                            Those are some examples of how schism are handled across religions, to help anyone interested in modeling how they would handle schism among the Setites.

                                One of the thing to note about religious schisms is that, you're correct, there's very rarely the heirarchy of the Catholic Church persecuting heretics. However, even among Christianity, there have been other variants on the subject. The Eastern Orthodox and Roman Catholic split that was not remotely amicable but neither side really ever went to war (4th Crusade may or may not count) due to them both being roughly equally powerful.

                                There's also the massive war between the various factions of the Thirty-Years War where both sides chose churches primarily based on their political benefits to them at the time. If you wanted help against a Catholic lord in a war, you went to a nearby Protestant lord and became Protestant.

                                In the case of the Followers of Set, the Fire Court of Typhonists are the "main" power of the Followers of Set as they have control over the oldest existing Setite Temples in Egypt. They also have some of the oldest active Elders. It's not quite Alamut, though, because plenty of Elders and Methuselahs have left to form their own churches in other countries with their own habits.

                                The Typhonists are attempting to exert their control over the others with a very specious logic that they are the "originalists" who have the most authority to determine Set's will. This is bullshit because there's a 4th generation Setite in Berlin and the Temple there has as much lineage back to Set as anyone but vampires will use ANY excuse to try to control one another.

                                I think in my campaign, I had the existing Typhonist Church "only" dating back to Cleopatra's time, which means it's a Hellenized Post-Alexandrian Temple that is actually pretty damn young.
