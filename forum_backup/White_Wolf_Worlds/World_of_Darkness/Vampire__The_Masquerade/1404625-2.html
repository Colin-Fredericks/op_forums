    Originally posted by Mister_Dunpeal View Post
    To me VtM treated 'humanity' as the label for the 'Vampire path for goodness/morality' rather than reflecting human nature. Or at most, it reflects a particular (narrow) subset of humanity (which isn't surprising when you consider how 'non-western' alternatives got treated in, say, Kindred of the Ebony Kingdom.) Humans can be pretty diverse (and even subjective) in how they define good/evil (across time, culture, or any number of factors) and I have always felt 'humanity' should reflect this as well (representing a series of 'paths' different from Sabbat oriented paths) - it may not inherently be 'good' but it brings you closer to human rather than vampiric nature.
    Vampire has always been a pretty explicitly Judeo-Christian myth based game, so Humanity is pretty directly based on that same conceptual frame. You can certainly change that, but it pulls up a lot of the mythic underpinnings of the game when you do.

                That's... you not entirely understanding Gnostic thought. With Gnostic thought, the thing is that higher Gods don't really come into/concern themselves with reality (which includes all the umbras and probably includes the far out stuff) Reality seems pretty well shielded from outsiders. Gnosticism has this idea of Emanations; Divine things from outside of reality that fall into reality, decline, and then rise up and out of reality. Perhaps Caine is an Emanation, and perhaps the Demiurge got to curse caine for entering his playground: You could infer True faith and certain other things as being sourced from outside of reality; A good bit of Gnosticism entered Christianity with the idea that the God from the old testament is the evil demiurge and the nice God Jesus represents is the True, real god from up high (one needs only imagine the Anti-semetism this line of thought would cause; But you could also imagine how it applies to the Sabbat's worship of Caine. Gnosticism was eventually deemed heretical and purged by the church)

                The Thematically Gnostic part of the old VTM editions was that, well, you could try to become human, but a big part of the Vampire species is the desire to fight the opperession from above and eat your way up; to unshackle yourself from earthly limitations. The Beast might've been an anchor but the morality of God was optional; you could grow to chose your own morality. It's all very nietzche. The Punk aspect of VTM was deeply Gnostic, and the Gothic aspect was... it fitted the bill too. Of course this sentiment was lessened come revised for a more conventional morality and is gone now.
