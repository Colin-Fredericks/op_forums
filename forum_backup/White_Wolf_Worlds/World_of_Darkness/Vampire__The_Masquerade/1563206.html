      Usually pcs belong to the same coterie. A coterie is the equivalent of a dnd party. A small group of vampires who work together.
      A coterie can be formed for many reasons. Security, some mutual work that needs to be done, pooling of skills and assets.

      A coterie does not have to be created by its members, younger vampires may find themselves in a coterie decided for them by their sires or by older vampires they are beholden to.

      Coteries are expected to hang together. Move across town in a group or at least two by two.

      Elysium or a favored hunting ground (the Rack) are common places vampires can meet each other but it can get cumbersome in play to always go to a place with other npcs to talk about stuff

              That's mostly a thing for the players to decide.

              Vampires usually take care of their own business, they keep in contact but "hanging out" among themselves isn't a regular widespread activity. It happens, but more as a case by case thing.

              Elysium is a common partial exception, but it is more a "neutral ground" than necessarily a hangout. What makes Elysia is the Keeper being able and wiling to both keep it open for all vampires but rigid on enforcing rules. From this most Elysia have at least a few social events a month as a means to spread news and most days someone will be there for part of the night, but commonalities end here.

              Overall that's a thing for you to decide about your setting, your city. Are there popular hangouts for vampires? Some cities have, others don't. Some have many. In some your political allegiances say a lot of where you're likely to be, in others each clan has its own living space, for others spaces where factions meet each other to argue are the point. You do you here.

              Just remember that unless a more powerful vampire is ordering them around, characters can have very personal tastes and takes on all this. Assuming everyone frequents a given space because that's normal is a very good way to piss off some player. Either force things in game through a bossy older vampire or let them figure out what they want themselves.

                  Many of those questions sadly are best answered with: it depends.

                  Elysium is a term for a neutral ground. But it can mean either a permanent fixture or just ad hoc decision. An art gallery where Toreador meet once a week can be Elysium as can be a conference hall that Prince hired for a weekend to host the meeting of all city vampires because of Sabbat infiltrator getting caught in his domain.

                  At the end of the day the details are up to you to come up with. Is a local Brujah running an "invite only" bar/meeting place where all the local Kindred meet every night? Is a Toreador run opera hall frequented by Cainites who have a special entrance and a meeting room guarded by ghouls? There are multiple possibilities.

                  As for why the coterie is working together it's the ages old question: why do PCs in RPG work together. And that is to you to come up with your players. Are they brought up by their patron/mawla? Are they part of some official group: Prince's strong arms, Justicar archons and so on? Or are they just brought together by events - they see something that they shouldn't have and now, in the crosshairs of dangerous enemy, they are banding together for safety in number?

                          In the game I'm currently playing the ad-hoc meeting place is an arcade run by one of the PCs. Initially we were contacted by the local regents (the city is divided between two elders who are on good terms) and there would be no main meeting ground, but the Sheriff really liked the arcade and call us there regularly, so it kind of stuck.

                          But it does help that out of 6 PCs, 4 are heavy on games: I'm a Tzimisce game developer, the place is from a Brujah nostalgic with arcades, we have a Lasombra videogame publisher and a Malkavian hacker Embraced as a child who's, well, a computer-using child, obviously she likes games.

                          The Gangrel old lady is the only one who's not used to videogames and arcades, while the mortal cop is lazy enough in his work that he genuinely prefers to find excuses to hang with the undead in a vintage arcade.

                          So, bottom line: the group will make the hangout, not the ST. If you want a reason for them to be in the same place at the same time, it's better to make a particular event for that than try to guess a common hangout.

                          Ok. If in D&D there's a classic air over the "you met at a tavern" starting point, I'd say the closer we get to it in Vampire is "the Prince called for something". It may be a call for the PCs to give them work, but can also be a party where all in the Domain are expected to appear, or a public announcement, maybe the formal presentation where the Fledglings will be deemed Neonates. Bloodlines 1 had a good one with the execution of your sire as something almost every NPC in LA felt they should attend to.

                          Whatever it is, the important part is putting the PCs in the same place to see the starting plot.

                                      I particularly think offering too much canon is not good advice for an assumed first time ST with a homebrew city. It's throwing confusion on someone who's still grasping the basics.

                                      This is particularly problematic when you start already throwing high consistency expectation with things like the Promise and Justicar authorization. I don't know Internetflexing, but most first time STs I've seen and myself when I was one feel paralyzing fear with this kind of suggestion, because it sounds they're actually completely unprepared and doing it wrong and running them game requires tons of studying and mastery over and entire library of setting lore.

                                      Not want to be too harsh, but I've literally seen this kind of pressure, even just as suggestions, cripple chronicles and stop people from ever trying to ST. I'm meeting today a friend that has been trying to overcome one of those for the last 25 years. He's still preparing his game.

                                      A Giovanni as a Prince isn't anything to pay much heed. I don't see a Justicar wasting time with this if it isn't causing bigger problems, and if it is key it run in the chronicle. Any need for explanation may wait for later, maybe never happen. Kindred in the city may never know if is uncommon, or it may not be, since this is in 1st edition and most of this lore does not apply.

                                              Don't take it too seriously. The details are quite hazy and on my a few novels and chronicles of partial canonicity put to much weight in it.

                                                  Originally posted by Internetflexing View Post
                                                  I can easily finagle a reason theyd all work together (venture, tremere, nosforatu), but where would they hang out exactly?
                                                  There are many conceivable places where vampires would gather for all sorts of reasons.

                                                  When it comes to the PC coterie itself, I suggest you ask them where they'd like to gather. That could be multiple locations depending on the reason. One might be a place that one or more of the PCs control where they could meet in private without any other people. Another could be a place open only to people who are a greater part of vampire society, and thus issues of the Masquerade aren't an issue. Maybe that location is a convenient spot where they could meet NPCs on a social basis, for discussing vampire politics, or to make deals. A third type of place might be a public or semi-public place where mortals ignorant of the Masquerade might also gather so it's important for the PCs to be careful what they say or do. However, such places could be useful in meeting their mortal Allies, Contacts, or people involved in their Influence.

                                                  I call these kind of places "sets" like a theatre set. It's an established location where any PC can set a scene they want. Such places could be a fortified haven known to the other PCs; a place of business where a PC runs his empire; a private social club that is member's only; an expensive, elite restaurant that has special VIP rooms in back for the vampires; a popular bar that one of their ghouls run; and so on.

                                                  If the PCs just need a place to hang out, that isn't something the ST actually needs to create. The PCs can create the description, and the ST just needs to approve it (perhaps revising it as needed before approval).

                                                  Things that the ST would need to create are those locations that are part of the greater chronicle setting itself. Examples might be established mortal artistic or cultural institutions that nevertheless host Elysiums. A special nightclub controlled by a vampire that is Masquerade friendly where Hunting rolls are at lower difficulty. A location like the above for PCs' sets, but belonging to an NPC vampire or coterie that is a place the PCs could easily discover to meet an NPC.

                                                  It'll be the ST created locations where the PCs will go out to meet other vampires.

                                                  Originally posted by Internetflexing View Post
                                                  Are Elysium's always in effect as hang out spots?
                                                  Elysium can be any place or event that the Prince (or perhaps the greater vampire community by consensus that the Prince simply acknowledges) wants to be an Elysium. So a museum might be a permanent Elysium where any time a vampire visits, that vampire knows the Elysium rules are in effect. Or it could be a permanent location, but the Elysium rules only apply on a specific weeknight, holiday, or other regular schedule when events are hosted there (maybe a session of the Prince's court, celebration of an important anniversary to the local Kindred, a regularly scheduled party thrown by the local Toreador salon, etc.) Or they could be special one time events whose location and dates are established in advanced and communicated to the greater Kindred community.

                                                  Some domains might have many Elysiums. Many locations designated as such, and many parties and events. Another domain might not have any Elysiums established although I think most domains will have at least 1-3. It all depends on the culture of the local domain (like which clans are most dominant or powerful) and how much power the Prince and his supporters have to enforce the sanctity of Elysium. A Prince who promotes lots of Elysiums better have the power to protect all of them, or she risks looking weak whenever some jackass flaunts the rules.

                                                  So as the ST, you have a lot of flexibility in terms of how you want to handle this.

                                                  Originally posted by Internetflexing View Post
                                                  Are there vampire run clubs or secret places mostly vamps frequent?
                                                  Yes. There are lots of canonical places described in many sourcebooks. These places may or may not be Elysium. However, the most popular places probably either have open or secret protection by one or more of the domain's power brokers, or are at least frequented by Kindred who respect the place enough for it to be safe (most of the time).

                                                      As others have said, unless you're forcing the Coterie's hand by having a powerful NPC invite/force them to a particular place, it might be better to let the players decide where they hang out.

                                                          I've always tried to treat the Coterie (or Pack, Cabal, etc.) as a character in and of itself, one that all of the players help create together. Everyone talks about which parts of the game/setting they are most interested in and want to explore, and try to work out a group concept everyone can agree on. Said concept can be pretty broad, and even multi-focused. Typical focuses include, but aren't limited to:
                                                          • Clan - everyone is the same Clan, obviously. And this clearly doesn't apply here, but various examples are in the backs of the revised Clanbooks
                                                          • Combat - Muscle for hire, body guards, a particular elder (or the Prince/Sheriff)'s hunting dogs, thrill seekers, or some other reason.
                                                          • Scholarly - Noddists, conspiracy theorists, art collectors, research scientists, information brokers, academics, etc.
                                                          • Financial - Making money, be it legally or not, either for its own ends or as a method of accumulating power or living an extravagant un-lifestyle.
                                                          • Politics - Working together to accumulate politics power and influence, both among mortals and the Kindred, as political fixers, Harpies, negotiators, or community organizers.
                                                          • Crime - The group is essentially a budding criminal syndicate looking to expand their turf.
                                                          • Lifestyle - Everyone shares a similar hobby or pursuit, be it underground gladiatorial games, street racing, cutting edge fashion, computer technology, boardgames, shared religious beliefs, common ethnicity/culture, or any of a million other things.

                                                          The place they gather will serve as a hangout, headquarters, barracks, safe house, place of business, or whatever else depending on what the Coterie does. It can potentially act as a shared Haven or even as starting Domain they've carved out for themselves (or been granted by a potential patron). Random ideas just off the top of my head:
                                                          • A rundown nightclub that's seen better days but could potentially become the next big thing for the Rack and potentially a future Elysium
                                                          • A private hospital or asylum that offers both a quiet and secluded hideout and a source of food
                                                          • A mostly abandoned train yard full of the rusting hulks of forgotten train cars (railway or subway) that offers privacy and defense
                                                          • One of the city's remaining War on Poverty era public housing projects that has been turned into an urban fortress
                                                          • An abandoned church, that can be kept as is or renovated into something else (night club, museum, etc.)
                                                          • An antique books store that brings in a source of revenue, intellectual contacts, and a certain degree of prestige when other vampires come looking for rare tomes
                                                          • A minor league baseball park that can host night games, making it a potential Rack or Elysium
                                                          • A chop shop that is the center of a city wide car theft ring.
                                                          • One of the city's smaller ethnic enclaves from which all or most of the Coterie are connected to
                                                          • A local radio station, one of the few not owned by a nationwide conglomerate and able to program its own music, which makes it a potential way for the city's Kindred to communicate with each other
                                                          • A lost subway station, one built decades or even a century ago and no longer on any city maps
                                                          • A forgotten Cold War bomb shelter
                                                          • An abandoned Gilded Age mansion, one that is somehow still standing and has a reputation for being haunted
                                                          • An RV that the Coterie uses to roam about the city and squat at different locations during daylight hours

                                                          As to the question of "are their vampire run clubs?", the answer is yes. First edition gave us Chicago's Succubus Club and San Fransisco's Vampire Club (run by a vampire Oscar Wilde of all people), so they've always been a part of the game and setting. See also the Théâtre des Vampires from Anne Rice's Interview with the Vampire. These places tend to serve as part of the Rack, an Elysium, or both. (While perhaps slightly dated at this point, the first Blade movie offers a number of locations that would work really well as potential Kindred hangouts.)
