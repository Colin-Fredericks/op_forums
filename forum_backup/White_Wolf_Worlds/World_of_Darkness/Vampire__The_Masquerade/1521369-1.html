    Well, if you’re going for mass market appeal, using the Abrahamic religion as your foundation nets you familiarity with a bit over 55% of the globe. The next highest is unaffiliated at 15.6% and includes a bunch of people who, while they may not believe in one of the Abrahamic religions likely belongs to a country where those are are least practiced.

    Hinduism is next at 15.2% and Buddism at 6.6%. Everything else combined makes up less than 9%.

    I get the appeal of wanting to go against the cultural consensus, but in terms of providing something familiar enough for the largest possible part of the population to have a familiar starting point; Cain and Abel is easily going to have the largest possible reach.

    From a business standpoint, unless you want to divorce vampires from religion entirely (akin the Underworld’s who are essentially a mutation; ergo work regardless of religious beliefs) or make Dracula your foundation (and even he becomes one via a deal with Satan in the novel), Abrahamic is definitely the best choice.

                Eh? WtA and Mage don't lean on Christian themes at all, and they sold lots and lots of books alongside VtM. There's no real indication that making the games specifically Christian themed actually made it a bigger selling product than it would have been with something else. There really isn't much evidence to back up the idea that you need to make TTRPGs that focus on Christian themes to be successful (D&D... most certainly isn't Christian in an direct way even if there are some obvious influences that come from its creators being from a Christian dominated country).

                Despite the fact that the primary TTRPG markets are in Christian majority countries, most explicitly Christian games don't really sell that well. The TTRPGs that involve a large amount of Christian themes and do well in sales and popularity tend to be ones that do more satirizing of Christianity than not.

                There's also the simple truth that you can't really cater to both major (in terms of population) monotheist faiths at the same time. If you make it too far in one direction you start losing interest from the faith relegated to the sidelines, and if you try to compromise you're far more likely to make nobody with it.

                    I don't think that Vampire has Christian elements because the creators wanted to market to Christians. The themes of the game and the general flavor isn't particularly appealing to people who care a lot about Christianity. I'm pretty certain that the Abrahamic vampiric creation myth comes from the fact that the creators grew up in a heavily Christian culture and the Old Testament carries emotional significance to them (and probably you too) and reminds us of ancientness, legends, and spirituality. Using a Biblical origin myth taps into a preexisting culture power that the Bible has and that isn't primarily a marketing decision, but a creative one.

                                                        In-character, the hegemonic vampire myths are pretty much the ones you’d expect to grow out of a predominantly Christian culture. People on the internet often claim that the game makes those myths objectively true, even though it’s been presenting them as myths since the very first edition, and the Book of Nod is obviously meant to be something written by people who grew up in a medieval Christian environment, in an attempt to mythologize their origins in a way they could understand.

                                                        Out-of-character, vampire lore and gothic style lean heavily on Christian elements for its aesthetics and themes, though not to the extent of actually endorsing the content of Christianity. It’s just a visual language people understand by osmosis and also another way to frame power.

                                                        Judaism is largely absent from the picture, as usual, except as filtered through the lens of Christianity and pop culture. Which is a shame, as specifically Jewish takes on the mythos would be interesting.
