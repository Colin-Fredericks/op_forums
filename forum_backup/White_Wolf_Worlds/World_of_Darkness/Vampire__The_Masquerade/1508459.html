    Have you Defended a City?

    04-15-2023, 01:38 PM
    It is one thing to defend a single building or small territory, but it is another to defend a town or city. Back in the day cities had walls as a default defensive measure against mortal armies, which in turn kept the shifters and other supernatural threats if not at bay, at least they had to consider them in their plans.

    But what about in the modern world? If you are a Prince/Baron/Cardinal and you influence the mortal world for a century or more, how do you guide the development of the city to promote ease of defense? The fluff states that every city has hordes of shifters circling in the unincorporated areas just waiting for kindred to step a toe outside of the city limits. Likewise, every splat seems to have a faction that lives in the same cities, so how do you set things up to counter them?

    The fluff is kind of obsessed with saying that vampires are city dwellers with loads of influence, endless resources, and that their enemies are legion. But somehow once the mortals stopped building walls around their cities, it seems that the vampires just gave up trying to do anything that dealt with known threats.

          In the wake of city growth and the ensuing change of defensive strategy, focusing more on the defense of forts around cities, many city walls were demolished. Also, the invention of gunpowder rendered walls less effective, as siege cannons could then be used to blast through walls, allowing armies to simply march through. Today, the presence of former city fortifications can often only be deduced from the presence of ditches, ring roads or parks. Furthermore, some street names hint at the presence of fortifications in times past, for example when words such as "wall" or "glacis" occur. In the 19th century, less emphasis was placed on preserving the fortifications for the sake of their architectural or historical value – on the one hand, complete fortifications were restored , on the other hand many structures were demolished in an effort to modernize the cities. One exception to this is the "monument preservation" law by the Bavarian King Ludwig I of Bavaria, which led to the nearly complete preservation of many monuments such as the Rothenburg ob der Tauber, Nördlingen and Dinkelsbühl. The focus shifted to enable the city to impose tolls on goods and, secondarily, also served to prevent the desertion of soldiers

Vertical concrete plates are put together so as to allow the least space in between them, and are rooted firmly in the ground. The top of the wall is often protruding and beset with barbed wire in order to make climbing them more difficult. These walls are usually built in straight lines and covered by watchtowers at the corners.

                          And this is probably the most you'd get of a siege in the traditional sense.

                          You don't siege a city in the World of Darkness. You invade a city like you'd invade territory, maybe, but you don't lay siege to it. You siege a building, if anything. Vampires could turn a building into a fortified strong point, like a toll booth castle.

                          But as with medieval sieges, taking the fort isn't the only thing an invading force would do. There's also all the stuff outside the fortified strongpoint that the Vampires inside care about. It's why when Sabbat declare a crusade on a city, they save Elysium for last in most cases. You destroy supply lines, sources of income, Domains, things the inhabitants of the city like (ex: art galleries for Toreador). Disrupt feeding grounds and Herds. Maybe even foment enough unrest to make the authorities impose martial law, so suddenly the native Kindred are inconvenienced by curfews, closed shops, and heightened security.

                          It's the modern equivalent of burning crops to force the local lord to ride out of his castle to face you.


                          All of this, I think, largely misses the point. Kindred don't do much to defend against Lupines because they aren't much of a threat anymore. In pre-modern times, the wilderness was deep and expansive. Lots of places for wolfmen to hide. Travel was slow going, so there was little hope of escape or outside aid if Lupines started beating down your door. (Or, for that matter, mundane soldiery. War was always a problem, hence why mortals built castle walls).

                          In modern times, the maps have been filled in and the forests have retreated from the cancerous growth of suburban sprawl. Kindred continue to tell their childer that horrifying werewolves stalk just beyond the city limits. Partly because Elders are slow to change and retain the fears from their younger days. But those with sense still perpetuate the threat in the minds of Kindred, regardless of how diminished Lupines are, because it serves their interests to keep Neonates where they can be watched. Rather than roaming around the endless rows of single family detached homes, growing broods and carving out Domains beyond the eyes and reach of their "betters".

                                                      Ways a prince could do well in a siege scenario

                                                      1- Actually be a popular prince that most would agree is the right man in charge.
                                                      2- have friends in the enemy faction
                                                      3- push your enemies into conflict in the name of the greater good/ punish them severely when they "rebel". Ideally you can also pretend to be fair and also assign your allies easy battles where they can accumulate credit
                                                      4- Be very generous to allies and unaligned parties you can win over, discount and deminish the war achievments of rivals and enemies.
                                                      5- If the Sabbat break the masquerade or too much trouble occurs in rival territory, use this as an excuse to take territory off them and reward an ally.
                                                      6: have shelters prepared beforehand. People will owe you big favours when they retreat into one.

                                                      As for guiding a city for the purpose of defense?
                                                      -Encourage lots of glass/shiny surfaces for the purpose of thwarting lasombra.
                                                      -Manage a shortlist of likely spots for enemy shelters.
                                                      -Have a fairly large area in the city where it's quiet at night and quite difficult to commit crimes, perhaps the oldtown or the finance sector, or somewhere near a well guarded government building. This is a good place to act -business as usual-. Ideally the Prince should be able to put up a strong front. It's not good for the prince to hide, as they're supposed to be leaders who can coordinate things. It's much better to put yourself in an area where the enemy dare not attack.
