                                Prior to 5E most Tzimisce were in the Sabbat. There were a handful in the Camarilla or the Anarchs, and then there were the Old Clan who were part of the True Black Hand. But most of them had little issue with the Sabbat, and indeed their monstrous nature typically made it difficult for them to function in the other Sects.

                                In 5E I believe the Sabbat Tzimisce, similar to the Lasombra, would have had a major split with many of them choosing to leave the Sabbat and join the Anarchs. Many Tzimisce were on the Path of Metamorphosis, which simply does not function at all, whatsoever, with the new Pact-based system that the Sabbat has devolved into. Those Tzimsci would have had little choice but to either leave their Path and get on one that was more Pack friendly, or leave the Sabbat, and since the Camarila doesn't welcome vampire on Paths, by default they would end up joining the Anarchs. I imagine the Anarchs largely have a "live and let" policy, and as long as these Tzimisce kept their horrors to their own domains and weren't attracting attention, the Anarchs would be more than happy to have them among their number.

                                Fortunately for the Brujah and the Ministry these Tzimisce would probably be more interested in their own personal studies and pursuit of Azi Dahka rather than in Sect politics, would tend to operate as allies kept at a slight distance rather than seeing the Tzimisce try to exert control over the Sect the way you're probably going to see the Lasombra do in the Camarilla.

A great many features of the Sabbat are basically just them defining themselves in opposition to the nascent Camarilla, not because the original Anarch Revolt actually entailed or required any of them.
