                            The two big emotional problems vampires face is the urge to eat to gluttony whenever even slightly hungry, and the urge to destroy when even slightly angry. All Paths, and I'm counting Humanity as a Path in this context, are attempts to fight this with intense belief.

                            Humanity - I will not eat to gluttony or rage at the slightest provocation because that would be evil.

                            Feral Heart - I will not eat to gluttony or rage at the slightest provocation because that is self-destructive and therefore beneath the cunning, forward-seeing, perfect predator I aspire to be.

                            Honorable Accord - I will not eat to gluttony or rage at the slightest provocation because that would violate my oaths, either directly or by rendering direct upholding of those oaths impossible.(In other words I can't protect the Sabbat if I am dead, running from the police, or have attracted hunters to the Sabbat with my foolishness. And I certainly can't uphold, say, my oath to protect the Archbishop if I attack him because he was slightly rude to me.)

                            That's why I've never understood the Path of Cathari, because it really doesn't seem to have a belief system that would prevent you from eating to gluttony at the slightest hunger or raging at the slightest provocation. Even the Path of Evil Revelations has better motive for that: I will not eat to gluttony or rage at the slightest provocation because that would be counterproductive to the interests of my master.

                                    Picking up from another thread...

                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                    The Sabbat seems to be the least free of any sect since, well, it's an army. It can really provide many benefits but freedom seems like the thing they can give least vs. the Anarchs.

                                                I agree a lot with Elphilim's take. One of the biggest problems I had with the Revised depiction of the Sabbat is that it basically ignored the Loyalists (and sympathetic moderates and opportunistic groups like the Panders who could go any way depending on what was at stake) so it could put "the Sword of Caine" onto the metaplot railroad that required lots of Crusades.

                                                There are certainly Sabbat that fit the "Sword of Caine" motif of Revised. They're called the Black Hand. There are a lot of ambitious and tyrannical elders who want the entire sect to be so centralized and obedient to them - primarily the elder Lasombra and Tzimisce of the episcopate along with some others. That's why there were the Sabbat Civil Wars. The sect is increasingly becoming more authoritarian, but the centralizers have not yet triumphed and they have a lot of opponents.

                                                But there are lots of other styles and groups of Sabbat out there. I imagine there are some Loyalist nomad packs or even covens that are practically indistinguishable from the Anarchs or even Camarilla loyal wandering Gangrel.

                                                I think it's an important issue of the Vaulderie and how often it is performed and with who. Some packs will perform it very rarely and only amidst themselves. I imagine many Loyalist nomad packs do this approach - they are very concerned about the freedom of its members. Then there will be packs that perform it more frequently, but still less so with others. And there's an entire spectrum where at the end are Sabbat that perform the Vaulderie all the time and with as many Sabbat as they can. I think many of the centralizers of the Sabbat encourage frequent and promiscuous Vaulderie as a way they can control other Sabbat, so a major issue of the Loyalists should be regulating these kind of calls.

                                                The organized militant hierarchy CTPhipps mentioned is one example of the Sabbat, but it is not the only. It needs a good mix of styles in order to show the diversity within the Sabbat, and how the sect's internal conflicts and contradicting impulses relate and interact.

                                                I think the game would benefit by having more By Night books that present different styles of Sabbat. Montreal was fine for for a stable, but hierarchical Sabbat really interested in outre occultism and spiritualism (though I find it a little too gonzo and should be toned down). But we need an example of a domain with a more practical tyrannical Sabbat approach of elders wanting to keep tight central control. And a place where Loyalist Sabbat dominant and show the sect in an aspect the idealistic remnants of the Anarch Revolt would like. These would all be very different styles of Sabbat, but all very much Sabbat.

                                                But I prefer a Sabbat that is highly divided and struggles with the antagonisms different factions produce with each other. I think it offers more opportunity for roleplay, but within a Sabbat only game and as an element in non-Sabbat games.

                                                    I think it was always the biggest mistake of the writing of the Anarchs to make them considered to be part of the Camarilla because I always felt that it nerfed them to the point that the Sabbat ate a huge chunk of what I consider to be the Movement's appeal. If you are actually against the Camarilla's feudal system of government, if you think the Camarilla is a bunch of tyrants and portray their leadership as bad guys, then it seems like the Anarchs just sit around doing absolutely nothing.

                                                    Part of this is the set up. With the exception of the Anarch Free States (established through violent revolution), there are no Anarch Free States. The Brujah Council was destroyed prior to 1st Edition in the backstory. The Camarilla doesn't negotiate with Anarchs. Not since the Treaty of Thorns, which could charitably be called the Anarchs surrendering after being beaten by the Elders. Which is not again anything for someone who wants to play defiant rebels.

                                                    Basically, the portrayal of the Anarchs form Second Edition to Revised emphasized them as the Camarilla's prison bitches. The Anarchs were even fighting tooth and claw by the side of the Camarilla's flunkies against the Sabbat. They're also doing this seemingly for no reward or benefit despite the fact that they're on the frontlines in 1999. This actually gets shown in 1999's WINTER'S TEETH where the Anarchs discuss fighting the Sabbat and how the Camarilla immediately turned on them afterward (which I feel is very appropriate and believable for the Ivory Tower).

                                                    I feel like having the Anarchs successfully overthrow multiple cities and take a hardline stance against the Camarilla actually makes them more dramatically interesting even in places where the Camarilla and Anarchs are being peaceful. In Chicago, say, you have Kevin Jackson tolerate Anita Wainwright and the other Anarchs because he CAN'T kill them and has to negotiate with them because war is a bad thing.

                                                    Not because he wouldn't kill them if he could.

                                                    That's my take at least.

                                                    Because a simmering Cold War that can break out into a hot war is better than a "peace" that has the Anarchs utterly powerless before the system.

                                                        My problem with the current setup is that even though the anarchs are not necessarily the good guys, they are infinitely more reasonable than any of the alternatives, and the no-brainer choice for every character who isn't a total shitbird. When they were presented as part of the Camarilla, I honestly found them a lot more sympathetic, because at least back then the anarchs had one major flaw: they were too decentralized and disorganized to ever affect any real change, at least on a scale larger than the example provided in the Anarch Free State.

                                                        Now that the anarchs are apparently organized, effective, and poised to make Anarch Free States the default model of society for all Kindred, what role can the other sects have but be pure antagonists? Why would any sensible person who just wants to unlive and let unlive ever choose the Camarilla over the Anarchs? What could the Sabbat possibly offer to anyone but total psychopaths when the anarchs make real equality, modern systems of government, the democratic process, and freedom from Blood Bonds look easy? I think this is what people mean when they say that the game treats the anarchs with kid gloves: they don't have a major flaw like the other sects do.

                                                        If there is no sensible reason why a character would choose another sect over the Anarch Movement, I feel like the sect has failed as a concept. One of the reasons why I like the old World of Darkness is that a newly made vampire only has crap options to choose from. Like every person in the real world, vampires find themselves subject to vast structures of law and tradition invented hundreds of years before they were born. It's up to the players to make something of the shit sandwich they are dealt at the start. I don't need a faction that is obviously morally superior to every other option and effective at pursuing its goals and objectives as well.

                                                            I think the major problem with the Anarchs after 1994 was the lack of specific examples of what they intended to do. Simply being against "the Man" only goes so far. We needed some specific groups and their aims. If all they are is "replace the Prince with a better Prince", there's only so much mileage you can get out of it. A lot of us expected Los Angeles By Night to be that sourcebook where we'd see these ideas in print. It wasn't. Even first edition Chicago By Night gave us different factions in the form of Maldavis, Hind's Socialists, and Juggler's Nihilists. It was still unclear what exactly they intended to replace Lodin with (or more ambitiously, the entire structure of the Inner Circle, Justicars, Conclaves, etc.), but at least we understood each stood for something different. The Barons of the LA Free States were just thugs as petty Princes with not much differentiating them except for personalities. We've discussed this on several threads now.

                                                            I also agree that the depiction in Revised was not the best. It makes complete sense for the Anarchs and Camarilla to tactically team up against their mutual enemy, the Sabbat. But after surviving the initial attack, the Anarchs shouldn't have just shown up to help like elves (or Rohirrim cavlary charge if you prefer) at the siege of Helm's Deep. Anarch representatives would have cut a deal. "We'll help you take New York, but in exchange we keep Brooklyn as a Free State." or "Of course we'll help you take back Buffalo and Atlanta. We'll do that by attacking Baltimore ourselves. That'll free up enough of your forces to take those other two cities, and we'll defeat the Sabbat all together in three of them. That's what this is about, right? Of course, we expect the Justicar to acknowledge our domain at the next Conclave. Which needs to be held right now with all of us here. Just to make it official and on the record." The Camarilla will still try to wiggle out of their commitments afterwards, but at least the Anarchs won't seem stupid.

                                                            The Anarchs have their elders and ancillae too, just like the Camarilla have their neonates. Anarch elders aren't going to be foot soldiers. They lead their own foot soldiers.

                                                            But the Anarchs as a sect weren't important to the metaplot railroad in Revised so they got the shaft just like so many other concepts did. Lots of things were simply ignored because they were inconvenient for the plot. It's why I quickly sorrowed at Revised and stopped buying sourcebooks.
