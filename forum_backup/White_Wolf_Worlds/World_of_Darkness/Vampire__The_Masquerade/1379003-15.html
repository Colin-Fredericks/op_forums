                            When the V20 corebook first came out, I got my group together and we ran a Tal'mahe'Ra game (this was before the v20 Guide to the TMR was released). I was able to whip out my well-worn Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand and use that, and the players were able to use older Disciplines and we just kind of eyeballed them and updated them into the new edition.

                            5th edition though has undergone enormous setting and mechanics changes though that I couldn't really do that. I mean, does the Tal'mahe'Ra even still exist? If it does, what does it look like in light of the Beckoning? How would elder Disciplines work in the new paradigm? I guess I could just try and homebrew all of that, but it would be a lot more work than I'd be willing to put in. Alternatively I could wait a year (or two, or five or possibly even more) for them to release the new rules and setting info on those things. Or as a third alternative I could just not play 5th edition and stick to the older editions (which, so far, is what my group has primarily decided to do).

                            I understand, of course, that White Wolf is functionally a new studio, they want to put their own spin on the setting, and also from a simple economic point of view, it's more profitable if players can't just buy and use older editions of the books and instead have to purchase the new products. Please don't think I'm ragging on White Wolf or 5th edition in that regard, because I'm not. Still, there's a certain level of aggravation in the rollover to 5th edition that didn't happen with the rollovers to the previous new editions, since the setting and rules were all mostly similar enough that you could get by with the old material until newer stuff was released.

                            Right now, at least with 5th edition, there's really only a couple of playstyles that are possible (Camarilla/Anarch neonates), and since I don't see them releasing a Guide to the Sabbat or Guide to the Elders type books any time in the near future, those will probably remain the only real playstyles for the foreseeable future.

                                                Fair enough, @MyWifeIsScary

                                                In any case, "Why not the Imbued?"

                                                I know, crazy, getting back to the original topic. I think the answer to that question is the fact that the Imbued are supernatural. It's the same issue that the Technocracy, Werewolves, or some sort of vampiric patron have with the undead. The Second Inquisition is based around two concepts that I think are intristic to its existence.

                                                1:] Do not introduce too much too soon

                                                One of the things that V5 has going for it is the fact it's a "back to basics" approach with the idea that newcomers don't have to absorb a very large amount of lore. It just wants to introduce vampires, some starting clans, and Hunters. A huge amount of content is chucked out, which isn't very good for longtime fans but this is meant to be a Starter Set for people. You can't introduce any of the supernatural factions of other gamelines without getting into the kudzu plot of lore that all of these groups are.
                                                Who, what, when, where, how, and so on of the Imbued is a whole book itself.

                                                2:] To make mortals scary again

                                                Carmilla and Dracula were the original vampire stories with both of them having plucky mortals put an end to their sinister villains. Vampire hunters are a inseperable piece of vampire lore and something that is at the core of the whole "Masquerade" concept. The idea that vampires cannot control humanity, just influence it, and that if they are ever found out then they will be exterminated by humanity.

                                                I honestly think they went too far with the SI the same way I think V5 did the Sabbat wrong. I think the SI is too big and powerful for the Masquerade to hold. I also feel its writing is a bit schizophrenic in how many people exist. However, I love the concept because vampires being on the backfoot is something that really adds a new layer to the game. Every edition should add something to the game and the SI is certainly an element that was previously overlooked. Hunters now have a lot more tools at their disposal and it allows new enemies as well as complications to the lives of PCs.
