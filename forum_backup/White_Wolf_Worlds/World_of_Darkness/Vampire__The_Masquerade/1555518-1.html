    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

    I think my favorite change to the Black Hand was one of the most random: the fact the Black Hand was letting the Baali join them. It was such a random and bizarre thing with the fact that Setites and Tremere aren't allowed that it almost certainly confirmed that the Aralu were probably the Baali founders instead of Antediluvians or at least the Tal'mahe're was compromised. However, it's a great moment for the players who want to be part of the Black Hand because it almost certainly is a sign that a car crash is about to happen and there's nothing you can do but bail out. Unfortunately, bailing out will almost certainly get you killed too.

    Basically, it gave a real stakes to being part of the vampire "Inner Illuminati" because you're now aware of it about to go boom.

    Similarly, I liked the idea of the Black Hand becoming a public sect in the Middle East as detailed in BJD. While not intended for 5th Edition, you can argue that the Black Hand is now at war with the True Hand in the Middle East and that is the public face of the Gehenna War. All of those Elders summoned by the Beckoning could be summoned by the Antediluvians but they might also simply be summoned by Ancients of the Black Hand either with their Presence 8 and 9 or Blood Sorcery.

    Michael, the Dracon, and Mary the Black might be some of these new True Hand/Baali behind this doing battle with the Sabbat and Ur-Shulgi's Shepherds with the Ashirra just wondering what the hell is going on and fighting all three sides.

    i haven't read Gehenna War but it certainly provides an actual coherent answer for what the Gehenna War could be without breaking canon.
    Agree on that one, the molochim Baali in the Tal'mahe'Ra is a rich source of stories and it was really well done by the writers. Now on the subject, the Baali really hate each other. Moloch's line never wanted what Shaitan/Nergal did back in 2nd ed (almost "Open the Way" and the destruction of Thera) and it was one of them who betrayed him (Hay Tau). Then again after Carthage it was Cybele of Shaitan's line who sealed Moloch and Troile. Becoming the most hated and hunted Cainites of them all but also the most proficient in dark lore made a great deal of sense to be recruited by the Tal'mahe'Ra. The introduction of Tanitbaal Sahar back in Road of Sin gave the Baali a different perspective beyond just being "evil". Gotta add that the Wyrd Sisters (Myranda Kalis, Sarah Roark and Janet Trautvetter) had a site back in the day were they posted amazing stories of their characters before DA Revised. I remember this is where I read about Marcus Justinus Camilus (changed to Titus Venturus in DA revised) before the release of Road of Sin. Sadly I never managed to save it, hope it's still there ("A World lit by fire" IIRC).

    The war between Hands I believe was introduced back in Caine's Chosen: The Black Hand (revised). Kazimir Savostin went to Jalan Aajav after the sect fell to the Smiling Lord's relic nuclear weapons (Ends of Empire). Lets recall that Dastur Anosh wasn't introduced yet, nor the Weeping Master, there was only the weeping stone which was believed to be Zillah.

    On V5's Beckoning I just see it as an excuse to remove the elders until rules were done for them and that still hasn't been published yet as to the why's and how's so we will have to create our own stories around for it.

    ur-Shulgi at war with the Ashirra makes sense, since it was him who purged the Assamites in favor of islam in the first place. I believe that the original idea was to make the methuselah the "third Baali" but that was later dropped from the Clanbook Assamite revised. I just posted on my blog an old entry that was left out that might changed everything if considered canon.

    On Gehenna War, same here, haven't read it so I hope its good because it really needs to explain a lot.

                                    Re: The Tzimsice

                                    I think the Tzimisce have always kind of been out of sync with the rest of the gameline and it was primarily because fans did enjoy gross out enemies that they managed to linger on as much as they do. As Necroscope vampires, the Tzimisce were always treated as "alien" and "weird" in a game that really didn't focus on that save as a function of Paths. Which means that they really weren't any more alien or weird than other Sabbat/Roads. The Soul Eaters, the Eldest, and attempts to make Vicissitude somehow cursed were substituted but the Tzimisce were always just "out there" in a way that didn't work in a game about Humanity vs. The Beast.

                                    I think there was also the fact that body modification being demonized by the Tzimisce (as a sign of how EVIL and ALIEN) they are also was noticed by a larger chunk of the fandom than people really ever intended, probably due to the queer and counterculture Goth fandom being a larger majority than in other gamelines. Paradox certainly noticed this and we've had quite a few trans characters since then but it was the same thing that happened with Cyberpunk 2020/2070 and cyberpsychosis. Basically, a lot of people really would love Vicissitude in real life.

                                    The Dragon Tzimisce are just more INTERESTING than their prior selves.​

                                            1. I think Mithras was going to be just sort of footnote in the World of Darkness if not for the fact that Matthew Dawkins managed to make him a character that had a complete arc across multiple editions. Other people used Mithras like Phillipe Boulle in the Victorian Age Vampire trilogy but I believe that the Gentleman Gamer is the guy who really set up the story in BJD that was paid off in Fall of London. Also, the creation of the Cult of Mithras as an actual religious group (and probably Path/Road) in [i}Cults of the Blood Gods[/i]. You may not like Fall of London's presentation or as a whole but I think that the central premise works very well. Also, the fact that the ending of the game with London as a Camarilla, Mithras Cult, or Second Inquisition victory are all on the players.

                                            (I'm not sure if this is canon but I also think The Gentleman Gamer may have been setting up Roger De Camden as the guy behind the Redcoats preparing to take over Boston for the British Camarilla as the ultimate villain of Swansong--its a nice tie in to The Fall of London if you want to do it that way)

                                            2. The Web of Knives retcon was mentioned above but it's interesting to really think about the idea of them being a bunch of murderous assholes in-universe and the Road of Blood being sort of a proto-Sabbat of diablerie hungry apocalypse warriors that are really antithetical to the clan as a whole. The Shepherds of Ur-Shulgi have a different name but I like that they exist in 5th Edition because it really does give a very "cheap" enemy that tabletop RPGs sometimes need. They devoted themselves to Ur-Shulgi above all things and now he's just gone. Alamut is gone. So, really, they're a shadow of their former self. I feel like if they hadn't been in Cult of the Blood Gods, a good chunk of the context for the Banu Haqim would have been lost.

                                            3. This is going back a long time but I think Justin Achilli deserves some credit for the revising of the Giovanni into the absolutely fucked up and demented take on them that he presented. I remember the original Giovanni felt like Diet Tremere with an Italian bent. Everyone treated them just like mafia and Italian. The expansion into the most personally fucked up family with branches all over the globe really made the Giovanni Chronicles possible. Are the Cappadocians and Hecata better? I mean, yes. There's some issues I have with the Hecata (there's no real need to have gotten rid of the Samedi and Nagaraja) but the clan that had already been rescued from being one note to being one of my favorites.

                                                            The defection plot is badly done, that's for sure. But I actually think it makes sense for them to come from the Sabbat.

                                                            Simply put, "real" Lasombra are a very poor fit in the sect. While the Tzimisce turned into something that not quite fit, but definitely works, the Lasombra "Ventrue-like" culture makes little sense in there unless you're one of the really successful ones. In the Ashirra they already have a stable place to fit in, I'm not sure if it makes sense for them to switch to the Camarilla in numbers.

                                                            I also as a rule of thumb think the Sabbat should have a large defection rate, precisely because it's a very "love it or die" group. Anyone who stays (and those would still be numerous) would be die hard loyal, if not straight up fanatics, but a lot of people would look for an escape opportunity. And I don't really buy the idea that the Sabbat is so effective at hunting down deserters, that's not easy and they have better things to do. So add the two and I do think Sabbat Lasombra defecting to the Camarilla isn't all that uncommon.

                                                            But not the main source of their numbers, only maybe for older ones. Instead their main source is good old Embrace.

                                                            Hecata are overall a good change to the lineages of Death. I think their unified mechanics is a bad thing, but their lore overall is one of the highlights of V5. And I said previously, while it's kinda strange for all those lines to come together, sacrificing Augustus does the trick for me, not because of any magical shenanigans, but because he is the main source of bad blood between most of them.

                                                            My only complaints are about following Revised footsteps with the Nagaraja and keeping them isolated from their original lore. While DSotBH is bonkers to no end if taken in its entirety, I think a second Mage-related lineage in addition to the Tremere, but with a different relationship with their living colleagues, was a really neat idea (and you can frame them as "witches and wizards" or whatever while in a Vampire-only book).
