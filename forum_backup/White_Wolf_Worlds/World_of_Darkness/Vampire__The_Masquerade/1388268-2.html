    "This is ridiculous! What am I doing here? I'm in the wrong story!" -the Baker's Wife, Into the Woods, Stephen Sondheim


    First, a couple of points. Don't think of them as criticism, but just some friendly comments from a critic. So, umm, yeah. Definitionally, they're criticisms.

    Originally posted by omenseer View Post
    In the game I run my players threw a curve at me. A friend of the teen that they're Guardian/Adopted parent of was suffering nightmares, bouts of temper, feeling like they were being watched. The kid was a kinfolk on the verge of turning into a garou and their first change and was being observed by a pack of bone gnawers.
    Are you familiar with Chekhov's Gun? If not, look here. It has been preached again and again on gaming forums for years... decades. Economy of detail. If there is a story element introduced, be prepared for it to matter because you're the one who put it there. Most of what players do in a game is sit there trying to figure out which parts matter and which don't. You gave them this kid, which implied he mattered. From the players' perspective, they were doing what you wanted.

    Once Garou were introduced to the story, Garou existed. It's a little like the genie and the lamp; you wished them into existence, now deal with it.

    It's also why cross-overs are a bad idea, but going on a tangent in which my player's pcs were turned into garden gnome statues would violate... wait for it... economy of detail.

    Originally posted by omenseer View Post
    The idea was they would take the kid in for a few days, keep an eye on him, get attacked, and either fend off the lupines or, more likely, take the kid and run from these mysterious pursuers. They would harry and herd them and then when they were pinned he would undergo his first change and they would subdue him and then the older lupines would take him away.
    And, this is why railroading your players is a bad idea. Not just because it makes for a boring game where the players feel like their choices don't matter, but because said railroad never, ever runs on time. You run the game world, but players run the game.

    Originally posted by omenseer View Post
    Instead, thinking the kid was marked and now both the kid and their teen were in the know about things supernatural and what little they did know about lupines being that they can be relentless, decided to embrace them to give them an edge in survival. I figured it would be a slim chance that the embrace would stick, kinfolk being resilient/blessed against such things. I rolled and the kid survived the embrace.
    And, over the loudspeakers the station manager just announced, "The 4:10 to Yuma will be leaving track 7 at midnight, an express bound for Tokyo. All aboard!"

    Your next mistake was rolling for it. Do you roll to see if gravity still works? Do you roll to see if the Atlantic Ocean is still wet? You did not want the embrace to stick, it shouldn't have stuck.

    Originally posted by omenseer View Post
    Thoughts and opinions would be welcome. What would the lupine's response be? Retaliation and rage seems appropriate, but what scale? Full on war? Does this include just that one pack or does it include all of the bone gnawers in the Culver City/Greater Los Angeles area? Also, what about the other vampires in the area? Will it drag others into the mess?

    Thank you.
    Okay. Deep breath. We're all friends here. Come in off that ledge. Here. Have a warm beverage. Should I turn on some calming music?

    Your chronicle is not ruined. You don't need to kill the whole party. This can be salvaged.

    Ideas:

    Dirty Cops Not every Garou is a paragon of honor. The Bone Gnawers were a pack that was on thin-ice to begin with. In losing track of this kid --their one and only duty for the sept-- they fucked up big time. They will kill the kid. They will tell themselves it is to put him out of his misery, not to cover up their failure. Killing witnesses and destroying all physical evidence is just... umm, protecting the veil or something. It's totally not that they panicked. Where will they claim the kid went? Who knows, it's no longer the players' problem. (cf "economy of detail")

    Clap if you believe in Lupines! This is VtM, where "Garou" do not exist, but "Lupines" do exist. Very different creatures. The players may have read every WtA book ever written. One player might even have a tribe glyph tattooed on their leg. Doesn't matter. The pcs know nothing about Lupines. Are Lupines organized, or psychotic marauders? Are they inflexible, or willing to deal? Who knows? Only you! I suggest that in your world Lupines believe Gaia preserves those who deserve the first change. The unworthy die before then. This is not unreasonable given the appalling rate of infant mortality prior to 1900, and Lupines seeming disregard for pointless comabt. Your Lupines shrug and figure the kid was weak and deserved to die. Cold, man, cold, but remember the weak make lousy warriors.

    Crossing the Glienicke Bridge The Lupines and the Kindred of LA had a treaty, which forbids killing full members of the other species in enemy territory. Both sides would like to continue to have a treaty. Some Shadow Lords kill the Bone Gnawers for failing to hide the kid, who was not technically covered under the treaty since he was in vampire-land. They also demand the lives of the coterie, so they can claim some sort of victory. (Consequences, dammit!) This now becomes a more manageable story of vampire v. vampire as the coterie has to convince other vampires not to hand them over. In the end, the Shadow Lords will accept any vampires the coterie can round up... or embrace; they just need the body count, not justice. Use this to thin out your cast list. (nb economy of detail)

    What Rough Beast...? The kid looks dead at first, but Abominations are weird like that. The kid arises, in Crinos form with vampiric disciplines, but Humanity zero and in permanent frenzy. "This is bad, this is bad, this is very, very bad!", says everybody on both sides. Actually, "shouts everyone", as they run away. The Bone Gnawers and the coterie realize they have created a monster, that must be destroyed. The next session is a bug-hunt which looks a lot like the movie Alien. Everyone works together to end this... Abomination. One session, then it's over. Make sure the Bone Gnawers all die, so no other Lupines hear what happened.

            There is no canon treaty in Los Angeles. New Orleans by Night had a Lupine-Leech treaty, though, so there is precedent.

            If it were my chronicle, I'd say the vampires and lupines drew up a treaty years ago. They divvied up Los Angeles. Each are allowed to pass through the others' territory. No permanent homes or vamp/wolf violence allowed if you're on foreign territory, though terra nullis is fair play. The rules only apply to the splats themselves, not kinfolk or ghouls. Vamps can feed anywhere, if they're discreet. Lupines can blow up all the oil refineries they want.

            Where are the lines? It doesn't matter. They are wherever you need them to be to prevent the pcs from ever, ever needing to go there again. This effectively writes Lupines out of your chronicle. At a guess, I'd say Simi Valley, Thousand Oaks, and, obviously, everything east of the 15 belong to the Lupines. Angeles National Forest is almost too obvious to mention.

            One successful Politics roll will allow a member of the coterie to "remember" this treaty. Except, never mind. Never, never hinge your plot on a single roll unless you are willing to follow through. (Learning is fun!)

            This keeps the Lupines existing in the world, but out of the story.

            You tell me the details. It's your chronicle. OTOH, maybe don't have details, so you can make it up as you go along.

                    Originally posted by omenseer View Post
                    In my defense I would say it was less of a railroad and more of a failure on my part to anticipate player possibilities.
                    And that has happened to every ST, GM, and DM in the hobby.

                    It happens.

                    Did I mention my coterie of statues? I decided to cross-over a Euthanatos mage who revered Greek myths. All rotes emulated a mythical figure's powers... Hercules, Sirens, Delphic Oracle, etc. I only thought of medusa when the pcs started buying mirrors. (My out, btw, was that they reverted at the next sunset, per usual. They took out the mage with a sniper rifle.)

                    As far as player agency goes, yes, players should control their characters. You, however, control the rest of the world. As long as you don't directly contradict any previously established facts of the world, it is fair play. In the normal course of play the ST is constantly expanding the universe. That bar they went to for the first time? It didn't exist last week. That cabbie they fed on, but rolled a botch? He wasn't a gangster's brother until they rolled. The chandelier filled with burning candles in the Prince's haven hanging from a weak chain? The chain was fine until a pc rolled a critical success on an attack roll.

                    All fair play.

                    Bottom lining on this, what brought your original post to the forum was how to handle things now that a story element got out of control and threatened to derail the story altogether. My advice? As much as possible write the Lupines out of the story due to previously unknown outside curcumstances or explain them as being something manageable, while not making it too, too obvious

                    Or, have the pcs massacred in a tpk. I'm guessing the players would rather lose their agency than their characters.
