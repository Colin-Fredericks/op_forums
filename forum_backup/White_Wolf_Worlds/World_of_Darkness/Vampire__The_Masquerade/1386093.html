    Sample locations in a city of World of Darkness

    05-06-2020, 11:56 AM
    There are a lot of 101 posts around (character ideas, plots, ...). The idea I had here was similar but for locations in a city. I plan on running a game of vampire set in a modern city. I looked up the city online, did my research and created the main vampire NPC's. I feel OK with what I wrote and prepared.

    Since I'm not an experienced Storyteller I need some help with "random possiblities" that my players could ask or interact or visit. There are some sample NPC's I found in the official books (police officer, vagrant, politician, even some ghouls, werevolves and vampires...) I need help with possible locations that aren't signature for a city but every (or almost every city) has them.

    There is a list I found in a Requiem book: Damnation City (link below). Its really good and it will help me a lot but there are a lot more "shady locations" that I can use for my city in a World of Darkness. Places where regular mortals would hang out, or vampires could use or other supernaturals would like to control/influence. That is where you fine people come in...

    Can you please help me fill up more of those locations. I thought of adding places like: library, museum, firestation, car wash, abandoned playground, shady bar, crack den ...

          If I may make one suggestion, don't focus on a simple list of locations. It's unlikely the pcs will make it to every location, and you will have put a lot of energy into story ideas you're not going to use.

          (If, however, you can manage to railroad the pcs to all of them, in alphabetical order, you will be my STing hero!... jk)

          Anyway, back to focus. Focus on your story needs, and how your locations can propel the story forward. Maybe, you could describe a particular scene you expect to need a setting for? And, what role the scene plays in the overall narrative? Also, what you want the mood to be?

          In the example below, note how the setting serves the scene, not the other way around.

          The players have scheduled a meeting with the Primogen to accuse a nomadic fledgling of poaching on the coterie's domain, killing favored members of the herd. The coterie has been less than perfectly loyal to the Camarilla in the past, and now they come to the primogen... hats in hand... looking for the sect's rules to be enforced to the coterie's benefit. The pcs want the primogen to scare the fledgling into staying out of their domain. The coterie knows this could, in theory, lead to an execution, since the fledgling is in town illegally.

          You want the scene to highlight the similarities between the elders and the pcs, as they begin to sell out to the Camarilla. Also, there should be a mood of tenseness, since the pcs have previously scoffed at the rigid laws of the Kindred, but now want to use them to their own advantage. Will the elders gleefully declare the pcs outlaws, or will they be smiling like the cat that ate the canary, knowing the pcs have taken one more step toward loyalty to the system?

          For this, the meeting could be hosted in the first-class lounge of an abandoned train station. The arrival departure/board is still hanging. Instead of cities, it has the names of local kindred chalked on it, roughly in order of Status. The coterie notice they have moved up a slot or two. The lounge itself has been made presentable, but work is ongoing. Most of the coffered woodceiling is clamped into place and could fall at any time. The wallpaper has been temporarily pasted back up after damage from a recent rainfall. The rest of the station outside the lounge is so bad it should be condemned... only bribes and threats of city officials has presented such a fate. Dozens of flickering railroad lanterns light the space. Almost, but not quite, enough to require a frenzy roll.

          If the players fail to convince the elders, they (and the fledgling) are told to handle their own disputes, since justice is for the loyal. All the outcasts are politely, but firmly, shown the door. This leads down a long, crumbling concrete ramp into a baggage-handling cellar. A series of open archways put them on level with the tracks. Rats are fighting in the cinders outside. A gunshot and a siren can be heard in the distance.

          If the players convince the elders to help them, the scene will shift to the Ladies Lounge. This lies through a carved marble archway, and the room beautifully restored to its beaux arts glory. Gold chandeliers, flocked wallpaper, and mahogany floors abound. As the players walk in, however, they notice the floor is spongy and uneven. Is it even safe to walk on? The fledgling is lead through a service door and down a long ramp.... but to what fate?

              Having settings to draw on though can inspire and some settings are the backdrop for several scenes. such as the club that the PC's mentor/Mwala owns will be a setting they visit quit often. I do agree that having the setting set the scene is a really good tool in an STs arsinal and i tend to use that a lot, but sometimes it is nice to not devote a ton of brainpower to create all of them and just pull a setting out of a hat. or have a bunch of settings to look at that may inspire scenes to come.

              Another reason to have some settings pre-set is to give the PCs something to strive or fight over fighting over a Setting can drive politics in vampire as feeding grounds buildings that have major importance politically and influentially in the setting is good to help create these dynamics even f the PCs have only heard of them and have never been their.

              But I don't want to stop by and not contribute. I will leave this system agnostic so anyone can use it if they wish.
              3rd Street Diner

              Description: The diner on third street is a small but well known establishment. It was built in the 50's and quickly became a hot spot for the local high school kids to hang out at night as one of the few places in town to get a burger and dance to the jukebox that never seemed to stop playing. As time moved on and the crowed shifted with the years so did the type of clientele that met at the diner. Kids though stayed the main clientele but the amount of drugs that filtered into the place started to grow. The area around the diner faded into despair and the diner itself took on a less safe atmosphere. Now it is but a shadow of its former self. After shutting down in 2004 it became a boarded up shadow of its former self. Today it is a crack house of some repute owned by Tyson Woodly and his south-side crew.

              Possible stories: Several stories can come form this Tyson maybe a vampire himself and is influential in the south-side gangs. Maybe he is the Primogen or a street level vampire that buts up next to the PCs domain and is currently trying to politic to expand his territory by taking the PCs territory from them. In Werewolf this could be the hive of a BSD that is slowly tailoring banes and feeding them into south-side neighborhoods in the PCs domain and the PCs now have to worry about this Werewolf, they could help him and gain a powerful ally who may prove more trouble than his worth or hinder him and clean up their domain so that the blood will flow more freely with untainted blood.

                          I actually considered making a thread like this a while back, but never got around to it. Since we've got one now, though...


                          Roadie's Bar
                          • 50s-style bar and grill, with a heavy car theme. Walls are covered in license plates and photographs/illustrations of cars from that era (think fins, polished chrome, etc). A whole sports car (or at least its outside) has been cut in half and planted above the front door, back end outside and front end inside.
                          • Has a jukebox in the corner, but also has a tiny stage for musicians (comfortably fits soloist, uncomfortably sits a rock band). Mostly gets used by a rockabilly band that are friends with the bar owner, and have been performing Saturdays for as long as anyone can remember. Sometimes gets used for up-and-coming bands, but only if they can bend their "sound" to the bar's theme.
                          • Owner - the eponymous Roadie (nickname) - was getting up in years back in the 90s. If he's still working in 2020, it's as an old curmudgeon. May have been forced out of retirement due to economic downturns. How he stays in business is anyone's guess (read: mystery to be solved). Definitely knows where multiple bodies are buried. Nostalgic for the road-rolling days of his youth.
                          • Heard in the Background: "Life is a highway..."
                          Green Lion Antiques
                          • Reference to Silent Hill
                          • Antique store, full of gently used furniture, knick knacks, pawned personal items, and general sentimental junk
                          • Owned by an alchemist (may or may not be Awakened, may or may not be Children of Knowledge/Solificati); sells herbal and alchemical substances from the back room, only to customers who specifically ask about it (and who have proper references from existing clients)
                          • Secret passage leads to tunnel connecting to property across the street
                          • Relies on revenue from alchemical sales to get by, when it isn't tourist season
                          • Will buy vampire blood, fangs, ashes

                              I'd only create locations that you know YOU as ST will be using to set scenes in, or as important locations of NPCs for purposes of havens, meeting places, influence centers, etc.

                              Anything that is needed for random purposes (the PCs want to go to X, regardless of whether X is a new nightclub, bar, fish & tackle shop, book store, motel, florist, etc.) can be thought up on the fly. Rather than put all the burden on yourself, feel free to go back to the PCs and collaborate on any essential features together. And you only need to put as many details as needed for the purposes of the PCs. All you need to do is veto anything unreasonable, and make necessary changes to better fit your setting. That way you can come up with things on the fly when you need them, the Players creativity can be used, and they feel like they are contributing to the setting.

                                  I try to do a bit of a balance between both approaches that have been mentioned. If I have some locations I think there's a decent chance the players might want to visit but isn't necessary, I'll try to write a few sentences about it to help with improvising. A random name list helps with the improv, too. For maybes just having a little something to work with can help keep the flow of the game going instead of just trying to come up with everything on the fly. Obviously you can't anticipate everywhere they might want to go, but a few sentences can really help. You can also reuse some of them in other cities if the players never visited them before or maybe decide to develop them further into a plot location later if you have an interesting idea.

                                  For the firehouse, here's something that actually happened at one a few blocks from where I live now back in the '90s. The cops in the neighborhood would bust people for drugs, then take some of them and bring them to the firehouse where the firemen would sell the drugs again out the back. Oftentimes they'd just let people go after taking their stuff so there'd be no paperwork. The firemen and cops would split the money, which was all profit. They could even take and resell the same drugs multiple times

                                      I think developing locations can be useful in a modular sense. If you don't use a location here, you can use it somewhere else. Remember, the game world is an entirely constructed space. How it all connects together is up to the Storyteller. If PCs need to go to a place, you can MAKE a location be where the adventure is. I don't see that as railroading, so much as efficient use of tools you already have.

                                      Moreover, as DrHappyAngry hinted at, there are levels of detail one can get into when making locations. It could be a richly developed location with decades of history, a dozen NPCs, and secrets hidden in the very foundations. Or it could be a loosely formed spot with a general theme and some notes for details that could be fleshed out as needed. The latter is the approach I took with the entries I posted earlier. Brief, broad strokes, leaves plenty of room for improv or elaboration.

                                      (For a further illustration of this approach, I might recommend this article from the Cyborgs and Sorcerers blog. It's about fantasy hexcrawls, yes, but its base conceits could be applied just as easily to an urban environment. Less rigid adherence to geography or distance, more about going into enough detail for functional use, and establishing connections to other locations. That's another important bit about designing adventures in any game: the players will be following breadcrumbs either way, so leave trails from where they are to places you've created.)

                                          Well, if what you really want is some plug-and-play locations, here goes.

                                          Furever Hearts Animal Shelter

                                          Proprietors: Joanie Zygumski, and Sarah Townley, DVM

                                          Ten years ago, Joanie retired from a corner-office corporate position. Sarah quit her job at a high-end vet office. She and Sarah used their nest-egg to pursue their lifelong dream of helping stray dogs, and they bought this place. Neither of them really knows much about fund-raising or grant writing. The couple have taken out mortgages on their house, and poured every penny into the shelter. They would love to run a no-kill shelter or simply fix the place up, but the funds just aren't there.

                                          Joanie is a tall, bony woman in her late 60's. She speaks with a clipped, suburban accent. Her movements are firm and intentional, like the military veteran she is. She has tan, heavily wrinkled skin, grey eyes, and a white buzz cut. She favors heavily patched khakis, and faded polo shirts. Joanie has a verbal tic: she repeatedly explains everything she is doing, as if those around her might misinterpret her actions. eg: "Now, then. I'll get your file to maintain our records. Yes, here it is. Let me make a note of your purchase, and remind you of our special on dog adoption starter kits. We have a special on starter kits. You'll need one." Main Goals: Keep this place afloat, as it is clearly the last important thing she will do before she dies. Be the perfect wife to Sarah, as Joanie is terrified she will die alone.

                                          Sarah, an inveterate fan of the vegan commercial bakery around the corner, spent her sixties growing from curvaceous to chunky. This 70-year-old wears long, salt-and-pepper dreads which fall down her back. She moves with a calm balance, as if walking on an uneven surface. (In fact, she suffers from knee and hip pain.) At work she wears loose denim coveralls, work boots, a funny t-shirt (eg: "Not all who wander are lost.", "Visualize whirled peas."), and a spotlessly white lab smock. She speaks in soft, calm tones acquired from years of putting animals at ease. When angry or sad, she curls inward and steps back from the situation, like a wary stray. In unguarded moments, she hums or sings softly. Main Goals: Help as many animals as she can. Some, she reluctantly admits, can only be euthanized in the least painful way possible. Those animals who might possibly find loving homes must get them. She spends a good bit of her time reminding Joanie to be slightly less prickly to people she has just met. Sarah knows that if people could just see Joanie when she's relaxed, they would love her, too. Sadly, relaxed moments are a rare pleasure.

                                          Now, faced with advancing age and tarnished dreams, they bicker incessantly.

                                          Appearance: The shelter, a former auto-parts wholesaler, occupies a squat, cinder-block building in a light industrial zone. At night, when the warehouses and machine shops are closed, you can hear dogs barking from several blocks away. The lobby is tiny. Just a few folding chairs, and a chipped formica counter. There is a faint smell of bleach and urine in the air. A lush pothos plant vines its way across the drop ceiling, throwing random shadows on the concrete floor.

                                          Behind the front counter is an doorway which leads to a very utilitarian vet's office with exam tables, small holding crates, and shelves of medical equipment and medications. The office door to this room is propped against a far wall, its broken window awaiting repair. It has been waiting for six months.

                                          A further door leads to a large room full of staked cages holding recent intakes and dogs awaiting eunthanasia.

                                          Turning left in the lobby, behind a heavy metal door is a long hallway with cage after cage after cage of dogs available for adoption. Every cage is full, some double and triple full with dogs of every size and description. A slightly mossy hose and a scrub broom lie abandoned on the floor. All the cages have dog-doors leading to individual outdoor spaces. These are in plain sight of a large toolshed, behind which is an abandoned acre of gravelly, glass-strewn dirt which has been turned and turned. If the pcs are here at just the right time, they will see dog carcasses being dumped in an open grave with a small bull-dozer.

                                          Story Role: Last year, Joanie and Sarah interrupted a thinblood fledgling feeding on a recent intake. The dog was killed, but was in no pain. In fact, it seemed elated.

                                          Joanie, ever the realist, has opened their shelter as a feeding center for Kindred. They charge twenty bucks per dog, but only offer animals that are unlikely to be adopted. Sarah is appalled at this turn of events, and wants nothing to do with it. After a vampire brutally drained a shih tzu mix, though, she now insists on being present to ensure the feedings are done humanely. She stands quietly in the corner, sometimes crying softly.

                                          PCs are likely to come here for a desperation feedlot, as are other kindred in town. The shelter is like a watering hole on the savannah, where you never who you might run into. Most are ashamed to use such services, but it has become an informal, de facto quasi-elysium. Local elders are undecided about Furever Hearts. It does keep the groundlings fed and quiet, but too reliable a source of blood might lead to rebelliousness. Given the shelter's growing importance in keeping the lowest of low-clan vampires fed, there would be dire repercussions if anyone harmed the owners or the business.

                                                      "...or talk to the local cops..."

                                                      On that note, it would probably be prudent to make some sample police stations. While other locations can be hit or miss, Vampire games especially tend to wind up visiting law enforcement, sooner or later. Whether it's to subtly coax information, or to bust a PC out of lockup before sunrise.

                                                      ---

                                                      Shrike Street Police Department
                                                      • Half the station is a modern, brutalist concrete structure. The other half were repurposed office buildings in Art Deco decor.
                                                      • Chief, of course, thoroughly subverted by the Prince (or whomever is in charge). Most of the force is corrupt.
                                                      • Owing to the above, the station has a leak problem, and we're not talking about the pipes. Most leaks are of criminal abuses (and the abuse of criminals), but it's just a matter of time until something Masquerade-related gets exposed in the shuffle. Some officers WILL kill to protect their secrets, given the chance, even if it wasn't related to the supernatural.
                                                      • Built on top of prohibition-era smugglers tunnels, one of which leads to lock-up. Kept around by vampires specifically to prevent Masquerade breaching vampire immolations. However, entrances to said tunnels have been accidentally (deliberately?) bricked up, as part of unrelated remodeling efforts in and around the station.
                                                      • FBI agents have a permanent office set up at the station, as part of cooperation between the Bureau and local law enforcement on a particular case. Aforementioned police corruption makes this arrangement a tense affair. FBI agents might also have their ears to the ground concerning "unusual" activity (read: may be connected, albeit tangentially, to SAD).
