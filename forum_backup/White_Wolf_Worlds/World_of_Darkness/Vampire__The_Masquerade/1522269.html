    V20 Health levels are not equivalent to soak dice. What do you think?

    09-08-2023, 12:31 PM
    Hello! My first thread on the forum I have been lurking here for quite sometime.

    Someone at my table asked about using health levels instead of soak after our last session I would like your comments on this change for their character.

    I have seen many arguments on both this forum and the reddit whenever fortitude or stamina and the soaking rules related to them to simply use health levels in place of the soak dice like VTM 5 and VTR use.

    I believe personally through regular play that this makes Vampires weaker overall if used. For instance if you gain a health level on a 1 to 1 basis for stamina and fortitude assuming a base health level of 7 the most health levels a non low generation vampire can have with stamina and fortitude of 5 is 17, this sounds pretty powerful but keep in mind health levels are exhausted for every point of damage a vampire suffers.

    A vampire with 10 soak dice on the other hand always has that benefit no matter how many attacks it suffers due to the success on a six that vampire will on average gain between 4-6 points of damage reduction per every attack it suffers barring some disciplines. Certain specialties like tough as nails which in inquisitor is a merit lower the soak difficulty further.

    Lets break it down with a friendly neighborhood Gangrel, our bestial best friend has fortitude 5 and stamina 5 but he decided to trade his soak dice for health levels. He is under attack by 4 thugs with Machetes/swords Strength +2 and being an undead monster he decides to spend all of his dice pool attacking and not focusing on defense, lets be charitable and say he goes first he kills one of the thugs outright. Now all he has to do is survive the other 3 thugs attacks.

    These thugs are fairly common street toughs they have strength 3, dex 3 sta 3, for a physical pool of 6 (1 point free for each attribute as per raw and mortal character sheet rules of 6,4,3 attributes) and weaponry 2.

    On their to hit lets say they each get 3 successes, subtract the first success as it was to establish a hit that would give them 2 successes to add to their damage roll they then would roll 7 dice lets say 3 successes each of the three thugs deals 3 points of lethal damage to our gangrel friend putting him down to 8 health levels in another turn or two he will be nearly torpored definitely torpored if he fails to kill another on his next turn or fudges the initiative roll.

    (If you go last in initiative can you still defend against all attackers? If so may survive another round or two.)


    Lets run that same gangrel with soak. With a soak pool of 10 the gangrel is averaging 5 successes on his soak meaning he is completely unharmed by the thugs and will likely have no problem beating them.

    Health levels although guaranteed are in no way in most situations as useful as soak dice, they also increase the blood costs of combat by forcing the vampire to heal far more often at 1 blood per point of lethal healed that gangrel in scenario 1wouldhave to expend nine blood points for the damage he accrued in turn 1. This to me completely changes the blood economy as well.

    A much simpler solution and one fitting the theme of soak is to simply halve the soak pool and make it automatic either by rounding up which will make them slightly tougher than rolling or rounding down which will make them ever so slightly weaker. This takes away the randomness for both good and bad rolls and makes it a constant like health levels.

    The downside of this comes only with aggravated damage which is far too common in v20 a character without fortitude has no defense but a character with stamina health levels has a very limited defense.

    (The only solution I can think of is letting vampires soak non bane aggravated with stamina like werewolves do. While letting fortitude soak bane aggravated as well.)

    What are your thoughts on this? Am I misunderstanding something?

    At my table we do not penalize 1's on rolls by subtracting dice and most combat actions have difficulty 6 for ease of play.

      The soak dice mechanic has always been clunky, which is the biggest strike against it in my opinion. One of the first major attempts at houseruling I ever did was to invent something close to what would eventually be used in Aberrant to reduce the clunkyness.

      Health levels are mechanically cleaner, and closer to how vampires are generally portrayed in fiction. Vampires generally take damage normally, but are indifferent to most damage. A random percentage of bullets don't bounce off them.

              Its different. Fortitude in pre-v5 is a discipline that’s only strong when you (choose to) loose. It may seem like it holds up good in your example, but starts to loose against Celerity in general, Potence in particular.
              it is always better to avoid being hurt than being hurt in pre-v5.
              At least in Revised, going up against 4 opponents is a death sentence, all rolls are against +3 difficulty, and it’s highly unlikely that they will bring one down, even without that. It’s not a good place to be in, and Fortitude offers no real exit as Celerity (run away!) and Potence (jump away!) does.

              That aside, v5 offers extra health as a power - not all Kindred who has Fortitude has it. It also has powers that soak (which is one of the few ways to counter that dangerous inhaled damage) but most importantly all rolls are in opposition. Requiem offers similar with downgraded aggravated damage, and a set difficulty rather than assuming someone isn’t defending themselves.

              So there is more to it than just health levels vs soak, like extra actions (which breaks health), an assumption of dodge or simply opposed dice pools. If you only shift one out of pre-v5, it ends up wonky.

              You can make Fortitude auto-soak anything (but fire and sun) and it will not break the game. It makes Fortitude still worse than Potence, but at least better.

              But the basics of pre-v5 is like early editions of Exalted. Do not get hit. Avoid, avoid, avoid. Celerity is not awesome because you can attack several times, it’s awesome because you can avoid several times and still be on the offensive with good dice pools. Potence because grapple shuts someone down, or single hits takes them out straight away. Fortitude is useful in certain white-room scenarios like the one you described, but once again unlikely to win the day. Unless the example character has Potence, they are unlikely to to take even one opponent out in short notice, while a few lucky rolls for the others will go through soak. Rolling 0 success on damage is not as painful as rolling 0 successes on soak when it’s your only defense.

                      While I actually like how combat works in other systems better than VtM, combat rules are fairly holistic things. Simply changing Fortitude to adding HLs (even -0 ones) instead of soak dice doesn't work. Resilience works in VtR because there's a bunch of other rules around how damage gets calculated, and a bunch of vampire specific rules that change various dynamics as well. Just as a simple example, armor in VtR 2e is ablative (if not 100% since the rules state that without magic, armor can't reduce damage below 1B). So a vampire wearing serious body armor (helps with the Masquerade if you want to tank a bunch of damage) can reduce damage by 2-3 levels each attack on top of the passive bonus Health from Resilience.

                      It's worth looking around the rest of the ST family games to see how things work differently for different combat rules. In Exalted as an example, combat started closer to the WoD than not (though Soak was always a static value instead of a rolled one). Exalted has a power called Ox-Body Technique, that you can take multiple times (generally five times), but each purchase gets you multiple Health Levels (how many and what type have varied by splat and edition). In Exalted 3e, Ox-Body also automatically gets better based on improving other things. If you follow this model as inspiration instead of VtR 2e, a 12th Gen neonate with Fortitude 5 might not feel that impressive, while a Gen 6 Fortitude specialist could have so many HLs that trying to do anything other than stake (or drop a bomb on) them would be silly.

                                  So there are two other ways to change this:

                                  1. Do the V20DA thing of static damage and soak. So, if you have base damage of 6 plus 2 extra successes, that's 8 damage. If the opponent has soak of 7, they take 8-7 damage = 1 damage. There are more rules around it, but that's basically the gist. It does mean certain attacks won't do much, though, and you'll still need to figure out what you're doing with sunlight/fire (I'd roll for "environmental" damage of these types, as otherwise 3 health levels of sunlight will never harm anyone with Fortitude 3+; you can keep static soak for non-environmental sources, such as lupine claw attacks and flamethrowers).

                                  2. Just "take half" for damage and soak rolls. So in the above example, the attacker does (8/2=4) damage. If you round up for vamps and down for humans, the defender would soak 4 or 3 damage, respectively.

                                  ​​​In either case, you may want some sort of "ping" damage, like in Exalted. So a successful attack that does lethal or aggravated damage always does a minimum of 1 after soak, but bashing doesn't.

                                  But HeavyArms is right that adjusting one thing affects others. I think the "take half" option probably affects the least, since it's closest to RAW anyway.
