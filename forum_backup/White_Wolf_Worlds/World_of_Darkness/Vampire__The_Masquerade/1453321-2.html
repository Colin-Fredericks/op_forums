                                    Originally posted by Houston810 View Post
                                    I've recently been watching L.A. By Night and in it the character Eva seems to have learned TBA, I've also done some searching and have seen some people allow non-thin-bloods to learn it. Let me preface this with the obvious "you're game, you're rules" but it is my contention that TBA is an EXCLUSIVLY thin-blood discipline on the bases that it require specifically thin-blood to work, as thin-blood has properties that "thicker-blood" does not have. I will defend this contention:

                                    (pg184) The Duskborn change as they feed. Thin-Blood Alchemy is the mastery of this process.

                                    From this we can see that TBA is an extension of the thin-blood’s ability to “pull” more from the blood they feed on. An ability the full-bloods do not have, at least not to the same functional extent (see below).

                                    (pg228) Drinking blood with intense temperament gives the drinker one additional die for dice pools involving a discipline that corresponds to that Resonance. This bonus lasts until the vampire’s next drink of blood dilutes it, or until the vampire’s system empties of blood when their Hunger reaches 5.

                                    From this we can see that a full-blood merely gets 1 additional die for pools, they must HAVE the discipline and even then they do not get extra dots or powers, they merely get a “boost” to their current powers.

                                    (pg113) Whenever a thin-blood feeds they gain one dot in one Discipline associated with the
                                    Resonance of the blood consumed, together with one level one power in that discipline. If the Resonance is Intense or stronger, they gain an additional dot together with a second power.

                                    Jason Carl has made extensive homebrew, and talked about it as such, for things Eva has like the salve, lighting magic or the Carna fire magic. He also plays very loose with the rules as written, and has forgotten major rules like things for Frenzy rolls. Nothing in LAbN should be used as any litmus test for the rules as written from the core book.

                                                    Ugh slow to get back to this. Anyhow I'm not aware of any 'superseding' of mechanics, since DAV Revised and 20th have flaws that simulate thin-blooded-ness after a fashion. They just don't call it that (two flaws specifically - 'cannot embrace' and 'weak blood' which differ from the earlier depictions. Revised at least even treats being 13th gen as a flaw with many of them having one of two previous due to 'thin blood') Mechanically the drawbacks are much different (and severe) than in ToTB or BJD in some ways (For weak blood disciplines are capped at 4 dots in addition to being incapable of embracing or creating ghouls and the double blood point costs. Cannot Embrace seems to be a lesser 'mutation' of the curse but still one all the same.) In modern nights ToTB suggests multiple times (mechnically and narratively) that its possible for lower generations to have thin blood, its just far rarer than it is for the highest generations. They don't flesh this out very well and there's no real mechanical benefit to it (some parts suggest its more 'alternative' rules in fact) but its not expressly forbidden either. Narratively Netchurch says its 'rare' but doesn't call it impossible among lower generations when discussing thin blood syndrome - its just more common among the higher generations particularly the 15th. Beckett's Jyhad Diary (more recent and has the mention of 16th generation) takes a similar tack: low-generation thin bloods are 'technically' possible but in practice it's more prevalent amongst the highest generations and there really is no mechanical benefit to a low-generation thin blood.
