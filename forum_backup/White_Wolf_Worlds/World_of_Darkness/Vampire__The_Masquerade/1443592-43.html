            I don't think you actually need to go to the extent of rolling for NPCs, but it is important they have the same guidelines and capabilities players do. If an NPC is using babies for firearm suppressors like how the postal dude uses cats, a player is going to want to know why that character isn't wassailing. If the players want to hurt an NPC by attacking his morals, then morality for that npc becomes important and the players should have a fair mechanic they can use to take that character down. Vampire before V5 excelled because you followed the same rules as others of your kind; If another vampire can do it, you can learn too, you just need to find the knowledge and experience. It's not DnD where you can expect to take out a magical plot device that only NPCs can use and have the players cool with it. Vampire is "fair" and it does so by holding the NPCs to the same standards as players, even if the storyteller isn't going to roll all the details.

                    Humanity has (and has always had) mechanical effect. It's not just a role-playing tool. It's an in-universe thing to be manipulated. You can try to help a fellow vampire maintain Humanity, or purposefully try to drive theirs into the dirt. Whether or not the ST rolls out NPC Humanity/Path changes doesn't matter to the fact that the PCs should be able to influence NPCs in this fashion if they choose to. In any of the editions, if you undermine a Prince's Humanity, you can sabotage that Prince's hold on power by making them less capable of leading effectively. The players have every right to expect to be able to engage with that story rather than NPC Humanity just being a number for the ST's reference.

                            Yeah humanity literally dictates a lot of the symptoms of the Beast. High humanity means you don't have to spend blood for blush of life, Low humanity and you forget how to blush of life. High humanity gives you a bonus for getting sympathy, low humanity makes you sketchy. Your Virtues are capped by your humanity score; you can roll no more dice than your humanity when it comes to ordinary virtue rolls. Lastly, Humanity/path dictates your upper limit when you're awake during the day, and also makes it easier to wake up during danger.


                            There are a few mechanical caveats to alignment in DnD, but not to that extent.

                                            Leaving humanity behind, human touchstones and inhuman vampires...

                                            I don't care about rats. I could theoretically eat a rat for sustenance. I could keep one as a pet. I could theoretically kill 'em for fun on account of not liking them. I could kill them for safety reasons. I could use them in lab experiments.
                                            -
                                            But I am indifferent to rats, unless they're threatening me somehow. I know rats share the world with me. They are intelligent, they are social, I am surrounded by thousands at any given time, and I pay them no more mind than is necessary. I don't care, they're nothing to do with me outside of specific circumstances. We don't perceive the world the same way, we don't survive the same way, we are totally different species existing in the same space.

                                            Said totally other species that I view, at best, as a mild annoyance is hardly going to be a cornerstone of my sanity.
                                            -
                                            Replace the word "rat" with "human" and you get the reason why human touchstones won't work as a universal concept. A vampire divorced from their former species has no kinship with the human animal and views them accordingly. If you want to play a vampire as a member of another species, you do not necessarily want human touchstones.

                                            Said vampires are also highly unlikely to care about "why" an enemy vampire is keeping rats. Maybe they like them or find them endearing or interesting, maybe they hate them and torture them in a sociopathic fashion. Who cares, except for how you can use it against them.
