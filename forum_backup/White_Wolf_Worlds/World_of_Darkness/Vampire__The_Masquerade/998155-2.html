                                What I am saying is that generations of tabletop and computer RPGs have defined the longsword as a one-handed weapon and the broadsword as a big-ish slashing/cutting weapon, so in terms of communicating what a weapon is, this is somewhat more accessible. That said, many of us are well aware of the difference between a hurlbat and a wimple full of rocks (the latter of which is a historically attested weapon for man/woman judicial combat),

                                Now to address some of the other things you've said:

                                * If Vampire had a realistic combat system, armour would be far too powerful. I do full contact armoured fighting with blunt steel. Mail over a gambeson wards off pretty much everything that isn't a thrust, and our mail isn't as good as historical examples. A strong cut might knock a few rings out. The course of evolution is really more of a matter of tradeoffs than armour suddenly becoming Super Next Level Shit. This is a complicated topic generally, not a simple Just So Story.
                                * Actually, we do in fact play with damage this way. Vampires halve bashing damage after soak. The average mortal has a trivial ability to shrug off non-penetrating injuries but still get Incapacitated at short notice. "Bashing" is not "bruising." It's simply blunt trauma (and possibly, superficial lacerations) of any severity.
                                * Nah, there are two-handed swords from this period (XIIa and XIIIa on Oakeshott typology) though they're prevalent in the latter half of the century. They're just not especially distinct from their predecessors except for size and length.
                                * Nah, an armed populace was common during the period -- in England you had to be armed if you had a certain amount of money.
                                * People will argue about how well arrows penetrate armour until the end of time. I admit, rock vs. dagger is a concession to genre, but I will also submit that the rules don't live or die on this.
                                * Why yes, clubbing people with your fists may require multiple intervals of three seconds if they're tough. The rules for mooks are on p. 351 of V20 Dark Ages. Mooks are not tough -- this is what makes them mooks. Now if you mean a high Stamina? Yes, mooks will take more time. If you don't want it to be a major fight, why would you give them high Stamina?

                                I mean, let's work this out. Average mooks who know how to have dice pools of 4 and 4 HLs. They soak with 2 dice. Let's make the vampire a bit peppier, with 6 dice, 3 Sta soak and of course, all the HL's. Now if nobody defends themselves (which is pretty common for folks just throwing haymakers, and the vampire is tough) Assuming diff 6 across the board, every turn, the mooks score an average of 2 bashing. The vampire soaks one or two of these, and if one gets through, halves that last level, and suffers no damage. The vampire contemptuously beats up one, then the other, scoring an average of 3 damage, which the mooks soak an average of 1 of, so it takes four turns -- 12 seconds -- for a capable vampire to just beat the shit out of two mooks, with the vampire in all likelihood never getting hurt. If you designed mooks with higher Stamina and wanted them to get beaten quickly . . . well, you were preparing something at cross purposes with your goals. If you wanted a less capable vampire to win, well, vampire should've boosted some Physical Attributes. While Vampire's combat system isn't exactly peppy (major revisions to it are not in the mission of the 20th lines), it does't take ages to beat a mook -- and this is without me asking why this Lamia doesn't have any Potence.
