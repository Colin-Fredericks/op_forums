                            Menele can destroy entire cities in blaze of fire and make volcanoes erupt; he is faster than any being on earth; he can use cars in the same way I use baseball bats, he can reshape any memories and possess any beings, just by touching them; he can become invisible and make people kneel just with his presence; his skin is harder than steel; he can read minds, project his spirit anywhere in the world; he can shift in any kind of animal, or just become wind; he is far more intelligent than most spirits, and he can control any spirits he wish thanks to his magic,or set thing aflame with a snap of finger, and make your blood boil like a cauldron...

                            Why Menele isn't a god?


                            If we must use the judaic meaning of god than nothing is a god, not even Incarnas (and not even Celestines for sure).

                            Spirit Incarna are not omnipowerful, nor omnipotent or omnipresent and for sure they are not eternal (some Incarna are younger than many Methuselah).
                            Not even the Triat would fit the judaic prerequisite of godhood.



                            Let's take a look at the Maeljin.

                            They are not eternal
                            The Duke of Steel is just 500 years old, Aliara is 1200 years old, Aife is 600 years old, Hellbringer is at most 1400 years old, and Kernel, Choke, Collum and Yul are probably no older than 1000 years. Which means that Baba Yaga is...14-7 times older than many Maeljin Incarna.


                            They are not creators of the universe (as every Incarna is created by a Celestine)

                            They are not omnipresent (Maeljin Incarna especially, since they are confinated to the Duchies of Malfeas).

                            They are not immortal, as any of them can be killed, and some have been.


                            They are not omnipowerful: Aliara's charms can be resisted, you can lie to her and you can betray her; The Duke of Steel can be captured and imprisoned, and he has no powers once you take his mask; Aife can be killed by ANY pattern spider, or by any creature that is indifferent to pain; Hellbringer can be tricked into killing himself, and MAliq Harjaq can be poisoned and killed; Knight Entropy can be killed by exposure to the Flux Realm; Maine duBois can fail the task you give him with a contract; Doge Klypse can be made mortal again if you find the box with his mortal memories; Nameless Angel can be killed with a powerful ritual;

                            And so on...nothing of these could be done to "a god". You cannot kidnapp God, or trick him into signing a contract he can't fulfill.

                                Yet in the context of the World of Darkness a god is a very specific kind of being and Baba Yaga or any other vampire is not a god. They can very well impersonate one, their followers may even have faith in them... but they are still not gods in the WoD sense of the word as they don't even have a way to channel the spiritual energy generated by faith (unlike creatures like Earthbound demons, that are still not gods but are at least more spiritually "advanced" than vampires).

                                In the context of the WoD a god is a spirit, usually a powerful one. Age is a less important factor for their power than it is for vampires because as spiritual beings gods have different ways to generate and accumulate power compared to vampires who havew to learn to exploit the curse placed on their blood (curses plural, actually).

                                And again, in the context of the WoD a vampire is a cursed being. The curse(s) gives the vampire power but also stunt their spiritual growth limiting them to the tellurian with the sole exception of the shadowlands for a few of them.

                                Now it's all well and good that the judeo/christian definition of God (note the capital G) does not conform to anything existing in the WoD (possibly not even the creator of the angels/demons), but that's simply stating the obvious because that definition implies an omniscent and omnipotent being who is the sum of everything that exists (I'm obviously simplyfing things here, the definition of what God was supposed to be was debated a lot in theology and is still debated today). Basically it has nothing to do with the world of darkness.
