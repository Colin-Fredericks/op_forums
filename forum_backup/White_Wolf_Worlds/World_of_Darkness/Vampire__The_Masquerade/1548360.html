                      I love the Revenant concept. Just like I love Ghoul Fatal Addiction's tone of a real messed up borderland of weird, psychotic and bizarre things that surround vampirism.

                      Many times it is way too clean and pretty, which is very fine, but Vampire must also show the gross size of vampirism. Ghouls as the (sometimes not so much) functional junkies that they are is very interesting and create a dicotomy of a servant and a ravaneous vampire of vampires, a few weeks away in abstinance to get nasty. The vitae twisting their guts out, spreading hunger and the void of pleasure.

                      Revenants are like the twisted sprouts spawned by mixing Vampire food with fuel through generations. Until they fuse together in an unholy creation of born and bred ghouls. It's disgusting and super cool. I wish there were less families in total, but that more clans had them, like maybe a blood farming Ventrue lineage, or a creepy familial brood of Malkavians.

                      I have been using them, but mostly non mechanically, in my Transylvania Chronicles campaign. With Sheerazina being a revenant, one PC from the Giovanni Chronicles being a descendant from her, and another PC who destroyed Shaagra in Prague adopting her Premysl revenant family too.

                      Strange... When coincidence seems too convenient, I prefer to call it fate.

                                          Since we're here, 13th Generation vs Revenant.

                                          13th Generation

                                          Blood Pool of 10, recovered by feeding, which can be nothing or 10 points in one go depending on circumstances, can use vampires, animals, humans.
                                          Huge array of possible Clans/Bloodlines.
                                          Generational Maximum of 5 for all Disciplines, can blood buff up to 6 and can spend 1 point of blood per turn.
                                          Start with 3 dots in Disciplines.
                                          XP is that of a normal PC, 5x current level for in-clan, 7x current level for out-of-clan, 10 for a new Discipline, or 6x current level for a Caitiff.
                                          Almost incapable of doing anything during the day, even indoors.
                                          Killed very swiftly by sunlight.
                                          Risks of Frenzying, Degeneration, Red Fear.
                                          Vulnerable to True Faith and various relics.
                                          Suffers from a Clan Curse.
                                          Soaks Lethal Damage.
                                          Halves Bashing Damage.
                                          Treats most bullets and gunfire as Bashing damage, unless it's a headshot, or some special circumstance.
                                          Masquerade risks such as being unable to eat, drink, pass basic medical inspections and so on.
                                          Slightly disturbing to animals compared to a normal human.
                                          Unable to heal naturally, must do so with blood.
                                          Unable to rise naturally, must spend a point of blood.
                                          Capable of entering Torpor to hibernate, can survive otherwise lethal situations as a result.
                                          Indefinitely long lifespan.
                                          Capable of reducing Generation via Diablerie.
                                          Treated as a Kindred/Cainite by Vampire society, with all the benefits and problems that entails.
                                          Able to blood bond other Vampires and mortals, and capable of embracing others (though those they embrace will be 14th generation, if they rise at all).

                                          Revenant

                                          Blood Pool of 10, recovered by daily regeneration (1 point) or by being fed blood by a Vampire (potentially unlimited, with complications).
                                          Smaller selection of Families.
                                          Maximum of 1 in all Disciplines, unless you have a Domitor, in which case your maximum could be much higher. Can blood buff up to 6 and can spend 1 point of blood per turn.
                                          Start with 2 dots in Disciplines.
                                          Learning a new Discipline costs 20xp, improving a Clan/Family Discipline costs 15x current level, improving a foreign Discipline costs 25x current level.
                                          Free from all of the Vampiric curses, acting normally during the day and enjoying all of the liberties of the living.
                                          Very slim risk of Frenzying in extreme circumstances.
                                          Suffers from a Family Flaw, typically more minor than a Clan Curse.
                                          Soaks Lethal Damage.
                                          Doesn't half Bashing Damage unless they are very old, at which point they start to do so as their organs begin to atrophy and their metabolism becomes more adapted to living off Vitae.
                                          Heals naturally as mortals do, in addition to healing with blood.
                                          No need to spend blood to rise.
                                          Ages at 1/4 normal rate after reaching maturity.
                                          Treated as a Ghoul by Vampire society, with all the benefits (invisibility) and problems (no rights) that entails.
                                          Depending on your table you may start with fewer dots in Attributes and Abilities, though you do get these points for 'free' when you're embraced, along with some dots in Disciplines!
                                          Can get embraced to any Clan or Bloodline, and any Generation.

                                          All in all my hot take is that playing a Revenant is favourable compared to playing a starting 13th Generation Vampire PC. If you get the opportunity to trade up and get embraced by a NPC with horrifically thick blood, or by a serial-diablerist PC, go for it. If you are happy staying as a Ghoul, go for it. This is purely illustrative however, and intentionally doesn't get into the huge narrative differences between the two 'roles'.

                                          If I were writing a new edition of VTM I'd probably make Revenants have a blood pool of 5, which can be topped up to 10 if they have a Domitor using blood they've been fed. They'd only regenerate blood naturally when their pool is lower than 5, essentially. As it stands I'm shocked that Pentex's board isn't stuffed full of Grimaldi!

                                              Revenants certainly have their redeeming qualities, and they're definitely an interesting player option, but I don't know about them comparing favourably to 13th generation vampires. Maybe Thin-Bloods...?

                                              The inability to access Discipline dots of 2–5 is not exactly a minor disadvantage. Nor are those XP costs. Even 13th generation vampires can purchase Disciplines at three times the rate of ghouls or faster. A revenant with 10 Discipline dots would still usually be weaker than a Kindred with 10 dots—the former would probably have 10 one-dot Disciplines, while the Kindred could do the same or have two five-dot Disciplines.

                                              While a vampire can just jump someone in an alleyway to top up their vitae, a revenant either has to do it the slow way or beg for blood from a vampire (getting increasingly blood bound as a result).

                                              While vampires may hate each other, they have social rules that at least limit outright slaying each other, and they're usually willing to at least bargain with one another as something approaching equals. Revenants' lives are simply less important to vampires, so they can be killed with relative impunity, and they never come to the negotiating table as equals.

                                              This is also why revenants don't just go around murdering vampires during the day. Sure, they may have a window of opportunity and a significant advantage at such times, but come nighttime, they're back to the bottom of the food chain again. Any vampires who want to make an example of the revenants still pose a significant threat.

                                              And while vampires can easily develop retainers around themselves (many of whom have similar strengths and advantages to the revenants), relying on the inbuilt power of their vitae and little else, it's much harder for revenants to do the same. A 13th generation vampire would have a much easier time of enslaving a revenant than the other way around.

                                              While revenants can be Embraced, that's relatively rare and usually requires making yourself especially useful to vampires. By the same token, vampires can diablerise one another to gain power anyway.

                                              Vampires also have centuries to acquire individual power and influence. Revenants certainly have inherited power and influence in many cases, but it's shared family power. Any individual power has to be obtained within their (admittedly extended but not indefinite) lifespans, unless they submit to Kindred vitae every month. So, to rub salt in the wound, most of these disparities only grow with age.

                                                  When it comes to which condition I would rather have in real life, I think that I might be tempted to be a Revenant. I don't have to prey on people for blood, I can go out in the sun, I don't have nearly the chance of a frenzy.

                                                  In a role playing game, the vampire by far the better option. Being able to get Disciplines over 1 and for far more cheaply along with being able to easily replenish blood and the power to blood bind humans - it is hard to compare. The disadvantage of not being able to go out in the daylight doesn't matter as much in a game as it would in real life. If anything, if I were going to run a game where people could be vampires or Revenants, I would probably give the Revenants a little boost in power.

                                                  In fact, I've developed an alternative V:tM setting focused on Revenants, where vampires have all disappeared centuries ago, and I give them quite a boost in power.
