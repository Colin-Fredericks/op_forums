        I like the mix. Player characters tend towards the Good Guy and Average Guy side, and I do this myself unless I'm making a point to play a Jerk or worse. I usually have worse-than-average vampires in positions of power, because vampire society is a lot like organised crime.

        It also varies with chronicles. Sometimes I lean heavily into the organised crime side where noblest motivation available is revenge, and sometimes the gloomy philosophy and soul-searching about whether this existence can be justified.

        I will generally make Good Guy an active choice, and generally not an easy one.

                                        For my own PCs and PCs at my game table (whether I play or ST), most PCs tend to want to achieve some sort of goal or power, which means they'll eventually use force or violence to accomplish it (even if only as defense against someone who will actively strive to prevent them from achieving that). So Humanity tends to be driven down to 4 by the end of the chronicle, in some cases to 3. It almost always tend to settle between 4-5. I've only seen it go lower than that when something unexpected happens in game, and the player reluctantly decides to send his character down a dark path that will lead to his destruction (usually because of revenge). But there's never been a starting character concept like that.

                                        I've had players express to be interested in playing a vampire who retains a high Humanity, the problem is that the game's plots tend to derail that. No matter how sympathetic the ST is to that concept; typically the only thing to do involves violence. There is not much for a high Humanity PC to do except take themselves out of the game (the rest of the coterie can do that, but I'll sit here and not do anything for several hours until they're done) and be bored for a lot of the game. Since that defeats the purpose of playing the game, players ether abandon a good guy concept or never play one. They want to have fun and do stuff. This is a statement more about the limits of the STs rather than the PCs, but it is an ongoing problem.

                                        The one thing I do notice is that should an NPC do anything to humiliate one of the PCs, the PC will almost always allow their Humanity to fall in order to get revenge on that NPC - particularly by killing the NPC. Players really, really hate being humiliated in game. They will hate the NPC that did it with a passion that exceeds their distate for NPCs that are by far morally worse.

                                        As for NPCs, every group of NPCs will be at a spectrum. There's essentially no moral difference between the Camarilla and Anarchs. Except that in the Camarilla, low Humanity vampires tend to be older, and among the Anarchs low Humanity vampires tend to be younger. It all depends on who wants power bad enough. The Sabbat though has a much more worse moral spectrum than the others. The Independent Clans tend to be worse than the Camarilla/Anarchs too.

                                            This is why I've always had mixed feelings about the traditional coterie structure for PCs. While it can have its place in games, there are a lot of games where it doesn't work. PCs may wish to pursue different goals with radically different approaches, while the setting as a whole encourages betrayal and infighting.

                                            I don't know that there's a good solution for traditional tabletop games, since the GM can only spend so much time running scenes without 1+ players before the ones sitting on their hands get bored. With text-based online games, though, I've found it to be a breeze. PCs don't need to belong to the same coterie, and can go off and do their own thing in different chat rooms the GM takes turns responding to.

                                            One of my players observed to me that when PCs don't belong to the same coterie, they lose a major advantage which neonates have traditionally used to even the odds against elder vampires (strength in numbers). I think one answer is for PCs to belong to coteries of NPCs who share similar goals and methodologies to theirs, though I have yet to see the idea play out in a game.

                                            The one thing I do notice is that should an NPC do anything to humiliate one of the PCs, the PC will almost always allow their Humanity to fall in order to get revenge on that NPC - particularly by killing the NPC. Players really, really hate being humiliated in game. They will hate the NPC that did it with a passion that exceeds their distate for NPCs that are by far morally worse.
                                            This is 100% true. Words I once said to a past GM after an NPC humiliated the PC I was playing:

                                            "You could turn [hated NPC] into a newborn kitten and I would still want [my PC] to wring her neck. I honestly bear her even more ill will than I do [by far morally worse NPC]. I mean, [by far morally worse NPC] [does morally awful things], but that basically makes him a bogeyman. You can't hate him for being what he is, anymore than you can hate a rabid dog for biting your hand. Both need to be put down for the public good. But they're not worthy of hate, because they are incapable of being anything other than what they are. You might as well get angry at water for being wet."

                                            "[Hated NPC] is worthy of hatred because the scope of her evil is so utterly small and petty that she should be capable of more, and yet she is not. [...] When [by far morally worse NPC] dies, it'll be a net benefit to the human race. When [hated NPC] suffers, I'll laugh with delight."

                                            Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.

                                                        My answer is that the vampires who survived into neonatehood tend to be at least some measure of "not nice". Because:
                                                        1. There's an element of self-selection in clan members embracing people similar to themselves. *
                                                        2. You either already are screwed up or will quickly become so if you are willing to go night to night violating people and stealing their blood.
                                                        3. By nature of the Masquerade vampires are deracinated - they are torn out of their natural community and forced into a new artificial hierarchy. That's a lot of stress.
                                                        4. Power corrupts, and a lot of vampires have the kind of power that really corrupts - blood bonding, Dominate, Presence, Obfuscate, etc. There are a lot of opportunities for vampires to embrace the worse angels of their nature.

                                                        That said, I generally play vampire-on-vampire murder as rare outside of Frenzy. Every vampire has a backer in form of their sire, and death of even an unloved childe would still cost sire face. Plus unsanctioned murders violate the authority of the Prince. You may be badly beat up, but killing is a no no.

                                                        My vampires lean more towards the Purple Man from Jessica Jones - they aren't vicious sociopaths, they are people given tools to spoil themselves at others' expense, who have been doing this for decades.

                                                        * There's going to be a fair amount of outliers however. Not everyone is a carefully groomed successor. Some people just have a nice ass.
                                                        In regards to the Baali, I stand by the assertion that no people are truly innately evil, but Baali have a baked-in Nihilism button. By the name of "Daimoinon 1: Sense the Sin". At any moment, with no cost, for minimal effort, the fledgeling Baali knows exactly how screwed up everyone around them is. There is no blissful ignorance to the Baali. They know who cheats on their spouse, who steals pencils at work, who beats their husband, who forgot to feed the kids. With any person they meet, the Baali knows them at their worst. They see Evil. They do not see Good. Baali are the true clan of judges.
