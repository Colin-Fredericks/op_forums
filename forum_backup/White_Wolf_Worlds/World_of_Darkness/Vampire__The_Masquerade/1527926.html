                  The V5 wiki has a list of Protean powers with brief summaries. Basically, Setite/Minister's Protean roster is likely to be pretty similar to a Gangrel's; they're both going to go for Feral Weapons, for example, but the Minister who clings to the snake theme would probably go for snake fangs or a lashing tail rather than claws (it isn't actually limited to fangs vs claws in the full description); thematically, a Gangrel's probably more likely to get Earth Meld than a Minister, but Shapechange suits the themes of both rather well and the only likely difference between them is a preference for snakes, jackals, and maybe other Egyptian-themed animals (assuming they don't eschew the Set stuff altogether). This is somewhat tangential, but I question how often they'd actually go for the Eyes of the Serpent amalgam via presence given how easily vampires can resist and how much more broadly useful Awe/Dread are.

                  I'm personally torn on the answer to trueann's question. I don't particularly care for Serpentis in previous editions given how frequently it lapses into snake-themed Protean, but if we're talking about the RAW V5 approach to Disciplines, I'd have to say that I find Ministry Protean to be a little bit worse because of the one power per Discipline dot restriction (and the Amalgam setup for Heart of Darkness which requires two dots of an out-of-clan Discipline) paired with the lack of non-Vicissitude Protean options for many levels. This makes things pretty samey across the board, even more than it was before with Serpentis.

                  Though trueann didn't bring it up, the choice to make the Chimerstry powers Obfuscate + Presence amalgams also means that Ministers can access them as easily as any Ravnos (who swapped Fortitude for Presence in this version). Probably not the best decision as far as drawing clear distinctions as far as clan identities and abilities go (and I think there's a decent rationale for making them Obfuscate + Fortitude amalgams anyway - with Fortitude serving to 'solidify' and externalize the normally internal perception-tampering that Obfuscate involves), but temptation through illusion is such a common trope that I'm not as cross about that as I might otherwise be.

                  Edit: If we're stepping outside WOD, then like Spencer from The Hills, I'd say that VTR2e's Protean does it best.

                                  After getting hands-on with the material and seeing how it all fits together, it's very much a change for the better and I like it.

                                  First, we have to confront that in past editions, Setites have been Uncomfortable Cultural Baggage with a neat candy shell of "not racist at all, you guys" pulp fantasy tropes. Yeah, later editions worked around it by tying it to actual Egyptian mythology as opposed to ridiculous Thulsa Doom/Mola Ram shenanigans, and treating that material with a "fair, for its time" level of respect. But at the end of the day, that was still the Setites' hat and there was no way around it without a complete overhaul of the clan. And, that's what we got, by and large -- with the added bonus of orthodox Setites still being around, should one be necessary as a story antagonist.

                                  And, that brings me to my second point: Setites were inherently antagonistic. If not outright so through path of enlightenment or blood sorcery, their discipline combinations -- Akhu included -- encouraged lone wolfy, scene-stealer type play, which tended to exclude other players while scenes were being run specifically for that character. That got fixed, too.

                                  What we got in v5, was much like Malkavians and Ravnos, the flavor and core powers of their proprietary disciplines were added to other, more appropriate and broadly available, ones. Eyes of the Serpent and Lingering Kiss -- which you'll note, stands in for at least one general blood sorcery ritual and merit which plays quite well for Setites, and one Setite-specific merit -- are in Presence. Nor do I believe it's by accident Setites can access the Chimerstry sub-discipline completely in-clan; as already mentioned, illusions and hallucinations are common temptation and seduction tropes.

                                  Now, as for having to "give up" awe or daunt for eyes of the serpent...if you buy a new discipline power, you do not have to take a power of that level. Presence 2 (and 4) are noteworthy for that, being every presence power scales to discipline level, and those levels are somewhat of a "dead zone" of supplementary powers built around a clear expectation players may well want to buy lower-level powers without having felt like they've missed out. Fortitude is the same way, given how nice level 1 and 3 powers are, while the only powerful or non-situational level 2 or 4 power is toughness.

                                  As for protean...

                                  You have no need for tongue of the asp's "darkvision", being you can get eyes of the beast and that power is a soft prerequisite for eyes of the serpent. Feral weapons perfectly replicates tongue and typhonic maw, consistent with v5's overall nerfing of damage and aggravated sources. Losing mummify is tough, but it's a power deprecated long before v5, and only useful situationally.

                                  Skin of the adder is likewise obsolete, considering soak doesn't really exist in v5 and that damage and aggravated sources were nerfed -- and being Fortitude 2 is a prerequisite for heart of darkness, you're getting the v5 analog to skin of the adder by taking resilience and toughness. Unfettered heart is in practice just as useful as heart of darkness, without the added vulnerability of having a meat-phylactery.

                                  Yes, fortitude is out-of-clan for Setites and that's a pain in the butt. But, one of the best predator types -- which just so happens to perfectly fit in with Setites' whole shtick and plays to their strengths -- gives a free dot of fortitude. Picking a predator type that's a no-brainer for a setite, puts you 14 whopping experience points away from meeting the prerequisite for what is now arguably the weakest protean 5 power.

                                  As for form of the cobra and/or typhonic avatar, once again for those in the back, v5 nerfed damage and aggravated sources. And part and parcel of that, as well as the overall setting nerf, is getting rid of war forms. The last one in the game is zulo form, and that got hit hard with the nerf bat. Shapechange and metamorphosis are still there, you can just no longer expect to take alternate forms, and suddenly punch in the heavyweight class despite dumping physical attributes and skills.

                                  So, in exchange for not really losing anything (provided a player builds competently), setites get in-clan access to earth meld and mist form. Otherwise known as two of the most powerful survival discipline powers in the whole-ass game.

                                  In v5, especially when it comes to clans and bloodlines which used to have proprietary disciplines, you can't look at one discipline (or the lack thereof) in a vacuum. You have to look at the entire package, amalgams included, to see how it fits together. You can't just look at protean and think, "aww they don't get serpentis anymore"; you have to look at obfuscate, presence, and protean to notice Setites don't just get serpentis, they get so much more.

                                      @Theodrim: I don't 100% disagree with you in regards to the alternatives and replacements that are available through V5. In particular, Tongue of the Asp's prior tremorsense/darkvision utility can be functionally replaced with Eyes of the Beast; hell, it probably wouldn't be much of an ask to get most STs to let you call it Tongue of the Asp if you want to keep the tasting/sensing vibrations with a forked tongue flavor - a darting forked tongue's possibly a less obvious masquerade breach than glowing red eyes under various circumstances but it's also likely to invite more wariness from Kindred who'd associate with the Ministry. I also agree that you can partially cover Skin of the Adder's ground for free via Resilience with certain Predator Types. Siren's hardly a stretch for most Ministers, and even Consensualist can work well for Serpents who particularly take to the notion of corruption of liberation/giving people enough rope to hang themselves.

                                      Still, if we're talking about RAW V5, 14 experience is 14 sessions, plus Vitae from a vampire who has the power you want, with all the usual risks that entails, and any boons that you have to trade in or concede to others along the way. That's a pretty rocky road to get something that was originally straightforwardly available to them. I do agree that Toughness would probably be the best thing a Minister gets out of angling for Heart of Darkness anyway since it does kind of suck, but I don't see the crappiness of the power itself as a point in favor of the Ministry's V5 arrangement when it was at least less work to get in previous editions. The Unfettered Heart is indeed a less costly alternative, but it's more resistance than immunity to staking; not actually quite as useful for that particular niche even if it's probably the less painful investment for someone that wouldn't otherwise be going for Fortitude.

                                      It's also true that a Minister can circle back for Eyes of the Serpent or Awe/Daunt at later levels in Presence, but I'm less convinced that makes a particularly compelling argument in favor of the V5 setup; deliberately making powers relatively lame/sparse at particular levels so that people feel less bad about taking a level one power later on doesn't change the fact that their cost is doubling at least, and those who already have those powers are still left with an assortment of alternatives that can be comfortably replaced with lower level abilities that would normally cost half as much (which seems kind of underwhelming if not outright problematic for balance). Granted, they could commit a free dot from a Predator Type to it, but that means they're probably sacrificing Lingering Kiss for it, which would otherwise fit quite well into the whole snaky tempter schtick for someone who wants to go all-in on it unless they're willing to kick it a bit further down the road as a substitute for Dread Grace, Entrancement, or True Love's Face, and that's a big ask mechanically and thematically. There's nothing wrong with hard choices per se, but this one's only hard if a player's going for a particular flavor. From a practical perspective, there are clear winners and losers here and the winners can still get up to pretty similar tricks.

                                      And that's the crux of my issue with the V5 shifts for the Ministry: there are technically ways to purchase all their formerly proprietary stuff but it tends to mean going out of your way and shooting yourself in the foot en route as you deliberately ignore obviously superior competing options that are more generic (Misty Form vs Heart of Darkness/The Unfettered Heart being another prominent example). In practice, this likely means that there's less to distinguish Ministry player characters from Gangrels or other Protean users than before when Serpentis was its own thing, given that they had Obfuscate and Presence in prior editions too (and there's a grand total of one Protean amalgam power associated with either of these Disciplines in this edition). Even so, I still only count the situation as slightly worse (in the sense of being blander) since, from my perspective, Serpentis was mostly unremarkable and redundant to begin with. Opening up Chimerstry to the Ministry is a wash as far as I'm concerned since it's both a good and bad thing: it's cool and fitting for Ministers, but I can't really ignore how it hoses the Ravnos in the process compared to, say, the Malkavians or the Ventrue, since other clans have at least work a bit harder to ape their particular tricks.

                                      Stepping away from RAW, though, I think there are some pretty easy fixes. Additional Protean powers, amalgams and otherwise, are easy to come up with and would enrich the tapestry quite a bit: making Eyes of the Serpent a level one Protean power with a Presence 1 prerequisite is about the most low-effort change imaginable, and a scaling level one Protean power - Sinuousness or whatever - that provides bonuses relating to flexibility (say vs grapples/mundane physical constraints and maybe an optional Rouse Check to squeeze into or out of tight spots ala the Rathead gift from prior editions of WTA) wouldn't take much more trouble to write out and it would also be a good fit for Tzimisce. Overall, I like that V5 wanted to expand the functional and thematic scope of Disciplines, I just think it leaves a bit to be desired in practice. Stepping away from powers themselves, a Horned Vipers/Warrior Setites Loresheet could potentially serve as a way to ease the costs for someone who's hellbent on Heart of Darkness or just wants a cleaner route to Fortitude/Potence. There isn't really a shortage of options within the edition for people who regard the situation as needing correcting.

                                          Originally posted by Paramecius View Post
                                          @Theodrim: I don't 100% disagree with you in regards to the alternatives and replacements...
                                          A character only needs to taste the blood of another vampire to learn the first dot of an out-of-clan discipline; once they have the first dot, it can be raised on its own. Hence, why predator type is such a powerful tool in character generation; it provides discipline access a character may not otherwise have (or realistically get, during play). A v5 setite who starts with fortitude as their bonus power through predator type, never has to taste the blood of a vampire with fortitude because they already have it.

                                          As far as skin of the adder versus v5 fortitude, to get to my conclusion we need to ask why skin of the adder was so good in the first place. Simply put, it allowed the most common and dangerous aggravated sources to be soaked with stamina at reduced difficulty; meaning, more dice pool thanks to being able to blood buff stamina, and more soak successes thanks to reduced difficulty. That was necessary, due to how blown out of proportion damage pools were, and how abusable action economy was, in past editions. Defensive powers had to be gonzo, just to make them worth taking or using in the first place.

                                          Nerfing damage pools, getting rid of action economy abuse, changing bashing and lethal to halved/unhalved superficial, and restricting sources of aggravated damage eliminated the root cause of the issue. Absent that root cause, gonzo defensive powers aren't necessary and that allows fortitude to shine. Toughness getting special mention, as it works against superficial Willpower damage as well as being the only source of reducing unhalved damage available.

                                          Powers having "dead zones" isn't the sign of crappy or lame powers, or poor design. One or more levels just happen to give a wealth of good options, and the level after it is based around supplementary or circumstantial powers to give players the ability to buy multiple powers of a previous level. When you raise a discipline like presence or fortitude you're not just buying a new power, you're buying synergistic increases to the powers you've already bought. Opportunity cost and careful power selection to build a theme or concept, is just the name of the game in v5: every character has to deal with it, and no individual character can be everything at once.

                                          And yeah, that means certain clans can cop other clans' hats. Let's be honest with ourselves, most proprietary disciplines were either not very good, laughably overpowered, barely had enough content to justify being a discipline in its own right, or were protean reskins (those groups not being mutually exclusive). Folding those powers into common disciplines is a good thing; yeah, you can play a setite with unfettered access to the chimerstry line, but ravnos can now play stealthy, social, or spymaster/information broker in turn. Nosferatu can play social, (ever stronger than before) combat monster, and "minion lord" (and have those minions matter). And so on.

                                          Players can still go whole hog on a concept, and yeah that has an opportunity cost. If, for example, I'm playing malkavian and take a predator type with presence, I'm 54 experience away from playing "OG dementation master". Start with sense the unseen, premonition, cloak of shadows, and daunt; then, buy chimerstry, dementation, melpominee, and fata morgana in that order. That's everything Dementation 1-5 did, crossing the line into OG dementation 6-7, but more broadly applicable while delivering greater utility on an individual level than any OG dementation power. All while still having room to grow through in-clan disciplines and presence alone.

                                          The price I pay for that is giving up the traditional discipline spreads for obfuscate, presence, and dominate, but that's just the name of the game. I can't do everything at once.

                                                      What you're missing is when you increase a discipline level, you're not just getting a new power. The powers you already have now function at the new level. The order in which you buy them doesn't matter, save for prerequisites.

                                                      If you already have awe from presence 1, and buy daunt leveling presence to 2, you're not just getting daunt. You're getting daunt as it functions at level 2 (+2 dice to intimidation), and awe now functions at level 2 (+2 to relevant Charisma-related rolls). Or, if you bought resilience at fortitude 1 and toughness at level 2, and then buy fortify the inner facade with fortitude 3, you're not just getting fortify the inner facade's effect; in addition to the 2 passive mental resistance and 3 active from fortify the inner facade, you're getting a third bonus health level and soaking three superficial damage per attack.

                                                      Or, if you have daunt from presence 1 and buy melpominee at level 2, your new +2 to intimidation dice pools (and the resolve + composure roll to attack) no longer requires line of sight or being in your immediate vicinity -- all you need is to be within earshot. By default, you still must be able to see another to affect them with a targeted presence power, or vice versa in the case of non-targeted presence powers; otherwise, those targets are not targetable or susceptible to presence.

                                                      That's why melpominee specifies working with awe, daunt, dread gaze, entrancement, or majesty. You can't entrance a vampire hidden by cloak of shadows, unseen presence, or vanish -- unless you have sense the unseen and have successfully pierced that obfuscate. With melpominee, you can as you no longer need to see them to target them -- you just need to know they're there. Even if the vampire is using mask of a thousand faces or imposter's guise to blend in with a crowd, without sense the unseen you're left targeting random mortals and hoping to get the right one; with melpominee, you can target them just by knowing they're somewhere in the crowd (but they still need to be able to hear you).

                                                      Again, you're missing the point. Clans and characters in v5, by design, are no longer defined by a single discipline or powers within that discipline. You build based on the totality of discipline powers available, to create a single, holistic theme. Choices that are suboptimal or not pragmatic in the white room, can and do have strong uses within that theme -- it just depends what the theme is, and how well individual powers work within that remit.

                                                      Here, I'll give you an example of a nagaraja NPC I just made for the chronicle I'm about to run.

                                                      She's a trapdoor -- she havens in a famous haunted location and feeds from investigators who go there, and her bonus discipline dot is obfuscate (and I gave her the two-dot nagaraja merit, among others). She's not a dick to wraiths -- in fact one of her convictions is "never be cruel to ghosts" -- and her haven is also a shallowing, so the wraiths come to her rather than the other way round. But at the same time, she's there to preserve the Masquerade, but keep the shallowing strong while protecting it from mortals and other vampires, and that means walking a razor-thin line between letting a steady stream of "evidence" out while keeping anything revelatory in.

                                                      So, with all that in mind I gave her sense the unseen, cloud memory, dementation, oblivion sight, where the veil thins, aura of decay, silence of death, and unseen passage. As she's not a dick to wraiths she doesn't need "be a dick to wraiths" ceremonies, which frees up her oblivion 1 spot from binding fetter and let me give her oblivion sight. Ashes to ashes might have been a good pick, but she has a better way to dispose of corpses being a nagaraja and all; so instead, she has two ways to see ghosts, a way to see other supernatural fuckery, and darkvision.

                                                      Where the veil thins is kind of a weak pick for this character's purposes other than wraith warding, but it's wraith warding. Must-have. Aura of decay and host spirit are her trump card: if and when she needs a skill set other than her own she has wraith allies and retainers to ride shotgun. Again, recall she is not a dick to wraiths, and is the one protecting the shallowing and keeping the pathos flowing, which means in turn wraiths have little reason to screw her over and every reason to help.

                                                      Obfuscate is still obfuscate, except hers works against wraiths thanks to her merit, and that allows her to sneaky-sneak when spectres are involved until such a time she's prepared to deal with it...which plays into her own skill set, which is focused on investigation, research, knowledge, forensics, and deduction. If she genuinely needs to know what a fetter may be, she's more than capable of doing it the old fashioned way. As it is, silence of death and unseen presence lets her sneak around investigators and do all the requisite "unexplained noises", "EMF interference", and "items moving and lights going on and off" that constitutes most paranormal "evidence".

                                                      Other than that, when it comes time for her to get a new discipline dot as the chronicle progresses (I like doing that to keep players on their toes), ghost in the machine is definitely next up as how it interferes with recording technology.

                                                      Cloud memory and dementation are important to her, to help maintain the Masquerade while having a shallowing as a haven. If an investigator learns a little too much or finds something they shouldn't, a little amnesia goes a long way while feeding the location's mystique. Failing that, the occasional investigator going stark raving mad perpetuates the location's rep while discrediting anything said about it, and as a side benefit is a nice tasty treat for the ghosties.

                                                      In other words, her entire discipline spread is based around a singular, holistic, theme: running a fake haunted house but making it look real, to hide the fact it really is haunted. And by coincidence it makes her a really good sheriff, so within Kindred society that's what she is.
