                            Originally posted by Baaldam View Post

                            Little WW Wiki nitpick - Gulfora actually comes from Chicago: By Night, 1st edition, not 2nd.

                            But yeah, the matter of what Gulfora actually is can be considered sketchy at best, but fluff (she's outright stated to be an unreliable narrator by nature) or crunch-wise - if there's some consistent about her, is her abilties NOT fitting with how demons are supposed to work, no matter the edition.
                            I think there was an intention to have succubi as a unique type of creature at some point, but this was never really handled consistently.

                            STGttS, for instance, also gives a description of another succubus, Lucrecia, which is not too dissimilar to Gulfura.

                            Unlike Gulfora, it isn't expressly stated whether Lucrecia occupies her own body or inhabits a mortal vessel, but she can teleport, which I assume is a rough approximation of Gulfura's ability to turn insubstantial using the system of Investments in that book. She also does the usual succubus things of feeding from sex, although this succubus has also learned how to blood bond vampires.

                            Also in that section of the book is a Scottish demon that lives off cat sacrifices, which is about as far from the Elohim as lupines are from Kindred.

                            It seems like the intent in early WoD books is that "demon" is a subjective category, covering various minor gods and spirits that don't fit into other splats.

                            This approach appears into other Sabbat books (e.g., The Chaos Factor and Mexico City by Night) which also have polytheistic gods and nature spirits working like demons who can grant Investments or teach powers. So I think they're definitely separate from the Elohim, on the whole.

                            Plus, depending on how one interprets the "when God created the beings of the sea and air" claim of hers, she might some sort of intelligent pre-human beast, nature spirit or elemental instead, specially when considered alongside the "She is also unaffected by the sun or fire but avoids both." blurb in her notes.
                            Yeah, she could be some sort of Umbrood, a leftover bygone or even a fae being (maybe an Inanimae, Lycian or ancient Thallain). Gulfura's write up suggests either a Fallen ("created along with the other angels, and fell from grace") or an Inanimae/Umbrood ("malevolent force of nature"). I could see her being a djinn from CTD, too.

                            I rather like the vaguer demons of the older books. But I think even DTF doesn't negate the role of various other spirits as being demons.

                            It's just that the Elohim see all other spirits through a Christian lens, just as the changelings see other supernaturals as "Prodigals" who've forgotten they're faeries.

                                    I'd say it's more of a backwards compatibility issue.

                                    STGttS provided us with a system for demons, pacts and related things with a clear goetic/medieval demonology flair (as shown by Barliagus' title/type, the Cataboligne - damn, many of their names have a medieval renaissance italy vibe to it) but also riffing from kindred mechanics for ST convenience.

                                    Meanwhile Werewolf. instead of "patching" things by representing spirits with gifts, atributes and skills, prefered emphasize their "otherness" as immaterial beings and giving a set of connected but separate mechanics of their own. One that Mage would also adopt for convenience out of consistency and due to its versatility. It's also in Mage's Book of Madness where the subject of demons as perhaps a label for a variety of beings that might or not relate to goetic or biblical mythologies is brought into the open, i think.

                                    Masquerade kind of becomes the odd man out in that it just sits on the whole "using the old or adopting the new" issue (as usual across too many editions...) and act like it doesn't even exist.

                                    THEN comes DtF with the Elohim/Fallen as a player option and allegedly THE OG rebellious angels exiled from heaven into hell/the Abyss for ages. (What is on itself sketchy like f##k when one considers that the idea of a host of angels rebelling against the Creator is a very late construction in biblical mythology - and possibly related to zoroastric influences during the hebrew's exile period, but i digress)


                                    Gulfora is something from so much before all of those editions and cross-splat retcons it's kind of hilarious the more you read on her on her own terms.
                                    Like the fact her Dominate 5 affects only men (gender preferences notwithstanding) what should include possession while her Presence suffers no such issue, the "feeding" through sex bit, her blood being useless to kindred and vice-versa among other little things.

                                    Bezariel,"the Blood Angel" from Rage Across Russia can be seen as an even more hilarious case of this, in that, even though the original was clearly made with an "alien deep umbral spirit claiming to be an angel" (maybe - i'm not even sure he does claim to or if it was the mad mage who summoned it in Baba Yaga's service) lens in mind, someone would try to make him into a sample Earthbound.

                                    The entity who was fascinated by people on Earth having easy access to glass spheres, because they were immensely valuable for him - and hard to come by wherever it comes from.
