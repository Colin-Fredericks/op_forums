                            Originally posted by Mallgoth View Post
                            Didnt their last financial report say that they had like 100+ licenecies, the majority not announced.
                            This is a fairly meaningless figure. When you're looking at these sorts of documents, you have to remember that it's Paradox painting themselves in the best possible light to keep their shareholders happy, entice new investments, and not spook existing investors.

                            Onyx Path is still a licensee in that group... which is just what Paradox gets for the Exalted books being made. I'm pretty sure that's a rounding error in Paradox's budget.

                            Paradox is also been engaged with multiple rounds of divestment. They went on a buying spree (of which the WoD/CofD/Exalted were only a small piece of) of both IPs and smaller video game studios. A decade ago they were feeling very bullish about expanding their company; and that was before the was a video game bubble that happened during COVID (though fortunately that seems to be deflating rather than bursting). Well beyond Bloodlines 2, Paradox had already been scaling back everything that wasn't part of their core profit makers from ten years ago. They had a lot of really rough lessons in the fact that expanding the company takes more than having a lot of cash on hand and just hiring developers/studios with more experience but no experience working within Paradox's corporate culture.

                            However, they still have a bunch of games they made, or haven't cancelled but are struggling with, but are promising to get out. And with the trimming, they've licensed out that work to other companies to take over what they can't do in-house. That's why the number by itself isn't really a positive thing that it could be; it's inflated by factors that have nothing to do with, "people want to pay us to make products with our IP."

                            I have no idea why there is all this doom and gloom in this thread. I dont find it reflected in any outwards communication from Paradox themselves.
                            Because some of us have decent experience reading between the lines when it comes to corporate statements, and it's not looking good. Paradox is in damage control because BL2 is a financial bomb.

                            And as I've mentioned earlier in the thread, the leadership at Paradox has a fiduciary responsibility to their shareholders as a publicly traded company. Paradox's leadership has to be able to present sound investment based logic to their decision making, or the company could get mired in shareholder votes going against the current board. They can't just say, "the WoD totally has value that will get all your money back and then get you the investment profits we said it would, trust us bros," even they kinda have to project that confidence anyway as they're trying to fix the problems going on.

                            I think people confuse Paradox plans for their computergames and how they view the license itself.
                            I'm not sure what you mean. We all seem pretty clear that Paradox bought the IPs largely to generate revenues by licensing them out to other media companies; but making a TTRPG edition and making BL2 were always part of the plan to demonstrate that the brand still has value in 2025 to major license partners that could bring in more money than a hundred Onyx Paths could hope to do. Other companies aren't going to pay Paradox for the right to use the WoD brand if that's not going to increase their sales.

                            If anything I think Fallouts outrageous success on Amazon makes alot of Paradox higher ups imagine dollar rain.
                            No, it doesn't. They've been "looking" for a streaming deal pretty much from the start and don't have anything to show for it. The highers ups wish they could be salivating over that kind of partnership, but nobody seemed interested in making a VtM show before BL2 had a bad release. The industry factors haven't gotten better for such a deal now.

                                        I think the big thing to keep in mind around that context is that Mark RH's attitudes extended into the business practices of the company as he was the head of it; Justin was never the head of WW or of Paradox's WoD structure. The Wieck brothers basically had to stage an internal coup (I'm oversimplifying a lot here) to keep Mark from bankrupting the company, after that pushed to limit his power a bit the first time he almost bankrupted the company and they had to fix things.

                                        In my own personal, and thus anecdotal, experience Justin is actually a nice guy in person, and is fairly self aware of his social limitations and even how much of a One True Way-ist he was when he was handed VtM Revised. He has his personal justifications and doesn't necessarily think he was wrong on the fundamentals of a lot of what he said, but he at least knows in hindsight that he didn't communicate certain things well in the books.

                                        The various Revised era devs that have spoken about their time at WW back in the day always stress how much each game was it's own island. While it was never openly acrimonious, there were some strong opinions about what Revised should be, and each game line was largely free to go in its own direction until the ToJ decision was made. Putting Justin in charge of W5 was kinda odd because he never really worked on WtA as he and Ethan Skemp were the most vocally opposites on design philosophy; so it's not surprising W5 was so different from what preexisting WtA fans wanted (and who knows how much pressure Justin was under from above as Paradox seems to have exercised more control than WW did). This also goes into a much quieter thing where Mage Revised's team - even if a bit closer in ideas to Justin - felt like Justin poisoned the well a bit because the end point of the Mage metaplot shake-ups were quiet different from VtM's Revised metaplot, but the fans largely reacted as if both teams had the same intentions; the Mage revises stuff was not necessarily what Brucato would have done, but it was done with a lot more input from him than Justin bothered getting from his predecessors.

                                        All of which is to get to part of how V5 became so disjointed. Mark didn't like what Justin did even if the two of them would abstractly agree on far more than not, and both of their contributions to 5th edition has their very strong authorial tone present.

And while he was one of the main designers behind four or five of the gamelines, he mostly stayed within the first, defining books, as he despised the gonzo of the sourcebooks and supplements (because of course there was no gonzo on the first edition core books, the hypocrisy), and eventually left WW to go to Holywood, where he got a lot of trouble for still openly hating when people "ruined his vision", and had a number of forays in making all sorts of games that all failed because apparently the world isn't prepared for his vision.
                                                            A little bit of MR-H goes a long way, so it seems.

Mark Rein•Hagen (yes, until a few years ago his name had this weird dot in the middle, not a hyphen, because artistic reasons) instead defended that rules don't matter because anyone trying to use them to build a good character is playing wrong, and true players choose objectively worse options.

                                                            Revised had more of a vitriol against badwrongfun because it had, in fact, a lot of ways to play it, a lot of elements to use, and a lot of legacy content to implement. So a lot of canonical badwrongfun for the purists. Mark didn't look as judging simply because his books usually precede the stuff he despises.
