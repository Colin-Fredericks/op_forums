    Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
    I forget, did LA by Night say anything about local mummies? I recall it coming out at that point when the 1st ed Mummy was almost forgotten and 2nd ed didn't exist yet, so I suspect they might have been overlooked.
    Great question.

    I don't recall any mention of mummies in the LABN sourcebook. I also don't recall any mention of Los Angeles in either the 1e or 2e Mummy sourcebook although I believe they did mention both Chicago and New York. (I don't own the Revised era version of Mummy, and I think it does a terrible job of emulating the traditional (cinematic) horror mummy. So for my own chronicles, I only use the earlier sourcebooks.)

    Given the importance of Los Angeles to the Followers of Set, I imagine the city has to be watched by at least one mummy loyal to Horus. And the many amenities the city has might have attracted at least another mummy not as active in Horus' war with the vampires. Because I believe there are not many actual mummies in existence (and also because I believe if you want to keep things special, don't overdo them), I'm loathe to have multiple mummies in any one city. However, given the size and importance of Los Angeles as a global city, I do think having more than one mummy can be justified. I wouldn't have more than three though. And in terms of any mummy actually meeting the vampire PCs, I'll only be keeping it to one unless there's some really unexpected roleplay happening.

    Unlike many crossover elements, I feel Mummy can fit very well with Vampire. It's not as potentially disruptive as other elements, and the two share a lot of the same gothic themes. I'll need to give some thought to a few mummy character concepts that would fit well. I'm less concerned about the mechanical/power aspects of the characters because I don't intend them to be antagonists to the PCs or Kindred in general. Possibly a potential ally against the Setites. So any powers displayed would be more for purposes of appropriate flavor.

                                    I am also considering possible inclusion of the demon cult, Cult of the Black Heart, mentioned in the first Book of Madness. The Black Heart cult is supposed to be world's largest demon cult with over 12,000 members worldwide and includes a few vampires as well. Individual covens are lead by a diabolist high priest with actual infernal investments and dark sorcery. They do classic Black Mass style rituals with classic Satanism type stuff. Its arch-priest that rules the cult was a former acolyte of the Celestial Chorus which I take is meaning is actually a defrocked Catholic priest, which is supposed to be a requirement to perform a Black Mass in classic folklore. The cult worships one of the Archdukes of Hell, Grostolis (though I imagine most cultists just call him Satan or the Devil).


                                    I'm very tempted at including this simply because the motif of aspiring celebrities selling their soul to Satan is classic Hollywood. I am a bit concerned that including this plus the Setites (as well as other occult groups I have planned) is a case of too many things covering the same ground. However, Infernalism and Typhonism is not the same thing, and I think as long as keep the Black Heart as an organization clearly separate from vampires, it won't be overlap. It'll provide a nice distraction from vampires in general. Like a lot of planned sandbox elements, running into them really depends what the PCs do. If one runs with the celebrity/LA glamor crowd, there's a good chance of encountering one of the covens in LA.

                                    I could easily make any Hollywood Satanism group it's own thing, but I figured tying it into an existing (but obscure) canon group just makes it a nice Easter Egg for the setting and alleviates some amount of work for me.

                                        I was intending to move onto Wraith next, but instead I'll be talking about hunters in the chronicle because of the recent discussion concerning some characters mentioned in the original Hunter's Head in another thread. It is very debatable whether hunters are considered "crossover", but I did not think there would be substantial enough discussion about them on their own and this was the next best spot for them.

                                        Hunters generally did not get much discussion in Vampire setting sourcebooks despite the corebook's Forged in Steel setting utilizing multiple hunter characters with strong ties to the NPCs. That generally holds true in LABN with one exception which is very original, but he's somewhat buried in the text. That would be Reverend Leon Cutler who is at the center of a vampire hunting group inside the Bloods criminal gang (or as the sourcebook names its fictional counterpart, the Blood). We're given very little information about Leon Cutler or the other members of the Blood who hunt the vampires of the Crypt's Sons.

                                        Cutler and his followers seem to target the Crypt's Sons exclusively, likely because the Crypts have exclusive feeding domain over much of the African-American neighborhoods in Los Angeles, and Cutler is defending his own people being preyed on by vampires (who all happen to be from the Crypts). As a result, the other vampires in LA seem to realize that there are vampire hunters among the Blood, but don't do anything about it because they fear the numbers and power of the Crypt's Sons. In fact, Salvador Garcia secretly helps Leon Cutler by donating to his church and providing him information, all through intermediaries. That's about all we get. Even though the introductory adventure at the end of the sourcebook is directly tied to the Crypts-Blood gang feud, the only additional information we get is that Cutler is an older black man and his church is a gleaming white church that takes up half a block in the middle of the Watts neighborhood (the church and Reverend Cutler himself does have True Faith).

                                        It's an intriguing premise that needs more detail. While I may have overlooked it, I don't even think we get the religious denomination of Reverend Cutler. Is he Baptist, Methodist, Pentecostal, or something else? So we'll need to figure that out, add a group of key Blood leaders who are also aware of the vampires, and decide on the lower gang members who do the actual hunting. Considering the Blood seems to battling the Crypts to a standstill despite the presence of two dozen or so vampires, this has to be a fairly potent organization. It also present a rare opportunity for most PCs in a hunter plot, a group of hunters who probably won't go after them and could be interacted with.

                                        I think there's a lot of potential here provided the hard work is put into it to flesh it out.

                                            The original Hunters Hunted sourcebook has several characters that can easily be used in a Free State chronicle. I'll start with one of No One of Consequence mentioned: Deborah Rose Keller. Keller was a suburban housewife of a physician when her son was kidnapped and murdered in an occult ceremony. In response she became a public advocate and began researching similar occult style murders and crimes across the country. She became known as a "rituals crime" expert and become frequently consulted by police departments across the USA. She's like a combination of John Walsh and Tipper Gore.

                                            Keller found enough evidence that she became convinced there was kind of occult conspiracy behind these crimes, and this being the World of Darkness, this could actually be true. Once she realized her theories about certain evidence was making police think she was a nut, she clammed up, toned down her public statements, and continued her investigations in private. At some point she encountered a real vampire and managed to escape alive, now knowing vampires are real. At the time of Hunters Hunted, she had not yet uncovered another vampire, but she's still well known enough that her name is recognized by many Kindred and Princes. She's also made some kind of connection with the Society of Leopold and presumably therefore has seen other evidence of vampire activity.

                                            Keller herself has some decent attributes on her character sheet. High Social and Mental skills, and three dots in Empathy, Leadership, Investigation, and Occult. She also has the Backgrounds of Allies, Contacts, and Resources, but I think that understates her influence. It's more of an indication of what she can bring directly to bear. She's fairly famous around the country and has a "huge following among other concerned housewives and citizen's groups." As a recognized expert on "ritual crimes", she also has some limited respect among law enforcement. She often tours the country giving seminars to police, citizens groups, and PTAs. Her ties to the Free States specifically is that it is mentioned she lives in California, and therefore I'm arbitrarily assigning her to Orange County specifically.

                                            By herself, she's not much of a threat. It's really her role as a public advocate against "occult murders" that can create problems for the Kindred and PCs. What she really needs is some kind of public group like the Parents Music Resource Center (the infamous committee created by four wives of prominent Washington DC politicos, including Tipper Gore that helped create the "Parental Advisory" warning label on music albums) and the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children. Like those, it should have some other members and have some big funders. It needs to act as an information clearinghouse and resource for parents, children, law enforcement agencies, schools, and communities to assist them in solving occult crimes and raise public awareness about ways to prevent those kinds of crimes. I imagine a not inconsiderable number of people involved have lost loved ones to vampires in some way - either as victims killed when feeding or "cult involvement" that lead to them disappearing as they became members of Herds or became ghouls or similar. Not to mention crimes committed by other supernaturals like infernalists, nephandi, Black Spiral Dancers, and other creatures.

                                            I envision most hunters in general likely target the vampires of the Sabbat instead of the Camarilla or independent clans. The Sabbat is just a lot more sloppy and leaves a lot more evidence. This group would not be different, plus the Sabbat's various ritae more closely resembles the kind of occult ritual crimes this group investigates. It's not that Camarilla Kindred do not engage in such activities, it's just that they are involved in things like the Sabbat Creation Rite, the Blood Bath, the Blood Feats, the Festival of the Dead, and the Palla Grande which involved a lot of ritual aspects and mortal deaths. I imagine these activities provide the basis for a lot of occult crime sites that are proof of a "occult conspiracy."

                                            Plus I'm sure Keller is documenting who among these local law enforcement seem to be real allies (police who have independently come to the conclusion of an "occult conspiracy" or vampires) and who may be secret enemies (which may number any number of clueless police or just those who don't want to be seen as crazy, but also has to be a significant number of police under vampire influence/domination/blood bond).

                                            I would probably never have Keller herself become involved in any plot. Instead, it would be local citizens groups, perhaps families of the victims or vampires, who use or assist Keller's organization. Keller herself simply runs the organization at a high level, builds networks, and is often on mass media. Make her and the group be seen as something potentially dangerous to the Kindred and lessen the idea that vampires control everything and don't have anyone pushing back against them. It's also a good group to use for any family members of the victims of the PCs to join or help fund. A parent whose daughter who mysteriously disappeared and whose body was later found drained of blood would be a good choice. Also, given the idea that such a group more likely than not exposes or identifies activities of the Sabbat more than the Camarilla, it could be a good way for other vampires to use in their struggle against the Sabbat. We can also utilize Keller - or more likely someone trained by her or her group in proper investigation techniques of occult crimes - to serve as a good outside consultant helping law enforcement. And possibly knowing a great deal about certain Sabbat rites, blood magic rituals, etc. in a lot more detail (but in a clinical way) than someone "should". In general, I think it's a good "low level" hunter threat in the sense that it can heighten concern for PCs to hear their name mentioned, but it isn't a threat that can be immediately solved with violence.

                                            The anarchs of the Free States, of course, are somewhere in the middle. They lack the obvious depravity and ritual of the Sabbat, but don't have the discipline or internal policing of the Camarilla. Many LA anarchs won't be exposed, but some of the more outrageous anarchs and their activities may be identified. Since I'm placing Keller somewhere in Orange County, I'll also base her organization in one of the cities there.

                                            We really a good name for this group. Names can really make or break concepts like this. Maybe something like the National( or American) Center/ (or Alliance, Foundation or similar) Against Occult Crimes. If people have any suggestions for the name of this group, I'd love to hear them!

                                                        There's another group of hunters mentioned in the Hunters Hunted that have direct relevance to the LA Free States. This is a motorcycle "gang" known as the Young Bloods. They're essentially a group of independent ghouls who now hunt vampires to get their vitae. They were just a group of young criminal hoodlums from LA. The book calls them "motorcycle enthusiasts" who would get into trouble so they were not actual criminals, but I'm sure they qualified for violating traffic laws, public disorder, some vandalism and that kind of thing.

                                                        Anyway their wacky hijinks attracted the attention of the Brujah vampire Lamar who made them ghouls. It was great fun for a while until they got tired of Lamar lording it over them, so they staked and destroyed him. Fearing that Lamar's vampire friends would go after them, they fled. However, they craved the rush of vitae in their system so they've begun to hunt vampires. They renamed themselves the Young Bloods because of course they would. In 1992 they really only just started, but they've already become known to the Kindred.

                                                        There are fifteen of them, four of which are women. The only name we know is their leader Bob and another guy named Rod. They are "becoming known to many Princes," one of who has declared a Blood Hunt on them. They are said to roam the highways and streets of America, but they seem to concentrate on the West Coast as there are rumors they are actually pawns of the Camarilla killing many off anarchs on the west coast. Their members have to have the five point flaw Hunted by Kindred. That is pretty much all we are told.

                                                        I wondered if this group was ever updated and checked out Ghouls: Fatal Addiction. While that sourcebook does mention a group called the Young Bloods (which seems to originally come from Midnight Circus), they are clearly not the same group. The name just gets used again.

                                                        That's a decent start on an unusual group of hunters with a non-typical motivation with strong ties to the LA area. However, there are a lot of details to figure out.

                                                        First, what do they do for money? They need money for gas, maintenance and repair of their motorcycles, food and lodging as they travel, and so on. Even outlaw biker hooligans need to earn a living. What the hell are they doing? Petty criminal acts can only get them too far. Real criminal enterprises would create too many problems from more established criminal bikers and law enforcement. Doing odd jobs only gets them so far and leaves a paper trail. The best I can come up with is that when they destroyed Lamar they found a big, but portable source of cash that has let them get by, and they supplement that as best they can whenever they destroy another vampire. It's not the best of plans as they'll eventually run out of ready cash, but I guess that can be part of their storyline.

                                                        Second, where exactly is their scope of activities? While we're initially given what appears to be them going all over the USA (presumably so an ST can use them wherever their chronicle is located), other details hint that it is mostly the west coast and their victims are mostly anarchs. I'm going to keep them mainly out west, mostly utilizing I-5 and the Pacific Coast Highway with some regular visits to Arizona, Nevada, and New Mexico. They do make occasional forays to the Midwest, South, and East Coast. It's enough that they've become "known" to many Princes, but they're mostly scene as a problem for the anarchs and left alone. For the one prince who has declared a blood hunt, it's either because the Young Bloods destroyed someone the prince really cared about or that they've become a local political issue. That implies it is probably one of the princes close to the Free States. Maybe Prince Vannevar of SF?

                                                        Third, while these are mostly goons, there has to be something special about them to account for their success. Even in their origin story, it is not typical for a group of ghouls to turn on their domitor and destroy him so quickly afterwards just because they got tired of his lordly ways. Is Bob unbondable? Could even more members be unbondable? They don't know much about vampire society, but nevertheless they are good at detecting lone vampires and picking them off. This just can't be due to luck. Do they just have good instincts? Does one or more of them have some strange Merit that makes them good at detection? Or perhaps a psychic power like aura reading? Are they being secretly assisted by someone? There's probably a lot of stupid young vampires among the anarchs who will leave too much evidence, but that can't happen all the time. And for those who do, how much evidence would be available to a group like this?

                                                        Fourth, assuming they've had a lot of success around the Anarch Free States, you need to identify characters in advance who have lost friends to them, particular any coterie mates of Lamar. In fact, maybe all of Lamar's friends are now destroyed, having gone after the Young Bloods at some point (perhaps individually), but underestimated them. Once the other anarchs put things together, they realized there was a dangerous group out there and put out word at the rants.

                                                        Fifth, while their mobility is the most important asset, I don't imagine they spend all their time on the road or that they only utilize cheap motels. Do they have regular places they crash - abandoned cabins, friends' houses, vacation homes or park trailers unused for most of the year by their owners? Give some thought to their logistics. That'll provide bread crumbs for any PCs pursuing them.

                                                        Although most of the fifteen members should be seen as essentially disposable by the ST and don't need much development, there does need to be some thought given by the ST. When the PCs run into them, the ST needs to know enough to portray them as more than ciphers. If the PCs decide to wipe out the Young Bloods, they may want to observe them for a while before deciding on a plan. Any one single member of the Young Bloods would be easy to defeat, but fifteen makes them a menace. They have enough members too that any action against them won't likely eliminate all of them. This means the group will likely show up again and can be used repeatedly, building on each previous encounter/story. So it is best if the surviving members have some real personality and flair so that they are memorable and make interesting repeating antagonists.

                                                        Since the Young Bloods are very opportunistic hunters, they are not going to stick around long. They should move on quickly after each kill or attempted hit. Whether they're successful or have failed, it's smart of them to just leave. Still plenty of evidence for PCs to investigate somewhat, but the next encounter will come when the ST desires, not them. They are not going to target any one specific vampire or stick around to defend a beloved mortal victim. They just want to drain the vampire and move on to their next hit. They should also target lone vampires, avoiding groups of multiple vampires. Their only real advantage is numbers so it's best for them to preserve that. As such, it's likely they'll never target the PCs themselves. Instead, the PCs might make it their mission to stop them.

                                                        A group like the Young Bloods is inherently disposable. They're young and stupid so if they die, it's not a big loss to the ST like losing a competent cell of the Society of Leopold would be. But they are large enough you can keep using them for multiple game sessions and hopefully build some personal stakes, memorable encounters, and interesting antagonists.
