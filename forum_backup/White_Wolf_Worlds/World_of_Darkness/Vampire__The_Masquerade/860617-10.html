    151) A historian and archaeologist, who got the attention of Clan Nosferatu when he began to dig (for figuratively and literally) where vampires would rather he not. Certain Nosferatu were impressed, however, by his refusal to both die and give up seeking the truth. Realizing this kind of stubbornness, intellectual acuity, and survivability could be assets, he was given the opportunity to continue his world for eternity and gain access to information no mortal is privy to, provided he abandon the sun forevermore. He accepted.

    For decades afterwards, this Nosferatu sought answers about ancient subjects. But, time and again, the subject that gnawed at him - that compelled him to study - was the origin of vampires themselves. For this reason, he pursued that oft verboten field: Noddism. So great was his resolve that he abandoned the Camarilla entirely - who did not publicly acknowledge Noddist theory or tolerate its study - in order to further his explorations among the Sabbat Nosferatu. Given the Clan has greater ties between its "Main Clan" and "Antitribu", though, he is often consulted by researchers on both sides of the divide.

    His current project - worked on between research trips - is the construction of a mural rendition of the Book of Nod, carved into the granite walls of tunnels beneath a major Sabbat city. To preserve the text in base relief - complete with illustrations - against the ravages of time. The underground complex of tunnels would, upon completion, serve as a new and vibrant center for Noddist research. At least, that's what he hopes.

        152) Gangrel are nomadic and solitary by nature, so it's a self-evident Big Deal when another vampire can inspire loyalty in one that isn't induced by Discipline or the Blood Bond. Even rarer are those who inspire loyalty in a Gangrel, who is not also a Gangrel themself.

        That's the sort of situation this Gangrel saw himself in. An Elder vampire managed - through nothing but word and deed - managed to earn his undying respect and eternal service. How tragic was it, then, that this Elder eventually fell to Wassail. The Gangrel staked his beloved master quickly, but could not bring himself to deliver Final Death. Their shared history was too great, as was the veneration - hell, the outright Love - the Outlander held for the old one. There had to be a better way. That night, he vowed to find a way to cure Wassail and bring a vampire back from cognitive oblivion. No matter how long it took.

        So ever since, the Gangrel has traveled the world, with the staked Wight in tow. Sometimes placed in a proper coffin, more often stuffed in a crate or large suitcase. It's a treatment the Gangrel regrets, but he doesn't feel secure letting his master's torpid body out of his sight. His master was a low generation vampire, and so makes a tempting target for Diablerists. Unable to afford leaving the old one alone for extended periods, he has to bring him along when he travels from city to city, territory to territory. He only deposits his master in places well fortified against intrusion, and even during extended stays the Gangrel will sometimes change the storage location.

        For now, he moves around, chasing leads about possible ways to bring a Wight back from over the edge. Dutifully bearing his heavy burden, like the good dog he is. One night, he WILL meet his master again, in an intellectual capacity. To finally talk to his Friend again, and have him talk back.

            153) A Setite that acts as a professional extortionist. Experienced in stealth, infiltration, investigation, and technology (mainly spy tech), he grows and maintains a long list of people who pay him to keep their secrets secret. His haven is full of filing cabinets and hard drives, stuffed with incriminating evidence. His night doesn't really start until after he's made a stop around any of a number of drop-off points, to collect his cash. His burner phone has dozens of numbers on speed dial, for when he needs to get in contact with his "clients". And he's quite difficult to track, as he sees to his collection duties wearing a different face every time, when he doesn't have some of his blackmail targets work off their "tab" by collecting stuff for him (which they don't dare steal).

            To his credit, he's not exactly rolling in dough from this gig, even with all the extortionist irons in the fire. Because, despite blackmailing people, he makes sure to keep the financial or "favor" burden on his victims light. Constant, but light. One hundred dollars a month here, an errand run there. Sometimes he'll wave payment entirely for a week, if the person agrees to come to a place so they can "chat" (read: sit still long enough for him to sneak up and feed on them). From this Setite's perspective, the key to a successful extortion is to make the burden of blackmail less than the price of letting the truth come to light. To make it so the cost is mild enough that it fades into the background of everyday expenses. Paying out the blackmail should be on the same cognitive level for the person as the payments they give to the power company or the grocery store.

            For his part, the Setite sees himself as a secret keeper. While keeping tabs on his clients, he looks for signs that their secrets might come to light, or if they come under the attention of a rival extortionist. Dispensing with those threats is, of course, the Setite merely looking out for his own self-interest, but he likes to think that solving such problems is part of his "services". That his "clients" ought to feel grateful for him looking out for them, and only asking for a little in return. Sometimes, the people he extorts even seem to believe that. These people could make for valuable tools down the line.

            However, if a person he tries to extort decides they won't be blackmailed, it garners a measure of respect from him. After all, if they are willing to let the truth come out, that means they have the will to accept the consequences, if it means being free. That means they won't allow things like reputation, jobs, or "the law" shackle them. As he follows through with his threat to expose them, he can't help but smile at the fact that they've shrugged off another snare of the Aeons. Which means, after the fallout of the exposure, they might just be the most useful of all. It depends on how well they come out on the other side. If they seem truly exceptional, he might pull strings to have their far pulled from the fire anyway...courtesy of a Setite cult, who are always looking for new members.

            He knows for a fact this is a valid strategy. How do you think he got embraced in the first place?

                154) An Assamite Vizier that is also a skilled and knowledgeable combatant. A rare sort, the Vizier caste embraced her specifically because their caste tended to lack people who could fight. While on paper, it may have been for the purpose of better coordinating with Warrior Assamites, in truth her embrace was motivated by a desire to bolster the fighting ability of the Preservationist faction within the Children of Haqim. With the Web of Knives growing increasingly belligerent and extreme - and scooping up the lion's share of Warriors - the part of the Clan that seeks schism and alliance with the Camarilla needs all the muscle they can get.

                Which is not to say this Vizier isn't also a scholar. Indeed, she was a scholar first, before a fighter. She sometimes thinks her sire overestimated her prowess.

                An academic and historian, her living days were largely sedentary. Growing self-conscious of her poor physique, she hit the gym to get in shape. Having done her thesis on Medieval history, she was fascinated by western martial arts, and so added these to her training regiment. Her desire to master these skills was half utilitarian, half academic. Coincidentally, the night school she trained at had another student, a Middle Eastern woman of dark complexion and a similarly scholarly bent. This fellow student was, of course, her future sire, taking lessons to shore up the Vizier caste's abilities. Their shared interests made them fast friends, eventually leading to the older Vizier embracing her.

                Since the embrace, the new Vizier's obsessive nature (a product of her caste) has only deepened and broadened her interest in learning. From lessons on European grappling techniques, she's moved on to brawling, then knife fighting skills. Then her current focus shifted again, this time to the knives themselves. She's collected dozens of them, and studies both their practical forms and the artistry put into them. Despite the dangers, she's having a forge installed in her haven, so she can attempt to start making her own sharp art pieces.

                To her, becoming "immortal" was the best thing that could have happened to her. Now she has all the time in the world to pursue any subject that catches her attention.

                    155) A Malkavian pharmacist and amateur psychologist, who operated in life during the latter years of the 19th century. As a mortal, he was staunchly atheist, see Faith as not merely a delusion, but an active impediment. When he became a vampire, his disdain for faith grew further, until he saw it as a disease.

                    That was, until he came face to face with a mortal endowed with that most rare of traits in modern times: True Faith.

                    Leaving him a sobbing wreck, the experience opened his eyes to new ways of thinking. So as he wiped the bloody tears from his eyes, he sat reevaluating the merits of the "theistic disease" he so often railed against. Being a man of science, he didn't have the passion nor the genuine fervor necessary to believe in Deities himself. But he began to wonder if there was advantages to faith, in those who could possess it. Both from a psychological angle, and from the plainly supernatural angle he experienced that night.

                    So his current ongoing field of study is in the matter of belief. He runs experiments, studies faithful people, and tries to understand the mechanism that allows sincere belief to translate into positive results. At first his study was clinical, and he remains obsessively focused on the Scientific Method concerning his experiments. Unbeknownst to him, True Faith comes in many forms. He may soon discover that absolute conviction to an ideal - of any sort, not merely theistic - can foster True Faith. It's a change that is slowly beginning to take place inside him. Perhaps one night, he will become the paragon of Science, in the way that faithful mortal was a paragon of his religion.

                        156) A Tremere programmer who first came into contact with Clan Tremere when he managed to crack techno-thaumaturgical defenses on a secure Tremere server. When the Clan tracked him down, they were impressed enough by his ability and his understanding of techno-magic that they embraced him, rather than just killing him. The higher ups - burned more than once by technomancers in their ranks - put him to work for a few years doing nothing but scanning important texts to digital backups. Not content to remain stagnant, he pursued his studies, and managed to graduate out of the dead-end assignment by effectively inventing a Ritual ward for an electronic copy of a text that mimicked the traditional one on its paper original. His encryption rituals are seeing adoption at many Chantries.

                        Growing more ambitious by the day, he's started researching a new project. Combining his knowledge of Thaumaturgy and coding with the added power of finance, he aims to bake in a powerful magical effect into a Cryto-Currency of his own devising - something he's calling ThaumCoin - that will give Clan Tremere the ability to cast spells on any person who owns some of the currency. Given the fervor with which speculators collect any crypto-currency, in the hopes one of them will be the next BitCoin, he expects the project could be at least mildly successful. If nothing else, placing one of his ThaumCoins in the same cache as other crypto-currencies might be what's needed to pivot into larger financial control.

                        At the moment, the project is in the research and planning stages. As such, he's in a bit of a rush to produce a viable product, before the crypto bubble inevitably bursts, and he loses his chance to exploit it. Clan Tremere, being skeptical of all technomancy, has refused to permit him working with fellow Tremere technomancers within the Pyramid, nor is he allowed to train or embrace researchers to his cause. Increasingly irked by these setbacks, he's begun to turn to technomancers outside the Pyramid, in secrecy. He might find like-minded spirits among Anarch technomancers, among tech-savvy Nosferatu, or even from non-vampire code wizards. All of these are potentially dangerous to involve on their own, and would certainly garner his sanction from the Tremere upper ranks if found out.

                            157) In the ultimate display of playing against type, a Scout Gargoyle (Lore of the Bloodlines, p. 36) that acts as a social butterfly. All clad in homemade Lolita Fashion.

                            Her first memory was awaking in a deserted car park, with her sire squatting on a fence nearby. Like all Gargoyles, the Embrace had stripped her of her living memories. Well, mostly. When she learned enough from her sire to survive on her own, she flew and sneaked about the cities. Wrapping herself in a cloud of Obfuscate, and looking for clues about who she was, and where she came from. One night, walking unseen amid the streets, she discovered a crosswalk that jogged a foggy memory. Following that wisp of recollection, she found a loft that seemed familiar. So she gained entry via a hatch on the roof.

                            The occupant there was a young woman - a student of fashion - who stocked her loft (what the Gargoyle was almost certain was once her own) with rack after rack of clothing. Unable to be seen and intrigued, the Gargoyle perched herself on a ceiling support beam, and watched the human work. The Gargoyle observed the mortal for many weeks, then months, as the human toiled long into the night on frilly dresses and colorful cloth. When the human would pass out at her work bench, the Gargoyle would slip a blanket over her, then curiously read through foreign fashion magazines. The vampire came to understand this "Lolita Fashion", and its emphasis on "cuteness" and "youth". She also began to become energized by the enthusiasm - and dedication - with which the mortal plied her craft.

                            Increasingly, the Gargoyle looked at herself in a mirror, and despaired. She wasn't cursed with outright hideousness, like some Gargoyles were, as she was a Scout. Nonetheless, while she could be construed as having cute qualities, she thought, she could not pass as human. Nor did she have the power, yet, to affect a more pleasing appearance. So her horns and granite-colored skin stood out, requiring her to go "invisibly" at all times. A fact which pained her markedly less, perhaps, than her own shoddy clothing; filthy pants and shirt plundered from a forgotten clothes line 100 miles and several weeks back. Having come to familiarity with more intricate, clean, and deliberate fashions, the Gargoyle became self-conscious about the way she presented herself.

                            Making up her mind, the Gargoyle spent further time watching "her human", this time not from a vantage of detached curiosity, but from the perspective of a student. She watched the human's deft hands working a sewing needle, and observed her work with sewing machine. When the human was asleep, the Gargoyle devoured (metaphorically) her stores of magazines, text books, and manuals. She taught herself to use the computer, so as to learn still more about the craft. Finally deriving a working understanding of the process, the Gargoyle "borrowed" a set of needle, thread, and scrap cloth, and took to a private perch on which to work. Her first several outfits were, predictably, awful and stumbling, but she remained persistent. And when she wandered the streets looking for sustenance, she listened to people converse, so she could practice social skills in private. When other Gargoyles were around, she practiced talking to them; others of her bloodline were baffled by her strange clothes, yet remained nominally supportive. Little ones had little enough to remain enthusiastic about, in this world dominated by more powerful vampires.

                            The Gargoyle was working up the courage to reveal herself to her "teacher", until she noticed the mortal growing pale. She thought it sickness, until the mortal collapsed when walking through her loft door. The Gargoyle, panicked, put her unconscious body to bed. As a vampire, the Gargoyle suspected the cause: another vampire was feeding on her "teacher". She jealously watched over the mortal for nights, leaving only to feed, while the mortal's health improved. But the mortal knew something was wrong, ever since she passed out at the door, only to end up in bed. Eventually, her paranoia - and perhaps some Awareness - allowed her to pierce the Gargoyle's veil, for a moment. She cried out to whoever was hidden, imploring them to reveal themselves. So, reluctantly, the Gargoyle did, appearing with back turned, so her wings obscured her face.

                            Getting over the initial shock, the human exhibited surprising courage, asking to see her mysterious benefactor's face, and complementing her on the outfit she wore. "I want to see the front, if you wouldn't mind," the mortal said, almost as if to break the tension. When the Gargoyle turned around, the mortal seemed taken aback by the horns and the skin, but was further shocked as a realization kicked in. "Sister?"

                            The Gargoyle did not have time to learn what the woman meant, as the mortal was suddenly struck by an irresistible urge to depart. The Gargoyle chased after - trying to stop the sister she didn't know she had - but had to cloak herself as the woman hit the streets. Following her for several miles, the Gargoyle lost her as they arrived at an aging brownstone, and the men at the door permitted the girl entry, yet seemed to notice and bar the way for the vampire. The were Ghouls, and old enough to see through a novice vampire's obfuscation. They commanded the neonate Gargoyle to leave, lest it make the Toreador Primogen angry. The Gargoyle knew enough of vampires to know that "Primogen" were powerful vampires, not to be trifled with or angered, and so could only watch as her sister disappeared into the building.

                            The mortal never returned to her loft. Grief overtook the Gargoyle for a long time.

                            Now, the grief is passed, and only a slow burning anger remains. Her only link to her past, stolen by an Elder. Probably dead - drained - or else made the Toreador's blood bound slave. Whatever the case, the Gargoyle cannot stand not knowing, nor can she allow this injustice to go unpunished. The Toreador Primogen had to pay. But a hasty attack would lead to Final Death, rather than answers or victory. To achieve her goals, the Gargoyle would need to take a more...subtle approach.

                            Some time passed, and a new person waltzed into Elysium. A Gargoyle, as powerful vampires could tell, but one who wore ostentatious and ridiculous fashions ("what a strange tableau of Victorian, Roccoco, and modern dress"), and affected a chipper, outgoing demeanor. The harpies had their fun mocking her behind her back, but the Gargoyle seemed to both visibly notice and not mind. She seemed incapable of getting embarrassed. Realizing this one fell into the same sort of outrageous, shameless foolery as a Malkavian or Nosferatu, the harpies ran out of steam quickly. It wasn't so much fun to mock, when the subject didn't seem to care. Moreover, she was curiously outgoing, for a Gargoyle. She seemed to want to make friends with everybody. And she seemed to master the art of disguising herself as mortal, so her appearance could easily be ignored.

                            While the ancillae and older neonates considered how best to use her as a pawn in their games, the Gargoyle remained focused under her childish mask. If she wanted to get a chance to destroy the Toreador Primogen and discover what happened to her sister (one way or another), she needed to insinuate herself into the vampire courts. No one told anything to the vampires who dwelt on the edge of vampire politics. Nor could one make friends and learn secrets about a Clan without really getting up in their business. So while the Gargoyle plied her craft and pursued social capital, she is always on the lookout for a chance to get close to the Toreador Primogen. Where better to slide in the knife, then standing in the same salon?

                                158) A Ventrue who fancies himself a "Grudge Eater", after his feeding restriction; he must feed on those who hold hatred in their heart, longing for revenge.

                                The Ventrue keeps tabs on various parts of society. Every evening begins with a check through the news, looking for disasters and crimes. Those left behind after a car crash, or those who lost loved ones, may harbor animosity in their hearts for the ones responsible. Especially when those responsible - or who are thought to be responsible - appear to have escaped justice. The Ventrue keeps an ear to the ground in other ways, having Contacts in the courts, major businesses, and the criminal underworld. Layoffs, messy divorces, and gangland killings leave people behind. People who hold grudges.

                                The World of Darkness being a dangerous, disappointing place, there are no shortages of folks with axes to grind.

                                Not content to merely let grudges form naturally - for no Ventrue in his right mind would leave his sustenance to chance - he will often get involved in the lives of his prospective vessels. If someone has reacted to personal offense with mere despair - or, worse, forgiveness - that's not so good for the Ventrue. So he will sidle up to his target and lend a sympathetic ear. One willing to affirm to the person what they'd never admit to themselves: that they hate someone or something, and want to go on hating them. The Ventrue nurses their hurt feelings (or rather aggravates them), so they maintain and deepen their grudge. Usually, he'll stop short of actually advocating or enabling revenge - they might come away satisfied (or dead) if they attempt as much, and that's also bad for him. On the other hand, he's seen tragedy and vendetta enough times to know that many mortals can harbor a lust for revenge that can never be satisfied. The pain - and the fury - doesn't go away, just because they vent it onto one person. These sorts of people - those whose grudge transcends their ability to resolve it - the Ventrue treasures above all others.

                                Except, sometimes, he doesn't keep them poised on the razor's edge between having hate and acting upon it. Sometimes, when the whim takes him or the mortal's passion is great enough, the Ventrue will decide to help them get payback. Because the Ventrue is no perfectly detached vampire, dispassionately cultivating grudges for his consumption. This Ventrue had a vendetta, once. A vendetta born of tragedy. And because the object of his enmity suffered an unexpected demise, not by his hand, it left the Ventrue with no recourse, in a period of his life when his hate burned blackest. He'd had everything taken from him, including his revenge.

                                Is it no wonder, then, that he'd wish, every once in a while, to help someone else achieve their's?

                                Lastly, the Ventrue loves to ride the waves of danger, when it comes to vampire hunters. Many of these folk harbor an overwhelming hate, if they strive to hunt blood-drinkers. The Ventrue keeps an ear to the ground of the Kindred grapevine, for word of hunters operating in the area. Where possible, he leads these sorts into a trap, dividing their numbers and feeding from them individually. Those who hold no antipathy to vampires - rare, but it happens - he simply disposes of permanently. They are only a danger alive, while their deaths will drive the hunters either to despair (forcing them to retire), or to deepen their grudge. Where possible, he'll try to implant deep commands into such hunters, making them engage in actions that he can exploit.

                                It's beyond dangerous, what he does with hunters. But he considers them a potent and persistent source of Vitae, to slake his thirst. Moreover, he simply adores the ability to stand over his defeated nemesis, making sure they know he's beaten them. He hopes to string some of them along for years to come.

                                Moreover, unlike many of his Clanmates, this Ventrue has not skimped on Fortitude. You cannot make yourself a target and not take steps to ensure your survival.

                                    159) He was the host on one of those dime-a-dozen ghost hunter "reality shows". His small crew would haul their recording equipment out to abandoned buildings, and pay people to fabricate "witness testimony" about supposed hauntings. He cut his teeth on acting from drama class and community theater, so playing the part of a stalwart expert on ghosts, braving spirit-wracked places came naturally. When a sufficient amount of footage and "evidence" got collected, he'd splice it together into a half-hour feature, and sell it off to networks to pad out broadcast slots. It took him all over the country, got him exercise, let him mug for the camera, and earned him a modest amount of money. Altogether a decent gig.

                                    Naturally, in a world full of real ghosts and other monsters, he was inevitably going to run afoul of something. His crew tackled a disused property on the edge of town, that came pre-packaged with rumors of hauntings and disappearances. The footage they captured throughout that night could have sold for a lot of money as a found-footage horror film, were it not for the whole crew being actually picked off one by one. Last one alive, he carried the camera valiantly (after the cameraman died horribly), staying in character to avoid breaking mentally. Apparently, the orchestrator of his misery - a Nagaraja - found his act amusing. Amusing enough to allow him to leave...just not as a living man.

                                    His new pointy teeth make him much less photogenic these nights. And the need to consume flesh weighs on him at times. Plus, he never did get the footage back, rendering all the horror and loss a complete waste. But he "survived", after a fashion. Moreover, he has incredible supernatural power, and the chance to "live" forever. Some of his crew even persisted as wraiths, so he still has someone to talk to. At present, he's doing what he always did: traveling around with his crew, hunting ghosts, and trying to make a profit off said ghost-hunting. In this case, he does a lot less recording (though he still keeps a private video diary), for Masquerade reasons. But mortals still need intercession with the undead, and the spirits often have their own unfinished business they can trade for favors.

                                    And if nothing else, there are folks out there who are willing to pay for bound ghosts. The Nagaraja's old crewmates are growing progressively more uncertain of their future prospects, with this guy around...

                                        160) A Tzimisce that, in life, acted as a drug mule, transporting cocaine across the Mexican border to the US. At first, he started just moving drugs hidden inside his car. When border patrol agents became better at finding it, he lost his car and barely got away. In need of cash, he took to transporting small quantities of drugs inside his body, by ingesting balloons filled with them. Sooner or later, his luck was going to turn worse, and he ended up almost dying when a balloon burst and induced an overdose.

                                        That's when a Tzimisce found him, and saved his life with his blood. Becoming a ghoul, the mule was..."convinced" to work for the Tzimisce, in order to provide him with a steady stream of cash and/or drugs (because the Tzimisce had a hand in drug dealing). To "make him more efficient", the Tzimisce flesh-crafted the mule's body, moving organs around to create a more accessible cavity that could hold more drugs. The physical changes and absolute servitude were distressing for the man, but he was enthralled, and furthermore had steady work. So that became the drug mule's life, for roughly ten years. He even mastered the rudiments of Vicissitude during that time, in order to change his appearance (an asset in his line of work) and provide minor alterations.

                                        Even this passed, and there came a time when ghoul and master were caught in a Camarilla attack. Both were riddled with bullets, and the Tzimisce master drained the mule of blood to restore himself. Immediately after, the poor drug mule woke again as an undead. The Tzimisce master intended to use the newly embraced whelp as a distraction to effect his own escape. This plan went awry, with the fledgling staked effortlessly by a Camarilla agent and the Tzimisce sire unceremoniously destroyed with fire. As he lay there on the ground, paralyzed and all-too-aware of his sire's demise, the fledgling thought himself thoroughly doomed. Was it really the end for him, after all he'd suffered?

                                        Not so, it seemed. The Camarilla agents took the fledgling back and interrogated him for information. Plying him with sweet, precious blood, and the promise for survival, at least, if he divulged all he knew of Sabbat activities. The drug mule knew enough about his sire's pack and contacts to be of use, to buy his unlife for the moment. Pressing his luck - having no loyalty to the sect that used and abused him, with the blood bond gone - he offered to act as a double agent for the Camarilla. The Camarilla were savvy enough to remain cautious, but knew an opportunity when they saw it. They agreed, but made sure to plant mental commands deep in his mind to keep him cooperative. He even had a method for dodging the Vinculum: he'd learned enough flesh-crafting to create a false "pipe" down his throat, so he didn't ingest the ritual blood.

                                        He returned to the Sabbat, who shrugged and brought him in. They needed more lackeys anyway, though this one was experienced and eager enough to please. They aren't (yet) aware that he's quite unbound by the Vinculum, though THEY are bound to the blood HE adds to the bowl. The mixture he would ingest goes into a vile, that he secrets back to the Camarilla, for use in arcane rituals when the need arises. Along with that blood, he carries all sorts of contraband. Usually information. But whenever his pack shares blood with other packs, at sect gatherings, he carries a generous helping back to his Camarilla masters. Lately, he's even been called upon to bring some blood with him to the Sabbat, hidden in his torso cavity, that he can add to the bowl. The Tremere say that he doesn't need to know what the blood will ultimately do to the Sabbat. He's smart enough not to ask too many questions. For now.

                                        In his line of work, it pays to be careful when to ask questions, and when to just listen.

                                                        164) A Moorish True Brujah, first Embraced in Iberia during the height of the Cordoba Caliphate. Son of a North African merchant and a minor Iberian noblewoman, he had access to Iberia's greatest libraries. His excellence in mathematics and other sciences of the day drew the attention of his sire. His love of Allah and the Prophet may have cooled following transition into a True Brujah, but he retained an intellectual belief in the cosmos as a work of divine craftsmanship. Even after centuries of political upheaval, he continued to study what would become the field of mechanical engineering long after the Muslims were driven from al-Andalus.

                                                        It was his continued work in the area that caught the attention of certain members of the Order of Reason, namely of the Artificers. Prohibitions against consorting with the undead prevented him from working in person, but he maintained written correspondence with multiple Artificers for many years. Dispassionate like all True Brujah, he considered animosity towards living Mages to be an absurd impediment to the more important work of invention. After two hundred years, when members of the Cabal of Pure Thought had punished his allies and come to destroy him, he merely found it irksome that such bright minds were extinguished before their time (and that he would have to relocate his laboratory). Still, his long collaboration had borne fruit, as he'd developed a form of blood sorcery revolving around machines, making him one of the earlier vampiric technomancers. His main focus of study has been the learning of what he calls the "Path of the Automaton" (a modified Path of Ushabti, that uses clockwork robots instead of wax figurines).

                                                        His subsequent travels saw him first in Northern Africa - where he paid his respects to the home of his African ancestors by paying for the construction of a school there (sadly long abandoned by modern times) - and then later to the Middle East, where he forged ties with many thinkers among the Ashirra. He provided clockwork servants for certain elder practitioners of Dur-An-Ki - what he considered a cheap service, in return for hospitality - yet he was surprised when they insisted they teach him a little of their own magic. He found many of their arts too mystical, except for the Music of the Spheres, which appealed to the fascination with music he held since his youth. There was something gratifying about interfacing with what he came to understand as the cosmic machine of creation. He didn't learn a great deal of this Path while he remained with the Ashirra, but continued to hone his theories on the subject ever since.

                                                        Finally, his travels saw him returned to Ibera - now Spain - where he dug up his old laboratory and worked for a number of years. Sometime in the 18th century, the True Brujah fell into a sort of funk. A dry period where all progress stalled, and he couldn't focus on his work. Moreover, he found himself alienated in Christian Spain, centuries after he left, which made fitting in difficult. Resolving to sleep off his existential ennui, he entered Torpor, which lasted until the 1930s, when his basement lair was nearly bombed during the Spanish Civil War. Waking amidst fighting going on topside - fighting of a nature and ferocity he'd not seek the like before - he elected to flee, though with regret that he could only take so much of his library with him. Not liking any part of the present war - nor what he could well see was a yet larger war brewing elsewhere in Europe and the Middle East - he finally decided to travel to those "American colonies" they'd all made such a fuss about, last time he was awake.

                                                        He currently maintains a new workshop beneath a university devoted to engineering. These new technologies - electricity, motors, and even computing - both daunt and intrigue him. He's had much to study - much to catch up on - and he's reconnected with members of his Bloodline. If he hasn't already, he considers Embracing his own progeny, so he has students to teach. And of course, there's that matter of his old haven, abandoned during the Spanish Civil War. Adapting to the times has taken all his attention, so he hasn't been able to travel himself. If he creates progeny, he may send them out to loot the workshop. However, he isn't against rewarding any Kindred who would return it for him...so long as they actually DO return his materials, rather than exploiting it themselves. If he found out about such a thing, he would positively HAVE to intervene. His work and research cannot fall into just any idiot's hands, now can it?
