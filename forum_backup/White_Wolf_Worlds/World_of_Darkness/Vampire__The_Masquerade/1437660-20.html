                                        Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                                        Setites often tend to recruit new vampires from their cults, and these cults frequently involve worshipping Set to one degree or another. Even if a cult ostensibly worships some other figure, as cultists get to higher levels it's revealed that Set was the true face of the cult all along.
                                        This is how I run the clan when I ST chronicles. The clan only recruits from mortals already in their cult. When recruiting mortals into their cult, they use a variety of front organizations (including new religious groups that most people would also call cults), and mortals "progress" through a series of new groups and initiations where more of the truth is revealed so they can evaluate each cult member's response, until they reach a final group where all is revealed, and they only select the best candidates from that group for the embrace. It's multiple vetting for the correct criteria, weeding out people for one reason or another before the embrace is offered. Because that is how cults actually operate.

                                        Of course, that is not how things are presented in the books. There it is the typical "I like this mortal for whatever reason and embrace her" and then hope you can indoctrinate that person afterwards. Which is way, way too risky. Too many things can go wrong. But I think that's how most players/STs handle it, so I know I am in the minority.

                                        Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                                        I tend to believe that it's actually moderately older Followers who have more extensive dealings with the Camarilla and therefore a much broader view of the vampiric condition who going to end up faltering in their beliefs whereas younger vampires (barring player characters, of course) probably don't interact with non-Follower of Set vampires on a regular basis so they don't necessarily have the knowledge of other beliefs which contradict the ones that they're taught.
                                        That's the danger point - contact with people outside the cult. Contact with other people means exposure to different ways of thinking - which is always dangerous to a cult. But at some point that needs to be done in order to achieve Set's grand plan or whatever. So you have the Setite temple lead by a trusted person, and that "priest" supervises the new Setite's first exposure to other vampires. They report back everything once a week, month, or whatever, so the trusted leader can evaluate if the programming is still working or if they see danger signs of independent thought. If so, they yank the person out and send them back to someone for "more training" before they are allowed back in contact with outsiders. There's probably some euphemism involved so that vampire thinks its a wonderful things as opposed to punishment.

                                        This may happen a few more times, but if it happens too much, the Setites realize this won't work out and they destroy the potential troublemaker in secret so as to not freak everyone else out, and they tell a cover story in case any associates or friends ask about her.

                                        The Setites have had around 3000 years to perfect their technique so it mostly works out for them. They want to prevent any situation like the Serpents of the Light, which is a case of something clearly going drastically wrong. Eventually something like that will happen, and probably has happened many times over the last 3000 years. But what usually happens is the rest of the Followers of Set successfully wipe out the local cult gone wrong. But the Serpents of the Light succeeded by a combination of good planning, luck, and the protection of the Sabbat.

                                        Only after that post-embrace vetting verifies that the programming holds and the person is not in danger of becoming a rebel, heretic or apostate, does the person become trusted enough to have more independence and eventual responsibility. Even then, there is likely frequent moving of individuals around so that they do not forge close association with other vampires. They only want a Setite to form close associations with other Setites. So operatives likley transfer around a lot and adopt new cover identities.

                                        Those Setites actually "in" the Camarilla are probably really trusted operatives whose main purpose is to act as PR and therefore undermine anti-Setite propaganda. And only in rare circumstances betray the Camarilla because that would defeat the purpose of them being there. Occasionally you may need to burn an agent, but that should be kept to a minimum.


                                        So to me, the V5 Ministry only makes sense if it is some concerted PR move to expand Setite operations. I don't think that actually makes sense because it exposes the clan too much. When the Ministry "front" cover has to be blown (and it will even if the plan is to take decades), it'll result in too severe an impact to the clan. Its reputation will be entirely destroyed. That can only be justified if the rewards are worth that cost, and I can't think of anything that would justify it outside resurrecting Set/Typhon himself.

                                        If it is an actual case of the cult breaking down, then this is he equivalent of an Anarch Revolt for the clan, and the Ministry become Setite anti-tribu at war with its clan.

                                        So I don't like the V5 depiction of the Ministry (No surprise there for anyone who's read my other thoughts on V5). However, that is based on my conception of the clan, and freely admit my idea is probably not most players'. So they may be fine with the new version which would fit the "I hope the random schmoe I embraced ends up being indoctrinated correctly" version of the clanbooks. That version of the clan can work too, it's just a very different version than what I think the clan was originally supposed to be.

                                            I'd actually argue that you're presenting it how the books present it but seeing the "human factor" as an abberation versus an inevitability.

                                            The Ministry strikes me as an inevitability too because religions are constantly undergoing revisions, schisms, doctrinal differences, debates, and conflicts over what might be otherwise small points of divergence that are SERIOUS BUSINESS. The Followers of Set in Egypt alone are going to have people who interpret Set as a Protector of Ra's Barge, those who believe he's allied with Apophis, monotheists that believe Set to be the only God worth worshiping, Coptic Christianized Gnostics who associate him with Satan, Islamic Setites who view him as Iblis, Hellenized Setites who associate him with Gaia as well as the Greek God Typhon, and Roman ones that believe he's Ares. Now imagine that plenty of these don't much care for the idea that their version is inferior to one of the others.

                                            As for the human factor, Followers of Set are subject to the exact same sort of longings that other vampires are and are going to be inclined to Embrace who they want when they want. Set embraced Kemintiri and that was a disaster but you can't really criticize your god. Hesha embraced Elizabeth because he was certain she was in love with him and events spiraled out of control. Maybe you're only getting permission to make a progeny NOW or they're terminally ill or you want them to be your inside vamp in another group. The Church encourages you to embrace only your hardened cultists for years as a reward but there's no rule to say you HAVE to and maybe you hold your followers in contempt (as most cult leaders do).

                                            I'd argue most of THESE Embraces are probably fine even if they're not as doctrinally consistent, most of them would be happy to give lip service to the cult of Set. Among the Setites there are bound to be at least a decent chunk of vampires who find the idea of Antediluvian worship to be stupid. Either because they notice that Set doesn't appear any different than the other 12 or the fact that the Setites acknowledge he's dead (probably at the hands of the Silent Striders) and it's about resurrecting him.

                                            For me the Ministry is basically just what happened when you kicked the ball down the road a long time and doctrinal drift happened. The cults of Loki, Eris, Satan, and so on became more focused on their local variants than Set themselves. The lip service element is still there or was but the Church of Set decided to eliminate some "heretics" and, well, they realized that was a LOT more than they expected.

                                                            Setites are rather laisse-faire in this edition. The Anarch sourcebook says:
                                                            "Many Embraced into the clan abandon its ways, or are never taught them. These vampires are rarely sought for re-recruitment by their peers, and are in most cases allowed to do as they please. Unless caught revealing revealing Ministry secrets, for the punishment is usually merciless, long, and creative, the clan does not force its creed on its children."

                                                            This policy makes sense to me. Wayward Ministers aren't taking away something from the orthodoxy and on whole, they're not going to make the clan's reputation worse than its core do. On the contrary, it allows a genuine #notallsetites phenomenon, which means that an individual of the clan could be:
                                                            • an orthodox follower of Set
                                                            • a basically orthodox follower of Set with a slightly different doctrine that they care about, but you don't, because they're not explicitly telling you about it
                                                            • part of front for either of the above
                                                            • part of genuinely independent cult
                                                            • someone who thinks that they're an apostate but in fact belong to a front for the orthodoxy
                                                            • someone who thinks they're orthodox but is in fact by being led by an apostatic cult
                                                            • a follower of a non-Ministry cult
                                                            • not connected to any cults at all

                                                            So a Minister gets chummy with you, you can't be sure if their angle, if they have one, is the same as that of another Minister. Ideally for the Church of Set, assuming that every Minister is one of them would be like assuming that every Ventrue has a role in the Camarilla.
