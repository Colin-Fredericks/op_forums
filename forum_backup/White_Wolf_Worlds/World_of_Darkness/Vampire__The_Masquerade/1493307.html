    Gehenna Cults of Elysium

    09-19-2022, 02:29 AM
    I think the Elysium (1994) supplement was one of the stronger sourcebooks for Vampire. I particularly liked its Appendix on Gehenna cults. It has many useful things. But like a lot of lore in VtM, it can be hard to translate what is written into something actually useful at the gaming table. So I will be taking each of the Gehenna cults mentioned in Elysium and trying to figure out how to make good use of them at the game table, and encourage everyone's comments.

    I am assuming people have actual knowledge of the description in Elysium of each of these cults, but understand there may be many people on this forum who don't have access to the books.

    First up is the Royal Order of Edenic Groundskeeper and the Imperial Order of the Master Edenic Groundskeepers. These cults present themselves as a social club for elders. The Royal Order is a Gehenna cult that views the antedeluvians as threats and seeks to gain information about them so they can destroy them. The Imperial Order is a more aggressive version of the cult that advocates action before the actual Gehenna.

    It's a fairly strong concept that has trouble only in figuring out implementing it in the game setting. What we have is a secret conspiracy in a more general club setting, similar to Marvel Comics' Hellfire Club with its Inner Circle. Building on what is mentioned in Elysium, these elders mostly meet at elysiums and have carefully coded conversations, likely withdrawing in private when they need to be more specific about the specific aims of the organization - information about the supposed resting sites of the Antedeluvians in torpor, and the Blood Bound ghouls and vampires they use to patrol them.

    So first we need an Elders only "social club" at elysiums, likely simply called the Edenic Groundskeepers. Or perhaps just "Eden". Membership should be exclusive - only actual elders, and perhaps limited to those of Status of 3+. In fact, membership to Eden is probably worth an increase in Status by itself. This would just be the cover story. If you qualify, you can likely be invited for membership. But as a member, you may or may not be invited to join the "Royal Order" which is the Gehenna cult. Likewise some members of the "Royal Order" want more aggressive action, and this faction is the "Imperial Order". They aren't actually separate Gehenna cults, but different factions within the same cult. Nevertheless, because of their size and numbers, the book treats them as separate.

    This is all well and good, but it's a bit of a problem when introducing it within the game because none of the other sourcebooks make use of it.

    I imagine "Eden" holds special member-only events at elysiums around the world. These events are different from the usual meets at an elysium. Most members are not members of the Gehenna cult. But there is a secret "Royal Order" of members who make up the Gehenna cult. They don't "run" Eden as a whole. They just use it as a cover.

    I imagine Europe and the Near East to be the place where most members belong, but North America would certainly have many members.

    Let's use Chicago as an example. (Other cities in North America might not even qualify to have members.) As the de facto "capital" of the Camarilla in North America, Eden should be present. Who would make up the potential members of "Eden" and possibly the "Royal Order"?

    Chicago has a lot of real elders who might qualify. Critias, Tyler, Inyanga, Khalid, Annabelle (and her sire Maria before her), Nicolai, Tyrus, possibly Horace Turnbull, and maybe Sir Henry Johnson. Ironically all the Ventrue of Chicago are far too young, as are many of the "elders" mentioned in the coterie section. They could be candidates in the future once they have aged enough. These are the potential members of the social club "Eden" who hold special events only for themselves, perhaps when hosting visiting elders from Europe and the Old World.

    Who might be actual members of the "Royal Order" and "Imperial Order" of the Gehenna cult? This is much harder to decide as CbN was not written with this in mind. But we can make decisions based on the information about each character.

    Critias, despite being a pawn of Menele, is said to not even suspect the two Methusalehs are in Chicago. He is likely not a member. Tyler is a former member of the Sabbat, whose anti-antedeluvian stance puts it aligned with the Edenic Groundskeepers. Inyanga is harder to place, but her high interest in occult members and general wisdom leads me to place her there. Tyrus also is hard to place, but seems less sophisticated so I'd place him as not. Khalid seems like an obvious member to me. Annabelle isn't, but Nicolai would be. Horace is generally paranoid so much I'm inclined to include him. Sir Henry Johnson probably isn't there yet. So I'd limit the Royal/Imperial Members to be only Tyler, Inyanga, Khalid, Nicolai, and Horace. With Tyler being the most likely member of the Imperial Order.

    In terms of the impact on game, the obvious change is that there's between a half dozen to a full dozen of Chicago elders who are members of "Eden", a long established social club of true elders - an extended coterie within the Camarilla that run high social vampire galas at elysiums around the world. Membership is restricted, but comes with an increase in Status. And then within it, a very select number of members who belong to an actual Gehenna cult that could provide lots of information that the ST could provide any PCs who manage to infiltrate it.

    I'll provide more posts on other Gehenna cults after enough time for people to comment (if they have any interest to do so).

                  Related to the social club of "Eden", an ST may want to populate in his games other types of Kindred social clubs so that Eden does not stick out too much. Local traditions like an annual "Harpies Ball" plus other exclusive or prestigious groups (some of which the PCs may qualify for) would make Eden less conspicuous, even if it is the most prestigious.

                  I've done such things in my own chronicles for the purpose of having an interesting background while I'm running some other plot, or a way to allow the PCs to make contacts with the greater Camarilla world beyond the local city. It also helps to just diversify events held at a domain's elysium and provide more interesting flavor to the PCs.

                  I've created groupings like the former Archons of the destroyed Justicar Petrodon holding a reunion, parties held every decade (location rotating around the world) for the Golden Circle (a group of vampires who were responsible for moving many vampires around the world during the Age of Discovery and Age of Sail), and the White Rose (an organization of Camarilla vampires known for their exploits against the Sabbat, particularly during the first Camarilla-Sabbat Wars of the Early Modern Period, but including others who fought the Sabbat much later). Some of these have membership requirements so exclusive nobody else can join (Petrodon isn't around anymore to create more Archons), some lax enough that the PCs could join in the short term, and other groups individual PCs might aspire to.

                  While not directly related to Gehenna cults, I do think having multiple such social clubs makes a concept like Eden more feasible. And having someone who is a member of Eden and another group would allow PCs to interact with a member of Eden and pick things up without actually being invited. Which is important if your chronicle isn't about Elder PCs (like the Elysium book is about), but your more typical neonate PCs.

                      The next Gehenna cult mentioned is the Way of the Ancient Lawgivers, also known as the Lawgivers or Enochians. Founded in 1312, these elders believe all Kindred must return to the values of the First City and serve the Antedeluvians. By doing so they will delay or even cancel Gehenna. Gehenna is simply a punishment by the Antedeluvians towards their recalcitrant descendants. If you obey the Antedeluvians by acting like vampires did in the First City, then you are safe from Gehenna. The Antedeluvians collectively serve Caine, so by serving the Antedeluvians well its members also serve Caine and make everyone a happy family.

                      It seems to basically disavow the Jyhad since the Antedeluvians as a group happily serve Caine and work together. There is no sense that pleasing one Antedeluvian might anger another. The pitfalls of this approach seem clear. But an ST could provide some flavor text to justify the belief.

                      Some members simply believe the cult works well in keeping in the younger Kindred in line, but are not set in actually following the Antedeluvians, even if they exist. A secret cell within the Lawgivers believe vampires should dispense with the Masquerade and rule humanity outright.

                      I have several comments about this group.

                      First, I would make them a front for the Tal'Mahe'Ra, The True Black Hand. In their description, it states they do not accept any Brujah members unless the clan itself forgoes rebellion. This is very close to the True Black Hand;'s refusal to allow any Brujah. I would not say that the True Black Hand controls the Ancient Lawgivers. Just that it has some of its Camarilla members be members of this group, and that they use it as a recruiting pool. I would be heavily tempted to follow the membership requirements of the True Black Hand, meaning the Tremere would also be unlikely members.

                      Second, I would encourage STs to actually read and use the Book of Nod (the actual White Wolf sourcebook) to run this group. If an organization is based on running vampire society according to the ideals of the First City, you need to know what that means. The cult description mentions that certain members violate the principles of "the Rules of the Cainalle". I then looked up the actual Book of Nod sourcebook and saw this is actually a section of the Book of Nod. It's part of the "Chronicles of Shadow" which describes how the First City was run. To understand what the Ancient Lawgivers want to do, understanding this section of the Book of Nod is essential.

                      Of course, not all members of the Way of Ancient Lawgivers actually care about that. Many just want to perpetuate elder rule, and the deference to the Antedeluvians - and therefore the elders as their proxies - is part of this. And for some members, that is more important than actually following the precepts of the Book of Nod in regards to the responsibility of elder vampires towards their childer and humanity. So not all members would fit with the True Black Hand. Many members, in fact, would not. But some members would fit, so I think it would be a good recruiting organization for that sect.

                      Third, that this cult was founded in 1312, before the Anarch Revolt, is important. Since it predates the Anarchs and Camarilla, it makes the cult very old.

                      In terms of how I would use this in the game, I would use them only if I wanted to introduce aspects or plots associated with the True Black Hand. If I wanted to ignore that sect, I would not use this group. I might mention them as some kind of rumor, but not actually introduce in game.

                      Using Chicago By Night as an example, I am not sure if any character would be a member. The characters in CbN aren't particularly interested in the Jyhad or Gehenna. There is a partial example of Hind's Socialists where in the coterie example it is mentioned that this coterie has become interested in Gehenna and trying to survive it. But obviously pleasing the Antedeluvians would not be a goal of a coterie of Anarchs. At most, I might make Modius a member since he occupies an odd place as someone who is occult oriented, a Prince, an Anarch, and generally hard to place in ordinary vampire politics. But through him, PCs might find other members.

                      I'd have to review other By Night books to find characters who are interested in Gehenna, but whose personality suggests someone who would try to please the Antedeluvians. There might be some, though no one comes to mind.

                      While Elysium is based on PCs being elders, and any membership in Gehenna cults based on that status, I do think that unlike the Edenic Groundskeepers (an explicit social club for elders), the non-elders could very well become members. Unlikely, but possibly so. Certainly many elders join the cult on the basis that the First City is a society based on deference to those older than you, there is also a reciprocal obligation - treat those younger than you well. So while neonates and ancillae might be minority members, it is not inconceivable.

                          The next Gehenna cult is the Servitors of Irad. At first glance, this cult seems similar to the Ways of the Ancient Lawgivers, but I think there's some important differences. The Servitors of Irad were founded in 1456 during the Anarch Revolt. It does not actively recruit new members. Instead its members work secretly to serve the Antedeluvians and perform the tasks they Antedeluvians want them to do. Unfortunately, they must only guess as what this will be. But they nevertheless seek to be tools of the Jyhad. By being useful, they ensure they will survive when everyone else dies. They kill other elders and try to divide them so that they will be easier pickings during Gehenna. Membership in this cult is illegal and members will be executed by the Archons.

                          I know this group is mentioned later in Cults of the Blood Gods, but the little I have read of their description in that book does not impress me. I'm going to ignore it since it was published way after Elysium for my discussion of it. And I think we can do a much better job of using this in games then the way that later book handled them.

                          The easiest thing to do is to somehow tie them also into the Tal'Mahe'Ra, but I won't go down that route. The point of having multiple Gehenna cults is to explore different ideas in the setting. Why repeat yourself? I think the Tal'Mahe'Ra may observe this group, maybe even try to manipulate them, but I think they'd try to steer their members away from belonging to it since it is too obvious and too likely to blow the cover of any of their operatives. I want them to be distinct and add something unique to the game, not be duplicative.

                          The key to me is the year this group was founded - 1456. This is in the middle of the Anarch Revolt. Several Antedeluvians have been diablerized in the recent past - Lasombra (1405), Tzimisce (1413), and Cappadocious (1444). And it is not that far from when Saulot fell (1133). The Camarilla has just been established, but we're still several decades from its victory culminating in the Treaty of Thorns. I don't think this timing should be considered irrelevant or a coincidence. I think it should be important in figuring out who the Servitors are and what they are trying to do.

                          What would a group dedicated to doing the will of the Antedeluvians be doing during this age? While their behavior in modern nights is to disrupt the elders to make them easier to defeat by the Antedeluvians, it seems to me in 1456 their goal would be to avenge the destruction of those Antedeluvians destroyed by the Anarchs, and to prevent more Antedeluvians from being destroyed. Only afterwards would the group begin to look for further goals and other services they could provide. Members might even think that the (some?) diableries actually failed - a trap to see who among their children are loyal or false.

                          What about the idea that the antedeluvians are fighting amongst themselves, and thus would not collaborate? There's several ways the Servitors of Irad might answer this objection. One would be that the spate of diablerie during the Anarch Revolt caused them to change plans. They now work together to prevent themselves from being destroyed. Another option might be that the stories of the Jyhad was a lie designed to turn their children against each other so they wouldn't work together and revel against them. Or that their differences will be resolved amicably once Gehenna happens. I'm sure there are more theories others on this forum could add.

                          Initially I see its members being from groups most wanting to avenge the destruction of their Antedeluvian. Probably some elder Lasombra (antitribu) that had survived the purge. I would want to add the Old Clan Tzimisce (or if you prefer, just elder Tzimisce that did not join the Sabbat), but I have the feeling they'd be fewer than the Lasombra because of their territoriality and less likely to want to work with others. And there might be some Cappadocians who were more martial supporters of their founder and less prone to pacifism or passiveness, not caring that their founder seemingly wanted to perish. In modern nights, the last two have dwindled, but the Lasombra anti-tribu should make up a sizable number of the modern Servitors.

                          At one point, there might even have been some of the last warrior Salubri as members. And one thing we might do is that one of the goals of the Servitors would be too protect any remaining Salubri, as that would be what Saulot (and the other Antedeluvians) would want. I like the irony of making the Servitors of Irad, a group that seems mad and dangerous and an obvious antagonist in the setting, as one of the main protectors of the "soul-sucking" and diablerist Salubri that the NPCs of the setting mainly hate, but whom PCs often like.

                          What about the other clans?

                          Both Ventrue and Brujah already have destroyed founders, and are proud of their independence. The Brujah because they are rebels, and the Ventrue because of the prestige that their clan is considered to be the least manipulated by the Antedeluvians as a result and thus trusted to provide leadership. I don't see many members of these clans in the Servitors except for those who are particularly perverse or paranoid. After all, they don't have an antedeluvian who will look after them, so they'd be relying on some other clan's founder to protect them which doesn't seem too smart. Likewise, Tremere would also be a bad fit. Any who would join would have to be a strange bird. Not impossible that there are members of these three clans, but not the most likely suspects.

                          That leaves the Gangrel, Malkavian, Toreador, and Nosferatu.

                          Nosferatu may seem like a strange choice since they believe their Antedeluvian is already after them. But that's exactly why you might have some Nosferatu who think they will be spared by doing the bidding of their Founder. If so, some rumors and legends of the Nictuku might actually be in reference to actions done by Nosferatu members of the Servitors of Irad.

                          The Gangrel, Malkavian, and Toreador would all seemingly have real motives to obey their founder. I would think members of these clans would make up the bulk of the Servitors of the Irad outside the Lasombra anti-tribu. I think the Malkavian members might even be key since it is possible some would think they might actually have some communication with their founder given the Madness Network. An elder Malkavian who claims to be given some kind of possible guidance would be someone the other Servitors would listen or even follow. Why not make a Malkavian the leader or one of the leaders of the group? (Assuming of course, you don't make Montano himself the leader of the Servitors as he is the most loyal childe of Lasombra himself. He might have even been the one who founded the group).

                          I'm undecided how to approach the Followers of Set and Ravnos in regards to the Servitors. Those clans are fairly distant from the elder elysium culture depicted in Elysium, and estranged from greater vampire society in general. I doubt there are any of their clan members among the Servitors. But it is an intriguing question as to how the Servitors service of the Antedeluvians see those clans, especially the Setites in regards to their own founder. Are they considered enemies? Allies? Potential allies who have gotten things wrong?

                              It is said the Servitors do not actively recruit. So many of its members are probably very old, and some even date to the original founding. Not actively recruiting is not the same as never recruiting. If they see someone who desires the same things as them, they probably make contact somehow and put her through various tests or verifications. But they are probably a conspiracy of some very powerful elders who have great powers and lots of influence.

                              I see the Servitors working as a kind of star chamber within the Camarilla and greater vampire society, waging their own Jyhad based on what they think the Antedeluvians want them to do. Which elders are to be slain? Which plots by the Inner Circle and Justicars are to be thwarted? Whom among the Anarchs will they destroy? How will they gain revenge on the Sabbat and destroy certain key leaders there (like Gratiano, Lambach, and others who participated in the attacks against the Antedeluvians)? Which ploys of the Tremere and Giovanni will they expose?

                              One important thing to remember is that they just don't go after Anarchs and enemies of the Camarilla. They want to disrupt the other elders as well because dividing the elders prevents them from uniting against the Antedeluvians. They might even see the Ventrue as one of their greatest enemies because of their supposed independence from the machinations of the Antedeluvians.

                              More than the other Gehenna cults in Elysium, the Servitors have the best shot at being active antagonists in a chronicle. An ST should make the most of them. I would probably elevate them to the same status or level as better known groups like the Children of Osiris or even the Black Hand within the Sabbat. Make their name a known element of the setting along with these other groups. "The Servitors of Irad are the hand of the Antedeluvians, if you cross them be prepared to pay the price." They would also make a great bogeymen for the Sabbat. Whenever they have an unexpected defeat, they might blame the Servitors.

                              I'd make all members of the Servitors make oaths similar to the Superman Revenge Squad of DC Comics, but in line to serving the Antedeluvians and destroying their enemies. (Only the first minute on this clip, I could not find a shorter one.)


                              They are not interested in recreating the First City. They may even think it is irrelevant in discussions about Gehenna. But they are loyal to their clan founders (whether out of filial piety, fear, or some other motivation) individually and collectively, and want to survive Gehenna and think they will be rewarded for their faithful service.

                              I wouldn't play them as buffoons or thugs. I'd play them as being very dangerous, and more likely to act through proxies and unwitting dupes than do things themselves. But they do dirty their hands when needed - when their personal power is necessary to see a task done. But secrecy is still very important to them. When things go wrong in a domain, when important Kindred mysteriously die, etc., they are one of the groups named as perhaps being responsible along with the other usual suspects. And of course, they act in other disruptive ways that do not deal in violence at all - sabotaging votes at Conclaves, spreading false rumors, and so on.

                              Instead of being antagonists to the PCs, they might make interesting patrons who use the PCs as unwitting pawns. That elder who helps the Anarch PC against the Prince really isn't sympathetic to the Anarchs. He just wants to weaken the Prince before the next Conclave is held so he won't rally the votes needed to pass an important resolution. Or because he wants the Prince dead as he's too effective in uniting the other elders together. Or he helps a PC get revenge on someone the Servitors have already targeted for destruction. It's just really good the blame will go to the PC instead of the elder.

                              I can't see any NPCs in Chicago By Night (or even other cities) being members of the Servitors. None really have the right mentality. We might be more successful by looking at Dark Ages sourcebooks and picking a character who would be a good member. As I said, I think Montano might be a good choice.

                              I do see characters like Son and Nathaniel Bordruff as being of possible use to the Servitors of Irad. But nothing in their background would indicate a propensity to join. Gehenna just doesn't seem a factor to them. But I would be willing to create an NPC in Chicago who is a member, who would try to make use of both of these characters when necessary.

                                      Every ST will come up with a different list of goals for the Servitors of Irad. Some goals and very specific, and others very vague. The vague ones would lead to any number of lesser plots. It is not important whether any of these goals are actually the will of one or more Antedeluvians. It's just that it would make sense for the Servitors of Irad to believe their goals reasonably reflect those wills. In fact, I like the idea that in certain goals the Servitors are likely helping the Antedeluvians, that others do nothing, and some are even against their wishes.

                                      But I do think if one is going to use the Servitors of Irad, they'd work best if they had specific goals. I must admit I have never used them (or any other Gehenna cult) in game. But now that I've given them some thought, the below would be the goals I'd consider using for them.
                                      • Hunt down and destroy Sabbat Archbishop Gratiano (of Rio de Janeiro I believe, though I don't know when this city was mentioned), the diablerist (or would-be diablerist) of Lasombra. Slay any of his surviving conspirators among the Lasombra and Assamites who were with him.
                                      • Find the secret torpor ground of the diablerist (or would be diablerist) of Tzimisce, Lugoj the Bloodbreaker, and destroy him. Slay his surviving conspirators like Lambach. (We know Lugoj is actually dead, but to everyone else - other than Lambach - in the setting, Lugoj succeeded but simply disappeared and put himself in torpor until he will rise again. So I think discovering his hiding place so they can destroy him would be a goal. It's not the Servitors' fault that their primary target is already destroyed.)
                                      • Hunt down and destroy Augustus Giovanni, Claudius Giovanni, and the surviving members of the Conspiracy of Isaac - those who destroyed Cappadocious.
                                      • Discover a way to ruin the Vaulderie and restore the ability of the blood bond.
                                      • Protect the surviving Salubri.
                                      • Protect the surviving true Cappadocians.
                                      • Undermine the Tremere Inner Council of Seven and thwart their plans.
                                      • Undermine the Giovanni and thwart their plans.
                                      • Prevent the Camarilla's Inner Circle, Justicars, and Archons from increasing their power and authority so that the elders are kept divided and weak.
                                      • Identify any elders actively working against the Antedeluvians or trying to stop Gehenna so they can be destroyed.
                                      • Aid the Nictuku by destroying select elders of the Nosferatu who can organize the others to oppose them.
                                      • Find and destroy the Ravnos Methusaleh Vestgeir who saved the Sabbat.
                                      • Thwart the Sabbat and destroy the Black Hand.
                                      • Punish and destroy all diablerists when able.
                                      • Suppress knowledge of the Thaumaturgical Ritual of the Bitter Rose.
                                      • Confirm the destruction of Troile at Carthage and if she was not, find her and destroy her.
                                      • Aid in defeating the modern Anarchs.
                                      • Overthrow the Anarch Free State in Los Angeles.
                                      • Learn the secrets of the Inconnu and determine if they are a threat to the Antedeluvians and their plans.
                                      • Discover the resting locations of the Antedeluvians and protect them from their enemies.
                                      • Infiltrate and destroy from within other Gehenna cults.
                                      This list is not meant to be comprehensive. I'm sure there are other good ideas that do not even occur to me. Nor do I think the Servitors need to be acting on so many goals. The actual things they do may be more limited.

                                      Even this list reveals A LOT of goals that the Servitors likely have. Many of them are probably impossible to achieve, but I don't think the Servitors are actually that concerned about fulfilling each one. It is more important that demonstrate sincerity in carrying out the desires of the Antedeluvians. The Servitors may not even coordinate as a group. It might be individual Kindred acting and asking support when needed. Or local cells cooperating. Or a mix of different levels of cooperation depending on the mission. And of course, being a member of the Servitors of Irad is not a full time job. Different members will have different levels of commitment, time, and effort that they spend.

                                      They may even think their contributions are meaningless since nothing can prevent Gehenna from happening and the Antedeluvians from winning. They don't even necessarily need to believe that Lasombra is actually destroyed for example. Destroying Gratiano doesn't need to be done out of avenging Lasombra, but simply because a hidden Lasombra would want such temerity punished and reward anyone who did after he returns.

Is someone trying to kill them? Or to explain some of the aid rendered to Salubri or Cappadocians over the years - the PCs might think that some elder is really just being a nice person, when in fact it is done simply out of spite against two clans the Servitors consider to be foes. Or to have the Servitors prioritize one particular diablerist as an enemy and make sure she is hounded until destroyed. That lets the STs to sprinkle all kind of rumors that the Servitors are responsible for this event or that one.

                                                  The Ninth Wave is a relatively late Gehenna cult being founded only in 1890. All the others were founded centuries earlier. This cult believes Gehenna will arrive when magic in the world reaches a critical mass, which they term “the second Flood”. The level of magic is what keeps the Antedeluvians in torpor or makes them active. Therefore, the Ninth Wave acts to destroy and suppress the level of magic in this world in order to prevent the Antedeluvians from waking.

                                                  The cult's name comes from an Alfred, Lord Tennyson poem inside the Idylls of the King. The Idylls is a cycle of twelve narrative poems in blank verse about the Arthurian legends. The Ninth Wave is an excerpt for the first idyll, the Coming of Arthur. The last idyll was published in 1885, only five years before the cult was established. This is likely not a coincidence. For convenience, I am posting an excerpt here.

                                                  Wave after wave, each mightier than the last,
                                                  Till last, a ninth one, gathering half the deep
                                                  And full of voices, slowly rose and plunged
                                                  Roaring, and all the wave was in a flame:
                                                  And down the wave and in the flame was borne
                                                  A naked babe, and rode to Merlin's feet,
                                                  Who stoopt and caught the babe, and cried, 'The King!
                                                  Here is an heir for Uther!' And the fringe
                                                  Of that great breaker, sweeping up the strand,
                                                  Lashed at the wizard as he spake the word,
                                                  And all at once all round him rose in fire,
                                                  So that the child and he were clothed in fire.
                                                  And presently thereafter followed calm,
                                                  Free sky and stars.

                                                  According to this webpage, the phrase the ninth wave is an old sailing expression. It refers to a monster-like wave following a succession of incrementally larger ones. Depicted in literature as the Wave of Transformation, writers and poets have portrayed this transformation as both startling and serene.

                                                  This leads to all sorts of crossover goodness if you like that kind of thing. It pits the vampires of this cult against any Mages except the Technocrachy. In fact, there is even an alliance between these vampires and the Technocracy of sorts, although it is mainly limited to the vampires providing funding for their labs. But if an ST wanted, this could easily change to something more ignorant and the vampires of the Ninth Wave only inadvertently supporting the Technocracy. But I think it was clearly intended to justify crossover with Mage.

                                                  These vampires also want to exterminate the fairies. They believe that the "return" of the faeries to Earth is a major reason Gehenna will begin. In fact, they call this return "the ninth wave" which could imply that fairy extermination is the major reason for the cult to exist in the first place. Elysium was published in 1994, and first edition Changeling was to be published the next year. I don't think certain specifics had been worked out by then, but I do think this Gehenna cult was written with Changeling in mind using what basic information they had at the time. Vampire-Changeling conflict has never really been a thing in either gameline, but if you want to justify it then the Ninth Wave is for you.

                                                  The Ninth Wave is also obsessed about the Masquerade since revealing that vampires exist would only make people think the supernatural is real, and that would lead to even more magic in the world. As a result, they absolutely hate the Sabbat because of its lack of concern over the Masquerade. Since the Lasombra and Tzimisce did not actually destroy their antedeluvian clan founders, and the Black Hand of the Sabbat was secretly controlled by the Tal'Mahe'Ra, this probably makes the Ninth Wave the most effective Gehenna cult so far. They seem to get a lot of things right, tying it into the greater World of Darkness lines being published, though the specifics of the level of magic and the antedeluvians waking has never been established.

                                                  I don't think it makes sense to use this Gehenna cult in a Vampire game unless the ST intends to use a lot of crossover. If you don't, you lose most of the reason the cult exists and would seem to be pretty boring. But there are some aspects of the cult that might be played.

                                                  First, the cult's understanding of the nature of magical reality is very much based on the hermetic understanding of the Tremere (and through them the Order of Hermes and Mage). Tremere can often make bad members of many Gehenna cults because they don't have ties to an Antedeluvian or the First City except in a roundabout way. But they would be very important members in this cult. Second, its relatively recent founding means this could be a Gehenna cult that is much more open to ancillae and neonates rather than elders. The cult is approaching Gehenna in a very different way than other Gehenna cults, suggesting a great deal of innovation and creativity. Elders are not known for that. I think these approaches should be emphasized to distinguish the Ninth Wave from other Gehenna cults. If you can think of any other indirect ways to prevent the Antedeluvians from waking (they don't involve direct assaults or attacks on their torpored bodies), I would add it to this group. I like the novelty that vampires might prevent Gehenna by doing something that indirectly affects the Antedeluvians.

                                                  The other trivia we are given about the cult is that they maintain an extensive library in Edinburgh, Scotland. The library contains a lot of occult information - magical tomes, ancient manuscripts, vials of Lupine, Faerie, and Mage blood to be used for experimentation, and a "complete map of Earth's ley lines".

                                                  So Edinburgh is probably the headquarters of the cult. Otherwise why maintain such an expensive library there? Traditionally, Scotland is supposed to be a stronghold of the Toreador. So I would include local Toreador as some prominent members of this cult. I would also create an Edinburgh Toreador that is the clan's foremost expert on Lord Tennyson and the Idylls of the King. After all, she was the one who probably named the cult after the poem.

                                                  Few vampires are able to maintain such an extensive occult library. I would posit then that the local Tremere Chantry at Edinburgh are members of this cult as well, and that this library is maintained by the Tremere (although it may not be an official part of the chantry since non-Tremere members of the cult will want to access it too). Edinburgh is not that big of a city. Metro area is only around 900,000 people. There might be ten vampires there (and we need to fit in some Dunsirn as well - though as an interesting thought maybe a Dunsirn is a member). So maybe the Tremere chantry is only one or two vampires. But if that is the case, it'd be very easy for this Tremere regent to devote his chantry to the needs of a Gehenna cult. To other Tremere, they're just getting a lot of reports about experiments with fairy blood. And who would associate that with membership in a Gehenna cult? Who knows, maybe much of the domain of Edinburgh is part of the Ninth Wave. It'd be interesting flavor for anyone who wants to do an Edinburgh (or Scotland) by Night chronicle.

                                                  Of course, any good Gehenna cult has to be found elsewhere. But if the Ninth Wave was founded in 1890 in Edinburgh, realistically it would spread when vampires who were members in Edinburgh moved to other cities and recruited new members in that city. This would make the cult rather small, and likely concentrated in the British Isles with only a few domains outside that area.

                                                  So we have limited options if we go to Chicago By Night. But there is one possible candidate found in the second edition book - the Malkavian Corbin from "the Trinity". I must admit that while I do like the character and background of these three characters, that they took up seven whole pages for them (plus one more in the Coteries description). That's 2+ pages per vampire when most vampires in the book were about a page. And their "coterie" was so self-contained that they could have been set anywhere. There is no reason for them to be in Chicago. This was clearly a case of someone's favorite character idea being planted in a city book that didn't need them.

                                                  But for our purposes, Corbin has established ties to Edinburgh and only left that area when other Kindred threatened her and she randomly picked Chicago as her next haven. Her dementia is extreme fixation so that if something catches her interest, she becomes obsessed with it. In other words, it would be really easy to justify her being a member of the Ninth Wave if the ST so wanted. We just need to say that as a Malkavian who spent much of her time in Edinburgh, she was introduced to the cult, thought it interesting, and is now an obsessed member of it. When she had to flee to Chicago, her obsession would continue and she would want to recruit new members to the Ninth Wave so she could continue working on her interest. In fact, if we went down that route, Corbin could easily have been one of its founding members.

                                                  If you wanted to use the Trinity in a Chicago chronicle, but couldn't figure out why any PC would want to enter that mess, then you now have one way to do so. If a PC would be interested in a group like the Ninth Wave, then they could become involved in Corbin through whatever means the ST devises to introduce them. Then that triangle becomes a nice complication to spring AFTER they become an associate of her.

                                                  I would likely not use the Ninth Wave myself unless I really wanted to include crossover, or have come up with other indirect means a cult might "stop" the Antedeluvians. I would not have it be a very large group, and would likely limit its presence to one vampire recently arrived in the domain who had been a member back in the British Isles. But one positive thing about the group is that its members seem reliably informed on a lot of things, and you have justification for travel to Edinburgh to visit the library if you ever want to run that in your chronicle. It would also provide an established, but still small Gehenna cult that the PCs might become important members in, and that would not be possible in most other cults that have many more members and been around much longer. And you can play a lot with an established theme - Tennyson's poem and his other works - as a way for members of the cult to provide secret clues to each other that they need to meet. That kind of flavor text and use in game is something that players tend to love, and it is lacking with some of the other Gehenna cults. This would be a very easy theme for STs and PCs to include.

                                                      The last Gehenna cult described in the Elysium sourcebook is the poorly named Cult of Enlightenment. After some very evocative names, I guess a banal one was inevitable. The Cult of Enlightenment was founded in AD 510. Its members believe that Gehenna can be prevented by elevating the general level of “enlightened” thinking in the world. Its original three founders were elders each of the Salubri, Tzimisce, and Nosferatu clans. Achieving Golconda is an important task, but not the only one for its members. The cult has declined in recent centuries as older members disappear, and new members aren't very concerned about the cult's actual purpose.

                                                      The three founding elders have not been seen for more than a thousand years, and its unknown if they are still active, destroyed, or in torpor. The three founders are more than just trivia, since they wrote down of body of knowledge that is the basis of cult doctrine. It includes their conversations with Tibetan monks, aboriginal wise people, Mages, elder Lupines, and so on - the typical shorthand in early WoD for "wisdom". The number of these "scrolls of enlightenment" are said to be either eight, nine, or thirteen. The cult today only has three of the original scrolls (they may have others as copies). There are various rumors on who have the other original scrolls including the usual suspects like "the Mages". The Arcanum might also have one.

                                                      Another rumor states that members of the Cult approached the Catholic Church, hoping to learn more wisdom, but it only confirmed that vampires existed and may have been one of the reasons the Inquisition (which I am assuming they mean the Society of Leopold) formed.

                                                      Early cult members were said to be very successful in achieving Golconda, but that has severely declined. Most of its members are very ancient, even for elders, and they are part of the cult in the hope it will help them save their last vestiges of Humanity and avoid the Wassail.

                                                      The weakest aspect of this cult is the weak reasoning behind why a "semi-universal enlightenment" would prevent Gehenna. How exactly was this idea reached? At least the theories of the Ninth Wave are actually plausible in terms of WoD metaphysics even if not proven. But the Gehenna aspect of this Gehenna cult is rather weak.

                                                      Like all the cults, it is also underdeveloped. An ST is going to have to do a lot of work preparing the Cult of Enlightenment for her game. She will need to create the names and backstories of the three founding elders, the basic ideas of their philosophy, lots of flavor text, and description of at least some of these scrolls.

                                                      However, there is a lot this cult potentially offers PCs. First, it can be used to pass information about Golconda. Second, I'm interpreting the oblique mention of "Tibetan monks" means the Children of Osiris, so it is a way to pass on knowledge about this small sect. Probably the Inconnu as well. Third, you could possibly use it to introduce certain crossover factions - say certain Mages and Stargazer tribe of Garou. Third, you can use the "original scrolls of enlightenment" as a MacGuffin hunt to send the PCs around the world trying to find the scrolls, or just one scroll as an important plot point in a single domain. Fourth, you have the mystery of the disappeared three founders. Were they destroyed? If so, by who and when? Can they be found and woken up? Is one or more still active?

                                                      The Western Roman Empire had fallen less than fifty years previously, but the Classical World had not yet died. Neither the destructive Gothic Wars of Justinian in Italy, or the Muslim conquests that disrupted the existing Mediterranean trade world had happened yet. But the old Roman order was in seriously decline. I would rule that the previous century's decline had worried the original founders, and they could see a coming civilizational collapse. They might have believed they were in the first stages of Gehenna much like vampires of the game's publishing era was supposed to believe.

Constantinople probably works best. Then its members would have moved around the rest of the world. Unfortunately, we can't use any of the canon characters in Constantinople By Night since the founders would have disappeared before then. But we can use it to identify characters who might have been members of the Cult of Enlightenment at the time of that sourcebook. They would fit the cult members descriptions as being ancient. I looked for people who were scholarly or spiritual, but not tied directly into the Michael plotline or have some other goal or interest that would be distracting.

                                                      I think it would be interesting if Myca Vykos was once a member, later changing his views about Gehenna and using any knowledge gained about "enlightenment" to help form the Sabbat Paths of Enlightenment.

                                                      Achmet the Dreamer of the Salubri, and leader of the Dream Circle (which also include Myca). Many of the other members of the Dream Circle would also make good members, but I'm reluctant to simply make this coterie and the Cult of Enlightenment one in the same. If having a Salubri was too obvious, I'd switch Achmet out for Gregory the Wonder Maker.

                                                      There are certainly other characters who could fit, and would be interested in hearing which ones people would select.

                                                      Once again, this is a cult that does not easily track to any characters in a more modern city like described in Chicago By Night. But I'd likely make Critias be AWARE of them, though never a member. And he might be able to put a PC into contact with an old acquaintance he knew was or is in the cult. If Critias' Humanity ever started declining or he began developing Methusaleh's Hunger, then he might change his mind about the Cult and attempt to join it in desperate hope it has some secret that could help him exactly as so many others have done.

                                                          Summary

                                                          Overall, I think there is a lot of good in this Appendix of Elysium. Each of the cults provides a different experience for a PC. Even cults like the Ancient Lawgivers and Servitors of Irad that have a similar purpose, have a very different way of implementing. I like that a lot. We don't have duplicate concepts, but a wide variety. But what does this mean for actual chronicles?

                                                          Like a lot of concepts in the World of Darkness, an easy mistake would be to use them too much. Few cities should have even one such cult represented, and very few would have more than one. There can only be so many cults and so many members.

                                                          While I used Chicago By Night characters as examples of how to use most of the individual cults, I actually would not use every cult there. I would probably introduce the "Eden" social club that meets several times a year for gala celebrations, and have a few members of it be secret members of the Edenic Groundskeepers (instead of presenting rival "Royal Order" and "Imperial Order", I'd just have the members argue a lot when they met). I might add the Ninth Wave after Corbin arrives into the city. And that would be it. Even a large domain like Chicago can have only so many Gehenna cultists. If I had a specific plot in mind that required one of the other Gehenna cults, I'd just run that.

                                                          For chronicles sent in other domains, I'd want to change it up and have no Gehenna cults at all or use a different one. If the elders were firmly in control, maybe use the Way of the Ancient Lawgivers for example.

                                                          I will probably start using a Camarilla-wide Elysium social clubs like "Eden" (as well as some others) as a general complement to the setting. But I would not place secret members of the Edenic Groundskeeper in every city where the general membership of Eden exists. The general membership of Eden should not know about the Edenic Groundskeepers, though that may be something that is gradually learned about.

                                                          Rather than trying to shoehorn in any of these Gehenna cults in a chronicle and fit NPCs to them, I think it is better to consider groups like these cults as STs create the NPCs for their chronicles. How might Chicago By Night have changed if the concept of these Gehenna cults existed beforehand, and it was intended certain NPCs would belong in them (or perhaps be potential invitees as the chronicle progresses). Thinking about these things beforehand can help diversify NPC concepts and give key characters ties to the greater setting.

                                                          I think mysteries are very important for a game like vampire. And Gehenna cult membership (or sympathies with such a cult) is something that can be easily added. It gives more depths to those characters, gives them something else to do, and can act as a means to tie the PCs to the greater world of Darkness.

                                                          For the most part, I'd restrict membership to elders and ancillae. Neonates just don't have the experience or mentality to truly be members. Their thoughts are not on Gehenna, but building their own power and complaining about domain politics. Though PCs might become involved as pawns or servants of the members of such cults. I would probably also try to keep membership to less than 10% of the total population of elders and ancillae. Gehenna cult membership should be rare, not typical. Fewer is better.

                                                          But what if you want to show off these concepts. Well, I'd expand the game then. Conclaves can be a great use here. Conclaves can last for months as many Kindred gather to debate whatever the issues are. Vampire population of a city swells in size. What a perfect time then for many such Gehenna cults to arrive, and its members find time to secretly meet and discuss business. That could be worth several game sessions of play, and leave some of the PCs with important contacts with vampires in other cities. By enlarging the setting that way, more concepts can be used without it seeming like everyone in the home domain is secretly a member of some cult or other group.
