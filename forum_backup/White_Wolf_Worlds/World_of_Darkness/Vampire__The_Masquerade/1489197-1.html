                    Explaining politics to people without the dots... I have no fucking clue how you guys could think mono-clan cities are a more natural state than multi clan cities.

                    I can't see any reason why any of the clans, besides the Assamites, would want to maintain a state of one-clan per city. Clans stereotype one another, but very few are actually hostile to one another. Ventrue and Brujah are rivals, but they're not enemies. Toreador and Nosferatu are polar opposites, but they're not enemies.

                    Toreador are set up to be fractious. Brujah are set up to be fractious. If you threw an even number of all clans into any city, A Toreador's worst enemies is very likely going to be another Toreador. They have comparable skills. They have similar hunting strategies. They don't really have a weakness that binds them together like a few clans. They don't need one another. They might have a few things that they have in common, they might enjoy eachother's company, but the same could be true for any other clan. Ironically, the Nosferatu and Toreador might have outward distaste from one another, but they make for better neighbours: The Nosferatu doesn't covet the Toreador's club, the Toreador doesn't covet the Nosferatu's warrens. The Toreador isn't likely to enroach on the Nosferatu's spy trade, and the Nos isn't likely to fight for many of the Toreador's friends.

                    A Toreador offers another Toreador... slightly different friends. A Nosferatu offers a Nosferatu... it's not much different, assuming similar ages. The Overlap is strong. Meanwhile Toreador and Nosferatu can offer eachother almost everything without much overlap. Pragmatically speaking, A Nosferatu is a better ally for a Toreador than another Toreador. If the city has a shortage of clubs and you're the 9th grandchild of the prince and space is limited, how are you realistically going to get your leg up on your totem pole against equal and more capable rivals? A Nosferatu would be a greater ally than the many Toreador, and if you turn him away, perhaps one of your rivals will make use of him. Potentially it'd be a waste of your resources to chase him away and that would give an advantage to your rivals.

                    Imagine you're the patriarch of all the city's Toreador. You need to control your sociopathic kids. If vampires from other clans want to settle in your city, are you really in an ideal position to say no? Mixed coteries will dominate single-clan coteries of a similar age. If mixed coteries lose a political battle in their home city, they will likely find success in taking over mono-clan cities. The clans probably evolved in the first place because specialized divisions of labour and prey types was a better option than non-specialized vampires.

                                                            Ehh, I think he's correct insofar as it's logical that if the Camarilla didn't exist then you'd certainly see clans looking to maximize their power over certain cities and territories and doing their best to keep "their" cities and the choicest of domains outside of the hands of other clans. For example I definitely think that the Ventrue would be doing their best to keep the number of vampires from "low clans" to a minimum in their own cities and domains. Which is how things operated before the Camarilla existed. Instead, because of the Camarilla, clans are essentially forced to play nice and share territory and resources with one another.

                                                            When you look at the Dark Ages and earlier settings, you can see that this was sort of a thing, especially with High Clan vampires, which often claimed control over specific geographical territories or areas. Ventrue tended to congregate in Italy and then moved up to Germany after the Roman Empire fell, the Lasombra congregated in the Iberian Peninsula and northern Africa, the Tzimsce congregated in Eastern Europe, the Brujah in northern Africa until Carthage was destroyed, etc. The Cappadocians were the only High Clan that wasn't really tied to a specific geographical range, though they did have a lot of influence in Turkey.

                                                            While not "mono-clans" many of those places ended up as majority-clans where the majority of vampires in that area were from a specific clan, and the rulers of most of the cities in that territory were members of that clan. And if vampires from a different clan attempted to overthrow a leader or take a city, they would often end up drawing the ire of other clanmates and leaders of nearby territories. Those clans controlled most of everything and other vampires were essentially guests in that territory. If clan Ventrue decided they didn't want you in Germany, you probably would not be sticking around for too long.

                                                            These days with the Camarilla, clans aren't really tied to geography the way they used to be, and cities and domains are far more equal than they were in the past. And the point of the Camarilla is that it's the explanation for why modern Kindred society is so much more accepting, diverse and equal than it used to be, because it makes post-Camarilla vampire society more interesting in terms of drama and story fodder than pre-Camarilla vampire society.
