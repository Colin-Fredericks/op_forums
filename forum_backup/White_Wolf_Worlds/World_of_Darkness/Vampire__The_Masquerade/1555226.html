    Bloodlines 2 And The Current State Of Paradox

    11-07-2025, 10:00 PM
    Last year September Paradox's leadership dropped a bit of a bombshell in what they thought about the (then upcoming) release of Bloodlines 2:

    "It is not in our strategic direction to make this kind of game," Lilja says. "So if Bloodlines 2, God willing, is successful, Bloodlines 3 [will be] done by someone else, on the licence from us. I would say it's the sort of strategic way this would work. So it's still an outlier from what we're supposed to do, we don't know that stuff, so we should probably let other people do it."

    Paradox has its own small-scale experimental label, Paradox Arc, where it can try different things, but a big RPG doesn't remotely fit that. It would have to be something "very different", says Lilja, like a CRPG, but even that's a "big investment", and nowadays expectations are higher than ever thanks to Baldur's Gate 3. So "regardless of outcome," he says, Bloodlines is a "dead end".
    Here we are, a little bit more than a year later and Bloodlines 2 has come out. I've mentioned in other threads what I've thought of the game but this is more about the stats. The game sits on Metacritic with a 63 (Mixed or Average), with the majority of reviews being in the Mixed-category. On Steam, it similarly has a Mixed-rating (57% of the Steam-user-reviews are positive).

    But what does that mean?

    Paradox's report of January - September 2025 has dropped a few weeks ago. Some highlights (source: wnhub.com);

    • Paradox Interactive earned a total of 1.3 billion Swedish kronor (140.3 million dollars) over the nine months. This is 12% less than the previous year.
    • Almost all the revenue came from PC games, which generated 1.14 billion Swedish kronor (122 million dollars) or 86.9% of the total revenue. Compared to last year, this amount dropped by 11%.
    • Console games contributed another 119.7 million Swedish kronor (12.75 million dollars, down 21%). Mobile games accounted for 36.5 million Swedish kronor (3.9 million dollars, down 5.5%).
    • Despite the decline in revenue, Paradox Interactive's operating profit increased by 20%. It amounted to 391.4 million Swedish kronor (41.7 million dollars).
    • The company attributed the decline in revenue to the strengthening of the Swedish krona and fewer releases. Moreover, in the third quarter of 2025 (July-September), they heavily invested in marketing for Vampire: The Masquerade — Bloodlines 2 and Europa Universalis V.
    • Paradox Interactive is not ready to draw conclusions about the release of Vampire: The Masquerade — Bloodlines 2 yet, as the game was released only a few days before the report's publication, making it too early to assess. However, the company is closely monitoring audience reactions. It should be noted that the game currently has a "mixed" rating on Steam, with only 55% of reviews being positive.
    • Currently, Paradox Interactive is working on eight new games.
    But we also got some estimates for how much Paradox spent on Bloodlines 2's development. Swedish analysts for Swedbank (the subsidiary Swedbank Robur is a shareholder of Paradox Interactive) think that Paradox has spent somewhere around 60 million dollars on Bloodlines 2 (without marketing). According to VG Insights, the game has earned 13 million dollars so far (that's also just a rough estimate).

    The question is that despite Paradox financing DLC for Bloodlines 2, whether they will stick to their mindset of "Bloodlines 2 is a dead end." with these sales-figures in mind. Obviously, it was always a huge challenge for the TCR-version of this game to get them out of the financial hole that the years of development-hell had created. But it matters who Paradox-leadership will blame for having gotten them into this situation. And how does Paradox evaluate the WOD-license as a whole after they've found who's to blame for all of this? Will it just be visual novels and text-adventures for WOD-games for the rest of time? Or will they consider ever doing something on the level of a Bloodlines 2 (minus the development-hell-bit, of course) again?

    Personally, I think this quarter will reaffirm this "Bloodlines 2 is a dead end"-mindset because the real money-getter for Paradox will probably end up being Europa Universalis 5 (another typical Paradox grand strategy game) rather than Bloodlines 2. I guess we'll see what Paradox will end up saying about the Bloodlines 2 release but right now I don't see how the leadership won't just stick to the ideas they expressed last year.

              I tend to agree that the World of Darkness brand has been significantly diminished. Not only through Bloodlines' long and messy development, but also through years of inconsistent messaging and missed opportunities. What once carried a strong identity (dark, edgy, angst or mature and narratively ambitious) has been diluted into something that feels hesitant and risk averse.
              I think we all agree that the problem is not that the IP lacks potential. It is that Paradox never found a coherent strategy for how to use it. They tried to fit the WoD into their existing publishing logic (small-scale AA projects, safe returns, outsourcing creative risk), but the setting demands something more unified and deliberate. Right now, it’s stuck between prestige narrative ambitions and low-cost exploitation of brand nostalgia (which I would actually prefer, if they can not find something cool to do).

              If Paradox wants to salvage what is left of the brand, I see a few possible paths:
              1. Sell or license the IP to a partner that actually wants to develop it long-term. (Oh hi onyxpath, what are you doing here at this time of the night?) Someone that knows the IP and understands the fanbase better. Someone with the creative will to make WoD a modern gothic IP. If Paradox wants to mix stuff up I would think of Focus Entertainment (warhammer 40k Space marine 2) or even CDPR (witcher/cyberpunk) if they weren’t already busy. It is better to have a smaller slice of a thriving brand than total control of a stagnant one.
              2. Reboot the WoD as a transmedia franchise again, but with clearer creative oversight.Let Bloodlines 2 mark the end of this chapter. Then start over with an integrated approach: align TTRPG, video games, and maybe even narrative media (Books, comics, etc.) under a single creative direction. Something like what Games Workshop finally learned to do with Warhammer after decades of chaos.
              3. Refocus on the TTRPG side and use it to rebuild trust.If they do not want to spend AAA money, they could still make WoD relevant by reinvesting in the tabletop ecosystem. New systems, digital tools, or narrative supplements that rebuild the fanbase's faith before touching big games again. Oh a wild onyx path appears… again.
              4. Go “boutique”. Treat WoD like a Paradox “small indie” approach.
                Smaller but high-quality but focussed narrative projects on kickstarter/backkit with expensive premium dice, books etc
              5. Or just mix between the points above.
              To me, it is not about how much the WoD costs Paradox. It is about whether it still fits their identity.                       One thing I forgot to add about the analysis from the Swedish bank is that they worry about the future outlook for Paradox is that aside from their core grand-strategy games their future release-schedule is a bit light. And that makes me wonder how happy the shareholders will be if Paradox-leadership really sticks with this "Bloodlines 2 is a dead end"-sentiment that essentially translates to "Making anything aside from grand strategy games is too hard and not worth it.". That isn't an exactly optimistic premise for a videogame-company's outlook. And while these grand strategy games are popular and make a profit, they're also just a tiny niche on the videogame-market. The whole point of Paradox as a publisher in the last couple of years was to expand and publish games in a lot of other genres as well. Acquiring the WOD-license was part of that. So, to now go back to "Let's just focus on grand strategy games" has a whiff of "We're back to square one.". And like with Bloodlines 2, saying things like "Making RPGs after the release of Baldurs Gate 3 is too hard." is a poor excuse for mismanaging the WOD-license.

                      But also hilariously, these Swedish analysts note Prison Architect 2 as the big 2026 release aside from their typical DLC releases for the grand strategy games. It's hilarious because the last update stated the game was delayed indefinitely. Maybe they know something as shareholders that the public doesn't but hey, maybe they're right. Maybe the game will come out next year...

                      Notably, Prison Architect 2's development history will sound very familiar in the context of Bloodlines 2. Here's a summary of its development-history from Wikipedia:

                      The game was revealed in January 2024 by Paradox Interactive, who acquired the title from Introversion Software in 2019. The game was initially developed by Double Eleven, who took over the developing duty of the first installment after the acquisition. However, a change in development studio was announced by Paradox Interactive on 13 May 2024, as Paradox Interactive and Double Eleven were unable to reach a commercial agreement. Kokku, who had been working alongside Double Eleven since mid 2023, were to take over development of the title.

                      Initially slated for release on 26 March 2024, the game was announced with a delay to 7 May 2024 in order to "fix more bugs and further optimize the game for all platforms". It was then further delayed to 3 September 2024 as the team encountered "new technical challenges". On 2 August 2024, the game was delayed indefinitely in the aim to improve the game's performance and quality.
                      But it's been odd that since last year, it hasn't exactly felt like there's a change of direction in what they publish. Rather, it seems they're just focusing on releasing what was already on the release-schedule anyway without putting anything new on it. Their release-schedule really is quite light compared to a few years ago. Then again, when you look at the whole set of releases of the last couple of years who knows how much money they've burned on all these excursions into other genres that didn't pan out as they hoped or turned out to be outright disasters. But you get this sense of unease in how the analysts talk about Paradox. As shareholders, the obvious demand is simply "Make line go up." but how is this supposed to happen when Paradox barely publishes any games in the upcoming years? At some point, Paradox really needs to deal with why they were unable to take the profits from their grand strategy games and expand like they planned. And the lack of success of the WOD-license is part of that.

                                      Originally posted by Crytash2 View Post
                                      True, but that is kind of the nature of the beast, isn't it? Investment is always risky.
                                      Sure, but this isn't a binary. Risk is something that gets evaluated in many ways and to many degrees.

                                      To go back to a tangent from one of the other threads about BL2: the idea of "AAA" "AA" and "A" video game studios? That's something that comes from investment lingo. Back in the brick & mortar days were retailers had to decide what games they were going to stock and in what quantities, an informal rating system the mirrored investment ratings came about. AAA studios were ones where you carried even their experimental games that weren't existing IP/etc. because the studios had proven track records. Customers that might be iffy on a title could see "Capcom" or "Rare" on the package and that could shift their hesitancy into a sale because that used to be trustworthy enough.

                                      Just like customers have every right to be suspicious of a Paradox game that's not a grand strategy game (given their past few years) and avoid/wait for it to be one sale/etc., investors are going to get skittish around the risks after Paradox has burnt this much money on an IP with potential. You can't just tell either group, "OK, I know we did bad the last few years, but trust us that we've completely learned the lesson, so give us more money and you'll totally get the games and profits everyone wants to see!" It's one thing to take a risk before you've seen what Paradox will do with a Bloodlines game, and another after.

                                      If Paradox decides to just sit on the IP and do nothing, there are short-term advantages: less financial risk, no more bad headlines, and shareholders stay calm because no new money is being burned. But long-term it is a slow bleed is it not? The brand loses relevance, the existing audience drifts away, and when they finally decide to do something again, they will be starting from zero instead of from a wounded but still-known name.
                                      Correct. That's why in these cases the shareholders generally are going to push for a sale of the IP. Let it become someone else's problems and minimize the loses rather than wait years and spend lots of money (not just bleed money, fixing things is going to cost too) for a potential payout in... 10, 15, 20 years? The buyer is in a better position because they'll have their own track record to sell people on why they're going to do the IP right, and the price is already going to be about as good as it's realistically going to get (the brand irrelevance hurts it's potential to sell to someone else too).

                                      Keep in mind that "buyer" here doesn't have to be some completely different organization. Onyx Path bought Trinity and Scion when CCP was selling off the WW IPs, and Onyx Path started as a group of CCP employees looking as a company about to eliminate their jobs (as had just happened to many of their friends) as CCP decided to get out of the physical games side of things. Why this worked though, is because Onyx Path was started by Rich and co. who had positive fan and industry reputations. Nobody thought CCP's decisions were because the people that CCP hired while taking over WW were messing things up, so those same people could have the trust to do right by the IPs after spinning off a new company.

                                      Paradox could back a spinoff like Onyx Path, but the would need to be really careful about who might go to it from Paradox so the broken trust people have in Paradox's handling of the IP doesn't get carried into the spinoff company.

                                              Originally posted by Crytash2 View Post
                                              True, but that is kind of the nature of the beast, isn't it?
                                              No, it isn't.

                                              Investment is about assessment of risks and likely returns. Going blind is gambling, and gamblers always lose in the end.

                                              Also, investment strategy is one thing, company strategy is another. They're tightly related, but not the same. The company has to have an actual strategy to get enough money in enough time as to pay its numerous bills, and then enough extra to appease stakeholders (in this case, mainly shareholders).

                                              Paradox just burned a shitton of money in Bloodlines 2 to release it more to mitigate losses than make an actual profit. Who they blame on their problems doesn't really change that they can't make proper money out of this type of game.

                                              So yes, Bloodlines 2 is definitely a dead-end. This has been the case with their every single foray into other genres and there's no reason to think otherwise, whatever the cause.

                                              Other common mistake is to think all those things have a lot to do with the TTRPG side of things, it doesn't. The RPG has meaningless costs and profit margins for them, by itself it's just to keep the IP relevant, and it does the job well enough as is, there's zero reason for them to invest more attention on that side of the business.

                                              They'll certainly close the Bloodlines avenue as something to put their money in, but they can still get something out of the IP depending on business strategy, but that's not about making a better game either TT or Video. They want to keep at the licensing strategy as long as they have the IP, so we'll depend exclusively from now on other people wanting to do something and pay Paradox a share of the profits (at margins they previously didn't knew how to negotiate, either).

                                              Will that pay enough to justify not selling the IP? Maybe. But shareholders will certainly want to know how much they can get selling it. If something may keep the IP in Paradox's hands it is a lack of buyers with deep enough pockets to pay the price they have to sell at. And I very much doubt Onyx Path will be that party, because Paradox spent quite some buck on it.

                                              For M5, the prospects aren't terrible. Unless Paradox is expecting selling the IP soon, there is no reason to not keep investing the small money a TTRPG needs and get some profit out of it instead of none. Any profit, whatsoever, is better than none (although marketing costs may be prohibitive at this point). It all depends, but they have a few avenues for changing gears that still have TTRPG books getting out. And yes, others that has it slow or stop altogether.

                                              My opinion is that they should make a Grand Strategy game with the IP. It is their strong suit, after all. And about not being able to do anything else, while it lessens their value a little it isn't a big problem. Doing what you know how to reliably make money with is far more important than being willing to do anything with random results. There's a problem with market risk, but they have to do with the tools they have, and investors can deal with this kind of risk in other ways.
