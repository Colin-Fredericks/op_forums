                                          I mean, most humans everywhere in history have had a lower age limit for what counted as an adult fit for marriage or other responsibilities. It is historically sensible that many ancient vampires would be very young by our standards.

                                          Also, one of the key lures of vampirism is the ability to preserve the appearance of youth indefinitely. Wisdom, knowledge, skill, etc. can be learned afterwards. A youthful appearance, the best one will enjoy in life (not counting that sweet spot when one still has some youth left but has lived long enough to know how to dress, wash, wear make up, etc. to also present oneself better), cannot be recovered later except by trading dangerous favours with the Tzimisce. It's perfectly sensible for me that most vampires for most of history would embrace or ghoulify humans in their teens whenever possible, and I'd have vampires in US domains have a recurrent problem and discussions over contemporary American sensibilities towards age, at least when it comes to teenagers.

                                          As for pre-teens and younger children, their embrace would have been an oddity in most times and places. There are multiple obvious drawbacks and not many advantages.

                                              Originally posted by Shadeprowler View Post
                                              Petaniqua is... a good question; by her own account she was born as Polyxena, the princess of Epirus ; Polyxena was described by the chronicler Malalas in his account of the Chronography as "tall, pure, very white, large-eyed, black-haired, with her hair worn long behind, a good nose and cheeks, blooming-lipped, small-footed, virgin, charming, very beautiful, 18 years old when they killed her" so... maybe 18?
                                              Unless the character was retconned after her profile in Kindred's Most Wanted (I know she was updated in Dread Names, Red List, but I haven't read that), Petaniqua can't be that Polyxena. The Polyxena from Malalas's Chronography was the legendary daughter of the King of Troy (not a princess of Epirus) during the Trojan War. Petaniqua is supposedly Olympias, the mother of Alexander the Great, who was a princess of Epirus and named Polyxena at birth (Olympias can be confusing because she had four different names at different parts of her life: Polyxena, Myrtale, Olympias, and Stratonike.) In KMW her apparent age was described as "30".

                                              Since the historical Olympias lived until she was 59, there's some discrepancy then with the character as described and the historical figure. However, Petaniqua's biography in KMW takes that into account. First, it says Olympias became a ghoul and second, it says Olympias was embraced after the assassination of her husband Philip II. While Olympias would have been closer to 40 at that time, not 30, her being made a full ghoul around 30 would have preserved her appearance to appear much younger.

                                              Sometimes the World of Darkness sourcebooks include various discrepancies like this to inform the well informed reader that the vampire was lying, and to take everything with a grain of salt. Petaniqua doesn't need to actually be Olympias, though she could believe that she is. I personally think it'd be hard for vampire Olympias to hide her true nature for twenty years, but at the same time the Masquerade wasn't back in the fourth century BC what it is now. It's your personal choice/taste here. Regardless, her appearance was clearly not of an eighteen year old in KMW.

                                                  Age of Embrace is a rather complicated matter.

                                                  On one hand, the apex of the human body is around 30 years, and that has always been well known, at least since Classical Antiquity. If a vampire wants the Childe to be in its prime, both physical and mental, they would look for someone at that age.

                                                  But then we have what Hermeticus said, preserving youth is a strong theme, both psychologically and as a genre thing. And for most of history, the apex of youthful appearance was around 15 years. Good appearance isn't necessarily youthful, but that's both common and in with the genre.

                                                  In the end, yes, VtM usually follows the Anne Rice stylistic choice of young vampires, and that does mean a lot of children and teens, specially among Elders and Methuselah. Even most of those that are in their 20s arguably shouldn't, as they're more based on modern people in their 20s than people of their times.

                                                  Either way, making a game with more vampires in their mid 20s to mid 30s is an option. It would mean a more mature vampire society, though, which may or may not affect some aspects of the setting.
