    [V20 and V5] Throwing around ideas with Disciplines and Discipline Powers

    08-15-2021, 12:22 PM
    I’ve been playing around with V20 and V5, deciding what things I prefer best out of both systems. I enjoy how V5’s disciplines let me customise them to my characters, but in general I prefer the tidiness V20's disciplines and the existence of elder powers, and while I was poking at vicissitude and oblivion a vague idea came to me on how to, maybe, incorporate both systems.

    All vampires have access to the standard three disciplines to 5+ discipline dots as written in whichever previous edition is being used. But a vampire as an individual also influences their disciplines. They may have an affinity for a specific use of their discipline (making it easier to succeed in using it in a certain way, or removing/lowering the blood/willpower cost), or they may also develop additional effects for that level of the discipline. Kind of like getting a relevant merit or combination discipline every time you gain a dot in a discipline. For example;

    Vicissitude typically requires time, concentration and anatomical knowledge. Any Tzimisce with ** of vicissitude/protean can mould soft tissue and cosmetically alter appearance by spending 1BP and rolling, but they also get two changes that they can perform on themselves automatically and without a roll (for example, changing their pigmentation. lengthening their neck/limbs (like making your arms into tentacles or simply extending your reach))

    A vampire with Feral Whispers has an affinity for, idk, pigeons and can understand them easier and/or have the difficulty halved for commanding the birds. Another character seems to have a knack for vampires themselves, they might be able to use Feral Whispers to tap into a vampire’s subconscious and listen to/influence their beast, be able to rate how hungry/unstable their fellow kindred are at the moment and may excel in using animalism for starting and stopping frenzies and other vampiric complications.

    A vampire with Earth Meld may have the ability to meld with additional materials (metal, water, etc), Mist Form may be invisible or take on a corrosive quality. Add extra forms for Shape of the Beast, or be able to alter the size of the transformation, (your friend can turn into a regular sized lizard and you can turn into a regular sized lizard or Godzilla). Maybe somebody who was a virologist in life can shape-shift into a pathogen.

    You can also assign amalgams/combination disciplines to a dot, if you meet the requirements.
    Fortitude, Celerity and Potence remain pretty much the same as in V20 unless you assign a combination discipline/amalgam to them, in which case you give up the bonus physical dot in exchange.
    Actually, I had another thought typing that. Rather than discipline powers, gaining a dot in a discipline could instead adds a dot to a relevant attribute or skill (Animalism = charisma/animal ken, Vicissitude = medicine, Auspex = perception/awareness/alertness. etc.)


    I get a bit fussier with blood sorcery. Thaumaturgy uses paths. There is no question in my mind that Tremere using anything other than the hierarchal path system is No.
    On the other hand, while I prefer the occult vibe of the path structure and don’t feel that Oblivion fully conveys the first principle of the necromantic paradigm drawing on spiritual pollution... I like the ritual focus of V5's blood sorcery and the fact that with V5’s necromancy I no longer have to either spend a shitton of xp or make the difficult decision between the family picnics in the Labyrinth offered by the Ash Path or using the Bone Path to raise my parents’ corpses and force them to tidy my room for eternity in fulfilment of a petty grudge nursed since childhood.
    Still thinking on that one.

      I like V20's very simple, refined disciplines. Ordinary Vampires shouldn't be thinking about their "build" like an optimizer, and the simplicity of disciplines makes them feel more like a natural attachment to the vampire state rather than something a game designer thought up for an arcade game. The only one that should really mutate is Protean, because that's all about changing (Protean literally means fast and frequently changing) and also quietus because... well... read my book (you don't have to pay for it!)

      Thaumaturgy (and Necromancy) are special because... they're not actually vampire disciplines. These powers are a product of the soul, something everyone with a soul can learn (except Ghosts, I think). Ordinary mortals can learn thaumaturgy (called hedge magic) and so too can the changing breeds (Who almost entirely don't with a few exceptions; Gifts and spirit rites are far, far more convenient for them.) The V5 term "blood magic" is really misleading, and brought about by a misunderstanding of the fundamentals.
      The WoD is largely build on a Hermetic/Gnostic paradigm where all souls are capable of great things if they'd just gain the knowledge and unlock their potential. I feel it's quite profound, and really don't like the way V5 has thrown away philosophy's influence in designing it's systems.

              I think an easy way to customise Disciplines is to add Modes to them, like with Exalted Essence's Charms. This is also a really good way to rebalance the core Disciplines that get a bit eclipsed by the rarer ones.

              So, while Obtenebration can sometimes be Protean+ currently, you can fix that by giving a boost to Protean through unique Modes. E.g., Gangrel who learn Protean might get three unique features at Protean 2, instead of just claws, and/or can make any of their features permanent (with the breach of Masquerade that may cost).

              Celerity, Fortitude and Potence could also be made more interesting this way, and you could give more flavour to clans that get these Disciplines.

                              Originally posted by Rhywbeth View Post

                              II've never played Exalted, what are the charms and how would this work?
                              So Exalted essentially has Charms that give you specific powers and impressive feats. They have daft, pseudo-East Asian names, but can boil down to things like 'leaping like a monkey' or 'make puny mortals cower in your presence'. Charms are grouped by the Ability (or Attribute, in some cases) they come from--i.e., they're extensions of Abilities for godlike beings. But for simplicity's sake, we'll just say they're Exalted's version of Disciplines.

                              In traditional Exalted, every splat would have its own version of each of the 'core' Charms. This means there are hundreds of Charms for each splat, which may have a lot of overlap. That made decision-making at chargen really difficult (especially for newbies) and meant you need reams of notes for your Charms.

                              Then, along came Exalted Essence (ExEss), billed as the lighter version of Exalted. Instead of hundreds, if not thousands, of Charms, many of them doing similar things, ExEss introduces generic Charms with Modes.

                              So now, the 'leaping like a monkey' Charm is one core Charm, but it has different Modes specific to different splats. Modes never make Charms weaker, they just add an extra bonus (or rarely, change a facet of the Charm) to make it a better fit for the splat.

                              So the Fire Terrestrials (each Terrestrial splat has a theme tied to one of the elements) get a special Mode for that 'leaping like a monkey' Charm that means they can vault into the sky on a plume of flame (mostly a fluff thing) and can reflexively rise from prone when they activate it (a mechanical bonus, since this saves them an action).

                              Meanwhile, Alchemicals, who are sort of transhumanist/cyborg Exalted, can change direction while they leap.

                              For that 'make puny mortals cower in your presence', Abyssals (goth vampires in servitude to the Neverborn) who succeed on the Charm may also inflict damage--literally scaring targets to death.

                              Lunars, meanwhile, gain a significant bonus to their intimidation actions when in animal form, and can add any fear bonuses from that animal form on top of that.

                              Applying this to VTM, you could therefore give vanilla Disciplines some cool bonuses for specific clans to bring them up to the level of the rarer Disciplines.

                              So Ventrue might get some exclusive bonuses to Dominate and Presence that reinforce their role as kings and leaders.

                              Toreador might be able to use their Presence especially well when gossiping, or standing beside a work of their own art, or when hosting parties.

                              The Brujah, meanwhile, might get Presence Modes that relate to rabble-rousing or making seductive/contrarian arguments that can't be countered, while their version of Potence might add some extra bonus that kicks in when fighting for a cause.

                              It's a way of individuating the Disciplines, adding extra oomph to the weaker Disciplines (especially those that are Clan Disciplines) and reinforcing the thematic niche of each clan.

                                  Changeling the Lost 2e also uses a similar idea to Exalted Essence that might be a bit easier to translate to VtM (since its example effects are more in-line with what you'd be aiming for power-level-wise).

                                  In CtL 2e, Arcadian Contracts (the common form of their powers, though there are others) all have Seeming bonuses for at least two of the six Seemings. When a changeling of that Seeming learns the Contract, they automatically get the bonus for free. Other changelings can learn it as well, but they have to convince a member of that Seeming that knows the Contract to teach it to them after the student swears an oath to the teacher. Characters can always opt to not use the bonus, and if they learn multiple for the same Contract they can use multiples at the same time.

                                  The Light-Shy Contract works a lot like Obfuscate's stealth powers: it's a mind-trick that doesn't work on cameras and the like by default, and stops working if you do something too obvious/aggressive. Darklings get a bonus that lets extends the power to avoid being seen by technology, while the Wizened gain the ability to extend the power to objects; making them both disappear from perceptions and people's minds.

                                  The Elemental Weapon Contract lets you create an archaic weapon out of any nearby element (not restricted to classic elements), with bonuses to the base weapon stats based on your roll. So you can reach into a bonfire and pull out a sword made of fire, grab some ice and turn it into a throwing spear-icicle, or turn some vines into a whip. Darklings get the bonus to use shadows as elements, and shadow-weapons can completely blind a target instead of doing damage on a hit. Elementals get a bonus that creates a terrifying display as the element permeates their visage, letting them make a special intimidation roll that stuns the target.

                                  However, since favoring a given Regalia (the larger sub-groupings within the Arcadian Contracts) doesn't inherently mean you also get a Seeming bonus, you don't have to worry about coming up with lots of extra powers (ExEss also doesn't have Modes for each type of Exalted for every Charm, sometimes the base Charm is all that given Exalted type gets)

                                  There's actually a few ways this is better with V5 than V20 (esp. since V20's larger Discipline list and ability to learn unlimited Combo Disciplines alleviates some of the need for these things):

                                  1) The breaking up of the physical Disciplines into multiple distinct powers gives you more places to add bonuses for Clans.

                                  2) You can take another page from CtL 2e. Characters only favor one Regalia from their Seeming, and get to freely pick a second Regalia to favor. Since any Seeming can favor any Regalia, the Seeming bonuses are fairly evenly divided between the Seeming that inherently favors that Regalia and ones that don't. If you're also thinking of ways to adjust Predator Types in V5, instead of the difficult to balance additional Discipline dot, you could increase access to these sorts of bonuses based on your Predator Type. So an Alley Cat might be able to pick up the Brujah Bonuses from Celerity (City Gangrel achieved!) or the Noferatu bonuses to Potence.
