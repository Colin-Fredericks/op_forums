    Originally posted by gamiem View Post
    V:tM has never quite conjured associations of approachable mechanics, so I'm simultaneously sympathetic to any calls for simplification (something V5 does not even aim to achieve by importing a totally unrelated system on top of V:tM's own system) and forgiving of failures to untagle the mess that is Masquerade's game design; what I find significantly less forgivable is the fact V5 didn't even have the decency to compensate for failures in game design by making itself a work of art, which has been the line's modus operandi since day one. The evocativeness and sincerity of classic V:tM justify investing in it even if one doesn't intend to make use of it as a TTRPG system; it is very hard for me to believe this level of artistic merit will be recognised in V5 three decades from now. Do people truly find more offence in misguided candor and unrepentant adolescent sincerity than in vapid self-referentiality and a pathological fear of vulnerability? Are katana-wielding vampires dressed in leather trench coats truly more distasteful than having a TTRPG in which you roleplay as a vampire in modern Milwaukee school you on the latest Drumpf tweet fail?

    I'm not trying to "defend" the worst excesses of V:tM/cWoD (though I maintain tone-deafness is in basically all cases preferable to insincerity). I'm saying there's still a stable consumer base of overly sincere social rejects to whom an updated, streamlined version of classic Masquerade (that maintains the artistic trappings of the original) would appeal to, and who maintain a right to creative expression without having to justify their weirdness in reference to the latest asinine concern of mainstream culture. V5 succeeded to the extent it managed to engage those to whom the conceits of Masquerade appeal to; I would quite like for any future edition of Masquerade to abstain from appeals to mainstream sensibilities in the pursuit of a fresh coat of paint.
    This. I've been buying vtm since 1992 but haven't gone near v5 for various reasons, including ludicrous modern day pandering. This is a game where players pretend to be undead blood sucking corpses FFS . Also, art work in earlier editions was awesome, photos of actors dressed up doesn't compare.

            Originally posted by literatzi View Post

            I haven't followed v5 but I've seen this sentiment a lot. What 'modern day pandering' is in the edition?
            I think the sort of pandering V5 likes to indulge in is only really an example of 'modern pandering' to the extent it is precisely the kind of philistine banality that allows for sentences like '[the vampire Prince of Mexico City] is an ascending kingpin [...] moving up societal ranks through NGOs and the UN(!), [who] knows everyone worth controlling in the Davos elite(!!!)' to be published in print that appeals to modern sensibilities.

            My desire is not for the game to be 'apolitical' - it's for the politics (and the psychoanalysis, and the poetry) to naturally flow out of the internal logic of the game. It is very, very obvious what the individual components of the game's setting stand for, if interpreted as clear-cut analogies for real-world events and institutions, to the extent it'd be gauche to even ask what do the Camarilla and its Inner Circle, the Sabbat and the Anarch Revolts, the Jyhad and the impotent struggle of young vampires against their elders 'mean' - the metaphors involved are simply too obvious. V5 commits the crime of killing the fantasy (inexcusable for a TTRPG) by saying what it all means: the Sabbat is no longer about the tragedy of angry young men tearing a world too small for them down only to find themselves trapped within the exact same cycle of misdirected violence and paranoia - it's Literally About ISIS and Atomwaffen and incels (and why would you want to play a terrorist Nazi incel?). The Camarilla is no longer about the mediaeval courtly intrigue reflected in an average office meeting, or the youngest member of an international crime syndicate seeking to make a change within a system he was never even asked to join - it is literally, actually about the current composition of the US Senate (and why would you want to play fucking vampire Mitch McConnell?). And here I was thinking Stoker made Dracula a 'count' for the Dark Academia vibes; what's next, is vampiric bloodthirst going to be a metaphor for drug addiction?

            There's a sense in which this is not solely the fault of us currently living through a cultural dark age. V5 chose to import the fundamental conceit of Requiem, that you're playing a vampire in what is literally our world with vampires and werewolves and witches added - Requiem is, to an extent, best played if you're playing as if it was you that was Embraced. This is not how Masquerade works, nor is it how the World of Darkness itself works - the whole idea is that you're not playing in the real world, that the events depicted during a chronicle did not happen and could never happen; the setting is exaggerated and distorted in a way that makes its fictional nature obvious. Blurring the line between this 'world of impressions' and the real world is artistically tasteless at best, and dangerous at worst: there is no pack of Sabbat vampires behind the latest cartel murder, and your CEO is not the ghoul of the Ventrue Prince; V5 openly claims otherwise, and is all the worse for it.

                                    Originally posted by literatzi View Post

                                    [This line struck me because I personally felt like this was a big issue with classic VtM. Is the issue you're describing that the kind of inferred massive control over world events from prior editions is replaced with explicit ties to present day events/organizations? I remember some sort of hubub over a V5 book a few years ago explicitly discussing vampires taking advantage of the anti-gay purges in Chechnya, which seems like an example of what I think you're talking about.]
                                    That's part of it, yeah. YMMV, but I think cVtM/cWoD suffers from a different if related flaw: beyond the fact any depiction of a subject based entirely on impressions will necessarily other the subject being depicted (this is, in essence, the critique levelled at Kindred of the East), the impressions WoD chooses to construct its setting out of are specifically the worst impressions of the real world. Someone, I think on this very forum, once said WoD Chicago is best imagined as being exactly like the depiction of the real city on Fox News - the setting takes the worst, most othering, most degrading fantasies and turns them into fact, to the point of sometimes venturing into outright alternate history (I think it was Rage Across New York that stated more than one of the state's IRL green zone laws never got approved in WoD?). Werewolf, being perhaps the line to take the idea of a 'World of Darkness' most seriously, contains some of the most flagrant examples of paranoid fiction being treated as fact - everything to do with Pentex, for one. This is a world whose building blocks are the feverish delusions of Lawrence Pazder and Jack Chick.

                                    What salvaged what could've easily been an exercise in right-wing hysteria was the fact cWoD never presented itself as an honest depiction of reality. I've memory-holed the entire Chechnya incident, but yes, it's an example of a general tendecy that pervades the entire product. Classic Masquerade, as I understand it, preferred to think of vampires as part of the ambience rather than a direct cause of the state of the world (an idea outright denied in Mexico City by Night, in fact); a world so awful must have place for literal bloodsuckers, I suppose. It's in V5 that this boundary crashes: beyond the obvious dangers, I think the conflation of impression with fact is just extremely tasteless in a very banal, trite sense. If I wanted to read about 'Davos elites' literally sucking the blood of unsuspecting commoners in order to stave off mortality, I'd go on Twitter.

                                                    The thing is that the Masquerade was kitsch. For the punk gothic scenario it was over the top, so the satyre could be delivered blow by blow.
