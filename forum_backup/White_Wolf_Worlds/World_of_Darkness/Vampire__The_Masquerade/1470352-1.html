                        I'll answer the questions in an objective and simple fashion. I'll also focus on how the two are presented in V5, only pulling from earlier editions to show inspiration for how they are in V5.

                        The Hecata are actual my favorite clan. Objectively they are Cappadocians reborn, mixed with the culture and practices of the other necromantic bloodlines. I like how Oblivion is, but hate that Obtenebration was lumped in. Regardless, fun clan/sect/cult.

                        The Path of Death and The Soul is interesting to put it simply. In V5 terms, a Path is an alternative form of morality which allows a vampire to exist without the burden of human ideas. V5 presents them in a similar format as clans to make it easier for Storytellers to create terrifying NPCs. Whereas the Hecata study death in general, The Path of Death studies violent death. Overall, they're bland murder-hobo NPCs with very little RAW nuance.

                        If you're noticing a similarity, that's actually for good reason. In earlier editions, the Path of Death and The Soul was originally developed from the Cappadocian morality system known as the Road of Bones. Those who followed the Road were often Cappadocian scholars of death, who studied the very concept of death and what happened after. As time moved on Roads were slowly phased out as the Camarilla encouraged the practice of Humanity alone. Oddly enough, V5 Humanity is very similar to how Roads use to be practiced and developed.

                        The Sabbat, who formed around the same time as the Camarilla, continued to practice the Roads, but also hypocritically altered them to be even more inhuman as to further separate vampires from humanity. From a meta perspective, we can see Paths become progressively more and more violent as the Editions went on after 2E.

                        So to put it simply, they're similar because the Hecata's predecessors were the original inventors of the Path before the Sabbat twisted it to fit their own agenda.
