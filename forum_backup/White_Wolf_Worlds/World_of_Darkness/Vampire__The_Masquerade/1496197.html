    Updating Transylvania Chronicles to later edition

    11-04-2022, 11:59 AM
    So, in the latest episode of my podcast (cheeky little plug https://wodarkages.squarespace.com/p...re-storyteller ), I got to talking about the idea of updating Transylvania Chronicles to a later edition of the game. The inspiration is the update of Warhammer's famous Enewmy Within campaign to the 4th edition of WFRP.

    So, it'd have to be 20th Anniversary Edition, since 5e doesn't have a Dark Ages version.

    Do you think it could be done? Do you think people would be interested? Can it be updated without it being changing so much it becomes way too different?
    What do you think?

                      One way to connect disparate events that occur throughout time is to have a campaign set in the Modern Nights with flashbacks that allow PCs to delve into the backstory of themselves and their sires.

                      E.g., maybe the game starts as a bunch of neonates in modern NYC are goaded into a mass assault upon their elders, diablerising them and thus gaining fragments of their memories. Then, as things kick off in NYC between [Tzimisce] and [Nosferatu] (with a secondary war between Tremere and Saulot also taking place later in Vienna and Mexico City), the PCs start getting flashbacks of their sires' unlives and come to realise why they were manipulated (their sires were supposed to stop this war between the two Ancients, but now they have to step in to stop it instead).

                      It doesn't even need to be diablerie -- you just need a way to share these flashbacks. So it could be messages from sleeping elders, memoriam (in the style of V5), Auspex/Dementation visions, or the equivalent of finding Claudia's diaries in the Vampire Chronicles.

                      The key would be tying all the bits together. So whatever you flash back to in the game should also have relevance to what's happening in the Modern Nights (e.g., finding Zelios' labyrinth, discovering Kupala, learning about the Wan Kuei, etc). The cycle of "Ancient versus Ancient" (Tzimisce/Nosferatu, Saulot/Tremere) would serve well to underscore the futility and interminability of the Jyhad.
