    Ways to make a Wraith Threatening

    12-17-2022, 04:26 PM
    Well, I decided to create this topic here instead of the Wraith's subforum because the Wraith's subforum is a bit dead (no pun intended lol).

    So, one thing that has always bothered me is that Wraith's were never taken seriously as a threat by players in general in all my years of experience with the setting, unlike Mages and Werewolves (understandably because of the powercreep often present in those splats). While I have a great deal of knowledge about Mages, Vampires and Werewolves, I admittedly don't have much knowledge about Wraiths. So, besides the fact that Wraiths are not well known for their powers, in this I mean they're not known as potentially broken as Mages, Vampires and Werewolves can be, I know next to nothing about their powers. Then, my question is: is it possible to make Wraiths really dangerous to the other splats in terms of powers? Do they have powers that could really threaten the other splats besides scaring them? I mean that not only in a narrative sense, because that's another thing, I mean really in their arsenal of powers etc. How can I make a Wraith threatening to the players so that they learn to stop thinking they can just "summon and compel any Wraith to do as they please at any time" (in case they're Giovanni, Cappadocian, etc.) or just outright ignore them? Btw, I have W20, so that if you have any suggestion of where to find information regarding their powers in the book, that would be also very helpful.

      Well first, if it's Vampire PCs, they can only see a Wraith if they have Auspex 2 (Aura Reading) or a specific Necromancy power. So, by default, Wraiths are invisible to vampire characters. Same with Mages who don't have Spirit 1. And if the character has no way to see the Wraith, they probably also lack the ability to directly hurt them, too. Any discussion of how threatening Wraiths are must begin with the assumption that they cannot be seen or touched by most characters.

      Now, Wraiths generally lack the ability to touch mortals in kind, but they're more likely to have powers that can circumvent the barrier and affect the material world. After all, if they had no such powers, they probably wouldn't be relevant to proceedings this side of the Shroud.

      Werewolves are a different matter, obviously, as Wraiths are kind of spirits in the Garou sense. Those more versed in Werewolf know better than I do. Just know that the ability to translate into the Middle Umbra (aka "Spirit Wilds") is not the same as the power to translate into the Low (Dark) Umbra. Garou characters can't necessarily follow the ghosts to where they "live". I assume.

              With regards to the Arcanoi, a number of them are usable in the Skinlands. For example the primary nature of Inhabit is to possess objects and machines. In Wr20 Inhabit 1 grains a wraith the ability to possess an object, Inhabit 2 allows them to cause that device to temporarily malfunction and Inhabit 3 allows them to control it. With could be a serious problem for a group of vampires at the wrong moment. Oh you're trying to drive somewhere? Suddenly you car fails. Or you lose control of it and it races into an intersection during a red light.

              There's also the possibility that a wraith might have a Passion somehow connected one of the vampires which could result in them being a reoccurring headache for them. After all, if you got killed by a vampire and ended up as a wraith, there would be a good chance that you'd want to make that vampire's life miserable.

              Then there's the ability of a wraith to become risen which can be much more directly dangerous, especially if again that wraith is out for blood against a member of the coterie as becoming a risen is difficult and many of those that do become risen are really, really motivated.

              Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. They're indomitable. Indomitable.

                              I have had the best luck when using Wraiths as indirect threats rather than a standup fight sort of threat.

                              Sticking with vampire & ghost interactions for simplicity, most vampires can't perceive the ghosts and the few disciplines that allow it generally aren't left constantly running with the characters constantly scanning their surroundings. Thus the ghost is the ears and eyes in every room, did the PCs just make a cool plan to defeat the necromancer, well too bad the ghosts just revealed it all to its master.

                              More importantly you have to remember that ghosts don't have the same daylight restriction that vampires do. Thus a ghost can wander into any haven at mid-day and look around. Now that might not sound like much of a threat, but consider how many things laying around your house that can be dangerous with only tiny amounts of manipulation. Starting fires, opening water taps, pulling back a window blind, etc can all be done, depending on the ghosts given load out. None of these things will directly harm a vampire, but it can create situations where the vampire is put in significantly greater danger.

                              In simpler terms, ghosts are kind of like fighting a streak of super bad luck. Trying pull off a heist in a getaway car, well it seems a couple of spark plug wires disconnected and stopped the car from running. Are you trying to sell a load of weapons to a gang, how did a hand grenade pin pop out on its own? Trying to hack the company files of a rival kindred, well your breakers keep tripping or there is a power surge that kills the computer.

                              Using ghosts to terrify the Players is more about understanding what tiny things in the mundane world can go wrong than knowing the ins and outs of Wraith rules. A new ghost probably can't telekinetically punch a vampire or throw them across the room, but they can tip a cup of coffee over to short out the laptop they are working with.

                              The best part is that unless the ghost or the necromancer makes a big display of their power, no one will have any idea what is going on or who is behind it. To use a Warhammer 40k reference, having a ghost as an enemy turns the PCs into Lamenter space marines crossed with the Afriel strain guardsmen clones. If it can go wrong, it will and in spades.
