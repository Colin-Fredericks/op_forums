        As hackneyed as it may seem, it really does depend on the story, but in general here's what I thought about the Antediluvians in my games.

        Antediluvian can mean a few different things in my games based on the character discussing it
        • A Third Generation Cainite
        • A Cainite who survived the flood that destroyed the First City
        • A Third Generation Cainite who survived the flood that destroyed the First City

        [A note on numbers, all of these assume a game set in modern (2016) times]

        Abilities: Assume they have high to max ranks in Abilities that make sense in association with their mindset and powers; other Abilities can vary wildly (when in doubt I err on the side of slightly higher than lower as most of these Cainites are immensely old and powerful). The exception are modern Abilities such as Firearms and Technology; access to these is dependent on the Cainite in question being active in the modern era for a reasonable amount of time to have learned such new Abilities.

        True Antediluvian: A Third Generation Cainite who survived the flood that destroyed the First City (the date of the flood varies, but I generally place it around 10k years ago). The number in my games is not always 13, I've had some games where others have survived but lay in torpor, or listened to Caine's rules and did not embrace.
        • They have reached level 10 in their clan disciplines and possibly one or two others. The power of a level 10 discipline depends on the needs of the game, anywhere from somewhat more powerful than level 9 to rivaling the archspheres of awakened magick, regardless a level 10 is a way to counter another level 10 (forcing an opposed roll).
        • They have level 5+ in all the other common disciplines (Animalism, Auspex, Celerity, Dominate, Fortitude, Obfuscate, Potence, Presence)
        • They know at least one (likely 2 or 3) 'obscure' disciplines (between level 1-7)
        • At least one Attribute at 10, other primary Attributes at 8 to 10, others at 5+ (barring a 0 from a clan weakness)
        • Often have Methuselah's Thirst or some other restrictions on what they can derive sustenance from
        • Some may have transcended Cainite flesh and become something else, some possible examples
          • [Lasombra] becoming a shadow being of the Abyss
          • [Tzimisce] suffusing Vicissitude and able to reform/control (imagine the Agents taking over someone in the Matrix) anyone with the discipline
          • [Malkav] become a psychic entity within or the Malkavian Madness Network itself
          • [Gangrel] becoming one with the Earth through Protean
        • Destroying such creatures is possible but supremely difficult, some examples of what one might need to do so
          • Caine
          • A Second Generation Cainite of appropriate age
          • The assistance of the True Antediluvian itself (example Cappadocius and Augustus Giovanni)
          • Another True Antediluvian
          • An Archmage with Archspheres
          • A Kuei-jin Arhat (Dharma level 10, assuming such entities can interact with our world)
          • Multiple (at least 5) Methuselahs (Fourth Generation) of great age (over a thousand years) or another supernatural with equivalent power and age, some examples Archmages (Arete 6+ without Archspheres), Rank 6 Garou/Fera, Bodhisattvas (Dharma 9). Some will likely die in the attempt.
          • Many (10+) Methuselahs (Fifth and Sixth Generation) of great age or another supernatural with equivalent power and age, some examples Master Mages (Arete 5, 3+ Spheres at 5), Rank 5 Garou/Fera, Ancestors (Dharma 7 and 8). Most will likely die in the attempt.
          • Lesser being might be able to destroy such a creature, but it would require catastrophic casualties (thousands or millions in the case of normal humans) and a great deal of luck or massive destuction (for example multiple successful nuclear strikes might kill such a creature).
          • Even if 'successful' the True Antediluvian might survive in some way, such as Saulot within Tremere's body or [Lasombra] if he entered the Abyss

        False Antediluvian: A Third Generation Cainite who is younger than the flood that destroyed the First City often attaining that power from diablerie. Canonically this could be Tremere, Augustus Giovanni, Gratiano de Veronese, Lugoj Blood-Breaker, and possibly Troile depending when you place the diablerie of [Brujah]/Ilyes). Numbers can vary wildly based on how long ago they were embraced and how active they were after they achieved Third Generation, the numbers below reflect the rough power level I place the canonical False Antediluvians.
        • They have reached level 10 in one of their clan disciplines (if possible depending on plot, a False Antediluvian may have believed they consumed a Third Generation, but may not actually succeeded or have been partially stopped by the machinations of their meal).
        • They have reached level 6+ in their other clan disciplines
        • Generally have some knowledge of 3+ common disciplines (between level 1-5) and one at level 6+
        • Generally have one Attribute at 10, other primary Attributes at 6+, others vary along 'normal' lines
        • Likely can no longer feed on animals, Methuselah's Thirst is also possible
        • Destroying such a creature is easier than a True Antediluvian as they are much younger, so assume everything as per True Antediluvians will work (and more easily) and lesser beings stand a much greater chance of success with lower (but still high) casualties. Indeed depending on their age a single ancient Methuselah could be a threat to a False Antediluvian.

        Flood Methuselahs: In some games I've had Cainites of Fourth Generation also survive the flood that destroyed the First City, and could technically be called Antediluvians because of that.
        • They have reached level 9 in their clan disciplines
        • They all have level 6+ in Fortitude or some other way to explain how they survived the flood (examples A high level Blood Magic ritual, or a high level Protean power)
        • They have level 5+ in all the common disciplines
        • They likely know at least one 'obscure' discipline (between level 1-5)
        • One Attribute at 9, other primary Attributes at 7 or 8, others at 5+ (again barring clan weaknesses)
        • Methuselah's Thirst is common, and inability to feed on animals is the norm
        • Three such creatures could be a threat to a True Antediluvian (although at least one will die in the attempt) and a single Flood Methuselah is a threat to a False Antediluvian
        • A Flood Methuselah is likely to be as hard to destroy as a False Antediluvian if not moreso considering they had the resilience to survive the flood

            As a person who primarily does Technocracy games:

            Antediluvians are Class GODLIKEs, they're stupidly powerful, ridiculously versatile and nigh-invulnerable to even Technocratic light and medium weapons. This means that they casually laugh at having pistols that shoot with the force of an RPG explode in their face, and respond to a strike team of HITMarks by ripping them apart and using their arms as slashing weapons. Generally on the side of powers, their abilities are huge and destructive, impacting the scene when they are invoked and honestly resemble Utterances from Mummy or the more powerful Charms from Exalted more than vampiric powers.

            There has been two scenes in my games where Antediluvians have been relevant, and both have made my players very afraid.

            The first was of course the Week of Nightmares where they were "lucky" enough to play characters at ground zero while Ravana was doing stuff like recreate biblical desires with illusions and using perfect defenses such as "nuh uh i was never there at all" or "no ur the one hit" or "faq u targeting systems are a lie". He also did stuff like spontaneously make new animal races whenever he felt like it to bolster his Animalism, and a lot of the fallout from that game has been containment teams trying to figure out what to do with all the new deadly wildlife made of solid murder that an ancient dead monster felt he needed for his defense.

            (This one was actually an interesting case since neither me, nor my players expected them to survive and indeed had the expectation of dying heroically to halt the ancient monster, but what did happen was that due to clever tactics and thinking on their part, they managed to harry it like wolves, keeping up the pressure and in some way still succeed their rolls all the time until the heavy ordnance arrived to cover them and an Aurora could extract them. They were named Heroes of The Technocracy for the vital information on it's defenses and were promoted to higher ranks each, only two of those characters are still alive, one having a place on the Void Engineer Admiralty while the other is an NWO Director. They both miss their Amalgam very much and the Director is a broken woman who barely pulls herself through every day, and keeps a picture of her love that died a few weeks later from having his mind broken by the many hazards from the Antediluvian's mental attacks.)

            The second one was in 2012, being a battle against Toreador. Toreador took the form of an androgynous figure of absolute beauty and was classified as a high-class memetic hazard, requiring that all agents who volunteered for the mission undergo invasive surgery to protect them from looking at it and getting caught worshipping it. Toreador moved with such speed that it simply walked through the battlefield, and when it actually ran, the shockwave knocked helicopters out of the air and liquidized any nearby agents. It possessed a radiant aura that made it reminiscent of a god incarnate and it simply saw through any plans and disguises. It was also fully awake and managed to wreak havoc on five cities simply by existing nearby before it was stopped with a casualty rate 87.6% among the Technocratic agents sent against it.

            I generally establish a dichotomy between the young/high-Generation vampires that my players can scythe through effortlessly with their Technocratic hardware, and the old/low-Generation vampires that are extreme threats and tend to pull some bullshit out of their ass whenever they are actually threatened.

            "There is a remedy for everything but death, a hope for everything but wickedness, and everything will lapse except righteousness."

Antediluvian's power levels, outside of being plot points, are very nearly god-like. Beyond the scope of even Mages in many ways, (an Antediluvian will overpower any mastery over Spheres in most cases on a one for one basis), except that Disciplines, Generation, and the like DO all have specific parameters.                                 They still need/use/are restricted to all the rules involving Path Ratings, Virtues, Frenzy, etc. . ., very, very likely have multiple Traits at 10 or close, but not all, or even most. While they probably haven't mastered it, I would think that at least a cursory understanding of things that they might find marvelous, if not magical would be exactly the sort of thing that they would seek out to fight off the never ending boredom that is their only real foe, and "micromanagement" (even of States/countries/continents) has grown pointless to them.
