              I've never used them and likely never will. My opinion on any bloodline is that they should only ever be introduced if the intention of the ST is for the PCs to explore the mystery of why that bloodline exists. The existing 13 clans already provide more than enough ground to cover almost any character concept they want, and probably 3 of these clans are already superfluous. Why have more unless they exist for a specific lore purpose?

              The only bloodline I've ever used in a game were those associated with the Cappadocian, and the "mystery" there is that the Cappadocians still exist. The Giovanni are full of themselves, and perhaps some time sooner than later, the Cappadocians will get their revenge. That plot line is actually played out now so in retrospect the Giovanni look entirely stupid. And I'm not sure how much of a mystery I could provide as ST since most players are going to know that by now. But as a result, the various lineages of the Cappadocians are figured out in my chronicles so they continue to get used in the setting though I don't follow the V5 version.

              But I've never figured out anything interesting to do with the other bloodlines.

              Now in an earlier thread, I reread the original clan description and came up with how I would run them. You can find that discussion starting here. The main points of how I would use them are 1) the magical number of seven Salubri exist for a reason, 2) Saulot intended these specific seven Salubri of eighth generation to survive, and 3) the diablerie they force their childer to perform on them is to ensure that each Salubri keeps adopting a new "disguise" every so often. As such, I'd never actually show these eighth generation Salubri in a chronicle lest they get killed. I'd only include their 9th generation childer who is still in the tutelage process of their master, but beginning to run their own blood cult around their master's ideals. That way if they get found and killed, it won't disrupt "the master plan".

              However, while I think I could run individual scenes very effectively, I would still not use the Salubri in my own chronicles. Why? I still need to figure out exactly what the hell they are trying to do. Because a magical number of Salubri going around perpetuating their bloodline by diablerie is obviously doing these things for a reason. But what that reason is? No idea. I would want some vague conception at the minimum before doing using them. This has to be some very high Jyhad level stuff going on here. But an intriguing reason for it still escapes me. Until I do, they won't be showing up in the game.

                              Storytelling is like cooking; each clan is an ingredient and as such lends itself to specific cuisines and genres. But that also means that mixing some things together just doesn't work for every application.

                              I have mostly used the Healer Salubri in my games rather than the other variants. But that was because I specifically wanted that almost extinct, non-combative, and moral quandary flavor in the given game. The Healer Salubri is also the default suggestion I have for any player who is really into roleplay and wants to do a drama heavy character, without needing to be the main character.

                              Personally, I like the restricted bloodline size and the potential for a "hard mode" survival build. Too many clans and bloodlines are more or less free for all's when it comes to numbers.

                              As for the Tremere entanglement backstory... I see that less of a Salubri issue and more of a Tremere issue. White Wolf used the Tremere as the default plot point creator for so many things that it imbalanced the lore and the clans associated with it.

                                                  I am currently running three games and they only feature in one of them, set in 5th century B.C.E. in Carthage.
                                                  Because I dislike the Baali concept, and Infernalism as such, I have given the Healer Salubri an interesting role in the game. Their leader is a Mathuselah who believes that Clan affiliation and curses can be broken if they all submit to Baal Hammon, punic god of heaven and bestower of the blessing of vampirism upon the Dark Father. His end goal is to end the Antediluvians power to break the Jyhad and bring peace
                                                  Sadly to hell is paved with good intentions and his project has become darker and darker as time goes by and their methods have gone beyond the questionable.
                                                  This will eventually lead to the Roman Ventrue sacking Carthage. They will branded demon worshipers and thus the idea of Baali will be born.
