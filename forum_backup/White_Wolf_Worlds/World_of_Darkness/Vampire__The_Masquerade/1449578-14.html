                                Originally posted by Professor Phobos View Post
                                Sure, but this feels like more hurt feelings than mere disinterest.
                                Because it is?

                                V5 is supposed to be the 5th edition of a long running RPG called Vampire: the Masquerade, not just a new RPG about playing vampires. When a company decides to take on the task of making a new entry into a long running series of products, they have account for expected continuity of product experience.

                                When you say you're making the 5th edition of something, a significant portion of your customer base are going to see that as a statement that you intend to make something that continues the previous four editions of the game in a meaningful way. If you make major changes, you will hurt some feelings. That's unavoidable, but there's strong reason to minimize it if you can (esp. if you're not having the greatest PR time as it is). You set expectations, and if you break those expectations, it hurts feelings; even if to an extent people made assumptions to base those expectations on that you didn't actively state.

                                That seems logical given most campaigns will feature Camarilla vampires where the Sabbat actually are relegated to a role as antagonists. It seems like a misplaced expectation to assume the game was going to expand on what was always a B-side campaign frame rather than focus on the A-side.
                                This logic doesn't hold up to the context of being a 5th edition with the history of the brand. As has been noted, repeatedly, the Sabbat have been a playable faction of VtM since 1992, when the game has published in 1991. Calling the Sabbat a "B-side" doesn't really work when it's more the second single of an album with the Cam being the first single. The Sabbat are an A-side track, even if not the biggest hit on the record.

                                As for placing expectations between (1) the history of the Sabbat being playable and (2) the actual B-side faction that is the Anarchs getting a player-facing book, the expectation that V5 would follow V2-V20 and keep the Sabbat a player facing option seems a perfectly justifiable assumption for fans to make. The Sabbat not being playable in the core book is one thing, but in the book dedicated to them? That's a different thing.

                                I agree it's a lost opportunity, product-value-wise given it's always nice to have B-sides available, but if the writers are genuinely uninterested in the subject, I'd rather they work on what they feel passionate on first and foremost.
                                There are plenty of people in the wider WoD freelance writer community that would have written a player-facing Sabbat V5 source book if given the chance. That's sort of the whole point of Paradox having so many partners: if Renegade doesn't have an interested party, see if Onyx Path does. Etc. It's not a one-person RPG shop where if one person isn't interested in writing it, nobody is.

                                                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                            Before, there was the idea that Paths of Enlightenment were followed by 100% of the Sabbat, which is hard to take seriously. The idea that you could remove a lifetime of indoctrination and moral values by plopping someone in a gravesite and letting them out. Instead, Justin created a long and fascinating journey where a Sabbat vampire would be required to lower their Humanity and then build themselves back up.
                                                            My problem with this take is that in order to hype up Achilli's supposed contributions, you have to build up a complete caricature of The Players Guide to the Sabbat that most people who have read the book will, I assume, recognize as a caricature. Nowhere in the book does it state that 100% of the Sabbat are Path followers. It explicitly states otherwise, saying that a minority of vampires survive the Creation Rites with their Humanity intact. The book only forbids characters with Humanity from starting the game with a rating higher than 5 (before freebie points), to reflect the trauma of their creation and the fact that simply running with the Sabbat takes its toll on the character's previous values (pages 59-60).

                                                            More importantly, the Sabbat of 2nd Edition are supposed to be a much smaller sect than the Camarilla. They are the elites, the products of not just the Creation Rites but of continual indoctrination through years of brutality and atrocity. It's not the sect for every Tom, Dick, and Harry, because those people fell by the wayside long before they could ever become Path followers. To me it seems that everything you say Revised Edition did to improve the Sabbat is already there in the original 2nd Edition depiction, and all Revised managed to do was bloat the numbers of the sect with additional low-Humanity goons. How is that supposed to make the Sabbat more interesting and compelling?
