    A few points:

    1. Speaking as a neurodivergent person, vampires are all naturally insane because they suffer from spontaneous emotional freak outs as well as sanguinary animism. Most are also narcissicists to begin with. So, I've always felt that quite a lot of Malkavians aren't going to stand out nearly as much as people think. I do note that I also think rather than "insane" as typical mental illness, the majority of "insane" vampire are simply suffering from possessing extreme personality traits. This is almost an inevitability given the freedom from consequence and traumatizing experiences they regularly endure.

    Which is a long way of saying Agata isn't that weird for me and I like her.

    2. The preacher and many other ancilla are people I think who would join the Anarchs because age is far less a barrier to power as well as "mortal" methods of gaining power are easier. One of my all time favorite Anarchs is 1st Edition Chicago by Night's Gordon Keaton. Gordon Keaton is exactly the opposite of your typical Anarch and is basically Patrick Bateman. He's a serial killing yuppie, WASP, snobbish, and rich. Why is he an Anarch?

    Well, he's a Caitiff and not a Ventrue. Also, because he can gain real power among the Anarchs that he could never gain among the Camarilla. Authority though money, charisma, and populism over Disciplines or ancient webs of pawns.

    3. I recommend locking the door twice and using the chain because some hotel staff don't observe that or it falls off. I also recommend using the closet or the bathroom bathtub because Kindred don't care about comfort while they're, well, dead.

    Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

                        Dawn of Stan Millennium

                        Here Karoliina Koski is interviewed by Chinasa Adeyemi. Koski’s name reminds me of Klausi Kinski, the late actor noted for his mellow temperament.

                        Koski is a vampire and a perennial groupie for Finnish black metal groups. Adeyemi informs us that the ratio of black metal to the general population is highest in the world. I’m not certain what to make of that fact.

                        This section of the book made me think of ‘Lords of Chaos,’ the 2019 mostly non-fiction movie about a Finnish black metal band that tried to overcome its own mediocrity by burning down churches, among other things. I will admit to knowing little about black metal or about the Finnish take on the artistic mode, but I can easily understand (through a reasonable understanding of human behavior) how some hacks might commit crimes in an effort to boost their brand, rather than doing something like practicing. Black metal, in form and substance, is a mode that rejects most convictable standards in terms of society, music and so on – but the performers with even a spoonful of skill and talent can make it work without committing arson, murder, and jaywalking (and possibly cannibalism).

                        The point being, if Koski were a performer she like would be one lucky to get a spot opening for no-name bands operating in the boonies. She is also one who would lean on the entire arson, murder, and cannibalism heavily. However, rather than being a performer, Koski is (to the credit of the human writer of this section) a walking-talking personification of the bad fan. The black metal scene is one of violent performances, so one groupie biting another during a concert would raise little suspicion. Or at least less suspicion than it would at a Celene Dion event.

                        If you can get past the relative obnoxiousness of Koski, the entire scene black metal scene is pure rebellion at its loudest and most theatrical. It rejects not simply the rule of ancients, but anything which would prohibit violence, spraying strangers with pig’s blood, glorifying suicide, corpse desecration, and so on. So it tacitly asks, how deep does your rebellion go? Where is the line between nihilistic music as rebellion and an expression of mental illness?

                                    A bunch of teens: Yahoo! We’re all going mountain climbing this weekend!

                                    Grizzled Old Man: Don’t do it. There is a beast, a monster, a walking death on the mountain, a blood hungry curse.

                                    Teens: …

                                    Old Man: He’ll be the death of each and everyone one of you!

                                    Teens: …

                                    Old Man: And he’s sporting the latest in mountain gear from REI and is a frequent contributor to Mountain Climbing Monthly!

                                    Survivor Girl: Guys, we don’t we just go to Santa Clara and go swimming, like I suggested in the first place?

                                    Death at the Roof of the World

                                    In this section roving vampire reporter Chinasa Adeyemi interviews Antoni
                                    Morawski, a gangrel who maintains a predilection for mountain climbing he possessed while alive.

                                    There are thematic notes connecting sections of the Anarch book, which are tonally stronger than the structural connections in the Camarilla book. This section, much like the last one, profiles a vampire who is the personification of the bad fan (they use their nominal fandom to murder and eat other people in subculture), who follows their interest around but who does not actually perform.

                                    And to reiterate something that is a reoccurring theme, if not outright stated; vampires are only ever bad people. Morawski murders a fellow mountain climber, whom he knew personally. Morawski says the other climber was dead when he found him, but the statement feels like a lie.

                                    The observation I have about both sections is that they do provide plot hooks, but more for a group running mortals or hunters in the WoD than for vampires. Both Morawski and Koski are apparently solitary vampires (no evident pack or coterie) running around in a human subculture, murdering and eating members of that subculture. Discovering the murders are occurring, that the murderer is a vampire, and then discovering that vampire is a respected and trusted fan… that would be an engaging horror story. For mortals. It might have less traction for a group of vampires. In fact, there is arguably little reason for a PC group to encounter either of them, let alone tangle with them over something.
