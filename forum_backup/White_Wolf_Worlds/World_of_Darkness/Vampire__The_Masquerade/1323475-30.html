                Overall

                I've said it before and I'll say it again that I think this is probably the best book ever written for the World of Darkness. The attention to detail, the tying together of metaplots, and the twists as well as turns make it amazing for the serious fan of the gameline. Honestly, the biggest flaw of the book might be that if you AREN'T a serious nerd of the subject then likeTHE LEAGUE OF EXTRAORDINARY GENTLEMANthat the majority of references will go over your head.

                The heart of the book is Beckett and the use of the signature characters, though. You don't need to get everyone one of the references as long as you enjoy the characters playing off one another. Your knowledge of their past relationships and appearances enriches the experiences but isn't 100% necessary. Sort of like seeing Grand Admiral Thrawn in STAR WARS: REBELS. Yes, I made that comparison.

                Now I'm going to make a confession because we've all been thinking it while reading my posts. Does this actually tie-into V5 as well as I'm constantly suggesting it does? No, not really. Obviously, this book was predicting a very different sort of future for the World of Darkness than the one we eventually got. The Beckoning, Society of Leopold, rise of the Anarchs, and breakup of the Sabbat are all alluded to here but the obvious absence of much in the way of the government as well as relatively minor role of the Gehenna War means that it is something that zigged instead of zagged.

                If BJD had been the basis for V5 then I believe the future of the line would have been more:

                * The Sabbat Civil War
                * The Drowned Legacies become a major power
                * Carna joins the Anarchs, The Co7 Tremere are against them
                * The Anarchs are more powerful but remain the 3rd Sect with Warlords like Sejanus and Monty Coven making use of them.
                * Probably no Chicago by Night or Kevin Jackson as Prince (this is my wildest guess)
                * More Tremere as Worm vs. Tremere as Goratrix intrigue
                * The government remains completely ignorant with HUNTERS HUNTED II implying the NSA, FBI, and CIA have gotten MORE ignorant and weak over the years rather than being supercharged badasses.
                * A continuation of very powerful ancient Elders doing their Jyhad-esque things rather than being sidelined.
                * The Lasombra don't join the Camarilla
                * Lucita becomes Regent of the Sabbat and maybe makes it more focused, less of an embarrassing band of psychopaths (and others break away)
                * The Tal'Mah'Ra as a major power in Africa and the Middle East
                * The Setites join the Camarilla alongside the Banu Haqim
                * A Red List Sect is created around Kemintri as implied by the Dread Names, Red List book.
                * The Hecata may have come into existence
                * A revised totally different Wan Kuei instead of writing them out.
                * The Tzimisce will be shifted from their super-monstrousness to something more closer to the Old Clan by destroying Kupala.

                Basically, a continuation of the globe-trotting, mystical, mysterious Revised of The Transylvania Chronicles and The Giovanni Chronicles versus the "Return to 1st Edition, Anarchs vs. Elders vs. Hunters."

                I don't think this is a bad thing, though, and I think there is more than enough connections in this that future supplements under the guidance of Modiphus and Onyx Path Publishing will follow up on a lot of these plots. Certainly, I have no doubt The Fall of London will basically be a sequel to the Monty Coven chapter mixed with the Second Inquisition. Basically, it's the same writers who will be taking the wheel from now on and I see them as never letting good ideas go to waste.

                Awesome stuff all round.

                            In terms of picked up plot hooks from BJD appearing in V5, I should urge patience. We've not seen too many V5 books yet, and many of those outstanding plots are still to be covered, but will be.

                                    Part One


                                    This chapter is titled “The Omega.” Capuchin first referenced the term in Giovanni Chronicles V, where he wrote “That is the moment Beckett will know God, Caine, the Alpha, and the Omega.” Perhaps by the end of this chapter Beckett does know too much. He had been improbably sceptical of the antediluvians for too long. Now, that scepticism has ended for him. But even if he has an answer, it will not give him a sense of completion or satisfaction.

                                    This chapter also covers a lot of ground, both literally (moving from Libya to Italy to France and to Egypt) and narratively (covering Kindred eBay, ghoul uprising, bloodline parties and plot, the red list, red list politics, and the 2nd Generation). The chapter is home to solid elements, and some that leave me… underwhelmed. I has taken me time to work out why I have mixed feelings.

                                    On the one hand, Serenna returns with guns blazing. The auction is amusing and insightful. The insight into the politics shaping the red list is perfect. That Kemintiri so effortlessly has Beckett over a barrel is also perfect. The ghoul uprising and the plotting by the Bloodline is also good. Frankly, it looks like a lot of elements – human surveillance technology, possibly Vitel, possibly the bloodlines – play a role in driving the Second Inquisition. That makes a great deal of sense.

                                    On the other hand, the scene of almost every signature character digging a hole in the desert just does not work for me. Most of them at least dislike each other and none of them -except possibly Anatole – have any reason to help Beckett out of a pickle. That a bunch of Alastors are shoved into the sarcophagus of Enoch, like clowns in a circus VW, just does not work for me at all. How long were they, and their weapons, supposed to be just chilling in that stone box waiting for Kemintiri to pop the lid?

                                    Alan Moore is wrong – things do end. The book could not go on forever. The narrative portion of BJD ends well, even if I have mixed feelings about this chapter. It ends on ambiguous note, refusing to state what is sarcophagus.

                                                    Re: V20 to V5

                                                    My inclination is that V5 is a "soft reboot" rather than a hard reboot like Requiem. The idea behind it is that it is a jumping on point for new fans or fans who want to start over. It's still the same universe as before but the edges have been filed off so that it's easier for people to jump in who have no experience with the original metaplot.

                                                    A lot of people have commented on the fact that it feels like that the series has gone back to 1st Edition but it makes a certain amount of sense. It provides only a short number of Clans you have to memorize the politics of, gets the sects to some basic principles (Haves vs. Have nots). The Hunters are a huge threat because people don't need to know the complicated and intricate religious values of the Sabbat to understand Fox Mulder, Van Helsing, or the Men in Black.

                                                    There's still all the stuff that used to be there as we see with the ridiculous amount of in-universe fiction at the start of the V5 sourcebook. This includes things that probably shouldn't have been there like the entirety of the Sabbat 9/11 plot.

                                                    In a way, you can view V5 as a "Starter Kit" for the setting.
