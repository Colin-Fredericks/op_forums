            Clanbook: Setites (part three)

            The final three.

            The Priest of Darkness
            "A mad eyed servant of absolute evil." That's literally the entire concept as written in the character write up. (I think this just made me realize what it is that irritates me so much about the whole "We are EVIL - muhahaha" presentation of the clan in this book: That's the fricking Baali. It makes one of the two groups redundant.) Anyway, we do at least get some backstory. A teen metalhead turned Satanist whose apparent dedication got him an invite to a Setite temple. Several years of ghouldom later, he graduated to full vampire and priest of the dark god. Now he spends his nights laughing insanely, rubbing his hands together, brooding, and waxing poetically about how awesome Set is. Seriously, that is what's listed under roleplaying hints. Socials are primary, with Manipulation maxed out to five, and Charisma at three. Mental is secondary, with Perception and Intelligence at three each. Intimidation, Leadership, Stealth, and Occult are all at three dots, as is Setite Knowledge. And he's got a few other secondary abilities. Camarilla Lore is at one. I don't know why he needs this. He also has one dot in Sense Deception. Again, I don't really see the need. And he has Public Speaking at two dots. I guess for leading temple services, though I'd think maybe Leadership could cover for that. I also find it a little odd that a priest of Set has no Subterfuge ability. You'd think that would be part of the basic curriculum. Disciplines are the one dot each, but this time with two extra dots, one in Presence and one in Serpentis. Just as a personal thing, I think you could drop the Obfuscate dot and either bump one of the others up to three or use the points for something else. Background is one in Generation, two in Herd and two in Retainers. I would've expected maybe at least one dot in Clan Status. Maybe Mentor as well. Path is five, and I'm pretty sure we can assume this is the Path of Typhon. Willpower is six. The concept of hardcore true believer Priest of Set is a perfectly solid one and potentially a good one. This does come anywhere near that potential. In fact it feels like it may have less thought put into it than the Gangsta. I would really enjoy seeing this concept done properly, because this interpretation of it is almost laughable.

            Sister Sin (The Sister of Sin?)
            A nun who apparently came from a family of career criminals, and who had a drinking problem. Apparently she kept stealing money from the poor box to cover her drinking. And this was enough to attract the attention of the Setites. I will pause here to note that this is one of those things were I think a little more effort or detail in the back story might strengthen things over all. Say, if her ruin was her sire's pet project from the moment she decided to take up holy orders. (I think this may be what I felt the Exotic Dancer was missing too; If, for example, the psychologist her parents sent her to as a child had ended up being her sire.) Anyway, now she's in charge of the orphanage, getting to corrupt all the little children. And no, not that way (thankfully). She's basically a modern day Fagin, training the kids to steal and commit other crimes. Socials are primary, with four each in Charisma and Manipulation. Mental is secondary, with a 3/2/3 spread. And holy crap does she have a bunch of secondary abilities. At three dots each, we have Streetwise, Forgery, Lockpicking, Pickpocket, and Theology. At two each we have Empathy, Leadership, Security, Medicine, Instruction, and Bribery. I remember with the original Tremere template at the start saying that it felt like someone had just grabbed every "magic" ability on the list and given it to him. Well, this is the same with all the "crime" abilities. (I am so glad Dark Ages came up with the Larceny ability.) I honestly think Bribery, Forgery and Lockpicking can be done away with (and Security maybe raised to three or four, along with some points in Subterfuge). And as much as it's a stereotype, I'm a little surprised at the idea of a nun without Intimidation. Disciplines are, again, the one each plus one. This time it's Presence. If I had to drop one, it'd probably be the dot in Serpentis. Backgrounds include five dots in Retainers (the orphans, I presume), four in Herd (the same), and one in Allies. I feel like there should maybe be at least one dot of criminal Contacts. Maybe also Resources from all the illicit money her little crime ring brings in. She's actually a Humanity case, at five, with Willpower of seven. I like this character a lot. The modern day Fagin is a great vampire concept, and the idea of it being the nun in charge of an orphanage works really well as a method of undermining a traditional symbol of morality and virtue (and thankfully not in a way that involves sexual depravity). The problems with the point allocations are easily fixed. I think this is probably my favorite one of the book.

            The Voodoo Houngan
            So, originally from Haiti - and this appears to draw on certain stuff about the country from the first edition A World of Darkness sourcebook, which I've not read in ages - and a junior initiate into the priesthood. Apparently, he was jilted or romantically betrayed by someone, and ended up going to one of the "red" sects in the hills for vengeance. The Setite who led the sect apparently decided he'd make a suitable Childe and embraced him. Eventually, he moved to Miami to run the clan's drug and corruption business there. So, apparently he's as much drug kingpin/crime lord as he is houngan. Socials are primary with Manipulation maxed out to five, and Charisma at three. Physicals are secondary, with Dexterity at four dots. I somehow feel Mental should be secondary or even primary with this character. Alertness, Leadership, Subterfuge, Survival, Occult and Politics are at three dots. Empathy, Streetwise, Stealth and Linguistics are two. Investigation is one. Curiously, he has no combat abilities. Disciplines are again one each, and this time with two extra going into Presence and Serpentis. I'm a little surprised one of them didn't go into Thaumaturgy. Backgrounds are two dots in Generation, three in Herd, and one in Contacts. Given the way he's described as being set up in Miami, I'm surprised at the lack of Retainers, Resources, Clan Status, and possibly Influence. He has Humanity at five, which again surprises me a little given how devoted he is said to be to Set and "the Darkness". Willpower is also five. The character is not bad. I rather like the back story and the idea of one's broken/betrayed heart putting them on a path of vengeance and eventual damnation is a pretty interesting one. I kind of wish the drug angle had been left out, with more focus on him just being a figure of supernatural fear and terror within the immigrant neighborhood(s) he reigns over. And I think that seven points spent on one of the discipline dots could've been better spent elsewhere. But other than that, it's not bad.

            And that's it. This is kind of a mixed bag. The drug war thing is a little omnipresent, with three characters being involved in it (and two others heavy consumers, at least when alive). The Sister Sin nun is my favorite of the entire bunch, followed by the Dilettante and probably the Exotic Dancer and Voodoo Houngan (in spite of some of their flaws). Most of the others are OK, but kind of weak in various areas. The Gangsta and Priest of Darkness are probably the weakest of the lot, lacking much thought and being one dimensional stereotypes. And the Closet Setite is entirely based around the idea that the Setites are apparently supposed to be seen as the equivalent of the Baali or something.
            Representation wise, four of the ten are women. Three of them are clearly black - the Closet Setite, the Gangsta and the Voodoo Houngan - and the rest seem to be white. Given how the Setites were frequently presented as "the African clan", that's certainly note worthy on some level. I'm a little surprised none of them appear to be Egyptian/Middle Eastern.
            I'm also a little surprised the nun is the only Christian religious figure. You'd have expected this parade of "Gen X corruption's greatest hits" to have included a televangelist. Also a little surprised that, given the whole snake angle, there's no snake handling holy roller type. No crooked accountant or Wall Street inside trader either. But I think the one I'm really kind of surprised not to see is some sort of sleazy porn producer along the lines of Larry Flint.
            The theme this book was going for was corruption, and all of the characters play in to that. Though personally I really prefer my Setites more along the lines of dangerous Dionysian rebels and Byronic antiheroes. But that's just me.

            Anyway, that's Clanbook: Setites. Next is, I believe, the Tzimisce. However, it'll probably be a few days before I'm able to get around to it.

They do it to weaken their enemies so they can seize power and ultimately take control. In essence, I view the Setites as being pimps and drug pushers. They are pimps and drug pushers with a grandiose sense of self. And as one works her way up the cult's hierarchy, they act less like low level pimps and drug pushers into the equivalent of caporegimes and godfathers. But as Don Corleone once said of a head of a rival crime family, "Tattaglia's a pimp." So some things never change.

                    I've been wary of the idea that since Setites push corruption on others, that it means their members need to be corrupt in those ways as well. After all, what crime boss wants their own dealers to be using their own product? It hurts the profit margins and leads to your underlings making dumb decisions. Ideally, the people you have selling the drugs should not be doing any drugs themselves.

                    I've mentioned before that I essentially see the Setites as being a cult, and as such they only embrace mortal members of that cult - although of course their membership is in front organizations that reflect some of the Setite teachings as opposed to the "real" cult and its teachings which have to be hidden for obvious reasons. There are various layers of the onion, and only those "members" (and not all front organizations are obviously religiously inspired, there are many mundane ones like criminal gangs) found "worthy" are inducted into the next layer. And only that layer's "worthies" get inducted into the next layer and so on. Until they have a layer of mortals ready for potential embrace. I think this way because why would the Setites ever embrace someone not already part of their religion? Too much can go wrong by embracing someone who may have ideas on their own. And if so, you've wasted time, resources, and vitae on a bad investment. Even people you think fit into the clan's culture can go wrong (I like CTPhipps comment on the Dilettante that she works best as someone broken by the clan instead of being a success story), so it's best to minimize it.

                    Setites need people who are part of that demimonde, twilight world of corruption so they have people who are capable of exploiting others. But not enough that they become corrupt themselves. It's a matter of distinguishing between the rubes (the people who buy the cult's BS - and that includes most of its own members among mortals), and the people who "get" it but go along with the exploitation because its part of their own path towards power. And the Setites can offer power to their members - first the embrace, then support from the clan's cultlike hierarchy, then by access to certain powers - especially clan specific blood magic, and lastly by that hoary vampire power source - knowledge. At high up in the clan, the elders likely have very powerful blood sorcery and other weird powers granted to them by the Wyrm, or whatever figurehead they use in place of it. That gives them things like demonic investments or ability to create fomori (like those "vampire" snakes in Berlin By Night).

                    Eventually you can move up from a slaver to a warlord and finally to James Earl Jones' Thulsa Doom. So it's not a bad racket if you're good at it.

                    Conflict within the clan comes from rival power groups within the cult (which in the case of Serpents of the Light caused a wholesale defection), and vampires who eventually become disappointed by what the cult offers - whether its because they didn't get the promotion, or because they realize how empty the "enlightenment" is of the Setite's teaching. "Heresy" of course is a convenient excuse to purge the clan of either group.

                        A couple of thoughts:

                        1. The Baali and Setites were both originally just the generic "evil" clans and also peculiarly divided. The Baali were never meant to be NPCs and basically just evil creatures at the heart of the Sabbat as embodied by Shaitan. [Editor's note: Interestingly enough, I am now adapting CHAOS FACTOR to my current Technocracy game.] The thing was that the Setites were not ACTUAL Satanists, though, just 90% Satanists and thus were available as a player character option. My view was the fact that this was perhaps a attempt to avoid being too controversial in what was already a controversial game.

                        Now, 25 years later, the two clans have diverged enough that they are completely different. The Baali have the dubious honor of being a combination of Cthulhu Cultists and Banetenders [See the Uktena tribe]. It's their job to try to keep the Cthulhuoid abominations sealed and from waking up by relaxing them with the screams of babies and sacrifice of innocent maidens among other things much-much nastier. Then there's that small group of them that actively want to bring about the end of the world.

                        The Followers of Set have since evolved into a kind of philosophical evil that I think is actually a better version of the Sabbat than the Sabbat generally are. The Sabbat claim to be philosophical warrior poets and "beyond good and evil" but are generally just douchebags with fangs. I think the Revised Setites are probably 90% people on Paths of Enlightenment and much better at being the inhuman intellectual evil. A large part of that would be the depiction of Hesha Ruhadze in SETITE where he was shown as being less "muhahahhaa evil" and more a guy who actually understands Nieztsche.

                        Basically, whenever I mock the Sabbat [and I do, a lot, as much as I love them], I use this clip from A FISH CALLED WANDA.


                        Which is why I think them joining the Anarchs is THE WORST THING. Because the Anarchs are a bunch of people dying for a unifying ideological, charismatic leaders, and thinkers rather than doers--which is just describing the Followers of Set Revised.

                        2. Which is actually why I love the Priest of Darkness in that I do think it'd be great to use this character as comic relief or basically as a card trick. The Priest of Darkness should be a 13th level Neonate who was Embraced solely to serve as a distraction to the other Kindred of the City. The character would then realize that he's nothing more than a obvious evil monster that other Kindred would use to justify ignoring the cult. The guy who recruits drug addicts, hookers, and bored soccer moms to his guilt while a much deeper set of mysteries was being denied them. The character is a joke and yet could be more than a joke--if he wants.

                        But as you may have noted, I like "failed" Kindred.

                        3. Sister Sin: I feel more like a NPC than a PC, though, but that's just me. I agree a televangelist would be a perfect Follower of Set character. I think the Prosperity Gospel which is, essentially, inverted Christianity already would be something that the Followers of Set would be all over. I actually note the FoS have ocassionally maintained Jesus was a face of Set. I could easily view them as thinking that Set as the Anti-Christ would be an easy sell in the Bible Belt. You're damned anyway so might as well get on the winning side.

                        4. The Voodoo Houngan: I'd be interested in how this figure would react to the discovery that his Blood Cult is something of a scam. I mean if you were a mortal and met a Kindred, it'd be very easy to have them display supernatural powers and convince you of their divinity [or not]. However, as a vampire you might discover that they don't have any particular spiritual insights or power [though the Followers of Set would beg to differ]. I tend to think the Serpents of Light broke away after this point as they have room in their theology for Kindred as something other than demons but the Followers of Set are so atavistically evil that no sane person would follow them (but geniuses are not always sane).

                        5. One of my most beloved character was Tet-Anubis who was a Follower of Set/Serpent of Light/True Hand member. Yes, I broke a bunch of rules there but Tet-Anubis' thing was that he believed the Caine myth was a misunderstanding of the Osiris and Set myth (or vice versa). He was a follower of the Path of Caine and believed strongly in transcending the typical morality of humans to act like gods. The thing was that he believed the more traditional Egyptian interpretation of Set that he guarded the barge of Ra against Apophis. Thus he believed that Setites and vampires in general were meant to test human virtue and not actually be evil for the sake of evil. Also to protect them against supernatural evils like demons.

                        He was a heretic many times over.

                                Clanbook: Tzimisce (part one)

                                Once again back to 1995. This book was written by Rob Hatch, who would go on to be Vampire developer for a while, and you may remember as the author of the Nosferatu book. Art is by John Cobb, who has a rather distinctive art style, in which I mean that almost none of it looks even remotely human or natural (and in some cases this includes even allegedly human looking characters seeming to have distorted features and/or anatomy). And almost every humanoid image in the book has these crazy bug eyes like they are rolling around in the creatures' heads. The only human looking image in this entire book is the cover image by Tim Bradstreet (which is pretty good, if totally at odds with the tone of the rest of the book; it kind of looks like a young version of Rob Zombie's wife). And I think the back cover image is the only one to rival that of the Malkavian nose-picker in the "what were they thinking?" category. I'm actually going to trigger warning this one.
                                It's a face that's been remade into a vulva/vagina with teeth. The "nose" is a clitoris, and is pierced, because of course it is.

                                This book was originally shipped in a plastic back with "Adults Only" printed on it, and a black square blocking said image on the back.
                                This book was developed by Jennifer Hartshorn, who during her tenure at WWGS was developer for Wraith, Vampire: The Dark Ages and Vampire at various points.
                                As I mentioned, this is clearly the point when the clan shifted from "slightly respectable creepy weirdoes" to "all out freak show". This is not exactly helped by what I've come to notice as a trend of "shock for shock's sake" aspect of Hatch's work, especially when dealing with the Tzimisce. While I think grotesque certainly has its place in Vampire, I think this tends to over do it a bit. Some of it starts to feel like it's just "look how edgy and adult I am for talking like this/about this subject" adolescent posturing. And I can't help but roll my eyes at it when it pops up.
                                These character use the Sabbat character sheet, with its paths and alternate virtues, but still have the standard character creation guidelines at the bottom. Unlike all of the previous ones, it doesn't have the clan Disciplines typed out on the sheet, instead having them written in. (This is weird as the four page sheet in the back actually has those.) Also, this is back when the Tzimisce and Lasombra lacked their own clan symbol, just having the Sabbat wax/blood seal with a T instead of an S. And all of these have a completely different concept written on the character sheet than what they are titled as. I find this a little annoying.

                                The Carnivorous Carny (Anthropophagic Yokel)
                                No, I'm not really sure why it was necessary to have an alternate concept on the character sheet using a super fancy term for "cannibal" and a slightly derogatory term for rural people. Anyway, this character was a member of the Zantosa revenant family. I'll note that this seems a little out of character for that family, as they are the wealthy hedonistic drug dealers/slavers known for their social graces, and this character is kind of the backwoods hillbilly Texas Chainsaw Massacre cannibal type one usually associates with the Bratovich. Apparently a Tzimisce being pursued by Camarilla archons stumbled upon the family compound, resulting in this guy saving him and managing to trap and stake the archons so said Tzimisce could eat them. This impressed the vampire so much that he embraced our character and took him off to join the Sabbat. Now he's the leader of a nomadic pack that apparently passes itself off as a traveling freak show, getting by mainly on his excess of rural charm and slick friendliness. All in all, I kind of like that as an idea for a Sabbat pack. Socials are primary, and for some odd reason he has Appearance at four (and the other two at three). I wouldn't find anything wrong with that if it weren't for the fact that the character image really doesn't reflect this. It looks like what you'd get if Rob Zombie tried to reimagine Grampa Munster. He barely looks human, let alone super attractive. Mental is secondary, and Physicals drop Stamina to one so Dexterity can be three. Abilities are ... literally all over the place. He has three dots in Subterfuge, and two dots in Acting and Leadership. The rest is just a lot of ones scattered all over the page. The write up says this is to reflect a lot of life (and unlife) experience, but instead it just feels like a total lack of focus. Disciplines are two each in Animalism and Vicissitude. Given that Zantosa dropped Animalism for Presence, this seems odd, even though I know embraced revenants don't exactly have to keep their living powers. Backgrounds are a lot, with two each in Allies, Contacts, and Resources, one in Status and three in Retainers. Path is Power and the Inner Voice at five, with Willpower at eight. In general, I like this character as a concept. It's an interesting idea for a Sabbat pack, and has a lot of potential for creepiness. However I wish both the art and the character sheet were a lot better. The character sheet doesn't add up. It's at least four points over with conventional rules and two points over with Sabbat ones. Rather than having dots just spread out all over, I think it would help to have just focused on the ones that best suit the concept. So, generally a good idea just hampered by bad implementation.

                                The Count (Exiled Aristocrat)
                                I supposed this was sort of inevitable. It's a picture of Bela Lugosi's Dracula. He's an Old Clan Tzimisce. The scion of an old and illustrious Romanian noble family that had to flee to the West after the Soviet's invaded, he grew up almost like some parody of a 19th century noble. Eventually his parents died and he found out the family was drowning in debt. Then an "uncle" from the old country arrived and embraced him into the "true" Tzimisce. And then the Sabbat came, ate his uncle, and gave him a "join or die" offer. So now he's an Old Clan in the Sabbat. He finds the sect interesting, even if it is overrun with annoying peasants. He lives in a creepy mansion and sleeps in a coffin. Socials are again primary, this time with Charisma at four. Mental are secondary. Abilities include three dots each in Intimidation, Leadership, Etiquette and Linguistics. Also in Balkan History. Then there are twos in Alertness and Dodge. The rest are a handful of ones. Disciplines are two each in Animalism and Auspex, and one in Dominate. Backgrounds include four dots in Retainers and two each in Generation and Resources. Path is Honorable Accord at five and Willpower at ... three. I think this may be the lowest of any template ever. This isn't a terrible character, but not a very good one either. In some ways it feels like an obligatory inclusion as a joke or something. The basic idea, especially that of a sort of stereotypical old world Tzimisce forced into the Sabbat against their will, is something that has a lot of potential. I think some sort of second draft refinement would've improved on this a lot.

                                The Fallen Surgeon (Victim of Circumstance)
                                A former top of her class surgeon from a family of doctors who fell into a life of drugs, gambling and debt. To pay off said debts, she found herself part of some sort of bizarre underground "porn" ring that used surgically modified freaks made from vagrants, junkies and runaways. Because grotesque. Anyway, apparently a bunch of Sabbat broke in and destroyed the place, but one Tzimisce was so impressed with our doctor's work that she embraced her. Now she still does odd research, mostly involving war ghouls, and tries to fool herself into believing that some sort of good might come out of her work. Mental is primary, with Perception maxed out to five and Intelligence at four, but Wits at one, which feels slightly wonky. Physicals are secondary, with Dexterity raised to four. Abilities have Medicine maxed out at five, which makes perfect sense. Melee and Body Alteration are at three each. Empathy, Etiquette, and Science are at two each. The rest are ones, including Torture. Disciplines have Vicissitude at three and Auspex at one. I'm actually happy to finally, after two clan books, see someone with a Discipline above two. Even more so in the clan's Special Sauce as this is a character that leans into that power and benefits from it. Backgrounds include Resources at three (I'm not sure from what), Retainers at two, and Sabbat Status and Herd at one each. Path of Death and the Soul is at eight, and I think the path fits the character. Willpower is only four. Overall, I like this character. I find the porn angle a little off putting, but that aside the character has a really good backstory, fits really well with the clan's overall image, and even has kind of an interesting if depressing personality. A few of the character points could maybe be improved, but otherwise I think this is a really good one.

                                The Seeker of the Forbidden (Supernatural Investigator)
                                And from the medical flesh freakery to the more scholarly side of the clan. A quiet bookish child who grew into an obsessive scholarly adult, her sense of wonder turning to cynicism and paranoia (if not borderline nihilism). Her encyclopedic grasp of paranormal and occult lore eventually attracted the attention of a Fiend that was so impressed that she embraced her so she'd have all eternity to continue her research. Now she travels, continuously looking for new secrets to unearth. Mental is primary, with Perception and Intelligence at four each. Physicals are secondary. Occult is at four dots, with Alertness, Investigation and Linguistics at three. Security and Stealth are at two each, and a couple of one dot abilities round things out. Among those are Lupine Lore, which I'm not really sure why she would have, and Cthulhu Mythos, which ... I guess I get what they're going for here, but it just seems redundant in the face of her high Occult score. Honestly, I'd much rather those two dots gone to something like Kindred Lore, which would feel a lot more fitting. Disciplines are four dots in Auspex and an extra dot in Thaumaturgy, which seems fitting. Also, after two books where almost every character had at least one dot in their clan's Special Sauce just because, it's nice to see one that doesn't do that. Backgrounds are a single dot each in Mentor and Resources. I'm a little surprised at the lack of Contacts. This also feels like a character where a single dot of Fame actually wouldn't feel out of place, which would be a novelty for a Sabbat character. Path of Caine is at five. Willpower is at seven. I really like this character. It plays to a side of the clan that I think often got overlooked with all the flesh crafting shenanigans, and feels like it'd be interesting to play or just include in a chronicle. So a win here.

                                That's all for this entry. Next will be the last three Tzimisce. Then we will move on to Lasombra.

                                ADDED: I forgot to mention the character art for the last two. They are, perhaps, the most human looking of any image in this book, save the cover. The Surgeon is fairly gaunt looking, which suits the character's backstory pretty well, and otherwise fairly restrained in appearance. Likewise the Seeker, who has this sort of blue-collar Goth look going on, and aside from what seems to be a third eye drawn or tattooed on the forehead, appears like a normal human being.

                                    The Vicissitude power really took over the clan. Signature Disciplines often did that because they were the most distinctive things about them. For me, one of the reasons I've had a very low interest in the Tzimisce is because of this. I found the concept of the Vicissitude Discipline to be completely inexplicable (and the statement in Dirty Secrets that it was also an umbral disease!). I wondered why this was in my Vampire game. It wasn't until a full decade later that I finally discovered it was because of the Necroscope series. A series none of my friends or I had ever read. But even after I learned that and understood why it was included, it wasn't an element I wanted in "my" Vampire. I thought it should have been an optional rule for STs to include it in chronicles if they wanted to go that route. I thought it was that off putting and genre-clashing.

                                    I know many other players ignorant of Necroscope as much as myself didn't mind and accepted it completely. But it was always head scratching for me.

                                    As a result, when I ST my own Vampire chronicles, I don't use Vicissitude at all. I replaced it with standard Protean twenty years ago. None of my friends minded. None of them liked that Discipline anyway. It did mean I had to throw out much of the canon established around the clan and reinvent archetypes. But I thought the basics of the clan being territorial (as a result of their clan weakness), being "fiends" in the sense they relished tormenting the living, and that had remnant ties to feudalism - whether as being tied to estates or having "serfs" among the Revenants - was more than enough to create a strong clan identity.

                                    There were some iconic elements in the Sabbat - the Blood Brothers and Vozhd - I thought had to be included, and they were somewhat dependent on Vicissitude. So my response was to make the Sabbat Tzimisce as possessing a unique Blood Magic like Bio-Thaumaturgy that allowed them to do that. Any kind of Dr Frankenstein concepts could work just as well with that instead and not "pollute" the rest of the setting.

                                    Strangely enough once I knew about the Necroscope concept, I had no problem taking that and applying it (and the Souleaters of Dirty Secrets) as a unique kind of fomori for the Garou to kill. But I always kept them limited in number and in isolated "colonies" because the Garou were effective at wiping out these umbral invaders.

                                                    One aspect of the Sabbat clans is that their prime concept clashes to what the clanbook is trying to do. There are many contradictions within the Sabbat, and the writers needed to handle that. I don't think they ultimately succeeded in doing so.

                                                    If the Camarilla's theme tends to be that the human was selected because there was something about their human life that caused the vampire to embrace them. That also ties into the theme of Humanity.

                                                    But the Sabbat isn't like that. They're more like the monstrous vampires of old that simply selects their victim because it's what they have on hand. The original shovelhead theme went along with that. The vampires of the Sabbat didn't care what you were like as a human. It was utterly unimportant. What was important was what you did AFTER you became a vampire. That ties into the themes of the Paths of Enlightenment.

                                                    As an example we can use Wendy Wade from Chicago By Night. She was simply a lawyer randomly abducted and embraced. Nothing about her in particular caused her embrace by the Sabbat. In first edition CbN no clan is listed (it just says Sabbat coven), but in second edition CbN it shows Tzimisce.

                                                    That fits with the stereotypical vampire in fiction before Anne Rice or VtM. If a vampire shows up in your town - whether one of the Hammer film vampires, Count Yorga, Kurt Barlow, or Marie from Innocent Blood - you get randomly turned into a vampire because a vampire needs to feed, and feeds on whomever. That vampire may indeed become obsessed about an individual and specifically try to embrace that person, but most of the new vampires created were simply selected at random.

                                                    My view was and is that the Sabbat was an attempt to emulate that style while making it work within the mechanics and setting created for the World of Darkness.

                                                    So in my mind, all the templates in the Lasombra and Tzimisce books are essentially wrong at a categorical level. They are more appropriate for a Camarilla type clan instead of the Sabbat. Sabbat templates should heavily include random embraces that worked out in the end (as opposed to those who never became True Sabbat, or met Final Death beforehand), and how they became something appropriate for the Sabbat. As opposed to people who already fit the archetype.

                                                    This was a problem I had in general with these clanbooks. They did well in describing the clan in pre-Sabbat terms, but failed to show what actually changed during the Anarch Revolt. Just like we saw differences between the anti-tribu and the main clans, we should be able to see the difference in the clans pre-Sabbat (and reflecting the Lasombra antitribu and Old Clan Tzimisce) and post-Sabbat. I don't think that was successfully done. There just seemed too much continuity between them.

                                                    Eventually the writers acknowledged this, and began the process of eroding those stylistic differences between the Camarilla and Sabbat. We're later told that shovelhead vampires aren't the norm, and that in fact the Sabbat vampires act much like the Camarilla only "more evil". Boring. The Sabbat just became another Camarilla as opposed to a different kind of sect that could be used to emulate a different style of vampire (the more traditional monster kind that dominated before Anne Rice). This was a categorical error, and it would lead to more bad decisions later on.

                                                    When I create NPC Sabbat packs for my chronicles (I do occasionally use them as antagonists in Vampire, but I usually use them as the de facto vampires in my Werewolf and other WoD games), my concepts for their human lives tend to be things like Farmer, Mailman, Runaway, and Housewife in order to reflect the random nature of their embrace by the Sabbat. Eventually those Sabbat vampires that live (not dying at the hands of the sect's enemies, or by other Sabbat!) get molded into a "proper" Sabbat Lasombra and Sabbat Tzimisce. Which is close to their antitribu and Old Clan versions, but not identical because after all the Anarch Revolt had some effect on those clans' cultures.

                                                    Since in my head canon history, many Lasombra and Tzimisce elders did join the Sabbat, there is a faction of Sabbat vampires who do keep to the old ways - they're the "Ultraconservatives". And the tension between them and the "real" Sabbat (in the Loyalist movement) is what lead to the sect's civil wars. So I do think some templates of the clanbook are acceptable Sabbat templates. But I thought the clanbooks should have made that distinction, and showed us examples of good archetypical T and L vampires that became that way after their embrace, but did not fit that before while giving us some of the templates they actually used.
