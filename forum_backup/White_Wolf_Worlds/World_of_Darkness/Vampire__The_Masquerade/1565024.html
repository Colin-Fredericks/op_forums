    Blood Brothers (a few musings)

    10-28-2026, 05:54 PM
    Blood Brothers are written as NPCs, and kinda inconsistently. V20 isn't really clear on how they.......... work, per se. We get a ritual for their creation, which is a bit over the top (the Diablerie is fine, whatever, but it goes on for months, and involves children being tortured in the worst possible way for a prolonged period, it's just silly really), and we're also told they have a hivemind, but also the hivemind is a level 3 power, and it isn't passive, it's activated. We're also told that within a given circle, the Generations of the Brothers will vary... despite us having every reason to think that the whole circle would have one generation, as they'd have the same creator? We also have very little insight into what their world is like to experience, which is very important if you're going to have BBs who aren't essentially lobotomised!

    Here are some rough positions I've found my way to.

    Creation of a Blood Brother Circle of Size (n)

    Requisite Skills:
    1) A Sabbat Priest capable of performing the Vaulderie Rite; this Priest should ideally be well-versed in various Ignoblis Ritae and have a strong grasp of the occult
    2) A Practitioner of Vicissitude 2+
    3) A Thaumaturge capable of performing the Ritual "Blood Brothers" (a Level 5 Ritual currently known only by Tremere Antitribu and their closest allies)
    4) A Practitioner of Dominate 4+

    Historically, 1) and 2) have been fulfilled by a single Tzimisce, and 3) and 4) by a single Tremere, but in theory the requisite skills could be divided between a larger group.

    Ingredients:
    1) a batch of (n) Kine
    2) a batch of (n) Cainites
    3) various Thaumaturgical instruments including a knife and vessel of varying specification, such as a silver dagger and a pool dug into a field of moist clay beneath the stars, or a rusty scalpel and IV bags, depending on ritual style
    4) Tzimisce Vitae

    The long and short of the rite is that the Cainites (usually captured Cammies or Anarchs, though this could serve as a means of execution) are bound and excoriated, while the Kine are stripped of their memories and individuality, being broken down into identical and obedient shells. These Kine are then embraced with the Vitae of the captured Cainites, and they proceed to consume their creators. This coordinated mass Diablerie is then immediately followed by an adapted form of Vaulderie, using the Tzimisce Vitae and the blood of the fresh embraces, before they can enter their "Becoming" and adopt the features of their Sires.

    If this is done correctly (and a Frenzy from one of the freshly-embraced Diablerists, or the would-be Sires escaping are real risks), the spiritual bond between the freshly created siblings stabilizes. They are all Blood Brothers (or Sisters) of a Pack, identical in appearance, fresh slates with semantic knowledge of the world but no episodic memories, but with slight differences. These differences are chiefly in Generation (depending on who their unlucky Sire was!) and some tiny quirks (inherited via Diablerie, especially from those with powerful personalities). That said there will also be natural gaps in Abilities (Talents, Skills and Knowledges). The Circle does, thankfully, have a single Humanity or Path Rating, and a single set of Virtues.

    What results is a powerful unit, shock troops, obedient guards... but they are not perfect. They must be carefully managed. If left to their own devices, a Blood Brother circle can learn eachother's tics (such as a desire to help the helpless, inherited from a high-Humanity Sire), and the results can be disastrous. This is why wise masters regularly apply Dominate to the circle and make liberal use of the Blood Bond. Blood Brother Player Characters (typically a single PC with the Retainer Background) are usually those whose master has died or has made huge errors in managing them, leading to a rebellion or escape. Alternatively it is possible for a Tzimisce or Tremere Master to intentionally elevate their servants, allowing them to develop a stronger personality and more independence... but suffice to say this is very unlikely.

    Blood Brother Hivemind

    The Frankensteins don't share a hivemind in the sense of ongoing Telepathy, though Sanguinus 3 does grant such communication. By default they have a strong instinct to sleep in the same space as eachother, and when doing so they go through a process which could be compared to various devices being synced through the cloud. While only one member of the Circle might have a memory of a Nosferatu being kind or cruel to it, the next night that memory will have 'synced' with the other members. In cases where Blood Brothers develop complex emotions and spend a lot of time thinking (typically any Frankenstein with an Intelligence of 2 or higher; most NPC circles will have Intelligence scores of 1 all around!) there may be a sort of 'desync'. A free-thinking Frankenstein with a strong urge towards an outsider may repress it, only for a member of his Circle to act on the impulse the next night! The horror of a 'divided self' can be explored in ways which deal with themes of mental illness, unconscious thoughts and urges, trauma and identity.

    Sanguinus 3 simply pushes the natural hivemind into overdrive; the 'sync' which normally only happens when the siblings are asleep is brought into their waking hours, allowing them to coordinate and share their Abilities.

    Making a Blood Brother PC

    - Discuss the idea carefully and ensure you and the ST are on the same page; such a PC would be better suited to a game where the PCs are experienced Neonates, Ancillae or Elders, rather than fresh Neonates with 0xp
    - Make a standard character per your table's character creation rules, choosing Blood Brother as their Clan/Bloodline
    - Use the Retainer Background to represent their siblings, who may be run by the player or by the ST depending on the ST's preference

    Blood Brothers as Retainers:

    - This application of the Retainers Background may be used by Tzimisce, Tremere Antitribu and Blood Brothers; others may use it at ST caveat
    - Different players may pool this Background (e.g. a Tzimisce and a Tremere who share ownership of a Circle, which may include a PC)
    - Every 2 dots in the Retainer Background represents a Blood Brother; this should be a starting PC with no XP

    For example, a Blood Brother PC might put 4 points into the Retainer Background to have two Brothers forming a Circle of three. Alternatively, the same Blood Brother PC might, after agreement between the players, pool Backgrounds with a Tzimisce or Tremere Antitribu PC to have a Retainers rating of 6, or 8, or even 10 or beyond, to form a larger Circle. The ST should always be the final arbiter on Circle Size for PCs. Blood Brothers are incredibly powerful warriors, and the larger the circle the more shenanigans will be possible in the long run when combined with other powers. In most games a Circle of three will be optimal, and should be all that is considered.

    Note that the relationship between the PCs is for the players and ST to negotiate. A Tzimisce who 'owns' a circle of Blood Brothers may have inherited it, and may have high ideals about how they can be proven to be true Cainites. Such a 'noble' outlook might be more conducive to an interesting and enjoyable RP experience than treating the Blood Brothers, including the Frankenstein PC, like a slave!

    Sanguinus at 6+ and Sanguinus Combination Disciplines

    Sanguinus 'Elder Powers' are a purely theoretical concept for the Tzimisce and Tremere who have created and studied Circles. The thickest blooded Blood Brother on record is of the 8th Generation... but if your pets were all 7th Generation, would you be quick to advertise it? As a result the effects are a matter of conjecture. Some have argued that Elder Sanguinus would simply improve on existing abilities, while others posit that entirely new powers would emerge, such as the ability to Embrace or to convert outsiders to join the Circle, 'subsuming' them entirely. One infamous scholar has claimed that all Blood Brothers should be destroyed before it is too late, as they will inevitably consume the whole Cainite Race, forming a single communion of blood and flesh.

    Combination Disciplines based on Sanguinus are more a matter of fact, but they are currently little-explored and poorly-understood. Most of the examples below are believed to be possible, but are in various stages of being proven or disproven.

    - Auspex 4 + Sanguinus 3: Allows outsiders to be added to the telepathic elements of the hivemind temporarily, allowing them to enjoy the benefits of sharing Initiative, perceptions and Abilities.

    - Potence 1 + Celerity 1 + Fortitude 1 + Sanguinus 1: The effect of "Brother's Blood" is expanded to allow a Blood Brother to spend blood to grant the 'blood buff' (or 'quickening') to a member of his Circle, within the limits of his own Generational per-turn limit, but not the recipient's.

    - Obfuscate 5 + Sanguinus 3: The Blood Brother may passively extend the benefits of his Obfuscate to all other members of the Circle, regardless of their physical distance; this is most often used to disguise the group with Mask of a 1000 Faces or permit a scattered infiltration.

    - Vicissitude 5 + Sanguinus 1: A Blood Brother permitted to master basic Vicissitude could pour himself (or crawl in bloody chunks, as with Body Impolitic) into the mouth of a fellow Blood Brother. Through some bizarre transference, he would then pour from the body of another member of the Circle, regardless of physical distance, much as blood might be spent by one Frankenstein to heal another 100s of miles away. If this were demonstrated the implications for the study of Blood Brothers, and indeed Vicissitude and the Cainite body, would be immense.

      I don't think it's so unclear.

      They don't have a hivemind and their write-up is clear enough at that. What they get without the Gestalt power is just the weakness of shared pain.

      Everything else is just personality. They have a tendency to like each other, want contact with each other and feel nervous when they notice a difference.

      And the last one is what makes the heavy lifting. Your take has the problem of forcing the Circle to act as a single extended character with unnecessarily complex and hard to track rules.

      V20 just sidestep this need entirely and creates more narrative space with that. Their similarity is a psychological drive and nothing else, and that means you:
      1. Don't need complex rules. They seek to be alike because they want to be alike;
      2. Circles don't need a consistent parameter of what must be uniform and what shouldn't;
      3. There's zero pressure to make their sheets similar. There's enough justification if you want to;
      4. If you want to make a Circle that breakes mold... You just do it. No need for changing rules.

      Personally I find it better. It's simpler and more versatile. It's already how several NPCs are run, as there is even an Archbishop that's a Blood Brother, he has a circle but isn't the circle. Same for the Blood Brother that's becoming one of the greatest researchers on them (and a living Haven for his circle). Or the leader of Las Hermanas Hambrientas.

      Fact is, they were never meant to BE identical. They're meant to LOOK identical, but that's mostly by deliberate effort. Heck, they don't even have to be same sex, a family can be turned into Blood Brothers.

      Generation can be different for whatever reason since we don't know how the ritual is, but otherwise it can change further with Diablerie since they have no property to share Diablerie effects.

      Ritual sacrifices are also consistently limited to the people that will become the Circle. This is already enough to make the Sabbat be careful as a Circle will require half a dozen disappearances and they want to keep it as low profile as possible.

          The Blood Brothers were introduced in the first Storyteller's Handbook (1992) along with the Gargoyles and Baali so they are an original component of the setting. But I always found them bizarre, and I don't know what was actually intended for them to be in the setting. Were they just intended to be a bizarre threat of the Sabbat? Or was something else intended? They weren't really developed. I think the next entry for them was as a proper PC splat write up in the Revised Guide to the Sabbat.

          However, based on their inclusion in the Storyteller's Handbook (and not the Player's Guide), and that while we got a Discipline write up, but not a full blown clan description, I do believe they were not intended as a PC, but strictly as NPC antagonists (as were the Gargoyles and Baali). So rather than look on them from the perspective as how to make them a good choice for PCs, I prefer to think of them as a tool for the ST only. Determine precisely what function the ST wants them to have in their chronicle setting, and then portray them accordingly.

                      twig I'm pretty sure I didn't fill many gaps. I'm not claiming there isn't some filling, or that my interpretation is objectively correct, but to say I extrapolated too much seems odd given the actual text.

                      What I did was to limit interpretation to a single denominator rather than maximize meaning for every word. Your take puts a lot of emphasis on what "sharing ... mind" means, even ignoring context to do so (Brother's Blood cites sharing flesh and blood, yet it's patently clear that there is no intention that they literally do so without a power).

                      You start from the intended apparent effect (they look like a hive mind of twins), assume its most complete form (they are a hive mind of twins), and ignores every piece of text that repeatedly state the effort behind this effect. And then proceed to add properties never before alluded to that reinforce the perceived goal rather than try to make what's actually there work.

                      First, I'll keep to V20, since the goal is function. The earlier inconsistencies mean no previous version is individually authoritative in a useful way.

                      So, what their write-up actually says?

                      1st Paragraph – the process of creation washes away individuality and personality, leaving them near mindless.

                      2nd Paragraph – the circle is a family connected by a kind of hivemind. Physical similarity is crafted.

                      3rd Paragraph – they excell in loyalty, they lack creativity. Creating a circle kills half a dozen people.

                      4th Paragraph – keeping their actual workings unknown to the Camarilla is a serious strategic decision. They specifically fear ritual reversion of mindlessness and loyalty.

                      Appearance section – they grow agitated when see each other as different.

                      Haven section – staying together is preference.

                      Character Creation section – no mention about similar attributes or abilities, Nature and Demeanor are usually the same.

                      Weakness section – pain is shared for 1 round, only. It's not a sustained shared sensation.

                      And what the Discipline says? That it's needed. Gestalt specifically says "Blood Brothers share a hive-mind ... the Gestalt power is that hive-mind".

                      So they're repeatedly referred as mindless and loyal.

                      They're repeatedly described as feeling a pull to stay together.

                      Whenever similarity is cited, it is a tendency or active effort, not a default.

                      Hive-mind is cited 3 times: one as approximation, one as stylistic exaggeration, one ostensibly as the specific Discipline power. The Weakness and personality fulfill the first and second.

                      The actual characters we have are indeed odd, but they only really violate the mindlessness thing, which is precisely the thing that makes them actually unsuitable for PCs.

                      Nowhere is said they share memories or even personality, outside the idea of being brainwashed before leaving the lab.

                          Here's what the first description of the Blood Brothers says in the original Storyteller's Handbook.

                          Sabbat Tremere and Tzimisces "recently" (as of 1992) gathered in Eastern Europe for an experiment. The Tremere used their blood magic while the Tzimisce used their shapechanging powers (their Koldunic sorcery had not yet been introduced).

                          Their plan was to refine the Blood Bond to create a pack of vampire warriors that would, for all practical purposes, think and act as one. That was the plan, not necessarily the result they achieved.

                          They captured Caitiffs as test subjects (presumably non-Sabbat Caitiffs) and after numerous failures, finally produced the first Blood Brothers. A pack of fanatically loyal soldiers with their own unique abilities related to their bond.

                          The Blood Brothers look strange and misshapen because of the use of the Tzimisce shapechanging portion of their creation ritual. However, that does not mean they have identical appearances even though Blood Brothers tend to shave their heads and dress similarly. Presumably the use of Vicissitude is a necessary component of their creation ritual, it cannot be done through Blood Magic alone.

                          Their creators, the Sabbat Tremere and Tzimisce, choose what people are to be subjected to the creation ritual. They tend to pick people without strong individuality and prefer criminal or violent backgrounds since they are intended to be shock troops.

                          Blood Brothers are "bound" in "circles" between three and seven people. These groups are socially tight, virtually communal, equal in status, but the most powerful generation is the nominal leader. By nominal leader, I believe that means the vampire their Sabbat masters give the instructions for their group to do since the "leader" doesn't really lead.

                          No Blood Brothers are more potent than eighth generation. It's not clear if this is a result of the ritual used by the Sabbat, if this is a self-imposed limit, or just what has happened so far.

                          While not directly stated, I assume that Blood Brothers don't actually embrace. Sabbat use captive Caitiff vampires (perhaps recently embraced through the creation rites, and select any resulting Caitiff), and through their creation ritual transform the various new vampires into a new batch of Blood Brothers. I also assume that the Sanguinus Discipline is limited to the Blood Brothers as a result of their creation rituals, and this Discipline is not possible for others to have who have not been turned into a Blood Brother. Presumably the process of creation resets the normal Caitiff rules on Disciplines and grants them their Blood Brothers Discipline spread. It might even "overwrite" any existing Disciplines they have. This is pure speculation on my part.

                          No hive-mind powers are described for the circle except through the Sanguinus Discipline (particularly levels 3 and 5) and their clan weakness which means all suffer the same Wound Penalties (for the next round only) whenever one gets hurt. However, victims are selected in part for not being strong individuals, and it's possible their creation ritual makes them more conformist and easily lead by their true leaders in the Sabbat. We're not given any example character stats, so it is not clear what this would mean in practice. Do they have lower than expected Willpower? Are their Natures more likely to be more submissive or conformist types? Do they have stronger than usual Vinculum to each other, or to their Sabbat creators? Everything is left to the ST's imagination.

                          As others have stated, canon is not consistent and subsequent writers did not always adhere to the specific details, just the general concept. So STs can interpret things widely. This will lead to different STs contradicting ideas of other STs even while both can claim they are keeping to canon.
