    The Free Clans

    This bit of flash fiction serves as a transition from the previous entirely in-character sections of the book and the follow chapters, which are a mix of game mechanics and in-character material. The section provides some insight into Anarch thinking, specifically they allegedly do not out the same emphasis on clan and lineage as does the Camarilla (and possibly the Sabbat, at least among the Lasombra and Tzimisce). As a bit of rhetoric, it makes sense and tracks with how the Camarilla is highly stratified and obsessed with status and position, and the Anarchs… are not as strongly obsessed with position and status based on things outside of their individual control (i.e. they care about what they actually do more than they do about the relative accident of clan or generation).

    It is a useful question to ask, how often do characters think in terms of clan and generation? They don’t think of things in terms of their dice pools, how many dots they have in something or the like. Those are things players tend to obsess over, so much so sometimes we can miss the forest for the trees. Clan is not terribly proscriptive. Take Starek – she is a highly atypical Ventrue. In this the Anarchs do provide more freedom.

        I got a bit ahead of myself when I said these sections are partly in and partly out of character. That is not true of all of these chapters.

        Brujah

        This section opens with a letter from Theo Bell to Damsel, the latter a character who first appeared in the original Bloodlines game and is noted for her disarming charm, her wit and ability to ingratiate herself to others.

        Bell writes about Brujah being chess masters and possessing the ability to manipulate people and move society. This is an interesting statement because it is not an idea often associated with the Brujah, but it is also not a wrong idea. The sense of wrongness might simply be in methods and goals, in a belief in how people are moved. Namely, the Ventrue manipulate things through boardrooms and banks. The Brujah manipulate with community organizing, unions, marches, protests and riots.

        Bell does make a distinction in the clan of the try-hards and the sell-outs. Bell is a try-hard. Balthazar is a sellout.

        Bell also mentions Damsel’s sire and I didn’t know that was an issue.

        There is small bit about the Brujah also enforcing the Masquerade and an awkwardly written section between Bell and Jack (probably Smiling Jack). This part feels like it had been written out of character and rewritten to be in character – the two all but use terms like Convictions and Touchstones.

        An interesting wrinkle here is the advice that Brujah, given their tendency towards violence in general and frenzy in particular, should surround themselves with people they do not like. In which case, they are less likely to hurt someone they care about if they snap. But in this case, are they agitating and supporting some group or cause they do not like or believe in? Further, what does that say about the coterie a Brujah joins?

                Gangrel

                This section opens with a bit of fluff where some gangrel chase down and eat a man and it doesn’t do anything for me. The following bit, a report by the Second Inquisition on the Gangrel, is a more interesting read.

                The third passage in this section reads like an out of character portion. It also, arguably, makes a minor recon of why the Gangrel left the Camarilla. Before it had been asserted that they left after their Justicar decided that the leadership of the Ivory Tower had been lying about the existence of the antediluvians. Here it is written “For centuries, the clan served as hunters and bodyguards, until the indignity grew too great. Now the clan is free of dictatorship, and claims the roads as their domain.” The new reason centers it more on the Gangrel getting sick of Camarilla shit in general, rather than making it about the Camarilla. This means the reason they left will have more resonance in an Anarch v Camarilla game.

                In the third passage, which has some connection with the S.I. report, discusses how Gangrel view their groupies.

                In the final passage Rudi discusses the concept and what it means to the Gangrel. It’s a good bit of rhetoric.

                    Right. Back to this… (This WIR is not the only way I squander the time on Earth God has given me)

                    Caitiff

                    This chapter discusses Caitiff in the vampire society of the unbound. And it is somewhat at odds with the flash fiction which moves the book into a discussion of the clans among the Anarchs. That bit of fluff expressly stated that Anarchs do not place much importance on bloodlines. Here it is expressly stated that even if that is true, that the Caitiff still have trouble fitting in with the Anarchs because of their utter lack of blood ties and easily identified vampire lineages.

                    It further states that “Toreador will gravitate to other Toreador, just as do the Gangrel with their own number. This kinship may not be one of friendship, so much as familiarity or Blood-borne impulse.”

                    In a meta-textual sense this is interesting because while that might be stated as something that happens in the world of vampires, most coeteries are groups of mixed clans (and mixed generations). Just because an RPG writer describes something as a challenge, or situation, in the setting doesn’t mean the players or Storytellers agree.

                    The chapter includes a bit of in-character fiction in the form of advice in an email, and a “Sins of the Caitiff” section which might be an interesting character nuance but isn’t a clan bane or compulsion either. So it can be ignored.

                                    Duskborn

                                    This section is about thin-blood, or vampires of generation 15+, and they are often not easily identified by clan. Many are in fact created by accident and they are not true vampires because they are - as Miracle Max might put it - only "mostly dead, which means partly alive." The oddness of the duskborn means they have no place in the Camarilla and, almost by default, find a place with the Anarchs. In that the Anarchs are everything that is vampiric but is not Camarilla.

                                    The section is a mix of in-character and out of character sections. One of the out of character sections is about "curing" the condition and returning the thing-blood to full humanity and life. It offers no standard route but provides a handful of possibilities, each one doubling as a plot hook. But a connection to humanity is a reoccurring theme to this section, even if they do not all entirely return to humanity. In one section a narrator talks about how because the thin-blood are semi-alive they do not have to fake morality and humanity the way full vampires fake it - for a thin-blood to talk to a human is not like a butcher talking to a cow. In another section a thin-blood has apparently fathered a child (and the couple are followed by a Tesla, which is an amusingly specific detail).

                                    Duskborn is a fun section to read and offers a lot of ideas for anyone wanting to play a thin-blood among the Anarchs.

                                        Traitor Clans

                                        This is a series of sections, each of which briefly explores the role of Anarch members of an otherwise nominally Camarilla Cla; Malkavians, Toreador, Tremere, Nosferatu and Ventrue. The nature of the clans remains the same if they are in the Ivory Tower or among the Rabble. That is, the Toreador are still artists, the Malkavians are still insane and the Nosferatu are still hogging up all the ugly. Perhaps the most interesting in the Ipsissimus Tremere and that is mostly because the Tremere went from a highly homogenous group to a highly heterogeneous group. A reoccurring theme is that for some elders members of these clans being among the Anarchs is unthinkable, even if it is a reality for many of the younger vampires.

                                        These section has a nice mix of in-character material, and reports from the Second Inquisition. There is nothing ground breaking, but it makes for an interesting read.

                                                Conclusion

                                                The Anarch book is interesting but it is also impossible to consider it on its own. It can only really be evaluated in comparison to the Camarilla book. In that sense, it is the weaker of the two books.

                                                Simply put, Anarch is too disorganized to provide as much use as the Camarilla book. The various parts of Camarilla society provide a lot of fodder for conflict, drama, bloodshed, hate, death, chaos, misery, murder, and so on. It’s all pretty evident. The same potentials are found among the Anarchs but it is less evident, and harder to exploit simply because there is no organization or definition to the Anarchs (as a group) except that they are not Camarilla. As a result the book and the group just becomes a great big pile of everything that is not Camarilla and it is difficult to sort out useful bits.

                                                A broad guide to how different factions of the Anarchs organize themselves would have been useful. The book did not really explore that – but it did get repetitive in terms of presenting us too many individual stories of people becoming Anarch vampires.

                                                So Anarchs as a book is a good source of general ideal and material for an Anarch campaign, but it is not as useful as the Camarilla book.

                                                            WIR Epilogue

                                                            The Anarch book did not have a singular failure in the same way the Camarilla book did in the form of its entry on The Abrek Blight. But even so over all the Anarchs book is a weaker product than the Camarilla book. The best metaphor I can come up with is if the Camarilla book is a properly ordered (with items separated) buffet or salad bar, then the Anarchs books is a buffet or salad bar with all the contents (still fresh and edible) poured out into a single pile. You can pick out the bits you want, but it quickly becomes tiresome.

                                                            One improvement would have been to make the entire book a singular document, similar to how the Camarilla book was a singular document. That book was compiled by Victoria Ash for a childre. The Anarch book could have been compiled by someone like Sterek doing an informal survey of all things Anarch.This would have given the book a more coherent voice and vision.

                                                            Another improvement would have been to suggest major trends in Anarch communities. For the same of simplicity I would take inspiration from the sects in Vampire the Requiem. So...
                                                            • Carthian Movement = Community and socialist Anarchs. They are often in, or from, formally community or socialist countries, such as Russia, East Germany, Cuba, etc.
                                                            • Circle of the Crone = Baharists, and House of Carna types who have gone full Anarch.
                                                            • Invictus = Camarilla by another name.
                                                            • Lancea et Sanctum = Religion Anarchs, Lasombra Anarchs, etc.
                                                            • Ordo Dracul = House Ipsissimus Tremere.

                                                            House Ipsissimus, and House Carna should be micro-sects and open to any clan or bloodline willing to pinky swear to protect the group.

                                                            Also, a step by step example of how the Camarilla tries to subvert an Anarch domain, and how the Anarchs try to overthrow Camarilla rule, would have been more useful than the Response Algorithm section.

                                                            The Anarch book is not bad, but it mediocre by comparison to the Camarilla book.
