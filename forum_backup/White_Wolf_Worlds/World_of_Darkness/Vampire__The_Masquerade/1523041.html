              It all depends on how you envision the vampires of your setting. I see many PCs go for having all five dots of a particular Discipline in most games, and in these cases the PCs are still neonates. So I don't see why there wouldn't be NPC equivalents for both neonates and ancillae.

              I don't consider the PCs to be typical of vampire characters in terms of how quickly they advance in power, but neither do I consider them to be unheard of prodigies. When designing the NPCs of the setting, I typically divide them into several categories. About 75% will advance slower than the PCs. This makes sure that the PCs have plenty of time to shine, and they can even catch up to some older vampires in power over the course of a chronicle. About 20% of NPCs advance at roughly the same pace. These are the characters that will either be rivals or important allies of the PCs in their own age group, and to explain some of the more powerful vampires older than them. About 5% of the NPCs will advance quicker than the PCs, making them powerful and well known NPCs in the setting for older vampires, and making them dangerous rivals for the PCs if the same age group. These numbers are not based on anything other than I, as the ST, think they are a helpful tool in creating an interesting setting for the players of various challenge levels.

              While I don't think the By Night books do a good job at designing NPCs (they tend to have strange builds and wildly out of place powers based on their age and description), I do think it can be interesting to compare what they did. Here is a breakdown of the original Chicago By Night with neonates and ancillae having at least one Discipline at 5 dots.

              Neonates:
              Hank Cave/Saint (Presence 5), Ramrod (Protean 5), Weltmann (Presence 6), Tammy (Potence 5), Sharon (Presence 5), DuSable (Auspex 5), Erictho (Auspex 5), Hinds (Presence 5), Capone (Presence 5), Brennon (Dominate 5)

              Ancillae:
              Balthazar/Sheriff (Dominate 5), Doyle (Animalism 6), O'Leary (Auspex 5), Elzbieta (Obfuscate 5), Lodin (Dominate 6), Ballard (Dominate 5), Yaryan (Animalism 5 & Obfuscate 6)

              So as you can see, the designers of the game did not think it odd that neonates and ancillae have 5 dots of any one Discipline, although we only have one example of an old ancillae having more than one Discipline at 5 ranks. But a lot of them have additional Disciplines at 4.

              I would personally reduuce some of the powers for some of these characters. Why would fairly unimportant characters that function more as background elements like Hank Cave, Sharon Payne, or Brennon Thornhill have such high Disciplines? Brennon was only supposed to have existed as a vampire for six years when CbN 13 was first published! But I don't object to interesting neonates that are supposed to be heavy hitters like Capone and DuSable have them.

              But I do think even neonates can have Disciplines at this level. At character creation, a person can spend enough Freebies to get one of their Disciplines to level 4, and then spend the XP to buy it up to 5 fairly early in a chronicle. Such ultra-specialization does have its drawbacks, but obviously it has a lot of attraction for some players.

                          I definitely think it's possible for an Ancillae to have maxed out a Discipline. Especially on the upper end of things, like 200+ years of age. Very feasible.

                          What I'd probably say is that the character would probably be well known among local Kindred as having been very active in their younger nights, if they aren't active now. The kind of Kindred who went on a lot of adventures to establish themselves, and in the process became very powerful very quickly. As others have stated, most Ancillae and Elders avoid taking risks. They find their niche in Kindred society, then spend years slowly accumulating influence.

                          The kind of Ancillae that topped off a Discipline is one who led an interesting unlife. Their place in Kindred society, and their relationships with other Kindred, should reflect that. They should have history, and maybe more than a few enemies (or at least rivals).

                          It also matters a great deal which Discipline the Ancillae has maxed out.

                          Something like the physical Disciplines would make them a martial threat (or, in the case of Fortitude, very tanky), but not greatly stand-out aside from this. It might imply they're a veteran of some major local conflict, like a Sabbat crusade, a violent Camarilla power struggle, or prolonged conflict with Lupines.

                          High Dominate implies the character is a bastard. You don't typically need higher than Dominate 3 for most Kindred matters. There's little morally defensible reason, if any, to be able to hollow out a person's will or hijack their body. A Dominate 5 Ancillae is likely a controlling, domineering personality, with few scruples.

                          Obfuscate 5 means they're not just able to come and go where they please, but able to plausibly remain hidden even against Kindred with Auspex 5. So unless they're an Elder with Elder levels of Auspex, most Kindred will be very careful what they say or do. Because the Ancillae in question could be around, difficult or impossible to perceive. Alternatively, local Kindred are at best vaguely aware of the Ancillae, and almost never think of them. Because the character is almost always skulking about unseen. Beneath the notice of most Kindred, as they rarely make themselves known. Implying they are harmless, or extremely dangerous.

                          Thaumaturgy 5, meanwhile, makes the Tremere in question very relevant, especially within the Pyramid. To achieve that level of blood magic mastery requires not merely loads of time and experience, but access to higher orders of occult knowledge. Implying they're either well connected/regarded within Clan Tremere, or they've acquired knowledge in some other fashion that makes them highly valuable. Valuable and suspicious. It's possible, especially in relatively new Clan holdings, that they're already the leader of a Chantry, or are being considered for same. This is a double-edged sword, as rival blood magicians (in and out of Clan & House Tremere) seek to undermine or steal from them, or hold a grudge at having been passed over by some "young" upstart. Alternatively, they may have been sent to the local area, to head up a Chantry, simply to get them out of the way. Let the obvious arcane prodigy carve out a new center of Tremere power, rather than disrupt existing Clan power structures.

                                                      The number of dots for Cainites has always seemed to me like something difficult to put down as it would depend so much on the individual Cainite.

                                                      But I would figure that the longer a Cainite has been, well, a Cainite then the less drive they have to do things and at 1000 years or above they have a serious problem with lethargy that is only broken by episodic flares of activity when something manages to stir them to action, before they sink back into lethargy again. That's been my favorite way to explain as to why ultra-old Cainites with godlike powers are not just being, well, godlike masters over everything and nothing can be done about it by any PC. And why the actions of neonates can have some impact rather than everything being run by elders who controls and foresees everything.

                                                      But to ge to the question, I don't at all find it strange that an Ancillae would have at least one of the in-Clan Disciplines at 5 dots and the other two pretty high unless that Cainite is really operating on a basis where they don't have much use for their in-Clan Disciplines.
