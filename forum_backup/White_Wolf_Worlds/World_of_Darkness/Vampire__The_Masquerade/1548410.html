    Running the Free States - 3 - Practical Governance

    02-11-2025, 07:23 PM
    I am still working out some practical details on how to run the Free States. Even after reviewing Los Angeles By Night, Anarch Cookbook, Clanbook: Brujah and other sourcebooks, not everything is clear. I must make a lot of things up, so hearing some feedback is good.

    So the basis of governance in the Free States is the Status Prefectus, which is more a statement of principals than any guide to governance. To sum up the major points:

    1 the Anarchs want to free themselves from any forces that rob them of their “Libertas”.

    2 They owe no allegiance to any creature or organization

    3 They will rule themselves with no ruler other than that they choose for themselves

    4 They recognize their responsibility to maintain, protect, and defend the Masquerade

    5 They declare their kinship and support of all Kindred still struggling for their own Libertas

    As long as any domain operates under the Status Perfectus, it is accepted as being part of the greater Anarch Free States themselves.

      There is also some practical information on how things actually work found in the sourcebook.

      Vampires stake out territories and forbid other vampires to hunt in them. So exclusive feeding territory is respected. These are the “baronies”. Gangs are defined by their exclusive feeding territory, not where they live. Vampires can dwell or visit any place they want, but in terms of feeding, they can only do so in their gang’s territory. The major exception is the so-called Barony of Angels which is controlled by Jeremy MacNeil and his buddies which is open to everyone (including other gangs).

      The Barony of Angels is not “ruled” by any gang, including MacNeil’s. He simply prevents anyone from claiming it. It is open hunting grounds unlike the other baronies, but Kindred are to be careful and clean up after themselves. Coteries are for companionship and self-protection only.

      The Barons and their gangs often destroy any Kindred who hunt in their area who are not given permission. The Crypt’s Sons only gives permission to members of their gang. Other “gangs” are more permissive and allow non-members allies and possibly even others to feed.

      Violence between coteries/gangs is normal. The way to protect oneself is to belong to a powerful coterie/gang that deters or avenges others. I take this to mean that Kindred can destroy other Kindred as they see fit. The only thing stopping them is the fear of initiating a feud, or that they may provoke other Kindred to ally and gang up against them. In other words, nobody is protected from destruction in the sense that the Sixth Tradition reserves this exclusively to the Prince.

      It is explicitly mentioned that any Kindred can embrace whenever they want.

      So if we compare this to the Six Traditions, both abide by the Masquerade. There is a limited form of domain in the sense that feeding territory is exclusive. The traditions of progeny, accounting, and destruction are thrown out. Hospitality isn't addressed. I imagine that while there is no obligation to announce yourself upon arrival to the Free States, or if you enter a barony, those "in charge" will probably make themselves known when convenient.

      The issue of blood bonds is not addressed in LABN. There are characters who are blood bonded in the sourcebook, but I think it’s supposed to be a secret. I cannot imagine the Free States would allow blood bonds at all. Although I do wonder if mutually agreed blood bonds between two Kindred in love would be tolerated. I am thinking blood bonding another vampire to you is probable justification for destruction by anyone who is willing to do it.

          The closest thing to a legislature are the Rants. Anarch Rants (open to everyone, not just Brujah) are held every other full moon. Often a hundred Kindred attend. Venues changed as they become larger, but currently they are held in a huge abandoned bus terminal just north of Hollywood Boulevard. It is now a converted nightclub called the Terminal.

          Any speaker can speak for however long they want, but are usually limited by the jeers of the audience.

          Speakers can call for votes on topics, but are no more binding than the speaker wishes them to be (I think it means he will enforce any decisions himself which means he must be important).

          If topics require individual action, then the speaker either does it himself or asks for volunteers to do so.

          Rants usually (peacefully) decide any dispute between groups provided a speaker brings up the matter and brings it to the group’s decision.

          Taken together, I believe they imply the following. 1) Rants can decide new "policy", but subsequent rants can easily revoke or change such policies. 2) Anything decided must be enforced on a personal basis. There is no government to do so. Policies adopted at a Rant can only be enforced if the speaker is willing to do it or themselves, or other Kindred step up to do it alongside with her or in place of her. This effectively means that anything decided which is not enforced probably lapses fairly quickly. The main purpose of speaking at a Rant is to see first hand how popular your proposed action is, and who among the powerful could be opposed to it.

          The Rants do seem to cover ALL the baronies. It isn't limited to MacNeil's Barony of Angels. The other gang leaders do show up and participate. It's not clear if other Free States outside the immediate LA urban area is also covered. It's mentioned that Tara in San Diego used to regularly attend, but doesn't anymore. There are also other "outlying" baronies like Fresno and San Jose. I imagine most of those baronies effectively govern themselves and work out any problems between themselves. Unless they have ambitions, there's no real reason to travel to LA and bring up your issues because in the end, you can only rely on yourself and your coterie anyway.

                  I would compare Rants more to a viking thing since they both lack police to enforce the law (you have to enforce anything by yourself and by those who support you) except the Rant is even weaker. It seems to have no judicial powers at all. At best you can see if the Kindred there are mostly sympathetic to you so you can justify the action you take.

                  It can probably serve as a forum for mediation and social pressure to compel two sides to come to an agreement, but only if the two sides aren't really willing to go to war.

                  I also see it as a forum for "alternative", more peaceful forms of competition when a decision is sought, but neither side wants violence and is willing to accept the "authority" of the rant. Similar to flyting, battle rap, or poetry slam. So it only really works if both sides are reasonable, relatively equal, and willing to accept a loss. One would be a fool to expect a more powerful Kindred to accept the loss in such a contest in a matter that is very important to her. But it could be an important way for Kindred to let off steam without things collapsing into violence.

                  More powerful Kindred likely appeal to the real leaders of the Free States (the most powerful gang leaders/barons) for mediation and negotiation directly rather than appeal to the crowd although this is even more informal and personal.

                  I expect for the powerful barons, the Rant's main importance is to build enough public support to justify violence that would ordinarily spark outrage, controversy, or other reaction that would otherwise allow for a backlash if not cloaked in support of the mob. Athenian democracy and mob rule at its worse.

                                              Go to the Canon Characters & Gangs thread I started, link in the first post of this thread and others.

                                              In addition to the general rules of the Free States, that each "gang" controls their own feeding territory de facto allows each of them to create their own mini-domain with their own rules. Since the Status Perfectus allows for Kindred to "rule themselves" and have a ruler "that they choose for themselves", I guess this is an out that each gang can justify as still following the status perfectus.

                                              I assume the larger and more powerful gangs have rules that members must follow, otherwise they get booted out of the gang and lose access to their feeding territory. Things like having an actual leader (no matter how determined), some type of internal hierarchy and command structure, and additional rules are all in place. They might even have rules that embracing new childer without authorization might get you kicked out. You can justify a lot by saying that "gang" membership is voluntary, and that anyone not accepted already has a home in MacNeil's Barony of Angels.

                                              The Crypt's Sons likely have strict rules since it is based on an existing gang structure. I imagine David Geduld's group also has some strict requirements. Even the anarchist La Hermandad probably has some internal rules because it comes out of a conspiratorial anarchist tradition, and secret societies tend to have things like sworn oaths and honor rules based on loyalty and commitment to the cause.

                                              I also intend to introduce some additional factions not found in LABN which likewise have their own membership requirements.

                                                      How should diablerie be treated in the Free States? Even among anarchs I think the vast majority of the Kindred of the Free States would be appalled by it. Nobody likes the idea of cannibalism. The Anarch Cookbook mentions that even most anarchs are unwilling to do it. It is a foul and perverted act.

                                                      While players often think of diablerie solely as the young and weak gaining power at the expense of the strong, it is important to remember Kindred cannibalism also works the other way. Ancient methusalehs devour their own childer, and there are stories of degenerate elders devouring their own childer or other weaker vampires. Drinking vitae of their fellow Kindred is extremely pleasurable to vampires, more than even drinking mortal blood. So even the weakest generation have reason to hate and fear it.

                                                      Yet we know that there are a significant number of anarchs who do it. Some of these are just the normal criminal sort who gravitate to the anarchs for safety in numbers and cover, but there are also other types. Diablerie is a way for the anarchs to gain power and weaken the power of the elders. It is also a tactic used to demoralize and spread fear among their enemies.

                                                      The Anarch Cookbook also mentions that some "mystically inclined anarchs" believe the world is a thought-construct of an ancient pre-Flood vampire (perhaps Caine), and that vitae is the only real connection to this true source of reality. By following that "connection" by improving one's generation through diablerie, the vampire can return to the "real" world. It's a religious excuse for diablerie, and supposedly is a popular rationale for some neonate anarchs who practice diablerie. It sounds very Malkavian to me. However, the Free States is exactly the kind of place that this kind of argument could be openly discussed and find adherents, and once a certain critical mass is achieved it would spread to anarchs in other domains.

                                                      I also like the idea in Blood Hearts - Diablerie: Britain that some anarch diablerists find Das Buch vom Grabkrieg (actually Dr Mortius's translation of that book) fascinating because it gives an insight into the methusalehs, and that's important if they are to go hunting for them for the purposes of diablerie. (It also foretells the coming of the anarchs so it is popular for that as well.) That the book itself is restricted and most likely only known through barely legible photocopies of photocopies of translations (at least in the early nineties, things would be different now) makes it an excellent "treasure" for the PCs to stumble upon in the Free States.

                                                      So I imagine there must be at least one open group of self-admitted diablerists, and probably even more secret ones. There are no laws against diablerie, but it remains unpopular. Self-admitted diablerists are probably fairly powerful and feared by many, but they run the risk of becoming hated and destroyed if the opponents of diablerie are provoked through a combination of anger and opportunity.

                                                      I just like the idea of the PCs running into open diablerists who justify the practice and argue there is no shame to it. I want to include both the "good" side of the Free States and the "bad" side.

                                                      I'm thinking of at least two coteries who are open about it. The first presents the more "practical" side of diablerie - it's a good weapon to weaken their enemy. The second talks about it more mystically. There's several bad photocopies of Dr Mortius's translations, and there might be wars over someone trying to produce a more legible copy by getting all the copies and replacing illegible pages with more legible pages shown in someone else's copies. There is even one group (maybe of secret diablerists) planning a big raid on a methusaleh like shown in the Diablerie adventure books. (I am not interested in running such an adventure myself, but I could make reference to some coterie planning to go down to the Yucatan and then never heard from again.)
