                                                        Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                                                        My takeaway from all that was that the Antediluvians aren't nearly as powerful, intelligent, dangerous or undefeatable as the hype would lead people to believe.
                                                        That's actually how I personally run the Antes, and I think some of the blunders make for better story elements than the whole god-like powers and shenanigans.

                                                        Looking at them, there are a number of ways in which an Ante can affect a story:

                                                        1 - Be the focus of cult/religion/legend;
                                                        2 - Influence events and people as ultimate string-pullers;
                                                        3 - Leave behind the aftereffects of their shenanigans;
                                                        4 - Actually show up, but not to put up a fight;
                                                        5 - Final Boss Mode.

                                                        None of those is superior or inferior, they all can be done well or badly, so their ideal power depends on what you want to take of it.

                                                        Here's the thing: most people like to work with #1 and #2, and so make their legend as true as possible. But that's completely unnecessary. Here's some considerations:

                                                        1 - The truth of the matter seems important because of the characters that so strongly believe in it, but it actually isn't. What people do with their beliefs is far more important than those beliefs being right, and being in the wrong here is not a sign of being dumb, as the characters live inside the setting, without the outside perspective there's no reason for them to know the truth;

                                                        2 - Personal power is irrelevant as what matters is the thread of manipulation. In this regard I actually find the notion of god-like powers over their progeny more of a bug than a feature. This sort of Super-Dominate leaves no meaningful threads to be explored and no way to interact with their plot, as anything you do is either a part of it or unable to affect it and ultimately irrelevant. The notion that god-like ancestors may be causing everyone's thoughts and actions is interesting and terrifying, but while it seems to be #2, ultimately is actually #1;

                                                        3 - Ancient vampires do not need god-like powers to leave behind god-like marks. Any number of factors may affect a given context to make the actions of someone there have huge proportions. They could have taken their time, used a powerful artifact, used combined power of many others, just because they left what appears to be the ruins of an impossibly big fortress of pure obsidian that legend says the Ancient built alone in a single night, it doesn't mean any of those is individually true;

                                                        4 - Here the more alien or powerful the character, the less options you have to actually build an interesting interaction. A protean, ninety stories high monstrosity that knows in details each animal on the planet due to its mastery of Vicissitude and is about to choose and eat everyone without hope of resistance is terrifying. But is also just as meaningful as a big enough meteor, that is, a falling rock; Meaning exists within boundaries, so your Ancient should be weak enough to not threaten your story's boundaries.

                                                        5 - You only have a meaningful benefit from a too big power level when you want the characters to put up a fight and be completely overpowered, or to highlight how much power they accrued in their bid to try and face gods. If you don't want any of those two specific things in your story, though, then by necessity it must be weak enough to work for the fight you actually intend to.

                                                        Caveat: Cosmic Horror is a fun genre, but a specific one. But even in it, while the characters face evidence that the power level of the Unspeakable Things is indeed too high, the actual story works mostly with #1, #2 or #3. Few of them use #4 and they don't have a lot of meaningful interaction (Call of Cthulhu), or they use #4 but the entity is limited, if not in actual power, in its willingness to exhibit that power, making it effectively less powerful in the actual context of the story (almost anything with Nyarlathotep).
