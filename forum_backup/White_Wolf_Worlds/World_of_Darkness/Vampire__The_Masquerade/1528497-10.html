    Originally posted by DarkKing View Post
    As much as I'm loath to say it, openly admitting the 5th edition was a reboot would have been far more preferable to them making it a reboot, but pretending it wasn't.

    Seriously, there are so many changes introduced in V5, that the game is practically unrecognizable now.

    Starting things over from scratch would have been the better route.
    O disagree because I think that was not the real problem.

    The problem with the changes made wasn't that they were many, it's that they were erratic, incomplete and incoherent. They threw a lot of new ideas, but didn't commit to any.

    Second Inquisition, Hecata, Tremere Bombing, Beckoning... Everything happened but made no difference. They were shallow rather than open, instead of giving options to explore they kind of said it didn't matter.

    A number of changes are just there. Mexico City and Montreal switching from Sabbat to Camarilla isn't a bad thing in itself, regardless of how polemic it would be regardless. But the way it was done? One day the SI came, killed everyone, then left and the Cam took as if there was no risk in a city that just got wiped out of vampires, and somehow it really is, and in 5 years it's as if they had been there for at least 50? What the fuck?

    Problems like those come from lack of vision for the larger product and that would bite then no matter what they tried.

    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    Mismanagement is such a broad term its kinda meaningless. It's kinda like saying, "Well, the biggest reason we didn't win the sports game is they scored more than we did."
    Overall agree, but I think in this case it applies because of the decision hierarchy level where the problems were worse, which we can see by patterns followed consistently.

    Paradox spent 10 years trying to grow outside of Grand Strategy games, but they committed a fundamental error over and over: assuming they had all the know-how they needed, when they didn't actually understood their own success at the corporate level.

    Every project, every move, every PR statement and analysis carried the indelible mark of the upper management assuming that success in one thing means competence by default, and that this is replicable across the board.

    There was one thing, one that any company in a similar position would have done if correctly managed: hire business experts in a new field. That's what give us a chance, not a certainty but a chance, that V6 will turn out better.

    And I say that mismanagement fits because it's a cascading effect. Lack of know-how was the source, but the manifestation of the problem was a series of bad decisions all over the place, and as generic as it is, mismanagement ends up being the best way to describe the common factor.

        The TTRPG space is a very hard place to hire specifically "business" experts. WotC is basically the only organization in the industry where business expertise can really be separately from some level of creative expertise. If the problem was not bringing in experts for their projects, then it doesn't seem like they failed to do so even if they made hires that didn't fix the problems just because of having expertise.

                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                Saying Paradox didn't hire experts feels a little off when, well, they did, and then they did again, and then again. If the problem was not bringing in experts for their projects, then it doesn't seem like they failed to do so even if they made hires that didn't fix the problems just because of having expertise.
                My impression was that they hired creatives more than business experts. A business needs to do market analysis, marketing strategy, estimate ROI, define logistics.

                A big part of Onyx Path success was figuring out production lines and cost assessment. It's not glamorous, but as incredible as their art is, it would have sunk without that. And that's the areas Paradox seemed, for all I could see, to sit in their own laurels when they should look for a grounded view on how things worked and how to adapt strategies and temper expectations.

                You can hire a creative first with experience in the back office part, but that's not what I saw, although I may have missed something. I saw them hiring creatives with experience in the hands on part, maybe public reach, but that's about that. The obvious, sure, but not where their weaknesses were.

                This isn't about TTRPGs, either, but everything they tried for those years outside their niche.

                Originally posted by DarkKing View Post
                From what I understand, White Wolf seems to have a recurring problem with this kind of thing. V5 was far from being the first book in their franchise where the creative team lacked communication with each other and just slapped together their own ideas.
                Not exactly. Or rather, not to this level.

                The main problem here is the non commitment to decisions, sometimes in the same book. The core of V5 has both an almost invincible SI and a Camarilla that's already running business as usual except when not.

                2nd Ed had a lot of stuff thrown almost at random, but within each book the matter was taken to its consequence and frequently enough later books would assume previous ones unless retcon was the point.

                Revised had a far tighter grasp on the goal within each line, even if treating the lines as more independent than before. Take a game line and the timeline through the books is mostly coherent, give or take a few hiccups.

                V5 tried to do everything without compromise. The things are there, but not the consequences or developments. In the very books were they happen they're usually stripped of much further effects. Big changes all over the place yet the society of the Kindred is mostly the same.

                It's less the lack of a single theme linking every book and more the lack of wanting the events to actually change something. That's what makes the impression you have: things change as time goes on, but you don't see the setting react as you would expect, so it doesn't feel the same setting.

                What would happen in Legacy if the Beckoning started? What would happen if Vienna got hit by missiles? What would happen if suddenly all tech hid surveillance for a new Inquisition? Or what would happen if all those high ranking government officials all over the world had this knowledge about vampires?

                All of those could happen in Legacy, but the result wouldn't be V5.
