    (Setting Riff) Vampire: The New Millennium

    08-11-2023, 03:40 PM
    So, the idea for this thread came about while discussing the myriad unfortunate setting changes in W5, mainly the ones regarding the Get of Fenris. For context, here is the post of mine that inspired me to make this thread...

    Originally posted by Matt Corleone View Post

    For sure. The Revised corebook was the first version of Masquerade I ever played, right before V20 came out. If you stick to just the corebook, it's perfect without being as bulky as V20.

    Most of the really bad metaplot stuff and Achilli's smarmy attitude came about in the supplements. Literally the only metaplot changes I remember from the Revised corebook were the different Assamite clan weakness and Malkavians getting Dementation by default.

    Otherwise, it's the OG setting. Gangrel are still listed as a Camarilla clan, the Ravnos are still a major clan and you even get a nice array of NPC's from the other denizens of the World of Darkness emulated via Disciplines while 1e and 2e only had very brief writeups for Lupines and Magi while ghosts and faeries got mentioned but no real stats.

    I do kinda miss the wider variety of human antagonist write-ups in the 1e and 2e cores. IIRC, in Revised you've got an Inquisitor, a Government Agent, and I think there might've been a mafia hitman too.

    Like, if I was going to do a Masquerade game, I'd set it in the late 90's/early 2000's and use the mysterious and brooding setting of 1e and early 2e but the rules of Revised. V20 works well too but if I'm gonna go for the real deal old-school feel, I'll use Revised rules.

    Maybe call that setting riff "Vampire: The New Millennium" or something? It's a slightly tweaked variant of the vanilla Masquerade setting where that late 90's/early 2000's attitude is played up to an over-the-top degree.

    From the release of the 1e corebook until Under A Blood Red Moon, the Vampire metaplot was nonexistent, and from Under A Blood Red Moon to the Revised corebook, the metaplot was pretty much confined to the Chicago Metropolitan Area. Hell, Werewolf and Mage probably had more metaplot in the first two editions than Vampire did and most of that concerned Samuel Haight.

    It wasn't until Guide to the Camarilla, the Clan Novels, and Time of Thin Blood that the metaplot really begins to go out of control for VTM.
    ​So, yeah. This can best be described as a setting riff of Vampire that is "The rules of Third Edition with the setting and style of First Edition" and yeah, you can do that with V20 too since that's mechanically close enough to the Revised rules.

    But when I say First Edition themes and settings, I mean the ACTUAL themes, settings, and moods of the First Edition (and very early Second Edition) books. Not the mythologized version of 1e that the V5 crowd likes to talk about.

    First Edition was cool. It was very much Trenchcoats, Katanas, pulpy "D&D With Fangs" Urban Fantasy, and a lot of BadWrongFun. This was the setting of magic items, Al Capone and Helen of Troy as vampires, Louis Pasteur inventing a cure for vampirism, guys LARP'ing as the Terminator, vampire rock bands, inquisitors with high levels of True Faith behind every corner, Magi who hunted vampires and consorted with elves, a modern London that was still paved with cobblestone streets and always cloaked in fog as if it were still the Victorian era, gargoyles on skyscrapers, Mesoamerican pyramids that functioned more like the Tomb of Horrors than any actual Mayan temple or palace, and the like.

    Yeah, it was mysterious (due to no metaplot) and very street-level, but it wasn't street level in the same way 5th Edition was.

    Yeah, there was lip service paid to Personal Horror, but good guy Kindred did exist and the Curse of Caine could be defeated via Golconda and then Rebirth, though it was extremely difficult. Plenty of figures from history and folklore were running around as Kindred. But keep in mind that in 1e, personal horror and "Trenchcoats & Katana badwrongfun" weren't seen as mutually exclusive and diametrically opposed like they are now.

    This whole idea of "True Art Personal Horror vs. D&D With Fangs badwrongfun" and the narratives around it all seem to be a more recent thing in the WoD fandom than many people realize.

    From what I can gather, it was largely spurred on by Justin Achilli and those who followed after him.

    Yeah, Justin Achilli probably wasn't the first one who took the early marketing pitches for VTM 1e at face value a bit too seriously and he certainly wasn't the last, but I think he was the most influential of those who did and he helped popularize a false dichotomy that haunts both WoD devs and fans alike to this very day. A false dichotomy that reached its apotheosis with V5.


    But what if we took the base Revised corebook and the little Storytellers Handbook that came with the ST Screen for it and then removed any metaplot stuff from Revised like the Clan novels and any of the major supplements after the corebook, but did include some tweaked 1e and very early 2e stuff instead?

    And we made it an explicit period piece and a love letter to the "Y2K Era" (1997-2004) on top of that?

    I know what you're thinking right now.

    "But the original game was set in the 90's! And Revised covers that exact era in question!"

    And you're right. But keep in mind, that was back when those years weren't so much "The Late 90's and Early 2000's" but just "The Present Day". There's a difference between a work that was set in 1999 because it came out that year, and a work that is explicitly a period piece set in 1999.

    So, yeah, we're taking VTM and playing up the Y2K period piece to its fullest extent. With all the campiness and edgy attitude that implies. But there's gonna be a lot more tweaks to this setting other than using the themes of 1e and chucking out the metaplot.

    First off, you're not going to find Garou, Awakened, Wraiths, or Kithain per se in this setting riff. But you will have Lupines, Magi, Ghosts, and Faeries. Their powers will be emulated with Disciplines, of course, and there's gonna be some other changes to the setting as well. But that's gonna be a whole other post worth of discussion.

    I'm just a trans guy and gaming fan, unabashed hedonist and unusually upbeat nihilist, fairly laid back. He/Him.

    If everything is political, then nothing is!

      Okay, here's a rough outline for the wider setting of "Vampire: The New Millennium"...

      Aesthetically, it's a lot less "Gothic-Punk" and a lot more "Y2K Aesthetics meets Hot Topic Mall Goth" (and there's nothing wrong with that, tbh I prefer the Hot Topic nu-metal mall goth vibe to the real Goth thing anyway)

      Timeframe wise, it's ideally 1997 to 2002 (the era of the "Y2K Aesthetic" overall) but you could extend it out to a general thirteen year period of Autumn 1995 to Autumn 2008, roughly corresponding with the PS1's release in North America on one end and the Great Recession and the election of Barack Obama on the other end.

      However, this version is a very stylized, fictionalized, and romanticized version of the late 90's and early 2000's as opposed to how the period actually was in the real world.

      At its most subtle, this is set in an idealized nostalgic version of the Clinton and Bush years while at its most over the top, it's the garish "Theme Park Version" of said era.

      Now, as for the other denizens of the World of Darkness, here's what we know.

      1. Lupines, Magi, Ghosts, and Faeries exist but they're a lot closer to the little tidbits we got in Vampire 1e overall as opposed to what they were like when they got their own gamelines. I might use stuff like The Hunters Hunted, the depictions of Lupines in Awakening: Diablerie Mexico and Milwaukee By Night, the Antagonist write-ups from the 1e and 2e corebooks, and maybe fan materials like The Darkness Owns: The Lupines or John Biles's Faerie fan game from 1993 as guides as well.

      2. The Order of Hermes is a thing but it's more of a Mage equivalent to the Camarilla than anything else. The 2e Ars Magica material may or may not be a historical prequel to this "New Millennium" version of WoD.

      3. Kuei-Jin do not exist. The Eastern Courts from KOTE exist, but they're regional Cainite sects instead. With that said, The Shih and Strike Force Zero exist pretty much as written.

      4. Imbued do exist but there is no Sixth Great Maelstrom. Imbued are a rare phenomenon but they've started to become more and more common within the last ten to fifteen years. Could this be a sign of a coming Gehenna event? Or is it due to new technology making the Masquerade more difficult to maintain? Zombies are becoming a more common sight as well for reasons unknown.

      5. Demons...Okay, I was tempted to write off Demon: The Fallen completely due to the uncomfortable ways that it deals with real-life religions, both past and present. But if I just stick to the base core book and omit stuff like the Earthbound book or Days of Fire, I might be able to work something out via homebrewing.

      In this setting variant, Demons do exist and Fallen are a thing but they are very rare. But much like the Imbued, they are becoming more common for reasons unknown. The backstory of Demon The Fallen regarding Lucifer and the fall is a lot less "the objective truth" and more like the narrative that demons tell their thralls. Lucifer is a pure evil villain through and through in this one. The Earthbound book is just pure non-canon as well.

      6. Senshi: The Merchandising is canon and is not treated as a parody. I might have to do some tweaks to get around the tidbits of Mage's backstory that Senshi relies on and maybe use more of Reckoning instead. The Messengers have been imbuing more and more humans. But not only that, the Messengers are also picking various kids and teens to become Senshi. Due to anime and manga experiencing a major boom period in the late 90's and early 2000's, these Senshi take up the mantle of their favorite anime heroes. These kids now quite literally have the power of God and Anime on their side as they fight the forces of Darkness.

      Why am I including Senshi of all things in this setting riff? Well, I just like Senshi and I grew up on Sailor Moon, Digimon, Monster Rancher, Cardcaptors, and DBZ as a kid, okay?

      I'm just a trans guy and gaming fan, unabashed hedonist and unusually upbeat nihilist, fairly laid back. He/Him.

                  There is one thing I've sometimes wondered about posting but never had, which I think is appropriate to mention here. And that is determining a more accurate clan description for the Giovanni/Cappadocians for the 1e/2e era that includes all the stuff that the players now know, but which should not really have been all that unknown at the time when the Giovanni first introduced.

                  The idea that Giovanni had eliminated all, or almost all of the Cappadocians, in retrospect is not really very convincing. Known survivors are probably ubiquitous even if not very common. There are probably a fair amount of known elder Giovanni in the domains of the Camarilla and many likely have younger childer. Even if one assumes that as clan members they are somehow dependent on clan rules now controlled by the Giovanni family and these internal matters are not regulated by the Camarilla's Inner Circle, nevertheless individual Princes still have their domain and control over destruction and progeny. The lineage of Augustus just can't willy nilly walk into a city and start killing them.

                  Plus the various offshoots - especially now that Necromancy Paths have absorbed a lot of the unique bloodline Disciplines - like the Samedi, are likely known to be some kind of offshoot of the Cappadocians, as opposed to a false mystery where people just don't know what they are.

                  The mystery of the Giovanni is just played out, and I don't think was ever really done well in setting for the characters (as opposed to players at the time trying to figure things out as an OOC mystery). So I think any reimagining of the early setting needs to determine a good "clan description" that more accurately assesses the situation which actually existed in the setting. It's the Hecata, but without the Nagaraja and with Augustus still in charge and his line trying to control the clan.

                          Apologies for the double-post on this one, but here's another update for the setting. But first, some context...

                          So, I was lurking an archived version of BJ Zanzibar's site last night and came across a fan game by the name of Illuminati: The Conspiracy, which basically had an elite mortal group that was opposed to the supernatural and was the ones that really pulled the strings. Now, I'm not going to include it in Vampire: The New Millennium, but I am going to take some inspiration from it.

                          While this setting has Lupines and Magi, it doesn't necessarily have Garou and Awakened per se. And as a result, there probably isn't a Technocratic Union, at least not like the one we got in the original canon. So I'm going to go with a different group...

                          The Philosophers

                          Or as they are known in the Western World circa the late 90's and early 2000's, The Patriots.

                          Yeah, I went there...

                          But this group is a little different than The Philosophers from Metal Gear's canon, though elements of MGS3 are used for its backstory. It's far older, going back to the time of King Arthur. Arthur was a moralistic feudal tyrant but one who ensured his legacy would be falsely seen as a heroic one. And now they want to return the world back to medieval feudalism, albeit with a modern techno-urban veneer. But they play the long game.

                          The one-two-three punch of the Enlightenment, the American and French Revolutions, and the entire Industrial Revolution put the early Philosophers in a bind and they had to adapt with the times and won't let themselves get caught off-guard. They're not going to openly champion feudalism or aristocracy but instead work behind the scenes and within the modern world of democracy and capitalism.

                          These are wealthy corporate leaders, high-ranking bureaucrats, and elite members of government agencies, the top one-tenth of the top one percent. The Philosophers are fully aware of the Camarilla, the Sabbat, the Order of Hermes, the True Black Hand, the Lupines, ghosts, faeries, and the like. The Camarilla, the Order of Hermes, the Inconnu, and the True Black Hand also know of The Patriots and rightfully fear them. There is a tense sense of unease and The Philosophers often manipulate supernatural groups to their own ends. While largely comprised of mortals, there are a handful of supernatural beings within their ranks. In fact, a Magus who belonged to the now defunct branch of the Russian Philosophers

                          The Philosophers are outwardly secular but their philosophy is essentially Crypto-Hyper-Calvinist in nature. They engineered both the Great War and the Russian Revolution, but the utter horrors and sheer atrocities of both World Wars had fractured them.

                          World War I was their doing as they promoted the Black Hand and placed Konrad Von Hotzendorf in the perfect position to warmonger and convinced Kaiser Wilhelm II to write the infamous "blank check" to back Austria-Hungary. The Philosophers would regret this deeply.

                          World War II was completely out of their control and happened pretty much as the IRL textbooks present it. Even the Philosophers were horrified by the countless crimes against humanity and they tried to ensure it would never happen again after its end. Unfortunately, the Philosophers were shattered by the aftermath of both World Wars and a new conflict arose within their ranks.

                          After the end of World War II, the world was split in two. East and West.
                          This marked the beginning of the era known as the Cold War..


                          The Philosophers were founded in Britain and had massive sway in the United States, the United Kingdom (and by extension, the British Empire), France, and then expanded into Russia and China after the Great War. But the Soviet and Chinese Philosophers had fully split with the Western Philosophers after World War II. A once grand and fully unified conspiracy was now split three ways. But there was a problem...

                          All three factions were trying to claim The Philosophers Legacy, a massive multi-trillion dollar treasure raised in the interwar period. The methods used to amass this fortune caused the Great Depression in 1929 and also was used to help fund the Allied war effort in WWII, up to and including the Manhattan Project. However, even after the war, it was still worth trillions and all three factions wanted full access to it.

                          In 1964, an incident happened, a bit of Cold War intrigue unknown to most people but one that would have great repercussions across history.

                          A Soviet Philosopher and a powerful renegade Mage by the name of Yegevny Borisovitch Volgin made a bold ploy to try and nab the entirety of The Philosophers' Legacy and through a series of very convoluted events, he was defeated in a top secret paramilitary operation known as Operation Snake Eater.

                          The Cold War ended with the Soviet Philosophers in shambles and essentially defunct while the Chinese branch of the group is active but wracked with their own infighting following the reforms of Deng Xiaoping in the late 1970's and early 1980's. Only the Western Philosophers remain strong in 2000.

                          After acquiring the entirety of The Philosophers Legacy, the Western Philosophers rebranded under a new name, The Patriots.

                          This name change was a way to invoke the spirit of The American Dream, of revolution and of liberal democracy, to hide their true neo-feudal intentions in the long term.

                          The Patriots have several subordinate organizations under their thumb, including The Arcanum (who are used as a de facto intelligence agency for them), The Knights of Saint George, the FBI's own Special Affairs Department, Pentex (the world's largest asset management firm. They're basically BlackRock combined with Vanguard and all jacked up on steroids and PCP), various and sundry plants within the NSA and other government agencies, and more.

                          The Patriots have a tenuous alliance with the other independent hunter groups such as the Society of Leopold, the Shih, Strike Force Zero, the Orphic Circle, and the like. They use these erstwhile allies along with their own assets to corral the supernatural every once in a while and remind them why humanity isn't something to be trivialized.

                          With that said, things are becoming uneasy within the ranks of The Patriots. The Messengers are becoming more and more active. Imbued are becoming more common than ever (though still rare for now) and as more Imbued and Senshi begin to pop up, The Patriots grow more paranoid and confused.

                              There's nothing wrong with campy pulp-inspired fun, but let me make the case for the understated intelligence of early-edition VtM. Yes, the obnoxiously arrogant, superficially political, progressive secularist morality play "theme" belched itself forth into the game in later editions and consumed it, however early editions had things to say and said them well.

                              The Y2K phenomena, frankly...wasn't really a thing. It was over and done with almost as soon as it hit the public consciousness. Most of the clamor, was mass media sensationalist fearmongering on slow news days. Millennialism in general...yeah, a bigger and more serious phenomenon, but it was far less widespread than it's remembered to be. Some terror attacks, lot of goofball doomsday preppers, way more sensationalist media coverage than appropriate. If you want a "meme game"...yeah, that's the way to do it, but you're not really capturing the spirit of the '90s that way.

                              So...what was really going on? History's cyclical, and the '90s were very much a repeat of the '50s. This is going to be long and appear tangential...but trust me, where I'm going, it's necessary roadwork.

                              Both were "best of times/worst of times" periods; the post-WWII economic boom fueled by the GI Bill and Marshall Plan led to an explosion of heavy industry, manufacturing, and export, while suburbanization and white flight were in full swing. The interstate highway system foundationally changed national commerce and logistics, the proliferation of personal automobiles changed how we traveled and lived, and the proliferation of radio and broadcast television changed how we communicated and shared news. The nuclear family became the definitive vision of the American dream, replete with its ticky-tacky ranch houses, monster gas guzzler family cars, the summer vacation for leisure instead of work, and any able-bodied American could walk into a factory with their hat in their hands and walk out with a good paycheck.

                              As long as you were northern, white, a man, straight or at least heavily closeted, Christian, and at least somewhat conservative. Otherwise, you were still waiting for rural electrification, suffering under Jim Crow, ostracized and blacklisted for suspected Communist sympathies, and/or still getting paid barely subsistence wages for some of the hardest, least safe, and thankless work the country had to offer.

                              Then black folks got sick and tired of being treated like disposable second-class citizens. And everything went to shit. Never happened before, we learned our lesson and fixed our shit, won't happen again.

                              Fast forward forty years. Reaganomics was in full swing, greed was good, investment bankers and stockbrokers were rock stars, the fall of the Iron Curtain and NAFTA gave the US entirely new untapped markets for consumer goods and agricultural products export, and the unipolar moment gave us the biggest, hardest, dick in the entire fucking world. The internet shifted the mass communication paradigm and the affordable home PC was its vehicle, cable television shifted the mass media paradigm and the telecommunications act was its vehicle.

                              If you had a comp sci, business, or finance degree and a good sales pitch, you could become a multi-millionaire practically overnight with whatever stupidass internet service or e-commerce platform you could imagine. Or you just stole the idea the person with the comp sci, business, or finance degree had, and pass it off as your own with your sales pitch. You filled up your fuel-injected K-car, minivan, or truck at an entire fucking dollar a gallon (damned government banning leaded gasoline) and drove past the mom-and-pop stores you used to go to, to save big at Wal-Mart. You turned off local broadcast television because it's nothing but reruns, feel-good crap, and sensationalist fearmongering, to watch Turner Broadcasting's channels which were largely nothing but reruns, feel-good crap, and sensationalist fearmongering.

                              Urban cores were places you didn't go, unless you wanted to get carjacked, get shot, or buy crack.

                              You were more concerned about whether the President got a blowjob, than whether he was bombing the dogshit out of Iraq for no good reason while some of the most inhumane economic sanctions ever to have been passed ravaged its people, while African dictators and terror groups were given free reign to commit ethnic cleansing. Rodney King was probably the first black man you ever saw get his ass beat by a pack of racist cops (I mean, that got fixed back in the '60s right?), and it was a national outrage OJ got acquitted (he was clearly faking the glove not fitting, it clearly shrunk after being left out in the rain, and the allegations about racism and neo-Nazi sympathies among the LAPD were clearly defamatory).

                              As long as you were a coastal, liberal, private managerial class, almost certainly but not exclusively white, almost certainly but not exclusively a man, boomer or generation joneser. Otherwise, you probably didn't have cable and almost certainly didn't have the internet (even if you could afford a PC), got your union busted, got your manufacturing job outsourced and had to go into poverty-wage service industry work, lost your decent and affordable health insurance for shitty HMO's and bankruptcy-inducing deductibles, saw your nest egg dwindle to nothing thanks to the depredations of an unregulated financial sector, watched as public services got privatized and cut back thanks to budget deficits, and could barely get a decent education and bank on it for gainful employment because college costs and debts started to skyrocket while the degree-holding labor pool finally reached the point of oversaturation.

                              Then the dot-com bubble burst. A year later, 9/11. And everything went to shit. Never happened before, we learned our lesson and fixed our shit, won't happen again.

                              So anyways there was this really cool roleplaying game that came out around that time, about people clinging onto the lowest rungs of the social ladder and struggling to survive in those dilapidated, abandoned urban cores. They fought gangland-style amongst themselves to seize and protect what little scraps of wealth, influence, and dignity were available to them, while others who lived anywhere else stole and hoarded everything for themselves. And they were vampires.

                                  Originally posted by Theodrim View Post
                                  There's nothing wrong with campy pulp-inspired fun, but let me make the case for the understated intelligence of early-edition VtM. Yes, the obnoxiously arrogant, superficially political, progressive secularist morality play "theme" belched itself forth into the game in later editions and consumed it, however early editions had things to say and said them well.

                                  The Y2K phenomena, frankly...wasn't really a thing. It was over and done with almost as soon as it hit the public consciousness. Most of the clamor, was mass media sensationalist fearmongering on slow news days.
                                  When I say "the Y2K Era", I'm not talking about the fearmongering over the so called "Y2K bug".

                                  I'm talking about a specific time in pop culture in the late 90's and early 2000's where certain types of aesthetics were but one part of a wider cultural zeitgeist. I was born in 1992, so the Y2K Era of 1997-2004 was the time of my early childhood and an era I have a LOT of nostalgia for.

                                  This is "Y2K Aesthetic", not "Y2K Computer Bug".

                                  It's the idealized mythic pop culture version of the late 1990's and early 2000's, as opposed to the actual Late 1990's and Early 2000's, if that makes any sense.

                                  Now, I do agree that the early editions of VTM did have an element of social commentary and satire to them (very clever commentary, I might add) and it wasn't as overly pretentious, heavy-handed, and sanctimonious as the commentary in later editions like V5 would be, but that's not my goal with this setting riff.

                                  I'm not really trying to say anything other than "Hey, I think this whole era's idealized aesthetic vibe is kinda fun" but I also firmly believe that not every work of art or entertainment has to say something deep or profound.

                                      Apologies for the double-post but here's another setting update.

                                      But first, kudos to Theodrim for his analysis of the 1990's and the parallels it has to the 1950's, as well as how that tied into Vampire 1e's initial appeal and how it resonated with the times. Back in 1E, you could have intelligent commentary, personal horror, and pulp action-horror all in the same space and nobody believed those things were mutually exclusive.

                                      Back to the setting updates...

                                      The Patriots and the rest of the World of Darkness

                                      Now, while The Patriots may be the most powerful conspiracy in the Millennium Nights despite being largely just a bunch of rich and well-connected mortals, they do realize the immense power that groups such as the Kindred, the Lupines, and the Magi all possess.

                                      The Camarilla is honestly one of the easier groups to deal with. They're largely self-policing and the existence of the Masquerade helps keeps any potential issues to a minimum and sometimes makes things a lot easier for the operations of The Patriots.

                                      The same applies to the Giovanni, who will often work with Patriot-aligned financial institutions if they feel like there's something to gain from it. They don't like the Camarilla or the Giovanni and the uppermost echelons of those groups both fear and hate The Patriots, but there is a begrudging mutual respect and a sense not to step on each other's toes so long as the Masquerade stays in place.

                                      The Sabbat is utterly despised by The Patriots and while they'll devote more resources to curbing the Sword of Caine, they also feel the Sabbat is self-destructive enough that their allied hunter groups along with Lupines and the Camarilla do enough of a good job in taking care of that problem.

                                      The Assamites, Ravnos, and Followers of Set are also despised by The Patriots but again, pragmatism takes the lead and they're willing to use these Independent Clans as unwitting catspaws from time to time. Most of the tiny handful of Kindred within the ranks of The Patriots tend to be Assamite Vizers and Old Clan Tzimisce.

                                      The Tal'Mahe'Ra is in an uneasy alliance with The Patriots, as they do most of the heavy-lifting dealing with more external and unusual threats like Soul Eaters and other horrors of the deeper reaches of the spirit world. The Patriots would rather have the True Black Hand on their side as erstwhile allies/occasional catspaws than have to deal with the weird shit from other worlds on their own.

                                      The Order of Hermes is on a similar "mutual begrudging respect" with The Patriots as the Camarilla is. They are the closest thing Magi have to a single unified society and are also self-regulating. There are more Magi within the ranks of The Patriots than any other supernatural creature (though they are still a rarity) and the Order of Hermes did help out the Western Philosophers by providing crucial information about Colonel Volgin (himself a renegade who betrayed the Hermetics) in the lead-up to Operation Snake Eater.

                                      The Patriots as such are more lenient to the Order of Hermes than any other supernatural group, even if they still don't like them.

                                      The Lupines utterly despise The Patriots and hate Pentex in particular. The Patriots try to fight back and clamp down hard, but they also know they cannot overextend themselves in these efforts if they want to maintain their control and equillibrium.

                                                      Originally posted by Matt Corleone View Post
                                                      Aesthetically, it's a lot less "Gothic-Punk" and a lot more "Y2K Aesthetics meets Hot Topic Mall Goth"
                                                      Gothic Punk was never meant to be an aesthetic; It was the overall Theme and Mood for the World of Darkness as a whole.

                                                      Gothic is the Mood. The world and its powers that be are large and imposing. Age, size, and power over people reinforces the protagonists' inherent weakness in the shadows of their oppressive looming and the greater schemes of the world. The alliance between the corporate and political classes, the bureaucracies and laws, pop culture and the mindless mob. All of them are against you. So is your past and your own personal failings.

                                                      Punk is the Theme. Be yourself and fuck anyone who has a problem with that. You are a person and an individual rather than a number or a cog. You - yes, YOU - are important and your voice is worth being heard, provided you are willing to speak up and accept the potential consequences of not following the crowd. Ultimately, the only person who can save you is you.​

                                                      The dignity and dreams of the individual must constantly strive against the uncaring and corrupt forces that want to crush such things. Films like The Godfather, Serpico, and Copland are things I point to as examples of Gothic Punk. Also, in their own way, things like Harry Potter, Frozen, and Zootopia. And, yes, I would also point to things like The Crow and Dark City, but not because of their set design and cinematography. Instead, it's Eric Draven's struggle against the corruption of Top Dollar and even against death itself and John Murdoch's struggle against the Strangers and their gnostic prison city.
                                                      In Vampire, it's the struggle to hold on to your Humanity against the Beast, the struggle to hold on to your own autonomy against your Sire and other Elder manipulators, and the struggle to hold on to your own place in Kindred society against the manipulations of the Camarilla and other forces, among other things.
