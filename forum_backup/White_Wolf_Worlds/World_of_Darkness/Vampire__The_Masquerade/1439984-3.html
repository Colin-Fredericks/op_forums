    Originally posted by Chris24601 View Post
    I’m going to buck the trend a bit and say if you really want to build a World of Darkness shared universe akin to the MCU... don’t START with Vampire; start with Mage.

    Mages, particularly newly Awakened ones are, bare none, the closest to mortals in abilities and perspective to normal humans of any of the main WoD splats. That makes them ideal for an opening viewpoint character and subtle fx requirements to start.

    But also important is that a big part of Mage is that everything man has ever believed in exists in somewhere in the shadows. This preps the stage for the larger universe with meeting vampires, werewolves, wraiths, changelings, etc. before spinning off an associated series where we get more details on the specific supernatural type.

    I also really think VtM on its own just doesn’t have enough unique on its own to make it a viable IP for a Netflix style series. But the larger WoD as a shared universe IS fairly unique (and rarely as well put together; ex. the Vampire Diaries and its spin-offs).
    I have to disagree for multiple reasons:

    1. Vampire is simply more popular.

    2. Vampire is also something proven to already have a very adaptable premise given things like Blade: The Series, Underworld, True Blood, and other stories about "vampire mafias."

    3. Mage is probably the hardest to actually explain. It is a VERY esoteric game in terms of factions and ideas.

    4. As for not being "unique" enough, you're not selling the premise anyway. You're selling the content. What will make a V:TM game would be acting and characters.

    But that's just me.

    Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

            [ETA] This is in reply to CTPhipps last post, I just didn’t feel like including a long quote block when his points were numbered for easy replies... [/ETA]

            You and I completely disagree on something? This is my shocked face... -_-

            1) Let’s be honest here, neither property is setting the world on fire at the moment. Compared to Blade or Dr. Strange they’re both F-tier IPs at best.

            2) The fact that you can mention so many properties that have already done what VtM does and are vastly more present in the public consciousness is all the more reason to NOT make VtM your tentpole property for a larger World of Darkness media universe.

            3) Elevator-Pitch Explanation: Mages are people who can push reality to align with their wills, but suffer pushback called paradox if they push it too far out of line. Mages with similar desires band together into traditions that are in conflict with each other over the direction to push the world.

            3a) Most people have read/watched Harry Potter... a hidden magical world existing alongside the mundane one isn’t something that needs lengthy explanations these days; just the nature of the factions, their goals and how each works their magic.

            3b) Most mages don’t understand how magic works at the metaphysics-level; each tradition has its own beliefs, practices and props and viewers only need be introduced to those one at a time. In keeping with 3a I’d personally open with an orphan who is found by a member of House Ex Miscellanea who’s Hermetic paradigm is close enough to what many consider “default wizardry” that it doesn’t need much explanation. Then you can start introducing people who do things differently.

            4) Just what content do you think is so unique you couldn’t just skip the virtually unheard of outside of RPG circles IPs and write your own vampire story where the lore can be exactly what you want instead of paying money so that superfans can incessantly bitch at you that you’re adapting it wrong?

            That last one is 1000% why this will never happen. Anything you’d want to do with VtM could just as easily be written in a generic urban fantasy universe with some of the names filed off for less money, less superfan backlash and less interference from the rights holders.

            Using VtM as the basis for a series is a Lose/Lose proposition. Frankly, so is Mage, it’s just slightly less so because it wouldn’t feel quite as derivative as VtM would look after decades of vampire shows and movies ripping off all the best ideas from it (it doesn’t matter if VtM did it first, it matters that they’ve already seen it if they’ve watched any vampire-themes genre series in the last 20 years). At least Mage has the potential to expand past looking like a Harry Potter rip-off.

                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                        I always found it funny that Hunter got three video games despite being one of the lower tier recognizable lines.
                        Always remember, if it’s not D&D then EVERY rpg game line is the lowest tier of recognizable outside of rpg circles. Hell, I know non-rper who use “D&D” as synonymous with “rpg” as in “What sort of D&D are you guys playing?”

                        In terms of percentage of the general population the difference in numbers between those who’ve heard of VtM vs. Hunter is in the fractions of a percent (remember 3.5 million copies of something wouldn’t even be 1% of the US population or 0.05% of the global population).

                        Bloodlines sold less than 100,000 copies. The pilot episode of The Vampire Diaries had 4.93 million viewers and even its lowest rated episodes in its eighth season had 10 times the viewers as Bloodlines sold copies. Twilight made $407 million in theatres and sold over 11 million DVDs.

                        In short, the difference in popularity between VtM and HtR outside of role-playing circles is a rounding error. As such, I’ll agree in a general sense with Hunter being the logical starting point with the caveat that “Hunters Hunted” Hunters are an even more sensible start point.

                        That said, HtR and HH have exactly the same problems as the rest of the World of Darkness... there’s just not enough unique to it to make the IP worth acquiring over just having some already famous screenwriter bang out an urban fantasy script.

                        Even the IP names are too damned generic. Take the maybe 10% of the content you’d actually want to use in season one, file off the names (they’re not Nosferatu, they’re Orlocks... they’re not Tremere they’re the House Dracul*), add one or two elements from elsewhere, and call the whole thing “Vampires.” You wouldn’t have to pay White Wolf a dime and any claim of infringement would be laughted out of court.

                        * This plays on the original novel’s lore that Dracula gained his many supernatural powers from a Satanic academy of magic... drinking blood was just the Satanic price for immortality. Take it a step further and replace the Caine/Antediluvian origin with various figures throughout history who reputably sold their souls to the Devil for supernatural power (so a bloodline of Rasputin, a bloodline of Caligula, etc.) becoming the first vampire of each “lineage” and because each bargain was a little different, each lineage has different strengths and weaknesses.

                        Inventing new vampire lore is stupid-easy at this point; there’s zero reason to pay the owners of VtM for theirs.

                                The single biggest obstacle to a Vampire: The Masquerade tv series is probably the "Elric Problem".

                                For those that have never heard of it, Elric of Melnibone is Michael Moorcock's dark fantasy albino swordsman who has been around since the early 1960s, and one of his nicknames, "The White Wolf", was the inspiration for the White Wolf Games Studios' name. There was recently an effort to get an Elric streaming series made. The problem was that everyone who looked at it said it was too much like The Witcher. The Witcher franchise, which started in the 90s, very clearly derives a lot of its influences from Elric.
                                Vampire has a similar issue, in that ever since the first Blade movie very clearly "borrowed" a lot of ideas from it, too many other things look like Vampire, especially to the intellectually challenged individuals who run most entertainment studios. With Disney+ already having announced an MCU Blade streaming series (or film, I forget which), and the existence of things like the Underworld franchise, which more Hollywood type people are likely to have heard of, trying to sell them on a V:tM series would probably be an uphill battle.
                                The likely exception is if you tried to sell them on turning LA by Night into a streaming fiction series. They might bite at that. (NPI)

                                                Hmm. I think if I were to try to make a Vampire series - let's say a Netflix style streaming one with 10 episodes - I would probably have it mainly focus on a Sire and Childe as the two main characters, with their interaction being the backbone, and their interactions with others branching off from there. Not sure which Clan, but most likely Brujah or Ventrue. They'd be a window into the worlds of Elders and Neonates, respectively. They aren't enemies, but aren't exactly bosom companions either. Their relationship should be complicated and multifaceted, with more than a few parallels and/or reflections in their stories.
                                                I'm not really sure which city would work best as a setting. While LA and Chicago would certainly be interesting, I think New York might be the most optimal choice due to size, age, and density.
                                                The Elder story would likely involve two or three other characters, having ties to each other going back to the 19th century or earlier. This is a very complex game, usually with very subtle moves and deep plots.
                                                The Neonate story is maybe three or four other characters, with more of a focus on the nightlife of the Rack, interpersonal relationships among vampires who are still young enough to still be thinking a lot like the humans they were in life. Possibly at least one trying to be in a relationship with a living mortal.
                                                The season (or series, if only a one and done) finale would have some sort of significant alteration in the Sire and Childe's relationship that has been built to over the season. Also probably the death (or apparent death) of at least one supporting character.
