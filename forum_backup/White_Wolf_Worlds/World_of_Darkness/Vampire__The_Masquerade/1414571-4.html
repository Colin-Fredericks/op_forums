    Originally posted by False Epiphany View Post
    This is why I've always had mixed feelings about the traditional coterie structure for PCs. While it can have its place in games, there are a lot of games where it doesn't work. PCs may wish to pursue different goals with radically different approaches, while the setting as a whole encourages betrayal and infighting.

    I don't know that there's a good solution for traditional tabletop games, since the GM can only spend so much time running scenes without 1+ players before the ones sitting on their hands get bored.
    I wonder if a possible solution is to allow the player (of the PC who is the odd man out) to switch from playing their PC to a second character, like an NPC ally or another PC's Retainer, who will participate in whatever group activity that would conflict against their character concept. That way they can have fun and do something as the player yet not compromise their PC.

    I have offered this option at least twice, but both times the players rejected it. I always thought it was a good compromise solution. Just hasn't worked out.

                        Originally posted by Kammerer View Post
                        In regards to the Baali, I stand by the assertion that no people are truly innately evil, but Baali have a baked-in Nihilism button. By the name of "Daimoinon 1: Sense the Sin". At any moment, with no cost, for minimal effort, the fledgeling Baali knows exactly how screwed up everyone around them is. There is no blissful ignorance to the Baali. They know who cheats on their spouse, who steals pencils at work, who beats their husband, who forgot to feed the kids. With any person they meet, the Baali knows them at their worst. They see Evil. They do not see Good. Baali are the true clan of judges.
                        Ooh, now this is neat.

                        In the last season of Buffy, there were a group called 'the Bringers' who gouged out their own eyes and only 'saw' via the illusions that their evil big bad master fed them, and, naturally, said visions were twisted to paint the world as the master wanted them to see it, so that a champion of good they saw as a horrific thing that must be destroyed. I like that Sense the Sin kind of perpetuates the evil, by showing only the world at it's worst, and 'proving' to the Baali that all people are horrible and corrupt and so it's totally normal to be equally depraved and to hold them all in contempt, because the magic flashlight conveniently doesn't show them the older brother being super-protective of his disabled kid brother, or the love of a nanny for the child she's watching, or any of the better parts of the human experience. All they get to see is the ugly, and they embrace the ugly, since, as far as they can see, that's all that there is in the world...
