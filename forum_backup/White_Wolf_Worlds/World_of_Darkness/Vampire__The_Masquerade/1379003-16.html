    Originally posted by Shakanaka View Post
    I don't understand the "back to the basic" thing with these V5 writers. oWoD has global acclaim, its already well known with a considerable fanbase over the years. Why did it have to soft retcon things "back to the basics" for? They could have angled it in writing where even though newcomers come on and be a little out the loop, they can still be engaged with the story, making them interesting to get the older books to look into as I did when I got introduced in oWoD in 2018. It isn't hard at all. This reintroduction is tedious to all fans because we didn't need it at all. Personally (I don't know about anyone else) I just wanted things to move along into the modern age past the 1990s. Also V5 did more than "reintroduce" clans and act as if the series is somehow back to 1e- it has made the nonsensical Hecata "clan" (which should've been a Sect), gas-lighted the Tremere down to bits (which we have discussed in this thread ad naseum), putting personal seemingly political bias in VTM, making the series cater to the Anarchs when by Revised and 2e they were merely a background third neutral faction for the most part, etc.
    Because they need new fans to keep the franchise alive. I'm not sure how that is even a question. 'Global acclaim' and 'well known' is a thing for WoD, sure, in very niche gaming circles. There has not been an in print game book for Vampire in years, and not all of the vampire fandom comes to these online forums, despite how much people claim otherwise. V5 is changing that by being the first new vampire book in stores in years. When V5 books first got out into stores, one of the game store owners locally was even surprised that there had been a 20th Anniversary edition, he had thought vampire had ended when they stopped having Requiem product to order. Another one thought the same and had thought that no one played anymore and that it was a dead line. So your 'considerable fanbase' is a very vocal portion, but hardly representative of every person who has played or will ever play vampire.

    Basically you're advocating for gatekeeping, instead of making it immediately accessible and then adding complexity through options. News Flash: You. And. The. Online. Community. Are. Not A. Representative. Of. All. Fans. Constantly saying 'we didn't need' and 'we don't want' represents some very vocal portion of the fanbase, but you do not represent the entire fanbase, just your echo chamber. Besides, largely, V5 has been well-received BECAUSE it's allowing new fans in.with less overwhelming material (sorry not sorry, V20 is a fucking joke for introducing new people because of overload, lack of context, and other factors). It's cool that YOU weren't overwhelmed by everything prior, but YOU are not EVERYONE.

    As far as political, vampire has always been political. Whether that is a plus or a minus is in individual tastes, but man.

    Now onto the Second Inquisition and why I believe the Imbued should've been the ones to be the Hunter faction against them? Because the Imbued make the most sense in the context of the oWoD universe. They are decentralized, have a hunter marking way of communication, imbued (as their namesake) abilities to go up against OP Vampires, they consistently delve in conspiracy theories where it would make sense for them to uncover all the shit about Vampire society (I would tack them on as the ones to infiltrate Schrecknet, not the freaking NSA), etc.
    'OP Vampires' here we go with more invincible vampire bullshit. Humans are a threat because numbers, technology, and tons of things that have been detailed ad nauseum in this thread. Just because you don't feel like it's logical, does not mean it is not within the INTERNAL LOGIC of the setting. Imbued have less numbers, less organization, and tend to burn out super fast. So unless there's a new Imbued being empowered every other day, they're going to burn out their numbers faster than 'a guy at the top in the NSA knowing and rerouting anti-terrorist materials to deal with vampires'. Not to mention a conspiracy theorist (or small group of them working to oust a vampire) is also much more trivially destroyed socially once their activities are know. It is more in the logic of what was written about the Imbued in general in past editions .

                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                Just for ease of marketing. I may be wrong because they've announced Werewolf 5E but it occurs to me they might devote more of their resources to the central game versus trying to dilute it.
                I think in the case of V5, you have to remember that Modiphius only came into taking on the license in the 11th hour after the White Wolf team was dissolved. As such, they didn’t really have plans in taking on all the WoD games, and Vampire was always the most profitable one. I expect Modiphius will probably make a few more supplements over the next year or two, and then let Onyx Path continue with supplemental support after then. I don’t know, of course, but I’d expect to see the Players’ Guide followed up by The Second Inquisition in the new year, and maybe it will be a standalone game to compliment V5. I don’t expect any other ‘fat splats’ though. Maybe they will get round to a Sabbat book, presumably with the Tzmisce at its core, but who knows. Maybe the Ravnos will join the Sabbat! Personally, I think they would make more truck if they made more City Chronicle books, but that is just me.

                We know that Werewolf 5th is to be taken on by Hunter’s Entertainment, a relatively new company albeit with some recognizable faces, and Matt Dawkins will still be co--ordinating it. Curiously, outside of some fans comparing its original 1st edition release unfavorably to V1 and it having a fairly overt political commentary, Werewolf has never really been as controversial as Vampire has when making new editions. I expect the fans will probably switch over to it quite agreeably, particularly if they tie it in effectively with the video game releases.

                I’m really not sure about M5. The current M20 is still planning releases and there has always been an argument that it should stand independent of the other WoD games. The current developer, Brucato, is passionate about his role and so are his fans, so there could be an even bigger negative reaction to any idea of a new edition. Mage is a more complex game than the others in a number of respects, and has a history of bitter flame wars, and any new development crew ought to be fully aware of this. There is a great possible game that could emerge in leaner format, but there is a lot of baggage to let go.

                Wraith and Changeling, bluntly, won’t be commercially lucrative enough to make new editions, I don’t think. Some fans will be upset to say that, but both games never got beyond their 2nd editions first time round, and again, they are pretty well served by the 20th Anniversary editions.
