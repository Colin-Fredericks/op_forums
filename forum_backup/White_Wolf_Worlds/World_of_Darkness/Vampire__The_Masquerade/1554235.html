      There's lots of ways to make WoD fights longer despite the power a dedicated combatant can bring to bear.

      The problem is "epic." You can't really "rules" your way into epic... even Exalted and Scion can only make epic easier to accomplish. Epic isn't a specific thing, it's an ephemeral experiential emotion that stems from game play. It doesn't inherently come from longer fights... longer fights can easy be boring and feel like you're spending hours to resolve the obvious. In many ways longer fights mechanically tend to make combat feel less epic because the emotional highs get bogged down in the tedium between anything significant happening.

      So, broadly speaking: longer fights are a negative to most people because that just means taking longer to do whatever you were going to do. Longer fights are something that you accept as a consequence of rules that you hope make the fight more engaging by having more for the players to do. That second part is were the WoD (and ST family of games broadly) struggle, because it's very hard to make anything other than "hit really hard and don't get hit" worth doing.

      Epic, if you want to try to apply rules to it, means defining some design goals. Do you want to make fights harder to win through raw force so victories feel more "earned" (which can be fast play, depending on how you make players earn advantages in combat)? Do you want flashier and more narrative driven combat? Etc.

                              Table consensus to fix the current system isn't really a consensus if you have to do all the work.

                              The idea that 4 dice is superior to 10 dice is not at all backed up by the math of the system; esp. if we're talking 20th.

                              The problem with the math of the WoD games, is that they're not intended for epic battles. They're intended for brutal street brawls. That's one of the reason they're so strongly oriented towards offense. Then even if you fix the issues with the base-line combat rules, you still need to redo the entire power set to make sure nothing gets broken on that side of things.

                              If your table wants to stick closer to the base-line rules, Exalted Essence or Storypath Ultra are pretty good starting places. Slightly cleaner base systems with combat meant to resolve faster but have a lot of scope and give more weigh to narrative functions.

                                          True, there are a lot of differences between WoD and D&D that make it less like rocket tag. Hit points are one of those differences.

                                          Another thing that D&D has is passive and scaling defense. You don’t have to sacrifice your ability to attack to defend yourself in D&D, which also helps. It also helps that your defense grows as your ability to attack (and face more attackers) grows. Like getting magic armor, more hit points, defensive feats, etc.

                                          In WoD, there are way more things you can do to significantly enhance attack than enhance defense. A vampire with feral claws and Potence 5 will annihilate a vampire with Fortitude 5.

                                          Personally, to reduce rocket tag, I would also take a look at changing aggravated damage a bit. Also, adding damage from successes on an attack roll could be eliminated too. Also, adding some degree of passive defense.

                                          Some of these changes have been made at various stages.

                                          Anyway, long story short, there are many changes that could be made to help, but adding health levels is a quick fix that could move in the right direction.

                                              I think the quickest fix would be to add a passive defense to the WoD with a full defense bonus of some sort (like most of the other ST family games have at this point). For V20 specifically, this helps considerably against the way Celerity sacrifices odds to hit for more attacks (as passive Celerity adds to Dex rolls to hit, while active multiple actions reduce that bonus). Potence and Agg have to hit you first to matter, and currently the way the action economy works lots of attacks easily overwhelms having to use actions for baseline defense so spamming lots of Celerity attacks means you're highly likely to not have to face dodge/block/parry so the imbalance of damage vs soak easily takes over.

                                              The reason I consider this the easiest is because you can just assume the average results of many combat rolls (as the default diff of 6 applies most of the time) and it's minimal work to put penalties onto the static value generated if necessary. If you have Dex 3/Athletics 3 you could round down in the math to 2 (because 1s) or round up for 3 (because the OP wants things to be a bit more epic) which then become static values that subtract successes from rolls to hit. One less roll per attack, and an obvious benefit to avoid alpha-strike style rocket tag.

                                              Most bonuses are already in dice, so if you have passive Celerity 2 or use blood to increase your Dex by 2, your Defense goes up 1; very little mucking around with difficulty penalties to deal with.

                                              If an attacker really wants to one-shot someone, they're moved into incentivizing putting everything into one big hit, but an active defense (there's a few models to pick from) is viable to contest that as the action economy gets on a more even footing.

                                                  Originally posted by Sergeant Brother View Post
                                                  Well, the way that D&D does this is by having more Hit Points.
                                                  Actually, what D&D does to solve this is to have more viable options in combat than "I hit it hard".

                                                  It's the problem Heavy Arms said in his first post, long battles don't become "epic" just because they're long. Even just making a lot of Combat Maneuvers won't solve anything if they're too similar or too obvious. Epic battles need choices with dramatic stakes, actions that change context, each turn must feel like something different from the previous turn, even if not by much. Otherwise you're just dragging things, it's boring.

                                                  Surviving past an OTK build is far from an actual solution.

                                                  Originally posted by Pnizzle View Post
                                                  The table consensus was fix the current system.
                                                  Can't be done, because at this point nothing you'll build will actually satisfy your players, as it will change the system too much.

                                                  The short version of it is, WoD simply isn't made for this. This experience isn't the goal of the system, and if forced into delivering it the system will behave poorly. The best you can get is Werewolf, and it still has a lot of problems.

                                                  If you want to run epic battles, work on their setup. Have foes that anticipate problems and fight from an advantageous point. Have multiple opponents, even if just mooks. Make sure hitting the foe isn't straightforward most of the time. Maybe have magical effects from items, strange rituals or whatever to protect them, NPC's shouldn't be limited to character sheet resources. That's the best you'll get out of WoD.

                                                  Other than that, go for a different system, you already considered solid options. Don't let your players dictate what the system should be like when they don't even understand how it really works in the first place, and can't nor will help you with such a daunting task.​
