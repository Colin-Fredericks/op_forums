    Metaplot Plot-Thread Reboot

    01-26-2023, 06:06 PM
    I think most people who frequent these forums regularly know that the Metaplot of our favorite gothic punk RPGs can be controversial, but I like to think that largely comes from a place of affection.

    That said, here’s the idea I had that led me to make this thread: I like the idea of having a bunch of little dials, optional threads, and story packages that I can use as I please for my games. This has come up with my admiration for the Scion 2e Companion book and the administration I’ve felt and seen others express for those books which contain optional threads one can implement in their game setting as a means to progress the world.

    IMO these threads and stories help to prevent the setting from feeling static and create all sorts of little ways that one can use them as inspiration for a story.

    So, here’s the idea: you have a blank document in front of you and the setting’s clock has been rolled back to 1991-1992. You can have as much or as little knowledge of what would be added to the setting in later books as you want. What sort of things do you put into the document as options.

    Here’s some examples of my own:

    •The Hag and the Wall
    -With the dissolution of the USSR the domains which have answered to the Brujah Council in Moscow for 3/4rs of a century are suddenly sundered from one another. The power scramble has begun. Every domain sees its own domestic claimants for prince come out of the woodwork. The Brujah Justicar Carlak has been planning for this moment for years and he’s moving quickly to get the kindred in his corner seats in prominent domains - by the time this is all over he plans to be the key power player in the former eastern bloc. The Ordea League is raising its own clarion call and seeking to see the old domains restored and furthermore to parcel off new territory for their broods; they’ve cooperated with the Camarilla against the Anarchs and Sabbat for this long and they’re calling in favors. The Mikaelites who fled Greece in the final nights of Byzantium see this as the perfect opportunity - now is the time to restore the third Rome and to rekindle the embers of Mikael’s Dream. Meanwhile, while all these powers scheme and most kindred are scrambling simply to survive, The Great Hag has risen from her torporous slumber and seeks to create a dark empire of her own - one decorated with the ashen remains of her so-called clan mates…
    (I personally don’t like the Shadow Curtain and feel that the Baba Yaga plot is more interesting without it.)

    •The domains around the Great Lakes our the heart of the North American Camarilla and embody the sect’s status quo on that continent. Lodin and all the other powers that be in those Midwestern cities want to keep it that way. Yet their are threats at every corner. On one hand the Anarchs have never been driven totally underground in Chicago and every year it seems that the Black Prince or some new upstart has more followers. Furthermore every concession made to keep the peace one more night, however slight, smacks Justicar Petrodon as the gravest display of weakness seen yet and the princes of the lakes suspect that if Petrodon had his way he would bury them all and have them replaced by the broods of old world princes. The Lupines who flock to the lakes from all over the continent certainly aren’t making matters any easier, nor are the Sabbat packs who come prowling out of Detroit or Smiling Jack’s free riders from out west. The domains of the Great Lakes have to be careful, lest the tentative balance maintained in the American Camarilla Heartlands is overthrown…

              I had my own piece of this, the Year of the Red Comet and the Week of Nightmares.

              THE WEEK OF NIGHTMARES Almost every Kindred Embraced in the 21st century has heard something about the Week of Nightmares, but most of them are fuzzy about the details. The ones who dwell in Cairo, despite being one of the most affected cities, are not much more in the clear, and possibly less.
              What basically everyone knows is that at some point at the beginning of the year 2000, a lot of Kindred starting seriously freaking out, killing each other in the process. The paranoia had started earlier, though, some say than during the second half of the 1990's. Gehena cults, millenarist prophets and apocalyptic texts had started popping out in Kindred society like mushrooms after heavy rain. In many cities around the world the Princes had decreed the hunting down of Caitiff and Thin-Bloods while the Sabbat had launched a large crusade in North America.
              Then, at the fall of 1999, a red star that could be seen only by Kindred , appeared on the skies, as an herald of impending doom. In Cairo a rumor that "the last daughter of Eve" was pregnant and hidden somewhere in the city, spread like wildfire, and many Kindred killed each other trying to find her before anyone else. Against the Prince's order, a rogue group started hunting the Thin-Bloods.
              Then came The Week. Because of its name, it seems clear that dreams and nightmares had to do with it. Whatever they dreamt of, Kindred started killing Kindred, scared senseless of the incoming arrival of the Antediluvians. Most likely many of the killers thought they would be saved if they followed the orders sent to them in dreams, or just tried to defend themselves against the ones who did.
              After one week the nightmares ended, but the millenarist hysteria grew. Everyone thought the Nightmares had been just the beginning of the end and the red star still hung defiantly in the nightly sky.
              Only it wasn't. Weeks, and then months went by and nothing happened. Of course, the Scourges still killed Thin-Bloods, the Sabbat kept fighting its apocalyptic north American crusade and the paranoid murdered anyone who approached them. But neither the Antediluvians nor Caine rose from their resting places and God didn't send the Wormwood to destroy the sinners. And suddenly, 369 nights after it had appeared, the red star left the sky without explanation. It took some time to readapt to the "new normal", but it eventually happened. The Camarilla, which had presented itself as a bastion of calm and common sense, came up reinforced as an institution in Europe. In the Muslim world, the surviving Ashirra, many of whom had banded together against the most extremist Gehena cults, regained a sense of community that helped restore order and keep the Kindred society together. Eventually, the Sabbat, which for a time seemed to have triumphed in America, crumbled down and most of the cities it had conquered were taken back by the reinforced Camarilla.
              The final nail in the coffin for end of the world paranoids was the Inner Circle 2011 meeting. The Camarilla Elders issued a declaration merely mentioning the Year of the Herald and the Week of Nightmares as a "freak event, followed by mass hysteria, that had endangered the Masquerade" and issued a ban on both Gehena cults and the use of digital media, "to avoid an irreversible misstep that would be fatal in these time of instantaneous worldwide communications".
              Additionally, to increase their ranks, Progeny creation rules were loosened a little, but the Accounting ones were tightened. The powers that be wanted to make sure the new generation of Neonates wouldn't fall into the rabbit hole of cults or conspiracy theories.

                          I greatly toned down the damage to the Sabbat.

                          In my games, Melinda Galbraith started acting oddly, then disappeared. In her absence, the Sabbat was taken over by Cathari demagogues who increased in political power mainly by raising shovelheads to True Sabbat status in a small fraction of the time that other Paths did, basically as soon as the shovelhead displayed devotion to the Cathari path. The Cathari demagogues also generally encouraged reckless hedonism, which was destructive to the sect long term, but greatly appealed to the young, who viewed them as champions of freedom.

                          This angered packs following the other Paths, but they were rapidly outnumbered.

                          Eventually this cost the Sabbat several important cities because the Sabbat under the Cathari Demagogues was so sloppy. Nowhere near the collapse in canon, but enough that the Cathari were scrambling to spin this as a good thing, saying that the Sabbat were "more dangerous than ever without territory", which many older Sabbat regarded as absolutely insulting propaganda.

                          The Sabbat Inquisition made moves to take control of the Sabbat, using the cover of the Siege of New Orleans to establish New Orleans as the new capital of the Sabbat, abolishing the Regent position, and making the Grand Inquisitor the top Sabbat position. She then declared the Path of Cathari heretical and started a Civil War with the goal of genociding the Cathari Demagogues and their followers.

                          Her ultimate goal has been to reforge the Sabbat as an alliance of those vampires who reject humanity, and to acquire a large territory with a safe border in which those vampires seeking to advance on Paths of Enlightment can do so in peace. The only vampires not welcome in this alliance of nonhuman religions are those whose beliefs are antithetical to the security of the Sabbat, such as Cathari, Evil Revelations, and Humanity.

                                          Originally posted by Koronus View Post

                                          Depend on if you go by 10.000 People per Cainite or 100.000. If 100.000 then the city would actually be overpopulated with him being married. But we go by 10.000 and so the city would suitable for 11 cainites.
                                          That's not how the 1 Kindred to 100,000 mortals guideline has ever worked in official publications, though. I don't know how many times I've seen people just look up city populations, apply the 1:100,000 ratio, come up with absurdly low vampire numbers, and conclude that the rule is junk. But the guideline is supposed to be used with the population numbers of metropolitan areas, including all the suburbs and hinterland surrounding a given city. The early By Night supplements were designed with this method in mind, so it's pretty obvious that the 1:100,000 ratio works just fine, especially if it is used (as intended) as a guideline and not a hard limit.

                                                  That's one of those things that I think people overthink. Let the story be your guide. Are you telling a murder mystery that requires a dozen vampires? Then your city has a dozen vampires. Are you telling a war story that requires seven-plus gangs, each with seven-plus members? Then your city has fiftyish vampires. Let that be your guide rather than the 1 to 100,000 rule.
