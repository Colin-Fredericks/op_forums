          This is an issue that deserves good brainstorming because it is often relevant in chronicles. It's a real problem because vampires don't have the same aversion to certain punishments like humans would - it is either trivial for them to endure or meaningless. Worse from a PC perspective, punishments that should still be deterrents to vampires - like torture - hold little fear for the players because at the end of the day there is no permanent harm on their character sheet (and if there was, they would bitch endlessly about it). (I've only ever seen players act completely defiant about any possible torture because, of course, we're all Hollywood action hero movie stars when we roleplay - I admit I am not much better.)

          I would break down crimes into the two major categories of misdemeanors and felonies, or minor and major violations or however you want to label them.

          The first category is basically about compensating the owner sufficiently for trespassing and property damage (property of course including things like ghouls or even trusted mortal pawns - wergeld should be a thing for vampires). The fine should be sufficient to allow the victim to recover from the damage plus an additional penalty to discourage things again. The exact fine can be any combination of money, service time (boons), or other commodities as agreed between the victim and the transgressor. The Prince is probably just there to mediate or arbitrate between the two parties. He's not acting as a judge. The goal here shouldn't really be punishment, but compensation (plus extra to indicate "sorry") and to restore the social peace between the two to prevent further violence.

          The second is when the violation is more major, and therefore the society expects something more to be done because of the egregiousness of the crime (which could be the cause for repeated instances of crimes that would otherwise fall in the first category, when the motive is malicious instead of negligent, the criminal has refused to pay earlier agreed damages, etc.) So at this point, what would vampires (and PCs) actually fear?

          At the lower category would be enforced boons to be owed to the aggrieved party. This might be the criminal owing the victim or Prince a boon, or perhaps the transfer of a boon owed by one vampire to the criminal that is now paid to the victim (although I might allow the person who owed the boon to object to the transfer if they had a valid reason). Ideally such a boon would need to be paid in the short term because you don't want boons gained in this manner to linger - it won't seem like a punishment, and it might encourage the criminal to be a scofflaw and never repay.

          The next category would likely be some stage of the blood bond - starting with one drink, but perhaps two or even three depending on the crime. This is the only thing that I've seen actually be a deterrent to PCs as they fear loss of autonomy over their characters more than anything else. But it is something best negotiated with the players out of character as to what crimes make sense for this punishment. You as the ST want a standard of law that the Players can buy into that they feel is correct for the setting (I won't say "just").

          The last category would be exile on pain of death - basically giving them some time before a Blood Hunt within the domain is called or takes effect. The Blood Hunt will mean the exile is real as they cannot come back. But everyone knows the Prince won't ask other Princes to extend the Blood Hunt to their own domain. The criminal is given some amount of reasonable time to flee. This might be only one day just to allow them to go somewhere safe, or it could be a longer time - three days, a week, a month so they can set their affairs in order (either to better defend the Masquerade, or allow someone to begin transferring matters like ghouls, retainers, influence, etc.).

          In any case, I think a big component of Kindred law is that if you don't kill the vampire, you want the punishment to be in some form that a "reasonable" Kindred won't seek to kill the Prince over it. They acknowledge the punishment is just and are thankful for the extent it allows them to restore the social peace. The Prince wants to minimize the possibility of making enemies, or at least deflect the enmity towards the aggrieved party and not herself. In these cases, enforced boons and a one step blood bond are best.

          Of course, many vampires are not "reasonable". When caught and punished, they will blame their victims and the law for the punishment, never themselves for committing the crime. In those cases, the Prince will want to take steps to minimize the criminal's future attempts at revenge. In that case, the Prince will likely lean towards deeper blood bonds (2 or 3 steps) or exile as a preventative means.

          These steps are not exclusive. A higher level punishment will likely combine elements of lower levels as the Prince sees fit.

          That's my current take.

                      I tend to refer back to the Revised Edition Guide to the Camarilla which listed some common (and uncommon) sentences for minor, moderate, and serious offenses. Minor offenses might warrant loss of status or title, or forced servitude to another Kindred over time. Moderate offenses might warrant loss of domain, stripping of debts or boons owed, removal of retainers, or forceful one or two step blood bond to the Prince. Serious offenses might warrant destruction of the vampire or their progeny, exile from the city, or, if they choose to stay, branding and/or forcible donation of their blood to the local Tremere Chantry to ensure good behavior.

                          Like IRL medieval times, crime and punishment would be what the prince could get away with, so the prince would need to measure a response that doesn't upset too many of the wrong people while solidifying the power of him and his allies. In "good" cities, the Prince is the arbiter because most agree with his judgement. If the prince is too harsh, he's attacking his constituents, if the prince is too soft, he's playing favourites. The prince can use his power to curb rivals or strengthen allies or to garner favour, but he should at least try to frame things as "fair". Obviously, Vampires can't expect fairness the way most of us see it: A neonate doesn't have the umph of an elder and can't expect as much consideration. But there's a certain fairness in "one day I too will be old and influentual and will be able to bend the rules more"

                          Law is good for determining what is a crime and what isn't, but Having the Politics knowledge should be good for predicting how harsh punishments should be. Ideally, there will be lots of backroom deals before a public sentencing.

                          I feel as a prince it would be best to publicly give perpetrators of minor crimes a choice. Similar to "pay a fine or serve a sentence".

                          Non-death examples of punishments:
                          give up on territory, give away a business or retainer or a lump sum of capital, become blood bound, get staked for a period of time, go into torpor, lose a limb for a time, gain an annoying responsibility, exile.

                              The loss of Reputation is also a sort of "punishment".

                              Of course it is not enough to quell the vampiric society thirsty for justice and social order, as this is not immediately visible, but it is one thing to keep in mind, particularly for players. If you have a bad rep as someone who doesn't play ball and that is a dishonest liar, at best you can expect others distancing themselves, at worst you may end up dead, caugh up in another kindred's web of manipulations and blamed as a scape goat. After all, it is just too easy to believe that one well know mischief would be responsible for whatever new crime in town.

                              This is something players should take heed, even more than a blood bond or something. If start to get marked by the community, you may find troubles constantly coming at your way, and that's something that I think most players - are STs - dont pay too much attention to
