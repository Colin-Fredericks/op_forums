    Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
    Is it really punk, though?
    See, this is a great argument... for why I'm right.

    We're dealing with Gothic punk here. CHOICE is what matters. If you've got your ancient prince on a stake ready for you to kill him, we will for simplicity's sake assert that circumstances don't allow you to show mercy; you can either devour him for his power, or lop off his head and deny his power. If his power is a great temptation, your choice matters; you killing that prince without taking his blood is a personal sacrifice. If eating him will make you hungry and miserable and further remove you from being a person... is killing him instead of eating him really a personal sacrifices? I mean, you're making the near-objectively correct choice. The "punk" who eats the prince in the first scenario can at least delude himself into thinking things will change when he's in charge, maybe he can actually create meaningful change when he's in power, but in the MT scenario the punk is litererally making everynight unlife significantly more difficult for themselves with the slight caveat that they're better prepared to deal with other vampires.


    Also from another perspective having morality ordained to you; "diablerie is badwrong and worse than ordinary murder and you shouldn't do it" is a slippery slope because, in vampire at least, it's a political issue. Who's the author to say what politics are good and bad? I keep thinking of the line "a man chooses, a slave obeys" because, y'know, a good narrative has people make choices that make sense from their perspective. Things get dicey when the author's opinions inform every decision made by characters who should have very different perspectives. By ordaining the morality of political issues, the author becomes The Man, not a Punk.

                                    Another possibility: since many Caitiff/Thin Bloods are incapable of blood-bonding others, it stands to reason that Methuselahs with the Thirst will either capture a load of them to feed on, or offer them protection in exchange for laundering human blood into blood their new master can drink.

                                    The former, incidentally, neatly gives greater reason why the Camarilla is so adamant about hunting these "trash" vampires. Capturing them means they can be surreptitiously brought to Methuselahs to be farmed. If even a fraction of all Caitiff or Thin Bloods are staked by Scourges rather than killed on the spot, that would mean a fair number being available for this purpose.

                                    Actually, the second scenario also contributes to the anti-Caitiff/Thin Blood crusades. If even existing is a crime for these Kindred, it's easy to win them over into an exploitative arrangement with a Methuselah. Having to tithe some of their blood earnings to the master is better than being dusted. Though perhaps not by much, depending on the Methuselah they're working for. Some may not even be given freedom of movement, but are instead confined to compounds, with human blood brought to them for "processing".
