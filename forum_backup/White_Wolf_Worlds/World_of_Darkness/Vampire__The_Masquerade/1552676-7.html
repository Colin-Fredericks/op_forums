        Bringing here what was said in the sister thread, I really, really think the Gangrel leaving the Camarilla made absolutely no sense at all.

        Neither the clan nor the Cam have an actual organization that gives meaning to it.

        In which sense you're a member to an organization that works just like a contact bridge between Princes to begin with? How do you talk in the name of a whole clan that isn't organized in the first place? How any of this actually work?

        Originally posted by Saga View Post
        The thing here was because the Camarilla denied the Antediluvians and Xaviar, being the Justicar at that time, fought what he believed was an Antediluvian (again, this was a yes and no situation). He was so pissed off that the elders disregarded his claims that he took the entire clan out, and his political position allowed him to back up his claim. Many left, out of respect for the elder, but many remained. All this background is on Clan Novel Gangrel, it's really good.
        The backstory of it I know, that's not the point.

        Xaviar left the Camarilla. Ok. What does that means, he went to live alone in the woods? It seems to be the case, so it makes sense for him, but isn't a big deal.

        How did he took the entire clan out? Only the Tremere has a centralized organization within which this even makes sense. His political position allowed what and how, if the clan barely pays heed to elders more than a stone throw away? Why the clan most given to self sufficiency followed him at all, even if they respected him? And to where, the same grove in the woods? What does a clan independence from a Sect even looks like? And why didn't the Camarilla simply chose a new Gangrel Justicar right away?

        A Justicar isn't a member of the Inner Council or something. One of them leaving just means the position was vacated. Where's the Camarilla law that says if a Justicar steps down you can't replace them with the people who stay and call it a day? It isn't a good background. It may be a good story, as I already said the Week of Nightmares also is, but the result of it being depicted as Clan Gangrel left the Camarilla doesn't make any sense in any way.

        Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
        To be honest, the Clan Gangrel doing a walk-out from the Camarilla would work best if it were contextualised as the Clan making it plain that they work with the Camarilla, they do not work for the Ivory Tower: this being not necessarily intended as a permenant state of affairs but as a Power Move to demonstrate Gangrel self-sufficiency and demonstrate that the Camarilla is useful, but not so overwhelmingly essential that it can simply dictate terms.
        ​The problem is that this is not how the Camarilla work.

        You could say that this is like treating the Camarilla as a government when it is the UN.

        What the Camarilla does isn't to control cities. Technically it doesn't control any territory at all. Instead, it is an understanding between Princes/Primogen. Whomever controls a Domain because they have the power and age to do so, accepts the Traditions and a couple other rules, namely Conclaves and Justicars, even then only to a certain degree. Otherwise they just keep in touch. Beyond that decisions are non-binding: no Prince is under any obligation to do whatever they don't want to. Other Princes may decide they'll take care of a troublesome colleague if they need, but that's less Camarilla Law and more people in contact self-organizing.

        Belonging to the Camarilla just means you accept the Traditions are a good idea and everyone should follow them. Even recognizing Conclaves and Justicars is completely optional (although probably healthy). Actually, not even that, it just means you live in an area where the local powers do that. Just as you can't personally leave the UN without physically leaving UN-abiding countries, leaving the Camarilla, from a personal level, isn't a thing except as self-exile.

        Camarilla's power before V5 isn't about dictating terms and has never been. It's about influence. The Inner Circle isn't a real institution, it's a conspiracy of sorts of the most influential Princes in the sect. That's all. They get things done because they have influence, not because anyone is required by some law to follow them. The Conclave is non-biding, it's just that paying heed to the collective will of lots of vampires is usually good policy. Usually. Justicars have authority in any city only when they're accepted by the local Prince, and they have been denied or expelled many times, even if letting them in is usually a good idea.

        So not belonging to the Camarilla in a non-V5 context is a weird thing to even say. By their reconing, everyone is a member in the sense that everyone is bound to follow the Traditions. The Sabbat is not Camarilla because it disregards the Traditions and actively try to control territory where they'll implement their beliefs. The Inconnu are not Camarilla because they do, actually, live alone in the woods or whatever. The Anarchs aren't even not Camarilla most of the time, that's the case only when they manage to control territories where they ban at least one Tradition.

        Most vampires don't even know the Camarilla exists, it's a concept, not a government. If you live within a Domain and by the Traditions, whether or not it's by choice, then whatever you call yourself means nothing, you're Camarilla for all intents and purposes that matter.

            I have held this opinion for about thirty years but it's one that I think I am about the only person who holds this. The only reason the Sabbat hate the Path of Evil Revelations and demon worship is because they know demons enslace them and are better at this than the Sabbat. The Sabbat are for freedom and demon worship just enslaves you.

            And...it's not a very persuasive argument.

            It exposes the hypocrisy of the Sabbat and confronts them with a conundrum. "Why DO I not embrace black magic and sell my soul if it gives me power? If I don't care about freedom in the slightest but allying with the biggest dog on the block?"

            It exposes how low vampires are on the totem pole and how much the Sabbat's own philosophy doesn't stand up under scrutiny
