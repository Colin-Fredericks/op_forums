    Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
    Paths make the horror, and serve as a great analogy of how shit goes to fuckery
    Humanity keeps vampires nice. It restrains them, it keeps them from doing really bad things... that greatly gets in the way of horror. Imagine a slasher flick where the killer wants to kill his victims as painlessly and as humanely as possible, or better yet, spends half the run time angsting about the one or two murders he plans to commit... that's not anywhere near so scary as a psycho sexual torture enthusiast. As the later can be just as introspective as the former, the former doesn't have advantages. But most vampires aren't slashers, they're worse, they're politicians. A greedy politician with a moral bottom line is bad, but it's not horror. It becomes horror when his morality is entirely alien to the common people and he's willing or even delighted to screw everyone else in his ambition. Horror comes from the unknown. It comes from extremes, it comes from separation. An Emo is more a threat to himself than to the rest of humanity, how does he scare other people? "Personal horror" is personal and it's not going to work if you thrust your own stupid issues onto someone else, but a horror that targets everyone? That's worthwhile.

    Except Paths are mostly used to side-step all the difficulties Humanities are meant to put on your Vampire. From my limited experience, they were far more a way to shift the game entirely away from horror for some sort of torture-porn or action flick.

    And I have a hard time understanding how Humanity is preventing you to be a scheming bastard even willing to have people murdered. Perhaps it's because I'm mostly a Requiem player but it isn't really that hard to build up a pure monster on Humanity only. In fact I find it far more frightening because that's what the so-called "monsters" of the real world are : humans. They don't have Paths and what not, they don't need to act in horrendous ways.

                                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                To be frank, the majority of the **** 1/2star rating comes immediately from the fact that it was absolutely absurd to have to wait as long as we did for a Sabbat supplement. Not only did we have to have the Tzimisce and Lasombra waited on in supplements away from the main book but we also had the Sabbat exiled to nowhere (the Middle East being a place I could reach in a day) as if it wasn't a third pillar sect. The Sabbat main book should have always been one of the original three supplements for V:TM 5th Edition.
                                I'm not sure why "this book should have been front and center, but wasn't," means it gets an improved rating.

                                I don't feel comfortable downgrading a book's rating for what it is NOT. Speaking as a guy who reviews books as part of his profession (I review at Booknest.EU and have over 200 reviews there), I feel like you cannot be upset with a book for not being the way you want it.
                                But, it's the 5th edition of an RPG, not a novel. Besides the fact that it's ridiculous to say not to judge something on what it isn't without any context (if I book sells itself as a horror story, and isn't scary, are you going to give it a high rating simply because the prose is still good, and ignore it failing to be what it says it is? And novels that exist as parts of a series get judged negatively all the time for things they lack when it comes to how they interact with the larger setting they're part of), it exists as an update to existing material into a new edition. It's not just there to be read, but to be used by people that want to have the Sabbat in their V5 games. RPG books are tools, not just coffee table books. A beautiful car that can't make it to the end of a block without breaking down should be judged for being a bad car however good it looks, because you're not just reviewing something with form, but with function.

                                Ratings are always a summary of my overall positivity to a subject and not going to cover all the actual ups and downs of something. What I liked, I liked very much. What I didn't like, I pointed out in the main part of the review.
                                Which is... kinda useless as a reviewing system? "4.5/5 stars because I don't include flaws in my ratings," doesn't actually tell people how good the book is in total. What's the point of a review that isn't critical (in terms of thinking, not in terms of negativity) and only looks at the positives?

                                Originally posted by SuperSabbatST View Post
                                I'm sorry the assement about sabbat, lasombra, and larps is just incorrect.
                                To add to this a bit:

                                The perception of Lasombra ATs being popular in LARPs is because LARPs and other large group oriented play have a problem TT style play doesn't: they're large. If you have 100 players, they're going to want ways to differentiate themselves. While every Clan has internal variety, it's natural for players to start looking for unusual options to set their characters apart.

                                However, when you're dealing with large group play, you have to keep unusual and rare parts of the game.... well unusual and rare or the tone of the game quickly becomes unmanageable. These things also have a tendency to be disruptive in the wrong hands, and thus there's additional pressure to be careful who gets to play them. So you have to balance things like making sure a few people can to have access to special things (oh the cries of favoritism if you only let one person play a rare Clan or Bloodline member), the need to maintain the feel of the game, and make sure the people that do play them don't abuse the privilege.

                                So what happens is that things like Lasombra ATs became desirable and coveted more than actually being popular. Only so many people are going to get to play one in your LARP. And there's a strong psychological pressure in most people to want to grab for the rare stuff if they can get it (after all, if you don't try for the rare you can still play the normal, but if you just play the normal you never get the rare).

                                        Originally posted by Ur-Than View Post
                                        Except Paths are mostly used to side-step all the difficulties Humanities are meant to put on your Vampire.
                                        If a player is just trying to sidestep an aspect of the game, you should consider that this player may want something different from the game. If a proposed experience in the game isn't attracting this player, maybe this player should be allowed to have fun in another way. If the ways players want to have fun aren't compatible and with no compromise, then maybe this particular group shouldn't play yet.

                                        Those things have nothing to do with Paths or rules.

                                        Paths add options to the game. They are tools. They can be used in several ways to bring other kinds of horror or drama to the table. They can also be used just as an excuse to ignore an aspect of the game, but if you want to do that you'll find ways to in Humanity too. As an ST or a player, the best way to deal with this is to discuss with your group the matter and decide if and how Paths should work in the game.

                                        Masquerade's Humanity have little to do with Requiem's Humanity, by the way, especially since Requiem 2nd ed. Despite all the superficial similarities the devil is, indeed, in the details. Same score, similar-but-not-so-much "sins", other rules for it going up or down, and the incredibly useful rule to get a supernatural weirdness in exchange of ignoring a specific sin. They are worlds apart and Requiem's Humanity is, indeed, wonderful. But don't belong in Masquerade because their themes are similar, but again, the devil is in the details.

                                        By the way, and that's just an anecdote, I used to be fiercely opposed to Paths. I hated them and the Sabbat as merely shitty stereotypes and power fantasies. With time I learned how much I was wrong, and started to appreciate what they can bring to my games. Yet, for one reason or another, as a player I never used anything but Humanity, not even when playing Sabbat. But other players being on Paths don't really bother me.
