    Originally posted by Black Fox View Post

    You must have a great ST (congrats if that is you) at your table and good players at the table.

    While I agree with everything you say in principle, in practice I've seen combat oriented characters often take over the game and dominate the other PCs. It takes a skilled ST to handle them and create the situations you describe. (Or to increase the stakes / change the story for them because they get noticed by more powerful vampires who can use such pawns or want to eliminate such a threat.) Then if such a ST does exist and is able to handle that PC so it is not unbalancing for the game, often the player of the unkillable combat monster throws a hissy fit and makes things uncomfortable for everyone else at the table. It leaves a bad taste in everyone's mouth even if that player later leaves.

    In my experience, the players who want to build the unkillable combat monster do not take "losing" in such ways gracefully and typically want to burn everything down with them. They don't tend to be the mature players. So players who do build such PCs (for whatever reason), yet accept any bad consequences from their lack of other useful skills and does not turn every conflict into "kill them all", are very rare.

    I don't have this kind of problem now with my gaming groups, but I had to churn throw a good number of them when I was an early VtM gamer.

    So this was my experience exactly, and believe I talked about this in an old post. Not all the D&D type players were able to make the switch. There were two combat junkies who wanted combat, combat, combat. One player felt the need to kill off party members as normal weekly activity. If a player puts everything into combat and you are playing even a bit more powerful vampires the player who didn't really stands no chance. If a player decides he wants to kill fellow players it's really not that hard. What is the point of letting people who think it's roll-playing instead of role-playing wreck all the work you put into chronicles on a regular basis?

    The best thing to do is drop the players who don't fit in your game.

            MyWifeIsScary

            You remind me how much I dislike Thaumaturgy for Vampires I don't want to look all that up.

            Let's just pretend you said Obtenebration 5 instead, or Protean 5 or Obfuscate 5, or Dominate 3 really. These choices will trivialize a good chunk of encounters in there own way and can be used as a starting character. I would suggest you insist on a little creativity from session to session. If it is a player that just sweeps the leg in street fighter the whole time is he really having fun after awhile? I don't think most players will get upset if you hit him with a well thought out counter from an opponent. Players like to be challenged.

            Doing the same thing over and over again will become noticed if it directed at fellow Kindred or even Ghouls. Those in power who are losing allies will look at the victims, sus out motivations and who is benefitting. Figure out who not only had motive, but the ability to pull it off. They will use disciplines themselves to unmask their opponents.

            If they keep taking out fellow vampires it will be noticed probably sooner rather than later and people will deal with the problem. It's pretty easy for an NPC to kill a player with a little prep time as well.

            I was going to say set the story up as a mystery or matter of diplomacy and bargaining, but I'm sure you already do various scenarios. If the players go too solution is to kill his fellow vampires they can't really complain when they meet a sticky end. Violence begets violence and all that. If their character is killed for taking out several people in an organization, they might think of other ways to do things next time. Call it a learning experience. Hacking the game to kill others with "prep time" does not make you powerful enough to fend off the consequences of your actions if they piss off the wrong people.

                    Invulnerable PCs is a side effect of the "white room" mentality.

                    Someone looks at some groups of mechanics and realizes you can put them together to form a new interaction. The issue is that they are often thinking of only one situation. Such as direct face to face combat, but that falls apart if the opponent is using a different approach.

                    Protean 2 / Celerity 5 / Potence 5 means pretty much anything nearby is shredded, so you use a sniper who is about a mile away. If someone is a lower generation level than you and has Dominate, then you only interact with them via phone calls. If someone has a lot of influence over the city, then you destroy the lives of every mortal that owes them a favor or allegiance.

                    Even if a character is largely unkillable, it doesn't mean that they can't be hurt or their interests can't be messed with. Furthermore, such unkillable characters are not omniscient so if you are doing something against them, unless you are very sloppy, there is no way to know who is attacking their interests or powerbase. Finally, they can't be everywhere at once, meaning that if they decide to protect a given thing or person, they can't be everywhere else.

                    So yeah, you might not be able to kill them in a head to head fight, but you can destroy every mortal connection they have, destroy their havens, crash their businesses, and decimate their influence. Eventually they will either move on to greener pastures or they will get isolated enough that someone from their past might see an opportunity to take them out and they might have a way around the invulnerable combo.

                        Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                        So some combinations of powers make individuals virtually unkillable and yet they remain super dangerous. How do you deal with that?
                        Characters with strong and definitive protection, avoidence, resistance, or evasion qualities or powers can still be challanged socially or through the use of environment. Especially if they are separated from their supporters, followers, or allies.
                        Research, scouting, or espionage can be used to find out what sort of physical upgrade or mystical protection will be used by an opponent. Standard tactics like use of extensive resources, planned preparation, and creating distractions can still be relevant.
                        Vampires using the mentioned powers or abilities can still be challanged directly by dedicated specialist.

                                What exactly are you complaining about? If everything is auto-balanced mechanically, then everything is in a permanent stalemate. The game of chess is balanced in the numbers of pieces each player has and the limited number of spaces on the board. Conversely a game like Poker has static values of the cards, but your ability to bluff (roleplay) and the size of your chip stack varies from play to play creating a dynamic game environment. In short, imbalance can often create the fun people are looking for in a "balanced game". Game design is weird like that.

                                If any game system is descending into a Dungeon Crawl with no greater depth than kill everything and take their stuff... then either that is exactly what the group is wanting or the group lacks bigger ideas of game play. I have lost track of the number of D&D games I have played where the majority of game play was composed of non-murder hobo activities. Game mechanics are just tools and ingredients, how you use them matters, just like in cooking.

                                Try building a game around using & building Backgrounds rather than Disciplines as your main way of getting things done and you will see what I am talking about. You will have to completely rethink how you approach encounters, how you tell stories, how you create tension, and all of that without actually changing a single rule or mechanic.
