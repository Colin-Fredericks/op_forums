    To switch topics:

    I think one of the interesting things to speculate regarding the Second Inquisition is the logistics of the organization and whether it's rising or falling as a group by the time the campaign has started for 5th Edition.

    https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.p...hingOutCthulhu

    Basically, the question is whether or not the Second Inquisition's victories have emboldened them or exhausted them and that's going to determine the best way to use the organization from then on in your campaign.

    The books don't say it so it's up to the ST whether:

    1. Exhausted: The attack on the Tremere Chantry took every bit of their resources, influences, and probably direct manpower to pull off. Even if you think they never should have pulled it off and needed an inside man, it's possible that such a victory resulted in the SI finally being taken seriously. The Camarilla and other forces could then launch their own counterattack on the agencies themselves let alone the people inside them to severely undermine or possibly even co-opt the group. London becomes the last city to fall or even any further cities that do fall are because they're being led around like a mouse in a maze to their eventual destruction.

    2. Emboldened: The Second Inquisition is an existential threat to Kindred as a whole. Kindred are coming together in larger cities because whole other cities are being wiped clean out. The Beckoning has gotten rid of the Ancients so the SI is able to handle the younger ones. If they don't find out a solution then vampires may be wiped off the face of the Earth completely someday.

    (Yay?)

    Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

        I still hold on to the -grand conspiracy- prospect.

        The Beckoning, the shrinking of the Camarilla, and the disappearance of the Sabbat are all to set the stage for the Second Inquisition.
        Too many Neonates. Too many 'liberals'. The fear of masquerade breaches was getting to an all-time high. And so the inner-circle of the cam, who are secretly buddy-buddy with the higher ups of the Sabbat (Cardinals? What're they called?) and no doubt some shadowy methuselah have aranged to massacre most of the neonates and "set the slate clean". The Beckoning is really a method to remove the dissenters (or vampires are leaving for their own safety because they know shit's going down) and the Gehenna war's a death trap.

        Once the 'problem children' have been dealt with (survivors are worthy) The Cam will perhaps begin embracing again, encouraging stricter standards, and will retake the barren anarch territories. After killing off the diehards, The surviving elder Sabbat who mostly used religion and antedeluvian fears to control the masses mostly won't mind filtering into the Camarilla.

        And then there will be order again.

        Now of course, this isn't going to go as well as planned, but it'll mostly work. What this means for the SI is that, well, it's not an impossible coalition of rogue agents who've somehow connected to rogue agents of other agencies, we have vampires all starting local-ish hunter groups at the same time, and we have a lot of misinformation thrown around to get it to work. We're mostly talking discharged agents and false agencies masquerading as real agencies with their unwitting dupes (a strategy often employed by those in corporate espionage; "I'm with the Federal police, we want you spy on your boss for your country"), Add in some mercenaries, militia-cults, hunter organization turncoats and homegrown, vampire trained, brainwashed hunters and you've got yourself a frightful 'inquisition.
        Best part is, with disciplines being what they are, and vampires being what they are, if someone gets caught making their own hunter group, people'll just assume that they're taking advantage of the chaos, not that this is happening everywhere and the chaos was entirely constructed by vampires on the local level. It's not that hard to plant evidence of a genuine arcanum-SoL-BigGov involvment in any case.

                    Post-ww1 Man-portable flamethrowers are actually really impressive. Don't be so hard on them. A backpack is more than enough for a nest of vampires.
                    Dragonsbreath rounds are a novelty; the burning stuff in them is light and small and completely loses the power of the shotgun. They'd provoke a fear-frenzy, but they're not that effective in killing vampires themselves.
                    Incendiary rounds are like... actual -I want to start fires- Incendiary rounds are generally only found for big bullets, like .50 BMG and above. If you're using .50 BMG and above, you don't need incendiary rounds to kill vampires. They're paste. If you wanted fire-bullets in an ordinary assault rifle, you're outa luck.

I'm not an expert though. You could certainly have good, pulpy fun by pouring it over a sleeping vampire.

                    Flaming spears might be a good way to -herd- a vampire to capture it, but for killing one, a flamethrower would be better.
                    Fire arrows, IRL, were garbage for killing people. You basically sacrificed a weighted pointy end and aerodynamics to get the fire, and There was like a 1% chance you'll start a fire using them, and you made up for that by shooting a lot of them at once. Maybe modern materials might make up for the shortcommings of historical items, but you'd be better off using a flamethrower or AM rifle.

                    I would stick to flamethrowers and explosives, or fluids/gas traps.

However, its willingness to use Therugy and closeness with the government as well as hatred of the undead has had unintended consequences:

                                                            * The number of True Faith members of the Inquisition is growing smaller.

                                                            * The Inquisition also fought evil spirits, wraiths, werewolves, and actual demons as part of its duties. It's lately been 99% vampires and the organization is starting to feel like they're missing the forest for a particularly nasty set of trees.
