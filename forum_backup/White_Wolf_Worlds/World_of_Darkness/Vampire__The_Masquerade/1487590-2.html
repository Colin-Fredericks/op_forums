                                I like the Cappadocians as a clan. I like many of the NPCs of the clan. And I like much of the lore behind them.

                                However, one thing I have never liked is how active the Cappadocian antedeluvian is, and how ancient and active so many of his childer are. According to official Lore, Japheth was over 8000 years old and still active at the time of the Conspiracy of Isaac. That is very different from how the other clans and their methusalehs are portrayed. It's a jarring element of the setting since we have to make an exception for him that isn't as present in the other clans.

                                Some of the other clans have similar problems - primarily the Lasombra and Tzimisce. I guess this is what happens when the game tries to deal with the events of the Anarch Revolt and the diablerie of some of the antedeluvians at this time.

                                I would be a lot less bothered if they pushed some of these characters down a generation or two, and included more flavor text on how certain ancients had only recently woke from torpor and were not known to be active. That's how I would handle it in my own chronicles. I just prefer certain powerful generations who were embraced long, long ago (like in the Bronze Age or earlier) to simply not be active. If a character is over 2000-3000 years old, it should be sleeping somewhere safely in torpor UNLESS it's recently woke up.

                                So I think Lazarus is fine as a character, but I'd probably make him a 5th or even 6th generation vampire who was a doted, precocious "grandchilde" or so of Cappadocious rather than a favorite childe.

                                                    Originally posted by Father Enoch View Post
                                                    The "Crypto" part of Cryptocurrency is meant to imply secrecy,
                                                    That's indeed a misconception, actually.

                                                    "Crypto" in cryptocurrency refers just to the fact that it uses a cryptographic system that relies on the community of users itself instead of a certifying authority. This is a purely technical reference.

                                                    And while this use as "dark money" does exist and has a number of defenders, the actual technology does the opposite: it makes the validation chain of transaction public, which means that anyone can audit the entire life of every unit at any time with little effort (the transfer must be certified by cryptography, the log itself isn't encrypted).

                                                    What makes anonymity possible is that while the wallets that hold them are identified, the owners aren't. But this isn't any technical challenge, requirement or anything, it is just a fact of the basic system as much as the basic system of your computer identifies your equipment by a unique code, but has no built-in slot to say it belongs to you. You can literally solve this with a spreadsheet.

                                                    If you note down somewhere who's the owner of a wallet, something many Exchanges already do to keep track of their customers and offer some services and safety, then there is no anonymity. A government requiring an Exchange to record this information is completely feasible. Outlawing ownership or trade with unidentified wallets is perfectly doable, and actually easy to implement due to the public aspect of the chain. That's why China is investing in developing its own state owned Crypto, because it will be stupidly easy for the government to track everything.

                                                    However you feel about the matter of privacy on that, and despite the system being intended to make the transaction itself impossible to stop (because it has no central register), granting privacy is not the goal of the system itself and it actually needs the opposite to work.
