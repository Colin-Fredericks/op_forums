        Clanbook: Tremere (part the second)

        Continuing.

        The Nexus
        So, in parapsychology and ghost lore, there are poltergeists - spirits that cause physical disturbances such as loud noises, moved or broken objects, and minor physical attacks. And some theories tie these instances to young adolescent or prepubescent girls, either as a fraud to get attention or some sort of psychic manifestation of rampant female emotions. If you've seen the 1982 film Poltergeist (and if you haven't, you really really should), then you are somewhat familiar with the concept. Stephen King's novel Carrie and its 1976 film adaption also tie into certain aspects of the latter theory to a degree. Our character is one of those young girls, and the parapsychologist her parents asked to investigate turned out to be a Tremere who was very interested in an apparently psychic child. One Amber Alert later, she's now a vampire, forever stuck in the age of apparently 14. (The image looks a little younger than this, and what I understand of poltergeist theories is that they start are supposed to start younger than that.) Apparently being a vampire isn't too bad for her, but she's resentful of having her teen years stollen from her. A lot of her mystical research involves trying to find a way to age herself. I could easily see her becoming very rebellious and acting out if this doesn't bear any fruit. Her Nature is Architect, but her Demeanor is Child, apparently because acting younger and more helpless than she really is is a useful strategy for manipulation and survival. She has four dots in Perception, Wits, and Charisma, and three each in Appearance and Dexterity. Strength is only one dot. Abilities include four dots in Masquerade and three in Empathy. Then two each in Acting, Alertness, Animal Ken, Computer, Investigation, and Occult. She's also got a couple of one dot abilities, including Mimicry for some reason. Her Disciplines are focused entirely on Thaumaturgy, I presume to make use of Movement of the Mind. Her Backgrounds are in Generation and Mentor. Humanity is at nine, as she has a Conscience of five (I guess no one wants to think about a cold blooded little girl) and Self-Control at four. Willpower is seven. I feel these may be too high. Over all, I rather like this concept. The problem with child vampires aside, I think it's interesting as both an example of how far the clan is willing to go in its quest for power, and a nice personal tragedy that echoes one of the story lines in Anne Rice's Interview with the Vampire. I do think it would be a bit stronger if it worked more with the idea of a character that is a mature and ambitious adult mind in an innocent looking child form, and if the traits were fine tuned to reflect that. But otherwise, I think it's a pretty good character.

        The Politico
        In the world of politics, there are figureheads out front, and the real power players in the back. This character always wanted to be one of the power players. And he's really good at it too. A natural manipulator with a keen sense for politics and for other peoples weaknesses. He knows exactly how to build his own candidates up and tear the opposing candidates down using any means necessary. And his skill attracted a like minded Tremere from the old days who decided he'd make a perfect apprentice. He excels at the clan's political games, but plays the dangerous game of making himself look good at the expense of older vampires. I rather like the character art. He looks like one of those generic guys in expensive suits you see in the background of every upper class political event. (I'm a little embarrassed to admit that this is the first time in 25 years I noticed that he's holding a martini in his hand. I think I'd always thought it was a pen or something in front of a white pattern part of his suit.) His Manipulation is maxed out at five dots. Charisma, Perception and Wits are at three each. Politics, Bureaucracy, and Subterfuge are at four dots each, which is very fitting. Acting and Etiquette are three each and Intimidation and Leadership are at two. This is rounded out with some one dot abilities that are all useful to the concept and feel like they fit. His Disciplines are two in Dominate and one in Auspex. This actually makes him one of only two characters in the book without Thaumaturgy (the Explorer is the other) and I've very happy to see a Tremere character who feels extremely Tremere-like without actually needing blood magic. He's got three dots in Contacts and two in Influence. I feel these could be higher. Humanity is seven and Willpower is six. I like this character a lot. Besides my quibble about his Backgrounds, all of his stats perfectly fit the character. And I like the fact that he fits so perfectly in to the clan archetype of power broker and manipulator without having anything to do with magic or the occult. This is probably one of my favorite ones in the books.

        The Professional Witch
        Some people practice magic for the belief in it. Others do it for power. And then there are those who do it for the money. That's this character. She's from a family of carneys who decided to settle down in New England so her mother could be a fortune telling witch for the tourists at Salem. When her dad left, it was just the two of them and her mother taught her everything she knew, and every so often, she might get an actual flash of something from other people. Eventually her reputation attracted the attention of a Tremere, who decided to embrace her. She's mostly adjusted fairly well, and continues to work as a professional "witch" and fortune teller. She's also trying to further develop the latent talents she possessed while alive. Charisma and Appearance are both at four dots, with Perception and Wits at three each. It might help if her Manipulation was higher than two, but that's probably debatable. She has the Fortune Telling secondary ability at four dots, and three each in Acting, Subterfuge, and Occult. This is rounded out with twos in Streetwise, Investigation, Linguistics and Sleight of Hand, and a few ones. Her Disciplines are two dots in Auspex and one in Thaumaturgy, which fits the character pretty well. I could see her getting extremely dangerous once she learns Dominate. Backgrounds are spread evenly between Allies, Contacts, Generation and Resources. She doesn't have Fame, which is a little odd given that the write up mentions her having a reputation. Humanity is eight and Willpower is seven. While I would prefer to see a few of these points shuffled around a little, I still like this character a lot. She seems intelligent, interesting and fun to play, and I like how she leans into the clan stereotype while at the same time subverting it. Another one that'll probably be my top three of the book.

        Thus ends part the second.

            I think it helped a lot that the early Vampire books had lots of art from Josh Timbrook, especially for the sample characters and NPC portraits. It provided a great deal of unity to the early game. It was cartoony, but every effective. And since anime influenced art hadn't exploded and become commonplace like it is now, it helped put the setting apart.

            I'll limit remarks on two characters from the Gangrel tribebook.

            I don't have the issues with the Babe in the Woods as you do. You say that you don't see how to integrate her into a coterie. I think it'd be the easiest thing imaginable. It's very much the kind of the character template you would give a brand new person to the game (or roleplaying, I know Vampire was the first RPG many people ever played that had zero interest in RPGs before). They don't need to have any starting knowledge. They learn about the game and its setting by playing. How the character develops is completely up to the specific player. It doesn't have a "theme" that all players would try to fill. I think it's great in that.

            I want to mention the Lupine Impersonator because I don't think it was a problem for the game to try to include elements of its entire gamelines. It is a world where vampires, werewolves, wizards, ghosts, and faeries exist. I am not a fan of the Monster Mash genre, but acknowledging that those elements exist and finding a good way to make reference to them isn't a turn off to me. I just don't want them to hijack the game.

            The problem with the Lupine Impersonator concept though is that as written, it can't work because werewolves have these other kinds of forms, and not being able to show them is a huge disadvantageous. But I don't think it is impossible to do so. First, it's not possible to be your typical Gaian Garou in a pack. That obviously fails. However, there are plenty of other concepts such a vampire could pretend to be - the kind of Garou known as Ronin. That is a social disadvantage, but better than getting killed. But it also limits how how much you can learn. It's also possible to play as a Lost Cub. that let's you get away with a lot - but also with the problem your new lupine friends want to bring you to the sept to educate you. You'd need a good story to stop that - but being adamant about rejecting Garou society could do it. But it depends very much on the kind of lupines you first meet. And in an urban game, there are lupines you can encounter that may not give a damn. Bone Gnawers actually have alliances with various kinds of vampires. And Glass Walkers have better things to do than kill vampires, so they like to keep the peace. But since this is a Gangrel template, we can expect it'd be actual wilderness lupines the impersonator runs against. Much more likely to get killed. But perhaps the impersonator ran into Children of Gaia, a lone Silent Strider, or a pack with Unicorm or Chimera totems. People less interested in killing others outright, and perhaps more respectful of letting someone find their own way.

            In other words, even in a Werewolf perspective, I think you can make it work. You just need an ST willing to craft it as an exception.

            I think your words about it feeling more like a Werewolf NPC (or crossover game where players come from different game lines) than a playable Vampire PC in a VtM game is more appropriate. But again, I think this is more of a template for someone to start with. A blank slate so the player can eventually do their own thing, but with one exception. This fits with a player who is attracted to the Gangrel because of their ties to nature. So they want to run in the woods like a wolf and do things like that. And if the coterie ever needed to have someone lead the group into the wilderness, but avoid the lupines, this is the perfect opportunity to allow them to do so. So I think this can be the basis for a starting PC.

            But they had to leave that life because recently they became spooked (they're asking too many questions!) and moved to the city where they ran into the other PCs. This background gives them, more or less, an alternate identity they can use if they run into lupines and present as a "get out of jail free" card. ST just needs to do a little work to allow that and write up how they were able to successfully impersonate a lupine, as that is something the ST needs to justify. A player simply can't do it and make it work because they don't know how to fit it into the setting in a way the ST would like. Plus it opens things for an ST to use this PC's background as a way to introduce future plots. Perhaps not even as a wilderness lupine, as some urban lupine in Central Park or whatever recognizes the "ronin".

                I think you just need to ignore the Humanity ratings in early edition work in the same way that one ignores Rage in Werewolf. Issues like Humanity compared to their moral actions, and the Curse's affect of interactions with humans, just don't make sense for a lot of NPCs.                             Now that I've had a chance to read a lot of these template synopsis, it seems to me that a lot of these come from the "spontaneous embrace" school. The sire did not originally intend to embrace a new childe, but ended up doing so for whatever reason.

                            I actually think this is a problem because for most vampires it takes a lot of effort to gain permission for the embrace. And if they're anarchs or whatever, an illegal embrace is grounds to get you killed when you otherwise might be tolerated.

                            Of course there are runarounds the ST can employ. If you're running the Chicago city sourcebook, you can use Modius post-approval as an escape. Or if you're originally from the Anarch Free State where they might not care about that. Or the sire subsequently flees with their childe and ends up in a new city. Or if the sire is prestigious enough, maybe they have prior approval in the back pocket, or can get the Prince to approve post facto. But it takes effort.

                            If there were as many spontaneous embraces in the setting as the templates indicate, you can see why Princes employ scourges. It really affects the setting.

                                                    Clanbook: Tremere (part the third)

                                                    And our final chapter.

                                                    The Psych Major
                                                    A college psychology student motivated by a desire to help the mentally ill, only to be embraced by the Tremere in the belief that he may have some sort of latent prophetic ability. Except that he apparently doesn't. So while his mentor and other prod him to start having prophetic dreams again, he mainly acts as a clan liaison to the local Malkavians, taking advantage of his training and natural empathy. He's moderately successful at it, but really hates being a vampire and keeps contemplating self destruction. He's got four dots in Charisma and Appearance, and three dots each in Perception and Wits. I feel like these are maybe backwards, especially as the character image is of disheveled looking near-burn out. I'd see him more as having 4/2/2 in Social and 4/3/3 in Mental. He's got four dots in Science, I presume with a specialty in Psychology, but it doesn't say. (It also doesn't use any of the secondary abilities.) Medicine, Empathy, and Investigation are all three dots. The rest are single dot skills (including Brawl, Dodge, Melee and Firearms, somewhat oddly), save two dots each in Linguistics and Drive (I presume he drives stick shift.) He's got the usual one each in Disciplines, plus an extra dot in Auspex. I feel like this is one of those characters that didn't need to have Thaumaturgy. Backgrounds are Contacts, Generation, Mentor, and Resources. Humanity is eight and Willpower seven. Overall, I think this is an OK character. He could probably benefit from some point adjustments. If he has a major issue, it's that he just doesn't seem to have that "Tremere" feel to him. He seems to lack the sort of ambition, be it personal, political or whatever, that I tend to associate with the clan. It's not an insurmountable hurdle, just one that holds me back from liking this character as much as I think I could have.

                                                    The Pyramid Climber
                                                    Another of those late 80s archetypes, but one that's in some ways eternal. A Wall Street executive and trader right out of the movies, with the full on "Greed is Good! Now get out of the way of my Porsche, peasants!" attitude. He wants it all, and he wants it now, but he's definitely ready to put in the time and effort to get it. While not above keeping an eye out for weaknesses to exploit in others, he much prefers the direct route of forcing his idea down other people's throats through sheer force of personality. As such, he's got four dots each in Charisma and Manipulation, with Mental secondary. Strangely, he's got three dots in Stamina at the expense of having only one in Dexterity. (Usually these are the kind of guys to be playing racketball at the office gym at least once a week.) He's only got twos in Bureaucracy, Finance and Politics, but three each in Etiquette, Intimidation, Leadership and Subterfuge. I think some of those abilities could stand a boost. Disciplines are again the three spread evenly, with an extra dot in Dominate. I really feel this is a character that could've done with just Dominate - maybe a single dot in Thaumaturgy if you wish - and spent those seven points on more abilities or backgrounds. His Backgrounds are just Generation five and Mentor three, which puzzles me to no end. This is a character that should have Resources, and possible Influence as well. Maybe even a Retainer and some Contacts. Humanity is seven and Willpower eight. Overall, this is another one of my favorites from the book. He has all that ambition I felt the last character was lacking and then some. I just think he needs some trait adjustments to better reflect the character.

                                                    The Underestimated
                                                    This is a female character who, in spite of being pretty intelligent and well educated, has always been judged mainly by her appearance. This includes by the Tremere, who apparently embraced her to use as a "honey trap" or something for mortals they wanted to manipulate. (This seems like something a ghoul would be just as suitable for, really.) But she's always, both in life and undeath, wanted to be in the back rooms cutting deals and taking part in the real action of power and influence. Everyone, even vampires, have always dismissed her as just a pretty face, and she's out to take full advantage of this as she begins her climb to the top using the skills everyone forgets she has. Her Appearance is maxed out at five dots, with three in Charisma and Manipulation. Mental is secondary, with Perception and Wits at three each. Besides a three in Politics, all of her skills are just twos and ones, including Acting, Leadership, Subterfuge, Etiquette, Bureaucracy, Finance, and Investigation. So she's reasonably solid but doesn't stand out. Disciplines are one each, which seems fitting. Backgrounds are two each in Contacts, Generation and Resources. Humanity is eight and Willpower is nine. Even thought this character does lean on certain gender stereotypes, especially ones from a few decades ago, I rather like the idea of someone who isn't taken seriously for superficial reasons planning to prove everyone wrong. I think it ties well into the clan's overall idea of ambition, as well as certain aspects of the whole "stodgy and hidebound traditionalists" stereotype they tend(ed) to have. If I were to play this character, I'd probably shift a few trait points around, but other than that, I like it.

                                                    And so that is the Tremere. Overall, I think this was a fairly solid effort. A lot of these are stronger characters than I remember them being, which is a good thing. The Politico, the Professional Witch, and the Pyramid Climber are my three favorites of the book. All three look fun to play and seem to exemplify what I think of when I look for a Tremere. The Cultist, Nexus, and Underestimated are also pretty good ones I think, and the Conspiracy Theorist and Explorer are both pretty solid ideas. I think the two weakest, at least from my perspective, at the Child of Tremere and the Psych Major. Both seem to lack any sense of ambition or goals, and the Child is especially lacking in personality. Which is a shame, as both are good ideas for a Tremere character. I just think they need some refinement.
                                                    One recurring bit I've noticed, and I suspect that this is going to become even more of a thing when I get to the non-Camarilla clans, is that all but two characters have Thaumaturgy, and in a few cases it feels like they have it just because they're Tremere and it's available. This ties into something I mentioned earlier regarding the Tzimisce, about a clan's hook getting confused with the clan's focus.
                                                    25 years ago, I was still kind of hung up on the Tremere's hook, so a lot of my efforts at coming up with character concepts tended to fixate on the "they're wizards" trope. I think I remember being a little surprised at the lack of some sort of vampire druid type based off of the Order of the Naturists secret society presented in the book. I think in hindsight, I'm a little surprised there was no Frankenstein type.
                                                    In terms of representation, we have three females, all of whom are in sort of "traditional" female roles - little girl, witch, and attractive eye candy. The Professional Witch and Underestimated are somewhat sexualized, but I don't think that's entirely outside the realm of what the characters are meant to represent. They all appear to be white, though I think the Explorer and the Underestimated could be interpreted as black if you wanted to.
                                                    I've long considered the Tremere's overall theme to be that of ambition and the pursuit of power, and I think most of these characters fit into that fairly well. The only exceptions are the Child of Tremere and the Psych Major, and that's part of why I consider them the weakest of the group. The others all have some clearly defined goals, seeking personal power for their own advancement or just to ensure their own sense of individual freedom or understanding. And I think that's what makes a good Tremere and makes them stand out. The Ventrue want power and influence for some sort of noblesse oblige and the Lasombra just to dominate the world around them, but the Tremere want it because it's there, and they deserve it.
