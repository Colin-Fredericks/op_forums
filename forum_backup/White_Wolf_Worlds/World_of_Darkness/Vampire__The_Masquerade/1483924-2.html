    Worth noting that the chance for losing Humanity when you use an Oblivion power is not all that high. It's a 1 in 5 chance for getting a stain, sure, but you get to make a remorse roll at the end of every session. The lower the character's humanity, the more likely they're going to pass the remorse roll. So yeah, an oblivion user is probably not going to stay at Humanity 7, but once they drop down to Humanity 5 or so, the chance of losing more gets pretty small. "Accessing the Abyss inevitably erodes your soul" is a design choice you can agree with or not, but it's not like the mechanics of it guarantee that your character will be shredded into a Wight in short order. If you see Humanity solely as a character sheet resource then I can see why this would bother you, but if you see it as a framework for storytelling, then it's more about how using Oblivion changes your character and less about how it makes your character lose stats. You didn't pay points for Humanity in character generation, you just got handed 7 or 8. Oblivion users will have less over time. Shrug. If you think the rate of Humanity loss is too fast, it's really easy to just say you only get a stain on a Rouse check result of 1, and if Humanity erosion from contact with the Abyss is not a theme you want to explore you can just ignore the stain cost like others have suggested. The game police aren't going to arrest you. But I don't think it's crazy to say that Oblivion is different from other disciplines and has a cost--it's a discipline that clearly interacts with forces outside of the user.

        Originally posted by Random Goblin View Post
        Worth noting that the chance for losing Humanity when you use an Oblivion power is not all that high.
        It is still a burden with too many consequences for the character. It works well for a limited range of concepts, but not for everyone.

        Also worth noting, it won't be the only source of Stains, just the only one that doesn't depend on Chronicle Tenets and yet is guaranteed to come by regularly, as it is linked to a trait you paid for.

        Every time you take a Stain, the decision to use this power in the same session becomes harder. Using this power to do something that is worth a Stain by itself increases your risk significantly. If you use it frequently you're virtually guaranteed to take Stains regularly, and in a particularly unlucky session you may end up with two, three Stains at the end just from your own character sheet and dice rolls, or you stop to use the power altogether in the first Stain. The power, again, that you bought and is a clan staple.

        Those risks don't ever go away completely. Either you're conservative with your birthright when everyone else can use theirs all the time, or you're almost guaranteed to end every single session with a Stain. Or, from an in-character perspective, sense the effects of the Beast every damn week at least. It doesn't matter at this point that it becomes harder to fall as Humanity gets lower, you're playing against yourself all the time.

        Finally, if you get dangerously low, or just lower than your character concept, then no fancy description will change that Humanity costs XP. It doesn't "just" requires a shit-ton of in-game effort, without house-rules it also costs a lot of XP, stunting your character growth significantly. For, and I repeat, a clan staple.

        You'll just end up, one way or the other, with a character that can't use their signature power frequently and that actually behaves better than their Humanity rating indicates, since they need to compensate the extra Stains, which are exactly the opposite behavior you want to further in Hecata or Lasombra PCs if you want to follow the themes if the clans. It isn't impossible to think a character that fits the bill, but it is objectively bad game design for what it is meant to do.

            I'm not sure it's fair to create a dichotomy between Humanity as a mechanical element and a story element. It's both. There are some pretty significant mechanics attached to Humanity. Dropping from Humanity 8 to Humanity 4 (esp. considering the XP values involved) is a drastic mechanical change in your character's ability to interact with humans, and remain hidden among them to those that know what to look for as penalties on social rolls increase and what Blush of Life can accomplish decreases.

            As well, the talk of odds tends to assume that the only Stains you're risking are Oblivion based ones (and at that, seem to be assuming you don't use Oblivion powers above level 1 more than once a session). If the session's activities have left the whole coterie with two Stains, but the Oblivion user didn't have luck on their side and got two extra ones (or that was statistically average because they rolled for a rouse ~10 times over the course of the session to activate Oblivion powers), that's a much faster track to low Humanity.

            Something that's sort of an on-going sticking point in V5 mechanics discussions is that some of new mechanics introduced in V5 are not easily adapted to different play styles; esp. regarding how much a group likes to toss dice. The game doesn't have any built-in dials to help adjust for the fact that some play groups are going to find the risk of Oblivion Stains incredibly minor (don't roll a lot in general, use of Disciplines as a last resort, low ratio of scenes to sessions), while others can easily feel it way harder.

                    As far as the thematic side of things...

                    I'm not sure why to keep debating it, but it's a real stretch to say that channeling the power of the spiritual curse that made you into a vampire to have superhuman strength enough to rip a human in half (Potence) doesn't carry extra risk towards Stains, but using the "energy of Oblivion" to create shadow tentacles to do it instead does. The complaint a lot of people have is that it's completely incoherent with what Humanity and Stains are meant to represent (losing touch with your former human self on the slow slide to pure monster as you fail to reconcile the actions you take over time). Vampires are undead parasites leeching off humanity. All of their powers are "tainted" but the "energy" of the dead because... they're dead things unnaturally running around stealing energy from the living to keep themselves going.

                    It's also a new conceit to V5 that wasn't present in the previous editions. The lore was changed just to justify the extra cost to using Oblivion... which doesn't really demonstrate a strong reason to have an extra cost attached to it in practice.

                                Originally posted by SetiteFriend View Post
                                At the end, it is channeling power from where the dead go to die, Oblivion itself, having it be a costly preventing trivial uses isn't a bad thing imo. At the end, the Lasombra and Hecata have other equally valuable disciplines.
                                First, this is not a reason, this is a justification. The rules exist to enforce a theme, the lore to justify the rules. The lore doesn't impact if the rules are good or not.

                                Every clan has three Disciplines, one of them being a Signature of sorts. You don't need to follow the stereotype, but being able to is the whole point of the option. The concept of the Hecata is a Necromancer, the concept of the Lasombra is a Shadow Manipulator (the freaking clan's name is literally "Theshadow"). If the rule isn't conducive of making such characters, something is wrong. If the rule isn't giving the clan three usable Disciplines, the clan is crippled.

                                If the power and/or the clan has a specific theme attached to it, the role of the rule, from a design standpoint, must be to encourage the player to follow the theme, even if its fluff punishes the character. Again, you want Hecata to be Necromancers and Lasombra to play with shadows. You also want them to be particularly prone to both act more deviously and seem less Humane.

                                But by causing Stains, instead of making the player want to follow the theme, it tries to make the power force the theme. It backfires, as some players may want the extra challenge, but most will instead avoid the very themes the rule should be helping to foster. You said it yourself, instead of relying on it more you avoided your very signature power. It worked as a character fine, but by being contrary to the typical Lasombra.

                                Nothing against your game or your character at all, but by making you act against the actual theme just for the character to work fine, the rule failed. It is plain bad game design.

                                You want an example of how to make the rule actually foster the theme? Instead of causing Stains, Oblivion could be capped as the opposite of your Humanity (Humanity + Oblivion can't be more than 10) and in exchange it acts as a Conviction by itself once per session: your first act that would cause Stains causes 1 less Stain if done using Oblivion. Now you want to lower your Humanity when increasing the Discipline and get to act more depraved by using it.

                                Second, for the theme, it has a history in the setting. And a part of the problem is that it is also ridiculous from the perspective of this history.

                                Originally it was two very distinct Disciplines, Necromancy and Obtenebration. Necromancy has a theme of not being healthy in the very long term, but in the short therm it is just Death-themed magic and those long term effects are avoidable with common sense. More importantly, in all its forms it has a theme in VtM of bringing the vampire closer to the other side of their nature in a philosophical way. So it may be either damning or healthy for a vampire. It is also not overtly dangerous because the Dark Umbra isn't overtly dangerous.

                                The other one, Obtenebration, had from the beginning a legend to it that it wasn't actual darkness, but a strange matters siphoned from another place, an alien dimension. Initially it was just for the Lovecraftian factor because they liked to "sneak" Lovecraftian themes in the Sabbat (at the time they really wanted to make the Sabbat be about the three-sides war between the Great Old Ones, Satanists and the Illuminati, on Earth and Beyond). But it had no side effects attached to it precisely because if it had it would be a certainty, not a legend, and most Lasombra would stop using it.

                                When they decided to explore this theme further, they created the Abyss Mysticism, a Pseudo-Discipline with several powers that was more strongly thematic, and arguably stronger indeed, but that was attached to side effects from the contact with the Abyss. Being less Humane was common among Abyss Mysticism, but less because it required such and more because you had to be more than a little nuts to deal with that shit and its possible consequences, you still could be perfectly good and fine, though. The effects weren't moral, but strange mutations to body and mind, because it was less about dehumanizing thoughts and more about Lovecraftian transformation.

                                V5 basically looked at this history, the role of the Abyss Mystics among the Lasombra, the many wise practitioners of Necromancy that weren't interested in enslaving ghosts, the body of secret vampiric knowledge on the Dark Umbra, the many characters, bloodlines and themes explored that way through the years, including a few heroic characters that wielded such powers... threw everything away and simplified the themes in the most direct and unrefined way possible.

                                Do not take any of this as hateful, bitter criticism, though. I'm just explaining my position in more detail. V5 is turning both system and setting upside down. For all the direr criticism I have on other aspects of the new lore, here it is just a normal mistake when you're trying on new paths. The themes are complex and hard to nail at once, they were originally build over several books, through many, many years. The whole system is new, it needs tempering all over the place. I don't think this is a terrible mistake they can't be excused for. But it is, no way around it, a bad rule. Happens.
