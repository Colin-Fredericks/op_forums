    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    It is not the REAL world and the fact people expect it to be is something I feel that's a mistake.
    It is not the real world and no one expects it to be, this is not the argument.

    Fiction relies on Suspension of Disbelief, but not every reader have the same bar for it, and indeed not everyone take the same elements at the same value. Too stretched it rips, and you lose some readers. That's the whole reason we have discussions about what's believable here.

    This is above metaphor, tropes and everything else. You have to convince the reader that the artistic value of the work is worth Suspension, not to demand the reader to Suspend so they may see the Art. It is always first and foremost on the author, not the reader.

    So let me talk about me, about my Suspension of Disbelief. self-consistency is extremely important to me. And in this, commitment to a conclusion. Since my childhood I always hated deeply those episodes in shows where a major development happens just to be scratched in name of the status quo. The hero reveals their secret identity to someone, then a silly happenstance convince the person that it was a mistake or this memory is erased? Please, erase this crap from my memory, watching it was the only mistake.

    You say the SI will be portrayed a number of ways. What does it mean? Are they so subtle that their actions and methods can't be confirmed? Are they so fragmented that different sections do different things? Are they powerful enough to force their way? Or are they just going to pull out all sorts of shenanigans and nothing happens because the status quo has plot armor?

    Each of those options is valid, but each have its own consequences. If you just ignore logical consequences your reasons don't matter to me anymore. If you go for the last option it won't work for me anyway. I don't care how close WoD is to RL, I don't care if the status quois maintained or not, I do expect commitment to a course of action and self-consistency.

    About the metaphor itself, both the War on Terror and the War on Drugs are full of unintended consequences all over the place. Scores of people that do not buy and never bought the official narrative, and not only people marked as conspiracy theorists. Indeed, sometimes the official narrative was itself denounced as baseless conspiracy theory, without prejudice to it being still the official narrative. And now, in the world post-wikileaks and 4chan, where anyone can generate lasting evidence with their phones, it's even harder to believe anyone have this level of control over the narrative.

    Let me repeat, my thing is to explore consequences. Can they pressure the official narrative to be such and such and authorities be completely unable to stop them? Of course they can. When I was born my country was still under a US-backed military dictatorship whose narratives are twisted and covered to this day and only recently we obtained enough evidence to justly call Bull Shit on it. Yet, while a lot of people bought the official narrative completely, enough people refused and dived deeper to form resistance groups, warfare and whatnot. The cover-up being effective never stopped the truth of having consequences, and I don't need an Italian to tell me how cover-ups work. My father was a resistance member at the time and personally knew a number of people whose disappearances are unexplained still. The conflict between narratives then still shapes Brazil's political landscape.

    When the Second Inquisition is high-profile enough to carry out drone strikes in the middle of Europe, but too low profile to turn the setting into a survival horror landscape for vampires, widespread enough to control the narratives in every country in the world, but narrow enough to not have whistle blowers, coordinated enough to stop any third party from messing up the scheme, but not enough to stop its members from engaging in fragmented action, people caring enough for the SI to exist, but then being completely indifferent to any terrorist action, there are just too many inconsistencies for me.
