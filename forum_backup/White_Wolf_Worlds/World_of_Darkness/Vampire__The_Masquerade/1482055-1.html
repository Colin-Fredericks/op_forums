    Had another idea inspired by ants, of all things. Honestly not sure how playable this is, so it might be bloodline material.

    Polyergus

    Curse: Mere mortal blood is like water to this clan, only the vitae of other vampires can sate their hunger. Taking blood as payment for services is the most socially acceptable way to feed. However, due to the obvious issues inherent in drinking from vampires, historically some Polyergus have been known to drain their victims dry or, better, keep a rotational larder of thralls or staked victims. As their own clan is rather difficult to sustain, kidnapping the childer of other clans who feed on mortal blood - or more likely, getting a vampire to sire a blood doll as a boon - is a common way to get food in that manner. Those who keep herds are very "fond" of them, in their own horrible, blood bond addled way.

    Other clans have found that promising to donate an upstart to the cannibals is a rather effective threat.

    Disciplines: Auspex, Vicissitude, Presence
    Thieves of thoughts, souls and faces who walk amongst society undetected. A Polyergus learns her target through listening and watching his every move and emotion, until the time is right and she catches him off guard, mesmerises him, eats him if he's a vampire, and steals his face and life. Presence is also used in tandem with the kiss and blood bonds as a form of conditioning upon a herd to keep them subservient. Other uses include toning down the hostility they're commonly met with.
    The clan has a tradition of blood sorcery, and have invented several rituals that make their hunts easier, but pay out of clan costs to learn it. Other popular choices include the physical disciplines and Dominate. Even with the Kiss and the ability to detach limbs like clay, vampires tend to be harder to feed on than mortals.

    Sect: Sabbat.
    For all that nobody likes them, their weakness makes them vulnerable to blood bonds, their flesh crafting is invaluable for blending in with kine, and their shape shifting capabilities makes them excellent at espionage and assassinations. Historically they've made very useful thralls, and their own elders certainly weren't above enslaving their childer.
    The Polyergus were large proponents of the Anarch revolt specifically due to that particular issue, so had no reason to join when the Camarilla wanted to put that status quo back in place (letting cannibals run loose is a strangely unpopular idea).
    The vinculum also somewhat does away with the side effect of that pesky feeding problem.

    There is a small number of the clan amongst the Camarilla, where they are a closely watched and invaluable tool in maintaining the Masquerade. Their devotion to the sect is ironclad - it's not like they could feel otherwise, after all.
    Another number, who value their humanity as much as their freedom and are disgusted by the Sabbat, are independent. Many use their ability to change identity and "age" to live amongst mortals, preying on vampires who abuse their neighbours while doing their best to stay under the local Prince's radar. Others do a quiet business trading their services for secrecy and protection.

    Prospective childer are usually the types who can spin a good lie, don't mind getting their hands filthy and can go unnoticed. Charm is also valued. These vampires were often, though not always, career criminals. Traffickers, spies, identity thieves, hitmen, vagrants, con artists and actors come to mind.
    Fledglings and neonates are often dependent on their sires, who pay them vitae from their own herds in payment for obedience and competent service.

    As vicissitude also allows the user to take on monstrous form, there's a popular opinion that, between that and their diet, these guys aren't really the same creatures as other vampires but some other kind of monster that hunts them. Some Polyergus agree, claiming to be purer than those who feed on weaker blood.

            Our alternative setting so far:

            Camarilla:
            Cicatrix: Auspex, Dominate, Fortitude
            Netjeru: Dominate, Fortitude, Necromancy
            Dracul: Daimoinon, Protean, Sorcery
            Bagava: Animalism, Potence, Presence

            Sabbat:
            Pontiki: Animalism, Celerity, Obfuscate
            Polyergus: Auspex, Presence, Vicissitude

            Independant:
            Cecaelia: Fortitude, Presence, Protean
            Hags: Animalism, Obfuscate, Sorcery

            Bloodlines:
            Mara-Ma: Fortitude, Potence, Vicissitude
            Savages: Fortitude, Potence, Protean



            Bagava
            The Bagava were among the warlords of the ancient Indo-Aryans who conquered much of Europe and Southern Asia. They were a Clan of warriors who believed in crushing or at least conquering the weak. Over the course of millennia, this attitude has persisted if only taking on different forms. The Bagava believe that struggle is essential to life and unlike, that the strong must vanquish the weak for the betterment of nations, species, or even Clans. In ancient days they were warlords and chieftains of tribal peoples, in antiquity they gravities towards the Spartans and their harsh lifestyle. In the Middle Ages they were feudal knights. Later in the 19th and 20th centuries they fully embraced the eugenics movement and Social Darwinism. Over the millennia they have carefully bred a number of animal breeds for ideal service and even have a number of human lineages who have been eugenically bred for excellence and many Bagava are embraced from these lines. In the last several decades, the Clan has declined somewhat in political power both among kindred and kine.

            Nickname: Bullies

            Sect: The Bagava are members of the Camarilla. They consider themselves the rightful rulers of mortal and vampire alike and so are drawn to the Camarilla and help organize it. There are some who join the Anarchs or Sabbat though, who believe that the Camarilla is too orderly to allow the strong to truly crush the weak.

            Appearance: the Bagava are usually attractive and fit, only those mortals who are flawless physical specimens are chosen for the embrace, only those who have gray physical prowess and usually combat ability. Bagava are seldom embraced in old age or young, so are usually young to middle aged adults. Most Bagava are men, though a few women are embraced into the Clan. They typically wear highly quality but also practical clothing.

            Haven
            Bagava usually have opulent but we’ll defendable havens. Large mansions surrounded by fences and security precautions. They usually prefer something outside of the heart of large cities, where their animal ghouls wont be as conspicuous.

            Background
            Many Bagava are drawn from military or sports backgrounds, places where people are tested physically or even mentally. There are a number of family lines who the Bagava have been eugenically breeding for generations and many Bagava are drawn from these lineages. In the last few hundred years, some Bagava choose to embrace their childer for mental rather than physical excellence, choosing scientists and scholars, but even then they still must meet certain physical standards.

            Clan Disciplines: Animalism, Potence, Presence

            Weakness: the bite of the Bagava causes no pleasure, only the sensation one might expect from being pierced by sharp teeth. It cannot paralyze foes nor entice them to receive the bliss of the kiss. Many Bagava believe that it is their destiny to struggle and fight for their blood, though some acquire it through more subtle means.

            Organization: The Bagava have a semi-feudal organization. Regions are controlled by a ruling Bagava lord and all Bagava within that region must pay homage and swear fealty to that elder, then beneath them are lords of small regions who receive similar oaths from lesser Bagava within. Sometimes there are wars between those lords or even revolts. There is no central ruler ship of the Clan.

                The Children of Veles
                Origin:
                The obscure vampiric Bloodline known as “The Children of Veles” originated in the Slavic regions during the high medieval period. They are one of the oldest and now, almost forgotten weapons of the Carpathian Tzimisce during the large-scale conflicts and open war they waged against the nascent Clan Tremere.
                Most kindred historians will, undoubtedly, during their long studies over mouldy old tomes, find some notion about the creation of the Gargoyle Bloodline and how this, one of the greatest known kindred atrocities, would turn the war in the Tremeres favour during the battles along the Slavic border and into Tzimisce homeland. What is far less known is how the Tzimisce answered this escalating conflict and at what lengths they were willing to go, to achieve victory.

                Truth to be told, the Tzimisce in the Slavic region were not sitting idly while they had their territory constantly assaulted by Clan Tremere. In a unified effort they pushed their spiritual sorcery to, new, previously unseen heights, in order to stave of the waves of Gargoyles that kept attacking their settlements, keeps and castles.
                With a large portion of the Clan working with the increased focus on the arcane and occult, not only did the effort bring about new Koldunic Sorcery Rituals and Kraina, but also resulted in the creation of the first, artificial hybrid lineage of Kindred, in the form of a small Bloodline: The Children of Veles. They were so aptly named because of the similarities between the new vampiric lineage and the mythological Slavic God of The Underworld, Trickery and Shapeshifting, Veles.

                The leaders and instigators behind the effort never told their Clan mates about their various projects and kept those a well-guarded secret until the many labours had born fruit. What little is known is that at some point in time, one of the pioneers, an ancient and powerful Koldun, decided to meet the Gargoyles in open combat with a new breed of kindred, artificially created and subservient to their Tzimisce masters. This was ironically inspired by the Tremers creation of the Gargoyle Bloodline, which the Fiends so despised. In order to meet the Gargoyles and their Tremere masters in open combat, a new mockery of life, or rather unlife, had to be created. The vision was simple but effective, they needed a more combat capable and durable, artificial Bloodline of kindreds that was expandable, easy to produce and specialized at hunting and killing kindred and human victims of the Tzimisce Clan.
                The process of how these first “Children” were created have been all but lost to time, only possessed by the highest ranking Kolduns of Clan Tzimisce and used only sparsely in times of need. The process involved binding a powerful spirit to a chosen human’s body, with the aid of powerful ritualistic binding tattoos, and then subsequently draining the body of all its blood until clinical death occurred, after which the dead body was forcefully feed a combination of altered Tzimisce and Nosferatu Vitae, the exact procedure unknown to all outside the creator Kolduns.

                If successful, the ritual would allow the deceased individual to be raised as a newly embraced kindred of the Children of Veles, having access to all previous memories and skills they had in life. However, the body would only provide a physical form and access to its former mind. The soul of the original human individual would be gone, having passed on into the afterlife as it was replaced by a powerful, but still subservient, spirit of the Carpathian soil, who, as the body and mind fused with the spirits essence, gave rise to a newly formed entity in the form of a kindred of the Children’s Bloodline.

                This new individual would be fiercely loyal to Clan Tzimisce, have an unusual aptitude for the spiritual and otherworldly, as well as being of the newly created Bloodline. These fledglings would then be given over to senior kindred mentors that would train and educate them into their new roles as assassins, hunters and warriors.
                Contrary to the original Gargoyles, The Children of Veles were in all aspects “true kindred” and could embrace new members into the fold if they saw the time and opportunity to do so.

                Organization:
                Almost non-existent. On their own the Children organizes into small covens of loosely affiliated groups or cells. There is no singular leader, rather the entirety of the leaders from all groups/cells has a vote and gets to speak for that group. Rarely, another leader structure is used, but even then, there is never any “One-man rules all” mentality. The short answer to why is simple: It’s too vulnerable having a single individual sitting on all the power of the collective coven. Better to have a number of leaders if someone meets final death and then appoint a new leader from the worthy, then losing all structure and hierarchy when one falls.

                Outside of their own, they are almost always accompanying a Tzimisce, either as bodyguards in disguise or as tracker/hunter pairs in service to the Clan of Fiends. The Tzimisce have the highest authority, at least that’s what everyone agrees to. The truth is more complicated: The highest-ranking War heroes of the Children carries the same or even higher standing among their own than even the most esteemed Koldun could hope to attain. The only exception to this rule would be if one of the ancient Tzimisce who created the Bloodline in ages past where to emerge and ask for service.

                Culture:
                The Children are spiritual and ritualistic while also being pragmatic and opportunistic. They all try to become the ultimate hunter, but the views on this subject are as varied as the individuals who constitutes the coven in its entirety. They tend to embrace seldom and only when a dire need demands such actions.

                General Information:

                Name: The Children of Veles

                Nicknames: Derogatory; Bogeymen or Cannibals, Polite; Hunters or Beasts.

                Antediluvian: None, as an Artificial Bloodline, or Tzimisce and Nosferatu

                Faction: Independent, Nominally Sabbat Affiliated

                Disciplines: Obfuscate, Carnophagia and Potence

                Weakness: The Children are sensitive to spells and effects that can target spirits of the land. To resist such spells/effects the Child rolls Willpower – 2 (Difficulty depending on the level and number of successes on the activation roll of an offensive power). The Child will also be susceptible to alterations in his or her path rating, which in turn twists and mutates the body in new and alien ways. The lower the path rating the more monstrous the kindred becomes, while a high rating maintains a humane appearance.

                Clan Symbol: The Tzimisce Dragon Ouroboros at the center of a ritualistic circle with arcane marks and symbols corresponding to the planets, moon and sun.

                Modern Night Child of Veles: If there are any Children still alive tonight, they are well hidden and mostly keep to themselves.

                New Bloodline Discipline: Carnophagia

                Basic Discipline Levels:

                • Devouring Maw - Allows the user to draw Vitae from the victim while making a standard bite attack.

                System: This power is always active once learned and allows the vampire to both perform a bite attack and feed as one single action.

                •• Blood Memories - Allows the user to gain memories and skim surface thoughts from a target by drinking their blood.

                System: Not complete yet!

                ••• Bloody Perseverance - By imbuing a dismembered limb/remain of a kill with Vitae, the decaying process is halted and the blood stored in this way preserved indefinitely until withdrawn by a vampire with this power at a later date.

                System: Not complete yet!

                •••• Sheep’s Clothing - By flaying a fresh victim, the skin can be transformed into a leathery, full-body armour that, while worn, also disguises the vampire as the dead individual in question. This power can also be used on animals with the same requirements and effects.

                System: Not complete yet!

                ••••• Hunting Ground - The vampire marks his chosen territory by spilling vitae around its borders and when activated, the vampire knows whenever an individual enters the territory and gains bonuses to any hunt and combat rolls within his chosen hunting ground. He is also capable of knowing wherever a person entering his domain is supernatural or not in nature.

                System: Not complete yet!

                Advanced Discipline Levels:

                •••••• Infected Wounds - All damage inflicted by this power can be “stored” and activated later, when it triggers, old wounds reopen and inflict the old, suffered, damage once more.

                System: Not complete yet!

                ••••••• Moroi - Transforms the vampire into a huge chameleon-like predatory beast that gains increased physical attributes (+3 in all the physical attributes), natural weapons with aggravated damage and a camouflaged skin which hides the vampire in all kinds of environments.

                System: Not complete yet!

                •••••••• Predatory Adaptation - The vampire’s body will reflexively change to any stimuli in order to improve its survivability and chosen specialization while hunting.

                System: Not complete yet!

                Combination Disciplines:

                Gruesome Wards: Carnophagia 3 + Animalism 3 (Cost: 24 Exp)
                This combination discipline lets the user place horrific trophies as a warning and markers for claimed territory. The user can then instantly know when someone is inside his chosen domain and gets a minor bonus to rolls made to hunt and kill the intruder.

                System: Not complete yet!

                Appalling Lure: Carnophagia 3 + Presence 4 (Cost: 28 Exp)
                By imbuing the remains of his victims with the alluring powers of Presence and placing the bones in mystical patterns around his territory, the user can attract and confuse potential victims, which will wander into the territory unknowingly and become lost or trapped.

                System: Not complete yet!

                The Warding Flesh: Carnophagia 4 + Vicissitude 3 (Cost: 30 Exp)
                This power imbues the flayed skin of a victim so that when worn by the user it inspires fear or even terror in onlookers unless they have a strong enough will to resist its effects. The skin also grants an armour rating of 3 when worn by the specific vampire which created it with this combination discipline.

                System: Not complete yet!

                Trackers Mark: Carnophagia 3 + Auspex 2 (Cost: 20 Exp)
                This power allows for the invisible marking of an individual whom the user can then faintly sense regarding relative distance and direction. It’s usually used to mark unusual or powerful prey so that it can be found and killed when it’s in a weak position and unable to properly defend itself.

                System: Not complete yet!

                Ant-lion's Pit: Carnophagia 3 + Protean 3 (Cost: 22 Exp)
                The vampire merges with the ground and ones a victim walks over his position, he makes the ground collapse and slide towards him in the center of the newly
                created pit of soil and sand. Victims trapped in this way can’t escape unless they are extremely strong and durable or have friends nearby.

                System: Not complete yet!

                The Flaying: Carnophagia 4 + Animalism 3 + Vicissitude 3 (Cost: 32 Exp)
                This truly horrible power allows the user to slowly flay a human victim without killing them in the process, which ends in the ghouling of said victim. Thanks to the application of this combination discipline, the victim is forever-more skinless and grisly deformed, forced to live a continuing nightmare in which all they have left is their own, pitiful life and a maddening addiction to the domitor's blood. However, the transformation into a ghoul, by this combination discipline, does grant some unique benefits: Lethal claws, teeth and bone spurs as well as a natural armour 3 and another free dot of the Potence Discipline. The skin itself is in pristine condition and can be used for all other powers that require a human skin to be performed, such as the fourth power of the Carnophagia Discipline or The Warding Flesh Combination Discipline.

                System: Not complete yet!

                Primordial Horror: Carnophagia 5 + Presence 5 + Vicissitude 4 (Cost: 40 Exp)
                With this horrible combination discipline, the user physically and spiritually transforms into the very embodiment of mankind’s oldest fears, gaining a monstrous form, befitting of a supernatural predator which hunts and devours humans and vampires alike. This form induces sheer terror into all but the strongest of minds, and is equipped with aggravated claws and teeth, natural armour of 3 and a + 3 dots increase in all physical attributes. Finally, all spilt blood in the vicinity of the user, using The Purest of Fears Combination Discipline, will start to crawl or trickle towards the vampire, as if he was the center of a gravitational well. All blood thus absorbed by the vampire in his monstrous form will automatically replenish his blood pool.

                System: Not complete yet!

                Predatory Pets: Carnophagia 3 + Presence 2 + Animalism 3 (Cost: 24 Exp)
                With this power the user are capable of ghouling and breeding naturally occurring vermin or predatory animals into gigantic, feral versions of their usual forms. These beasts are fiercely loyal and have a telepathic link with their domitor vampire. They also have lethal inflicting natural weapons and the normal bonuses of being ghouls in addition to the benefits of this power. The increased size confers a + 4 Physical Attributes, distributed as the Storyteller sees fit, and a natural lethal soak. The beasts created with this power can’t survive without their regular upkeep of the domitor's Vitae and will start to starve to death without Vitae in their system at the point where a ghoul stops being a ghoul and reverts back to a mortal creature.

                System: Not complete yet!

                            Sukkal
                            The Sukkal have a long history as a Clan of scholars, philosophers, and theologians. In ancient days they documented the legends of the kindred and recorded histories of kindred and kind alike. They sought out mortals of great intellect or learning for the embrace and have often influenced vampiric politics with their deep thoughts. Often kindreds of great power used the Sukkal as advisers and allies.

                            In the Middle Ages, Sukkal philosophers popularized the ideas of rights and liberties, ideas which helped to foment the Anarch Revolts. Many of the Sukkal joined with the Anarch Revolt, though the mainstream of the Clan stood by the elders and as advisers among the Clans, they helped the elders to create the system that would later become the Camarilla.

                            At some point during the Renaissance the Clan began to undergo a change. Formerly aloof scholars and advisers, the Sukkal began to adopt an ideology of humanism - that vampires should be compassionate towards mortals,should not use their powers to control humans but rather to protect them. Some say that this was an ideological change by the Clan founder himself, becoming more humane and making it difficult for his descendants to drink the blood of mortals. The Sukkal began to use their influence with the Camarilla to push the institution in a kinder and gentler direction: sparing human life, avoiding manipulating humans, seeking to maintain their humanity. Because many vampires in power are resistant to this, the Sukkal often find themselves in the position of Anarchs yet again and they often are at odds with the Camarilla leadership. Some still serve as advisers to princes or justicars.

                            Nickname: Vegetarians

                            Sect: the Sukkal are officially members of the Camarilla and many high ranking members of other Clans often keep a Sukkal scholar or adviser on hand. Outside of this, the Sukkal are very prominent among Anarchs. There are also a sizable contingent of Sukkal antitribu among the Sabbat.

                            Appearance: The Sukkal vary in appearance, as few are selected for the embrace based on physical prowess or beauty, but rather for knowledge, intellect, or ideology. Often older people are selected for the embrace, but this isn’t universal. Vampires of this Clan often dress in formal, yet understated, fashions.

                            Haven
                            The Sukkal like to have havens near places of learning or cultural importance. Universities are favorite locations for Sukkal havens. Sometimes they select havens outside of the inner cities and depend on their power over animals to supply them with sustenance.

                            Background:Historically, the Sukkal came from academic backgrounds, even priests, philosophers, or intellectuals. Those sorts of people still comprise many of those embraced into the Clan, but more recently they tend to prefer people with a record of humanitarian activity - activists, health care professionals, social or charity workers, etc.

                            Clan Disciplines: Animalism, Auspex, Celerity

                            Weakness:
                            The Sukkal the blood of humans tastes bitter to the Sukkal and they must drink two points of blood from a human to gain one blood point. They do not suffer from this effect when drinking the blood of vampires or animals. Furthermore, their base maximum Blood Pool is their Humanity, not 10. So a 10th generation Sukkal with a Humanity of 8 could only hold up to 11 Blood Points.

                            Organization: the Clan has no formal organization, though the Sukkal do tend to keep firendly relationships with each other and share information or aid each other whenever possible.


                            Antitribu:
                            Antitribu of the Sukkal will claim that the humanitarian change was the shifting will of the Antediluvians and that they, rejecting him as they do, were able to resist the change. What ever the reason, Antitribu of the Sukkal are different than the Camarilla version of the Clan. In a Dark Ages setting, this is the dominant version of the Clan.

                            Disciplines: Auspex, Celerity, Dominate
                            Weakness: the Antitribu of this Clan suffer from their traditional weakness that extends through the ages. When the Sukkal encounters an interesting new idea, be it science, philosophy, or social issues, it must succeed at a Self Control or Instincts roll with a difficulty of 6 or become either lost in thought or embroiled in conversation over the topic. This effect lasts for a scene but may be ended prematurely by spending a point of Willpower.

                                Vegulans
                                The ancient myth of the clan claims its founder was once a powerful mage, whom the first vampire embraced as an experiment. The result was Vegas, the founder of all blood sorcery. Devastation at the loss of his magic, Vegas began experimenting with the blood. From these experiments, he discovered the Path of Blood, and by extension all other sorceries of the blood.

                                Nickname: The Fallen Stars

                                Sect: The Vegalin are predominately Camarilla aligned. As a clan, they desire unity and structure and knowledge of all things. However, since any mage that is embraced will become a Fallen Star means many Vegulans can easily find themselves in any political sect.

                                Background: The Fallen come from one of two backgrounds. One is those sired by another Vegulan, which makes up the bulk of the clan. However, a rare few were once members of the Awakened, and the power of the blood made them into a Fallen Star. Vegulan Elders often seek out of clan embraces in order to add them to the fold, something many such Vegulans do not wish at all.

                                Clan Disciplines: The Fallen Stars have the innate ability to learn all disciplines equally and spontaneously. The cost to increase any discipline is the same as the Caitiff.

                                Weakness: Magic runs in the veins of all the Fallen Stars. All Vegulans suffer from some Supernatural flaw at character creation, which does not give you additional freebie points and does not count against your maximum flaws. In addition, the Fallen Stars all suffer a +2 Difficulty to hide their Supernatural nature to all would be observers.

                                Organization: The Vegulan clan seeks to unite under a single banner and creed. At least, that is what many of the Elders seek, a desire which sees the clan collecting itself into a complicated web of blood bonds and political hierarchy. Status within the clan is determined purely by a member’s knowledge of blood sorceries such as Thaumaturgy and Necromancy.


                                The Strix
                                Born from the original experiments of unknown blood sorcery, the Strix quickly spread to become the most common bloodline in the world. Acting more of an infection than an actual bloodline, Strix pose a deadly risk to the Masquerade where-ever they appear, as most of the bloodline become Wights before their first year. In addition, there are rumors that those who consume the blood of the Strix are doomed to become like them as well.

                                Nickname: The Rabid

                                Sect: The Sabbat at one time used the Strix as foot soldiers in their sieges and raids. However, the dangers of this practice became apparent, as many of the sect fell to the raging fangs of the Rabid. Since then, all cities and sects that discover even one Strix will quickly begin a Blood Hunt for the rest that are in the city.

                                Background: Anyone can become a Rabid, as long as their luck is bad enough.

                                Clan Disciplines: Potence, Celerity, and Foritude

                                Weakness: The Rabid’s curse is three-fold. First, all kine that fall to their fangs will rise again as if they were embraced, even if the corpse had never tasted vampiric vitae after death. Second, the Strix are all doomed to fall to the beast, and can never increase their Humanity, and can never adopt a Path of Enlightenment. Finally, their Intelligence and Conscience scores can never be higher than their Humanity rating.

                                New Flaws

                                Strix Rabies (5 Point Supernatural Flaw)
                                You've tasted the blood of a Strix. You gain the Strix's weakness and replace your in-clan discipline list with that of the Strix. You do not lose levels in the Disciplines you knew, but further levels in those Disciplines are purchased at out-of-clan cost. You count as both a member of your original clan as well as a Strix, but anyone you embrace is always a Strix.

                                    Never really attempted to make a new clan wholesale in my games to be honest.

                                    That said, it's in no small part because i find throwing a "lore reveal" that casts one of the existing clans in a whole different perspective much simpler to implement, not to mention more impactful in the table than throwing a homebrew clan. Making the familiar once more unfamiliar to the experienced players and have them discuss and explore the implications.


                                    Some examples:
                                    - Giovanni are not descendants of the Cappadocians, never were, that's post-Anarch Revolt nonsense resulting of stupid collation of facts, rumors, half-truths and blatant lies. They and the Lasombra were branches of the same in-clan conspiracy, now each claiming to have consumed their antediluvian back in the middle ages during all the fighting in the italian peninsula, while glossing over the fact its the same one, ergo (at least) one of them is blatantly lying about who took down the founder of the "nameless" clan.

                                    - The Giovanni x Cappies "purge" was just a case of a rising clan backstabbing the flagging one for its many mortuary treasures - and truth be told, far more Cappadocians were destroyed by witch-hunters, inquisitors and random christians with True Faith rising against the maniacal black death-inducing undead parasites ensonced within their own church than by any venetian necromancers and their ilk.

                                    - Cappadocians in fact were the wayward branch of the brood of Saulot, those who refused the mystic teachings he brought along with the 3rd eye from the far east. Their clan status was a extensive work of fabrication, through their strong connection to early christian writers and involvment with establishing the Book of Nod and Caine Mythos as core tale of vampiric origins as presently known to most kindred.

                                    - Brujah's original brood is still around, quite active and well. They just call themselves Toreador and mostly nobody still knows or cares about an ancient consumed where the mists of mythology mix and lose themselves amidst the sands of time. The "True Brujah" of the Black Hand are utterly deluded fools.
