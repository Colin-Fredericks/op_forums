                        I think they could be downgraded in spectacle and numbers, you could say either Maelstrom (through other names), death of the 3 Bodhisattvas at Bangladesh, endless battle against the Sabbat that is also advancing on their territory imbued with powerful blood... or going to fight the Yama Kings in their own Gehenna/Apocalypse/Reckoning, although that probably would mean a complete annihilation of the Wan Kuei, with only a handful being left.
                        They could work better as a very small society in each city or hermits, because their society is a bit too big as it was in the corebook. But the way they work internally, the Dharmas, the struggles, the magic/power systems, the possible stories and chronicles, are just too cool to throw away. Of course it has a lot of exaggeration and stereotype, which isn't a good thing, but 100% "Westernize" it doesn't seem the right call neither. And yeah, I think there probably are "Cainites" (vamps that drink only blood and burn in the sun) in Asia, and probably always were, many times called Kuei-Jin or Cathayans by Europeans and Americans by mistake and ignorance. That's how I imagine, and the "real" Wan Kuei probably wouldn't respect them either, thinking them as lesser spiritually.

                        This very thread said there was some talk that they wouldn't officially do anything with Wan Kuei until something like a local company or studio do it, so it really isn't something we can be sure is going or not to happen. I do hope it does, cuz I would love Wan Kuei in 5th edition Storyteller. But I understand they're a bit niche, and mostly misunderstood or downright hated. Also, it seems the IPs are taking longer to release, and we kind of don't have any confirmation on Mage, Wraith or Changeling, much less Demon, Mummy or Kindred of the East, so it really might take one hell of a time before we even get to know if it is a "100% no", "maybe, or "yes".

                                                Again, mostly reiterating the gist of what I got from the conversations, I think the issue is three-fold with Wan Kuei.

                                                1. The initial presentation really was a chop-sockey set of stereotypes that combined Japanese, Indian, and Chinese legends together that really have almost nothing in common. The basis for the Wan Kuei is a distinctly Chinese legend and India's own vampire mythology is totally different. Japan also has plenty of scary undead, albeit they tend to be ghosts, and the three just do not work together very well as one creature.

                                                This isn't to say that European legends work together that well but the V:TM vampire is based on the CINEMATIC vampire rather than the mythological one and there's already a distance from the "source material" so to speak that most people already have a distance from the monster's cultural heritage. Or, to put it as a friend put it, "I know what a vampire is. I've watched Hellsing. I'm not sure what these things are." Another guy had a less flattering, "Kuei Jin are a game about Big Trouble in Little China except without the implicit assumption Jack Burton has no idea what the fuck he's seeing or dealing with."

                                                So untangling the Wan Kuei to an iconic monster that Asian people recognize and want to play is already hard because they are an entirely new thing. Ironically due to the attempt to make something closer to RL myths.

                                                2. Wan Kuei are troublesome for the fact that they're too close to V:TM to be their own line but don't fit into V:TM themselves. I do think that if the Kindred of the East didn't intrude on local Chinese, Japanese, and Indian Cainite courts that people wouldn't mind them so much. However, lore-wise they are supposed to fill the local niche. Except the thing about the local niche is that Kindred of the East games are nothing like V:TM games.

                                                They're about kung fu fighting zombies fighting demons and practicing deep spirituality to transcend your physical weaknesses. It's a very-very different game. To use another European example, it's like wanting to play Interview with a Vampire or Underworld and instead playing the Crow crossed with the Prophecy. The Crow is awesome but not the same.

                                                They should be separate gamelines and communities.

                                                3. Wan Kuei are not very LARP friendly by contrast as the big selling point of V:TM is it is primarily a game about talking. What puts it above Werewolf: The Apocalypse is that game where you don't need big action scenes or battles to do games. KOTE leans heavily into the metaphor of fighting and tabletop friendly things whereas the V:TM community as a whole is the more social aspect. So the very DNA of the Kindred of the East is divorced from some of the social aspects of gaming that make V:TM so popular.

                                                So the issue isn't so much they can't be fixed but....how does one go about untangling this? What is the end goal and what should it look like?

                                                    Said it before and I'll say it again... The best way to make the Wan Kuei non-problematic is to stop trying to pretend they should slot into the "Vampire" category of the WoD mythology, but rather link it and Mummy to the generic Wraith concept of the Risen (called such because VtM had already ripped off Revenant for their super-ghouls). This makes the Wan Kuei just a particular cultural expression of the Risen concept in the same way that "Hengeyoki" is just a particular cultural expression of a Garou or the "Spirit Folk" are just a particular cultural expression of Changelings.

                                                    With that accomplished, its simply a matter of making the Wan Kuei's feeding a particular means for gathering the "mana" needed to maintain their animate body... with the idea that needing to eat flesh (or brains in the case of low tier Western Risen/Zombies), blood, breath and eventually just drink from the cosmos reflect differing levels of ability among all Risen. That most Risen need to feed by consuming parts of living humans puts them into natural competition with vampires for resources, but you're no longer pretending they're remotely the same type of creature; one is a cursed human, the other is basically a ghost animating its corpse (and covering it up with a pretty illusion in the process).

                                                    Refine the feeding criteria a bit and, for the Risen/Wan Kuei "flesh and blood" could be basically the same level and drinking blood instead of eating someone's flesh is just a less lethal means of obtaining sustenance for the more moral Risen. The next step up might be the "strong emotions" part where they feed off fear or pain or desire, which lets them feed without physically hurting someone, but flesh and blood is sometimes just easier (or a way of doubling up... feeding on both the pain from the wound they drink from and the blood proper).

                                                    Basically, slide the Wan Kuei into a cultural expression of the universal Risen concept and just about all the problematic aspects fall away. The Eastern Courts reflect their culture's stronger traditions towards organization... a social construct like the Camarilla vs. something intrinsic to the Risen state.

                                                    Thus, the Wan Kuei becomes a creature type that a Masquerade vampire might encounter in the same way they encounter any other type of Risen (or werewolves or generic ghosts); as a supernatural being, but not even intended to be "a vampire, but oriental."

                                                        Originally posted by CTPhipps
                                                        Wan Kuei are not very LARP friendly by contrast as the big selling point of V:TM is it is primarily a game about talking.
                                                        VTM was/is popular because you got to be a Vampire. Beyond that before certain new additions forced you into one particular style, you could do anything with it on the type of stories you wanted to tell. Not everything was purely only in the speaking lens, and even within the speaking "primacy" of the game it wasn't about angsty typecast stuff such as attending to a "Touchstone" or "we make the game just more speakey 'cuz LARP"; it was about the cold hard realities of the Kindred existence and the Jyhad, which itself has always accompanied some form of fighting; whether it be an Elder manipulating human authorities to assault a gang in turn manipulated by another Elder, or full on sieges among the night.

                                                        To divorce all action from the setting negates one of the primary drivers of VTM. Even V5 itself has violence a centerpiece of driving the plot along, with the Anarchs apparently overpowering the Sabbat from many of their territories or the subsequent Beckoning" where all the Elders proceeded to the Middle-East for some massive battle.

                                                        And remember, Tabletop should always be the primary focus in the first place (I know I'm probably stepping on a few toes here, oh well). To say or imply KOTE is bad because it "leans heavily into the metaphor (what exactly did you mean by "metaphor" in this instance, specifically?) and tabletop friendly things" doesn't make it bad.. because its primarily Tabletop, so that'd be the main focus; its first job would be ensuring that aspect is up to par, before worrying about anything else (i.e in the case for accounting for LARP).

                                                        EDIT:

                                                        Originally posted by Chris24601
                                                        but rather link it and Mummy to the generic Wraith concept of the Risen
                                                        Terrible idea, since the Wan Kuei are way too different and have a way too different powerbase and lore than Risen. First of all; Risen don't eat brains to fuel themselves, they use Pathos. Second; Becoming a Wan Kuei is one of clawing yourself from the Thousand Hells, one of the hardest things for a previously unsupernatural/mortal person can do. Becoming a Risen is easy as getting the right teacher in Puppetry and bargaining with your Shadow, but that comes with its own issues. Three; Like I mention before, Risen can be strong, yes, but they're nothing compared to Wan Kuei with have levels of Haxx that basically make them certainly NOT Risen... and also the fact that Wan Kuei cannot use Arcanos nor Risen can even use Dharma powers.

                                                        EDIT: (Oh, and don't forget depending on whether the particular Wan Kuei is either Yang or Yin aspected, they can commune with Spirits from the Middle and High Umbra, something the Risen can never do. Not to mention the Wan Kuei like the Garou or Awakened can travel to said Middle or High Umbra)

                                                        I think the best path for the Wan Kuei is that they already ARE their own thing. Pretending they aren't.. is iffy and just causes more plot contention. They didn't come from Caine, they weren't a central part to the chronicles of the First or Second cities, they have a completely different backstory. They aren't "Vampires, but oriental" (that was the Anda, Gaki, and others I forgot)... and even if it THAT really was the case isn't that what the side that is clamoring to remove the Wan Kuei want in the first place?

                                                        The best path for the part of the oWoD fandom that what Kindred stories in Asia is just have the current writers make the formerly existing few Kindred there just have newfound prominence, while not stupefying or outright retconning the Wan Kuei.
