    [V5]/[V20?] New Resonance Systems

    06-27-2026, 01:42 PM
    In V5 we're presented with rules and fluff for Resonance, the jê ne sais quoi that gives taste and distinction to Blood for vampires.

    The idea is good. It existed conceptually in the game since the beginning, but finally got rules that can cover it without bloat. I dislike a lot of things in V5, but this is a good concept.

    But the execution is passable at best. We're given a humour-based system (with alternative name slapped that don't really apply) and intensity. The shallow explanation leaves it unclear what a humour is and seems to be just mood (it isn't, but work, I guess). And then we get the system of 50% nothing, 30% fluff, 20% maybe too strong for just a fix.

    Here I want to work the two different axes of the rule: Resonance Taxonomy and Gradation Rules.

    RESONANCE TAXONOMY

    Even if the game does a poor job working on it, the fluff gives us vital information: those four/six humours are not the end of the story. The undead have discussed for ages the taxonomy of Resonance, including things like blood type.

    This is not a small thing. We're talking taste as basic, objective sense. Give or take some small nuance on how to apply, no human culture has disagreed on the basic tastes of our palate. If this discussion exists at this level among the undead, that means other ways of dividing are possible.

    Resonance Taxonomy is where we make new types of Resonance. The default approach is to make a full set of six, tied to two Disciplines each, but other arrangements are ok.

    One important point: Resonance Taxonomy also affects vampires' detection. If something leave taste in the blood, then vampires can track that in a mortal through taste. Currently that means humour, which is not a small information. It can be nerfed into random bullshit, or far more valuable. Think it through.

    GRADATION RULES

    Here is where the actual rules live, it says what happens when you drink the blood, currently using Intensity. The current problem is that most of the time it's nothing, or allowing you to RP your XP expenditure through your eating habits.

    Change these rules can take many forms. You can change modifiers, decide they go to other things than Disciplines, tweak the bar where they kick in. Particularly they serve to suggest baselines and lists of Dyscrasias.

    The only thing I suggest not to touch lightly is the marker of how long the benefits apply: until new feeding or Hunger 5. This is easy to track.

    THIN-BLOOD ALCHEMY

    Your best bet for Thin Blood Alchemy is to use the system in the core book, if for no other reason because it's already a theory used in alchemy. If in your game vampires don't taste Humours, Alchemists can find the Resonance of blood with their Athanor.

    If you want, you can map the Resonances between systems to use only your new one, which should take a few minutes, or rewrite the recipes with new ingredients, which will take longer.

    Finally, if you use multiple layers or just keep Alchemy in the old system, you have the option of adding new recipes using the new Resonances.

    ABOUT V20 AND DARK AGES

    These rules are V5, but they're simple enough to apply to V20. Even then, anyone et everyone is free to propose edition-specific rules, as are rules for specific time periods.

    Also mix and match as you see fit. The simplest way to manage this is obviously to assemble a single system to use on a chronicle and call it a day, but you can have different mortals matching different systems, vampires may be affected by the theory they follow, and/or you can let them stack: each mortal has a combination of 1 to 5 layers that combine effects in some fashion.

    I want to see what people can come up with, including reformulations of regular rules rather than revamp, but first let me present my first contribution:

    ---

    RESONANCE TAXONOMY: MIND FRACTURE

    Everyone has breaking points. Enough stress, trauma or something, and your mind start to show cracks, things you may pretend to control but you don't. Mental health professionals call then symptoms.

    And this is normal. Breaking points aren't all about big pressure producing total collapse. It is impossible to exist in this world from the very beginning without breaking a little. We get hurt, recover, rinse and repeat, our symptoms fluctuating in intensity as we move in life.

    While anyone develop any type of symptom if exposed to the right circumstances, each mind has particular tendencies, and at least a few ongoing symptoms. The Mind Fracture Theory says the greatest influence on the taste of the blood is how a mind is breaking, which type of symptom prevail.

    Taxonomy:

    - Psychotic

    Symptoms: Delusion, hallucination, reality distortion, disorganized thought/speech.

    The mind steps outside shared reality. It may perceive what isn't there, or see what is and still reach bizarre conclusions. It may mix words as if switching between conversations, not holding coherence in any. Feel persecuted, or most grandiose. Good coping means the character can recognize the symptoms and navigate them. They can loose it either by giving in to them or being so overwhelmed they can't see or think straight through the storm.

    What it isn't: voices in your head don't make you obey. The psychosis can cause intense distress and equivocal beliefs, but alone it doesn't control your body.

    Disciplines boosted: Dominate, Presence

    - Dissociative

    Symptoms: Depersonalization, derealization, compartmentalization, disconnection from body/environment, dissociative amnesia, identity fragmentation, fugue.

    Present in body, absent in mind. It may feel that the world isn't real, or you aren't real. Your body isn't your own, you're just like a passenger. You may not remember what you do at work, you may be several people in one. Space out, lost time, key pieces of your life are a blank slate. You don't loose your real mind, if you're well enough the lapse is unsettling and people may notice, but you stay functional during symptoms. If you're not, though, you may end up stopping what you should do, or even act against yourself.

    What it isn't: a real fragmentation. Crucially, you don't loose skills, even if you forget you have them, which isn't common. Symptoms are real, but usually not incapacitating.

    Disciplines boosted: Fortitude, Special

    Special: Dissociation’s second Discipline depends on the goal of the symptom. Is it withdrawal (Obfuscate)? Hyperfocus (Auspex)? Autopilot (Celerity)? Survival reflex (Animalism)? This is fixed though, even if the person changes Resonance then comes back, they'll have the same link every time they're Dissociative.

    - Anxious

    Symptoms: Panic, worry, hypervigilance, anticipatory dread, somatic symptoms (racing heart, breathlessness, tremor), avoidance.

    Wrung tight with fear of what might happen. Anxiety puts your threat response systems in overdrive, then takes residence at your ability to project outcomes. You see the worse possible scenario and you know it will happen. Something terrible is going to happen, facing it may be so scary you'd die of a heart attack trying. Paralysis, weak legs, numbness or pain as your body prefers to shut down rather than face the coming catastrophe. A weak response is healthy, increases preparedness when it is needed. A strong one can cripple you more directly than anything else.

    What it isn't: just a fear. You don't control your anxiety. It doesn't come from the trigger. Knowledge won't make it disappear, the trigger may be harmless, Anxiety is not.

    Disciplines boosted: Animalism, Celerity

    - Depressive

    Symptoms: Anhedonia (loss of pleasure), depressed mood, emotional blunting, hopelessness, fatigue, reduced motivation, suicidal ideation, guilt.

    Feeling hollowed out. Everything is lost. No pleasure, no hope, no will. The external state of your life means nothing compared to the inner bleakness. Everything tastes like ash. Very slowly or unusually fast, you move through your life wishing only to stop, it's tiring and meaningless. Feeling pain may be better than feeling this void. And when people show you how easy it is to look up and smile, you just feel worse for your complete failure at living. Good coping is hard to define because the pain doesn't correlate, you may feel awful in a good day and almost joyful in the most dangerous ones. Well you push through, accomplish things you'll be glad you did. Bad means either you have no strength to move from bed, or you're feeling so energized you can muster the effort to do something you'll really regret.

    What it isn't: your fault. It is usually tied to your context and conditions, but above all is a chemical switch malfunctioning, and you can't fix it yourself.

    Disciplines boosted: Obfuscate, Oblivion

    - Dyscontrol

    Symptoms: Impulsivity, aggression, emotional lability (rapid mood swings), poor behavioral inhibition, risk-taking, self-harm, explosive anger, loss of control.

    Extreme emotional outbursts. One minor trigger causes disproportionate response. A body that obeys emotion too fast to even think. A mind that blacks out with enough build up, and comes back only to see the results with vague memories of a process. Going through a day switching through all correct emotions per context, but all locked at an unnecessarily high intensity. Facing danger not out of excitement, but because you're feeling too well to be in danger. And sometimes you're not in the throes of emotion, you're seeking it like an addict. In a good spot it's a little intense, but you can pull the breaks no problem, even if making your feelings crystal clear. In a bad day you are a vortex of catastrophic action.

    What it isn't: intention. Your actions aren't thought through. They don't represent what you want out of life, or the complexity of your thoughts, or how much you care or not.

    Disciplines boosted: Potence, Protean

    - Obsessive-Compulsive

    manifestations: Intrusive thoughts, rigid rumination, compulsive behavior (checking, ordering, cleansing, counting), magical thinking, perfectionism, need for control.

    Recursive mental loops hold a line between you and catastrophe. Everything must be in place, every detail must be correct, a single mistake and disaster is certain, or at least something too bad to be worth the risk. It may be just an impression or a complete prediction of total collapse, but if you don't follow the rules and controls every variable you're actively inviting the worse to happen. And if the last time things went well you were swearing in German, it won't harm to do it every time, will it? Maybe just a little louder to be safe. With mild symptoms you are just slightly too concerned with things and a little slow to deliver work. In the other end of the spectrum your life stops to a halt, taken by endless rituals that rob you of family, friends, leasure, even food and rest, no project ever reaching completion even if you get to work on them.

    What it isn't: preference. You're not a little OCD because you like things clean. This is about order taking you hostage and forcing you to work under threat. Work that accomplishes nothing.

    Disciplines boosted: Auspex, Blood Sorcery

    ---

    GRADATION RULES: CONTROL/AGENCY

    Most people have quite some control over their selves. Even if external constraints are limiting options severely, they still act from their, however unwilling, decision.

    But the things that cause Resonance aren't always mere passenger traits. Both a Mind Fracture symptom or a Humour imbalance can affect behavior, robbing the person of autonomy, sometimes drastically.

    Curiously, this makes Vampires uniquely capable of identifying the criminally insane: what defines them isn't the nature of their mental state, but how much actual choice they had in the matter. Do with this what you will.

    Agency:

    1. Managed — The person is mostly aware of their actions and reasons; their coping mechanisms are mostly working; they are in full control. Actions are deliberate.

    Effect: buying a dot on the Discipline costs 1 XP less if you're under this Resonance for a full week.

    2. Struggling — The person is slipping; they may miss something as awareness falters; cope badly, with partial or irregular submission to symptoms; or sometimes the body and/or mind moves without full control. Control requires significant struggle and sometimes is lost.

    Effect: Discipline rolls suffer 1 less Hunger dice.

    3. Overwhelmed — The person is reactive rather than an actor: whatever combination of lack of awareness, coping failure or thoughtless action, the person is caught in a cycle of struggle, loss and consequence. Symptom management dominates their life.

    Effect: Discipline rolls get +1 dice.

    4. Consumed — No meaningful agency; resonance is the person's action at that moment.

    Effect: the Dyscrasia stipulates a context or action. You ignore 1 Rouse Check in it, no more than once per scene.

    ---

    DYSCRASIAS: MIND FRACTURE + AGENCY

    - Psychotic

    Paranoid Delusion: if you fear a hidden immediate threat, ignore one Rouse Check.

    Grandeur Delusion: if you're being watched by a Kindred of lower Generation, ignore one Rouse Check.

    Audiovisual Hallucinations: if you reveal your full vampiric glory to someone who doesn't believe, ignore one Rouse Check.

    Tactile-Olfactory Hallucinations: ignore one Rouse Check, the Dyscrasia is consumed.

    Disorganized Thought-Speech: if you want, ignore one Rouse Check once per scene, but if you speak any coherent sentence for the rest of the night, make twice as much Rouse Checks.

    - Dissociative

    Depersonalization/Derealization: ignore one Rouse Check, the Dyscrasia is consumed.

    Compartmentalization: when you enter a building, you can choose to ignore one Rouse Check per scene inside. If you do, you won't be able to notice in any way anything coming from the outside, including internet and phone calls.

    Dissociative Amnesia: if no one present knows you by any alias or your past, ignore one Rouse Check.

    Identity Fragmentation: when adopting a completely new identity, you can ignore one Rouse Check, but only one per identity.

    Fugue: once per scene you can choose to ignore one Rouse Check. In the next night you will raise in a place you never rested at before, or you'll have to make twice as many additional Rouse Checks to wakeup.

    - Anxious

    Panic: if someone flees the scene in fear, including you, ignore one Rouse Check.

    Hypervigilance: you can ignore one Rouse Check per scene. Each becomes a bonus dice to the next attack against you this night.

    Anticipatory dread: ignore one Rouse Check, the Dyscrasia is consumed.

    Somatic symptoms: ignore one Rouse Check. For the rest of the scene you have a -2 penalty on every physical roll from crippling pain.

    Avoidance: ignore one Rouse Check per scene. If any intelligent being sees or hears you, you'll roll twice as many Rouse Checks as you ignored this night.

    - Depressive

    Anhedonia: you can forego any instance of Willpower healing. If you do, for each point you would heal, you can later ignore one Rouse Check.

    Depressed mood: ignore one Rouse Check, the Dyscrasia is consumed.

    Hopelessness: ignore one Rouse Check, but instead of being special dice, your Hunger is a dice penalty for the roll.

    Fatigue: upon nightfall you can choose to ignore one Rouse Check per scene. If you do, your rolls are limited by Humanity as if it were day.

    Suicidal ideation: if you have a positive interaction with the source of this Dyscrasia for a full scene after drinking, you ignore one Rouse Check per scene thereafter. If the source suffer any damage, to Health or Willpower, the effect ends.

    - Dyscontrol

    Impulsivity: ignore one Rouse Check, the Dyscrasia is consumed.

    Emotional lability: if someone's opinion about you changes drastically this scene, ignore one Rouse Check.

    Risk-taking: if the action is completely inconsequential, ignore one Rouse Check.

    Self-harm: after any Health damage, ignore one Rouse Check.

    Loss of control: ignore one Rouse Check and roll to Ride the Wave. The ST decides the type of your Frenzy. If you would already roll to Ride the Wave, you forego the roll and frenzies as in a failure.

    - Obsessive-Compulsive

    Intrusive thoughts: ignore one Rouse Check and ask the ST to decide an action for you to take. The action must not involve damage or risk of damage. From the next scene on you're at -2 penalty on mental rolls from obsessive distress until you fulfill the action.

    Compulsive behavior: for each power, after its first use of the night, ignore one Rouse Check on other uses.

    Magical thinking: ask the ST to decide on two actions for you to take. If you do, ignore one Rouse Check.

    Perfectionism: ask the ST if there's any further action you know you can take within this scene to improve chances of success. Keep asking until you have amassed every possible advantage, even if it risks loosing the timing of the action. If the ST can't think of anything else, ignore one Rouse Check.

    Need for control: ignore one Rouse Check, the Dyscrasia is consumed.

    ---

    FAST REFERENCE

    1. Psychotic → Dominate, Presence
    2. Dissociative → Fortitude, Special
    3. Anxious → Animalism, Celerity
    4. Depressive → Obfuscate, Oblivion
    5. Dyscontrol → Potence, Protean
    6. Obsessive-Compulsive → Auspex, Blood Sorcery

    Random roll: use the number, 7-10 roll again

    ---

    INCORPORATION

    If the Mind Fracture taxonomy isn't the only one you use, it was popular among the Malkavian clan since always, though not dominant, then expanded a little to other clans with the studies in Psychology and Psychiatry in the 19th century.

    Taste is both a learned thing and derived from physiology. Some Kindred just are attuned to this taxonomy from start. To others, learning it take time slaking Hunger on it with proper guidance and can take anything from a month to a decade.

    Keeping systems apart means a vampire that's attuned to Mind Fracture can't benefit from Humours and vice-versa. The ST can let the character descriptively taste both, but remember that this isn't just fluff, it's information on the target. Otherwise, the character cannot taste any during transition between systems.

    If you apply both at the same time and people are layered (same person has a type in each independently), then solve combined bonuses by the following options:

    1. No combination, the drinker chooses which to benefit from.
    2. No addition, any overlap is ignored.
    3. Overlap let the character reroll one dice once in the Discipline.
    4. Overlap let the character reroll three dice once in the Discipline.
    5. Overlap let the character ignore one Rouse Check.
    6. Multiple instances of ignore one Rouse Check don't add, still only one per scene.
    7. Multiple instances of ignore one Rouse Check add, it's one per scene per system.

    Test and adjust. Too much benefit may cause Resonance hunt and farm to dominate the game. Which may be the point, or may be bad. It will also make Discipline powers fly more frequently and potently.

    But the effect shouldn't be that big, just try and see, start low benefit if you want to play safe.
