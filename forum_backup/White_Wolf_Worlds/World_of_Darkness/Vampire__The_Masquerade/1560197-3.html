                I think both are very poor lines of argument, to try to say the Masquerade makes sense because it worked for centuries in the world of make believe, and to close focus on sunlight being a big reason it wouldn't.

                On why so harsh? A number of reasons, but none is terribly important. It's setting flavor, few to no balancing benefits come from this, changing it in anyway or removing entirely will ruffle a lot of feathers but not make your game less fun, balanced, or true to source.

                Some actual reasons:
                1. D&D was still the default for this kind of reference at the time, especially for creatures that have no actual legend outside pop culture. In it vampires die under sunlight in a few turns.
                2. Focus on the night life aesthetics. They want a game of nocturnal action, period, even if with protagonists that don't see in the dark and face abject fear at the sight of any source of light prior to power stations.
                3. Making mortals dangerous. As much as the PCs are powerful, and aside the many players who would be happier with a D&D adaptation but are too cool for that, the premise of high lethality is important to the game design, and the sun is the best reason to fear mortals.
                4. Pedantic assumptions. A lot of VtM players are the kind that think weaknesses and harsh constraints aren't just fun to play, but actually mark them as superior artists, and that include our old pal Rein-Hagen, so that's that.
                5. Tradition. Time tends to consolidate decisions that aren't too directly damaging. At this point the harshness is a lot of the identity of the game. Not something it really needs, not something everyone cares (or should), but it is something a lot of people wouldn't feel like they're playing VtM if it changed.

                All said and done, I think it's a problem, but for another reason: it severely limits options for actual play. Plenty of interesting story options could use a PC that can wander at day, even if in a limited capacity. Very few really benefit from a PC that badly needs electric light to be a functional person because any other light, even if dim, is a potential death sentence.

                Best thing they did in Requiem 2nd Ed was to make daylight far easier to endure, and no one suddenly stopped making vampire society nocturnal because of it.

                EDIT:

                Special powers are no solution. It doesn't matter if a dozen Disciplines change the basic constraints, the question isn't "how character X can take a tan in the beach every other Saturday or navigate total darkness". It's why the basic constraints are what they are and how it affects the setting.

                Listing optional traits that let you not engage with the question is a wonderful way of missing the point.

                Watsonian explanations on why the Curse is so terrible equally miss the fact this is about design decisions by several kilometers.

                Vampires don't exist, not even in legends, no one was cursed by YHWH and his lovecraftian envoys who are sometimes mistaken for guys with wings, even if you believe they are real. This is a convention from within the game, not its cause.

                                            Originally posted by Amalasuintha View Post
                                            the context of a world that's historically being exceptionally hostile to all forms of special needs.
                                            Here's where I want to push back, not the core idea, but the lack of nuance. And importantly, ignoring that Vampire is already predicated on the idea that you can only count on yourself to cover your special needs, everyone else will just exploit them against you.

                                            I'm part of the disabled advocacy community and understand well what you mean. But the uniform approach loses a lot of details that not only historically matter, but here specifically matter to the stories we tell.

                                            First and most important, this has never been a uniform problem. Context matter, and power dynamics matter considerably. Ableism has so many times resulted in the children of the rich losing their privileges or lives for being disabled, but just as frequently or more they have gotten the adaptation denied to others.

                                            Especially if we're talking established adults. The same societies that abandoned mutilated people to poverty after a lifetime of services also left a rich record of prosthetic designs. The same societies that confined and isolated those who behaved weird, sometimes praised the same weirdness in someone else as a sign of genius or even divine favor.

                                            Add to it that the question isn't if vampires would be well adapted citizens. They're not even trying. It is if they would be noticed as vampires. They are getting their adaptations their way, not asking for them, and the premise is already secrecy about their condition, not acceptance.

                                            So the vampire would be found out for being pale and averse to sunlight? First, most would not be seen to begin with while they could avoid, people in general wouldn't know they're there. Then, an adult with aversion to sunlight is weird, but not unheard of, and would be pale as consequence. And sometimes work or other circumstance caused this.

                                            You'd need a lot of this vampire's preparations to fail to even get to the point where they're for real expected to get out during day, and then the vampire would first try to get away with any excuse not to, including pretending they already do, instead of saying it's a personal adaptation. Even today you can get away with that with "made a religious oath" or something, not to say how many adults can just say "no".

                                            Special needs only enter the picture after every barrier went down royally, and then who will deny the needs of someone who's already the richest in town and probably the boss of the boss of your boss? A vampire who really needs the society around them to respect the special needs on principle is already beyond screwed.

                                            The lack of accommodation for special needs is a terrible problem in our society. I'm currently myself fighting in the justice to get a few very basic accommodations I need for exceedingly well documented needs that have been neglected for years systematically in a company where they have negligible cost, just because the bureaucratic system is not built to make it happen.

                                            But the vampire as a hidden monster is not a creature that would particularly suffer this as a barrier to their conspiracy, when the conspiracy is already predicated on not revealing such needs. The accommodation can't be a cause of the revelation, because revelation has to occur first for accommodation to be required.

                                            Now, would it be a problem after the fact? For a vampire that's an overall common working class, definitely. For rich bastards that can buy their own accommodations, I don't think so.
