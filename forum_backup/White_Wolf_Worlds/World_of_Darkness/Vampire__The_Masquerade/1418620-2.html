                        Very notable,

                        For me, the Anarchs occupy the role of the "urban resistance" that does draw elements from real life social movements but it doesn't replace them or control them. In the original 1st Edition Chicago by Night, one of the subtler things about the Anarchs was the generational divide between the 1920s Socialist Unionists (Tarponski), the 70s Civil Rights Era activists (Anita Blake, Maldavis), and the modern day Punks (Gengis, Damien, Evelyn Stephens)

                        The Anarchs should always include idealists among them but also people trying to take advantage of that idealism and people are just the criminals of the Camarilla. The Camarilla is the Godfather and Michael Corleone while the Anarchs are the street level thugs and deals.

                        With the Sabbat, I generally think of them as, "Just a reminder these are the REAL vampires." V:TM is awesome and I do love the Louis, Angel, Forever Knight, and "Oh woe is me, I am damned vampires." However, vampire stories started with them as horrifying evil monsters. Dracula, Carmilla, Ruthven, and Nosferatu. The Sabbat fills a role in the setting that the Humanity rules kind of prevent from happening that there definitely needs to be vampires who rejoice in their evil as well as destroying lives. The Sabbat may go over the top but as you mentioned, the Necroscope and Buffy vampires are in need of someonew who can represent them too.

                        Without the Sabbat, you don't have anyone who can (with a straight face) say THE SATANIC RITES OF DRACULA.


                        I feel like V:TM is a lesser setting without the Sabbat because the whole "fight for your humanity" business loses significant edge if you don't have someone who represents the Far End of What You Can BecomeTM if you're playing Camarilla or Anarch and if you're playing Sabbat then you can go areas where you normally wouldn't like alien cults, rituals, and more. It's still a horror game but the question is not, "can you be good?" but "How far are you willing to go even after you're damned?"

                        In the original UNDER A BLOOD RED MOON, one of the best moments is in the Sabbat campaign when Phillipe Rigaud orders your pack to Embrace a bunch of Werewolf Kinfolk and Dominate them into eating the rest of their family, including a baby (or Embrace the children to eat their parents).

                        It's pointlessly sadistic and evil and designed just to piss the werewolves off. However, some of the Paths would actually object to that and perhaps even cause some to wonder at their Humanity's loss. I feel it's a great scene for the kind of horror that vampires can do. Mind you, I also hated when they got rid of the Path of Harmony as it meant that the one "decent-ish" group in the Sabbat was gone and thus not there to reign it all back.
