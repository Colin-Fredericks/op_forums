    What doesn't make a lot of sense.

    One of the problems with Book of the Weaver is that it generally has a far higher bar for signs of Bleach Kegare than the rest of WtA has for Trash Kegare, and that depiction is the most extreme of this.
    Probably to keep some degree of plausibility with the Glass Walkers not thinking that the Weaver was as bad as the Wyrm. To keep them from looking like total idiots and dupes, or to make the Weaver seem much subtler and cunning (which it kind of is compared to the Wyrm, seeing as how it's not trapped and biting itself trying to get free).

    Vampires are supernatural stasis more than anything else. Their corruption tends to follow from them living a shitty life, but their stasis is abnormal from the get go, with a body that stubbornly goes back to their initial state of death, rejecting absolutely any change to it, mundane or preternatural, superficial or invasive.
    But the things that their body does to the blood they consume could be considered an incredibly horrid corruption. Normal fluid connective tissue goes in, a horribly-addicting animating force that chains others to the holder comes out. The blood bond is a true thing of horror, and even if it instills some degree of stasis to turn a living being unaging, the psychological corruption it can produce from repeated drinks is far more noticeable in an immediate sense.

    That isn't just the Vampires, though. Drones take a lot more to form, for example, than Fomori. More extreme conditions, more time dedicated to dysfunctional behavior, more everything.
    That's because whatever force was behind it saw fomori, realized that they were kind of slap dash, and developed an alternative that thoroughly binds spirit and flesh together much more deeply. It's why Drones also cannot be exorcised easily, read by any force that most characters have access to.

    It makes sense from a design perspective because the game isn't meant to focus too much on the Weaver like that, but it doesn't really follow from a setting where, regardless of the Wyrm's tactical advantage, the assumption is that the Weaver basically covered the world. And with Vampires it makes especially little sense.

    But that's that, anyway. No few ancient vampires would stink of the Wyrm royally, especially in the later versions of the setting (as Vampire gets more Grimm-Dark-Kick-the-Pup-Evil as editions go).
    Well, the Gauntlet was spun by the Weaver, so there might be some acclimation going on.

                    I've rarely liked nor hated Metaplot or Lore advancements - I've never been a big fan of WoD "canon characters" or novels anyway. But I do dislike when those changes make the world smaller, with less options. Such as killing off the Ravnos on WoN, or deciding to devastate all off-world realms of mages on the Avatar Storm

                    I didn't like the choice to make Elders go away with the Beckoning on V5. But it's the choice to collapse the structure of the Sabbat, with a lot "going East", and the rest becoming a monster rabble labeled as "unplayable" for no reason, what single handedly collapsed any hope I may have had to play on that edition.

                    So I guess you could say I hate it. The whole "off to the middle east" arc.

                    The explicit intention was to collapse the power structures of vampires to force change, so it's the "put on a bus" trope ... but isn't it weird to send the social structure of Vampire to some place?, why would all Elders go to one place?, what's going on there?...

                    From the perspective of someone that plays Vampire on the Middle East, it must've been pretty wild - like, suddenly the place HAS to be full of Elders figthing some strange magical war between Super-Elders?. And the core book had 0 support for that kind of game.

                    Personaly, I think that choice was much more related with the whole conflict betweeen USA and Afganistan, that was a pretty hot topic at the time V5 was written and released, than any other reason (much like how comic books often send the heroes to fight wars on places that are strangely similar to countries that are at odds with USA at the time). Ultimately, WoD games are set in USA, are supposed to be played there, and the setting "looks at the world", so to speak, from that perspective. This one was just a pretty noticeable example of something that happens all the time, really, if you think about it.

                    To me, the exclusive location and the lack of support at the moment made that metaplot fell like everyone that's someone going to a party you're not really invited to. I guess part of the intent was to return some "mistery" to WoD, and that's allright - I can dig that "gehenna vive". That's one thing I can say I like about the whole things as a concept.

                    But it really didn't seemed that V5 was trying to engage players with that mistery - rather it seems the idea was to play the game everywhere else, and to give Anarchs more relevance while shoving the Sabbat out of the players hand and more into the STs hand. As it was on 1e. Felt like them trying to take a toy from my hands and then giving me another toy, saying I should like the new toy more.
