    Running the Free States - 9 - Crossover Elements

    06-29-2025, 12:45 AM
    At this point I want to address the non-vampire stuff. This includes crossover elements (werewolves, mages, wraiths, changelings), but also things like hunters and the Arcanum. I am not interested in them for the concepts themselves, but how they might be used in a Vampire game with Kindred PCs specifically in LA.

    Now whether I actually use any of it depends on the players. Some players love the inclusion of these elements and others hate them. Some may like them in theory, but complain in practice (implementation matters). Or they like ghost stories, but hate fairies. So certain elements can be used, but not others. When we do our final pre-game prep, I will be having a discussion with my players as to what they like and don’t like.

    However, even if they don’t want any of these elements in game, this doesn’t mean these elements don’t exist. In the World of Darkness, all these things are just as real and present as vampires. It just means that 1) I shouldn’t introduce them as plot elements, and 2) I should let the players/PCs what areas these things are in so they can avoid them. If they as players want to avoid Lupines for example so should their characters. (It's just important that the characters know about them.) The presence of those elements define what things are actually off-limits. I believe having such limits in the forms of danger zones and such are useful in sandbox style play.

    By the time the LABN sourcebook came out in 1994, besides Vampire the World of Darkness game line had also released Werewolf and Mage so that gets covered. Wraith would be released later that year and Changeling not for a year after that, so they are not covered. So we’ll start with the Lupines.

      LABN actually does a very good job establishing the Lupine presence in Los Angeles in a fairly short amount of word count. We’re not given many details, but I don’t think their needed for a Vampire sourcebook (this would become an error in future sourcebooks - especially in other game lines - where the crossover element took up way too much space and crowded out page count that would have been better developing their own game more).

      We have three basic areas of Lupine activity, four if you count the Black Spiral Dancers. On the western edge of metro LA we have Lupines at Topanga Canyon in the Santa Monica mountains (Black Furies plus some Uktena). They reactivated a caern back in the early thirties. The sourcebook says the caern is “minor”, but one of the last in this part of the country so it is defended fiercely. The Santa Monica Mountains and nearby Malibu are thus off limits to Kindred. Those who keep showing up here eventually get killed.

      The other area is the Angeles National Forest north of LA. A pack of Get of Fenris and Shadow Lords are here. Nearby is also the Glass Walker tribe in Pasadena and Glendale. They control a lot of agribusiness in southern California, and have a strong interest in Caltech and the Jet Propulsion laboratory. Because of their relative proximity to each other, I am including these two groups together despite their differences. The Glasswalkers are supposed to be the most influential group of Garou in the LA area and have been there the longest (at least among non-Uktena) since 1917.

      Third is a “huge pack” of Bone Gnawers and Uktena in downtown and South Central Los Angeles. Some of the Bone Gnawers belong to the Hood camp and help the downtrodden as best they can.

      The fourth group are the Black Spiral Dancers who occupy a level two Wyrm caern in a pit deep underneath Griffith Park.

      I'll talk about these in detail in future posts.

          The Santa Monica Mountains and Angeles National Forest make easy borders for the Los Angeles Free States. The Kindred know the Lupines are there and usually avoid them. Vampire characters who want to avoid them can do so easily. We know there is an actual caern and sept in the Santa Monica Mountains.

          No caern is mentioned for the Angeles National Forest. However, if there is not a caern there, the Garou there still need to participate in a sept somewhere. It's highly unlikely it is at the Black Fury and Uktena sept simply because those other tribes are not mentioned. So I prefer to create a new caern there that both groups (Glasswalkers and Fenrir/Lords) can be a part of. It's also possible to create two caerns, one for each group, but I want to limit how many caerns are in the LA area because we've already been told the Topanga Canyon caern is "one of the last".

          The “huge pack” near downtown LA is a possible point of contention. They are located in the heart of the Kindred domain, and as “the downtrodden” make convenient food for vampires this would seemingly put Kindred in conflict with them. Yet no such mention is ever made. It’s if the Kindred don’t even know about this pack of Lupines deep inside the city. I imagine this “huge pack” is mostly engaged in scouting and espionage, and probably concentrating more on other Wyrm menaces than vampires. So they avoid combat with vampires entirely. Some pack members might even negotiate with specific vampires for information or favors so if an ST wants to include some less hostile encounters with Lupines this would be the place to do it. Of course, should there ever actually be a war with the Lupines for whatever reason (the sourcebook certainly includes multiple reasons why this might happen), this pack likely strikes fast and violently using whatever knowledge they’ve gleamed about local vampires. However, I intend for this pack to be neutral to Kindred unless provoked. If for any reason players want some Lupine crossover for their characters, this is probably the group of Lupines I'd do it with as encounters don't need to be immediately hostile.

          I'm making this a pack under Coyote. Most members are Ragabash and Theurges with few members of other auspices. That'll make them much more likely to have non-hostile encounters that might lead to cool and interesting scenes with PCs. They are either an anruth pack that frequently travels out of area to different caerns, or perhaps have rediscovered a lost rank 1 caern similar to the Smiling Buddha Sept in Dark Alliance: Vancouver.

          The Black Spiral Dancers underneath Griffith Park presents more flexibility for STs. You can easily ignore them. Use them as some sort of local Griffith Park plot - perhaps involving the Setites. Utilize them as “friendly” Lupines willing to make deals. Or even activate that tribe’s sometimes alliance with the Sabbat in a Sabbat plot. This pit is supposed to be the major spiritual reason why there is so much "corruption" in Los Angeles, so it does have some relevance to me as a reason for Setite activity. The BSDs themselves won't be active against any vampires, and I can't see why any PCs would ever want to delve underground looking for the pit.

          Not relevant to our needs, the sourcebook also names drops various PENTEX subsidiaries in the LA metro area that can easily explain what the Lupines are doing to fight the Wyrm if they aren’t destroying vampires.

          This is decent enough detail for most STs if they want to consider the inclusion of Lupines in the city or at least map out what areas should be off limits. Three low level caerns around LA does seem a lot to me, but it's the only way I can really explain tribal politics. Keeping all the caerns low level does help me justifying low key Lupine activity.

                  Mage is the other splat given some word count in LABN, but we’re told a lot less about them than we are about the Lupines. This is probably because Mage was still in its early period of development, and there wasn’t as many obvious hooks between vampires and mages as there are about vampires and werewolves.

                  There is a Verbena coven in Santa Monica Mountains that tries not to aggravate the Lupines there, and there are Hollow Ones and other Orphans in Hollywood. Otherwise the only mentions of Mages are various Technocracy fronts around Los Angeles.

                  Needless to say any ST wanting to run a Mage chronicle set in LA probably adds a lot more mages and sorcerers into the city. However, since vampires are mostly ignorant of what mages do, I don’t find that much of a problem. STs can easily ignore mages unless they have a specific idea for a crossover plot. However, as future posts will show, we do have some other canonical Mage references to LA that can fill some holes for an ST who wants to develop more.

                  I don’t plan on having one unless the PCs specifically request something. However, just in case I have already made the decision to transplant the Hollow Ones chantry in Book of Chantries from Chicago to the Hollywood environs. That is some low hanging fruit with established ties to vampires. I can make some easy adjustments to fit it better in the local setting. They’ll be there for some easy plot just in case the PCs want something, and I can probably build on this element easily in case it increases instead of whets PC interest in mages.

                  I suspect there will be zero crossover with the PCs in this regard. If they do encounter any "magicians", it's much more likely to be a Sorcerer using the rules in World of Darkness: Sorcerer. However, simply due to my anal need to figure out things beforehand just in case they become relevant, I do like knowing the canonical lay of the land simply because while it does mean more work for me, it's certainly less time than me coming up with things myself.

                      A mention to LA is made in the Book of Chantries. Since the Book of Chantries was published a year before LABN, it upsets me that cross mentions like this are rarely made in so many regional sourcebooks. You only need a sentence or two to include the mention, and it might have increased sales on that other sourcebook.

                      The Vali Shallar chantry is in a Horizon Realm based on the Lost City Z (though called City X in the sourcebook) and inspired by the lost continent of Mu, and therefore the chantry itself is well outside the scope of the chronicle. However, the chantry does have a link to LA with an estate there. One of its cabals, the Yultasi (consisting of Akashic Brothers and Dreamspeakers), controls a cult of Sleepers in LA. These have to be controlled by cabal members visiting the chantry’s Los Angeles Estate. This Sleeper cult serve as scouts and informants, and the cabal is particularly interested in the Progenitors (who attacked the chantry in the past). That estate is also visited by a second cabal, the Hands of Hope (which has some other Tradition members than just Dreamspeakers and Akashic Brothers).

                      Not many details are given about the “Los Angeles Estate”. It is a large beachfront estate. It can’t be seen from the road and has typical defenses for a wealthy estate like a large walled courtyard, securtity sensors, five guard dogs, and five servants. It is near multiple ley lines hidden below the Pacific Ocean. A Virtual Adept of the Hands of Hope cabal, Alvin Reynolds, spends most of his time at this estate. He also occasionally plays guitar in a band that has played in a number of local LA clubs.

                      We’re not told where in LA the estate is. Likely it is not in LA proper. We could place it in Malibu if we want more mages there than just the Verbena, but I also think it would fit somewhere in the South Bay, either one of the Beach Cities or the Palos Verdes peninsula. That's where I'll probably place it.

                      Normally I would think this is just trivia, but the description also states that the Yultasi are at odds with several powerful anarch vampires in Los Angeles. The only reason this would happen is that they are trying to protect their Sleeper cult. I don’t think the estate itself is in trouble because otherwise the Hands of Hope cabal would be mentioned. The cultists then must be somehow noteworthy enough to have attracted the attention of some powerful anarchs.

                      I presume the Sleeper cultists are some sort of pseudo-Carlos Castenada type New Agers that would fit in well with Dreamspeakers maybe combined with yoga and other alternative medicine types that fit with the Akashic Brotherhood. It’s the type of California mysticism that fits well with the local setting. For them to attract anarch attention, it’s likely something occult in nature. Vali Shallar was founded to “preserve the old teachings.” The Yultasi also have their own secret library in a safehouse on Earth containing much arcane lore. Unless we want to invent a new location, this library must either be at either Shanghai or Los Angeles (the two places where it is mentioned the chantry has estates on Earth). It’s possible one responsibility of their Sleeper cults is to expand and maintain this library, and perhaps this is one reason they came under the eyes of the anarchs. Once vampire intentions became known, the Yultasi stepped in to protect their informants.

                      Another possibility is for the anarch vampires to be lackeys of the chantry’s enemies, but that would be a better idea for a Mage chronicle using Valli Shallar rather than a Vampire chronicle based in LA. So I'll be ignoring this option.

                      For my purposes, I’ll have an NPC anarch coterie already inclined to occultism so they can have several run ins with the Yultasi and be warned off. This coterie won’t quite know how to proceed because they don’t understand the nature of the threat. They just know they have angered some powerful magi.

                      It’s not clear how these Kindred are “at odds” with the Yultasi. That’s a very vague term. It doesn’t necessarily mean enemies or rivals, just that they have contradictory interests. I like the idea that being at odds does not necessarily mean physical violence. Perhaps its conflicts within the astral realm, spirit world, competition for occult resources, and other things that allow for more variety of plots. I'd rather have interesting interactions between PCs and NPCs that can carry over into multiple game sessions than an encounter that ends with the PCs trying to kill everyone.

                      If the PCs ever become interested in the more occult aspects of the setting, I might have them learn about this group of Sleeper cultists. I’ll just then see what happens based on what the PCs do. It's entirely possible that I won't bring any Mages at all into this.

                          There is another small reference to LA found elsewhere in the Book of Chantries. While not given a full description of its own, Balador, a Cult of Ecstasy chantry, has an earthly aspect in Los Angeles. The chantry is important because the canon mage Marianna, possibly the greatest Master of the Cult of Ecstasy, oversees it. It makes a lot of sense to me that the Cult of Ecstasy has some representation in the LA area.

                          We aren’t told anything more about its aspect in Los Angeles, but we can easily place it somewhere corresponding to a place where people could have intense physical pleasure that could lead to an ecstatic response. Given the nature of the Tradition, I think it best to not be associated with negative aspects of the sex industry or drug use. I would want something more benign. It’s probably some very posh, exclusive resort where all kinds of consensual hanky panky and bacchanialian celebrations occur. I think free love communes were already passe by 1992.

                          That means it would probably be close to LA, but not in the urban environments itself. The Cult of Ecstasy would want something more isolated and exclusive. Perhaps something in Orange County, Ventura County, or possible a small island off the coast (Santa Catalina island is the only one of the Channel Islands that has permanent civilian settlement - five are part of a national park, and the other two are used by the US Navy as training grounds). An exclusive private resort at Santa Catalina would work well IMO.

                          Hardly a place vampire PCs might come across, but you never know how actions by the PCs may turn out.

                              I’ve done some quick-and-dirty research into the Garou history in California (In WEREWOLF: THE WILD WEST and those WtA sourcebooks immediately available to me) so what follows is fairly sketchy - also, I need to reread your own posts on the LA situation, since other commitments required me to skim them - so here’s what I have so far.

                              BUT FIRST, I absolutely wanted to endorse my pet notion for any ‘Werewolves in LA’ sourcebook to be titled RAGE ACROSS THE VALLEY.

                              No, this doesn’t quite make sense based on the actual geography of Garou LA, but the mental image of a high school hallway with some hilariously cliche Valley Girl in pink slamming a fomori into the lockers whilst a dead ringer for Seth Green looks on haunts me (No, she is NOT in Crinos, she’s in Glabro, I am not a hack).


                              Anyway, back to the actual facts:-

                              - As noted, California was a hub for the Uktena (Presumably a major hub, given there seems to have been a significant local population), but the Tribe had lost it’s primacy by primacy by the 1830s.

                              At that point just about all the Tribes could be found on the Pacific Coast, but the Black Furies, Bone Gnawers, Iron Riders and Shadow Lords were already associated with San Francisco & LA by this point.


                              - NOTE: The Silver Fangs, Fenrir and Fianna are said to have led the drive from the East Coast to the Pacific (Though there’s some evidence that Silver Fangs also entered the area through Russian North America).

                              In any case the Silver Fangs seem to have become more invested in the Pacific Northwest (Although this is mentioned in a brief, plain statement without explanation).


                              - The Furies appear to have arrived in California with the Spanish, presumably via Mexico: they seem to have made common cause with Elder Brother and Younger Brother whilst shunning the larger settlements, though one would be surprised if familial connections to the settlers never bred trouble between Elder Brother and the Black Furies.

                              Given the importance of the mission system to early California, one would be surprised if the Order of Our Merciful Mother played no role in the local Fury population.


                              NOTE: Younger Brother almost certainly entered California from the Pacific Northwest, after Alaska and Canada their particular stronghold in North America (It should also be considered that Uktena in the West were reinforced by refugees from the East and Southeast: this may well apply to California).


                              - The Bone Gnawers wouod likely have entered California as Mountain Men: in fact it’s possible a few relict populations of the tribe linger in the Sierras and show up in LA from time to time (One wonders how they might differ from their hillbilly counterparts back East?).


                              - The Glass Walkers arrived in California as the Iron Riders (Presumably following the Continental Railroad) and it’s therefore easy to image the region as a place where old Iron Riders made the switch to Corporate Wolves: it’s worth noting that San Francisco and Los Angeles are said to be “meccas for Urban Werewolves” (and that the San Francisco & LA areas were of interest to the Garou even before serious urbanisation began).

                              Why this should be the case I’m not certain, but it occurs to me that the long-term oresence of Bone Gnawers, Glass Walkers and Shadow Lords in the region (Coupled with the apparent preference for the redwoods region found in other ‘European’ tribes) has resulted in a strongly progressive character for the Werewolf populations (Especially coupled with the curious absence of a Silver Fang royal house in the area).


                              NOTE: The fact that many West Coast Silver Fangs have Russian ancestry may help explain their relatively weak position in the area - courtesy of the first and second Red Scares, though the often republican tenor of US Garou presumably doesn’t help.


                              - Speaking of the Shadow Lords, there’s no real explanation for their presence in the region, but I wonder if they may have been drawn in by the general absence of Silver Fang houses and the presence of at least one seriously potent vampire: on the other hand it’s been noted that Black Spiral Dancers entered ‘The West’ with the Spanish Conquistadors, so it’s possible that both the Black Furies and Shadow Lords were following that particular spoor.

                              It’s also quite possible they were sick of dodging the Sabbat in increasingly-urban Mexico and liked what they saw in California (Especially the general absence of Silver Fangs until the 1800s).


                              - It also bears noting that the Stargazers have a moderately strong association with California: with San Francisco, obviously, but it’s mentioned that there is at least one tradition that Stargazers might have kept in touch with the ‘Three Brothers’ tribes even before Columbus arrived in the New World, possibly through their relicts in the future Russian Far East (and the Pacific Coast is the obvious place for these tribes to touch bases).


                              NOTE: I do wonder if Gaian Garou of non-Native extraction might have been pulled towards the region by the Cataclysm which affected the region in the 1600s, a conclusion that shook the Umbra so hard that the physical landscape of San Francisco felt it (Resulting in a major shift that is said to have opened up the Golden Gate itself, though this may be exaggeration).

                              One can only wonder if this may have been felt outside the San Francisco Bay region as well? If it were then it’s not hard to imagine interested parties sending packs on the sort of scouting expedition usually followed up by settlement parties.


                              That’s all I have so far (Other than the fact that John Blackfeather, a ‘Wendigo’, has a career in Hollywood films - presumably in a ‘Danny Trejo before he was famous’ or ‘Young Russel Means’ sort of capacity - which might mean he occasionally visits Los Angeles).

                              Please pardon the rather rough and ready mix of lore & speculation in this hasty post.
