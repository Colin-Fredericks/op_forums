    Question about Eightfold Yin Mantle (Yin Prana 4)

    02-11-2024, 11:04 AM
    Hello there!
    I was wondering how do you approach Yin Prana 4 in your chronicle (we are running KOTE in conjunction with V20/W20/M20/WTO20/C20).


    The problem lies in the fact that the book doesn't specify anything about duration, nor sets limits to the amount of Yin Chi the Cathayan can spend while conjuring the item.
    That means nothing would stop a seasoned Bone Flower from spending all 10 Yin Chi points to conjure a weapon that deals Strength + 9/Aggravated, which is not only unbreakable, but lasts indefinitely...

    Now you might be thinking, "well, just house rule that these conjured items last for a scene". Unfortunately, that would turn the use of this power to conjure weapons almost useless.
    Any Kuei-jin (without even Yin Prana) can make a Katana out of Black Jade, and spend one Yin point to charge it. By doing that, you will not only deal Strength+3 (+4 when two-handed) / Aggravated, but also strip Gnosis/Pathos away when striking a spirit/hengeyokai/wraith.

    Making a similar Katana with Eightfold Yin Mantle would cost somewhere between 4 to 5 Yin Chi points... and if it lasts for a scene I see no point to conjure it.

      Actually this is easy to answer

      "Spending Chi" - P.139
      Lesser Kuei-jin may spend only one point of Chi per turn. However, if a character has a high rating in her Dharma, she may spend more than one point per turn.
      See p. 54 for more information.

      Dharma lvl 4-5 - 2 Chi/turn
      Dharma level 6 - 3 Chi/turn
      Dharma level 7 - 4 Ci/turn
      Dharma level 8 - 6 Chi/turn
      Dharma level 9 - 8 chi/turn
      Dharma level 10 - ?

                  The Chi/turn limitation actually doesn't limit how much you can pump into the power. You can charge up an open-ended power over successive turns. The downside to this is that you'll probably end up quite low on juice; there was also some potential that spirits might take offense at "atomizing the local chi-scape", but that seems like it would take more power than you could muster at Dharma levels less than 6 or 7 unless you soaked up more chi than you could safely hold, which brings on the danger of making one of the Hungry Dead blow themselves up (a much more tangible danger than spirits not liking you when a lot of them probably already don't like you).

                                  In my chronicle I kind of merged it with the rules of Path of Conjuring (V20), though I am not a fan of deviating from the official books.
                                  Objects conjured using the Eightfold Yin Mantle must be mundane and non-complex. Such objects last for the remainder of the night, and crumble to dust upon sunlight exposure. Conjured weapons operate at difficulty 6, and inflict Strength +0/Aggravated damage, with the option to increase damage at the expense of additional Yin Chi points. Alternatively, the Cathayan can conjure weapons with default stats from the Official Arsenal by expending one Yin Chi, plus an extra Yin Chi per damage die or reduced difficulty. For example, conjuring a dagger (difficulty 4, Strength +1) would require a total of 4 Yin Chi. Eightfold Yin Prana users may never conjure anything that requires more Yin Chi points than their Yin Prana rating. Finally, objects can be made permanent by spending a Willpower point and two additional Yin Chi points.
