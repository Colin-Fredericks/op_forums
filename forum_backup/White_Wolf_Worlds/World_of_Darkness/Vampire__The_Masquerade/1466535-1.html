                Under Our Mission Statement in V5's guide, there are a few paragraphs and a speech about this. The former says that it organises kindred and by extension, humanity, to "improve our common world" based on the "single will" of the Inner Council. The latter describes how kindred conserved culture through the Dark Ages and will be able to conserve civilisation through global warming or nuclear armageddon. Both come across a bit unsubstantiated, but there's room for aspiration in the Camarilla, at least along its middle ranks, and it helps if you consider humanity to be incapable of looking after itself, but still valuable in some way. They also make the point that survival as a goal doesn't justify itself; they need purpose or failing that, to feel like they serve a purpose.

                    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                    1- OoC the Camarilla is limited to main membership of the 7 OG clans just because they're OG. It's a stupid restriction writers created for themselves just to make their work easier, but goes against the Cam stated purposes and have no weight on how it worked pre-V5. IC the idea of a clan asking for membership wasn't even a thing until Revised. Everyone is a member by default and the only thing keeping other clans from having Justicars and Councilors is lack of suitable candidates.
                    I think you are on to something here.

                    I make the distinction between the Camarilla as a global conspiracy that theoretically govern all vampires, and the clans that set on the Inner Circle. So on a certain level, it's never been about "these clans are in the Camarilla" it's about "these clans are represented on the Inner Circle." I think that's an important difference. In addition to the Camarilla itself, there's also what I call the "Camarilla system" which extends beyond it.

                    The Camarilla's relationship to the Assamites and Giovanni are good examples of that. Despite being Independent Clans, both clans were in fact heavily regulated by the Camarilla. The Treaty of Tyre explicitly controlled a lot of actions the Assamites could take. It regulated their hunts and regulated interactions the Assamites could do among the Camarilla domains. In the original clan description it stated the Assamites were called in as assassins by the Justicars, Archons, and Princes. So even though they were independent, the Camarilla had a lot of control over them. They operated within the system the Camarilla had created. The Assamites could theoretically have joined and been on the Inner Circle and become just like any other clan, but didn't want to because they preferred their weird diablerie religion and preferred to be heavily regulated contract assassins instead.

                    The Giovanni had a similar system. While the Promise of 1528 wasn't created until later, even in the original clan description we knew there had been a deal between them and the Camarilla. In exchange for not being destroyed, the Giovanni would have to be "neutral" and not interfere in Kindred affairs, aka not go against the Camarilla. Then we learned about restrictions like not embracing more than 3 Kindred every generation, and while not explicitly tied to the first part, it always made sense to me that was the reason for it. So again, while independent and not participating in Camarilla politics, the clan was regulated to a heavy degree. It was left alone provided it obeyed the rules. (This pretty much was thrown outside the window once Justin Achilli wrote the first Giovanni tribebook. As a result, I've encountered a lot of players who don't see how the Promise of 1528 controlled anything the Giovanni did - which completely ignored the original presentation.)

                    The other two independent clans didn't have anything like that, so obviously there were gaps. The nomadic Ravnos were just tolerated, but the clan had no organization at all so there was really nothing that could be done. Without some kind of organ that could speak of behalf of the clan, there was no way relations could be regulated. On the other hand, the Sabbat was equally ignored by the Ravnos.

                    The Followers of Set were an interesting case because originally they seemed to have been built as the "big bad" of the setting given their role as priests of the Wyrm. Whenever compared against the Sabbat, the Setites were supposed to come out as more evil. The most we get about their relationship with the Camarilla is a quote that the Camarilla wishes to use the Setites, but it is the Setites who are using them. Later in the Sabbat quote about the Setites, a Tzimisce elder complains that the Camarilla don't realize what a threat the Setites are, and that you "don't use a snake." That implies some kind of one hand washes the other thing is going on between the Camarilla and Setites. In the first Followers of Set clanbook gave us the interesting detail that the Ventrue actually invited them to join the Camarilla at the beginning. We're told IC that the Setites refused, but for all we know the other clans vetoed it after telling Hardestadt he was crazy for inviting the Setites. We also know that the Setites are also an enemy of the Sabbat since the Sabbat took in the Serpents of the Light. So we have a vague undefined relationship between the Camarilla and the Setites. The Setites are doing something that the Camarilla wants done.

                    So it's very clear - the Independent Clans are not the enemy of the Camarilla, and in fact work with them to some degree. They have their own reasons why they're not members, but still have regulations governing them and individual members could in theory work within Camarilla domains. Two of them have agreements, backed by force, that explicitly defines certain behavior. Another has some kind of quid pro quo relationship. All have some kind of autonomy. They don't have to worry about some Justicar coming up and judging their internal works, but it's very likely two or even three of these clans could have a Justicar show up to enforce a potential violation of some other agreement. Only the nomadic and unorganized Ravnos are ignored being left to the local domains to do with whatever they wanted. There's a system in place that govern these relationships. They don't affect internal Camarilla politics directly except by individuals showing up to Conclaves (since we're told those are open to all Kindred), and in return are left autonomous with their own internal affairs.

                    Now let's look at the Lasombra antitribu. We don't get details on them until much later. But it's very apparent that these Lasombra are in fact members of the Camarilla. They just don't have a set on the Inner Circle or have a Justicar. Presumably they have Princes and even have members who are archons. So that's another clan that excepts the authority of the Camarilla. Presumably if there were enough of them, they could have representation.

                    The Old Clan Tzimisce do their own thing in the Oradea League (or hidden among the True Black Hand). But I don't believe there isn't some kind of arrangement between the Oradea League and the Camarilla since they have common enemies in the Sabbat, and the two aren't in conflict. Just enough so there is recognition on borders and what to do if there's trouble between vampires between them. And of course we know there are some very few Tzimisce in the Camarilla itself, but probably so few as to be negligible in influence.

                    So it's never been the case that the Camarilla was actually only limited to 7 clans. It's just that only those seven clans actually had influence enough to sit on the Inner Circle and be politically important. The Assamites and Giovanni were heavily regulated and denied a spot at the table for a reason. The Setites could have been sitting on the Inner Circle, but didn't for plot purposes. But as the game got away from the idea that they were the Big Bad Guys of the setting in Revised, it made less and less sense that they wouldn't be members. And the only thing that prevented the Lasombra antitribu from having a seat at the Inner Circle was that there were just too few of them, but you could always change that in an organic way that still respected the setting. That really only left the Ravnos and Old Clan Tzimisce outside the system, mostly because they're numbers were small and they didn't organize among themselves.

                    Now this isn't how the game actually developed once the Revised era was reached. At that point, any subtle relationships were ditched so the metaplot railroad could go move from station to station. The Treaty of Tyre and the Blood Curse was jettisoned, the Promise of 1528 seemed to not restrict Giovanni in any conceivable way begging the question of what was its purpose then, clans could begin willy nilly leave and join the Camarilla just by having one of its members say so. But in the actual core setting, we do have some inkling of the Camarilla fulfilling its potential as an actual worldwide conspiracy of vampires. In practice, the Justicars and archons are too few and not powerful enough to enforce everything they want. But there is certainly quite a number of vampires who at least theoretically accept the Camarilla's authority (in some capacity) despite their clan not being on the Inner Circle or having a Justicar.

                        Is survival still really an issue when you're centuries or even millenia old? Is it something you consider on a daily basis? Sure, you don't want to be obliterated, and you've probably become accustomed to your condition over time and grown fond of it, but survival is very much a notion to the frail - or those in a frail situation. Camarilla Elders are rarely in that category. Particularly not in modern nights.

                        Don't get me wrong, I find the question interesting, as I'm running a Victorian Age campaign, where I have to find the main purpose for the Camarilla in a growing London with tons of new technology and innovations. In this setting I have made the Cam's main purpose to affect and in some cases control innovations and creations, so that it fits the Vampire world underneath. I don't see them as a sect or the UN (as suggested) - I simply see them as a group of Elders who're using old conventions as a mean to control younger vampires, who they use as pawns to further their goals. The Masquerade is still in effect, but where the younger vampires (who are often more frail) consider it a mean for survival and keeping the Sabbat at bay, the Elders mainly see it as a way to keep the younger vampires and possible intruders in check.

                        But that's just one take on it, I guess.

                                Originally posted by Spencer from The Hills View Post
                                Under Our Mission Statement in V5's guide.
                                Despite my personal opinion on how the Cam changed in V5, this does highlights one important aspect the sect always had, that is its "guidance" of the mortal world.

                                The Camarilla want a stable world, and that isn't just about safety, but also progress. And they understand that in this regard excessive depredation must be controlled, because Kindred prosperity derives from Human prosperity.

                                Which comes first and if you see this as primary a moral or practical stand is besides the point. For the Camarilla the strength of society is paramount and the mortal side can't be ignored. Corruption is obviously accepted, as they are still hypocrites, but greater depredation must be tamed. After all a vampire only benefits from a herd that is healthy and can provide luxury.


                                Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                                I make the distinction between the Camarilla as a global conspiracy that theoretically govern all vampires, and the clans that set on the Inner Circle.
                                Personally I like the Camarilla as Compromise more than the Camarilla as Global Conspiracy, but even keeping the later the notion of membership in it wasn't that straightforward before Revised.

                                The history of membership is convoluted mostly due to Doylist decisions that had to be then justified from the Watsonian perspective against original intent. But even then, we're talking mostly about how Clans decide to react to them instead of how the Cam receives the clans.

                                All the non-Camarilla clans are so because they decided that. Assamites and Giovanni chose to enter a pact to operate in a Prince's Domain but keep their clan business independent. Followers of Set just don't show up in any big event, but their individual members usually follow the rules of a Camarilla city they're in. Ravnos can't really answer as a clan outside India, period. Even the Sabbat clans just don't have enough numbers in the Camarilla, they're not legally regarded as Sabbat clans instead of Camarilla.

                                You can picture this difference clearly in the Promise, despite it being a later development. At first it seems like the Promise is just the Camarilla imposing a limit on the Giovanni, but... actually, it is the pre-approval of their Embraces on a global level, so they can do so without asking (but of course informing) the local Prince. A Giovanni that decides to live full on as Camarilla don't need to follow the Promise. They just ask to the Prince like everyone else.

                                None of those things apply to individuals. If any individual Kindred decides to just take part in Camarilla society, they follow the same rules and have the same rights as everyone else. Of course that things may not be simple in practical terms, but no actual rule or above-Prince decision keep them from achieving anything or occupying any position. You could have a city with a loyal Camarilla Tzimisce or Assamite Prince and that's that.

                                Indeed this happens at Archon level. Justicars may nominate anyone they want as Archons and almost everything have some precedence here. Karl Schrekt, the Tremere Justicar that established many of the practices of the office and in the nomination of Archons already had a Setite under his command, so anything goes.

                                Revised started to change things to railroad metaplot, and created the very notion of a clan leaving or joining the Camarilla. V5 fully embraced the idea. Of course this is all well for anyone who likes it, and makes a lot of sense if you think the Cam as a closed organization as the Sabbat is, which is good to emphasize sect conflicts. I personally don't like it.

                                                        I’m coming a bit late although I do think that leaving aside the Masquerade as a given alongside general survival does it’s importance as a mission statement some disservice.

                                                        [*Disclaimer: most of my understanding is based on pre-V5 lore so YMMV as far as V5 is concerned]

                                                        [**Disclaimer II: This post does not take into account any other gamelines]

                                                        The Masquerade is best understood as both a passive and active goal imo. Obviously the passive is very important in that breaches need to be covered to prevent individual vampires from being found out or vampiric society as a whole being revealed.

                                                        I do think though that if you look at the Masquerade as an active goal of the Camarilla it gives an insight into the mindset of the elders and the sect’s goals.

                                                        The founders of the Camarilla touted the Masquerade as a banner for vampirdom to rally behind. All the elders of Christendom were wary of ceding any of their hard won and maintained power but circumstances were truly desperate. Warfare, plague, and general calamity had devastated the mortals and so the hunting fields had been winnowed while a sense of apocalyptic hysteria was in the air. The Inquisition represented both the reaction of the mortal institutions of Christendom against the chaos of the time and vampires would be on the block as a cause, but even more then the Papacy’s desperate efforts to restore order the general populace feared that the Time of Judgement was near at hand. A desperate and zealous people were relatively easy to provoke into either revolt or monster hunting - after all their souls would surely be redeemed if they lost their lives pursuing the destruction of demonic forces. This environment would have been bad enough if it were the mortals alone in tumult but the young had risen up in revolt against their elders because of these material circumstances and violence tore through every clan in Christendom. To the Noddists it would have been one more sign that the promised reckoning of Gehenna was near at hand.

                                                        It was in this environment of fear and desperation that the elders were susceptible to a revolutionary proposal. The Founders of the Camarilla provided that proposal and the Masquerade would be a linch pin for their argument: these catastrophes had happened because vampires had rejected ancient wisdom and exposed themselves to the Children of Seth. If there was ever to be peace again then every kindred vampire needed to unite their power to hide themselves from humanity. They would have to guide from the shadows, lest they one day be totally destroyed. It would require not only a confederacy of elders to accomplish this but a centralized body with the power to enforce the Masquerade and the other necessary traditions for ensuring peace. Enough elders were convinced and so the Camarilla was born.

                                                        In the centuries since the Camarilla formed the Masquerade was the central focus: not only as a passive mandate but in active measures. The Camarilla had a vested interest in supporting every possible measure which would not only hide individual vampires from the herds but would persuade humanity that vampires did not exist at all. Supporting the Protestant Reformation would be critical to undermining the power of the Catholic Church in Christendom - one organization so vested in the mystical world that it would be effectively impossible to remove belief in demons and vampires from them. They supported the rise of the Italian Renaissance, which also formed the basis for much of the tradition Camarilla social system. Humanism, Rationalism, Enlightenment, and the Scientific Revolution. Every one of these received support from the gestalt resources of the Camarilla so that they could succeed.

                                                        The goals of the Camarilla hasn’t changed. It benefits them to bankroll a world without any place for the mystical or unnatural. So they continue to dedicate resources into actively creating a world governed by philosophies which reject even the remotest possibilities of their existence.

                                                        I think that through the lenses of the Masquerade you can learn a great deal about the mind of the Camarilla and what motivates it to act tonight as much as it motivated it to act before. The Camarilla has always been a gothic allegory for institutions of power and the Masquerade provides a key part of the explanation for why it invests as actively as it does in those institutions of power to these very nights.
