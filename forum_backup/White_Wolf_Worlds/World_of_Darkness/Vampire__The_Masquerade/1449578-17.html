            Originally posted by Professor Phobos View Post
            Morality isn't as simple as "beliefs go in, behavior comes out." People have empathy, intuitions, and social instincts that are not produced by specific social pressures or by philosophical convictions.

            Paths didn't turn off the game's fundamental conflict, they just altered it. If you're on Humanity and you act against your beliefs you risk falling into wassail. If you're on a Path of Enlightenment and you act against your beliefs, you risk falling into wassail. It's the same conflict, it's just that someone on a Path and someone on Humanity have different beliefs. The risk and conflict and all of that are still fundamentally there though.

            So I don't think your Cal of Cthulhu comparison is terribly fair. To make the Call of Cthulhu comparison more accurate, rather than making the character immune to SAN lose, it would be like changing out the list of events that can cause a character to suffer SAN loss. So as you play the game your character is still at risk of losing SAN, and the results of losing SAN are the same, it's just that the things that cause the character to lose SAN are different than the default base list.

                    I've never used the old mechanics for switching from humanity to a path in my game, mostly because I find the idea of the player randomly becoming a wight very dull. Instead, I've made it a pure roleplay thing. For example, a player wants to transition to Honorable Accord, and so has to hold to a series of sworn oaths to his teacher even when physically or emotionally painful. Being ordered to just stand there stoically while having his hand chopped off. Killing a friend from his human days. Everything designed to instill a sense of icy cold stoicism in the face of difficulties keeping your word.

                    Honorable Accord is easily the easiest to do this with. The lessons are basically military discipline training combined with fraternity hazing, taken up a notch or three.

                    Feral Heart is the next easiest. Half of it is instilling rank tribalism, a tribalism so extreme that it creates indifference to anything not immediately advantageous to the pack. The other half is about letting go of material concerns beyond immediate survival, rather like Diogenes.

                    Path of Caine is basically just acting like an Honorable Accord mentor half the time, and a fundamentalist preacher the other half.

                    Death and the Soul is fairly easy as well, since it is mostly about purging emotions. I can basically have mentors pontificate like a Vulcan while performing vivisections. Metamorphosis is similar, just with a few details changed.

                    Past that it gets more difficult. I am aware that Power/Inner Voice is based off of Ayn Rand thinking, and I've slogged through 'The Fountainhead", but I confess that STing someone transitioning to this Path is something I've been mediocre at best at accomplishing. Cathari is a stuggle for me as well. Fortunately it doesn't often come up, as I usually start my games with characters already on their Paths, and it is up to them to bring it to life. Still, would love some advice on roleplaying mentors of characters seeking to switch to Power/Inner Voice or Cathari. I'm honestly at a loss when trying to roleplay an NPC mentoring someone in these two paths.

                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                                    They roll pretty much a normal amount of times for a game, IMHO. I think one thing this is illustrating is that V5 was meant to leave rolling for critical actions versus roleplay.
                                                    I don't think there is a 'right' amount of rolling. Some tables are all roleplay and narrative, and hence a form of storytelling; others are more game-like, and rolling dice is a big part of the tension there. Most games are somewhere in between.

                                                    Some people don't like social interaction in-character for various reasons. They find it socially painful. Those players might rely on dice rolling more frequently.

                                                    The only way I could get one neurodiverse player to join us was to allow him not to have to say anything IC and roll for all his social interactions instead.

                                                    Such a player is not catered for if your system is 'try not to roll wherever possible'. He also really enjoys rolling dice, so there's that, too.

                                                    Gamers can also be superstitious about their dice (putting them in the freezer, blowing on them, etc) and it's a big part of the gaming experience.

                                                    Unless you actively design a diceless roleplaying game, you should accept that the right number of rolls is highly subjective, so your rules should work if you have lots of rolls as well as if you have fewer rolls.

                                                        Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post
                                                        I've never used the old mechanics for switching from humanity to a path in my game, mostly because I find the idea of the player randomly becoming a wight very dull. Instead, I've made it a pure roleplay thing.
                                                        Yeah, I privately grade the player on their roleplay while they're still being mentored during their humanity days, then decide whether they survived the process based on that. A bad roll can spoil a lot of effort, so I'd say the roll determines how smoothly the process went. Do it perfectly and transition cleanly into your path, mess it up and spend time in control of the beast before your new sanity slowly puts itself into place.

                                                        I confess that STing someone transitioning to this Path is something I've been mediocre at best at accomplishing. Cathari is a stuggle for me as well. Fortunately it doesn't often come up, as I usually start my games with characters already on their Paths, and it is up to them to bring it to life. Still, would love some advice on roleplaying mentors of characters seeking to switch to Power/Inner Voice or Cathari. I'm honestly at a loss when trying to roleplay an NPC mentoring someone in these two paths.
                                                        The way I would interpret these paths and the learning (hopefully this is coherent, because my thought processes tend to ramble):

                                                        Power and the Inner Voice
                                                        The student is here to learn absolute mastery of their own self and then how to exert this control over the world around them. And pursuit of power begins with power over oneself.
                                                        Expect punishment, a lot.

                                                        Make a point of illustrating every single tiny failure and flaw, and make sure that all of these are punished. From your student, from your lessers, from your superiors, from yourself. The message must be diven home: Fuck ups are never tolerated. Ever. Only when one is perfect can one acheive enlightenment.

                                                        Wealthy victorians used to test the maids by putting coins under the rug. If the maid found the coins and didn't return them to the house owners, she was a thief and was fired. If she didn't find them she was incompetent, and was fired. Students can look forward to things like this, because the mentor will make mistakes on purpose to test that the student can spot them and will hold them responsible. Find a situation to illustrate how those who are not fit to lead can always be replaced.

                                                        Give your students projects and responsibilities. Maybe grade them, or something. Reprimand them for missing an oportunity to advance. Point out where they went wrong. Point out and explain their mistakes, but do not give them advice on how to fix it, they must improve through their own understanding.

                                                        Your student is to develop a keen insight into human behaviour and how to manipulate it. They'll learn how to use every emotion under the sun, and then they'll learn which ones matter. Fear, hate, desire.
                                                        Illustrate through your lessons how love fades, how mere loyalty is bought and sold. And when the student finds their underlings have screwed up because of these things, punish them. If the underlings fail, it means that the superior has failed to control them adequately.

                                                        Test that they understand they subtleties of what they are currently manipulating. Review their conduct. Question them, have them write essays or talk you through it or something. They will learn that to know how to manipulate you must know how the pieces fit together. The more you understand, the more you can manipulate, the more you can manipulate, the deeper your knowledge of creation runs and the closer you get to Enlightenment.

                                                        Stamp out weakness. Provide opportunities for them to discover their weaknesses, and make the punishment memorable so that it sticks out to them. Gaslight them. Smoke cigarettes in front of them and then put them out on their eye if they flinch from the flame. Break bones at random times, punish them again if they cry out. Teach them not to unintentionally make expressions or display body language. This will continue until the student has perfect control of their reactions at all times.
                                                        The student is expected to start punishing themselves for mistakes too. If you can't recognise your flaws for what they are and demand better of yourself, you should not be here.

                                                        But despite all this talk of punishment, do not punish if there's nothing to punish. Reward success adequately, but not to the degree that the student expects reward as a given. Unifiers are to seek power and enlightenment, not to indulge in meaningless sadism. This is your student, and you are responsible for them. If you cannot control their tutelage well enough to lead them to success, then that is your failure.



                                                        Path of Cathari
                                                        (Not one I typically allow except for NPCs.)
                                                        All reasoning that leads you away from indulgence in vulgar material needs comes from the higher soul, which, as a servant of the demiurge and jailer of souls, you should not have. It deters vampires from their divinely ordained role, ergo they must learn to destroy that voice of higher reason in their head. Kill off sympathy, trust, adherance to expectations and laws of society.

                                                        Learn your student's hangups and desires. Invade their privacy, gain their trust, then use what you've learned to errode their soul.

                                                        The mentor will do what they want, take what they want, say what they want, all the second a whim pops into their head because there's nothing in there to tell them not to. They'll do anything to anything and anyone, ranging from the helpful to the innocuous to the mildly creepy and cruel to the utterly violating and monstrous, and then they'll probably move on and forget about it, because to them it's all no different than making a passing remark about the weather. Desire is desire. The Cathar experiences all their wants and whims as the same, with no distinction (they understand that others distinguish in degrees however, and know how to use it to their advantage, and in the education of their students)
                                                        It's supposed to be degrading, it's supposed to be shaming. Your student must grow numb to these instincts of restraint through the acts of kindness and abuse you inflict on them (gnosis is a futile goal made for humans to seek, you are damned and don't need these things), and later learn how to inflict these lessons on others to fufill their duty.

                                                        A student will learn betrayal. Test them often, betray them often in unexpected ways. Reward them for passing their tests with gifts that later turn out to be poisonous. Actually reward them sometimes, so they know not to trust in your predictability. Reward them for nothing at all. Expose them to the betrayals that others commit in the world, from the little things to the largest.

                                                        Killing is the only thing the Cathar is forbidden from. I'd expect tests where the student is meant to kill somebody without remorse and is (without prior explanation) brutally punished. Then the mentor explains the fruitlessness of killing and how it unravels all the work put into their duty of corrupting them.

                                                        The student will be abused until they are incapable of feeling shame, compassion and trust. They must learn not to give a damn about any reasons not to do what they want except for a direct threat to their existence (reincarnating will reset all they've learnt).
                                                        On the plus side, a Cathar mentor will probably offer every luxury and indulgence you could dream of when they're not busy abusing you out of your mind. Or during.

                                                        I'd also expect some conflict with the Setites, considering they're sort of heretical to each other.

                                                            On the plus side, a Cathar mentor will probably offer every luxury and indulgence you could dream of when they're not busy abusing you out of your mind. Or during.

                                                            I'd also expect some conflict with the Setites, considering they're sort of heretical to each other.
                                                            This is one of the reasons I love Paths so much! So much story potential right there.
