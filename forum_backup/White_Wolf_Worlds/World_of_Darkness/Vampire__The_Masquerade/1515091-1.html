    When it comes to vampire populations throughout history, I tend to follow this kind of line of thought;

    In Antiquity the local vampire club membership might not even register in double digits; outside of "metropolitan" areas, that's a population that can be spread across (what will one day be) entire countries. Ghouls take the place of neonates (with your domitors being, like, 8th generation or lower, and the 4th generation being active, disciplines past the first dot are way more common).
    Most embraces take place when a vampire decides to go into torpor and needs somebody to look after their stuff for a few centuries while they're gone (with a clear warning that they'll be back for it one night)

    - The vamps are claiming tribes and notable families/institutions and playing long distance strategy games with them, much like they will later. The toys and the rules may adapt, but the spirit of the game stays the same.
    - Respecting and obeying your elders is way more important when they're what's protecting you when the Garou are nowhere near critically endangered.
    - In many places the other clans are considered a myth or are totally unheard of.

    The numbers of vampires in an area often fluctuates hard for much of human history. Club membership increases with population growth (allowing siring more frequently) and immigration from foreign childer looking to strike out from under their sire's thumbs accompanying human invasions and migration periods (which slowly introduces the 13 clans into every area over the centuries). It also decreases when the herd population crashes during times of plagues and war, which may wipe entire tribes and permanent settlements off the map.

    The "modern" system starts shaping in the Medieval period, when stuff like that newfangled feudal system comes into fashion. London has about 6-10 vamps. Paris had around 20 whole vampires at one point. Cairo can support somewhere just over 30. Rural areas are "family" affairs, with one elder plus a childe or two and a few ghouls claiming domain over a town, village, fiefdom, whatever. It's pretty much 1 domain = 1 coterie.
    Meanwhile, the Inquisition does a damn good job of almost driving vampires totally extinct.

    So far, embraces have been uncommon enough that generations above 10th haven't been seen yet (at least not enough to be common knowledge).

    The Industrial Revolution sees rapid population growth amongst the kine, allowing for vamp population growth. There are enough vampires to form rival coteries within the same city. Generation 11+ start to show themselves, introducing the first generation of thinbloods (in the modern nights, generations 11-13 are shocked to learn that the Elders file them under this umbrella). World Wars start putting in a few dents, as well as clearing space for new blood in places.

    Growth continues. By the time we've reached the 21st century vampire populations can be sustained in the triple digits. No longer is the undead club a tight knit community where everyone knows everybody else. The kids have forgotten that 10th generation is the cutoff for a "real" vampire; the reasons the Traditions were codified seem out of touch; they don't feel threatened with the notion that human hunters can successfully wipe them *all* from the earth; they aren't convinced that "filial piety" towards the Elders is required, because are werewolves even real? 9 times out of 10, if you go out into the woods you won't even see one, and those that do don't live to spread the news.

    The Elders have spent centuries in a totally different environment before the world suddenly sprung this set up on them and are just a bit irritated. Jesus Christ, there are childer everywhere, when did their domains turn into playgrounds full of entitled screeching brats??​

    Prone to hyperbole
