    Originally posted by Amethyst View Post
    Nothing wrong with playing an emo teenager.
    I agree, an emo teenager is a good starting place for a character. But I also feel that given the harshness of the setting, they must eventually change, grow stronger, become more cunning, more self disciplined, etc. to survive.

    The Kindred of the East books had a term for this exact sort of fledgling, a "running monkey" which covered the first century of unlife for a new vampire. They were expected to revel in their new power, tend to left over business from their mortal days, and more or less be non-stop collateral damage. But after that point they were expected to act like a proper monster, rather than a spring breaker with fangs. This is why I tend to frown whenever I see a vampire write up which makes them centuries old, never gone into torpor, and yet are still acting like a highschooler.

    Alternatively, I could be just old enough that I should start looking to invest in a rocking chair for the front porch so I can properly holler at the whippersnappers to get off my lawn.

            Using 1/4th rather than 3/4ths of the day is horrendous, and drinking blood to live is going to be difficult at first, but these are things you get used to. The main concern is the political horror, but even that's mitigatable: If you go by pre-5, the Beast is something you can deal with, if you go by V5, you'll probably experience the power of agentic shift in regards to the Beast. It's fine. The real issues are political, but even then you can mitigate them pretty well and live for hundreds of years.

            My early characters were always ambitious and paranoid. In-game Paranoia gets you to do some crazy shit. This would spill over into a bit of out of game paranoia (IE The ST will screw me over if I don't detail or justify everything) also helps. Later on, I realised I was doing things wrong: you're not up against the world, you're part of the conspiracy along with every other vampire: don't lose yourself to short term ambition, be everyone's supportive best friend, don't be afraid of a little debt, don't worry about resources, don't worry too much about the other players, The tighter you grasp, the more everything slips away. Just accept your relative lack of control. Also, OoC, I think that if the ST knows you're careful and paranoid, he's more likely to have fun screwing with you than the less careful characters.

            Rather than take 5 dots of Generation like a noob, it's far better for your character's mental health to take a good social starting point. This will lead to better decisions. Just like RL, A good relationship with your parental figure is the best thing for the development of a neonate: Mentor is one of the best backgrounds for staying sane. It's help when you need it, shelter when you need it, and it's deterrence. Unless you're planning to be an interdependent coterie, it's essential for neonates. 1 mostly just wards off small fries, 2+ is enough to keep most happy with you. Status kinda works in it's place if you don't want Mentor (I haven't seen it really used so much). The second thing on the list is Domain. If you've got at least two dots to yourself, you've got a great foundation to accumulate other backgrounds; you don't need to have your businesses or herd in your domain, but it sure makes things easier. After you've got these two things (and perhaps pool domain with your mentor) what comes next is up to you.

            If the sire (and by extension, the player) plans his neonate's starting point smartly and properly educates a half-decent choice of childer, that childe will have few serious difficulties and will become a valued and productive member of the sire's brood and the wider cityscape and sect. If we're very filial, the childe can even run the sire's things while he's in Torpor. A wholesome, long lasting partnership of mutual benefit.

                            Blood Potency
                            1 - Fledgeling
                            2 - 1 year
                            3 - 5 years
                            4 - 10 years
                            5 - 50 years
                            6 - 100 years
                            7 - 500 years
                            8 - 1000 years
                            9 - 5000 years
                            10 - 10.000 years

                            So basically, a 1 Blood Potency would be like a 13th gen, and 10 like a 3rd gen.

                            Now, there would be some downsizes of higher potency; I was initially thinking that for each point of potency you'd need the same amount of blood per vitae restored. For example, if you have Potency 3, you need to drink 3 points of blood from a mortal in order to restore 1 vitae to your pool.

                            That would go up to 5 Potency. After that, you'd need vampiric Blood only; so, Potency 6 means 1 Vitae from a Potency 1 vampire to restore 1 vitae of your pool, Potency 7 needs 2 vitae per point restored and so on.

                            Also, drinking from vamps with higher Potency is better - each Potency 2 vamps vitae is like 2 vitae from a Potency 1, Potency 3 is 3 times as nourishing and so on.

                            And at Potency 10, only diablerri can quenchy your thristy.

                            So, there would be some advantages to reducing your Potency, with Potency 5 being the rule for older vampires that still live in the vampiric society.

                            How do vampires reduce their Potency? The first way is by going into Torpor. Potency is reduced in the inversed time. For instance, to reduce from Potency 6 to 5, you would have to go into Torpor for 100 years.

                            The second and easier way, is to sireing progeny.

                            Each progeny that you sire reduces your Potency by 1. And your Progeny's Potence equals your new rating -1. Therefore, if you have Potency 6 and you Embrace, you'll go down to Potency 5 and your Progeny's Potency will already start at 4.

                            This also means that ANY vampire needs AT LEAST Potency 3 to Embrace; if a Potency 2 Embraces someone, the vamp loses 1 Potency but the target DIES; if a Potency 1 tries, BOTH DIE.

                            This means that no vampire can Embrace more than once every 5 years.

                            This leads to several changes in vampiric society - including elders methusas becoming hunters hunted, and Princes FORCING all vampires to embrace AS OFTEN AS THEY CAN in order to keep cainite pops stable, and having to keep a balance between decent stable numbers and somewhat decent Potency - as I was also planning to have Potency to be added to dice pools or some such

                                    The smiling gun-knight and Kakost the way both of you are viewing the Prince is dependent on VtM being a street level game.

                                    1. The tradition of the elder giving permission was originally supposed to be permission from your sire or higher of your own bloodline to expand the family, Princes have argued that they also have that power. But if you are in a different domain and get permission from your sire or from a different prince, then the first prince doesn't have a right to complain. Yes they can proclaim that the domain can not sustain additional vampires, but since no crime was inherently committed, the childer can only stay temporarily, just like any other kindred who is just passing through.

                                    2. Speaking of temporary, how long is that exactly? If a vampire in the domain owns property, has mortal retainers who maintain it, and the vampire goes into Torpor for a couple decades, does the vampire lose their property just because he missed some club house meetings? If a vampire often travels, does that mean he can't have a permanent home to come back to? While during the Dark Ages it made sense that cities were islands of survival for vampires, once we hit the Victorian Age, the Old West or the Modern era, vampires aren't restricted from being mobile or getting boons from multiple Princes.

                                    3. How does a Princes authority apply when you own property, an industry, or some other power in multiple cities? Most STs and players tend to think in single city/domain storyline terms. In reality though an individual can have "domain" across multiple cities and not need to be kindred. From character generation you can make an Elon Musk, Jeff Bezos, or Bill Gates type, but if you have houses/havens in multiple states, factories in multiple countries, and a thriving internet business, exactly which Prince do you have to listen to? Does the prince who rules where your house is or where your server farm have rule over you?

                                    This is why I poke at the concept that V:tM lore, traditions, and fluff is more Lost Boys biker gang vampires than vampire power brokers or CEOs. The rules and mechanics just don't connect with business realties after the age of discovery. Once you have colonies or international business groups, those notions of city states kind of fall apart.

                                    Playing an emo teen vampire drifter, professional assassin, or Lestat style rocker can be cool, but occasionally running a business CEO type can be fun as well. The rules and mechanics just tend to face plant into a brick wall when you start thinking in those terms.
