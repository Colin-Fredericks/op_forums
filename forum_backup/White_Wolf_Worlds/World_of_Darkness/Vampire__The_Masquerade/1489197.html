    Nosferatu - sewer orientation

    08-05-2022, 03:56 AM
    In our last session (Victorian Age) we went on dodgy quest in the London Sewers to engage the local Nosferatu Brood. While debating rules of engagement and all the usual shit it suddenly dawned on us that as scary as the Nosferatu are on their own turf, they don't have any clan disciplines which'll enable them to actually see, hear or smell better than any mortal down there.

    How do you go about their impaired senses in the sewers?

      There are several ways, but first I think it is relevant to keep in mind that generally they won't be engaging in hunting or combat within the sewers. The Nosferatu still hunt above ground more than not, or rely on Animalism, and most of the sewer system is used as transit and escape route, with only the Warren itself being something more. The Warren may be less lit than the above ground, but it is still lit and fitted to have basic conveniences. In Victorian Age London it is possible that the Warren is in an old cave, maybe even at a hidden Mithraeum, connected to the sewers but not a part of them, and even in modern settings it is usually a cave beneath the system.

      So when we talk about the "average" Nosferatu, their needs to navigate the sewers aren't that exceptional. While exploring an unknown passage you'll probably bring light, as you'll do at home, while you're more likely to go in the dark when wandering through known ways. They may even favor tunnels that are usually lit either through grates or by maintenance crew, so they don't have to worry about that as their Obfuscate already takes care of not being seen by such mortals.

      With that, you only really need a select few of them to be actual dark stalkers. One or two members of the Warren may be encouraged to develop such abilities for strategic use, anything else would be left to the quirks of its members, so you may have some Nosferatu that develop Auspex OoC or any other tactic to deal with it, but as a matter of personal development, not a standard clan-wide tactic.

                              The Nosferatu are an interesting clan that can be implemented in many different ways which each solve and create various problems.

                              If you treat the Nosferatu as a bunch of hackers, then really all you need is a basement within digging range of a communications backbone line. Not much to it other than simply knowing that the warren exists.

                              If you treat the Nosferatu as a mixed bag of stock types, like the Warren shown in VtM: Bloodlines, then yeah whatever they can find or modify will make do. Each clan member has a small cubby hole to call their own. The path to get down there will be winding, but most of it will be lit by sanitation lights or other rigged systems. Connection to the surface will be relatively close by since warren members will need to hunt or gather intel for trade.

                              Then there is the last way to use the Nosferatu, which is to create a completely separate society underground. Between Potence, Animalism, and mutated animal Ghouls, the Nosferatu can build entire cities underground and at depths that wouldn't interact with most human cities. The access points would be guarded, and guides would accompany coteries to get them up to the levels we normally think of as deep. The abandoned and sealed off train stations we normally think of as a Nosferatu lair would actually be a waypoint to stop off at when going to or returning from the surface. This sort of design that everything below the human made tunnels is completely setup for the Nosferatu to have an advantage.

                              Which setup you use determines how much the Nosferatu need help in seeing or knowing their surroundings, though I would point out that Animalism as a clan discipline means you can use animal guides whose senses are sharper than yours to get around.

                                  Clan Disciplines are over rated. Every vampire can learn any Discipline. And they would.

                                  Because every non-Camarilla clan had a signature Discipline associated with them that no one else had, and they were meant to be antagonists with mystery powers, STs began clamping down on players having access to other Disciplines that weren't "in-clan". Otherwise you'd have your Camarilla coterie already knowing Obtenebration or Quietus, and the Sabbat and Assamites wouldn't be so mysterious and scary.

                                  And in LARP, certain powers were much more useful in the artificial constraints of any LARP game. So everyone wanted Potence and Celerity because LARP combat absolutely required you have that. And pretty soon you had vampires regardless of clan all knowing the same stuff. The different clans didn't seem to be different. So many STs there clamped down and issued restrictive rules on what it took to learn out of clan Disciplines. But all it did was slow down players a little bit. But it got many people used to the idea you could only learn your in-clans naturally, and needed something special if you wanted out of clan - other clans to teach you their Disciplines, maybe drinking some blood, or whatever artificial constraints STs try to impose so clan members seem to be from their clan instead of all having Brujah Disciplines.

                                  But that kind of dynamic only exists in artificial environments like LARP. They don't make sense in the setting. Paying a few points of XP more for an out-of-clan Discipline doesn't make it that much harder to gain a Discipline.

                                  Naturally tall people are better adapted to play basketball than shorter people. But that does not mean all tall people become NBA players, like basketball, or are even good at basketball; or that shorter people can't ever become good basketball players or even play in the NBA. Having a slight advantage in potential (which is all that an in-clan Discipline is versus an out-of-clan) to develop a skill is not the same as only relying on that skill as opposed to learning other skills.

                                  So what is my solution to the idea that Nosferatu need a means to see in the dark while they are down in the sewer? Easy, many learn the first rank of Protean if that's what they need. Just like many other vampires also learned it despite not having it in-clan. Protean should not be some "secret" known only to the Gangrel. It's just easier for them to learn, and their clan culture encourages it because they selectively embrace many people who don't live in dense cities and need many of the powers of Protean to survive.

                                  It's also fine to have some amount of torches, siphoned electricity to power light bulbs, oil lamps, and other artificial light sources inside the warrens. They can have networks of animals they control and instruct to issue warnings to others based on when they notice certain things so the clan gets informed.

                                                  MyWifeIsScary You didn't actually prove your point, you just stated it was better and then discussed things that don't connect.

                                                  In a single clan city, there is no primogen council, there is no competing clan interests, I won't say there is a lack of politics but I will say there are fewer politics.

                                                  The result of this means that the city and clan is run more like a business/corporation than a community of monsters that try to tolerate each other. If a vampire steps out to line there are a lot fewer hurdles to overcome if there are no factions or clans to negotiate with. Like wise if a mortal is unlikely to maintain the status quo then they would never be considered for the embrace based on clan standards, and with a single clan system you don't have to worry about someone else embracing a problematic mortal which you have to deal with.

                                                  This sort of regimented setup means that rather than have a few dozen vampires hunting every night, you have official feeding houses, rather than hunting grounds. Not to mention that Hunters will have a much harder time isolating a vampire to kill them if they aren't scattered and disunited. Additionally, how much easier would it be for visiting kindred to be kept in pre setup havens rather than just hoping they moved on? The mixed clan city is a logistics nightmare, a single clan city is also a headache, but it has fewer problems.

                                                  In the case of Nosferatu the benefits become even more pronounced. They could have an entire undercity a couple thousand feet below the surface, their herds are the offspring of tunnel people so there is no record of them ever existing, an animal ghoul army worthy of Clan Moulder from Warhammer Fantasy could be on standby, and the surface is watched by specially trained human ghouls and Obfuscated Nosferatu patrols. Anyone who tries to set up shop would be told to leave or the entire clan would crush them.

                                                  If you think about how Vampires function, the need to feed, the avoidance of daylight, and the need to stay hidden, I feel that vampire society would gravitate away from the psuedo-adventure hook design. Would the design be adventure friendly, no of course not, but at the same it wouldn't be stupidly contradictory either.

                                                  A good example of contradictory design is that all vampires are nocturnal predators, all vampires are afraid of fire, only a few clans can see in the dark to some extent, but the electric lightbulb is only about a century and a half old. Technically most if not all vampires wouldn't be able to function in pre-light bulb cities because every single candle flame would run the risk of exposing them due to vampiric freak outs. Most end up hand waving this, but if it ignored that much, it shouldn't be part of the design.

                                                  So how then would vampires' function? Pre-electric lights there wasn't a lot of nighttime travel, especially since technically gas streetlights are open flames. Then add on the concept that in theory there are baying mobs of lupines circling every city just waiting for a random vampire to wander too far from home, despite the fact that a vampire would have a hard time functioning in an old-time city.

                                                      Your argument hinges on the idea that you need a Primogen council if you have multiple clans. You don't. So it's an argument built upon a fallacy.

                                                      "Primogen council" is just some shlock that got popularized by... Chicago by night I think? Clan Primogen aren't an office mandated by the Camarilla, it's an idea a few cities liked and a few too many authors latched onto it not knowing they could do different. It's not a thing that's going to happen in a city with fewer than 60 vampires. Realistically, you're just going to have the most involved/influential/powerful vampires have meetings, and while titles might be involved there's very rarely going to be political impetus to equally represent clans. A Ventrue, two Toreador, three Brujah, A Nosferatu and a Samedi will likely make a more realistic elder council than one each for BGMNTTV. If all the Malkavians the city are some combination of weak/despised, there's no reason to give them a seat of office. Similarly, with fractious clans like Toreador or Brujah, it's not likely that they are going to want a single representative. .

                                                      Single clan cities are an inherently destructive idea. Parents have a big effect on their kids, grandkids are either spoiled rotten or despised, but great grandkids? cute strangers. Imagine your great-great grandparents/kids travel through time to meet you. What's the connection there? Let's not even get into all the brothers and cousins competing for Grandma's good grace. Now imagine you're all territorial bloodsucking predators who can't all hunt in the same neighbourhood for fear of drawing in hostile forces. You're also all specialized in the same career path when there's limited job openings.... The only bloodline that can do this is the Tremere, and that's because they've got a lot of different ways to specialize. .

                                                      If your city is composed of only Nosferatu, there's not really much purpose in making friends with other Nosferatu. They've largely got the same skills you do. Sure they can OoC and pretend to be Ventrue, but going OoC like that puts them at a disadvantage. Being friends with an Actual Ventrue is just better for the Nosferatu. He's got skills you don't have, he needs skills you have, it's a virtuous relationship. It's simple economics/politics/sociology/whatever. A four Nosferatu team would struggle to compare to a Nosferatu/Ventrue Duo.
