                        Originally posted by Murder-of-Crows View Post

                        To quote the Lexicon in V20:
                        "Generation: The number of “steps” between a vampire and the mythical Caine; how far descended from the First Vampire a given vampire is."

                        Caine cannot be first generation, because Caine cannot be one step removed from himself. Someone even addressed that bit in Clanbook Toreador revised, but not in a satisfactory way.
                        (I know you are having an ongoing conversation already about this, but let me just jump in as one of the "old school"-guard to help clarify that particular point...)

                        It's a compounded perceptual error -- the first occurring almost 25 years ago and the second occurring whenever someone (a reader) takes the applied use of the term out-of-context.

                        When vampire first came out (1st Ed.), and in to some carry-over into 2nd, the term "generation" was *misused* by Mark and several others (that is, they misused the term as genealogically-used in The Real World(tm) when addressing Caine); this was actually addressed on the forums almost 20 years ago). On an in-narrative-cum-gaming-level, "generation" in the above cited usage was simply to indicate the number of steps from *the inception/creation of vampires*, with Caine being the "first generation" as he was the first vampire. The later, more commonly "referenced" use of (G)eneration incorporated two different applied definitions as if they were one. Again, a misuse of the term compared to real-life definitions. (Contextually, Caine is the 1st Generation when one considers Lilith to be his blood-creator, which she was according to most accepted in-narrative myths as most all of the various books agree that she is while albeit not always agreeing on how much of an influence she was/had over time.)

                        Caine *is* "the first generation", contextual to the setting and applied meanings. He *wouldn't* be if, say, this was all be kept track of by The Daughters of the Revolution, or similar real-life genealogical groups. But in this fictional setting, the double-meaning of the term stands and has been used in its seemingly contradictory manner for (in-narrative) many thousands of years and (meta-narratively) for almost 25 and across multiple editions/settings of the game. Much like with how words can often have self-contradictory meanings due to public-shift of word usage***, the term "generation" in Vampire has a different meaning than it does in conventional use.

                        (***: It's like how "figurative" and "literal" have become mixed and cross-matched until now the two words have contradictory-yet-simultaneous meanings both for-and-against each other and themselves. Originally, "figurative" meant the actual physical manifestation of something -- the figure, as it were -- while "literal" meant the abstract concept/expression of something, the ideal rather than the concrete -- the literary concept of something. During the 19th century, however, the public -- particularly in America -- began misusing the two terms, crossing their meanings and applied uses, until by the 20th century their definitions became reversed from what they were. In recent decades, however, the two are now used in such a fluxed manner that in many cases only the context for when something is said indicates which definition should be applied/interpreted. (This means, funnily enough, that when younger people are "corrected" today for "misusing" the word "literal-ly", those young people are actually using the original, proper definition of the word: when you say "My head hurt so much, it *literally* exploded...", you *aren't* saying it physically exploded, but rather it hyperbolically did within a literary, abstract context. What all that has to do with this is to demonstrate that the shift in applied usage of a given word always has to be taken contextually with where/how it is used. Caine *is*, within the context of the game setting, the First Vampire and The First Generation Vampire (that is, the first "generated" vampire). Any seeming contradiction of usage is simply another case of "figurative vs. literal".)

                        Hope that helps....
