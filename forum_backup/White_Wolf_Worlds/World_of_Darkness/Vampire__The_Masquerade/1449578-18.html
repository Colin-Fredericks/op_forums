                        Similarly, several people here have said that people who like playing Sabbat characters basically enjoy playing fascism or eating babies. The above-mentioned evil is apparently all fine, though, because it's done by the Camarilla and Anarchs, I guess.

                        'Yeah, maybe we're bad, but look at those *other* vampires over there! See how much worse they are? And you think we're edgelords? Check out this other group.'

                        That sort of thing.
                        A lot of the game's mechanics really assume you will take tenets in the Hallmark movie to Sin City range. Tenets can be anything, but creating ghouls always gives you stains. Tenets can be anything, but being a Farmer Pred gets you Humanity 8 and Allycat Pred gets you Humanity 6. Tenets can be anything, but your Touchstones have to be human. It all assumes very conventional tenets. You can do things to like the Dyscrasia stuff you mentioned, but the game will consider you wrong, and punish you karmically for it. A lot of their mechanics actually break if you don't pick conventional tenets.

                        It was something I noticed immediately when trying to adapt the current system to Sabbat. It wasn't enough simply to use the Long Road Out Of Hell Sabbat faction tenets and Beast Marks system, though that was a tremendous, tremendous help. I had to drop the Predator types and replace it with simply giving everyone one more dot in their Clan Disciplines.

                        I also dropped things like getting stains for creating ghouls and progeny, which I'm pleased to say hasn't created any of the problems you might expect. Players want to solve problems themselves. They don't want to watch a bunch of NPCs solve their problems for them, so giving them stains for creating ghouls and childer is a solution in search of a non-existent problem. Plus, creating a bunch of ghouls and childer has plenty of complications that don't require humanity, such as feeding them all, keeping them all from killing each other out of jealousy, problems the players are always keenly aware of, in addition to just not wanting to stand around waiting for the horde of NPCs they created to solve the problem for them.

                        I'm digressing: the creators only want you to do stuff outside of conventional morality if they can punish you for it with humanity loss.

                                Originally posted by Rhywbeth View Post
                                I wonder if there's any overlap between people who play vampire and people who like playing underdark races and similar.
                                There's certainly some, but there's lots of other reactions.

                                I know a lot of people that I mentally think of as "setting extreme" style players. Rather than necessarily being super into dark characters, they want to play exemplars of what the setting is about. If the setting is about daring heroes doing quests for the good of the fantasy-world? They're going to play the most morally upstanding, righteous, and just dealer of adventuring action there is. If they're playing in a setting about brooding monsters struggling with their nature, they want to dial their angst up to 11. If they're playing a character study in evil character, they're going to dive right at the most vile thing allowed.

                                While a bit of an over-the-top explanation, it explains players I know that seem to always play things like Paladins in D&D, but Sabbat in VtM. So the motivations here can sometimes lead to overlap, and sometimes to divergence.

                                            In any case, every version of vampire society is a variation on Feudalism. Camarilla are blatantly feudalistic. Anarchs are like the Barons who created the Magna Carta, or the those Aristocrats who wanted what they called "democracy" in ancient Greece and the "Republic" in ancient Rome, or the Aristocrats who created the pre-bill of rights version of American "democracy" and those who formed the Confederacy because they wanted a continuance of that early version of American "democracy": they want rights for themselves, but still want to lord it over the peasants.

                                            Camarilla vs Anarch is a fight between a version of Feudalism with power mainly in the hands of the King and High Nobles versus a version of Feudalism where the Low Nobles have more collective power than the King and High Nobles. The Sabbat are the version of Feudalism where the Pope and those Crusader type nobles who serve the Pope have most of the power. But nobody in this is really in it for the peasants.

                                            Join the Confederacy feudalists, the Absolute Monarchy feudalists, the Papacy/Crusader feudalists, or just go it completely alone and be the enemy of all, those are your options as a vampire.
