                            Originally posted by Technoma View Post
                            From what I know, it goes like this.

                            WTA Oblivion: The Nothingness at the bottom of the Shadowlands. Most likely the Void mentioned in DTF. Hates all of existence.

                            MTA Abyss: The thing Nephandi worships in one form or the other. DTF Void. Hates all light, life and existence.

                            VTM Abyss: The Oblivion in the Underworld and the DTF The Void. Hates all light, life and existence.
                            It’s a good interpretation, but still an interpretation, that’s my point.

                            What we do have is this:

                            WtA Abyss: it’s a Realm in the Middle Umbra, called The Chasm by Mages. It actually looks like a rocky plateau full of caves and leading to a cliff, an actual, physical abyss. It has winds and is quite inhabited by a lot of things. It numbs the senses little by little, and can cause Harano.

                            WtO Abyss/Oblivion: it’s a place in the center/bottom of the Labyrinth. Since nothing that goes into it comes back, anything about it is speculation or legend. Neverborn and the Grandmother seem to come from this place, but they may just reside near it or whatever.

                            VtM Abyss: it’s a realm of complete sensory deprivation. Due to this, almost nothing can be said about it, except it doesn’t actually trap people, it is just obviously hard to navigate. It is inhabited by beings seemingly made of shadow that can be summoned by some Abyss Mysticism rituals, but whether the rituals create them or just summon them, or if it has some effect on the forms they take, that’s all in the air, as is any relationship between them and any other inhabitant of any other realm.

                            MtA Void: it’s actually on the outer reaches of the Umbra, so it’s reached by going up instead of down, but it may have some link to the realms within the Earth. Nothing about it, even if it is just one place or actually several unrelated realms, is certain.

                            DtF Abyss: it’s a non-physical realm in the absolute sense, its inhabitants allegedly existing only as pure thought. It only contains demons, and as far as we know can’t contain anything else. It has enough of a connection to other realms as to make it possible for some sort of sensory info to be perceived at intervals, but the details are muddled. It seems to be sensitive to Maelstroms at least, and to the Avatar Storm.

                            As we can see, there’s a lot of distinctions between those “Abysses”. Their common point is that more than unknown, something about them always remains unknowable. Either you won’t come back from certain points, or you’re sensory deprived and can’t say for certain what’s going on.

                            But that’s it. They could very well be completely unrelated and still work as they do just fine, or they can be distinct parts or distinct perspectives over the same phenomenon and work just fine all the same. What’s important is that they fill different roles in their respective splats.

                                        AHEM. We'll start with every splat not VtM, because they have the most fleshed out lore as well as the most connections. Lots of metaphysics here and I'll list specific books and pages for your own reading if you wish. We have a lot to cover...

                                        The Chasm of the Abyss, (also known as Umbral Realm of the Abyss, and the Maw of the Abyss,) exists both in WtA and MtA, within the Middle Umbra. A mage character of the supplement the MtA: Book of Worlds (pg 56-59) visits and describes it nearly exactly the same as it is described in both WtA Umbra The Velvet Shadow (pg 26-30) and WtA Umbra (pg 35-40.)I'm going to refer to it here as just the Chasm for simplicity sake.

                                        These books posit that the Chasm is a passage into/extension of the Umbral Realm of Malfeas (which is sometimes known as the Qlippoth Shard Realm of Entropy [MtA Book of Madness pg33]) which is tangentially connected to the WtO realm of the Labyrinth. These are all the path Entropy takes through the Umbra with, the Middle Umbra (Chasm), the Dark Umbra (Labyrinth, Abyss, Oblivion,) and the Deep Umbra (Malfeas.)

                                        DtF Houses of the Fallen pg 177-179 further explains that the Alabaster Legion of the host, built a fortress called Kasdeja and it was subsequently destroyed by Michael's host, so the Slayer Charon (DtF angel not WtO wraith) worked to carve out a realm called Haven from the nothingness below the world, which would later become known as the Underworld/Dark Umbra. Here he wanted to protect the souls of humans, and allow them to play with memory, till they could be shepherded instead of consigned to nothingness. Underneath the prime city which became known as Stygia, he carved out a fortress which would later be known as the Labyrinth. The Labyrinth helped separate Haven from the absolutely nothingness that the universe was carved out of.

                                        This nothingness is known in the splats as Oblivion, and has a consciousness which is both associated with the Wyrm (WtA,) Ialdabaoth (MtA,) and the Grand Maw(WtO)/Grandmother(Orpheus.) Entropy/Oblivion is suggested to be the opposite of Prime, making Grand Maw the opposite or dark reflection of God as they are thought to be avatars of their respective energies. This ties into the whole Yin/Yang aspect that all the splats follow. Vampire and the Beast, Wraiths and their Shadows, Mages and their Avatars, and Garou and their Gnosis/Rage are some examples of this.

                                        In MtA: Book of Worlds (pg 72-77) a character goes and meets with the Guildmaster of the Artificers, the Wraith Lord Ember, and enters the Labyrinth below Stygia for a time and talks about it from the standpoint of the Euthanatoi philosophies.

                                        Below the Labyrinth are two types of "beings" that exist as WtO explains the Once Born, which are just grossly powerful Spectres infused with powers of Entropy leaking from the nothingness of Oblivion, and the ever more powerful Neverborne, slumbering creatures that never had physical lives in the world.

                                            The Abyss. Part two. Where VtM comes in.

                                            Mainly the practitioners of Abyss manipulation come the kindred line of the being known as Lasombra. The art is known as Obtenebration, and the further study of it codified into blood magic paths known as Abyss Mysticism. In the current 5th edition this power has been merged with the Necromancy abilities of the Revised and V20 eras into the discipline now known as Oblivion. This makes sense to be honest since both abilities are dealing with energies of Entropy and the Dark Umbra.

                                            In VtDA: Player's Guide to the High Clans pg 33,we get a story about how Lasombra, though it had rudimentry power of shadows, became obsessed with a more metaphysical Darkness and the Abyss during the time of the Flood that wiped away the First City Enoch.

                                            The Shadowed One was reborn into darkness through blood and pain. His mortal life, like his mortal name, is of no consequence. Sufficient to say that it was unremarkable in nearly every way. It is his immortal existence that concerns us, when he rose as a grandchilde of the Dark Father, one of the first of the third generation of the childer of Caine. He arose in
                                            the First City, ruled by the Dark Father and those of the second generation.

                                            From the very beginning, the Shadowed One remained close to the darkness that was his by right of blood. Shadows clung to him and the night was his home. Great cats stalked at his feet as he hunted and feasted, and slaves and servants feared and worshiped him. So it was for many nights, until the coming of the Great Flood that washed away the First City, showing the Shadowed One a greater darkness than even the starless night, the depths of the endless sea.

                                            How long he wandered its depths, none can say, existing wrapped in the arms of darkness, like being submerged in ink, beyond the reach of the sun. He existed and he hunted, the Shadowed One of the Deeps, in darkness and silence profound, the most feared predator of the seas, until the time when the floodwaters receded and the childer of Caine embraced the dry land once more.
                                            This timeline of Caine's transformation into a vampire, then founding of Enoch to the Flood, coincides with the timeline of the Age of Wrath. The Age of Wrath is the thousand year war that lead to the fall and imprisonment of Lucifer's host in DtF. The Fallen first rebelled as an act of love for humans, and wanting to raise them up with knowledge but were barred from doing so. The war begins as non lethal duels, but turns when Caine murder's Abel, introducing a new concept into the universe. The Fallen help the humans build an empire known as Civilization of Ashes​, and until it is all destroyed in an event called the Shattering. During the Shattering the Abyss itself is created and the majority of the Fallen are imprisoned.

                                            In VtDA: Player's Guide to the High Clans Pg158, and Pg 175-176 we have these descriptions.

                                            Staying in the Abyss for too long is not a good idea. Demonic creatures lurk in the Abyss and might decide to attack the character. In addition, the Abyss itself has an insidious influence upon those who stay there, and anyone spending too long in it might find himself becoming more and more obsessed with suffering and darkness.​
                                            Abyss Mysticism

                                            As pragmatic as they are ruthless, most Lasombra regard Obtenebration as a tool. It is an important tool and a gift from their founder, but ultimately just one power among many. Only the most eccentric and arcane savants among the clan understand the true significance of their dark art. Obtenebration is more than mere shadow-play. It is a window to the Abyss itself, that great and terrible unknown that lies at the center of all things. The Abyss gnaws at the heart of the underworld and the doubt in every question never answered. It is present in the absence of light and lurks
                                            in every shadow. Ghosts rightly fear its hunger, but they do not understand it. Demons call it Hell, and they only begin to comprehend. No Lasombra knows where it comes from or its purpose or even the purpose of its strange denizens, but the clan’s mystics know the Abyss is the ultimate source of their power. So the mystics vainly pry into the dark places in search of secrets and answers and perhaps more questions. For these driven souls, conventional Obtenebration is only the beginning. With rituals they unlock the hidden powers of shadow and summon the unnamed primordial horrors that lie beyond.​
                                            I'll be honest there is scant information about the Lasombra, Obtenebration, and Abyss Mysticism. Most books want to talk about the events of the Anarch Revolt, and the Sabbat/Antitribu split. The books that talk about the nature of Obtenebration are mostly in the Dark Ages version of the setting, and then the V20 era, which divorces itself from the World of Darkness as a cohesive whole.

                                            With the above you can see the intent of the authors, that was later built upon in revised editions of Mage the Ascension and the release of Demon the Fallen. Ultimately in your own game you get to decide what the Abyss actually is but if you read across all the splats, there is to me at least, a clear connection between these places and concepts.
