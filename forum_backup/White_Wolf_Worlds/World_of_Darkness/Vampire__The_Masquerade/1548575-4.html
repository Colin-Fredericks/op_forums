    There is a mention of an "evangelical anarch" named Laecanus in the Anarch Cookbook. It's in the same section on philosophy that mentions perspectives on diablerie and Libertas. It's part of the same mess of gnosticism that pervades a lot of early WoD and Vampire. Laecanus proclaims a "new moral perspective" on Caine. Basically, the villain is God and Caine was unjustly punished. Since he was punished unjustly, all vampires are also unjustly punished. That frenzy where you killed all those people? Not your fault. The blame is on God.

    Laecanus believes that is Kindred can just absolve themselves of any guilt, they can free themselves of frenzy since their cause is external. It's just another strange belief that is not supported by game mechanics, but as a philosophy in the setting it does have some internal logic. However, I can't see this as possibly working. Someone who tries to feel no guilt at all is just going to fuel the Beast and let him take over.

    Like a lot of oddball anarch characters mentioned in the Anarch Cookbook and elsewhere, the best place to put this character is in the Free States. It's another missed opportunity of LABN that it did not include him. This is the kind of thing that can make an interesting NPC for the PCs to interact with.

    However, as far as I know there is nothing else known about this character. We get just one paragraph. The most likely clans for him would either be Brujah or Malkavian. His theory is so outlandish it would be easy to just label him Malkavian. However, then it'd be too easy to dismiss in my opinion. The Brujah are supposed to be a clan of philosophers and radical thinkers, and I think this also fits in well with them.

    As "apostle of Caine" brings up connotations of the Path of Caine and thus the Sabbat, but I won't have this character be connected with them. Once you start with the idea that the first vampire was Cain, you are going to have ideas like this pop up independently. Sabbat on the Path of Caine might want to contact such a character if they hear about him, but I would prefer Laecanus to be sincere in his anarch loyalties.

        This is somewhat tangential but Laecanus’ philosophy reminds me of an idea I’ve been playing around with to flesh out the Sabbat’s and other vampiro-gnostics positive reinterpretation of Caine, which may be somewhat useful here.

        If we look at Genesis and the punishments laid upon Adam & Eve for eating the Fruit, what have we got? They are banished from the Garden, a land apparently without death or strife. Adam is punished by being forced to struggle with the earth for his sustenance. Eve is punished with subservience to Adam and the pains of childbirth (necessarily linked, particularly in the nascent agrarian states that Adam’s new labors imply). Both are punished with mortality.

        Then let’s look at their children. Interestingly we see the disdain laid upon Cain while praise is laid upon Abel. Now part of this has to do with the preference for pastoralism over sedentary agriculture (which itself is telling). However if we want to do a little amateur psychoanalysis it’s not difficult to imagine that God’s unfavorable response to Cain’s offerings could also be reflective of Adam and Eve’s animosity for Cain (who would represent for Adam a vision of himself as sedentary laborer, as opposed to his status in the Garden; and for Eve the living proof of her subordination to Adam and the pains of child-bearing). In contrast the pastoralist Abel is laden with favor, perhaps because of Cain’s usefulness to his parents as scapegoat for their frustrations, perhaps because of pastoralism representing a break with sedentary agriculture, a sort of half step back to the implied Hunter-gatherer lifestyle of the Garden.

        Regardless Cain’s frustration with his mistreatment (whether real or perceived) leads him to the murder of Abel (itself an interesting reflection of Abel’s sacrifice of his flock of course) and by extension the invention of murder itself, here used in both a vengeful venting of spleen and presumably an attempt to take what was Abel’s for himself (nominally his flock, in truth the approval and love of his parents/god).

        Now we take the pivot to vampire lore.

        If you look at what vampirism provides, you may notice something curious. It resembles a dark reflection of the Garden in some ways. Vampires are described as predators, but what does that mean in human terms, other than a return to a Hunter-gatherer lifestyle (sans the gathering)?

        Let’s look at what else vampirism provides. It seems to provide a reversal of the punishments of the fall. Eve was punished by subordination to Adam and the pain of childbirth: as vampire has oft-pointed out the vampiric condition does not discriminate in sharing its gifts on basis of sex or gender, and furthermore the pains of human birth are replaced with the ecstasies of vampiric birth (both done in blood of course). Adam was punished with his harsh labors upon the earth, which the vampires are excused from, instead deriving their substance from the fruits of earthly living. Both were punished with mortality - which the vampire is definitionally excluded from.

        When one looks at vampirism from this perspective, particularly if one wants to add in a liberal dose of Lilith inflected Gnosticism, it doesn’t seem too bizarre how vampires such as Laecanus or the Sabbat priesthood could justify Caine as a messianic figure and even extend this into a just rebellion against a tyrannical demiurge

            Originally posted by Gryffon15 View Post
            Now part of this has to do with the preference for pastoralism over sedentary agriculture (which itself is telling).
            I have long felt this particular interpretation to be extremely odd. While the narrative in the Bible is not explicit, I think there's a lot more obvious answers as to why God preferred Abel's sacrifice. I think emphasizing Cain's farming and Abel's shepherding as opposed to other elements in the story misses the point. But that debate is beyond the point of the game or this thread.

            Originally posted by Gryffon15 View Post
            When one looks at vampirism from this perspective, particularly if one wants to add in a liberal dose of Lilith inflected Gnosticism, it doesn’t seem too bizarre how vampires such as Laecanus or the Sabbat priesthood could justify Caine as a messianic figure and even extend this into a just rebellion against a tyrannical demiurge
            Once you place Cain as as the first vampire, certain ideas are going to come up.

            Of more importance is what kind of character should Laecanus be. Is he more of an intellectual and theologian speculating, or is he some kind of cult leader? He's called an evagelical anarch, but is he evangelical to this "new moral perspective" or the anarch cause? Did he develop this perspective after becoming an anarch, or did his speculations on the nature of Caine and the cursed cause the local Prince, or even Justicar and archons, to go after him and banish from polite Camarilla society? He's called a "popular proponent" of the idea, so he may not have invented it. He's just the best known Kindred to develop the idea and popularize it.

            Saying Caine was unjustly punished is not the same as being pro-Caine. After all, Caine created the Six Traditions. As an anarch, Laecanus likely does not believe they should be followed. Unlike the Sabbat that worships Caine because they believe he saved the early sect while in the guise of the Ravnos Methusaleh Vestgeir, there's no such story or legend among the anarchs. Caine is just another authority figure to be defied, not some daddy figure who is going to come in and save you from your older brothers picking on you. Caine may be a tragic or even sympathetic figure, but he is not necessarily someone to emulate.

            Instead, Laecanus is much more interested in eliminating frenzy (as a practical matter), and transferring the moral responsibility of a vampire's actions under frenzy onto a third person to eliminate the idea that vampires are evil. It seems like an excuse for vampires to do whatever they want - a get out of jail free card that lasts for all of eternity. Perhaps Laecanus does have some kind of moral code of his own, but he rejects the idea that vampires are simply evil per se. If an evil or unfortunate act comes from "the Beast", the moral responsibility is not the vampire's. Presumably this is just one more thing the anarchs need to free themselves from. An ultimate expression of one's Libertas. Free yourself not just from political bondage, but spiritual bondage. "I didn't ask for this, but I am going to free myself from this condition."

            This implies that Laecanus hopes somehow to eliminate or reduce "the Beast" inside vampires. Presumably the existential act of realizing the Beast is external to the vampire will either somehow remove it or put the vampire more in control of it. This is another way that Laecanus is different from the Sabbat which approves of any part of the vampire condition.

            Like some of the other elements in this philosophy section, the idea that a vampire can overcome one's own nature and weaknesses isn't developed in any mechanical sense in Vampire: the Masquerade. However, some of these ideas are found in the Ordo Dracul covenant in Vampire: The Requiem. While I am extremely reluctant to introduce anything like the Coils into the VtM setting, I think we may be able to steal some elements from the Ordo Dracul to Laecanus and his followers, especially in how Requiem/Ordo Dracul depict Dracula. Someone who may have dabbled in many things (blood magic, paths of enlightenment, noddism,) and found them wanting. He is now involved in the Great Work, an alchemy of the soul, the personal transcendence which will rescue his soul from the forces that bind it. The ultimate act of liberation.

            The followers of Laecanus are anarchs less concerned with politics, but with metaphysics. The Free States are a good thing because it provides them a safe haven to overcome the limitations of the Kindred condition. Of course, what makes the Ordo Dracul an interesting faction in Requiem is that they actually have accomplishments to share. Laecanus and the other Libertas philosophers have nothing but theories. This doesn't mean they are useless as a setting element. Everyone starts from nothing. This is the part of the Requiem setting's Dracula after he abandons his dabblings with Theban Sorcery and Cruac, but before he achieves his first Coil. That can be a compelling setting element even if there is no payoff (ever, or even just in the timeframe of the chronicle). I imagine The Grand Undying King of the Children of Osiris was at this stage before he finally discovered Bardo, or the first vampire to achieve Golconda. So this does have some precedent in the VtM setting as well. This could be part of some characters' Golconda search or story arc.

            Use some of the techniques of the Ordo Dracul for Laecanus and some of his followers (though without the results of the Coils). These will be the small number of devoted ones who truly believe. Have more hangers on that are intrigued, but until they see some benefit won't participate, or perhaps be inconsistent in their participation. More that like what they hear, but are otherwise uninvolved in Laecanus' metaphysics. Then the vast majority that pays no attention or sees them only as crackpots.

            This is certainly not the only thing an ST can do with Laecanus, but right now this is my starting point. By the time I start the actual chronicle, I may end up going in another direction.

                            This post is about an extremely minor character mentioned in the Anarch Cookbook. "Character" is probably being generous because all it is a brief exchange in letters between Petrodon and this character used as introductory quotes for that book's first chapter. However, they hint at something intriguing which I feel could be an interesting NPC.

                            I once again, with the deepest sincerity, ask you to respond to my inquiries. These tensions are not necessary and only serve to endanger us all. If I could understand your grievances, then I could please your case before the Camarilla. Understand this, I am only attempting to save us all. But I cannot do this unless I understand: what do you want? That is all I want to know: what do you want?”
                            - Justicar Petrodon in a letter to Topaz

                            Get this first - I am only writing because I am sick of your damn letters. Are you so stupid, or is this another trick of yours? I don’t belong to you or your damn prince - I’m my own person, free and loving it. If you can’t understand why me and my friends don’t like kissing your ass, then maybe you need to quit lickin’ the blood of your elders and learn to think for yourself - so get off my ass!
                            - Topaz in response

                            There are some interesting things about this exchange.
                            • Why would Petrodon attempt to reach out to this person in the first place? She seems to be someone important enough for him to not only contact, but try to reconcile with the Camarilla.
                            • Topaz seems to be a public figure as he could actually send a written letter to her. So her location, or at least a way to send her a letter, seems publicly known. It implies she is in a safe location, but one that Petrodon cannot reach. The LA Free States is an obvious choice of location, perhaps checked into a hotel under real name.
                            • Petrodon is concerned with “tensions” that would “endanger” everyone. He only wants to “save” us. What exactly is the danger here?
                            • Topaz seems to have committed some crime or at least scandalous action or utterance, but Petrodon thinks he could plead her case. This seems to imply that she has legitimate grievances, and that her crime is perhaps not so severe. Yet if that is the case, why is Petrodon involved at all?
                            • Topaz sounds very young and immature in her writing. Not only is she young as a vampire, she seems to have been embraced young as well. She doesn't seem to understand the position of someone like Justicar Petrodon. She seems to imagine he's a flunky to his damn prince, not one of the most important vampires in the world.
                            ​While there are multiple ways to imagine this, I believe Topaz is probably a very young childe of some important figure in the Camarilla. A powerful prince perhaps since Topaz mentions "your damn prince". Maybe she has insider information that could be damaging. Or her defection is just very humiliating for her sire, perhaps weakening him fatally. Or she has not been released under the Tradition of Accounting, and thus her actions could technically cause her sire to be punished. At the same time, this seems more than something that affects just one domain as the matter seems to be something that requires addressing "before the Camarilla." But what does that mean? Clearly not the Inner Circle. Perhaps to a conclave?

                            Such a character might not only be interesting to meet - how many people have correspondence with a Justicar (and given a return address!) - but I feel her situation could easily involve new PCs in actual plot for the game session. Topaz seems totally unimportant. Fairly weak neonate along the lines of new PCs. Nobody important. Someone new PCs could realistically become involved in. Yet they could encounter some real stakes with her. Petrodon (or her sire) could send agents to kidnap her in LA. Perhaps send in ghouls, bribe some local anarchs, hire some disposable Caitiff or other desperate vampire to go LA and retrieve her. They don't even necessarily want to harm her or destroy her (her disposable and unimportant friends though...). Nobody else in the Free States really knows or cares about her. The stakes don't seem high for the PCs initially. They just need to befriend another new arrival and care enough to help her. But they could potentially figure out that some very important people want her their own personal reasons that are very important to them.


                            Of course, all the fun bits still need to be figured out. What's her clan, who's her sire, what dangerous knowledge she possesses, what are her interesting quirks and description? Make her someone that the PCs want to help, and would like after everything is resolved.

                                    I recently found something on the White Wolf Wiki that I am wondering if I should include in some capacity. It concerns some characters in the Chaos Factor sourcebook. I don't own or have access to that sourcebook, so my only information is what is on the wiki. I know its one of the more gonzo sourcebooks which is usually not too my taste, but I'll take inspiration from wherever I find it.

                                    It concerns two characters that have some tie to the Anarch Free States. The first is Delilah Monroe and the second is Mad Mary.

                                    First, the stuff I like. Delilah was embraced in southern California. She originally joined the Sabbat, but later defected to the Anarchs and made trips to Los Angeles and Seattle to recruit support to overthrow the Sabbat in Mexico City. She's very much a SoCal punk musician in appearance. She'd be a good fit for the chronicle.

                                    Mad Mary is a Malkavian anti-tribu turned in New York City who somehow made her way to Mexico City. She has Medicine 3 so maybe she was some kind of nurse or doctor back in New York. She knows Delilah and has been convinced by her to go anarch. Presumably she was already someone with Loyalist allegiances. She's tried of being told what to do. Mad Mary isn't a very deep character or have any reason to be affiliated with LA, but her connection with Delilah is good enough. And if she's really interested in the Loyalist cause (even for purely selfish reasons), then it's not that much of a leap to want to defect. Interestingly, while Delilah has Linguistics 1 indicating she probably knows Spanish, Mad Mary has no such skill suggesting she still only knows English. She probably just wants to leave Mexico City just for that.

                                    Second, the stuff I don't like. Delilah is a Daughter of Cacophony. I will be changing that. I believe if you are going to include a rare bloodline like the Daughters of Cacophony, it should be for the purposes of running a story/plot about the mystery of that bloodline. Otherwise it's just gratuitous. I don't have any plans or interest to do that. Furthermore, there isn't anything that really makes this character a DoC. Delilah could be from any clan, and the character would be just as good (or bad). Delilah could just as easily be a Toreador. I'll probably just make her that.

                                    It's not clear why exactly she became Sabbat. Presumably her sire wasn't. I imagine she was recruited very early by some Loyalist nomad pack (perhaps with Mad Mary) and quickly found out not all Sabbat was like that aftern she arrived in Mexico City. Perhaps she spent a lot of time on the road with the pack (she was embraced in 1981) and had only recently arrived in Mexico City With weak vinculum she was able to leave, and Mad Mary went with her. (Maybe most of that nomad pack was recently killed, and a new ductus went to Mexico City to recruit more, and that's when she decided to leave.)

                                    It's also not exactly clear why she would want to go back to Mexico City once she got out. It's not like she has any ties down there. Does she really like Mexico City that much despite the obvious danger there? It makes more sense she'd go to somewhere she knows (SoCal) and wants to be at (the Free States). Obviously, this isn't canon per Chaos Factor, but it'll work for my purposes.

                                    I'm not sure what to do about Delilah's morality being on the Path of Evil Revelations. At least on the Wiki, there's no real justification for it. Is this just another example of gratuitous inclusion of rare lore just to have it? Or is there more to it? Dealing with someone on that Path might be interesting, but it may also just be distracting from the more obvious hooks with the character. Also not sure how Delilah could continue to improve on it without an actual mentor to guide her, and I'm leery to add some minor demon or imp just to do it. Maybe just give her the same Path as Mad Mary (Cathari) or just keep her on the Path of Humanity and assume this version never got to the point of abandoning her default path. There are various musicians who claim to be Satanists just for the shock value, and I'm incline to limit it to just this. I'm open about this question.

                                    A duo of Sabbat defectors who can bring some knowledge of the Sabbat to the Free States would be useful, and the characters in and of themselves are fine. It aligns of some of what I've already discussed here like anarchs being active in Mexico and various levels of support given to them. Some of the characters we've discussed now have strong ties to Mexico. This just builds on that.

                                    I also think I can use them to start several plots hooks with the PCs. If they just arrive in the city, the first Kindred they find could be the PCs. The PCs would need to decide what to do. Just eliminate these NPCs as a risk? Talk with established anarch NPCs on what to do? Try to get information out of them before deciding anything? I think this could become something that could fill up a lot of time for one game session or possibly even leading to multiple sessions. It would be a way to introduce more Sabbat plot elements.

                                    Mad Mary is much more Sabbat mentality than Delilah is, and presumably would have more trouble adjusting to Los Angeles. if the PCs intervene in their favor to be accepted into the Anarchs, it might be interesting to see how they try to mentor Mad Mary and if they are successful or if they just alienate her by always stopping what she wants to do. Delilah would be much easier to integrate, so this is one thing I could do with Mad Mary that I couldn't with Delilah.

                                    If there's more information on these characters in Chaos Factor that would be relevant, and someone knows it, I'm interested in hearing it. These are very minor characters, but I've already come up with several ideas on how I can use them. Precisely because they are minor, I don't mind if they end up being disposable if the PCs go down that route.

                                                    A very old canon character is Ehrich Weiss, who we know better by his stage name Harry Houdini. In canon, he was embraced without permission by a 10th generation Tremere, but was accepted anyway by the Tremere Inner Circle and blood bonded to them (note: in 1e, Tremere characters were fully blood bonded to the Inner Circle, not just partial). While in real life Houdini died in 1926, canon seems to state that he was embraced in 1926, but kept performing (though only at night) publicly until 1930 when he staged his death. Not sure why this would be the case. Did the writer just get his facts wrong? Or was this an attempt to show how the WoD's history is altered from our own? Anyway, Houdini managed to destroy his blood bond and then disappeared, travelling the world in secret while Clan Tremere attempted to hunt him as a rogue.

                                                    One time when he was in Chicago, he met Tracy Graves (Erichtho) whom he had known previously through written correspondence, and discovered she was also a Tremere vampire. She concealed his secret (because she was not blood bonded to the Inner Circle, but Nicolai). Whatever he did to break his blood bond does not seem to work for others, as he is still trying to figure out how to break Tracy's bond. Ehrich still travels around the world, but would visit Chicago at least twice a year and became indirectly involved in Anarch politics there because of Tracy. For our purposes, I don't need to go into further detail.

                                                    While the biography of vampire character of Ehrich Weiss never mentions Los Angeles, we know the real life Houdini briefly live there in 1919 while filming some movies, and his wife later moved back there after his death. I think his wife lived there until she died in 1943, but travelled much between there and New York City. I also have to think that even someone constantly travelling the world does need to take a break every now and again. And if he's willing to to be predictable enough to visit Chicago twice a year to see his friend/correspondent Tracy Graves then he's not adverse at stopping over at the same city time and again.

                                                    Plus if you are a rogue Tremere constantly hunted by the clan, why wouldn't you utilize the California Free States? The Tremere don't hold power there, and if he has anarch sympathies, it makes sense.

                                                    So I am putting LA as a place that Ehrich Weiss extensively visits. He doesn't involve himself in politics and stays away from rants or other public appearances. Despite his anarch sympathies, his real passion (in the WoD as a vampire) is exploring the afterlife and the occult. He just needs a place he can relax for a while before moving on (much like Germaine and other well travelled Kindred who are not aligned with the Camarilla). I imagine the most important vampires in LA do know of him, but respect his privacy.

                                                    I imagine he is in contact with the more serious or learned mystical or occult oriented anarchs in LA (and perhaps magi and sorcerers as well). Any contact with the PCs will need to come through their involvement in that scene.
