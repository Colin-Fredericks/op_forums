    Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
    From a business perspective, the Hecata is stupid:

    -There aren't enough necromancers to really pose much competition, so you're not really getting much here.
    -Joining the Hecatta, for most bloodlines, guarantees the shitter. Nice city needs a necromancer: who gets the job; your rotting ass or Claudio, the boss's cousin? Shit city wants some trivial boons done? Off you go.
    -There aren't enough necromancers around to hunt you down when you leave; who'd notice you? What are they going to do when you're in good with the locals and they come along and try to kill a good relationship? Especially now that most guys who were powerful are dead, it should be easier to be an independent.
    - There were enough necromancers to have feuding factions in the past, and (notably) enough to have different bloodlines in different sects, who tried to kill each other off.
    - Part of the deal when the Hecata formed involved the Samedi and Harbingers getting more prestige and influence; most older Hecata are Harbingers now, with the oldest Giovanni gone.
    - Even if they decide to lay low to avoid the Hecata, independent necromancers are contending with the Second Inquisition without anyone having their back.

    Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
    From a game standpoint, the Hecata are stupid:

    -All the different bloodlines were cool, and shoving them together negates that to a degree based on opinion.
    -All the different discipline spreads gave us a lot to work with (I won't touch on oblivion; I don't need to) and now they're largely gone.
    -The death of giovanni leadership presented an opportunity to spread out the necromancers and present them as a playable clan in more games, but the writers have doubled down on putting them in the corner.
    -The book could've done us a huge favour and set the uprising in the present, that would've been cool, but it didn't, that all happened in the past.
    - The bloodlines' cultures all still exist, like how the Banu Haqim didn't suddenly lose all their lore when they joined the Cam.
    - The book provides different spreads for each bloodline. (It's a sidebar, and I wish it weren't, but at least it's in there.)
    - Except that the Hecata are explicitly working with various sects; the sample Hecata-based chronicle in the back of the book takes place in a Camarilla-controlled city.
    - Don't most VtM books set major events in the past? The only real exceptions I can think of (setting world-changing events in the present so players can mess with them) are the Lasombra defection and Gehenna itself.

                                                            08-06-2020, 05:45 PM
                                                            Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                                                            But the thing with Dark ages Antes is that, well, they didn't really do much. Their behavior back then could hardly be conducive to the belief that they will rise and devour their children, send the world into war and darkness, and that young vampires have an absolute duty to prepare for the coming apocalypse. You can't really have Gehenna on the horizon if everyone's aware that, when the antes just wake up, they'll go about their business and nothing will seriously change.
                                                            That's always been a problem with the game's setting, especially when they introduced the Dark Ages setting and we got more details of the period. Certainly having so many antedeluvians seemingly die in a few centuries (Saulot, Cappadocious, Lasombra, Tzimisce - although we the players know more troublesome details about this than any real character), and the lack of any seeming threat when they (and perhaps others) were active, undercut this. And it's a big problem because not only is one sect motivated by this, it is supposed to be a subject of paranoia everywhere.

                                                            So in my own chronicles, I've made adjustments that I think address this. It works for me. Sharing this out of possible interest to others.

                                                            First, I heavily restrict any post-common era fourth generation vampires.
                                                            • Neither Gratiano or Montano were of the fourth generation, but fifth. And their sire (known only as "the founder's favorite") told them that he had received instructions from the founder to do so, but their sire mysteriously vanished after their accounting.
                                                            • Augustus was supposedly of the fourth generation, but only because of a "cold embrace" where once again a mysterious Methusaleh proxy sired Augustus on the founder's behalf. So there is real confusion here in the general population as to the truth.
                                                            • Neither Saulot nor Tzimisce were known to have sired any new childer since well before the Classical era.
                                                            Second, I made it clear that the supposed destruction and diablerie of any of these founders is controversial in all sects. There are many that doubt these stories ever happened because there is no proof that the supposed diablerists ever found the antedeluvian bodies. Not only does the Camarilla scoff at any claims that "Lasombra" and "Tzimisce" were slain, even in the Sabbat there are many that question this. Some think it was mere propaganda, useful at the time, but not credible. They don't speak up much. But another group thinks that the slayings of these two antedeluvians not only didn't happen, but are merely a claim provided in order to justify Lasombra and Tzimisce control/domination of the sect. At most, they killed some Methusaleh. This is openly spoken by many Loyalists and the leadership of both the Brujah Anti-tribu and Ventrue Anti-tribu. Then there is a smaller group which believes in a conspiracy that is actually close to the truth - the antedeluvians may have been found, but they were not destroyed. This is a popular, but spoken only in hushes, conspiracy theory, even if only a very few believe it.

                                                            The only true "we killed him" antedeluvian was Saulot. Meaning other vampires actually had proof the body was destroyed.
                                                            • The Tremere absolutely knew they got him, and this claim was popularly believed at the time (for various reasons if I ever need to invent them for PCs). (In modern nights, this is often disputed because of the debate over the existence/activities of the antedeluvians, but it is a pedigree that is at least claimed). Although there have always been those who scoffed at it (though they do accept at least a powerful, ancient Methusaleh must have been killed).
                                                            • The only survivor of the attack of Tzimisce of course, was Lugoj. All everyone else truly knows is that many Tzimisce went into some underground hole, and none came out except Lugoj who refuses to provide many details and has the reputation as a coward and liar.
                                                            • There is persistent rumor that the Lasombra anarchs who attacked the Castle of Shadows that they found only an empty tomb of darkness. While both claims of the destruction of the antedeluvian's destruction became vital Anarch propaganda, both have always been disputed even by their own side.
                                                            • Augustus' claim to destroying Cappadocious is undermined by vastly changed stories of the reasons and events. Part of this has to do with the nature of the Black Death, the general confusion concerning the Cappadocians during the years of the plague, and initial portrayal/claims of the Giovanni as "Cappadocian Anarchs" when it supposedly occurred in 1444, and the story told after the Giovanni became "neutral" in 1528. The diablerie is accepted "fact" among the Giovanni, but the Camarilla vampires discount it and it is another reason they look down on the Devil Kindred.
                                                            Third, I say that there is only one antedeluvian which was "popularly" accepted to have been active in historical times (other than the Setites claims about their founder) - Cappadocious. And the only time it's been confirmed he woke, it was always at a time of a great plague. The Antonine Plague (165-180), the Plague of Justinian (541-549), and the Black Death (1346-1353). It was during the Antonine Plague that Cappadocious supposedly awakened and converted to Christianity - news that shocked the vampiric world when rumors first spread. It was during the Plague of Justinian that vast numbers of Cappadocians mysteriously disappeared and stories of the Feast of Folly were first heard. And the last apparent rising of Cappadocious took place during the Black Death, and I used old first edition lore that the disease also shockingly infected and killed some vampires as well. So the coincidence of Cappadocious being active with these terrible plagues helped spread a lot of panic about Gehenna, and it also helped explain why the other clans looked the other way when the Giovanni began a purge of the Cappadocians (there was an ongoing belief that many Cappadocian vampires themselves spread the plague/infected others while being immune, but there are other stories which contradict this). But not everyone believes, thinking many of the stories about Cappadocious to be fairy tale stories. But those who think it is true, shudder at what might happen should other antedeluvians arise.

                                                            So there is deniability of any active role of the other antedeluvians so we don't have non-threatening examples of them, massive dispute that they were relatively easy to kill, and the only "reputable" accounts of an active antedeluvian are associated with lots of bad things. I think this creates a different impression of the antedeluvians than canonical depiction, and one that better fits the mood the game was trying to create. It may not work for your games, but I find it useful.
