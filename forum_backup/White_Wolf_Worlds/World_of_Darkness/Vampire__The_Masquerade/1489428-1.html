        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
        There's a rather enormous BUT that accompanies worrying about diversity here in the fact that V:TM was notable for being the most diverse game in tabletop gaming history by the time Chicago by Night came out. Gay people, people of color, Asians, bisexuals, and more. It leaned in heavily to the fact vampirism was embraced as both transgressive yet also incredibly accepting in a time when America was coming out of a very conformist reactionary phase in its history.

        Besides, V:TM doesn't have a main plot to adapt so it's almost certainly original characters.
        I disagree with handing that entire statement to WW and V:tM.

        It was different for sexuality being more front and center, but not for racial or cultural representation. D&D under TSR had whole settings where africans, asians, and middle eastern groups were the default or only human races/cultures, and this was before V:tM first edition was on its feet. Even Games Workshop with Warhammer Fantasy had diversity on those fronts.

        As for America coming out of a "conformist phase", that is outright untrue. The 1950s were a conformist phase, the 1960s and 1970s were nothing but social upheaval and recalibration. The 1980s were a business and cold war phase, then the 1990s when V:tM was written and released was a technology revolution.

        As for being transgressive and accepting, once again D&D was letting you play the good, the bad, and the ugly back in the 1980s. You want to be the hero, the rules supported it, if you wanted to be the villain, the rules supported it. The big difference was that D&D didn't bank on emo-goth adult themes outside of Ravenloft, but seeing how D&D had to survive the Satanic Panic, its not like they could be too blatant about those non-heroic options.

        If I was to give credit to what was revolutionary about V:tM I would say it was the Storyteller system, where XP was gained by playing rather than butchering X amount of monsters for Y amount of xp to gain a level and steal all of their stuff. The D10 dot system with difficulty 6 can be learned in a few minutes, where as explaining THAC0 still causes mild forms of PTSD.

        I agree that the shows will probably have original characters, but if the show writers are going to make original characters and don't have a core plot from the WoD to follow, exactly what will differentiate these shows from other vampire shows like Moonlight, True Blood, Angel, Buffy, or Forever Knight?

        The problem is that what makes WoD different is the society of vampires, the factions, the clans, the different powers, the different weaknesses, and how all those things mix together. But you can't front load all of that, which means the writers will shift gears away from the setting and try to swing for the mythical larger audience by trying to check as many boxes on a list somewhere when it comes to viewer demographics all while trying to sprinkle in some setting fluff here and there.

        When we were young, we would say "oh man this is going to be awesome", now we say "I wonder how badly they are going to screw this up", and that is coming from someone who isn't a cynic or a pessimist.
