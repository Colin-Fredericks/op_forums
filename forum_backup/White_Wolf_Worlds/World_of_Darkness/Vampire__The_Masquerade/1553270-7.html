        It does, although overall I get from the review the game is pretty good, despite problems.

        Setting aside the already mentioned cases of franchises that ditched classic mechanics, in general they're a great way of polishing good games. In many successful franchises you have good installments that are essentially polished versions of a previous one. Heck, Pokemon basically live off of this, and while it's getting old it succeeded with this formula for decades.

        Given what they did, TCR seems reasonably capable of solving most problems, and even add the RPG mechanics absent in the game, if they're given the opportunity to continue working in BL. Not a guaranteed thing, but worth a shot.
        The two DLC that have been announced sound more like story-DLC where in one you're playing another linear story through the eyes of the Brujah Sheriff in one instance and through the eyes of that Toreador lady in the other. Maybe they're giving them unique combat-powers and it sounds like Phyre gets new outfits - but that's it. There's no indication of Paradox tasking TCR to expand the game's core-mechanics or make it more of an actual RPG.

        The review I quoted may be overall positive and that is up to how the reviewer in question weighs various aspects of the game, but as a value-proposition the current BL2 doesn't sound that great. Like when it also mentions that it doesn't really make a difference if you play through that main-story as a Tremere or a Toreador. The differences you get through that choice are minuscule. In terms of replayability, that's not great either.

        And Pokemon is another bad franchise. Sure, it's still printing money. It also benefits from a bit of a monopoly because, as Palworld proves, Nintendo will come after you if you get too close to actually making a direct competitor to Pokemon. But they just released another Pokemon game on Switch 2 - and it still doesn't even have voice-acting. There's barely any innovation in those games.


        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
        While this is true, I think a lot of the comparison really isn't fair in this particular case.

        Bloodlines isn't really a franchise. This is nostalgia, not continuation.

        Nothing really continues from one to the other from a development standpoint, it's just the IP. This is related to BL 1 in the same way Sands of Time is related to the original Prince of Persia.

        It's hard to say this is a franchise at all, at least for now. I don't really think comparing the two BLs is the best approach.
        Oh, the comparison is absolutely fair and valid, as @HeavyArms said.

        One, when the game was originally announced back in the day, not only did they bring on people who worked on BL1 but listen to how Paradox talked a big game back then. From an article about the announcement of the game in 2019:

        "Fans of the original Bloodlines have been hungry for a sequel for a long time, and we began to hear those suggestions — well, demands — as soon as we began to work with the World of Darkness," said Ebba Ljungerud, CEO of Paradox Interactive. "We all understood what this opportunity could mean, but it was essential for both us and for Hardsuit Labs that any sequel should be done properly; a true successor guided by the people who knew what made the original so special. Keeping this game a secret for the last few years has been quite the Masquerade for us! It's both exciting and relieving to finally let everybody know what we have in store for them."
        Yeah, I wonder who created the expectation that BL2 would be a true sequel...

        Two, there's been an interview with Creative Director Alex Skidrow on GameInformer I mentioned in this thread before;

        When I ask creative director Alex Skidmore why the game is called Bloodlines 2 instead of something else, he thinks for a while, then starts his answer by saying, "It's what we were asked to do." It's an honest response. Notoriously, Bloodlines 2 was originally being made by Hardsuit Labs and got far enough in development to be shown at E3 2019, but the next two years were filled with delays, departures, and ultimately, the decision to remove Hardsuit Labs from the project entirely. The Chinese Room took its place, and since it was working on a project that had already been announced, it didn't have much of a choice – this game would always be called Bloodlines 2.
        Now that we have a better sense of how the game turned out, I do get the impression that the pretty linear main-story plus the Fabien-detective-stuff. That's what TCR wanted to do. The focus on combat, the side-quests and the bare-bones skill-system: That stuff only entered the picture during development once Paradox decided that the game should be called Bloodlines 2.

        Three, for the last year, there's been this handwringing about the released version. You got talk of a "spiritual sequel" and the like. But it was all this wishy-washy marketing-speak. And I think the conundrum Paradox faced here is that once the first trailers for the TCR version started appearing, you got a lot of criticisms along the lines of "This isn't a true sequel, is it?". The problem after that was... that the critics were right. This wouldn't be a true sequel. And more, if it isn't a true sequel, why call it Bloodlines 2 to begin with? And the problem Paradox faced on that front is that the honest answer to that question is that there isn't a good answer. Because I think ultimately the real reason this thing was released as Bloodlines 2 is because Paradox just wanted to finally put something out called "Bloodlines 2". Of course, the pressure to actually finally release something despite all the drama/development-hell-stuff was just yet another self-inflicted wound created by Paradox. But considering how they talked last year, I get the sense they were at the point where they had simply given up and they just wanted something, anything really, to be put out there that would allow them to say "We released Bloodlines 2.". Of course, it's not nice for TCR that Paradox pushed them to release the game with that sort of mindset but well, here we are...
