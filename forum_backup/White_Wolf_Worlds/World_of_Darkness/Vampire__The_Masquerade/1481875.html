    The Salubri who are known as the good guys, regularly consorted with the Tzimisce

    05-24-2022, 07:10 PM
    In the dark ages salubri clanbook, we learn that the salubri and tzimisce have a very close relationship. In fact it is stated that warriors and healers of clan salubri were able to learn more about their powers by watching the tzimisce torture their victims. So why is it that a clan that's supposed to be filled with paragons of justice are willing to consort with such sadistic monsters?

    It becomes kind of funny when you realize that they talk bad about almost every other clan, but one of the clans they seem to have only good things to say about are the tzimisce. Looking at this, its easy to see why the other clans were willing to turn their backs on the salubri, you cant preach justice and at the same time hangout with tyrants that enjoy torturing their subjects.

    -You can find it in Salubri Dark ages clanbook page 35

              This is mostly an artifact that there isn't really canonical continuity between the versions of the World of Darkness after 30 years (or even 10). There's at least 3, maybe 5 or even more "worlds" out there that are similar, but not identical like the old Earth-1, Earth-2, Earth-3, etc. of DC comics. There's enough changes between the Vampire: the Dark Ages setting and the implied backstory of 1e/2e Vampire that the two aren't actually compatible if you look close enough at the details. Different developers had their own ideas and goals, they weren't necessarily interested in keeping with the original ideas of the game's founders.

              However, how good the Salubri were is always something to question. Even when first introduced as a tiny seven member bloodline, there were enough bizarre customs and actions about the Salubri that made a lot of people scratch their heads. And while the Salubri as "good guys" is something of an OOC take because of their quest for Golconda and caregiver natures, IC their reputation was mostly BAD. And the idea that there were two kinds of Salubri - the original kind, and then the second kind after Saulot had supposedly found Golconda - always left some ambiguity (In essence, I see them as two different clans albeit with the same founder).

              I've always had ambivalent feelings about the Dark Ages Salubri clanbook. While there's some parts that I like, I didn't like the presentation as a whole. It didn't present the Salubri in a way I felt they should have been portrayed before they reduced to the strange bloodline that existed in the Modern Day. But I felt that about most of the Dark Ages line, and it got only worse when V:tDA became DA:V. But obviously those sourcebooks have lots of fans, and their canon is held to whatever WoD-5 version that exists today.

                                      Provocative hot takes are the OP's whole thing, though. I don't mean that as a criticism, necessarily, I think these threads can be entertaining if approached with the right mindset.

                                      As for the main topic, my thoughts and preferences are once again closely aligned with those of Black Fox. From the beginning, the Salubri have been presented as a group with very strange beliefs and practices. The popular conception among White Wolf fandom that the Salubri are nothing but a designated good guy group does not survive close examination. This does not mean that they should be presented as an equally simplistic asshole group, either, only that the Salubri have always (in terms of publication history, not ingame lore) exhibited cultish or otherwise questionable behavior. It's one reason why I always quite enjoyed the Dark Ages idea of mutual respect between the Salubri and the Tzimisce... Even if it is another example of later White Wolf writers treating vampires as clan members first and individuals second. In the Dark Medieval setting, clan association should mean even less than it does in modern nights because of how scattered and disorganized Cainite society is during the Long Night, but that's not where the Dark Ages developers took the game line.
