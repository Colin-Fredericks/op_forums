    V5's Gehenna: A Serious Oversight

    01-10-2026, 09:09 AM
    Alright, am I the only person who saw a problem with how effortlessly the Tremere and Giovanni got dealt with after the 5th edition Gehenna reboot? I don't just mean in the sense that these were (arguably) popular clans, and shattering them was a bitter pill. I mean in the sense of a narrative context.

    Because I don't think the writers fully realize just what kind of precedent they've set going forward with this. They've effectively demonstrated that the Sabbat actually have a viable plan for the future. Seek out the Antedeluvians, slay them, reign over the night eternal.

    It can't be that hard, after all. The Second Inquisition managed to take down two of them with some drone strikes, and Interpol agents. So an army of mass embraced Shovelheads hopped up on Potence / Celerity should make short work of them!


    And yes, I know that Giovanni was technically a 3.5th generation, but Tremere had the soul of Tremere, Saulot, and a piece of The Eldest inside him. So combined with Ravnos, that's at least 4 dead Antes that the Sabbat didn't even have to lift a finger for.

                              5th always wanted to copy some of the good aspects of Requiem 2e from the CofD without necessarily thinking about the impact of context for how rules play out.

                              Requiem 2e changed the Clan weaknesses so that they scale, and start very weak. This isn't about the "street vs. epic" scope issue. The Requiem developers were pretty clear that they wanted being a vampire to be "sexy" for awhile. Newly Embraced vampires seem to be a ton of bonuses with few penalties. It's only over time that the costs start to set in. It's only long since a vampire has completely accepted their new condition and started to justify the necessity of certain immoral actions to continue their unlives, that vampires start to really get hit with the "curse" side of things.

                              V5 copied the idea of scaling weaknesses, but didn't really tie it into the thematic thrust of the game the way Requiem did.

                                                  One of the big issues with the Ministry/Followers of Set is that the clan still hasn't really been explored all that well in V5.

                                                  Traditionally, the Setites were one of the clans that actually had a pretty powerful clan structure, with the clan leadership being composed of a council of ancient millennial old methuselahs and new members being indoctrinated pretty heavily. It wasn't quite on the level of the Tremere, but the Followers had one of the more centralized and unified clan structures. Given their location, when the Beckoning happened, it's likely that many of the leaders within the Followers of Set vanished pretty quickly leaving a big power vacuum at the top, which is what caused the clan to fracture.

                                                  Rather than the split being a rebranding it seems like this would be a full on civil war between different sects of religious fanatics. You have one sect (the Ministry) being iconoclasts claiming that the clan's fundamental understanding of Set has been wrong for thousands and thousands of years, and then you'd have the other sect which would insist on following the traditional teachings about Set. They spent millennia following Set's teaching and literally called themselves the Followers of Set, and now this new faction calling itself the Ministry no longer even pays lip service to the idea of following Set. There's no way that this would be any sort of peaceful separation or a simple rebranding, especially with the clan leadership missing and everyone fighting over their scraps.

                                                  I think there's some really neat conceptual space around the Ministry/Setite split, but it often ends up being kind of head-canony because the game itself hasn't explored any of it.
