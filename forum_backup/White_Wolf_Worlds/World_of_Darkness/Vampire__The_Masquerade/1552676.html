      This thread will most probably devolve into edition warring, because most of the things V5 detractors didn't like were lore-related.

      But trying to avoid that... I would say the Week of Nightmares as it was done. It was definitely a fun reading by itself, but its effects in the setting run from poorly thought out to outright harmful. On one hand, thinking that no one noticed three nukes in Bangladesh is just completely ridiculous. On the other hand, however one felt about the Ravnos clan, all but whipping them out was an asshole move for the player community.

      Another problem lore change was the awakening of Ur-Shulgi. The character is insufferable, and that's not even because he's "so evil", but he's simply bland as Hell. Most of the changes brought with him to the setting were actually good, and even necessary, but they could and should be implemented in a better way, from a better story behind the end of the Tremere Curse (instead of "humorless mummy breaks the curse with his pinky) to a better development for the Web of Knifes (instead of "most of the clan decides to follow the child with the personality of a spoon"). And definitely the way the Banu-Haqim who were Sabbat members switched places was not a good thing at all, as it put unnecessary pressure on players wanting to play Banu-Haqim in the Sabbat.

          I think we can avoid it getting into edition warring... but yeah V5 has a lot of lore-change related things people dislike. A big problem with V5's lore is that it's very inconsistent on when it explains things as an in-universe change, and when a change is just there an has to be treated as effectively a retcon in order to make any sense. There's plenty of good ideas in the changes V5 made, but a lot of them are also just shoved in (explained or not) that it interferes with using the changes effectively. An example here for me is the Hecata. I don't actually mind the idea of having the death themed groups reconnect and join forces, but the centuries of antagonism between some of them just feel weakly hand-waved away; esp. for the WoD and its themes it's kinda jarring for a bunch of lethally antagonistic vampires to suddenly all work together as a functional social unit.

          Kindred of the East and Kindred of the Ebony Kingdom have massive problems with how their introductions interacted with the existing lore; not helped by how much they didn't respect the cultures being focused on. KotE's "entirely different kind of vampire" has its advantages but it's also pretty unnecessary and makes running games where they are gaining power (mirroring the rise of south east Asia's power on the global scale) much more difficult as they're not just a new faction of vampires fighting for control, but a "race" of non-white vampires invading white vampire territories with the glaring issues that faces as a framework for exploring issues in a game.

          KotEK's "we're the same kinda vampire, but also just a bit different," wasn't an improvement in helping make setting games in Africa easier, or handling clashes between African vampires and vampires from other places as imperialism and colonialism have faded only to be replaced by globalization. I appreciate the intent to show that vampires can have a completely functional non-Noddist mythology, it's so late in the game for such a big push to finally explore the topic in depth that too many people just react to the African myths as wrong instead of different because different wasn't represented well enough in the older books.

          The Revised crossover event between the Tremere and the Hermetics was also the worst kind of "big event in the books that actually acknowledge it, but somehow it didn't actually change anything in the rest of the books," was just annoying as it messed with the lores of VtM and MtA with basically no payoff; and is largely ignored anyway.

          There's also a whole category of lore stuff that you can technically say doesn't count for this topic (as it's stuff from non-VtM books), but if you want to include other games (esp. the games introduced in Revised like Demon and Hunter) you're adding in a lot of weird changes that exemplify what WoD crossover is hard beyond just mechanics.
