    Originally posted by Banu_Saulot View Post
    Transylvania By Night is... cool, but it is spread WAY too thin. We have an entire book trying to cover with detail 1/3 of Europe and going only by single characters, instead of structures or societies. And for a book about "Tzimisce land" we get very little Tzimisce in comparison. Almost no Voivodes, surely no content for how the Voivodates work, or examples of Knezes. So cool ideas, but too pulverized for my liking, focusing on characters that many times are unrelated (which should be the basis of any By Night). Showing the Balkans, or just Hungary and Romania should've been more than enough.
    I would broadly agree with this; they should have dropped Russia, Poland and Lithuania, maybe also Bohemia, and focused on Hungary, Bulgaria, and maybe Serbia and Bosnia. I mean, Bosnia was a centre of heterodox Christianity at the time (i.e. what most Christians would have regarded as heresy) so very thematically in keeping with the book. Still, I would argue that there is a lot of quality writing in the book, which is enough to keep it in my top tier for these types of supplements.

    Originally posted by Banu_Saulot View Post
    Iberia By Night I thought was hot garbage. It should've been one of the most interesting regions, with Brujah, Toreador, Lasombra and Banu Haqim politics going wild. But it mostly is "Muslisms are Haquimites" and "Christians are Lasombra", period. There are no freaking Brujah characters to speak of (and even worst, they stick a damn Ventrue as a great general for the Lasombra, out of nowhere), and Toreador basically a passing fad. No plots , just Amicti Noctis deciding "ok, we'll go full-Christian", and no Banu Haqim putting the Laws of Haqim first (like Ur-Shulgi would many centuries later). There is no theme, no struggle, dilemma or campaign to take part in. It would just turn into War board game, which isn't functional for Vampire (just try the third chapter of Under the Black Cross to see how vampire open-war is very unfun to play). Besides, lots of important Lasombra characters aren't even in the book (like Eliser de Polanco and Tercio Bravo).
    Again, my regard for the book is slightly higher than yours ... but I'm not going to argue against any of these criticisms. There's this weird way of thinking in some of these supplements that a Muslim army conquers a region and, all of a sudden, all the vampires become Muslim, and then you have the inevitable conflict between Assamites and others. This appears implicitly in Iberia by Night. It's one of the books that lacks coherence because it had too many uncoordinated writers. If you look at the writing credits, you'll see that one writer's primary contribution was writing the Lasombra characters. Just the Lasombra characters.

    The sections on geography are very good, though. Compare how locations are handled here to the far inferior approach in Dark Ages: British Isles.

    Originally posted by Banu_Saulot View Post
    Damascus By Night I agree that is really underwhelming. Nothing in it justifies using Damascus, nor it has anything to do with Ashirra. The plot could've been in any city, and at any era nonetheless. For a book about Ashirra it has very little to do with it, and it does not use a venerable and great city like Damascus with any propriety. It's just the old plot of "degenerate Elder is actually a puppet of a Baali", which I tire heavily every single time I see it.
    This is a complex one; the Baali originate in Syria, and there's potentially a good set up here, with the notion in Cainite society that the Baali are long defeated and marginalized, whereas they actually hold considerable power within one of the most important cities in Syria. There are two problems, though. The first is that this is a concept that requires a lot of development (and word count) to come across as more than a generic set up, which is something that isn't going to be achieved in a single chapter of a book. The second is that Veil of Night is a book primarily about Islam and the Ashirra, and, if you are going to spend a chapter on a city, it should be a chapter that reinforces and enhances the concepts in the rest of the book. Had Veil of Night included a chapter called, for, Aleppo by Night, which focused on the nature of an Ashirra domain, whilst we had Damascus by Night as a separate, full-length book, I think people's opinions may have been higher.

        Montreal By Night stands out in my mind as a favorite, while still having brutal flaws.                                             Originally posted by Black Fox View Post

                                            I believe originally Steve Jackson Games intended to do a Dallas By Night back when they were doing GURPS versions of the World of Darkness, but that all fell apart when SJG got tired of what they considered excessive editing and changing of minds by White Wolf in approving content.
                                            There is something quite fascinating about books which never got into print. The original San Francisco by Night was due for publication in 1992, ten years before the (terrible) final version. In the early Vampire: the Masquerade books there were a lot of references to San Francisco, effectively trailing the publication.

                                            The other location sourcebook which didn't make it into print was Rio by Night; this one is a bit more murky, but was talked about a little in the mid-1990s, and would have been a book for second edition Vampire. It was even mentioned in the White Wolf writing / pitching guide at one point.

                                            (The other location sourcebook purportedly in development and which ended up never making it to print was Rage Across India for Werewolf the Apocalypse. Possibly a good thing...)
