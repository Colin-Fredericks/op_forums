    Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post
    I'm surprised no one seems to like the rule that already exists: each trait can only be raised one dot per story. That rule, which has existed forever, means that even if you dish out 200 xp in the course of one story, each discipline can only be raised one dot. So if you begin with, let's say, Protean 1, Dominate 1, Potence 1, then the most you will have by the end of the story is Protean 2, Dominate 2, Potence 2, and 1 in all other Disciplines, and that's true even if you get 200, 300, even 400 xp. You literally have to spend the points to get well-rounded, because there's nothing else to do with your pile of XP.

    By RAW, a character who received a literal billion XP in one story would, at most, have plus-one to each Attribute, Ability, Discipline, and Humanity/Path, while all the rest of the XP sat there unspendable until the next story.

    I think maybe it never caught on because the end of stories isn't always clearly defined. Many games can be rather soap-opera-y, a series of subplots that get don't get resolved until new subplots are already in play.
    That's a very, very good point.

        "Story" isn't a helpful term given that it could be two one hour sessions or it could be twenty ten hour sessions (19 of which are spent on one combat encounter)
        I kinda want my games to be untidy too. I can just give them a nice, neat self-contained story, but ideally my players should be throwing fuel onto whatever fire I've got in store for them.

        You should also consider that players might still hoard XP they can't use. We play a "story" and let's say I buy a dot of Potence and Obfuscate. I want to buy more Potence and obfuscate, but the story hasn't ended, so I keep my XP. The story ends, I use my stockpile to buy more Potence and Obfuscate, and mostly wait for the next story. In the beggining, it seems I'm just hoarding XP that I could put to good use, but as Potence and Obfuscate get more expensive, the XP surplus becomes necessary as Obfuscate is now costing more than what I could get in a single story.

        A lot of XP is also spent on disciplines because, well, you can really be rocking out the gate when it comes to abilities and attributes, but disciplines aren't that cost efficient at character creation. 2 freebies to push your 3 dots skill into a 4 is generally a better investment than doing the same with disciplines. You can start the game as a "master" of all the skills you want to use, but you can't really do that so much with disciplines, and if you do throw all your freebies into disciplines it generally feels like you're a janky powergamer who doesn't care for story, which might not work at some tables. Lastly, with abilities and attributes your target is generally 4 dots, which grants you a speciality; each dot grants you a proportionately lesser boost and as 5 dots doesn't offer a speciality, it is extremely cost inefficient. However, buying the 5th dot of a discipline usually grants you something more than just another die, so it makes sense to keep investing in them.

        That seems a little obtuse, so let me give an example.
        I want to play a nosferatu, an agressive but otherwise fairly typical member of the clan. I prioritize the Mentals, Strength, Manipulation... I can get 4 4's there without freebies. The abilities I'll want high are Alertness, stealth, larceny, brawl, Intimidation and subterfuge. I can spend 12 freebies to have 4's in all these abilities, though I probably won't do that for all of them. After picking some flaws I spend most of the rest of my freebies on willpower; I am now -good- at everything I need to "succeed" in the game. Making my best skills better is a poor use of XP. I can either improve some skills I'm bad at and improve my range, or I can spend all my XP on disciplines; because Potence 1 is a much better investment than strength 5.

        From an RP standpoint it also makes sense to have more normal skills than vampire stuff.

            I stopped giving out exp in my White Wolf games years and years ago. Instead I went with a dot system, so it would be like, "Okay everyone, at the end of the session everyone gets a dot they can put into an Attribute of your choice, as well as one dot into a non-favored Ability. Also Joe, I noticed you had some great roleplaying this session and your character has been actively working on studying a lot about ghosts, so he also gets an additional dot of Occult as well as a specialty involving Ghosts. At the end of the next session everyone will be getting an additional dot in a Clan Discipline so spend the next week thinking about what you might want to grab, but keep in mind you won't be able to increase any of your Disciplines above 4 just yet."

            I've found that by giving out dots, but sometimes relegating those dots to unfavored Abilities, or "any Ability with less than 3 dots" or "increase your lowest Attribute by 1," etc, it tends to end up making characters feel more organic. It also lets me give out more rewards to players since it's a little bit harder to min-max. Finally it helps me to control the power-scale of the game a little bit more. My group's always generally liked it, since they end up getting more rewards than they would under the exp system, even if those rewards aren't always things that they would choose to spend exp on if we were using the actual exp system.

                This is the system I use, I have been testing it for more than 5 years and it works pretty well.

                - Each session every PC gets 1 XP. Maybe 2 if the session has been very intense or formative.
                - In case of downtime, every PC gets 1 XP per year.

                We recently run a one-shot playing ancillae, 80 year old. We generated the PCs as usual and then gave them 80 XP. It worked pretty well. I have found is quite useful as a rough guideline for non-overpowered NPCs, and then you can adjust as needed.

                If we play as RAW the XP inflation is ridiculous.

                        Why not use the Magical Character Beans rules from the last chapter of Chronicles of Darkness: Mirrors? The name is intentionally silly but they suggest you can also call it destiny points or fate points or something like that.

                        That system lets you set a PC's potential growth upfront like so:

                        All players start with 30, 40, 50, 60 or 100 MCBs. These can be spent like Freebies. The ST may rule that you can only spend up to half of these (rounded down) at chargen. The rest are your future potential, which you will grow into over the course of a chronicle, but with a helping hand to get you there until the points are all spent.

                        For each MCB left over after chargen, you get that many dice per story to affect your destiny. So you can add a die to a dice pool (up to +5 dice on any one roll), trade a die to buy and frame a scene of your choice, or permanently sacrifice a die to use as a Freebie Point and advance your character. I'd probably also add things like buying a clue or introducing a fact, depending on the expertise of the PCs.

                        Any spent MCBs are replenished at the end of the story, except those spent on advancement (which are gone forever). You may also recover one or two for good roleplaying, as the ST deems fit.

                        In addition, the character with the highest remaining MCBs counts as the 'main' protagonist for the purposes of getting the spotlight. Those with fewer MCBs have less room for growth, and so the story isn't really about them.

                        This creates some nice incentives: you probably don't want to blow everything right away, because you lose those extra dice, the dramatic editing and the spotlight. But from time to time, you want to increase your traits too. Additionally, some players will be happy to spend everything early so they can play a powerful supporting role--the muscle or the mentor, for example. So long as they're okay with the game focussing on the characters with more scope for advancement, it's cool.

                        It also fixes the XP issue totally. And you can use the same rules for NPCs. If you want more powerful NPCs, have them spend all their MCBs upfront. They're powerful, but have no dramatic editing, so the PCs still have a chance and can save up those extra dice for important rolls.

                        I would probably also take a leaf from Warhammer's Fate Points and let a PC spend, say, five MCBs to completely avoid death. They get a miraculous Hollywood survival like Loomis after Halloween II or like any of those 'dead' soap opera characters that returns later and goes, 'Surprise! I didn't really die, after all!' It lets players take a bigger risk every now and again, but not all the time. You could limit it to one lucky escape per story (or even per chronicle!), or you could be really harsh and say it costs you all your remaining MCBs.

                        For more open-ended games, you could perhaps add new MCBs to the total to extend the life of the characters and give them further room to grow. In that case, you might want to start lower at the start (e.g., with 30 MCBs), so that PCs are still only growing a small amount over the course of a story. Each new story, they gain another +15 MCBs or so.

                            Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                            I stopped giving out exp in my White Wolf games years and years ago. Instead I went with a dot system, so it would be like, "Okay everyone, at the end of the session everyone gets a dot they can put into an Attribute of your choice, as well as one dot into a non-favored Ability.
                            I also do this. I've stopped bothering with XP and just give PCs increases to their traits on an ad hoc basis.

                            It's worked out pretty great for my group. You can tailor advancement rates to your game much more conveniently than XP rates, since the GM always has a complete picture of what PC stats do and could look like. If a game doesn't last long, you can give out lots of stats increases per session; if a game spans multiple RL years, you can hand out only one stat increase per session, or go some sessions without awarding any stat increases.

                            It's also satisfying as a player just to get the extra dot right there, no dicking around with XP as a middleman.

                            It's basically like getting rid of XP in D&D/PF and telling PCs to level up at GM-determined story points. I've had nothing but good experiences doing that too.
