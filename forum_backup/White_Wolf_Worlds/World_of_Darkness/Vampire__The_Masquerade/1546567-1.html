    Originally posted by Lysander View Post
    Most Blood God's and Methuselah's would have their in clan Disciplines max out and one or two more near max with a smattering of elder level powers. At this point they're more of supernatural force of nature then anything human like.
    The situation is significantly worse than that. The rule of thumb has more or less always been to assign discipline dots equal to square root of age. That's well and good, until you get to the 3,000-year-old point.

    That's 55 discipline dots, rounding up. That gets you all three clan disciplines at level 9, some common discipline elder powers, and respectable levels of all common disciplines. That's fine.

    Then, you get to "blood god" Ur-Shulgi, Japheth, Baba Yaga, Yorak age. With Japheth (the youngest "blood god") and his published age, that's 95 dots. At that point, you're running out of disciplines to justify giving these characters. The math just falls apart, and you're just better off assuming they have anything and everything they might need for a story's purposes.

                            That's my approach as well, then I make files about them and their interctions with other NPCs and depending on their importance I even write short stories about them, with my most important characters I do it, to the point where I even joke with my players that "I'm making scenes with my NPCs, so always beware that if the group doesn't do anything, the NPCs are going to do something about it". I make it because one of the things I always like to emphasize in my stories is that the "worlds keeps turning", so there's always something happening behind the scenes.

                            Regarding your commentary about relationships, I fel like WoD games, imo especially vampire, is a lot character's driven, so it's rarely a waste of time to invest in characters. I used to tell ST that were beginning to ST the game and asking advice (since I'm playing for such a long time and I actively take part in many events) that if you want to "shake things up" with a given story, add an interesting and deep character to the plot, it almost always works like a charm, players get invested and it changes the dynamics of the game and sometimes a single "not so thrilling" game session can become very much alive with a good introduction to an intereting character.
