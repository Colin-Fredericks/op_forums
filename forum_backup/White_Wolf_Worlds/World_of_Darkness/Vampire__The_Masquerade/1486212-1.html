                                    In case it's not clear to you:
                                    In V20, the Camarilla have won, the Tremere have won. The Assamites have imploded, split down the middle, with one half a monstrous parody of the clan destined to unite everyone against it and the other half a desperate, disunited diaspora with a considerable amount of well-earned prejudice directed towards them. Politically speaking, The Assamites are a toxic dumpster fire spewing radioactive waste. Why should the Tremere of V20, well established in their localities, deign to treat newcomer Assamite Sorcerers as equals? Vampires are politically minded creatures, and the only individuals who would choose to patron the in-trouble sorcerers rather than the centuries-established warlocks are those who've already burned their bridges with the clan. In most Domains the Warlocks have accumulated significant prestation and clout, no other clan is quite so good as creating debt as the Tremere, and for all their faults, their position is predictably stable in most domains (barring the cruel whims of omnipotent writers) Meanwhile the Assamites might end up dead tomorrow when a loyalist comes around and cleans them up or when they run afoul of the law. If you like to take risks from an investment standpoint, you might benefit greatly from getting into debt with an assamite and then loosing that debt when the Assamite dies, as such has a considerable likelyhood given the situation, but from a "long term strategy because you're a vampire- standpoint the Tremere are just a better deal and it's not worth humouring a sorcerer if it'll piss off the warlocks. The Assamites are risk incarnate.

                                                To summarize so far: I agree with Black Fox's observation that the Tremere's near-monopoly on blood sorcery within the Camarilla was an idea that developed over the years, with ancients from across the spectrum of clans having some utility with it in early editions and the Tremere just having a focus and a method that made them the best at it in the modern nights.

                                                With that, Revised takes Thaumaturgy's status as the go-to method for a Kindred out to create new powers and shunts that downstream to the Thinbloods in order to make them interesting.

                                                The Assamite wizard diaspora makes overtures to join the Camarilla because they're fleeing Ur-Shulgi in Revised. They do the same in V20 with the Web of Knives swapped in as a replacement for Ur-Shulgi in the name of metaplot agnosticism, but there's plenty of wiggle room for the Web of Knives to just be Ur-Shulgi's enforcers if the story you're making with V20's toolkit uses him.

                                                The net result of the Banu Haqim sorcerors joining the Camarilla is definitely to create competition for the Tremere, in either edition. And yes, the Tremere would predictably have an opinion about competition for their niche and trash talk the newcomers from a variety of predictable angles.

                                                Personally, I find it hard to picture the Prince of a city being excited about the number of blood sorcerers growing in their domain, regardless of clan background. They're a necessary evil to have on one's side because otherwise they'll be on someone else's side, but the ocean of blood that goes into research and rituals makes any chantry or wizard cult a gigantic Masquerade breach waiting to happen - but then I'm not a big fan of blood sorcery for a number of reasons, and realize that I bring my own bias to the topic.
