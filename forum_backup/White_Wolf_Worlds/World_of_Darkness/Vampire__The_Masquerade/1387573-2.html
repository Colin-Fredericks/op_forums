    Clanbook: Toreador (part two)

    Continuing our look at the Clan of the Rose.

    The Cult Leader
    I'm actually a little surprised it took us this long to get to one of these types. It's one of those concepts that works really well with a number of clans. Our character was one of those clean cut, good looking and easily charming people who had a mild interest in the occult and the sociological aspects of it. He was embraced after picking his sire up at a party after convincing her and another girl that he had psychic abilities and taking them back to his place. He got turned into a vampire; the other girl got turned into a snack. Now that he's been released and his sire gone on to do other things, he's decided to start his own cult as a means of feeding and material comfort. He's a little bit paranoid about other vampires harming his "flock" to harm him, so he's started teaching some of them how to actually hunt vampires. He's also pretty much addicted to the sense of power he has over the lives of his followers. He runs mostly off of Charisma, with five dots in that and only three in Manipulation. He's also got four dots in Wits, but is otherwise fairly average Attribute-wise. He's got four dots each in Acting, Empathy, and Leadership, and a scattering of twos and ones, including two dots in Occult, as well as in Drive and Firearms. (I'm not sure why, but Steven Brown seems to like making his characters excellent drivers. Only one character in this book lacks it, and everyone else has one or two dots in it, as well as all the ones with the skill in the Sabbat book.) I think this character might benefit from switching which of Skills and Knowledge is secondary. I think he could get by with two dots in Etiquette, and one each in Firearms, Security, and Stealth, then use the extra dots to get a few levels of Psychology and maybe a dot in Finance and/or Bureaucracy. His Disciplines focus entirely on Presence, which is fitting. (And I can't help but wonder how much more effective this guy will be as a cult leader when he starts getting a few dots in Auspex.) He's got four dots of Herd, three of Resources, two of Retainers and one of Mentor. Humanity and Willpower are both seven. Overall, I think this is a pretty good character. He's got a very strong vampire concept that I think both plays very strongly into the parallels between vampires and cult leaders as parasites preying on people and highlights his clan's tropes and strengths. The artwork is good and cuts a fairly striking image of what feels like a late 80s or early 90s West Coast cult leader. And, my above suggestion aside, the stats are pretty good. I like it.
    As a historical note, I'll mention that apparently some material about blood cults and how to run them/quantify them with backgrounds was apparently cut for space. This material ended up being printed in issue #40 of White Wolf Magazine, which is available on drivethrurpg.com.

    The Horror Writer
    This is a pretty straightforward idea. Your typical moderately successful writer of horror fiction whose work attracted the attention of another writer who happened to be a vampire. Interestingly, the character still lives with his wife and kids, and the wife knows he's a vampire. This concept is one meant to draw on two things the clanbook spends some time talking about. One is, naturally, the idea of being a vampire and famous at the same time. (I honestly wish the line had devoted an entire book to this subject, given what a big influence Anne Rice's Queen of the Damned novel was on the game and the fact that it's one of the biggest Masquerade-related questions there is.) The other is the idea that Toreador, more than any other clan, will often pretend to still be mortal, to the point of keeping mortal lovers/spouses and children. Revised would move away from this idea, but I actually find it kind of interesting. It ties in to a few aspects of Dark Ages' Road of Humanity, and makes a certain level of sense from the perspective of a clan devoted to art can't really carry out the human activity of creating art unless they actually spend time around the living. Out character is slightly alienated from Kindred society. Other vampires are wary they might end up in his stories, while he's a little worried his work - and even existence - might come to be seen as a Masquerade breach. On top of that, he's concerned by the fact that his work seems to be getting darker than before. I rather like that touch, as it plays into a number of Stephen King works, particularly The Dark Half. As far as traits go, for some reason his Physicals are secondary and Socials tertiary, resulting in threes in Strength, Stamina, and Charisma, but a one in Appearance, which doesn't match up with the art image of a fairly good looking young guy. His abilities include a four dots of Occult, which is pretty impressive. He's also got three dots each in Empathy and Poetic Expression. The rest is a collection of ones and two, including two in Drive. His Disciplines are all in Auspex. I'm a little surprised he doesn't have some Celerity, just as a subtle joke about Stephen King's voluminous level of output. He has four dots each of Resources and Fame. His Humanity is nine, which feels maybe a little high. Willpower is seven. Overall, this is a fairly solid character. I think if he has one weakness, he's probably more suited to a chronicle set in a small town (the sort seen in books by King or Bradbury), which isn't really a bad thing. I think it'd make an interesting idea for a Vampire chronicle.

    The Idol of Millions
    And now one totally built around the earlier idea of famous vampires. Our character is a big time up and coming movie star who was embraced by a production assistant. One the plus side, she doesn't have to worry about getting old and aging out of the best roles. One the minus side, at some point she becomes a walking Masquerade breach. She's very personable and charming to the media and other people who's opinions she cares about, but cold and arrogant to most everyone else. So your average Hollywood celebrity. And there's not much more to her than that. The image is sort of what I'd imagine Marilyn Monroe would've looked like if she'd be a young star in the early 80s and late 90s, which fits this character pretty well. Obviously, this is a Social primary character. She has a five in Appearance, but only three in Charisma and two in Manipulation. I kind of feel like those could be higher. Physicals are secondary, with threes in Dexterity and Stamina. Abilities include three dots each in Etiquette, Masquerade, and Seduction. Acting is only two dots, as are Athletics, Dodge, Music, and Finance. This is rounded out with a number of ones. Disciplines are all focused on Presence. Backgrounds include five dots in Fame and four in Resources, as well as a few more in Contacts, Retainer, and Status. Humanity is seven and Willpower is six. This isn't a bad character. It's just a little shallow, and she's just kind of there. I feel like she needs a purpose or goal of some sort. Maybe becoming the next huge Hollywood mogul or something. But other than that, it's not a bad character. (I will mention a rather odd bit about both the equipment lists for both this character and the Horror Writer is they both have large caliber revolvers and shoulder holsters. I'm not sure it fits either character, especially the idea of walking around with shoulder rigs.)

    That's all for this time. Next will be part three of Toreador.

        Clanbook: Toreador (part three)

        So, interestingly, our first five were all fairly conventional ideas of what most people would generally think about when you picture a clan that is built around a love of art and beauty and their amazing social prowess and attractiveness. Three are artists and the other two are based on their social abilities. The next five, in contrast, are not the archetypical artist/socialite types, and help expand a little on just what qualifies as "art" and what the clan ultimately is.

        The Martial Artist
        I like this character as an idea. But I think the execution is lacking in a few areas. He's a martial arts prodigy, having grown up for several years in Hong Kong until his uncle's untimely death forced him to return to the States and his parents. He continued to perfect his art until he drew the eye of a Toreador and was embraced. Now he still tries to perfect his art and the idea of a union of mind, body, and spirit. Except that the fact that he no longer breaths is a minor snag. And also, other Kindred keep trying to use him as a tool or weapon and that's an annoying distraction. To the point he has some vague idea of trying to build his own power base to get them to leave him alone. He's Physical primary, with three each in Strength and Stamina and four dots in Dexterity and Wits. Brawl is, of course, at five dots, with threes in Alertness, Athletics, Dodge, Melee, and Stealth. And then a few ones and twos scattered about. (This is the only character without the Drive skill.) Disciplines are focused entirely on Celerity, and Backgrounds mainly on Generation. Humanity is nine, which feels a little high, but this is mainly the result of his Virtues all being nearly maxed out, with five in Self-Control and four each in the other two. This is a character built for combat, and almost nothing else. The character art even looks like your stereotypical "young guy in a trench coat, band t-shirt and combat boots" trope. Almost like the WoD equivalent of a D&D Fighter, that shines in combat encounters but is effectively furniture anywhere else. This is a shame, because, as I said, I really like the idea of Toreador martial artist as a conceptual idea. There were a lot of ways this character could've gone that would've made him more interesting. The guy who's questing for Golconda through his marital arts studies. Someone who's become a martial arts teacher with his students as both Herd and Retainers. Someone embraced to be a weapon but rebelled and is now an Anarch. Even someone devoted to trying to master other traditional abilities as a way to further master himself and the Beast. All of these would've been really cool and fun to play ideas, I think. But this guy is your generic Lone Wolf McBadass. And while I appreciate the idea of showing people that, yes, you can do that character with a Toreador as well as Brujah or Gangrel, and the utility of going ahead and statting out such a character for anyone wanting to play it, I can't help but wish for more.

        The Outcast
        This character was always one to buck authority. Juvenile delinquent, kicked out of the Marines, and becoming a tattoo artist, only to be embraced by a Toreador. Except that the embrace was apparently on a dare, and all the other Toreador turned their noses up at him. A gang of Anarchs got him to turn on his sire, only for force him to help commit diablerie on her, and threatened to tell everyone what he did if he didn't join them. (I can't help but wonder if they were really Sabbat pretending to be Anarchs.) So eventually he led a pack of lupines to the Anarchs and ran away as they were slaughtered. Now he's pretty much bitter and alone, with only his art and a desire to be accepted just as himself. His Charisma is a one, which feels maybe low, but I guess is supposed to represent his bad attitude. Streetwise, Artistic Expression, and Firearms are all at three dots. The rest are ones and a few twos in stuff that feels like a list of "abilities most likely to come up in a street based game." Disciplines are spread evenly, with an extra dot in Presence. Humanity and Willpower are both seven. The artwork is that of an early 90s hip urban pretty boy (and actually looks more like Vanilla Ice than the Fake Rapper did), which fits the character. He's got a Desert Eagle hand cannon. There's nothing really wrong with this character. As an example of how shallow certain parts of the clan are in regards to who and what they consider worthy of their admiration, he works pretty well. I like the idea that he wants to belong somewhere but just can't because of his personality getting in the way. I think there's a lot to play with there. If his stats had a little more fine tuning, I'd probably like him better. But overall, he's not bad. I think he's probably make an interesting character in an Anarch game.

        The Plastic Surgeon
        This is another of those characters I actually like quite a bit. From a wealthy Southern California family, top of her class in high school, college and med school, and with a successful practice catering to the rich and famous. A Toreador embraced her because she loved the work she did on several of her ghouls. (I could actually see this character being the Childe of the Idol of Millions character, which is kind of cool, I think.) Unfortunately, a lot of other Toreador don't see what she does as real art and look down on her. This infuriates her to no end, as she and her work have been fawned over her entire existence. But she's honestly too scared of them, especially the elders, to actually do anything about it. Meanwhile, she's fascinated by the rumors about Vicissitude and would do anything to learn it. The character art looks like a serious minded upper class medical professional, which I approve of. She's Mental primary, with also four dots in Dexterity. Medicine is at five dots (I'm a little surprised she doesn't have a specialty), and this is accompanied by two dots of Artistic Expression. She's also got three dots each in Subterfuge and Finance, and twos in Computer, Intimidation, Melee (mainly surgical tools), and Drive (again). This is rounded out with a scattering of ones. Disciplines are two dots in Auspex and one in Presence. I'm a little surprised it's not one each, as I could see her having Celerity very easily. Backgrounds include four in Resources, as well as ones in Allies, Fame, Generation and Status. I'm not exactly sure the Status fits what is said about her relation with other Toreador in the write up. Humanity is seven and Willpower is six. All in all, I like this one. It's a cool, atypical concept, but one that still ties into the clan's fixation on beauty and the idea of just what constitutes "art". She's got a pretty strongly defined personality and a two clear goals. Probably one of my favorites in the books.

        That's all for this one. Next will be the final part of Toreador, with the last two templates and final thoughts. Then Tremere.

            Okay, I fell behind in my commentary but I'm editing one of my books and that's crunch-time for an author. As usual you've done a fantastic job.

            Tzimisce Priest: I've always felt the Tzimisce became Flanderized (see TVtropes.org) over time by the over-focus on Vicissitude. They should not just be Necroscope vampires but also have the elements of Dracula, dark occultism, pagan nature worship, and Eastern European nobility that makes them fascinating. Honestly, the flesh-crafting stuff seems kind of overplayed now and is probably the least interesting thing about them to me.

            Ventrue Antitribu Know it All: I admit, I really liked the take on them for Revised that the Ventrue Antitribu are descended from all the knight errants and heroic warriors of the Ventrue clan that were essentially exiled from the Clan when they switched from being feudal lords to merchants. It makes me think the entirety of the Road of Chivalry got screwed over because it was no longer profitable. The Know It All is a cute concept but doesn't feel very fun to play.

            Malkavian Neurotic Comedian: The Malkavians have always had a serious problem and that's the fact that they're some fundamentally incompatible ideas shoved together. The people who want to play serious mental illness don't get along with the people who just want to psychopathic serial killers as well as slashers while they don't get along with the people who want to play Tricksters as well as gadflies. Then there's the Mad Prophet people and religious ectastics. I feel like the Malkavians should have a bunch of sub-groups that include all of these but no one has ever really thought to do that. I find this archetype would go over like a lead balloon.

            Tremere Stage Magician: I always liked this one because I feel like stage magicians are a perfectly valid cover for Tremere. It allows them to act in an eccentric manner, potentially covers minor violations of the Masquerade, and you can set up places like the Magic Castle to cover your chantry. Mind you, I'm just biased because I've had several Stage Magician NPCs based on Zatanna and Zatara (who are both based on Mandrake).

            Comic Book Celebrity: Honestly, I feel like part of the fun of this thing is the fact Rob Liefeld is actually an artiste and the majority of Toreador would put him down for his medium rather than his art. "Comic books aren't real art!" However, Rob had the business savvy and media skill to really push forward not only independent comic creator rights but also his work. He may not be to everyone's tastes but that makes him more the stereotypical Toreador "creator" than the majority of their kind.

            After all the Toreador would rather have someone that 50-50 love/hate than someone who is loved by all. That would be Stan Lee the vampire.

            The Horror Writer: I wish they'd used Anne Rice as the basis rather than Stephen King. Mind you, I always felt Anne Rice stopping her vampire writing for decades is just begging to be stated to have been the result of Dominate by New Orleans' Prince. I also kind of wish that they'd mentioned that the Horror Writer has a motivation for adventuring. Perhaps the Embrace has caused his work to become dull and derivative. He needs actual adventure to get the blood flowing, so to speak. Maybe vampires being worried about being put in his books is accurate.

            Martial Artist: This is meant to be Bruce Lee meets Karate Kid and is a decent enough concept, albeit a bit stereotypical with the view of them seeking Golconda and still being involved in the spiritual side of things. I would much prefer if they'd based this one on Count Dante who is one of my top ten people I would suggest could be a vampire in real life. If anyone was a martial artist a Toreador would Embrace, it would be him.

                Clanbook: Toreador (part four)

                And our final part.

                The Speed Metal Guitarist
                This is another of those sort of period archetypes. While by 1994, heavy metal - especially late 80s hair metal - had gone out of fashion in favor of grunge and hip hop, it still had its aficionados, especially among 20-something gamers. Our character is a middle class suburban kid who formed a band in college, went off to LA and had some success only for the band to break up. A Toreador fan embraced him. (And I've noticed this seems to be a recurring origin story for many of the characters in this book. Which I guess is somewhat fitting for the clan, but it tends to make the atypical ones, like the Cult Leader, stand out.) He styles himself as a rebel, spreading a message of rebellion and intellectual and emotional freedom through his music. Apparently he rides that line between Anarch and not-Anarch/Camarilla because he's more focused on his music than actual politics, and also has this idea of trying to influence the powers that be within the clan of the worth of his art and ideals. I suspect this may be a doomed undertaking. His Dexterity is maxed out at five dots. Music is "only" four dots. He's got three dots in Drive, giving him the highest in the book. I presume this has something to do with him having a motorcycle listed among his equipment. (A Harley-Davidson no less.) Athletics is also at three dots. He's got two dots each in Brawl, Firearms and Occult, which feel a little random. He doesn't exactly strike me as the athletic punch-em-up type for some reason. The rest are single dots in things like Acting, Dodge, Etiquette, Melee, Repair, Security, Finance and Politics. I feel like this might could have benefited from some better focus. Disciplines include three dots in Celerity and one in Presence. He's got two dots in Fame. Humanity is eight and Willpower seven. Overall, this isn't a bad character. I do like the idea of someone torn between going full rebel and trying to convince the powers that be of his own worth. I think with some trait modifications, he'd be a little stronger of a character, but otherwise, I can't really complain about him. I actually think it'd be interesting to see him in a coterie with the Nosferatu Shock Rocker.

                The Televangelist
                Here's an odd on. Again, this probably owes a lot to period cultural archetypes. If you're at all familiar with the likes of modern megachurches, I'll tell you that 80s televangelists were even weirder. And they kept getting caught up in financial and sex scandals. (The 1989 film Fletch Lives is a great send up of the entire thing, especially the shady financial ends of things, and makes a perfect basis for a Werewolf chronicle.) I will note that the character art is a little odd. It kind of looks like a televangelist as interpreted for a BL manga. Very bishonen. It's not bad, just a little strange. It is very visually striking though. As far as the character, he was never very religious, and still isn't, but somehow fell into being a revivalist preacher after high school. No hint as to how or why. He managed to gradually weed out the true believers and over a ten year period, managed to make a name for himself on TV. Eventually this grew into his own 24 hour cable station. And then a Toreador fan embraced him. OK, not really. It doesn't say anything about why he was embraced. And I think that's a shame. The idea of a character like this being embraced specifically for his influence among mortals, and especially for his ability to undermine an aspect of human society that the Kindred have always seen as a potential threat is kind of cool, and I wish this had spelled that out. Anyway, he doesn't know much about Kindred society, and continues on as before, which makes him a potential mark for any vampire looking for someone easy to manipulate. His Demeanor is listed as Fanatic, which I don't think fits. I honestly would've gone for Visionary, Traditionalist or even Judge as the false persona he was trying to project. Charisma and Manipulation are at four dots each, and Perfection and Intelligence at three. Acting and Leadership are at three dots, with Empathy, Intimidation, Subterfuge, Etiquette, Finance, Linguistics and Occult at two. That last one seems a little out of place. He also lacks any ability in Theology, even if just to fake it. Disciplines are two dots each in Auspex and Presence, which seems fitting. He's got four dots in Resources and two each in Fame and Allies, as well as one each in Contacts and Influence. I'm kind of surprised he has no Retainers. He'd also be the type of character I could see as having a high level of Influence. Humanity is six and Willpower seven. Overall, this is a pretty decent character. He plays well into the idea of style over substance, and in hindsight almost seems like a harbinger of the Dark Ages Toreador's interest in the church and Christianity as a focus of both art and adoration. I'd like to see him more integrated into Kindred society, but that's something that can easily happen during an initial chronicle.

                So that's our Toreador. Over all, I feel this was a fairly solid bunch. Several of them I ended up liking a lot more than I remember from 25 years ago. I think the Cult Leader and the Plastic Surgeon are my favorites of the group, but almost all of the others have a lot going for them as well, even if I think some of them could benefit from some fine tuning. If I had to pick a weakest one, I'd probably have to say the Martial Artist, just because he doesn't really have a lot beyond "I'm min/maxed for combat" going on. And I think that could've easily been fixed with just a few altered lines in the write up. So, overall a good collection.
                However, three things I will note. One, as I previously mentioned, is this recurring thing with the Drive skill. I suspect part of this is the way 1st and 2nd edition have the skill written up to make it sound like your character can't operate a motor vehicle without it. (At least not without a plus one to difficulty.) Revised would thankfully change/clarify that. The other is the strange fixation on so many of them carrying firearms. It feels like there was this assumption that characters were just going to be getting in gunfights on a nightly basis. And finally, several of the character sheets don't add up. Much like several of the ones from the core book, they've spent too many freebie points. I think what happened here is that when Werewolf came out, it had Willpower costing only one freebie point per dot, and Vampire just switched over to that, but I don't recall any sort of official notice about the change ever happening. If so, this does create an issue in that the pre-made characters from before this change are somewhat weaker than the ones after it.
                In terms of representation, we have two women - the Idol of Millions and the Plastic Surgeon - to seven men, and one totally androgynous character, the Burnout. Given that the Toreador always got kind of stereotyped as a feminine clan, I'm not sure if this is a plus or a negative. All of them are white, save the Martial Artist, who is implied to be at least half Chinese. The Idol is somewhat sexualized, but that's in keeping with the character. (If you like cute guys, the Televangelist is almost absurdly pretty.)
                As far as additional ones or what's missing, I think the only major one is that I'm a little surprised we don't have a Harpy character totally ingrained into the Kindred social scene. I also think a Gardener, fixated on perfect flowers and the like, and an Etiquette Maven who teaches others how to behave in polite society would've been interesting. A Fashion Designer also.
                If I were to give the Toreador a theme, I'd say it would revolve around the idea of perfection. The desire for it, the way master craftsmen and artists strive for it, and the way we are often awestruck by it. Toreador crave that perfection. The want to witness it, create it, even be it. It ultimately doesn't matter if it's a perfect angel or a perfect devil. It's the flame that draws the moth, and they want to be both the moth and the flame. I think most of these characters reflect that idea to some degree. Mostly in their art, but sometimes with a need for vengeance, wanting to be the center of attention, or just wanting to belong and be accepted.

                And that concludes Clanbook: Toreador. Next will be the Tremere.

With the Brujah, for example, the hook is the leather jackets and rebellion. The core, on the other hand, is that they're driven by - almost consumed by - their passions, be it politics, religion, rebellion, intellectual pursuits, or what have you. With the Tzimsce, the flesh crafting and mad scientist stuff is the hook, but I think a lot of times the shock value of that ended up overshadowing the core idea of a bunch of people trying to transcend the mundane. Sometimes this is a lot of biological mutative weirdness or extreme sadism. But it's just as often those nobles trying to transcend the common rabble, witches trying to transcend the material world for the spiritual, and even those trying to transcend vampirism for Golconda or godhood.

I also really liked the Ventrue antitribu in the revised Sabbat book, and its call back to the stuff from the Dark Ages. But at the same time, I ended up liking this version of the group a lot better than I remember from two decades ago, and I think there's room for both of them in the Sabbat.

                            The Drive skill's omnipresence, I believe, is an artifact of game mechanics and the publishing date.

                            From First Edition through V20, the rules for the Drive skill described dot one as "can drive an automatic transmission", and dot two as "can drive a stick shift/five speed"... or the rough equivalent of that phrasing. In addition, Drive was classified as a Skill, so driving untrained raised the difficulty by 1.

                            Most STs handwaved it, or only asked for a roll when performing a difficult maneuver. However, this was a time when ttrpgs tended to have much crunchier rules. So, a strict ST could argue that without one dot you literally could not drive a car at all. (What you could drive with zero dots, but taking the +1 difficulty was never explained. A golf cart? A wheelchair?)

                            This rule, arguably, made some small amount of sense in the early games. At the time, book standard pcs could be created with up to fifty years since their embrace. This means, technically, a pc could have been embraced in 1941. Automobile ownership was much less common then, and knowing how to drive was far from universal, especially among women and city dwellers. Plus, stick shifts were more common in the 90's, so not being able to drive one was less socially acceptable.

                            (As a side note, the fifty-year rule may go a long way toward explaining why so many concepts make reference to the 1970's. It was roughly the median year of embrace for pcs.)

                                The Gross Childe
                                This is one of those names with a double meaning, being both a Childe in the Kindred sense and a child in the literal "this is a kid turned into a vampire" sense. By the time of the Revised era, there was a move away from the idea of children being embraced. It was already kind of frowned upon, but Revised went and spelled out a number of reasons why. It's a very serious potential Masquerade weakness, though that is perhaps not as big an issue with the Nosferatu, and is also seen as an act of cruelty, condemning someone to eternal unlike in a child's body. I'm not really sure this character really cares about that. This is your basic elementary school gross out artist and class clown who never really outgrew that phase in middle school. But where most would've been lucky enough to mature and turn this into a future career as a special effects makeup artist, horror film maker, or what have you, this kid was unfortunate enough to be embraced by a Nosferatu who found his antics entertaining. And like I said, he doesn't really care. In fact, the idea of being a gross looking monster for all eternity sounds down right fun. This is one of those characters I have mixed feelings about. One the one hand, I like the idea of something that plays into the idea that the the Nosferatu are gross and disturbing and will sometimes even revel in it in a "shock the mundanes" kind of way, and this character plays into that in a kind of odd and unique way. On the other hand, there's not a tremendous amount of depth here. That's not necessarily a bad thing, as the character is essentially a child. But given his focus, I can easily see this character being an outlet for certain problem players, effectively being the Nosferatu equivalent of the FishMalks I mentioned at the start of that book. Statistically speaking, he's pretty good. A Wits of five, a Manipulation and Dexterity of four each. Also a Charisma of one, which might be a little crippling in the long run. Three dots each in Stealth, Subterfuge, Dodge, and Toxicology (which is quite frankly disturbing, but in a good way). He's got a pet snake that has apparently gotten bigger after being fed his blood, making him the only character in the book with an animal Retainer. (The book spent a bit of space talking about Nosferatu spawning pits, so I'm a little surprised by that.) He has a Willpower of ten. I find that maybe too high, but I will admit it fits a little with certain aspects of the character's dogged commitment to his interests and lifestyle even in the face of all opposition. All in all, I like this character, but I'm very wary of it being abused by problem players. It's kind of like the Malkavian Waking Dreamer in that I'd have to really trust a player before letting them do this. I also kind of wish it had gone in a little bit more of a darker slant that the Toxicology ability seems to hint at. But it's still a good one.

                                The Luchador Profesional
                                I won't lie, this is my favorite one in the book. (The Equalizer comes close, but my love for professional wrestling makes this the winner.) This is your archetypical masked Mexican wrestler. And much in the way that the Detached Scientist birthed Dr. Netchurch, this one birthed https://whitewolf.fandom.com/wiki/El_Diablo_Verde"]El Diablo Verde[/URL]. At first glance, it might seem that a professional wrestler, even a masked one, is out of place in Vampire. However, Mexico has a long tradition through the 1950s, '60s and '70s of wrestling films in which masked heroes fought vampires, werewolves, mummies and other monsters (as well as gangsters and other enemies of the people), and I find this a fun nod to that. Our character was a champion selected to be part of a mass Sabbat embrace, only to be rescued by Camarilla vampires. Now he wrestles in the US, and I suspect still smashes the enemies of the Camarilla when necessary. He's got five dots in Strength and Charisma, and fours in Stamina and Brawl. I'm a little surprised his Athletics is only at a two. All his discipline points are in Potence. He's got Fame, Herd, and Resources all based around his career as a professional wrestler. (While it may seem odd for a Nosferatu, it's traditional for masked Mexican wresters to never remove their masks in public, and even in the presence of anyone.) As I said, I like this character a lot. He's a little over the top, but he's a really interesting subversion of the Nosferatu stereotype who can fill a number of roles in a chronicle or coterie.
                                Fun facts about this two characters: they could work as a Werewolf/Vampire crossover. Gross Childe has Gooshy-Gooze (TM) in his equipment which is some kind of of jelly toy slime (I believe it was mentioned either in Book of Wyrm or Subsidiaries: Pentex) which comes alive at night, comes down thethe throat of the unfortunate kid and kills it (or makeit a fomori, this is the part that I have to check). And Luchador is later mentioned in Children of the Night (but his stats is largely modified), that he fights for Extreme Wrestling Warfare, a wrestling show sponsored and televised by Pentex where supernaturals of any kind could fight without regarding the Masquerade. And that's make this federation an ideal target for Garou.

                                                            Clanbook: Tremere (part the first)

                                                            So, still in 1994, and now the sixth of the Clanbooks. Out cover is a bit strange. Much like the Toreador one before it, it kind of lacks anything that makes it stand up and say, "this is what the Tremere are about", the way the first four books did. If anything, the guy kind of looks like he's standing in a corner pouting because the other people at the party made fun of his soul patch. Worse, there's an image on page 124 of the second edition core book of a guy with a cane who looks like more of an archetypical Tremere to me, and one much more sure of himself than this guy. Anyway, this book was written by Keith Herber, with additional material by Kerry Thornley. "Additional material" could mean nearly anything, so I've no idea which of them wrote the templates. Nor do I recognize either name off the top of my head, so I'm not sure what else they've done for Vampire or any other games. Artwork is once again by Josh Timbrook.

                                                            Child of the Tremere
                                                            This is an interesting one. He's described as being from a family with a history of supernatural gifts. In the WoD, this could be any number of things. As this predates the introduction of the Tremere's own family of rogue Tzimisce reverents, the Ducheski, it's entirely possible he could be interpreted as one of them if you wanted to. He was taken away at age five and trained by the Tremere for 18 years, then embraced. He doesn't particularly like being a tool of the clan his entire existence, but he doesn't really have any alternative to compare it to either. Beyond that, he's kind of devoid of personality. It doesn't say anything about what he was taught or why, what he's actually interested in or hopes to achieve, or if he even knows how to fit in with mortal or Kindred society. His Nature is Child, which would imply that he's sheltered and used to having someone else make all his important decisions for him. So I guess you could play him as a sort of fish out of water type if you wanted. He's got five dots in Intelligence, while Socials have him with one dot in Charisma and three in Manipulation. I've mixed feelings about if that is slightly skewed or if it fits his weird upbringing. He's got three dots each in Linguistics, Investigation, Occult, Etiquette, and Subterfuge, and twos in Intimidation, Repair and Security. All other abilities - Melee, Music, Computer, Law, Medicine, and Politics, are only one dot. His disciplines are all one each, with an extra dot in Thaumaturgy. Backgrounds are spread between Generation, Herd, Mentor, Resources, and Retainer. Humanity and Willpower are both at seven. I like this idea as a concept. Unfortunately, that's all it is. It's a basic idea of a backstory and some traits, lacking any personality or motivation. Which is a shame, because I think if he had those things, as well as a better focus on spending his points that would come with it, he'd be a pretty cool character. But as he is, he's just sort of incomplete and fuzzy.

                                                            The Conspiracy Expert
                                                            I suspect that it is intentional that this character image's clothes bring to mind Darren McGavin in Kolchak: The Night Stalker. This character is a life long conspiracy buff. And by that, I mean a fanatic. He spent his entire life searching for some sort of universal truth behind it all, only to end up embraced by a Tremere promising to give him answers. Now that he knows the truth - or at least thinks he does - he wants to research and investigate and interview every single facet of it he can. He's very enthusiastic about this, to the point I don't think he's aware of what a dangerous world he just stepped into. I feel like his Nature and Demeanor of Visionary and Fanatic may be backwards. He seems more like a fanatic that doesn't quite understand how obsessed he is than a guy with some greater ideal who is just acting as a fanatic. Somewhat oddly, his Socials are primary, with a four in Charisma and threes in Manipulation and Appearance. Mental is secondary, and with a three in Wits and a four in Intelligence, that leaves him with only one dot in Perception. While this character is a little out there, he doesn't come across as oblivious. He's got three dots each in Security, Stealth, Investigation, and Occult. Also twos in Alertness, Empathy, and Drive, then ones in Dodge, Streetwise, Animal Ken, Etiquette, Firearms, Repair, Survival, Computer, Linguistics, Medicine, and Science. These feel sort of all over the place. I'm a little surprised he doesn't have any secondary abilities like Kindred Lore, History, Cryptography, or the like. He's got three dots in Auspex and one dot in Thaumaturgy. Backgrounds are just Contacts at two and Resources at three. I feel like this is the kind of character who should really be maximizing his Contacts more. Humanity is eight and Willpower six. This is not a bad character, and I think he certainly fits with the whole idea of the Tremere - and in extension the Kindred - as this massive conspiratorial secret society. He's kind of an interesting counterpart to the Malkavian Conspiracy Theorist in that he wants to study and understand this thing while she feels she instinctively understands it and wants to destroy it. However, I think he would strongly benefit from having most of his stats reexamined and refocused.

                                                            The Cultist
                                                            At first glance, this might seem to be a rehash of the Cult Leader in Toreador. However, it's actually quite a bit different. While the Toreador character never believed anything he was selling his followers, this guy is a true believer whose delved into the occult all his life in search of personal power. And finally he found what he was looking for when a Tremere showed up at a graveyard summoning and embraced him. He greatly enjoys the power of being a vampire, and is very likely soon to want even more. He's abandoned or otherwise betrayed almost every group he belonged to in life, and would likely try to sell out the Tremere at some point. He'd probably be voted Most Likely to Join the Sabbat or become an Infernalist. The character image is pretty good. He's got the whole "Goth Satanist" look down. His Charisma is at five dots and Manipulation at four. (Appearance is only two.) Abilities include four dots in Acting, and three each in Intimidation, Subterfuge, and Drive. (I guess this is so he can ride a motercycle.) Strangely, he's only got two dots in Occult. Disciplines are two dots in Dominate and one in Thaumaturgy. Backgrounds are spread evenly between Allies, Contacts, Mentor and Retainer. Humanity is seven, which feels maybe one too high for this guy. Willpower is eight. Overall, I kind of like this character. He's essentially an ass, as likely to throw his coterie-mates under a bus as he is to help them. But I think that plays pretty well into the Tremere clan concept. The only real problem is that I think his traits need the same reexamination and refocusing as the previous character. Other than that, he seem potentially fun.

                                                            The Explorer
                                                            I kind of have mixed feelings about this character. Overall, I like it. But at the same time, I think it treads way too close to covering a lot of the same ground as the Conspiracy Expert. He's an archaeologist and Egyptologist who far preferred field work to university life. Another Egyptologist pointed him in the direction of a lost tomb in exchange for smuggling back some of the scrolls and artifacts he would find there. At this point, one of those "Curse of the Mummy's Tomb" scenarios unfolded (a nice touch which I greatly appreciate), with him as the final surviving member of the team. Then his Egyptologist "friend" embraced him, because his skills were too good to go to waste. Now he's somewhat stuck acting as a translator and scholar for the Tremere, which feels too much like the stuffy and bureaucratic university life he'd always avoided. He wants the excitement of being out in the field again. His Physicals are primary, which I find a little off for some reason. I appreciate the desire to want to do a physical Tremere, but I'm not sure this is the right one for it. This leaves his Mental as secondary, having a three in each of them. None of his abilities go above three either. Archaeology, Investigation and Linguistics - the things he was specifically embraced for - are each three dots. Science and Occult are only one dot each. He does have twos in Melee, Survival and Leadership. Meanwhile, he's got three dots in Auspex and one in Dominate. I honestly feel those seven freebie points could've been better spent on raising some of his abilities. His Backgrounds are spread between Allies, Contacts and Mentor, with none higher than two. (He feels like one of those characters were a level of Fame wouldn't be out of place.) Humanity is seven and Willpower is six. Overall, this isn't a bad character. He's got an interesting background and is fairly straightforward for a Tremere. But I think this one also suffers from the need to have his traits adjusted to better fit the concept. Also, for some reason I feel like there isn't anything specifically "Tremere-like" about this character. I think if the aspect that this was a guy perfectly willing to break the law and smuggle artifacts just in the quest for his own knowledge was played up a little more, it would solve that issue for me. But otherwise, I generally like this one.
