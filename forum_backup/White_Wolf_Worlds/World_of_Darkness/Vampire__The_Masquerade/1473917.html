                      Another thing to consider is the proper role of leadership and the definition of "superiority" to differing peoples.

                      If you see the Sabbat episcopate as a top down organization where lower ranking people must obey their superiors, it causes problems for characters on the Path of Night. But if you believe the Sabbat is a sect with two equal principles - loyalty and freedom, it isn't an obstacle. The Loyalist faction believes in "loyalty to the Sabbat through disloyalty to leaders." That fits in quite well with the Path of Night, so we should assume the Path of Night is probably one of the bulwarks of the Loyalist faction.

                      Even a Ductus isn't necessarily an issue if you think the leader is merely a first among equals, or someone who helps settles disputes between equals so a group can accomplish something, versus a military style leader whose orders are absolute. To a pack following the Path of Night, a Ductus might simply be the designated person who speaks for the group when dealing with outsiders, not someone who tells the others what to do.

                      None of this presents a problem with the way the Sabbat was originally portrayed which was a sect heavily divided. It only becomes an issue due to metaplot in Revised and V5 which essentially says the entire Sabbat is now like the Black Hand, which is a military organization with a strict hierarchy of obedience. This is one reason I ignore metaplot, stopped buying Revised materials, and don't support V5.

                          Agreed with Black Fox.

                          The inherent nature of the Sabbat - a decentralized confederation of Cainite packs brought together by mutual, communal blood bonds and for united protection against the Antedeluvians.

                          While hierarchies exist within the larger sect (for the Sabbat is, if nothing else, a mass of hypocrisies and contradictions), a Path of Night adherent doesn't need to respect anyone in a position of authority.

                          This is also why, even when crusades are nominally run by one Cainite Elder or another, they coach their marching orders in the form of requests, suggestions, challenges, or opportunities. Because savvy Cainites know that many Sabbat packs or individuals will not respond well to being ordered around. This is just Sabbat in general, not just Path of Night licks. Rebels and contrarians all, Cainites on crusade must by tricked, goaded, or convinced to do anything. This is both a strength and a weakness of the sect, and why every crusade involves throwing as many Cainites at a problem to see what sticks.

                          The Path of Night simply codifies this penchant for militant "you can't tell me what to do" attitude into an ethical law.

We should have seen just as much vandalism and desecration of certain Lasombra culture in the same way the French Revolutionaries did to the Ancien Regime, or the Bolsheviks did to the Tsarist and aristocratic heritage, or the Maoists did to traditional Confucian culture. The Sabbat Lasombra should be recognizably distinct from the Lasombra antitribu in some fashion, and I don't think this was ever well presented.

                                      I have no problem with seeing many Lasombra of the time of the Anarch Revolt becoming hypocrites and power hungry, betraying their old ideals for power. I also don't have a problem for some opportunistic Lasombra who heed the old ways having joined the sect. Or that as time passed on the fires of youth faded, that some aspects of the old Lasombra culture came back. But there should also be a large number of Lasombra who continue to have the ideals of the original Lasombra anarchs who supposedly fought for freedom from the elders. And this last group of Lasombra has always been neglected in the game.

                                      If you wanted to figure out a way on how to combine the original ideals of the Anarch Revolt and the core Lasombra identity of them being ambitious and masters of intrigue, you could do a lot worse than the Path of Night.

                                      I think one issue is that the Path of Night was not one of the original Paths in the first Players Guide to Sabbat. So there obviously could not be any attempt to explain the clan using that Path. Instead, it was probably felt that all Sabbat Lasombra would adhere to a path like Power and the Inner Voice, Honorable Accord, or other more obvious leadership Path. I think the Path of Night first appears in the Revised VtM corebook where it's presented as a Path almost exclusively by very young Lasombra, who are scorned by the other Lasombra. (So your black sheep comment is on the mark).

                                      And like many concepts of the game, once its out there, it is often developed further by people who see potential in something, but don't adhere closely to details. So we can get to the point where we think the Path of Night is iconic to the Lasombra until it occurs to us this undermines other aspects of the clan like the OP mentioned. So if you want to say both aspects are true, it may be better to state that the Path of Night is something distinctive to the Lasombra (in the sense it reveals something unique about the clan), but isn't iconic (in the sense that it defines the clan).

                                                      Originally posted by Cadmiumcadamium View Post
                                                      But I still don't really comprehend the concept of "First among equals".
                                                      "First among equals" is simply a phrase that indicates that while one person may have more prestige, or higher skill, or whatever, that fundamentally that person is still just an equal member of the group without any more authority or ranking than the other members. They're in a group of friends or peers, there's not hierarchy among them. Someone might choose to follow or defer to such a person, but the person isn't obeyed as if he is a superior.

                                                      Such a status might be an accurate description if we're talking about the most charismatic and athletic guy in a group of friends. Or it might be a polite fiction to hide real authority like in the case when Augustus Caesar was supposedly simply the "First Citizen" of Rome.

                                                      Originally posted by Cadmiumcadamium View Post
                                                      Sure the Bishop doesn't order a pack to do something, he lets them know what he wishes and either presents it as a challenge, or an opportunity.
                                                      And a pack might just nod their heads and do their own thing, or just laugh in the guy's face. These are also options available to them. Whether they are the smart options or credible actions may depend on a lot of circumstances. Depending on the Sabbat domain, the power of the Bishop, the packs involved, etc., a Bishop's "request" might be a veiled order, letting others know there is an opportunity to kiss his ass and earn his favor, just a request for help from someone he thinks is an ally, or a pathetic beg from a weak and impotent man.

                                                      Originally posted by Cadmiumcadamium View Post
                                                      But he still expects Cainites to recognize his status and respect him due to meritocratic structure of the Sabbat.
                                                      I am dying laughing at the supposed "meritocratic" structure of the Sabbat.
                                                      Certainly someone on the Path of Night (or anyone else) can acknowledge someone's status as a Bishop and be polite to him. But that does not mean the person must recognize the Bishop's authority over him. This is the entire dispute by the Loyalist faction of the Sabbat. What does a sect based on total freedom owe to its nominal leaders? We know what the Status Quo and Ultraconservatives factions want it to be - obedience to the Sabbat hierarchy as governed by the revised Code of Milan. We also know what the Moderates and Loyalists believe - the sect needs to lean towards more freedom.

                                                      To a Loyalist or even a Moderate, "respect" may simply mean "I will listen to your plan and consider its merits. But it is up to me if I decide to join it. I am willing to perform the Vaulderie with you to show I do not consider you an enemy, but that will bind you to me as much as it binds me to you."

                                                      A Bishop might consider that insufficient. But that's politics.

                                                      Originally posted by Cadmiumcadamium View Post
                                                      If a lick on the Path of Night blatantly ignores him or disrespects him he will most likely either Frenzy or work towards getting that lick removed from his domain.
                                                      That's true, but the opposite also holds. I think you are assuming that the Bishop is in a superior position of power to the other Sabbat. That may or may not be true.

                                                      In certain Sabbat domains, the Ultraconservatives might dominate and run a very strict domain, and any signs of disobedience will be met with terrible force. But that is not true in all domains. If you're a member of a Nomad pack that spends most of their time travelling and rarely spending any time at any domain, it might be completely irrelevant what any Bishop wants. He has no means of truly enforcing his rule over you. You just leave when it becomes inconvenient. Or maybe the Bishop is new or weak, and the follower of the Path of Night could easily slay him in monomacy. Or your pack/coven is so experienced, old, large, or whatever that you have in effect more power and influence in the domain than the Bishop. Or perhaps the entire Sabbat domain is a stronghold of the Loyalist faction, and the Bishop is one of the Brujah anti-tribu who shares the same conception of leadership that the follower of the Path of Night does.

                                                      Lots of scenarios are possible. It's not that what you said is wrong, it is just an incomplete analysis.

                                                      Originally posted by Cadmiumcadamium View Post
                                                      And even if the adherent of the Path of Night doesn't outright scream "you're not my dad" to the Bishop he will still clean his room because "dad" told him to. Which to me would constitute as a violation against the lvl 2 sin.
                                                      As I explained, not necessarily. Why should a Sabbat bishop necessarily command greater obedience than does a Prince of the Camarilla? In vampire society, you can only command to the extent that you can convince or compel someone. In some domains, a Bishop's orders may be law because of the Bishop's power, his allies, trusted enforcers among the Paladins, or because of the backing of other forces like the Sabbat Inquisition or Black Hand. To defy them means death. In other domains, the Bishop might lack the power of other Sabbat in the domain, have few allies, or is being undermined by others. To defy them is normal, provided you can justify it to the other local Sabbat as being loyal to the principles of the Sect.

                                                      A Bishop who doesn't have the personal power, prestige, allies, or political skill who attempts to enforce his will beyond his capabilities will suffer drawbacks. At worse, he gets killed - in secret or through monomacy. Or he might find his prestige collapsing when he's not obeyed, and then when word gets out to the other Bishops that he can't enforce his will, he loses even the respect of his peers. Or maybe the most powerful or charismatic packs simply leave his domain and go elsewhere, causing him to lose tools he might have otherwise used to shore up his power, and now his domain is weak and might fall to archons or hunters.

                                                      What this means for followers of the Path of Night is that they need to choose where they live carefully. It needs to be conducive to how they act. The relative power of the Bishops need to be in their favor. Don't go to Mexico City where the Regent lives and the Ultraconservatives are in force. But there may be other domains where the Loyalists are much stronger and the authority of the Bishops is less. Or they can go on the road as a Nomad pack and mostly ignore the Bishops entirely. Don't visit that domain until you hear the Bishop has turned the Panders against him, and a founded coven there is calling in friends from elsewhere to help them put the Bishop in his place.

                                                      The Sabbat hierarchy is strong in certain places and weak in others. It's ruled in some places by hardass tyrants, and others by charismatic Loyalists.

                                                      Ultimately it's how the ST builds the world and whether he allows there to be places where that attitude can be done. In 2e Sabbat, there were lots of places where this could be done. But in Revised, this aspect of the sect was ignored because it got in the way of the Crusade storyline metaplot that Justin Achilli wanted to tell. In 2e, the existing power relationship that favored the Ultraconservatives was a result of the Panders siding with them in the Third Sabbat Civil War. But their rule is not absolute, and any number of changes could tilt favor back towards the Loyalists. Depending on how the ST wants to balance things and portray the setting, a lot is possible.

                                                          The Lasombra are described more as "Ok, old elites recognized the winning horse and joined", a la Nazi Germany or Fascist Italy: it's appropriate, in this context, that we have an ideologized minority of radical believers in Revolutionary Ideology, scoffing at the bourgeois habits of the upper echelons.
