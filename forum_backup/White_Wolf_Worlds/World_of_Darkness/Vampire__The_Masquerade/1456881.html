    TC, part 18: "Imagine the Lannisters went to Hogwarts"

    08-15-2021, 01:24 PM
    Last report's here. The long story short is...they survived.

    Barely.

    At one point I had Bulscu's attack dice pool already in hand, when the Setite's player managed to shut himself up before overselling the story and blowing things up in his own face. But getting there and beyond that point...let's just say it could have gone very wrong at any time.

    Before we get there, though: the great deck chair shuffling continues. The PC roster now stands at one Lasombra, one Gangrel, one Setite (original PC's), one Brujah, one Assamite sorcerer soon to be in very unfortunate circumstances whose player hasn't been able to make games since we restarted, and the newest PC, one Tzimisce. The Brujah, Assamite, and Tzimisce being replacement or new players. The new Tzimisce is being played by one of the two Gangrels' players -- the one who was the bag-holder prince in the last game session who, due to a combination of Auspex and the Danger Sense merit, realized their best chances of survival lied in being as far away from Transylvania as inhumanly possible. So, that character just straight up peace'd out.

    But, it's a Tzimisce with caveats. This is the result of a mistake on my part, I originally said only OCT which was the source of a healthy dose of confusion as the V20/DAV20 information on OCT isn't the best -- and I don't want them delving too deeply in the TMR book. So, I ended up settling on this awkward, strange place of reverting to the generational and age rift in the clan upon which the Anarch revolt is built, that will only continue to grow wider in the future, and clarifying to my players I'd only be accepting Tzimisce PC's of the "elder" side of that rift. From there, things fell into place quite nicely as I worked with the player to develop an appropriate backstory that fits the chronicle, its many subtexts, and the bigger plotline of TC.

    Oh, who am I kidding. I managed to bullshit them into playing an Inconnu mole who happens to be Danika Ruthven's childe. They're actually quite happy with their character, and blissfully unaware the deeper implications of words like "Ruthven" and "Inconnu" for now. I even managed to keep a straight face when I told this player the Inconnu are basically just the Roman version of the Camarilla, and they're a society of elders and methuselahs who watch the Jyhad for signs of the Antediluvians, and work behind the scenes to fight the Antediluvians and avert Gehenna.

    Probably should have been paying closer attention in the previous game session, when I pointed out the vampire who saw through the Eldest's ruse murdering Lugoj and replacing him to fake its own diablerie, was Lambach Ruthven. Yes, I am an asshole storyteller.

    The quote in the title comes from my attempts to explain who the Ruthvens precisely are to this player, seeing they haven't been playing VtM for nearly thirty years now like some of us in the group and aren't fluent in metaplot. I went through the broad strokes -- the oldest and greatest family among the clan, first among equals, the name alone commands respect and fear even among the clan's enemies, embody the best and grandest of the clan -- but they weren't really getting a feel for how they might play the character on a scene-by-scene basis, so I told them to imagine the Lannisters at Hogwarts, and their sire is Minerva McGonagall. Except evil.

    And, because I told everyone explicitly to make backup characters (they're going to need them anyways when the main characters start slipping into torpor due to age), the Lasombra player and this one went for making linked character backstories, so I ended up with two Ruthvens who happen Danika's childer in the group. The Lasombra's player wasn't sure if he'd be able to make the game session but turned out able to, and we already worked out what's going on with his Lasombra in the meantime, so he played his backup this game session to give the newer player a leg-up in playing a Tzimisce, and have someone to bounce off in their new character's introductory game session.

    That Tzimisce will be important in the next few stories, as they're not Inconnu and actually on Path of the Grey Hunter which makes her a bit of a rogue childe, but this childe's domain is a free company with ties to the Hussites. I already talked the immediate future for the character through with the player, and the plan as things stand is they're going to end up in Matthias Corvinus' Black Army. So as if things weren't weird enough, I'm gonna end up with a Tzimisce PC on Team Ventrue...because this PC is nominally old clan, and would rather work with the clan's enemies to beat its traitorous elements into submission rather than stand by as her own clan goes to shit. This is a time for strange bedfellows, indeed.

    At this point I should make a brief aside as far as backup characters go, because this just gets funny. The other Gangrel player said he wanted to play a Malkavian, and came up with this backstory of a Society of Leopold member who was embraced by a Christian vampire to save their unlife. So he's playing a childe of Anatole. The Setite is rolling up a Molochim Baali, which is going to be hilarious on its own, but I decided to up the ante by allowing him to take the Lasombra PC's sire -- Faustina -- as a mentor.

    But trolltastic metaplot fuckery has to wait. As this game session stands, it's two Tzimisce, one Gangrel, one Setite, and one Brujah.

    I started things off with the two Tzimisce. They were called to Danika's side, she told the good childe Bulscu has recently arisen from torpor and that he is gunning for this coterie of Cainites, who have made themselves obvious pawns in the Jyhad, and are apparently responsible for his torpor. The good childe is to monitor the situation, report back, and if Bulscu decides to kill them all ensure whatever occult lore this coterie has collected falls into Danika's hands before the Tremere get to it. The rogue childe is to guard the good one and ensure no harm falls to her.

    So off they go to Mediasch. They get there and are surprised by what they see -- the Roma caravans are not only departing in peace, but they're departing in peace being given alms by the Hungarian soldiers camped out there and not an Inquisitor in sight. They're predictably confused by this, and continue to court to find not Nova in praxis but instead Bulscu's childe -- Albrecht, the same fledgling who completely no-sold the Setite ancilla in the previous game session. They introduce themselves and it goes predictably well, stating they're only passing through, and Albrecht mentions he's only sitting as seneschal for Bulscu himself. As such he offers the two Tzimisce one week of hospitality and full privileges, but that they'll be considered under suspicion because of the tiny Tzimisce problem going on right now.

    The two Tzimisce return to their mercenary encampment outside the city, and they're eventually approached by Vassily Taltos. Vassily reasoned the appearance of two Cainites at the front of a mercenary host not a night after the previous management fled is no coincidence, and figured there might be a good opportunity for information barter. Vassily lets it slip the Roma and Ravnos were there to administer the Treatment, Albrecht is on the Road of Heaven, and the orders to distribute alms to the Roma came directly from Bulscu. Vassily does let it slip (for prestation) Bulscu also left with a contingent of solders to Sibiu in a hurry, hot on the heels of the Cainites who fled the city who are apparently fugitives from Ventrue justice.

    Having gotten the information they're looking for, the two Tzimisce order their mercenary company to break camp at dawn and proceed to Sibiu after having left Albrecht suitable gifts as thanks for his hospitality. They daysleep on the way, and barely after they're awoken they're both told a Cainite approached their camp and desires parley. They permit it, and it's none other than Bulscu. Bulscu's no fool, his pawns recognized Ruthven heraldry when they saw it, they woke him up early specifically to tell him of this development, and he wants to know why two Ruthvens just happened to show up at this exact city, at this exact time.

    That meeting actually goes by entirely civilly. Bulscu correctly guesses their own lineage before they've had the opportunity to even state it -- none of Tabak's lineage would have honored the old ways and Damek only has one surviving childe, meaning they had to be Danika's childer. As if that wasn't enough, Bulscu mentions he had gone to war with Damek several times, reminisces a bit about how great a leader and warrior the departed Tzimisce methuselah was, and extends his belated condolences, stating the Tzimisce are greatly diminished by his absence.

    I didn't intend for horrendously threatening subtext to be part of Bulscu's characterization, but here we are. It fits, the players have realized they're dealing with an OG motherfucker Ventrue super-heavyweight in stark contrast to Hardestadt's entirely off-stage, long distance, manipulative bullshit, I'm rolling with it.

    Bulscu's even so kind as to extend the offer for them to enter the city at his side. So of course, they take it.

    Meanwhile, inside the city...

    Immediately after the meeting that concluded the previous game session, the PC's go their separate ways to do their own thing in the city. The Brujah remains at court, and witnesses the Lasombra's sire -- Faustina -- show up. The PC's sire -- the Prince, Eyulf -- is not happy with her arrival, and they gnash fangs and threaten each other before Faustina announces she's there to retrieve her childe because they have clan business. Eyulf tells her to take him, get the fuck out of his city, and never return on pain of Lextalionis -- not just in Sibiu, but in any domain in which Eyulf has the scantest of influence or favor. She leaves, and Eyulf immediately frenzies, throwing his own throne across the room and trashing several tapestries and columns.

    Once Eyulf calms down, the PC comes out from hiding and inquires what that was about (in the politest possible way). Eyulf tells him Faustina is a Baali apostate, something he learned two hundred years ago. Worse, is she's old and powerful enough there's not much Eyulf can do about it -- at least on terms Brujah would prefer. Worse than that is her propensity for calling situations as she sees them and being right. So if she has important enough business to recall her childe now of all times, it's something Eyulf would be smart to not interfere in. And as if that amount of "fuck" wasn't enough, she's an Archon and to interfere with her business would endanger his newfound standing in the Camarilla, putting him at the Ventrue's mercy.

    And worst of all, Eyulf owes her and what she's up to would probably be something of which he'd approve anyways. And knowing full well Faustina could have been in and out of the city without him ever being the wiser, means she showed up in court for the express purpose of...baiting him into frenzy for her own amusement. The Brujah PC wisely declines commentary on the situation's irony, and goes to find the Gangrel PC who's holed up in a pub nearby to start getting a feel for the city's current state.

    And that brings us to the Setite...

    So, he's walking around the city when his Auspex sense goes off and he realizes he's being followed. He can't pierce the Obfuscate of the person following him, so he find a nice mortal population to surround himself by with a combination of Awe and some impromptu public speaking. At this point I need to point out this player's favorite clan is the Followers of Set, and more than anything else he loves playing lector-priests. So, it's to my great joy as a storyteller and player at this point he takes a nice, big blood sorcery gobsmack to the tune of 5 temporary Willpower, and he feels his addiction shift from vitae to the adoration of a crowd.

    In his own words, "wait, I recognize that path...you motherfucker!"

    The Setite makes his Occult roll to realize what's happening to him, and he wrenches himself away from the crowd and dismisses his ghouls to have a chat with the new boss.

    Now, way back at the start of the chronicle there was a Setite lector-priest NPC. This NPC, Adolf the Lucky, was part of the coterie that rescued Nova Arpad from Ruxandra. Adolf was the one intended to sneak in on Operation Fuck Bulscu, and that's why he was sent there by the Followers. But when the Setite PC got in on the same action -- he was never intended to be there to begin with, he was following visions and prophecies he received from eating a Malkavian -- that left Adolf high and dry, and Adolf ended up snuffing it to the Inquisition in the late-13th Century.

    The Setites' intent was to convert Geza Arpad and Bulscu, and make them the ideal Hungarian pawns. When Jurgen found out about Operation Fuck Bulscu those plans came to a screeching halt. From there it blew up in the Setites' face, and they ended up the target of a shadow crusade waged by the Ventrue and Tremere across their domains for nearly two hundred years. It's only with the chaos of the Anarch revolt the Followers have been able to once again make serious inroads in eastern Europe.

    Needless to say, the new boss isn't exactly happy with the Setite PC inadvertently fucking over his own clan for the better part of two centuries. But, because of brotherhood in service to Set and all, the Setite PC gets a chance to account for himself. Which he does, truthfully explaining Adolf cut him out of the loop on what the clan's schemes were, and that once Jurgen found out (through no action of his own) he had no real choice in the matter -- had Adolf been forthright with the PC, there might have been something he could have done to ensure Operation Fuck Bulscu went as intended. That missing bit of information is enough for the lector-priest to spare the Setite PC's life, and the lector-priest explains in no uncertain terms he'll need to provide a proper accounting to Bulscu when the time comes, to shut up and accept whatever punishment Bulscu deems appropriate, and maybe the lector-priest will allow the PC to return to the fold.

    It's then the lector-priest reveals himself, and he's not a Setite -- he's a Cappadocian. And one that isn't just an emaciated, sullen, shriveled corpse, but a rotting corpse. The Setite's player makes enough successes on an Occult roll to have heard rumors there's a lineage of old and really low-generation Cappadocians who count themselves among Set's chosen, who roll harder than the clan proper, tend to be enforcers and cleaners, and don't fuck around when it comes to their job.

    So now do we not only have Lasombra antitribu, Brujah and Baali frenemies, and Tzimisce working with Ventrue, we have lazarenes.

    Having been properly chastised and warned of the delicacy of the coming night or two, the Setite joins the Brujah and Gangrel in the pub and discuss relevant matters, and how they're going to talk their way out of this.

    The next night, Bulscu and the two Tzimisce enter the city, and everyone relevant is called to court. At this point the only two PC's present who are being held to account for Operation Fuck Bulscu, are the Gangrel and Setite. They barely talk their way out of it.

    The Gangrel had fuck-all to do with it other than being in the same coterie and dominated by Jurgen to believe he played a role, and the Setite legitimately had no idea where the drugs and slaves were going until after Jurgen muscled his way into it, at which point the matter was completely out of his hands. The Giovanni PC was the one who knew, and the one who unfortunately blabbed it to Vykos, who in turn told Nova -- this part of the Setite's testimony, of course, piquing the interest of the two Tzimisce. None of them knew how the disparate pieces fit together, until well after the fact when it took Constancia showing up (as a favor from Radu) to warn them how badly Vykos fucked them all over, and under no circumstances should Vykos ever be trusted again.

    Bulscu considered the PC's testimony, and concluded neither could be held truly culpable for what happened. Fault ultimately lied on Jurgen's head, and his own for losing himself to addiction and degeneracy. But, honor still demanded restitution, which would come in the form of boons owed to him by members of the coterie.

    Which left the matter of tradition-breaking on the Setite's part, having violated hospitality by attacking Albrecht two nights previous. The punishment could well have been Lextalionis but that may have been a stretch for Bulscu to demand, being in a Brujah's domain and the Prince of the domain when and where it happened having fled for his unlife. So, Bulscu decided to play it safe, having decided for himself the humiliation of an ancilla being publicly bested by a fledgling who hadn't even made the Accounting was already enough. He demanded a written letter of apology and an additional boon owed to Albrecht, from the Setite.

    The players realized Bulscu could well have simply walked in, started claiming heads, and Eyulf would happily have given him permission to do it. Despite his hatred for the Ventrue, Eyulf's political connections and status were more important than any individual Cainite in the room. Rather than result to barbarism of a bygone age, Bulscu instead decided to navigate politically-treacherous waters and offer damned good terms -- because naturally, the continued presence of the PC's among the unliving was a stain on Hardestadt's dignitas that may well be used in Bulscu's favor in the future.

    Nobody walked away happy, but everyone walked away with their unlives. But for a few moments, it was very sketchy.

    And as Bulscu left to deal with the minor matter of the Transylvanian peasant revolt, the Tzimisce packed up camp. The rogue childe, being a Grey Hunter, decided to take a walk. And off in the distance barely over the horizon, she saw an explosion of green flame that manifested into a small mushroom-looking cloud and resultant wild fire.

    Miles away, Faustina and the Lasombra PC stand on a hill, watching a cave nearby explode leaving little but a smoking crater. The Baali apostate doubles over in barely-controlled laughter and amazement, commenting that is one fantastic way to deal with a pack of Lasombra anarchs on the hunt, and that once word spreads, Gratiano's puppets won't be so quick to sniff around Transylania for a while.

          Originally posted by Vendrin View Post
          Do you feel the constant change of pc characters adds or detracts from the game?
          Honestly, I don't have strong opinions on the matter one way or the other. It...just is what it is. Having both played TC and run TC, I have unique insight on it. For good or ill, Giovanni Chronicles this ain't.

          On one hand the chronicle's 900 years long and I'm running a hell of a lot more than the published stories, since the published stories are barely a skeleton to build an actual chronicle upon. Players are potentially going to get bored playing the one character, feel their story has concluded, a character's own personal story moves them away from the scope of the chronicle itself for various reasons, or that character's going to succumb to torpor due to age. A consistent rotation of PC's is just something to be expected in a chronicle of this sheer scale.

          I believe it's in book three there's an entire sidebar about this. At that point the PC's are elders, and expected to be slipping into torpor, or becoming too hoary and risk-averse to be chancing their hides gallivanting around Europe in various unceasing states of unrest or outright warfare. If they're not there by the end of book three, they're definitely there by the beginning of book four when the spectre of methuselah's thirst raises its head. The sad reality is, by that point any original characters still standing are borderline unplayable, and players are all but expected to have moved onto others by then.

          It's fun to play a game balance-wrecking sledgehammer that's centuries old, likely has all their clan disciplines at 6 or 7 and a monstrous smattering of out-of-clan disciplines to complement them, and accordingly huge dice pools for anything that might matter. But...not so fun when your blue book is "I sleep or hide in my haven for a century, my childer begrudgingly dig me out of it to do this story, and I go right back to it when the story's over", and your character has fuck-all in dice pools that do matter in modern nights, like Drive, Technology, Science, Computers, Firearms, and the like.

          One the other hand, the chronicle's 900 years long...you know the drill. It gets a hell of a lot harder to integrate new characters into the storyline as the chronicle goes on, whereas it's easier for players to transition to characters that are already intricately tied into the chronicle as it develops, or have strong hooks to bring them in later. That's where the idea of PC's developing legacies, embracing childer, developing alliances, or finding proteges to expand upon and continue their work come in. A new character that already has stake in the story is easier to integrate than a completely random befanged fucko.

          It does suck to lose a PC one has been potentially playing for years, to Final Death, torpor, or their role in the story simply having reached its conclusion. But, as much as that can suck, it's not necessarily about any individual PC as it is the legacy the characters leave as a whole.

          For instance, I can already tell the next original PC likely to go is the Lasombra. That character's near the point of "practically unkillable save plot device" elder critical mass -- he's only a dot or two of Auspex from it. The PC just in the previous game session got Tenebrous Form, and has enough Fortitude to resist the fire/sunlight damage that could kill him long enough to get away. But the bottom line is, the character's antitribu and his blue book is going to only get more bogged down finding ways to escape the fangs of his own clan unless he just goes Sabbat out of nowhere.

          I played a Tremere on the fucking Red List, and let me tell you -- that "I spend my blue book finding new and creative ways to not die" shit gets old. Especially when your PC's so powerful they single-handedly trivialize any forthcoming encounter, up to the ST just sending a fourth-gen methuselah your way for no express purpose other than to kill your PC.

          I straight up gave him the option for his PC to go apostate like his sire, but his answer was "not yet". And really, the subtext there is the character's personal story is on a timer, and going Baali is the escape hatch, plot-wise. And really, I caught that he enjoyed the Tzimisce just enough he's already considering retiring the Lasombra and moving forward with her instead.

              And...a brief postscript, since I've already announced the next story to the players and written up my master blue book. Years after I started the chronicle with a prologue, in which the characters met and left on crusade to seek their fortune...I finally was able to call the most recent story (the one this report is about) the epilogue to book one.

              The big plotline to Transylvania Chronicles -- Kupala, Saulot, and the Jyhad -- aside, it really found its feet and momentum with Operation Fuck Bulscu. That one ill-fated power play dominated practically everything the PC's experienced this entire time: having their own petty schemes wrenched from their hands by an ambitious Ventrue, the ultimate failure of those schemes leading to their unwitting subjugation to Vykos for a century, finally taking their destinies back into their own hands only for relations with the Ventrue to sour, having their destinies ripped away from them by the direct intervention of an Antediluvian, and finally facing the consequences of it all at the hands of the very elder whose downfall they'd unwittingly caused.

              It's only fitting closure for Operation Fuck Bulscu concludes book one. If I ended it here, I'd call it one hell of a chronicle. It's kind of bittersweet in its own right, to run something with that degree of continuity, cohesiveness, and self-containment, and say at the end of it, "that was a hell of a thing".

              But I'm not done. For their minor -- and not so minor -- roles, the PC's set in motion far, far greater threads of Jyhad. They built the geomantic web that binds Kupala. They caused the downfall of one luminary Ventrue elder, and two others rose in his place. They've released two active Antediluvians into the wild, even as they believe two have fallen and a third not long for the world. They've -- inadvertently and unwillingly -- aided Vykos' ascent to the apex of its might in the medieval age.

              They're at the cusp of elderhood at a period when they're too old to be truly trusted by Anarchs, yet too young and battered to claim power in the nascent Camarilla. And...just as the chronicle began, they're now at the forefront of the next great crusade of the age. And they owe boons to the Hungarian Ventrue warlord who now, due to the PC's actions, fancies himself the tip of Christendom's spear.

              Because in 1442, an army of Turks invade Transylvania. When the sun falls, boons owed as a formality come due, and Transylvanian Cainites' steel will at long last cross Turkish Cainites'.

              And as history dictates, the Turks will be pushed back by an army led by Janos Hunyadi. As a result of these battles, the Voivode of Wallachia, Vlad Dracul, will be accused of treachery by the Turks and captured. Vlad Dracul will be held captive for a year and restored to power, but his two youngest sons, Radu and Vlad Dracula, will be held hostage by the Turks as a guarantor of his loyalty...

              ​Time for book two.
