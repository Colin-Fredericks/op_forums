        Originally posted by Herr Meister View Post
        But at least in this case, if I'm not mistaken, it was before there was a difference in power between generations, right? So that serves as a "good excuse".[/FONT]
        Maybe, but that's actually a fairly recent theory. There's no guarantee that this was the case and the books usually seem to assume that there was no such a time and the second and third generations did had a reasonable gap of power between them. What is stated in the Book of Nod IIRC is that the 3rd Generation was the last to have god-like powers, which means less that they had the same level of power and more that those three Generations would see the 4th as Thin Bloods.

        In general the tale of the murder of the 2nd Generation is regarded as a cautionary tale for the dangers of the higher Gen when they get in numbers, and despite the dubious results this theme is repeated during the destruction of Antes in the Dark Ages and even the destruction of Ventrue and Brujah in the Classic times. Those stories don't need to be true to be taken seriously by the characters, so they definitely perceive this as possible. Add to this more low-profile Diableries and political or military victories and the setting is full of lore examples of low gens falling to newer and higher gen vampires.

        This is something the mechanics have a poor time explaining, they simply do not model such outcomes, yet they're clearly meant to be a thing in the setting. This is not a big problem, it is normal that rules have trouble modeling some things. But it does imply that rules shouldn't be taken too literally when analyzing or even defining lore for your game. They're not the characters, they're a representation of the characters, and can fail at that on some aspects.

        Both their survival and their demise are legends. Their very existence is a legend. A Nefandi master may decide to hide for a few centuries to gather enough power, and one day simply decide to make a show in an expectacular display of destructive abandom. Or a Baali vampire could do that too.
                                                This is exactly the reason why I prefer to make such things costly. Of course there's also the natural consequence of creating a chain reaction, so to speak, but still I've always felt that many highly destructive powers are rather way too cheap in WoD. I always have to think about "which reason besides narrative could explain why this all-powerful NPC isn't going to simply destroy the PCs from afar?" and I think adding in costs is also interesting. I like the idea of characters who "spent" a great deal of their powers through the ages and, as a consequence, are not as powerful as they used to be. It kind of reminds me of LOTR or Silmarillion, as well.
