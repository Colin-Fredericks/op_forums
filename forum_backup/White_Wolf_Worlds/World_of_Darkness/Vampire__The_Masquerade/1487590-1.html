                    Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                    So we had that thread talking about our favorite vampires and I figured I'd type out some thoughts I have on Lazarus. He's always been my favorite vampire in the game. He's had a cool history and done a lot of pretty big things. He's had a fairly outsized impact on the setting compared to many other 4th generation vampires. And, looking at what 5th Edition is doing with him and the Hecata, it feels like they're pushing him to become a pretty central figure in the ongoing metaplot. Note that I'm by no means a big 5E fan but I am very interested in where they're planning to take Lazarus.

                    So first off, Lazarus has two stated goals. His first is to become a god. Not God like Cappadocius, he's okay with merely becoming "a" god. Maybe he always had that goal in mind, or maybe he came up with the idea while studying with the Setites. He spent a lot of time with them, he learned Serpentis and their magic. Though note that it doesn't seem that he moved onto any of the Setite paths nor does it appear he worships Set. He's definitely not a Setite, but there probably aren't many non-Setites who are more knowledgeable about them than he is. At least he likely buys into the idea that Set is a divine ascended vampire and he probably wants to do something similar.

                    Lazarus' other goal is to recreate the Spell of Life designed by Isis and Osiris in their creation of mummies. He must have learned about that and come up with this goal during his time in Egypt. And he may be much, much closer to this goal than most people would think.

                    According to the books, the Capuchin is a persona used by three individuals who work closely together. Lazarus, Mahatma (another one of Cappadocius' childer) and the wraith of Japheth (who was another one of Cappadocius' childer). So basically two 4th generation Cappadocians and the wraith of a third 4th Generation Cappadician.

                    The Capuchin "found" the Harbringers in the Underworld. The Harbringers were the Cappadocians that had been trapped in the Underworld during the Feast of Folly. Now importantly in 5E Cults of the Blood Gods it's revealed that the Harbringers aren't actually vampires, or at least they aren't regular vampires. Rather they're wraiths inhabiting vampiric bodies. Seemingly, they're somewhat closer to Risen or even Mummies or Wan Kuei. Note that we don't have any hard rules, so the suggestion is to treat them like vampires, just with higher resistance against Dominate but lower resistance against Oblivion rituals that can command wraiths. What happens when you kill one? Does the wraith die and cease to exist? Or does it simply go back to the Underworld to wait until its body is recreated or it can inhabit a new one? If so, they really would resemble mummies (or even Wan Kuei) quite a bit because that's how those guys work.

                    My theory is that, rather than "rescuing" the Harbringers, Lazarus used his own version of the Spell of Life (or more accurately a Spell of Unlife) to return the wraiths trapped in Kaymakli to unlife in the bodies of vampires.

                    The other fact revealed in Cults of the Blood Gods is that the Harbringers have their own hidden agenda. They plan to murder all of the other vampires descended from Cappadocius - the surviving Cappadocians, the Giovanni, the Samedi and, oh yeah also probably the Nagaraja (poor bastards picked the wrong clan to join). As Lazarus is directly responsible for the existence of the Harbringers, it's likely that he approves and supports their goal to slaughter their clan mates.

                    What's driving the Harbringers? Is simply anger and hatred? Or maybe it's due to Lazarus' Spell of Unlife. My theory is that this Spell can only restore existence to the wraith of a vampire using the remains of a vampire from the same clan and same Generation. They can't kill Brujah or Gangrel and use their bodies to free the trapped Cappadocian wraiths, so the only way for the Harbringers to return the thousands and thousands of trapped vampire-wraiths back into the world is through the killing of thousands and thousands of vampires who are members of the Clan of Death. For the Harbingers and Lazarus, the Family Reunion was simply a way to get all of their victims, which were previously scattered across the world, into one place.

                    Another note:
                    At one point in the past the Capuchin approached Augustus Giovanni with information on the whereabouts of the True Vessel (which held Cappadocius' blood and part of his soul and was necessary for Augustus to fully ascend to the Third Generation). The Capuchin claimed that it was located in the library of Ericiyes, but the library was sadly destroyed by Augustus' son Claudius when the latter burned the temple. Augustus killed his son for that failure and then handed his remains over to the Capuchin. But this seems too easy though, too neat and too tidy. Remember that Lazarus shares the Capuchin's identity with Japheth's wraith, and Japheth is the one who created the True Vessel in the first place. So it's very likely that Lazarus did know where the True Vessel was. But why would Lazarus tell Augustus the location? Why wouldn't he take it for himself? The True Vessel is unique, one-of-a-kind magical artifact of immeasurable power. Surely Lazarus would have laid claim to it.

                    And if Lazarus has the True Vessel, and if he's diablarized Augustus, then it's plausible that he's able to perform the ascension to Third Generation that Augustus never was. Maybe Lazarus is already a 3rd Generation in full. Maybe that explains why the Hecata are immune to the Beckoning. Or perhaps Lazarus has other goals, ones that could use the power of the True Vessal and Giovanni's blood to exceed even the power of the Third Generation. He has expressed interest in godhood, so who knows.

                    Also, maybe let's look a bit closer at the Beckoning because I think the fact that the Hecata do not suffer from the Beckoning is kind of an important detail. There are many possible reasons for this as the book notes. Maybe Lazarus is already an Antediluvian. Maybe the Beckoning can only be used a clan a single time and then that's it. And since Cappadocius used it for the Feast of Folly it'll never work on his bloodline again. Maybe the ritual the bloodlines used to become made them immune to it.

                    Or maybe, let's consider another possibility. And note that I'm going way out into Conspiracy Theory territory on this one, but bare with me. What if the Hecata are immune to the Beckoning because Lazarus is the one who is behind the Beckoning? What if he was able to use the True Vessel to trigger the Beckoning? The Beckoning causes many older vampires to go off and fight and die, so we have a lot of old dead vampires from various clans. And Lazarus' Spell of Unlife can only be used to resurrect a vampire-wraith by using the remains of a vampire from the same clan and Generation. So what's the point? Does Lazarus actually know a bunch of wraiths of ancient vampires from various clans that are trapped in the Underworld and would like to return to Earth?

                    Oh yeah, actually he does. He totally does! Lazarus, as the Capuchin, had fairly extensive dealings with the Tal'mahe'Ra. A group of very ancient elder vampires from pretty much every clan of vampires. Their city in the Underworld was destroyed and all of those vampires died in the Underworld. Many of them could have ended up becoming wraiths (the nuclear explosion of the city could have been retconned or exaggerated).

                    If Lazarus was attempting to use his Spell of Unlife to restore those guys, then he'd need the bodies of a lot of other very old vampires from various clans. And the Beckoning basically provides that. It would also explain why his own clan isn't affected by it - because Lazarus is behind the Beckoning. That could also explain why vampires who leave and engage in the Gehenna War return different and changed - they're different and changed because now they're being possessed by ancient wraiths from the Tal'mahe'Ra.

                    So if Lazarus really does have a Spell of Unlife, and if he is now a Third Generation vampire, and if he is using the Beckoning to create an army of ancient wraith-vampires under his control and maybe he's on his way to becoming a god on top of all of that... well shit, sounds like he might as well just declare victory in the Jyhad because what chance does anyone else have?

                    Is this all too gonzo for 5th Edition? Oh hell yes. There's no way that's what's actually going on. But there's a lot of pieces there and regardless, it was fun smooshing them together to turn my favorite vampire into the secret puppetmaster who's on the verge of winning Jyhad.

                    Whatever the case though, it's clear that between the weird Harbringer wraith vampires who are planning to murder the other Hecata, and Lazarus possibly possessing the True Vessel and having plausibly diablarized Uncle Auggie, the authors likely have something pretty big things planned for him. I'm looking forward to seeing what that might be.

                    I really liked your take on Lazarus, there are many interesting ideas in your post. He's also one of my favorite characters in the WoD and, even though I hated 90% of what they did in V5, if they do something interesting with him, I'll certainly enjoy reading. I'm just hoping V5 won't ruin him as they did with more than half the setting. Also, the new bloodline seems much more interesting than "Italian mafia" to me.
