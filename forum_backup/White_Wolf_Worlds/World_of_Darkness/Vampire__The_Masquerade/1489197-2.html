    Clan-dominated cities would be a natural development simply because people wouldn't travel far and wide just to meet others and build diverse communities on purpose, carefully planning things. And as interesting as Prestation is, the main resources vampires need aren't provided by other vampires, they're provided by mortals. The whole notion that commerce between vampires is fundamental to their existence is preposterous.

    Without outside interference most cities would have evolved from small Domains with a Prince and their Childer, most vampires leaving their sire's territory wouldn't go far, so most areas would be dominated by a given clan. Newcomers from other clans would have to abide to the rule of the dominant monsters and to whatever organization they have for themselves.

    Societies naturally form around ethnicity. Clan is ethnicity. You are what your sire made you to be, nothing else. Context dictates how cities are formed and the people living there learn to adapt instead of magically summoning the perfect set of inhabitants. In this sense (and I'm not agreeing with everything else Thoth says on this matter) he's completely right: the setting encourages multi-clan cities as a thing that doesn't really make much sense from the perspective of simulating natural-like migration patterns, social contact and contract, or the actual needs of the race. It is a Faction-based distribution that is purely a narrative device.

    Like most things about the game, really, because while RL isn't rainbows and shiny unicorns, the level of unavoidable shitty-ness in the setting is just as artificial a narrative device. Or, you know, having blood-feeding magical corpses walking around. I'm not saying that you can't take a few fictional premises and run them at face value to their logical conclusion, you definitely can. But WoD never intended to be such a setting, and trying to tie it to natural rules and say that the setting as-is is the most logical from the perspective of Humanities is both absurd and completely misses the point.

                                                            When you look at the Dark Ages and earlier settings, you can see that this was sort of a thing, especially with High Clan vampires, which often claimed control over specific geographical territories or areas. Ventrue tended to congregate in Italy and then moved up to Germany after the Roman Empire fell, the Lasombra congregated in the Iberian Peninsula and northern Africa, the Tzimsce congregated in Eastern Europe, the Brujah in northern Africa until Carthage was destroyed, etc. The Cappadocians were the only High Clan that wasn't really tied to a specific geographical range, though they did have a lot of influence in Turkey.

                                                            While not "mono-clans" many of those places ended up as majority-clans where the majority of vampires in that area were from a specific clan, and the rulers of most of the cities in that territory were members of that clan. And if vampires from a different clan attempted to overthrow a leader or take a city, they would often end up drawing the ire of other clanmates and leaders of nearby territories. Those clans controlled most of everything and other vampires were essentially guests in that territory. If clan Ventrue decided they didn't want you in Germany, you probably would not be sticking around for too long.

                                                            These days with the Camarilla, clans aren't really tied to geography the way they used to be, and cities and domains are far more equal than they were in the past. And the point of the Camarilla is that it's the explanation for why modern Kindred society is so much more accepting, diverse and equal than it used to be, because it makes post-Camarilla vampire society more interesting in terms of drama and story fodder than pre-Camarilla vampire society.
                                                            Part of that is also that the meaning of a "clan" has changed over time. In the earliest days, a clan meant a family composed of an Antedeluvian, its children and grandchildren. As generations spread and the ancients spent more and more time sleeping, "clan" gradually changed to mean "race". You're no longer a member of the family of Arikel, you're a member of the Toreador race.
