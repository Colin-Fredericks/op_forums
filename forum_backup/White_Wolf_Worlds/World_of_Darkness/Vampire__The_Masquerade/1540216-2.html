    Animalism(V20). Gets slept on and most only take 2 levels for the convenience.

    lvl1 is a toolset. But you have to establish rules/guidelines/expectations with the ST so neither parties get disappointed. So many uses but you kinda need to study what animals can do and how they navigate their environments. Most dogs can scent track well and even tell different blood types, if someone has cancer, or if someone has mated recently. Ravens/Crows recognize individual people and can communicate that info. They can also solve logic puzzles. Rats can chew through about anything given time. there are tons of easy uses a neophyte can use to get boons its silly.

    Sabbat? Hey Tzimisce guy, craft this ordinance into this dog will ya?

    Combat is less efficient but it can be a good assassination tool vs ghouls/regular people. They dont hold up vs most vamps but they deal Lethal.

    Lvl2 is generally seen as 'i need a snack and im outside the city'. True but think how this lets DA vamps travel without needing juicebags in tow. That enemy general on horseback during that night attack? Call him, the horse, over. Summon other messenger animals that other Animalism vamps use - heres your DA interwebs.

    lvl3 is seen as 'crappy Dominate' but thats weak. Great for DA when language barriers are a thing. great for disabling enemy/frienemy ghouls. That love interest of that Toreador isnt so feisty anymore and gets tossed aside. Be a shame if you scooped them up. Ghouls that dont perform well usually dont survive well either. Gang/Mobsters. Chief Accountants, etc. Remember it takes a while for their WP to regenerate.

    lvl4 Others have called this a 'weaker Protean4' but they cant read. lvl4 allows so much it gets stupid once you think about it. Its a launching platform for other disciplines, infiltration, or daytime raids. daytime ops usually needs lots of WP and preferably a few Merits but it can work. racoons are very useful.

    lvl5 can ruin plots. Its that powerful. Risky but powerful.

        Originally posted by InspectorG View Post
        Animalism(V20). Gets slept on and most only take 2 levels for the convenience...
        Breaking down pre-V5 Animalism by-level, its panoply of powers, and the potential it represents to characters in turn on an individual level pretty much highlights the issues with the discipline, who gets it, and how/why it gets slept on for the most part. All the relevant utility is frontloaded in levels 1-2. Most characters just won't have use cases for higher levels, those use cases come up too rarely to justify the purchase, and/or those levels don't fit within players' plans or the opportunity cost for buying them is just too high (it's experience that could be spent on attributes, skills, or other disciplines with greater utility). The "DA versus modern nights" dichotomy only exacerbates that.

        Level 4 is the best (worst?) example of that in play. Possessing animals is great, but to get the most out of it the character needs a host of social or mental disciplines clans with Animalism tend to not have in-clan...and those who do, have preferable alternatives in-clan (and those alternatives often exist within the boundaries of those disciplines themselves). With the right merits, no place accessible to an animal isn't also accessible to an obfuscated Nosferatu, astral projection is straightforwardly more useful for a Tzimisce, and Gangrel get Protean for crying out loud. Ravnos are the only clan for which animal possession is a premium, simply because they have no other alternatives to do what it does (which coincidentally means their effective use of it is limited).

        Animalism 5 is amazing, but getting the most of it means taking the "short-tempered"/"being a Brujah with ECD" synergy -- that in itself is a major expenditure with a potentially negative risk-reward analysis. Side note, that's one of the minor reasons I've always thought Brujah and Gangrel both would be more interesting if they swapped Animalism and Presence.

        Animalism is great, but past level 2 the question stops being "is this discipline good?" and becomes "are other disciplines better for what I'm trying to do?". All of this boils down to one thing: designers never seemed able to figure out what they wanted Animalism to be, and left it a bipolar split between control over animals and influence over the Beast (and mortals' animal instinct) where both aspects are partially explored without creating a singular thematic whole.

        That's why I think V5 Animalism represents what's great about V5 mechanics. Players have the agency to explore which thematic side of Animalism they wish, and to a degree of specificity and utility past editions lacked the mechanics to provide.
