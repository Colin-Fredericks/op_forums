    Originally posted by DarkKing View Post
    Okay, I might need a bit of clarification on this subject, because it seems like I have misinterpreted what exactly the metaplot is supposed to be?
    A metaplot is simply a narrative that extends through multiple gaming supplements that aren't designated as being part of a specific ongoing story (so the Transylvania Chronicles don't count as having a metaplot as it's one story explicitly split up over a few books). Metaplot can be internal to the gamelines, or include multiple, or all of them. Most of the WoD metaplot is internal to the gamelines, though the end of 2e and through Revised tended to lean more into the "unify the setting" stuff to setup major changes from 2e to Revised, and then to help facilitate the ToJ; that is, it wasn't one concerted effort but an accumulation of different design goals moving the same direction.

                But I get where you are coming from because as much as I love the ambiguity and open options I am a bit annoyed that Keith Baker refuses to give any concrete information what's caused the Mourning in Eberron.

                So that is the issue with metaplot, RPGs are not novels. When you are reading Lord of the Rings you are following the story that the writer put his merry gang of hobbits, humans and assorted miscreants in. But in RPGs you are creating your own stories and you're using as much or as little of what the writers conceived of the world. And hence metaplot is a "damned if you do, damned if you don't" kinda thing. If you make the metaplot too rigid some people will be annoyed that it's hamstringing their play (kinda what happened with WoD5 what with blowing up Sabbat, making them a bunch of homicidal hobos or making a nazi cult out of Get of Fenris). On the other hand if you make the story to vague and open-ended you risk of angering players like you, that prefer to have the answers spelled out. Ultimately the vagueness is understandable when you consider one of Rein-Hagen's answers about the concept for Caine and VtM as a whole

                "Actually, Steward Wieck had a comic book where Caine was a Vampire and I asked him if I could borrow it... and since he was my business partner, he said hell yes. That was the big turning point actually. Until then I was trying to shy away from religion. After that... I went all in. The game and the world became about religion and belief.

                My father was a Lutheran minister, and I think that played a huge role in not only Vampire but the whole WoD series. I was always fascinated by what made people believe so strongly when I didn't seem to believe at all. Talking about that theme, the power of belief, fueled the second half of Vampire game design."

                To my mind this is the big reason why WoD5 rubs a lot of people wrong way because they have tried to mix up the approach of oWoD (heavy ambiguous metaplot) with nWoD (metaplot agnostic) and ultimately they made both groups of fans unhappy. YMMV of course.

                                                No, that's not what the metaplot does.

                                                        Originally posted by DarkKing View Post

                                                        I still don't like it. Given my neurological condition, I don't like when things are left up to interpretation, or surrounded by nuance.                                                         I do love the World of Darkness franchise, don't get me wrong. But the metaplot was a mistake, and getting rid of it was the right call.
                                                        I understand this frustration, and it is valid.

                                                        That said, let's unpack some things:

                                                        1. Let's judge a work on its proposal. There are things we don't like because they're bad at what they try, and there are things we don't like because we personally don't enjoy what they try, but they're meant for people who do.

                                                        This distinction matters.

                                                        2. You're mistaking ambiguity as obfuscation with ambiguity as goal. The unreliable narrator in VtM isn't there to make finding the truth hard and frustrating for people like us, they're there to say the ST decides how much they're correct.

                                                        The author isn't waiting that you understand the answer, they're giving you an open question to answer as you see fit, exactly like CofD does, if not the same style of delivery.

                                                        3. The game did spelled it out, several times. Not as clearly, not nearly clear enough, but did. There are several reasons why it is easy to miss, but when you know what to pay attention to, it's all over the place.

                                                        4. The Metaplot was... A thing. An artifact of its time and place, and a mixed bag. A lot of things could have been done better. Some are a result of their literary purpose. Some are the reason why the game went so far.

                                                        You're right in the end for their execution, and other gamelines show that many authors agree and learned from it. V5 is also there to show that some of the things you find awful are the actual selling point for others.

                                                        5. At the end of the day we're discussing old books and lore. Whatever we think of them won't change the past. That's relevant for analysis: you can get rid of ambiguity at your table or headcanon, but not at lore discussion.

                                                        Instead, give it time. Learn the ins and outs of its nuance. As something past and done, VtM from the 90s is now a fixed thing, so as big and hard as the problem is, you can take it with time.

                                                        This will help exercise the understanding of nuance and stuff within your condition, which is satisfying in the long run and useful. It will not make you normal, but you'll understand why people do certain things better than them by acquiring as knowledge and analytical training the abilities they take for granted.

                                                        VtM is what it is, what we can do is appreciate it's reality even if not what we would have preferred.
