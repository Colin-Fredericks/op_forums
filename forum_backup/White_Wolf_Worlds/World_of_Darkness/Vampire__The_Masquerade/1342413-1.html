                        Originally posted by Penelope View Post
                        I always thought the Camarilla’s official position was that the Antediluvians were all destroyed during the Great Flood. I mean, if they never existed, then where did the clans come from?
                        It's worth keeping in mind that the term "Antediluvian" carries a lot of in-setting baggage; ancient, all powerful blood gods who engage in a massive millennia long war with one another and who will rise up to devour their progeny. Between things like the Book of Nod, stories of Enoch and Caine, the Sabbat, etc, there are certain concepts that tend to be intrinsic to the Antediluvian and I believe those concepts are more what the Camarilla denies.

                        When the Camarilla says that, "the Antediluvians don't exist" I believe it's more that the Antediluvians you hear about as ancient blood gods who are going to destroy the world don't exist. The Clans have founders of course, but those founders were just like, vampires, you know? Powerful ones sure, but not legendary or mythical ones and definitely not vampires that pose an existential threat to the world. They're mostly dead and even if they're not and one of them woke up, they'd be more confused about the modern world than any kind of danger to vampires as a whole.

                        It's also worth remembering that members of the Inner Council did meet Antediluvians such as Cappadocius. And keep in mind what happened to him. He was basically a bumblefuck who got himself diablarized and his Clan ended up in the dustbin of history. The End. Omniscient, all powerful blood god he certainly was not. If other Clan Founders were as comically incompetent as he was (and considering most of them are widely believed to be dead or diablarized it wouldn't exactly be wrong to think that about them) then there's really no reason to be so freaked out over them waking up.

                        The awakening of Ravnos obviously changes that up quite a bit because suddenly Antediluvian as ancient, (nearly) all powerful blood god who rises up to devour their progeny turns out to be true, or at least close enough that it's worth reassessing the ideas about Clan founders.

                                                A few points.

                                                First, as Reasor said, the Camarilla weren't just a sect in first edition, they were THE vampire government/society. Any given vampire was assumed to be Camarilla by default, or else a rebellious Anarch (which in first edition was synonymous with "Neonate"). Later editions would pivot to saying that the Camarilla declares all Kindred to be under their purview; that's their party line. The Sabbat in those early days were a mysterious, probably satanic doomsday cult that operated as scattered cells (packs). It's only later that they were expanded, and deepened, and organized, and broken up into dozens of factions and political stances and hierarchies.

                                                Point being, it's not so strange the Gangrel were made Camarilla. What the Camarilla even WAS took a while to solidify, and was subject to drift.

                                                Second, and related to the first, because the Sabbat was considered a doomsday cult, it's assumed that their entire ethos - Noddism included - is mysterious to most non-Sabbat Kindred. WE, as players/storytellers, know all about the mytho-history of VtM. The people living IN IT, meanwhile, cannot be assumed to. Vampires operate under an all-encompassing veil of secrecy, both to conceal themselves from mortals AND to keep a given vampire group's operations and secrets hidden from other vampires. Even what Kindred think they "know" should be shrouded in multiple layers of disinformation, in-group biases against out-groups, and a healthy amount of cultural assumptions informed by over a century of mass media depictions of vampires.

                                                (As an aside, I wish the "Kindred Lore" ability were more prominent as a tool for determining, mechanically, how much a character knows about their splat.)

                                                Third, the Camarilla's "public" stance is less "the Antedeluvians don't exist", and more "the Antedeluvians are not RELEVANT". The Ivory Tower maintains its power by every other vampire in their domains bending the knee to the ones in charge. They have a vested interest in suppressing any knowledge of Noddism. Both because it's the theology espoused by the Sabbat, who are the Camarilla's greatest enemies, and because if no one knows who founded the Clans and what they were about, the Camarilla can make up whatever "history" is most convenient. Why do you think Archons and Alastors spend so much time hunting down Noddist "heresies"? Young vampires who hear about Noddist teachings - who start to believe in horrific blood gods from antiquity - will stop listening to their elders in the Camarilla, and start listening to the Sabbat recruiters who preach about an end-of-nights to come.

                                                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                                            I feel like this assumes the Camarilla isn't the kind of people who wouldn't bald face lie to the majority of vampiredom about water being wet.

                                                            They're awful people and don't care about the truth.

                                                            Why should they?
                                                            Oh, I think they absolutely would give a bald face lie to vampires, if they had something to get out of it. In this case, I'm not sure they would?

                                                            I mean, what would they have to get out of saying, "There are no antediluvians, there are no Clan founders, the Clans just sprung up, fully formed like Athena sprung forth from the mind of Zeus," rather than, "Yeah, there were presumably Clan founders. They weren't blood gods, they weren't mythical antediluvians, honestly, they weren't shit. They were just vampires, quite a few of them have been killed and the rest are gone or asleep. If any show up, they'll also get killed, so why worry about them?"

                                                            Of course, my interpretation is a bit colored by the various Clanbooks. Those books tend to be written from an in-character perspective, and represent a sort of generic "here's how things work" primer that would be a reasonable example for player characters have received. And when it comes to the Camarilla clans, those also mention Clan founders and antediluvians.

                                                            So it's always seemed to me that in-setting, if the Camarilla's argument was that there are no Clan founders, nobody believes that for a second which would leave the Camarilla just looking like obvious dummies for trying to insist that the Clans don't have founders. Which is why I tend to believe they don't try to push that and instead just push the idea that Clan founders weren't particularly powerful, they weren't boogymen and that if any of them do still exist, they're no longer relevant, so who cares?
