        Ones I use in my games (Or used, rather, it's been a few years since I've run a Vampire game, but this was what I settled on for a slightly alternate head canon, one involving blood potency instead of generation, no second generation and no one really knowing what came before the Clan founders, and everyone being part of the Camarilla except the hardcore cultists in the Sabbat. And I may have forgotten one or two.)

        Strains (ie, offshoots within the Clans)

        Brujah - None known, though there is the perpetual rumor of a group who've managed to harness and internalize their rage into an icy cold lack of emotion. If they did exist, it'd be more of an alteration to the clan weakness than anything else.

        Gangrel - Maybe the kings of strains. One of the biggest variations is how both Protean and the clan weakness tends to adapt itself the local region and its wildlife, especially regarding the animal forms.
        • Shaman - Possessing an affinity for Auspex instead of Fortitude; Part of their weakness includes taking on behavioral taboos.
        • Urban - An uncommon variation which first appeared in some of the larger ancient and medieval cities. They have Celerity or Obfuscate in place of Fortitude (we could never decide which fit best), and their weakness focuses on urban animals such as alley cats, rodents, and the like.
        • Mariners - Those tied to the seas. Their Protean has minor variations, and their weakness tends to result in things that bear a resemblance to Deep Ones, shark men, kappa, and darker versions of the mermaid legend.
        Malkavians - They still have the classic Auspex, Dominate, and Obfuscate discipline affinities, with the Dementation powers being alloyed devotions of the three above. There aren't many Strains, so to speak, aside from certain "family" lines who share similar Derangements.

        Nosferatu - Many of their variations are based around common mutations, deformities, and living conditions, but they don't usually have any changes to disciplines or weakness. One example is the Jotun, a lineage mainly found in Scandinavia and neighboring regions who all tend to have the Huge Size merit offset with things like blunt teach, misshapen limbs, and other flaws.

        Toreador - None that anyone is aware of.

        Tremere - Because of their origins, the clan has never really encouraged the development of Strains. Much of their variations tend to come in internal factions who focus on specific areas of Thaumaturgy. But there is one exception.
        • Wardens - A holdover from the Dark Ages and the early clan's need for soldiers and guardians, they have an affinity for Fortitude in place of Dominate. Their study of Thaumaturgy tends to focus on warding or combat effective paths.
        Ventrue - None known, a fact those Ventrue who study such things tend to take pride in. The closest they have to Strains are family lineages who tend to share similar feeding needs.

        Assamites - Unusual in that they are divided almost equally into three groups usually referred to as Castes. All three have an affinity for Auspex, Celerity, and Quietus.
        • Philosophers - The main face of the clan has most Western Kindred have known them since the post-Roman period. Scholars, politicos, artists, scientists, engineers, and other people of letters. Their weakness is a fixation on their scholarly areas of interests.
        • Judges - Considered the more martially oriented side of the clan, they focus on law, judgment, faith, honor, and responsibility. Their weakness is in their incredibly stubbornness and obsession with rules.
        • Magi - The facet of the clan most concerned with the mystical, metaphysical, and arcane, made up of shaman, exorcists, occultists, demon hunters, and other oddities. Their weakness is a focus on occult matters often to the point of distraction from every day affairs. They can, optionally, swap out their affinity for for one of the clan disciplines for that of Assamite Sorcery.
        [Yes, this is a clan focused on being truth seekers rather than assassins, and who are a part of regular Kindred society even in Europe and the West rather than some sort of "exotic other."]

        Giovanni - No Strains. The only real variation is the various internal families and their preferred focuses, but all of them have the standard clan discipline affinities and weakness.

        Ravnos - While there are a number of Jati (or Households as some younger Western Kindred call them) around the globe, there is only one major Strain.
        • Seers - who replace Fortitude with Auspex, and are prone to unwanted visions.
        Setites - While a rather diverse number of factions exist within the clan, these are all based around interpretations of the clan's origins, what sort of myth-lore they draw inspiration from, and how they implement those beliefs. None of them deviate from the clan's discipline affinities or weakness, though some are able to develop an affinity for Setite blood magic when their blood becomes potent enough. There is a single exception.
        • Warriors - With an affinity for Potence in place of Obfuscate. Masters or war, they include everything from revolutionaries and holy warriors to chaotic terrorists and cult leaders.
        Lasombra - None known.

        Tzimisce - Rivaling the Gangrel for their diversity.
        • The Families or the Brood - Those embraced from the clan's carefully cultivated thrall families, retaining that family's discipline affinities and weakness in addition to the clan's weakness.
        • The Koldun or the Strigori - Witches who practice the clan's own version of blood magic, usually developing an affinity for it in place of Vicissitude.
        • The Dracul - A group who eschew affinity for Vicissitude in favor of Dominate, and often keep much closer ties to mortals than others of their clan.

            Bloodlines (those "lesser" lineages separate from the 13 Clans; they fall into three broad groups)

            The Fallen - The remains of those clans usurped by the Tremere and the Giovanni, having withered over the centuries since their down fall, with members scattered around the globe.
            • Salubri - One of the High Clans until the end of the Dark Ages and the uprisings. Once of group of mystics, healers, seers, and exorcists, they had an affinity for Auspex, Fortitude, and their own power of Valeren, and only able to feed on those who don't resist. Survivors include various closeted clan impersonators hidden among the Camarilla, isolated cult leaders, Middle Eastern Sufi mystics, Asian demon hunters, and African soul eaters.
            • Cappadocian - Another of the High Clans, destroyed from within during the Renaissance. Devotees to understanding the mysteries of death in all its forms, they had an affinity for Auspex, Fortitude, and Mortis, and a deathly pallor that was difficult to disguise. Since the fall and purge by the Giovanni, surviving groups have grown isolated from each other and sometimes diverge quite a bit. The Sons of Samedi, found mainly in the Caribbean, Brazil, and parts of North America, are primarily voodoo bokkur and mercenaries. Other groups exist in Africa, the Middle East, and India.
            [In a perfect world, a sourcebook about the Fallen would've had a full chapter on each of the above, and then an follow up section discussing creating your own Fallen clan as a bloodline, with the True Brujah - with a better name - as the created example.]

            The Created - Groups created artificially by other vampires, usually through forbidden blood magics and other questionable (and sometimes unreliable) methods. There are two main ones.
            • The Gargoyles - Created by Tremere experiments to create the perfect warrior-servants, they rebelled during the uprisings at the end of the Dark Ages. For the most part, they look human, save when their Blood Potency reaches higher levels and they aren't using their powers. They have an affinity for Potence, Fortitude, and their own version of Protean which allows them to merge with stone, grow wings, and turn to stone. Their skin is cold and hard to humans, and they have difficulty resisting mental domination.
            • The Kiasyd - The product of bizarre experimentations by a Lasombra during the late Dark Ages, involving excursions into Abyss mysticism, the Unseelie Courts, and the Underworld. The end result was something that could best be described as alien and arcane. They universally tend to be tall (but not excessively so) and thin, with skin that is pale even by Kindred standards. They also skew heavily toward intellectual pursuits, some of which make little sense to outsiders. Most prefer their privacy, the better to carry our their studies or experimentations, but small groups will occasionally gather for esoteric purposes. They have an affinity for the disciplines of Auspex, Obfuscate, and Obtenebration, with they are able to combine into alloyed devotions that mimic most of the Mytherceria powers. They take aggravated damage from Iron.
            The Mysteries - Bloodlines whose origins are completely unknown, and whose existence only raises questions most Kindred probably don't want to know the answers to. The two most prominent, at least in North America are as follows.
            • The Daughters of Cacophony - An all female lineage who've only appeared in the past 150 or so years. They tend to bring chaos and upheaval in their wake, and many believe they are a harbinger of the Final Nights. They have an affinity for the disciplines of Fortitude, Presence, and their own unique power of Melpominee, which requires vocal-oriented Performance to use (not limited to singing). Each member is haunted by music only she can hear, which often inevitably drives her to destructive frenzy.
            • The Tlacique - Originating in Mesoamerica and claiming lineage from none of the 13 Clans. They hold that they are dependents of the blood gods of ancient Mexico and Central America. Their existence points to the strong possibility of Kindred unique to the Americas, totally separate from the 13 known survivors of the Deluge. They have an affinity for Potence, Presence, and Protean (they usually turn into jaguars and vultures rather than wolves and bats). They require extra blood to rise each night.
            Anyway, those were the ones we settled on a decade or so ago.

                            I confess I was being tongue-in-cheek above. To me, both ghoul and revenant make perfect sense as Kindred slang terms (and that's what they are -- in-character slang, just like all the other terms in the lexicon sections common to all White Wolf game lines). The terms ghoul and revenant even use the same naming logic in the World of Darkness.

                            Why are vampire thralls called ghouls? Because folkloric ghouls are eaters of the dead. What must mortals do to become vampire thralls? They eat the blood of the dead. It's perfectly sensible that Kindred would come up with a term for their thralls that emphasizes the role of vitae, the only thing they truly care about.

                            Why are hereditary ghouls called revenants? Because the word doesn't just mean "vengeful spirits risen from the grave." That is one of the connotations of the term, but revenant is simply the present participle of the French revenir, and can mean any person or thing returning (and not just from beyond the grave). What is the one thing that is unique to hereditary ghouls in the World of Darkness? They are the only creatures, living or dead, who produce vampiric vitae naturally. It's the blood that keeps returning, and again the slang term emphasizes this fact.

                            I don't think Steven C. Brown and Andrew Greenberg were nearly as bad as was suggested above when it came to choosing names and terms for the material they developed. On the contrary, I find that often their additions to early Vampire are more in line with the logic and thinking behind Rein-Hagen's original naming conventions than the work of later authors is. For example, I have thoughts about the Nagaraja that are not kind to the V20 era writers, but maybe that's a topic for another time.
