                Christopher Houghton is one of the least interesting aspects of Los Angeles by Night. There's no reason for him to be such a powerful generation like 5, and that makes his 4th generation sire, Sir Matthew Lubbock, a cipher. (Sir Matthew is actually called LUDLOW in the text, but called Lubbock on the character sheet as Christopher's sire). (Lubbock is an English name. Ludlow is a Welsh name.) All other methusalehs tend to be ancient vampires with mythological names, meaning they either were those figures of legend, inspired them, or were mistaken for them. Instead all we know is that he woke up from torpor in the late 17th century. So we know Sir Matthew has been around since at least the late 1300s. So is he incredibly ancient, having gone through extended series of torpors, and only uses Sir Matthew Lubbock as an alias to fit in? Or is that his real name? If so, it's highly unlikely the Toreador Antedeluvian sired him - he would have diablerized his way to 4th generation.

                Lubbock is referred to as "one of the Toreador hostages in London". This is in reference to lore first mentioned in the original World of Darkness where the 1693 Treaty of Durham that ended the conflict between Ventrue and Toreador in the British Isles. The Toreador would be safe in their traditional stronghold of Edinburgh, and in return they would not seek power in London outside Elysium or support the Tremere. Any Toreador in London now held the status of "hostages". Presumably if the Toreador reneged, Mithras would have killed all the London Toreador. As such, I cannot think Sir Matthew must be all that powerful. He's clearly weak enough that Mithras felt he could destroy him and all other Toreador in London. We also know that when Bonnie Prince Charlie did land in Scotland and rebelled, that Mithras did eventually destroy some London Toreador in reprisal and blood bond others to him. So when Sir Matthew left afterwards for Boston, it must have been in response to the failure. It was his way of avoiding being destroyed or blood bond to Mithras.

                As such, I believe he must have diablerized his way to 4th generation, and is likely far less powerful than Mithras. Possibly during the time of the Anarch Revolt. He seems influential enough in Toreador salons that he must have some status, so it is likely he's an elder. He might have been an early Anarch Toreador that defected to the Camarilla, or he diablerized some 4th generation Anarch/Sabbat. But I think that period of time is when he must have done it. Still that begs the question of what 4th generation vampire could he have diablerized?

                The basic story of Lubbock and Houghton is OK as long as you don't make them 4th and 5th generation. If they were say 6th and 7th, it'd work much better IMO. The only reason for Christopher to be 5th is that the writers seemed incapable of not having some powerfully potent blooded vampire secretly responsible for everything that has happened. The setting suffered for essentially including a joke comment about the status of Hollywood movies as "art". I think he'd just be as effective in that role as someone around say Tyler's, Khalid's, or Annabelle's power level in Chicago. No need to have such potent blood.

                However one may change the details of their generation, I don't think there's a problem to include Sir Matthew and Christopher to be in Boston between 1746-1830 or so. If neither sired any children, it's just a curiosity. So the best thing in my opinion is to have at least one local Toreador descended from one of them. Again, if so better for them to be of less potent generation. We might also want to tie Sir Matthew and Christopher to other Toreador refugees from London arriving in Boston. In any event, the arrival of these Toreador just prior to the American Revolution makes them natural allies of Brujah like Jeremy MacNeil and others who overthrew the original Ventrue princes here. So some nice things can happen by including these characters. I'd personally just make their generation less potent.

                    That seems a sensible idea - one must admit that the mental image of Sir Matthew (By whatever surname) being more or less Lazlo Cravendale played with an emphasis on the ‘horror’ in ‘horror-comedy’ (and possibly even putting on a deeply suspect Scots accent to boot) visited me at some

                    - Concerning Christopher Houghton himself, a review of LA BY NIGHT on another forum suggested that he might well be a parody of the tendency to depict some shadowy ancient of potent bites being behind everything and I really like the idea: LA BY NIGHT sounds quite gleefully OTT to start with, so making the local shadowy mastermind exactly the sort of Cainite who would cultivate a city where katanas & trench coats are not optional and mayhem a nightly facet of existence (and an affectionate parody of ‘A methuselah did it!’ plotting to boot) works for me.

                    I should probably admit that making ‘Hollywood Dracula’ the LA Kindred’s answer to ‘Emperor Norton’ of San Francisco wins that sourcebook a warm place in my heart, whatever it’s actual quality: this is 100% more entertaining than the ACTUAL World of Darkness Dracula (and probably 75-88% of other Draculas).


                    - I tend to lean more towards ‘Matthew Lubbock/Ludlow’ (Ludlow is, if memory serves, specifically a castle in the Marches of Wales) an alias employed by a Methselah; this makes his inclusion as one of the Toreador hostages in London more interesting, since it suggests that he’s something of a ‘Ringer’: a vampire potent enough (or thought to be potent enough) that Mithras himself might actually think before ordering the execution of his hostages (or at least enough of a Tough Nut that Mithras would lose a few teeth biting down).

                    It also occurs to me that having Sir Matthew quit London following the ‘45 as much because he couldn’t be bothered to risk his neck for the sake of clan-mates foolish enough to get caught up in something as quixotic as Jacobite pretensions might be darkly amusing (Especially if this comes as the anticlimax to Mithras & Sir Matthew spending a century or so genteelly trying to puzzle out which of them is more murderously potent without ever quite coming to blows).


                    - On a more serious note, it’s interesting to wonder if Sir Matthew played some role in Roman Pendragon’s failed putsch in Victorian Britain: it’s easy to imagine the former embracing the opportunity to simultaneously put some distance between his Idiot Fail Childe’s Ultimate Failure AND pay off some grudges against Mithras by making mischief in Edinburgh (Which, going by the White Wolf wiki’s list of Princes seems to have been contested between rival princes at some point during the Victorian era), for the sake of making it easier to overthrow the Ventrue supremacy in the Isles that Mithras et al had so proudly established.


                    - As for the possibility of there being a childe or grand-childe of Sir Matthew in New England, it’s easy to imagine Christopher’s Grand Project being an effort to win his sire’s support for Christopher’s appeal to finally be allowed to embrace a child’s of his own (Though it’s not impossible Christopher might have made life more difficult for everyone by illegally embracing a childe and then trying to get his sire to pass the new Kindred off as his own).

                    Doubtless any local descendant would STILL hide his face in shame at the mere mention of Christopher’s complete failure as a Toreador (I am, of course, convinced that neither Christopher or his Sire would be taking very much attention in the fairer sex, even when everything else about a possible Childe is open to speculation).

                                        As it happens I was also drawn to the ‘Chantry War’ between the backers of Madeline Coventry and ‘Baladin’, whom the Tremere sent to replace her (With the Salem chantry backing Coventry and Boston accepting Baladin’s authority: I wonder if Providence would have done it’s best to keep clear or would instead have sought to play each side against the other?).

                                        Actually, there’s more than a few unanswered questions hanging over this incident from the Dark Colony’s history that leave me wondering if the reason for Coventry being ousted (or at least given her marching orders) was because the Tremere leadership (Fairly or unfairly) suspected that Madeline Coventry was a bit too close to the Sabbat, after spending so long in the wilds, a long way off from the pyramid.

                                        My understanding of the timeline is:-

                                        AD 1635 - Madeline Coventry sets out from the Winchester chantry in Old England at the order of her elders, seeking to evade Mithras after he resumes his old feud against their Clan

                                        Notably she is supposed to have been the first Kindred in Boston (and to have been ousted, along with the Brujah, not long thereafter as the Ventrue sweep in - though the exact period of time is unspecified, one might assume that the Ventrue victory in the English Civil War had something to do with it)


                                        AD 1692 - Coventry establishes her chantry in the wake of the Witch Hunts (Apparently embracing mortal mages of some sort, to the point where she is described as having several childer: Mention of something called the ‘Ur Codex’ playing a key role in their rituals)


                                        AD 1783 - Ventrue ousted in New England, Coventry having allied with the Brujah and others to ensure that this would be the case, after which point Sabbat (Operating from what is now Maine) launched their own efforts to exploit the disruptions of the Revolutionary War to fill a perceived leadership vacuum; it is at this time that the Tremere Reestablish themselves in Boston and create their chantry (Even as the Sabbat attack Camarilla holdings in the Boston area)


                                        1783-1795 - At some point in this period Sprenger, a local (It’s not specified in my source whether ‘local’ means ‘New England’ or ‘Salem’) Sabbat leader wages what appears to be a personal, though unspecified Grudge against Madeline Coventry; it is possible she diablerises him at some point during this period

                                        Given that the ‘Chantry War’ is said to have lasted almost half a century, despite Baladin and Coventry being known to have contested for the supremacy only between AD 1795 and some point before 1831, I wonder if the divide between Salem and Boston may have begun at some point during this period, possibly due to objections to Coventry’s conduct of operations during the conflict.

                                        N.B. I’m assuming that Coventry split her time between Salem and Boston during this period, which may or may not have helped.


                                        AD 1795 - One Baladin, a Tremere Elder, is sent out by the Winchester chantry to replace her as leader of the Clan in New England; feeling betrayed, Coventry engages Baladin in a spectacular duel, but is wounded and flees to Salem, where she enters torpor.

                                        Despite Baladin’s best efforts he cannot locate her and when Coventry arises again, she “built her own chantry” and a coven loyal to her, entering into a long conflict with Baladin and Boston.

                                        Interestingly, the exact phrasing of the original suggests that either the Salem chantry had been abandoned by this point (and Coventry rebuilt it) or that Madeline Coventry, under the very noses of the existing Salem chantry, built her own establishment.

                                        It’s also unclear as to how long her torpor lasted.


                                        AD 1831 - At some point prior to this Baladin has been found dead in the Boston chantry, presumed dead through the efforts of Madeline or the Black Hand (Known enemies of his); in this year Madeline Coventry disappears and is also presumed dead.

                                        Shortly afterwards the Sabbat launch a raid on the Salem Chantry: rumours that the Inner Circle of the Camarilla become involved after this point abound, but in any case the Chantry War is over.


                                        Black Fox, I hope you can see why all this set my antennae a-twitching: the timing of Coventry’s disappearance and the ensuing raid on the Salem chantry is ‘interesting’ and the Tremere elders deciding to replace her after their woman in New England has played a key role in restoring their fortunes in the region (After a century or so in the wilderness) is also curious, especially after her apparent destruction of a Sabbat leader of real significance.

                                        In all honesty, I’m wonder if somebody in the late 1700s assumed that Madeline Coventry accused her of being hand in glove with the Sabbat and the Tremere elders of Winchester (An ocean away, it must be said) decided there was enough substance to the allegations for a replacement to be sent out, which may or may not have ultimately led to Coventry actually joining the Sabbat (Willingly or unwillingly), with her disappearance and the ensuing raid being used to cover her tracks.

                                        Either that or her defeat of Baladin might have left her exposed to observing Sabbat with grudges lingering from the days after the Revolutionary War … or elements within the Camarilla itself might have decided to get rid of her on the quiet, possibly to make sure there would be no purges by the victors of this bitter struggle.


                                        In any case, there are quite a lot of question marks hanging over this conflict and the characters involved: I’m certainly going to offer my character sketches for Baladin and Sorenger at some point, since very little is known about either (Heck, we don’t even know much about who Madeline Coventry WAS, as opposed to what she did, so that’s another blank space that may need to be filled in).

                                        More on this later (and my apologies if I haven’t explained my point as clearly as I would like to).

                                            The Tremere infighting is definitely underdeveloped. It's one of those things you think is going to play a large part in the regional setting, but then is just forgotten about. So it's minor local flavor. STs could definitely take it as a plot hook and develop it on their own, but one would think it'd be something the writers of Dark Colony should have provided more details on.

                                            I believe Dark Colony said Coventry's torpor lasted "more than a year" which implies it was between 1-2 years.

                                            I also have some questions about the Tremere during this time.

                                            First, when exactly did Councillor Meerlinda take over responsibility for North America and move there? The first Tremere clanbook is very vague. It says that as Puritan colonists arrived in New England in the 17th century, Meerlinda was responsible for her role of leading the Tremere in the British Isles. Then over 100 years as the potential of the New World grew, the Tremere would establish the rank of Pontifex and the geographic spheres of the Tremere Council would change with Meerlinda being given North America, and that she succeeded growing the clan there more than her counterpart Xavier de Cincao did with South America.

                                            The absence of any mention of Meerlinda in Dark Colony implies she went elsewhere - perhaps New York or Philadelphia before eventually moving on? But New England seems to have been very important to the Tremere, so it is somewhat surprising she decided not to go there. Though if we assume prior to the Revolution that New England was a stronghold of Mithras aligned Ventrue, it would make sense Meerlinda would have wanted to go elsewhere to set up shop given the problems the Tremere had with Mithras in Britain. Or maybe Meerlinda did not pack up and leave until after the 1831 feud ended and the Tremere had already lost much power in New England. I'm leaning towards that second option.

                                            Incidentally in her character description at the back of the clanbook, it's mentioned that Meerlinda's Tremere have had several failures in New England including the destruction of the Boston Chantry several years ago (as of 1994). While this could possibly have referred to the 1831 feud resulting in Boston chantry Tremere fleeing to Hartford (along with the survivors of the Salem Chantry), a 150 year old even is unlikely to be described as "several years ago". But Dark Colony only mentions a Boston chantry post-1831 in the context of Zachariah Slane wanting to rebuild it, but denied by John Reiss.

                                            I suspect despite Dark Colony coming out before the Tremere Clanbook, that the clanbook was not really written trying to incorporate the lore of the other, and there's just a lot of cracks if trying to view them as a whole.

                                            Second, when did House Goratrix of the Tremere anti-tribu arrive in North America? The first Tremere clanbook says it was only in the mid-18th century that it was discovered that Goratrix joined the Sabbat. When Goratrix actually joined is more of a mystery. But certainly there could have been secret Tremere anti-tribu in New England very early posing as Camarilla loyal Tremere. That might be one explanation behind the "Chantry War." It's possible some members of Coventry's chantry were secretly of House Goratrix and that this was finally revealed after the rite was done to reveal the Tremere anti-tribu. Coventry might have tried to hide that and pass it off as apprentices infighting. Maybe Meerlinda got suspicious, sent Baladin to take charge, and asked Coventry to return to Britain. Coventry, fearing she'd be killed as a traitor, then tried to kill Baladin and remain in North America.

                                                    There are excellent points - and it’s interesting that Melinda Coventry and Meerlinda of Wessex have such similar names (At least as I understand their pronunciation, ‘MEH-lin-da’ and ‘Mere-Linda’ respectively): could ‘Melinda Coventry’ have been an alias adopted to throw possible rivals off the scent of a much older vampire? (Probably as a decoy, rather than a nom-de-plume).

                                                    - A related thought: could Meerlinda of Wessex have originally headed for Mexico, rather than for New England, when she was sent to North America? (After all, in the early nights of European settlement Spanish North America would have seemed the richer prospect by far and the Camarilla - or at least elements within same - might well have wanted to take steps to prevent Sabbat domination, however unsuccessfully).


                                                    If Meerlinda were not already in-country at that point, then showing her arrive in North America circa AD 1831 would make a great deal of sense: in this version of things the death of Baladin and the disappearance of Melinda Coventry would represent the new Head Wizard quietly cleaning up an old and embarrassing mess, while putting the pieces into place for a triumphant arrival in the United States (Free from any potential for such unpleasantness as greeted Baladin on his arrival).

                                                    This take on things would work especially well if Melinda Coventry were herself a childe of Meerlinda sent to keep an eye on the American colonies of her sire’s troublesome British assignment: Coventry’s success in ousting the Ventrue might therefore prove a dangerous success under the circumstances, requiring her sire to send out a replacement as a sop to the overmighty British Ventrue (Nursing a grudge over the American Revolution aggravated by the much more directly dangerous French Revolution that was it’s direct sequel).

                                                    Melinda Coventry’s sense of betrayal under these circumstances would therefore be all the more acute - especially if she had some reason to dislike or distrust Baladin, or at least regard those Tremere willing to support him with particular suspicion.

                                                    Honestly, a lot depends on the exact personalities involved, so we’d have to work out the key players involved before we could say more in detail: I do like the idea that Meerlinda only arrived in the United States after AD 1831: it would have probably taken that long for Kindred elders to be convinced that the United States was destined to be not only Big, but enduring (and by that point Mithras would have been absent from London on one of his sojourns for almost thirty years, allowing Meerlinda much more freedom to relocate than would have been the case if a Big, Angry Methuselah were watching the Tremere like an angry buzzard).


                                                    One scenario that I think might work especially well would be if Melinda and Baladin were both from the line of Meerlinda, probably as rough contemporaries: My assumption is that Melinda Coventry would be an ancilla at the time of her passage to the New World (Probably a fairly young one whom Meerlinda herself could depend on to handle independent operations, but not so valuable her services were indispensable) suggesting she had been a vampire between 50-100 years (I would suggest placing her at the younger end of that range, young enough to understand, and possibly have family connections to, the Pilgrim Fathers), probably embraced in the mid-to-late 1500s.

                                                    If Baladin were an elder in 1795, he’d probably have been Embraced no later than the mid-1500s: it’s possible he may have been her broodmate, but an even more interesting possibility is that he was her sire and she was his first childe - their dynamic becomes even more interesting and their rivalry more painful if there’s a blood connection.

                                                    Even if they were broodmates, rather than Sire and Childe, the idea that he was her ‘handler’ (Keeping her updated on European affairs, pleading her case to the Elders and receiving her information from North America) is an angle that I think would help make the selection of Baladin as her replacement institutionally-logical and personally-painful.

                                                    I’ll try to post my character sketches for Melinda Coventry, Baladin and Sprenger tomorrow: my only addition for tonight is to suggest that Sprenger (With that Germanic name) might work best as an antitribu from a Clan with reasons to hold a grudge against the Tremere (Gangrel or Nosferatu?) possibly even an individual with personal connections to the Omen War, though not necessarily personal experience of that era.
