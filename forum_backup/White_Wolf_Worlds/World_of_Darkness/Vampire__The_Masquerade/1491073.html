    V20 - When should an NPC be allowed to resist Presence?

    08-22-2022, 10:59 PM
    Hi there. After a hiatus of maybe 15 or 20 years, it looks like I'll be getting involved with a V:tM game in the future. I'll probably end up running a WoD game myself at as well. Someone's got their hands on the V20 rules, so it looks like that's what we're using. A big question has come up regarding the Presence Discipline however. It's the fact that a character MAY choose to spend a Willpower to just ignore it.

    So before I explain my difficulty, let me just acknowledge a few points. First, as a Storyteller, I could just declare Presence doesn't work on any given NPC. I could simply decide I don't want a particular NPC to be effected and he wouldn't be. Let's assume that's NOT something I want to do. I want rules based system about when someone will try and resist. (In an ideal world, there's even some relevant section in a rulebook to refer to. I'm not holding out for that, but if there's something relevant, that would be nice.)

    OK. That out of the way, the issue is that it seems really easy to resist Presence. If our NPC has about 2 or 3 Willpower, then half the time he resists Presence when the PC roll successfully. At even 5 or 6 Willpower, and we're getting to the point where Presence is very unlikely to have any effect even in the cases where the Vampire succeeds in using it. So much so, that a Storyteller making a choice as to whether the NPC attempts to resist feel tantamount to the Storyteller deciding whether Presence works. Which is what we're trying to avoid here.


    Example 1: PC uses Dread Gaze
    Why wouldn't virtually any NPC attempt to resist this? Is this not a must-use-Willpower situation for almost anyone?

    Example 2: PC uses Entrancement.
    Why would anyone attempt to resist? Fully half of the sentences in the 2-Paragraph description explain that the subject is a willing servant. They literally use the word willing 3 times. (Unless their trying to resist falling into that state. The question, of course again becomes: Who the heck wouldn't try to resist that?)


    I really don't want to fall back on whether I, as a Storyteller, prefer the NPC to be effected. After all, the Player is taking the action (and spending resources) specifically because his desired outcome for the scene is that the NPC actually is effected. I also don't want to fall back on something like whether the NPC is being asked to do something they wouldn't normally do. Players will be using Presence largely because they want other people to do something they wouldn't normally do, so that's probably close to 100% of the time. And they already generally won't do suicidal or overly dangerous things by rule.

    Finally, I want Players Characters to have a consistent feel for when their Discipline would work or not. Rules-based systems work best for this.


    So what rule for determining this do you think is closest to the Rules As Intended for V20? And if you have a rule for this that is shared by the Storyteller and Players in your group, what is it?

          Just use RAW, but consider some quick points:
          • Presence is mostly meant for groups. If a PC wants a targeted effect, they should use Dominate;
          • Willpower have other uses. NPCs shouldn't always have a full reserve and resisting will deplete it;
          • They may decide not to resist because either they don't notice it or want to save WP;
          • Resisting isn't just spending WP, they must also succeed a WP dif 8 roll, even if not contested;
          • Usually you don't use Presence on your bitter enemy, but on your common target;

              I like the idea about presence being a subtle discipline, so I use these caveats:
              • My players know that using Presence (like Domination, etc...) against a kindred is a declaration of hostility, if identified by the target (or someone of his allies)
              • I checked the power level of the target and the effect: is the leader of a faction? is someone to have experience a lot of times Presence 1-2-3 in his unlife? is a player success going to distrupt the narrative? -> if so i allow the expenditure of the Wp and the roll to fight Presence. Otherwise Presence is never resisted.
              • If I haven't a clear answer (or, like in your case, I want a strict rulewise approach) I check a table I prepared (so it's a home rule guideline) for recognise a Discipline (so I have a common test for NPC and PC to use in this case)
                • IF the NPC/PC has the exact power, he know is being targeted and he will recognise it.
                • IF the NPC/PC has a lesser power or is studying under someone that has the same power, he has a decrease CD (usually -2) to be aware of the discipline and recognise it.
                • So a contested roll: the discipline user rolls DEX/MAN/INT + Subterfuge (8) and the target rolls PER + Awareness (8).
                  • IF nothing between DEX/MAN/INT is meaningful, i use the Total Discipline Level (because for a master of Presence should be easier to hide lesser powers than for a neonate).
                  • Partial success: the target is aware that something is being used
                  • Complete success: the target know the source of the power
                • To recognise the power: INT + Occult (LV+3). The DC can be increase for rare disciplines (combination disciplines, bloodlines, alternative forms...)

              Sometimes I think it's possible to roll the Willpower not at the insurgence of the Presence power but instead during some meaningful act. This is an example I saved from a thread somewhere in this forum (but I don't remember the source) to help me visualize my goal in this process:
              Yes, someone with Presence, or Clan Lore from a Clan with Presence, or Kindred Lore, might be able to instantly know the subtle changes in things that indicate Presence is being used and use Willpower... But beyond that, Presence 3 is like real love. Sometimes the person asks too much, demands too much, gives too little or treats you too poorly and, in the end, even though you love them and it hurts to do it, you have to put your foot down and say "No". It's the same with Presence.
              Let's take an example:

              A Toreador uses Entrancement on you and you fawn over them and start agreeing with them. Then, one day, they enact their terrible plan, using you to get at your sire. They convince you, Presence or otherwise, that your sire needs to be taken down a peg... Maybe they even shed a few bloody crocodile tears, telling you all the horrible things your sire "did" to them. You help them and, before long, with you betraying him, your sire is on his knees!

              The Toreador, high on vengeance, knows the most painful sting of all will be for you to betray your sire in the ultimate way: "Kill him!" she says "And drain his heart's blood! Then we will be of the same strength of vitae and our partnership will be all the stronger!"

              Struck, suddenly, by how awful she truly is, asking you not only to kill your sire, something you might have even considered for a moment, but to drain his immortal soul, you finally muster up the strength of character to stand up to her and put an end to her plotting and scheming.

              "No!" you say. "This is too far!", spending a Willpower.

              So, yeah, knowing it's specifically Presence is one way, but the user of Presence not being subtle and careful and starting to make demands of you that you wouldn't agree to, even for someone you love, is another. We know that abusive relationships often stop and start over and over, with one partner leaving the other, only to go back to them later... Spending a Willpower only lasts so long, and in the end, those people give into that feeling again and come running back...

              ... Don't worry, this time it will be different...

                      By NPCs, are you talking about just vampire NPCs, or mortals and other supernaturals? I don't have these separate NPCs react the same.

                      A big complaint for my players is when they feel their PC isn't able to defeat mortals as it doesn't seem the PC is able to do anything. Especially when they are trying to not turn the scene into combat and using Presence to avoid it, but it fails and then leads to an unwanted physical confrontation. So for mortals, unless the mortal is aware or "special", I generally don't have mortals roll to resist. Presence just works. If the mortal NPC suspects or knows that the PC is a vampire or some other kind of being, then they can resist. Someone hunting a vampire knows what to expect, but the unsuspecting victim they are saving them from is not. "Special" mortals are either significant NPCs in the setting who I want to be able to resist for story purposes (despite being mortals, I intend for them to be significant NPCs in the setting at some level), or a less important mortal who is "special" only because of the dramatic needs of a specific scene (example: the PC is still pretending to be alive, and uses Presence against a friend or family member who is beginning to suspect something is wrong).

                      Vampire NPCs almost always get a chance to resist because they should be able to recognize Presence. But there may be some vampires who are still ignorant. Fledglings and even some Neonates may still be in a position like mortals, but only because of their inexperience. If Presence is used subtly so that an NPC doesn't suspect it is being used, I may not resist especially if it is a good scene. But if it is used against an NPC to clearly generate a different emotional response than should happen, the NPC can almost always resist.

                      In areas where you are unsure of whether the NPC should resist or not, you may want to their actual Willpower rating as a guide. Roll a single D10. If it is their Permanent Willpower rating or lower, they'll try to resist. If it is higher than their Permanent Willpower, they do not try to resist. Yeah, it's a die roll for the ST, but it is a quick resolution for those times. Since regaining Willpower is not impossible, but isn't easy, it is a limited resource. An NPC should want to preserve it. You don't want to be without it when it is really needed. And while an NPC is only "alive" in a story when the PCs come around, as characters they have to worry about a lot more than just the PCs.

                          I particularly don't like the idea of tying the decision on resisting to the power level of the NPC.

                          This is an emotional power, and there is a reason why it is so simple and relatively easy to overcome.

                          Drama should be the main rule of thumb, and the target as a person the next one. Is the use preposterous? Resist. Was the user careful enough to make it seems logical? Probably don't resist. Will the scene be obviously more interesting in one way or another? Go with it.

                          More importantly, supernatural characters with even a little experience do not need to identify the specific power. Nothing in Presence says you can only resist if you know precisely what you're going through. Most supernaturals are at least aware that strange fucked up things exist and emotion alteration isn't unexpected. Noticing that your emotions aren't what they should be by context is probably a fair litmus test for anyone. And nothing in Presence says that the emotions caused by the Discipline have any particular tinge to them that should alert those who knows it more than others.

                          Also, Presence controls emotions, not behavior or thoughts, something people forget frequently. The story with the Presence user bringing the victim to the point of invading their Sire's haven and subduing them is preposterous. Unless it is a very specific chain of events, Presence won't force even a single decision on your mind. It gives you an emotion, some leanings, but that's it. Your mind is still your own. You're free to make your own decisions. And deciding to go against your emotions isn't really anything alien to most people more than 5 years old.

                          Presence is not a power to guide others to do what you want. It is not just AoE Dominate. It isn't weaker or stronger, it is another thing entirely.

                                      A good option might be to restrict temporary willpower for NPCs. For player characters managing willpower across sessions is important since it comes back slowly. NPCs generally don't have to worry about this since they just have to manage resources for a single scene.

                                      Some options...
                                      • NPCs don't have temporary willpower they can spend (some exceptions for powers that use willpower would need to be made)
                                      • NPCs start with 0 temporary willpower in a scene, but can gain it from fulfilling archetypes or other sources, meaning they'll have a very small pool so resisting Presence or spending it for other uses is a real choice.
                                      • NPCs start a scene with a variable amount of willpower, not their full pool, perhaps set by a dice (roll Willpower diff 5, each success is a point of willpower), or set by their prominence in the story (supernatural beings start with 1, important npcs get another, etc...).
                                      • The Storypath system Onyx Path made uses a concept in some games called Tension, which is a pool the storytelling can spend for NPCs that isn't specific to any one NPC, it's a resource the storyteller manages that is refilled by player actions. You could adapt the concept so it becomes a choice you need to make as to if depleting the pool to resist Presence is worth it, so it is a narrative decision and not a NPC decision.

                                                  Originally posted by MrDrika View Post
                                                  Well, personality weights too, beyond WP. Soldier, child, are easier to compel. Director, commander, may be harder to impossible. But above all else, the chronicle is the final judge. Maybe, to keep the flow a key NPC can't be influenced on that moment or to that end. Perks like iron mind, true faith, true love (depending if the influence may harm the object of affection), may break that too.
                                                  I think WP and the other virtues are there to represent personality... Leader-type character, harder to influence, should simply be statted with higher WP, while easily influenced characters will have a lower one. And in terms of social position, anyone in a position of authority should have high WP since that is, even among humans, what's rolled to resist social rolls and they probably had to spend WP points on their way to power... If they have low WP they're easily corruptible and likely didn't get to their position through their own efforts, but due to someone else wanting them there, perhaps precisely because they're easily influenced.

                                                  I think that in Revised one's Nature could raise or lower the difficulty of social influencing attempts by 1. I haven't seen that in VDA20, which I'm playing, and haven't checked V20. But as I said, the approach that convinces me is that WP is enough if given the narrative weight it should have (because I think traits should be understood as the measure of a narrative element, like personality, rather than just dots on a sheet).
                                                  Someone with a Soldier nature and high WP is highly motivated to stay true and carry out the orders given by those he respects: attempts to sway him from duty will be difficult precisely because his WP is high, and so the system of the resisted rolls will make it difficult for him to fail and be influenced. A Soldier with low WP is normally motivated to follow the orders of those he respects or the cause he has embraced, but he's easily swayed by difficulties (doesn't have many WP points to spend to push through difficult rolls) or social manipulation (because he rolls less dice when resisting with WP, has lower difficulty to be Dominated, etc.). Analogous reasonings can be extended to other Natures, as appropriate.

                                                  The system is always geared to give advantage to the "attacker" over the "defender", both in physical fights and social maneuvering: WP can't get specialisations and can't go beyond 10 except with True Faith - though it's easier to raise at character creation, so low gen vampires (with traits at 6+) focused on social influence tend to have more dice and less difficulty for their influencing rolls than anyone - even other low gen vampires - has to resist (if the resistance is based on WP or Road), but then what keeps the system in check is that there's always a legitimate fear of repercussions.

                                                  Overall, I think a ST should just choose how easy or difficult to influence they want a NPC to be using the table of difficulty ratings: absurd? 10. Challenging? 7. Easy? 4. That should be the PNG's WP rating. After that, let the rolls play out as written. Spending WP can be automatic if the victim is being pushed in an uncomfortable direction. Rolling a dice to decide how much WP points the NPC has left seems like a quick and simple system, or one can assume they have enough if it's just a 1 scene interaction and nothing relevant has happened to lower it, such as the players plotting to stress the NPC in advance by making things happen on the side, such as busting one of his operations even if they have never met before.
