            I can absolutely agree that "practical Embrace" versus "emotional Embrace" is a bit of a false dichotomy, and that many Embraces contain elements of both. I don't necessarily agree with your claim about Cleopatras being chosen because they're "scheming vindictive bitches who'll fit right in"; the closest I have ever seen to a statement supporting that is the idea that some Cleopatras are chosen because their Sire thinks they are leaning too much on their beauty and wasting their other talents.             I also don't think that discussing later developments is relevant to the question of why there were chosen for the Embrace; circumstances ending the emotional value and leaving only the pragmatic value do not change the decision-making process of an Embrace that happened in the past. Similarly, the ability of someone subjected to an emotional Embrace to survive does not nullify the fact that their Embrace was primarily emotional, it's just good fortune for the recipient.

            Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
            Now, how does this relate to Generational conflict?
            Because the crippling alienation brought about by the Generational conflict drives kindred to create emotional embraces. Its comparative to income disparity in the real world.
            A lot of crime and deviance isn't immediately practical. A lot of low-income kids at school behave poorly, not because they're stupid (though a good chunk of them are), but because they believe the system has failed them, the teachers are against them, the values of the system are thus wrong, and that to 'succeed' they need to work by their own values; toughness, comedic skills, promiscuity, an inordinate amount of attention on expensive branded clothing; these signify success. As the rest of society further alienates them, they lash out more. To the establishment, these people are failures, but to them and their peers, they are successful, because they've built up groups that celebrate what they see as success. A Vandal doesn't break stuff for money (usually) but to feel better. Similarly, as vampires find themselves marginalized by their elders or hated by their childer, they feel alienated, and as they feel alienated, they need to create support networks around them. The more horrific kindred society is, the more vampires are going to do seemingly irrational moves like sire childer for love. The more accepting vampire society becomes, the less vampires will lash out or need to build their own support networks. Desire for Generation really does an amazing job at keeping things messy, just like divides between rich and poor keep things messy in the real world. From a sociological point of view, Generation makes things wonderfully interesting, while blood potency really mitigates the Jyhad.

            This goes really well with explaining Ravnos/Sabbat actually....
            To go back to one of my favourite examples, Beckett sired a childer purely out of spite. He literally took someone with the skills to hunt him down and kill him, and then turned them into a super-powered immortal with a personal grudge against him, because she pissed him off. Once again, it has nothing to do with Generational conflict.

            And again, I think you are assuming that Generation is the primary cause of Kindred conflict when Havens and Vitae are equally good explanation, arguably better given your focus on economic metaphors. Generation is capital, but Haven and Vitae are food and shelter. People fighting purely over capital, with little to no interest in the basic necessities that can be secured by capital, are extremely rare. People who only care about food or shelter are often co-opted into the struggles of people fighting over capital because the people holding the capital hold a significant amount of power and influence, but that doesn't mean that capital is their primary concern. I would argue that Generation is similar.

            I'm not arguing against the idea that Generation has a significant impact on Kindred conflict. I am denying the position that it is the primary or defining motive/influence. And I think we have too many examples of Kindred who don't give a damn about climbing the Generation ladder through Diablerie for that argument to be dismissed without strong evidence to the contrary.

Again, Kindred-as-unchanging was a bit like the Caine myth in 1E; it was a core narrative, but not the only one.

                                                        I've never liked or used this idea of vampire emotions being a "pale echo of human emotions". It doesn't fit vampires, who are generally presented as operatic creatures in fiction. It sounds like it's rooted in a throwaway line in "Interview" about Louis feeling like he's gotten dead inside.

                                                        The thing is, Louis is an unreliable narrator, and the actual story heavily, heavily implies that he feels intense emotions, and that he is just misinterpreting his depression and apathy as a lack of emotion. Anne Ricepires are operatically emotional. And they are far from the only one. Wamphyri are specifically stated to have amplified emotions in the Necroscope book, and this is the root of why they are so dangerous: they overreact to everything. They exist in a state of perpetual melodrama acting like exponentially worse versions of angsty teens, on top of having an appetite for humans and horrific superpowers with witch to act out their urges and emotions.

                                                        It's a very common thing in fiction that vampires are melodramatically emotional, so the game having their emotions being pale shadows of human's is something I throw right in the garbage, and I don't think I've ever even played in a game where it is enforced or even suggested by the ST that you play unemotionally. The closest would be some of my Death and the Soul players, and even they tend to roleplay struggling with repressing their emotions in service to their path, rather than it being some default thing.
