    I really wish we'd find out Lucita's fate. Did Talley REALLY send her to the Final Death? I never liked they turned her into just another Sabbat stooge (and a child killer to boot) but I'm still invested in her storyline.

    1. Gehenna happens and its very likely the Sabbat ran right into the fangs of the Antediluvians.
    2. All of the Lasombra find out the Antediluvian is alive.
    3. The Amis Noctis' response is to join the Camarilla because, "Well, we failed to kill him. Now we should join the side that are the Antediluvian's pawns."
    4. The Sabbat effectively collapses into either remnants (The Seven Fires), is defeated by the Second Inquisition/Camarilla, or joins the rest of the Gehenna Crusade.

    I feel like this would make an awesome novel.

                                    Originally posted by SuperSabbatST View Post
                                    See I find a LOT of diversity in Revised Era Sabbat. I think Mexico City by Night did a great job fleshing out the fact that the Sabbat is really a sect of factions and each with a very different political agenda and what the sabbat means to each of them.                                     I'll definitely buy a copy. I admit, I am going to say that the Sabbat is not gone in my home games and I've flat out ignores some of V5's changes for them.

                                    How CT Phipps Runs the Sabbat in V5

                                    * The Sabbat is REDUCED but not destroyed or all off in the Gehenna Crusade.

                                    * The loss of Mexico City and Montreal as well as the fact they're the obvious targets for the Second Inquisition [with the help of the Camarilla] hit them hard. So did defections to the Anarchs, Church of Caine, and other Blood Cults.

                                    * Polonia won the Regency of the Sabbat and led the majority of the Sabbat's military forces through the Middle East but they're not the totality of the Sabbat.

                                    * Already a bunch of Low Generation Sabbat warped by the souls of the ancient Kindred they've fed upon are returning and forming their own cults as well as branches of the organization. These are a bunch of mysterious and unpleasant Kindred who are more or less rebuilding the "True Hand" (without the joke elements) within the Sabbat as Antediluvian worshiping Fifth Columnists.

                                    * Miami, Juarez/El Paso, Detroit, Kansas City, San Salvador, and a handful of other cities are "Fortress Cities" where the Sabbat rule and are independently maintained by Archbishops. Basically, look up "Most Violent Cities in the World" for which cities the Sabbat still control. The exceptions are Saint Paul and Baltimore.

                                    * The fortress cities give Polonia only the barest of acknowledgements and quite a few consider other parties to be Regents or themselves in the role. Polonia doesn't have the reach to dissuade these "traitor" Sabbat.

                                    * The Tremere antitribu are back, ruled by "Goratrix." Given he's an Antediluvian, this is incredibly hilarious. He's also why the Sabbat around him are utterly safe from Camarilla oppression. Oddly, he's allied with the Salubri Antitribu who have put aside their ancient feud.

                                    * The Tzimisce have made peace with the Old Clan and are acting as an independent clan for the most part. Members maintain Sabbat menbership and defend their packs but have largely decided the Sabbat is a failed experiment. Half of the Tzimisce remain commited and are usually the rulers of the most stable Fortress Cities.

                                    * Lucita is missing and is considered "The Real Regent" by many who hate Polonia.

                                    * The Ministry includes many Serpents of Light who helped them defect. This is looked at with suspicion by other Sabbat.

                                    * A lot of Sabbat have gone "undercover" in Camarilla or Anarch cities with the defecting Lasombra. They believe the Lasombra are playing the long game and that they'll rejoin the Sabbat or revolt when the Sabbat gets their shit straight. Whether this is true or not doesn't matter as they're allies with the new defectors. Quite a few find that the local Blood Cults and tolerated viciousness (as long as its hidden) isn't THAT different.

                                    * Devil worship is now tolerated in many domains where the Inquisition is weak. This pisses them off to no end. However, the Sabbat have plenty of people who don't see anything wrong with the aid of demons. The Path of the Devil has replaced the Path of Evil Revelations and is the fastest growing religion among Sabbat.

                                    * The Grimaldi have defected to the Giovanni and Pentex, working to destroy their former masters.

                                    * For all the shit the Sabbat have taken, the Gehenna Crusade has killed more Elders of the Camarilla that arrived via the Beckoning than the previous 600 years combined. They have also destroyed multiple 4th generation vampires and seriously impacted the post-Crusade Jyhad--if there is one.

                                    * Thin Bloods are officially considered vermin and to be "cured" by offering them diablerie. Some Sabbat believe their powers warrants membership, though.

                                                Originally posted by The Gentleman Gamer View Post
                                                Don't be mistaken: I don't run my V5 games incorporating every change made to this edition, and like all other fans, I'm waiting to see how it resolves in print. But the difference is, I don't equate my waiting with suffering or express that change is needed right now. Mechanical changes are, however (and in my opinion), good for a new edition, and the Brujah leaving the Camarilla has been a long time coming. As for the Camarilla becoming a more clandestine, elite organisation, I'm all for it.
                                                The Camarilla changing it's face is something that I think might become a very good thing. It might also... not really go where I'm hoping it will and just be a foil for the much cooler anarchs. A Monopoly is a powerful thing, and they gave it up; there best be a clever reason for that.

                                                As for Brujah; Disagree strongly. They're always fighting themselves, so they'd never let someone with a weird idea spoil it for the rest of them. There's no way they could possibly agree to losing their seat at the table. Many, many Brujah (and it is a big, big clan) would surely want to remain within the Camarilla, and they surely wouldn't the actions of one of their number screw the whole clan.

                                                The V5 stance on the political spectrum is not new.
                                                Stance and approach are two different things. This is... off topic, but I thought one of the golden rules of writing was to 'show, not tell'? I want a subversive masterpiece, not a tirade.

                                                It's not inconvenient if the players and ST discuss what they want ahead of the game. It's actually very easy. It's why there's a Kiasyd in my V5 game, a cleric of a god dead in the present Forgotten Realms in my Pathfinder game, a lady knight in my Pendragon game, and a character with hedge magic in my Call of Cthulhu game. Improvisation and adapting to one's gaming group is the mark of a decent Storyteller, just as the players investing in a new world can be an exciting experience for them, if they want to do it. I'd advise against demanding concessions from your Storyteller, because yes, you would indeed be that guy.


                                                Regardless of whether or not you like the table, you're not addressing the various factors I presented that make elders more powerful than fledglings. They are, and as Scott Steiner says: the numbers don't lie.
                                                The numbers suggest a methuselah would be in a bit of trouble against four neonates, and quite screwed against more. Of course, a competent Methuselah would never suffer such odds, but V5 has flattened the power curve quite a bit.


                                                Yep, it is a new move because very few people played thin-bloods in previous editions (which were introduced in third edition and changed in V20, so every edition has been different). As for whether thin-bloods can access normal Disciplines, they can, and there's a Merit for it. I recommend re-reading that section of the book again, too.
                                                If you wanted more people to play thinbloods, a thematic change was all that's neccessary. People weren't discoraged by thinbloods because they were weak, they were discouraged because you weren't really supposed to play them or hang with them for social or political reasons. Now that Anarchs seem to be the default faction or near enough, that should be enough to play them.
                                                Giving them an absurd drug cooking power that'll make a Tremere want to self immolate is not the answer to a 'problem' that infact a feature.

                                                Vampire was about ambition. Between the new generation rules and thin bloods getting unique toys, it seems the message is 'be happy about knowing your place'. Some people are just born stronger than you, and you should accept that, if you be like them you'll be a sinner and you'll be sick with your sin.


                                                Customisation is good. It makes characters stand apart from each other. I you want Potence to cover every physical action, I'd suggest you're looking for an overpowered Discipline.
                                                Potence was not considered overpowered in any edition before it. You should also look into the concept of overchoice: simple is good. New players should just chose the discipline that sounds good, not have to map a path to power or else screw themselves.



                                                It's funny you say that, because Talley, Sierra, and Malenkov in Chicago by Night are none of them "fools and whipping boys". I suggest examining the way you play them if you can't work out how to play an interesting Lasombra in V5.
                                                Storyteller characters don't suffer from the rigors a player character has to go through. I've seen a couple of characters that surely would not fly if a player made them.
                                                (reading the specifics on such characters would require me to buy the book)


                                                I'd advise against insulting a proportion of the fanbase by saying they didn't really think about the Lasombra weakness, and thereby weren't playing the clan properly. That's a bit gatekeepery, and I'd prefer people just play however they like. As for anguish regarding Oblivion, I must disagree as I'm happy to say I've seen and heard from a lot of people who like Oblivion now, when they disliked Obtenebration before. Different strokes for different folks.
                                                Obtenebration was too strong. No argument there. The clan also attracted and encouraged 'that guy' while now it doesn't do such.
                                                But oblivion...
                                                I suppose Lasombra can enjoy necromancy now. That stuff might be totally great if you are new or otherwise didn't care for the Lasombra identity. But shadow play? nah. It's less useful than that one path Tremere made to combat Lasombra, which was budget darkness.

                                                Tremere
                                                Look, I like playing a genuine OG wizard in my vampire games. The philosophy, the organization, the power you could in-character gloat about but not-really because it's expensive and not all that powerful if you don't take a few broken paths? Great stuff.

                                                V5, in this among other things, has cut off my options. Sure I can join House Carna now (I can even pretend I don't know it's a sham and even go along with the idea that you don't need discipline, training and a viable paradigm) but I can't fly or throw fire or become one with the machine or speak with the spirits or do some wicked-sick gardening or invest far too many experience points into creating a dope-ass magic sword (I might still be able to make the sword, but not to the extent of prior)

                                                My options as a Tremere are inexplicably gone because a few people have decided the so-far normal idea of a vampire sorcerer no longer fits the narrow vision of what they think vampire sorcerers should be doing. Apparently I can no longer be an Abyss mystic because it's gone from merely -sketchy- to -infernal- (and also the Abyss really lost it's interest). Oh and I'd struggle to ask to be a proper wizard again because it's maybe-balanced against everything else, XP is weird now, and... Quietus is now Thaumaturgy because fuck their culture and the culture of sorcerers everywhere.
                                                For all the options V5 has added it's sure slammed down a lot of new restrictions.
