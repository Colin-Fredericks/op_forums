    So I must admit I haven't given the Ruthvens much thought the last year, until recently stumbling upon Nevesa Zekistrya in the Hunters Hunted II.
    Apparently she can trace her lineage back to Lambach Ruthven, and thus being a descendant but not sharing the Ruthven family name. There are a lot of stuff that's pretty weird in the section where she's mentioned which I will not go into here, but what captured my attention was Nevesa being presented as a Old Clan Tzimisce.
    That may not be controversial to some of you, I don't know, but for me it started the slow realization that I really don't know just how the Old Clan Tzimisce works. Does this Nevesa being a Old Clan means Lambach is too, since the share the same blood? Or is it more like a sect allegiance? Or something else?
    Just what kind of bloodline are the Old Clan Tzimisce?

    So I realize that the Old Clan has been its fair share of revisions since Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand, that they are presented by a host of different authors through different editions and so on.
    But still there must be a way to find some consensus in the material.
    I collected the material I could find below. So if your up for it, take a look at it, tell me if I missed any, and let me know how you treat the Old Clan ^^

    So are the Old Clan Tzimisce a bloodline similar to the the Tlacique?
    By this I mean that they are a bloodline that split from the parent Clan a long, long time ago in matter of philosophy but more importantly in Disciplines shared through the blood. They have one or several progenitors that continue the bloodline from sire to childe.
    That all OCT have a shared trait in Dominate (according to character creation at least), that several sources take note that OCT is a ancient bloodline, and that it seems really clear to all Tzimisce that the OCT is a separate body may speak for this view.
    This view complicates a descendant of Lambach being OCT, since Lambach, Dracula and the rest of the family tree is presented as regular Tzimisce.
    Also, if you look at Kazimir Savostin's write up in Caine's Chosen, his sire seems pretty damn Old Clan including banning any use of Vicissitude, but Kazimir learns it and is presented as a regular Tzimsce.
    Sources supporting this might include: The Hunters Hunted II, V20, CB Tzimisce, Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand, Lore of the Clans preview.

    Or are the Old Clan Tzimisce a bloodline similar to the Serpents of the Light?
    By this I mean that they are a bloodline of ideology first and foremost. They are a coven of members that don't need to be relatives in the blood. A member of the parent Clan is considered to have joined the bloodline when joining (or leaving) the defining vampiric sect of the parent clan. It may also have some ties to geographical area.
    Speaking for this view is the sources mentioning the Anarch Revolt as a dividing factor between the Tzimisce and the OCT, like the rest of the antitribu factions of the clans. There are also sources stressing that the OCT is not a bloodline proper, but a small eastern European cult, and sources that say that the OCT usually(!) favors Dominate for Vicissitude – not having it as the defining factor.
    With this view Nevesa can be a OCT and yet having regular Tzimisce in the family tree.
    However, Danika Ruthven or Dracula not being a member of the Sabbat for ages should be considered OCT with this definition.
    Sources supporting this might include: Encyclopaedia Vampirica, Guide to the Sabbat, Storytellers Handbook, Storytellers Guide to the Sabbat.

    Or are the Old Clan Tzimisce a bloodline similar to the Warrior Setites?
    By this I mean that any Tzimisce can join the bloodline out of choice, and that choice will define the blood of themselves and future childer. The Old Clan chose not use their clan discipline of Vicissitude, and magically they get Dominate instead.
    This is more of a compromise suggestion. Speaking for this is that OCT-character creation describes Dominate in-clan, meaning they all have it, but yet it is not something carried within a collective lineage, being a cult or a gang of associates. There are also sources mentioning the choice not to use Vicissitude.
    With this view Nevesa or her sire, can have chosen to join the OCT, leaving the larger Tzimisce/Ruthven lineage.
    If this is the case, it is badly explained just how a group like the OCT (or the Warrior Setites for that matter) just can decide they want a new Clan Discipline.
    Sources supporting this might include: Encyclopaedia Vampirica, V20, CB Tzimisce, Transylvania by Night, Lore of the Clans preview.

    But surely the Old Clan Tzimisce are not similar to the True Brujah?
    By this I mean that it's been established that the OCT is not the original Clan, while the rest of the Tzimisce with the antediluvian itself have been infected and warped into something else. It was retconned away as the delusions of a few OCT in Storytellers Handbook.
    Sources supporting this is basically Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand, even though V20 mentions the OCT predating fleshcrafting.
    Still, this view we've repeatedly been told is a myth.


    So any thoughts?
    I'll post my sources used in the thread below.

        “Her real name is Nevesa Zekistrya, and she is a Tzimisce claiming to be a Caitiff. To be specific, she descends not from the modern metamorphs of the Sabbat, but from the Old Clan, those who did not acquire Vicissitude.         - The Hunters Hunted 2 , page 150


        “Tzimisce, Old World : A term used to describe some of the very old Tzimisce who remained in Europe and are nominally a part of the Sabbat. In fact, a very few of the chose to involve themselves in the politics of the sect at all, rather preferring to to lead their unlives as they wish. A small group within the Old World Tzimisce is obsessed with the idea that Vicissitude is in some way tainted or corrupted. These Old World Tzimisce refrain from employing the Discipline and prefer to cultivate Dominate.“
        - Encyclopaedia Vampirica, page 176


        “Old World Tzimisce.
        Not every Tzimisce supports the Sabbat with her whole heart. Indeed when it comes to the arcane Tzimisce of the Old World, few supports it at all. Of course aligning oneself against the Sabbat is a good way to end up facing a down a pack of self-important rabble who want to strike at the elders for the good of the Sabbat and all such rubbish, so it's often easier to join the society yet abstain from its meetings.”
        - Guide to the Sabbat, page 39

        The Old Clan would prefer never to deal with the 'defiled' members of the Clan Tzimisce, but family is family and they can't survive the modern nights with no support. They make treaties and trade favors with other Fiends, sometimes lending support to the Sabbat as a whole in exchange for help and support when they need.”
        - V20, page 439

A few believed (or claimed to) that Vicissitude wasn't a Discipline but a disease. Most Old Clan Tzimisce rejected that claim and focused instead on the obvious facts of insubordination. The Old Clan blamed the main clan's elders for allowing such a catastrophically successful revolt and concentrated on building an endless succession of schemes to reclaim the clan homelands for themselves.”
        - Storytellers Handbook, page 181

        “The Old Clan Tzimisce – for no such thing as a Tzimisce antitribu exists- include both fairly disgruntled elders (and their well-indoctrinated, loyal childer) and a handful outright lunatics. […] Vicissitude strikes the Old Clan as unnatural and largely unwholesome, a perennial temptation to lose control and do something weird that provokes hostile response. The Old Clan Tzimisce fancy themselves as leaders steeped in wisdom and knowledge, not constantly mutating freak shows. However, they seldom pass up an opportunity to slay a Sabbat Tzimisce as long as the danger is not too great.                                         The authors chose their words carefully, keeping the Old Clan very vague. From the Revised Clanbook I get the feeling that it's more a matter of choice to belong to the Old Clan than it is a Bloodline united by the same blood and powers. I would compare them to Angellis Ater which I personally think is more a choice to belong to than being born into. They are still Lasombra, just with a very different agenda and politics than most Lasombra, making them an extreme faction within the Lasombra Clan. I would say that the same goes for Old Clan Tzimisce. They are under the title Factions with other, lets say ”real Tzimisce” groups. But also in the text about the Old Clan on page 45 the author writes to Methuselah this: ”The Old Clan still practices the ceremonies of coronation and rites-of-passage to childer. The ritae are abhorrent to them. Perhaps you would find more identifiable company among their ranks, old one”. Now that sentence could be read in two different ways, that's why I say that the authors chose their words carefully. It could be read in a way where the author suggests that among the Old Clan the ancient one could find vampires that still share many of the ideas and customs from when the old one was active last time and speaking with them might please him. Or it could be read like a suggestion that maybe he should join their ranks for the same reasons since he would belong better amongst them. If you read it like the latter then that would suggest that it is a matter of choice since Methuselah is confirmed ”real” Tzimisce.
                                        I don't own the Gangrel Clanbook or the Brujah Clanbook, but in the Revised Clanbooks I do own, all Faction sections include more ”a-matter-of-choice-to-belong”-factions than pure Bloodlines. So if you check the Brujah Clanbook or the Gangrel Clanbook it could be interesting to see how they adress Anda or True Brujah which we know is pure Bloodlines. I checked the Setite Clanbook and they used a different title than Factions there, they called it Setite Divisions, also notice how they split them up a bit, seperating The Chort of Wepwawet, The Scandinavian Setites, The sisterhood of Sekhmet and the Cult of Typhon Trismegistus from Children of Damballah, Serpents of the Light, Daitya, Tlacique and the Warrior Setites.
