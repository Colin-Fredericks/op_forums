    Two Baali Theories, lets discuss

    04-19-2020, 06:35 PM
    So normally people go with the assumed three progenitor Baali embraced by an Antediluvian named Ashur, who was implied to be either Saulot as a gamble in the Jyhad or a mistake, or Cappadocius as Ashur is a reference to his geographical namesake. We got an apocryphal tale of Haquim embracing a shepherd boy with a fated damnation to shepherd the Baali into a form that could be fought. Later we got an account of Tzimisce slaying the First Tribe and siring the Baali, and also the idea Ashur is the name of the site of the Baali rather then the Ante who sired them. Last we got from Beckett's investigations the idea Ashur was the state Antediluvians went into when giving into their Beast.

    So I bring up the idea we discuss two theories not often discussed. First that the Baali instead of being a Bloodline of the Antediluvians are the Children of their own Antediluvian, suggested to either be Namtaru, or the being sleeping in the Coffin labelled Nergal in the Ghost of Enoch. This could be interesting to explore, and it could explain why they were so hard to snuff out by the Cainite's of old.

    The 2nd theory is instead of the Baali being sired by a single Antediluvian, or my personal theory of three (merging the accounts of Haquim, Tzimisce, and Zao-lat as all being true), that instead they were sired by the entire Antediluvian Host at the time. This would mean maybe 13 original Baali 4th gens at the time, and the traditional 3 Baali fathers could represent just the ones that survived past the ages of antiquity. This holds some promising story potential, like maybe why Moloch and Troile were lovers was because they were both Sired by Ilyes and had many shared pains and regrets. The Assamite Revised clanbook certainly makes it seem like there were a ton of 4th gen, considering Haquim who was charged with culling the unwanted illicitly sired Cainites himself sired well over a dozen in those ancient nights of yore.


    And with this idea I wonder, could it be possible Ur-Shulgi doesn't have a Beast, if Haquim plucked the demon possessing him out of his burning form. I personally like the idea of Ur-Shulgi being a Ferectoi, a child born a fomor, but he could have just been a child possessed by a spirit, or more popularly suggested by fans is the idea he was a Widderslant mage. But I like to think when possessed spirits bind and root into a beings lower soul, and its intriguing to consider how Ur-Shulgi possibly going from Baali to Banu Haquim because he had his Demon removed, or that removing the Demon gripping strongly into his beast caused Ur-Shulgi to be so cold and calculating because he is only a Hun souled vessel.

    But these present a lot of seldom discussed options.

    If the Baali were from a true Baali Antediluvian I would have it that this being's 2nd Generation progenitor was in fact Apep, as it fits with the idea that a Mysterious being named Typhon Embraced Osiris and maybe Osiris was also a 3rd Gen who never embraced rather then a 4th gen. Sutekh was said to have diablerized Apep, a being normally considered a Demon/Neverborn/Wyrm. But it could be interesting if this was some sort of Dark Apotheosis for this being and it became the entity it is now in a similar fashion that Cappadocius tried to accomplish by having his childe eat him while his soul escaped, or how Lasombra faked his demise, and maybe even had his body destroyed so he could become a being of the Abyss.

      You've certainly connected some dots in a different way that I usually do, and I like that a lot. You've put some time and thought into adding your own creativity to the gaps in the lore, something that more World of Darkness fans should feel free to do.

      Here's one argument in favor of the plausibility of the Baali coming from an otherwise unknown Antedeluvian: they have Obfuscate as a clan discipline, and one suggested power for rank 10 Obfuscate from an old Vampire supplement is the ability to remove oneself completely from history, disappearing from the memory of everyone in the world. Obviously, we don't know of any character who has ever done this, which might as well be proof that the power works.

      Ur-Shulgi being a monster from another game line before his embrace may or may not be problematic depending on how much you want the mythologies and lores of other game lines to cross over into your Vampire game. As someone who gravitated very strongly to the end-of-the-world/Y2K hysteria of Vampire 3rd Edition, I believe that Ur-Shulgi's primary importance to the story was that his conduct showed his ancient sire's attitude to the modern clan and its culture. Ur-Shulgi's lethal hostility to the clan's embrace of Abrahamic faiths, in particular, comes across as a dire warning when taken in the context of the lore and mythology specific to the world of Vampire. That well full of Haqim's blood form in the clan's mountain stronghold sounds far too much like a Baali worship site to be a coincidence, and I believe that Haqim has been playing a very, very long game. The appeal of this theory, to me, is partly that it works with just Vampire lore tools, without any addons or plug-ins from other game lines needed.

              Some Baali theories of my own:

              1. The Baali are a fuck up, not a master plan: The original story of the Baali was Ashur Embracing them in a fit of pique rather than as part of a 3-dimensional Jyhad stroke. My view is that this actually is more interesting than the "Antes being super-geniuses in all things." Saulot Embraced a bunch of Infernalists to show them what real evil was because he viewed his condition as a curse and was disgusted by what they were up to. The problem was that he created a bunch of 4th generation super-beings as a result because not everyone will view vampirism through his Sad LouisTM attitude. So he spent something like the next 8000 years hunting them down with Haqim.

              2. Ur-Shulgi is a brainwashed Baali: I generally go with the idea that the 3 Baali are Ur-Shulgi, Moloch, and Shaitan. My idea is Ur-Shulgi was grabbed by Haqim, brainwashed, and promptly set upon his fellows by the others. I actually think Ur-Shulgi is sincere in his desire to purge his fellow infernalists (as much as someone can be sincere after an Ante wipes their brain and soul clean with Blood Sorcery as well as Dominate). However, it adds an interesting new perspective to the idea that he hates Islam and other religions among the Banu Haqim because he'd be tortured by it as a Baali. He still has the Clan Flaw of being repulsed by religious symbols after all and Clan Flaws get worse not better the more powerful you become.

              3. The True Hand is subverted by Moloch and Troile: The True Hand uncharacteristically accepting the Baali into their ranks strikes me as very likely that they are brainwashed by a powerful force in the Jyhad that more or less wiped their minds of better sense. My view for maximum humor value is that Moloch and Troile are the parties responsible. The current Del Rol is, in fact, the female Antediluvian of the Brujah that is more bemused than anything else at the ridiculousness of the "True Brujah." Either way, the Baali have effectively taken the True Hand and its resources into its ranks and allow the Bloodline access to its previous levels of power.

              The big irony being the True Hand's most ludicrous and quixotic quest of fighting "Soul Eaters" (misunderstood banes) is something the Baali actually approve of.

Due to this he learned from beyond entities to re-embrace cainites from other clans so perhaps Nergal from the Cappadocian and Moloch from the Salubri could have been their chosen for the re-embrace in order to have that more than one antediluvian sire for the original three. Back in the day, before Clanbook Baali, it was believed that Shaitan was the first and there was one Baali who abandoned their antediluvian sire from each clan (Dark Ages Companion '97) so that can suit better the several clan progenitor's theory.

                  The Eldest being a sire for Baali is something I can't consider. He stole or learned his magic from a demon bound to the carpathians and his pursue of azhi dahaka is enough already for the need to include even more infernal origins beyond what they already know from Koldunic Sorcery.

                  I never like the idea of ur-Shulgi being more than a really powerful 4th generation sorcerer.                       1. Ur-Shulgi being the third Baali is something that I think is interesting because there's a connection between the Children of Haqim and the Baali that rarely gets mentioned. The two clans were archenemies long before the Salubri and the Baali were. We also note that Ur-Shulgi has a charcoal skin cover for his features while his only known child has lily-white skin. This was more of a thing before the Banu Haqim were retconned as not turning coal black as they aged (perhaps to avoid racial implications).

                      2. A good Antediluvian for Embracing the Baali alternative would be Malkav. Set embracing the Baali feels like a flanderization of his character since they embody corruption but it's a tool to enlightenment not an end to their own sake. Ventrue is an interesting optional twist as Tiamat and other vampires of his lineage pretended to be gods as well as engaged in infernalism.

                      3. Some people have speculated that Shaitan may not actually be a Baali founder at all. Aside from the fact that he's one of the Baali that does want to destroy the world and he looks like an enormous goat-monster from all his Infernal investments, he could be a vampire that was converted from his previous Clan to being a Baali.

                      4. I've sometimes speculated that Troile was sired by Ventrue and that the True Brujah are missing that vital information. That would mean that the Ventrue and Brujah are brother and sister clans.

                          I have previously posted my head canon on the subject of the Baali origins here, down in post 6.

                          I reprint it here for simplicity sake:

                          Imagine you're in Enoch, the Second City, or just Ye Olde Hyborean Age back in the day. There's all kinds of crazy stuff going on: cannibal cults, demon worship, Cthulhu summoning, shape shifting soul eating plagues, defiling of sacred temples, cats sleeping with dogs... real Sodom and Gemorrah stuff.

                          Caine, or maybe an unnamed Antedeluvian, looks around and says, "Hmmm, this is getting out of hand. I should create a sort of morality secret police to tamp down the worst excesses. They need to be sneaky to infiltrate the doomsday cults, but also charismatic enough to lead the usual torch wielding band of moralists. Let's have a specialty discipline that detects evil, convinces people it's the wrong thing to do, blasts the unrepentant, forces anyone who survives that to battle their own inner demons and, then, finally, levies a judgment against the guilty in the form of a curse. Let's name the line after the local legitimate deities that have respectable, established religions so the common folk will accept their moral authority."

                          That's my guess at the origin of the Baali.

                          Of course, after a while the narcs always wind up sampling from the evidence locker and it all goes south, which brings us the modern Baali.
