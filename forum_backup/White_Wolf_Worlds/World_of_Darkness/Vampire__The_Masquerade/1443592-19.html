    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    I find this doubly ironic since the reverse is usually encountered.
    I'm not sure it's ironic at all. The idea that there are phrases on both sides that tend to set the other side's emotions on edge is fairly usual with any divisive issue (hell it happens all the time even in far less contentious settings).

    V20 fans are perfectly capable of phrasing things in ways that get under V5 fans' skin too. That's not irony that just.. human.

    "V5 isn't REALLY Vampire: The Masquerade. It's too different. It's an alternate universe!" is an expression I've heard far too many times.
    I don't particularly like this thing either. I'd like to think it's one I've generally avoided using personally; mostly because I'm a bigger fan of VtR than VtM, and VtR got hit with all sorts of this sort of thing.

    However, I do think these are meaningfully different in terms of what's being expressed even if not so different in how annoying the phrasing is.

    It's also ridiculous as it should be one community with different preferences.
    The problem is that it feels like a lot of those preferences get expressed to the point of seeming to be mutually incompatible. Should it be one community? I don't know really. Should implies things I'm not sure about. It could be one community with different preferences, but I'm not sure enough stakeholders are invested in that outcome to naturally generate it in current conditions.

                    To an extent, that's moving beyond "one community" and just being positive people in general.

                    To expand on CajunKhan's point: RPG.net is "one community" in a sense, but there are multiple major sub-forums that are also their own sub-communities (large enough and old enough that they have lingo that most people don't know). They have a separate d20 subforum so D&D and Pathfinder discussions don't drown out everything else, and due to the edition wars that have happened in the d20 umbrella, have different rules to manage different needs.

                    Even here, we split things up because there's a level of practicality involved.

                    Communities exist, in a web of concentric circles of Venn-diagrams. Every user here is part of the Onyx Path community by virtue of participating here, but that doesn't mean everyone here sees themselves are a part of the Scared Lands community just because it falls under the Onyx Path umbrella.

                    Whatever the ideal state of everyone being supportive of everyone else is, we're going to self-segregate into sub-units.

                        First I’d like to say that I have lots of older edition books (haven’t read them all) and most V20 books but, unlike most of the people on this forum I haven’t played it enough to know the ins and outs of the different clans and sects and what it means to be this or that. I have, what I feel, is a superficial engagement with the game. The commenters here are way above me when it comes to the knowledge of all things VtM. But I also feel like surely there are others like me that enjoy it on a minimal level. Surely people like me matter with how a game fares. I am possibly wrong about the following things but I might possess the ignorance that unfortunately other people might have about the game.

                        V20 to me, embraces the essence of the time period of when VtM was released. While it has modern stuff in what the characters can do. The art, the look, the clans and sects all still invoke the sense of the early 90s. A time period I think fondly of that has a texture about it. The music, the fashion, the movies, the vibe. VtM came out at a time when all these things gelled and became part of the game. Part of me wants to recapture those great times in a game that belongs in that decade. Any game I run is set in the 90s. I’m a grognard.

                        In contrast V5 contains none of those things. Because of the decision to place the game in modern times the game suffers from it. Personally I feel no attachment to the past 20 years. It could be my age, in my early 40s. Modern times have no “texture” that enhances the mood of the game. Music? Movies? Fashion? Nothing. While yes I could play V5 set in the 90s why would I when V20 has the look and feel of those times.

                        The “fashion magazine” photos is one of the most pretentious cringe inducing things I’ve seen in a rpg. A huge nail in coffin. Overall the book physically is a huge negative. The Anarch book cover literally has 2 people walking on the sidewalk. Terrible.

                        The changes of publisher seem to hurt the release schedule.

                        The anarchs are so modern politics, pass. Don’t want to spotlight something that we are inundated with daily. The Sabbat rules. I know they are coming but oh well. Also goes into the political horror (actual politics not the jyhad) angle which I don’t want. See also the delving into modern historical drama that makes me roll my eyes and go “See? That’s what you get for being modern and politically edgy.”

                        The lack of focus on elders. I love the generations, the elders, the lore and history.

                        The lack of feeling unique and uniquely powerful. I guess I prefer superheroes with fangs.

                        The Hunger mechanic seems gimmicky and I don’t want it to influence my actions. Also it seems to force a certain type of play.

                        I didn’t like Gangrel leaving the Camarilla back in the day so obviously I don’t like any other big clan changes.

                        Again I might be ignorant but that’s my 2 cents and I can’t help to think others share my ignorance and are staying away for the same issues.

                                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                            This image was always a bit jarring for me, but it was explained at the time that the USA and UK both understand different meanings for ’New Wave’ music.

                                            In the UK, ’New Wave’ was another name for ‘post-punk’ music from the late 1970s - probably most embodied by groups like Joy Division/New Order, The Slits, Souxie and the Banshees, etc - where groups originally influenced by punk rock, or actual punk bands, were moving on from it and experimenting with forms. In my view, gothic-punk originates in these groups - notably Bauhaus’ Bela Lugosi’s Dead was the song that had the term ‘Goth Rock’ coined for it. The Cure also came from this type of music. It is also where you saw a trend of independent labels, or ‘Indie Rock’ becoming prevalent and would later on include bands like The Smiths or The Jesus and Mary Chain. In America, they basically refer to this as Alternative rock, these days.

                                            In the USA, though ’New Wave' was sort of what people in the UK would refer to as 'New Romantic’. It was more encapsulated in 80s ostentatiousness and a glam style.
