                                            I don't know, something about trying to get the old setting up and running again seems...incorrect. The cWoD was all about the impending apocalypses, and since we actually got those it seems weird to keep moving forward with it (Not that the apocalypses we got we all that great). Plus, the entire point of the old system ending was because the well had run dry, and the metaplot had stretched things to their limit. Not to mention after ten or so years a lot of the flaws in the setting had become too huge to ignore and too ingrained to alter.

                                            ...

                                            That being said, a 4th Edition could work, so long as Onyx Path used it as a chance to overhaul a lot of the shortcomings, both mechanical (multiple actions, ugh!) and lore-based. I for one would like to see the Followers of Set expanded, tweaking their lore enough to give people greater room to play Setites that want nothing to do with their cult. Reorder the whole Giovanni/Cappadocian debacle so the Cappadocians are allowed to be a clan in the modern day, while still allowing the Giovanni to be running around.

                                            You know, alter the setting so it has a coherent history. Many of the growing pains VtM experienced were directly a result of the original designers 1) having no series bible, and 2) working from a specifically European/American mindset. Meaning a lot of the history of the Clans was determined retroactively to justify the current state of affairs, and the vampiric political landscape was uneven, and pulled from broad stereotypes. Then there's the fact that the Vampire splat was developed largely independently of the Werewolf splat and the Mage splat and the Changeling splat, etc. Good in the sense that they could all stand on their own without confusing entanglements, but bad in that it made it seem like the cWoD was in fact several separate, disconnected worlds. I'd like to see Onyx Path take all the elements of the CWoD and establish a concrete history from the ground up. How different clans interact with all others, how different regions of the world interact with the others, and how vampires as a whole interact with other supernaturals.

                                                        What?

                                                        I mean, ok, I have only a basic familiarity with the cWoD, but...

                                                        For Real? Freakin' For Real?

                                                        This Just got a new edition. They spent 4+ years establishing and releasing products for this framework, to revitalize all these old but very popular games...

                                                        And now that's just being swept out for something newer? What?

                                                        I saw something similar a number of times. The most prominent I remember was when DnD 4th was replaced in three years by its Essentials remodeling. After an initial wave of products, the line petered out in about a year and a half, replaced again in a few more years by 5th. And sure, some said they were addressing valid complaints, and besides, the Onyx Path crew are very competent. But they are still producing new content for the 20thA.                                                         VtM has always been a favorite, and using it as a focus for launching initiatives like editions and outlier products (board/card/video games) is a sound choice, but this feels like an oversaturation.
