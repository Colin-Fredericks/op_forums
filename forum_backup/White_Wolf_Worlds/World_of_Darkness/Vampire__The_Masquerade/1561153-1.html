        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

        This is utter nonsense and a barrage of pure, abject orientalism.
        So the basic idea is that many Wan Kuei don't actually know much about Kindred. Kindred were always rare in Asia and for thousands of years the only Court that had extensive dealings with Kindred was the Thunder Court, and the other Courts consider them a hairsbreath away from all being heretical Akuma. The existence of Kindred was known, but the details were much less well known and most Wan Kuei didn't care.

        It was similar to how most Kindred would be hard pressed to tell you the difference between a Troll and an Ogre.

        Things changed during the Mongol invasion, as Kindred were more common among the Mongols. This is when the Wan Kuei really started researching Kindred, figured out how Clans worked and created rituals that could affect vampires of higher generation by targeting their ancestors with lower generation.

        Then, during the colonial era, when Kindred showed up again as invaders, they brought back a lot of their old research and knowledge.

        Currently, most older Wan Kuei have at least a surface level understanding of Kindred, and how clans and the Curse of Caine works. But for younger Wan Kuei, a lot of it is kept a mystery. This is very much by design.

        One of the recurring conflicts in Kindred of the East is the idea that older Wan Kuei intentionally try and keep younger Wan Kuei ignorant about a lot of things. They claim that by giving them too much unnecessary information, it will cause Running Monkeys to focus on things other than their spiritual enlightenment. Worse, knowledge they're not ready for can drive them into the arms of the Yama Kings.

        This paternalistic attitude is the root of a lot of the conflict in KotE, both between different Courts, Dharmas and Wan Kuei of different ages, especially in the modern world where younger Wan Kuei probably know a lot more than elders who still think electricity is a fad.

                    So
                    1) Wan Kuei don't really view themselves and Kindred as the same thing. Wan Kuei would consider themselves closer to Wraiths or Risen than to Kindred. The idea that they've actually died and gone to the underworld and then came back is central to their existence. Kindred never really die, at least not to the same extent. To Wan Kuei, Kindred are just an unusual type of supernatural being, hardly anything special beyond the fact that they share some superficial similarities to Yin cycled Wan Kuei. They would consider themselves as similar to Kindred as a Kindred considers itself similar to a werespider. There are some similarities, but they're not the same thing at all, and historically they were rare enough that they never mattered. Again, it's similar to how historically Vampires have never cared about Changelings.

                    2) The basic idea is that Kindred are outsiders to the supernatural world of Asia. Wan Kuei have extensive contact, including diplomatic contact and defense treaties with the Hegeyokai, Eastern mages, Hsien, etc. Kindred exist outside of that entire structure. This means that while there may be repercussions for a werewolf that kills a Wan Kuei, anyone can kill a Kindred at any time without any risk of political blowback. Conversely when Kindred try to kill Wan Kuei, this can end up pulling the Hengeyokai or other groups into the conflict and on the Wan Kuei's side. The fact that the various supernatural groups in Asia work together to a limited degree (due to shared opposition to the Yama Kings) has always been the main reason as to why Kindred struggled to penetrate Asia prior to colonialism.

                    3) Again, Wu Zhao exist outside of the supernatural structure in Asia. They have no friends and no allies. The Courts have ruled that interacting with a Wu Zao is illegal outside of certain intermediaries, so few younger vampires are willing to risk talking to one and being labeled akuma. The Wan Kuei Courts intentionally try and keep younger members ignorant because they think that will keep them more loyal. Basically they believe that allowing young vampires extensive interactions with individuals outside of, not just the court system, but the entire supernatural political structure, creates a slippery slope where those wan kuei may turn their backs on the courts and the 5 True Dharmas and eventually become akuma. That said there are younger wan kuei who interact with Wu Zao, or interact with demons, or do lots of other stuff that the Courts have deemed illegal. It does happen, but everyone tries to keep it secret and nobody will actually admit to doing it.

                    Anyway, ultimately Kindred were always fairly rare in Asia until recently and, interacting with them was typically against the rules of the various Courts, so doing so risked being labeled an akuma and killed. As a result, historically most Wan Kuei had as little to do with Kindred as possible. It's only been fairly recent that Kindred have gotten more common and this is one of those conflicts within Wan Kuei society between the Courts and the Bamboo Princes, as the Courts try to pretend that the situation regarding Kindred hasn't changed in the last 100 years and refuse to update the rules, while younger vampires are out partying with Kindred every night at the local night club.

                        Assuming that the documents that player characters may find and translate in The Transylvania Chronicle are authentic, the goal of either Saulot or the P'o of Saulot's divided self, drawing inspiration from the Wheel of Ages model of history, is:

                        To advance the turning of the Wheel to the point where the August Personage abdicates the Throne of Heaven and a Demon Emperor ascends it;

                        To be that Demon Emperor; and

                        To then break the wheel and prevent its further turning, drowning the world in a demonic age that never ends.

                        The first two parts of compatible with the teachings of Xue, in that *someone* will be the Demon Emperor, but the third part is a novel heresy. The Demon Emperor is supposed to have its victory and reign, according to those who believe in the Wheel, but the universe is then supposed to continue turning until the cycle starts over in a global or universal rebirth. Saulot is depicted in the Transylvania documents as out to rule as an anti-God forever and ever.

                        The Wu Zao, for their part, are Saulot's eyes and ears in this, continuing their master's research into Hell's secrets for him while their clanmates to the west are discarded and hunted.

                                        Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post

                                        I think one of the big issues with KotE is that the gameline is primarily written with the focus on China, mainly eastern China.
                                        Basically that, plus the trope of isolation.

                                        In the 90s, particularly the early 90s, the idea of China as not only far, but closed, was very strong. The Cold War, the hard borders in Hong Kong and Taiwan and the Opium Wars still being mostly portrayed as China trying to stop foreign culture, all contributed to a vision in the US and its allies that the country was a cultural fortress.

                                        KotE drinks primarily from this source. It really is primarily Eastern China, basically Beijing and a few other provinces along the Yellow River, and it is China from the POV of then recent history of interaction with UK and US. It uses the regional influence of China as an excuse to generalize an already skewed view over a small area to a way larger one.

                                        Japan was a complicating factor, as it indeed had an isolation policy (historically specific), were now a US ally, had a strong conservative nationalist bent (due to how it was dealt with at the end of the War) mixed with a strong move to modernization, overall it was a country in internal conflict about its relationship with the US and that reflected on the US view over the rest of Southeast Asia.

                                        All that said, those are all things the games were in course to improve were it not for KotE getting such a backlash that it was mostly left aside despite having a strong fan base. And the result would probably be a lot more nuance and variation across courts, and less "tongue in cheek" references to the policies of China's Communist Party.
