    Does the Ashirra make sense?

    04-24-2021, 07:54 AM

    From both a Doyle and Watson perspective, they're an ill-baked idea. Now, I don't think there's a comprehensive book on them, so perhaps I'm missing something, but with the information I do have, they're not a good idea.

    This thread isn't here to take shots at a real world religion. Don't use it as such. Please be respectful.

    First, why would the exist from a "doylist" out-of-character perspective

    1- Nobody wants to be othered. The Ashirra serves to do exactly that. You wanna play the Camarilla like everyone else? Well you wanna set the game in Medina so that'd be an Ashirra heartland. You're a muslim at a larp? Some prick is expecting you to play an Ashirra. It's a special sect for a special land, faith and people. It just seems like Orientalism and an exercise for "othering" .

    2- The Ashirra isn't that different from the Camarilla, a supposedly global organization, why not just use the Camarilla but call the prince an Emir and have him promote islam if you want to do that?

    Second, Why would they exist in the first place from an in-universe perspective?

    1: Islam's core values are pretty antithetical to being a vampire; don't drink blood, pray during the day, go to -holy site that's going to fucking burn you- once in your time. Can't see how that'd be endearing enough to vampires. Either you're going to warp and pervert the religion like crazy or you're just nominally Muslim. Neither works.

    2: As vampires go, Islam isn't an old religion, rising in the 7th century, and doing so by displacing other faiths. Ancient vampires aren't likely to convert and are going to hold significant.. reservations for the faith that swept through their lands. The Assamites, Settites, and anyone who can cast a spell are going to have significant objections to a sect defined by faith. It'd be a revolution of the young, but as discussed, the young don't really have a good reason to hold onto the faith as a sect of vampires.

    3; Europeans didn't fuck them over with colonization? The Camarilla didn't take advantage of that? Are you sure? There's also the Ottoman empire, who, while muslim, had significant non-muslim population and make more sense dominated by the Camarilla than the Ashirra.

    Just seems like a bad idea really.

                                  I personally quite like the Ashirra, as well as the idea of regional sects like an Indian Jati and other regional sects, although that’s my personal opinion. But this is a thread of opinions so I’ll share my own.

                                  I think the Ashirra is interesting. It’s a reaction to a sudden, radical shift, just like the Camarilla & Sabbat of European origin are. Just as those sects arose as a result of the dichotomy shift of the 15th century and in response to the Black Death and Inquisition, and how the earlier proto-sects (Courts of Love, Black Fiefs, etc.) arose in response to the fall of the western Roman Empire. For the Ashirra this dichotomy shift would be the creation of Islam and the conquests of the Prophet Mohammed (Peace Be Upon Him) and his successors.

                                  The Ashirra arose with an inspiration founded in Islam, both as a pragmatic response to the paradigm shift occurring first within Arabia and then in the broader Muslim world as well as a spiritual revolution akin to the rise of Christianity throughout the Roman Empire and its successor states.

                                  As far as the theological justification for the sect, from a Doyalist perspective it has just as much justification as the ideas like the Cainite Heresy or how Laibon adapt African spirituality for their story, etc. It stands to reason that vampires, if they existed, within a Muslim culture would try to find a way to square their beliefs with their condition - this might manifest as it did from the Watsonian perspective in the WoD. Their are references in the Qaran to the Prophet Mohammed (PBUH) being sent to teach humanity and jinn alike. From that point, as well as stories like the follower who ate some of the Prophet’s (PBUH) blood and nails instead of burying them, it’s not hard to see how vampires could create a justification for themselves to follow a form of Islam adapted to their circumstances - especially if founders of the Ashirra’s theology claim to have been followers of the Prophet (PBUH) themselves.

                                  The Ashirra is not simply a religious organization alone though. It also gives guidance for a governing, scholastic, and military infrastructure for the defense of the Ashirra’s domains. It’s a political body as well, as sure as the Camarilla or any of the European proto-sects are/were.

                                  A good millennium feels like enough time for the average kindred of the region to be Ashirra, certainly the average vampire walking around in the region that the Ashirra claims would be culturally Muslim by that time, as surely as the average vampire of medieval France in the Courts of Love would be culturally Christian even if a few of the more venerable elders maintain their pre-Christian beliefs (if any beliefs at all have endured the crush of time).

                                  I do agree however that by the modern nights there should be an influx of European sects trying to gain prominence within the region. While the Camarilla may go between alliances of convenience and attempted inroads, the Sabbat crusading and trying to stoke young kindred into breaking their blood bonds and rising up, and the Anarchs burning through the young and revolutionary kindred and challenging the Ashirra Status Quo. The modern Ashirra is probably suffering from the same causes of the regions suffering as well as an assault on their domains from European and American kindred, but I don’t see any narrative reason to remove it entirely and I think it offers a greater opportunity for setting exploration and storytelling to have them around.

                                      I never liked the Ashirra. The Camarilla is supposed to be a worldwide conspiracy of vampires. The original books clearly showed vampires from Muslim lands were already part of the Camarilla, as were vampires from India, sub-Saharan Africa, and elsewhere. The Camarilla was everywhere except the Far East because of the intention to make that area especially mysterious and introduce new clans there (an idea neve properly developed before the KotE - which was an awful game on its own IMO). There wasn't a need for a separate sect.

                                      I think it's perfectly fine for a number of vampires within the Camarilla to struggle with how to reconcile their Muslim faith with the vampire condition, just like there are probably sincere Christians or Jews or Buddhists, etc. who do the same thing. But in so far as vampires are sincere and devout with their religion are very rare, such internal religious "sects" within the Camarilla would be very small. And primarily concerned about saving the souls of their adherents, not exercising political power over other vampires.

                                      In the late revised era, the Developers desperately wanted to expand the game in order to milk the fans for more revenue. They thought one way they could do so was to take a modern multicultural approach and divide up vampires according to modern human cultures and civilizations (so that the existing fanbase would buy more still more supplements on the idea that renaming one or more of the existing Clans constitutes real value). I didn't think that ever made sense. Elder vampires abandon the details of their humanity and their vampirism and the affairs of vampires become important to them. Newly embraced vampires from the Middle East shouldn't be any more or less devout that those embrace elsewhere. And it was obviously a retcon. Mentions in various sourcebooks were made about the Prince of this or that city in the Middle East, and it was clearly understand they were under the Camarilla.

                                      It took a cool and interesting core concept of the game - a worldwide conspiracy of vampires everywhere - and eliminated it in favor of tired and cliched multicultural approach of vampires being primarily the same as the local human culture. One of the reasons why Chicago By Night was great was because of characters like Khalid and Inyanga, both as individual characters and working alongside an ancient Greek philosopher and a Parisian courtesan.

                                                          The Ashirra is fairly problematic as an aspect of the World of Darkness. One of the striking features is that it treats religion as essentially an aesthetic choice by the holder of that religion. Which is to say that the underlying religious beliefs and influences only exist to shape how you act and think at a purely social and stylistic level. This is a terribly America way of looking at religion, so prevalent amongst the largely American writers in the early years of White Wolf, and those writers who have been influenced heavily by Internet culture. Real people tend not to treat their religion quite like this, and the creation of the Ashirra tends to raise some interesting problems for anyone who has a reasonable knowledge of Islam in the real world.

                                                          Firstly, there is the is the somewhat unresolved issue of what the Ashirra actually represents. Are its members Muslim vampires? The vampires of the Islamic world? The vampires of the "Middle East"? Published writings on the Ashirra tend to skip between these three ideas for what constitutes the Ashirra, without ever acknowledging that they are quite different, and that the answer to the question ought to inform how the sect is portrayed. Remember, of course, that in large parts of the "Islamic world", Muslims were a minority until fairly recently. Baghdad, for instance, was probably a city in which less than half the population was Muslim at the end of the nineteenth century.

                                                          The next question is what does the Ashirra actually do? All other sects are framed in ideologies which are both positive ("We believe in these principles") and negative ("We believe that this other sect is wrong and dangerous"). The Ashirra, somewhat uniquely for sects in the game, doesn't actually do this, with the ideology of the sect being "We are Muslim". This is not that interesting, but is also slightly problematic; whilst most vampires have agency, Muslim vampires get defined by their religion. It is easy to imagine why this might be considered objectionable.

                                                          It should also be noted that, according to orthodox Muslim theology, members of the Ashirra either heretics or, worse, apostates, for what should be fairly obvious and straightforward reasons. Actual Muslim vampires should be trying to murder members of the Ashirra for this on a fairly regular basis.

                                                          It is probably pretty clear that I don't have a very high opinion of the Ashirra as written as part of the game. It would probably be not too hard to re-write the sect into something meaningful, but, at the moment, the sect presents a number of significant issues which have never been properly resolved.
