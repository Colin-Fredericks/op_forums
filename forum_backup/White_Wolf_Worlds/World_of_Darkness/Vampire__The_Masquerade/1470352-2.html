        Originally posted by Vilenecromancer View Post
        I'll answer the questions in an objective and simple fashion. I'll also focus on how the two are presented in V5, only pulling from earlier editions to show inspiration for how they are in V5.

        The Hecata are actual my favorite clan. Objectively they are Cappadocians reborn, mixed with the culture and practices of the other necromantic bloodlines. I like how Oblivion is, but hate that Obtenebration was lumped in. Regardless, fun clan/sect/cult.

        The Path of Death and The Soul is interesting to put it simply. In V5 terms, a Path is an alternative form of morality which allows a vampire to exist without the burden of human ideas. V5 presents them in a similar format as clans to make it easier for Storytellers to create terrifying NPCs. Whereas the Hecata study death in general, The Path of Death studies violent death. Overall, they're bland murder-hobo NPCs with very little RAW nuance.

        If you're noticing a similarity, that's actually for good reason. In earlier editions, the Path of Death and The Soul was originally developed from the Cappadocian morality system known as the Road of Bones. Those who followed the Road were often Cappadocian scholars of death, who studied the very concept of death and what happened after. As time moved on Roads were slowly phased out as the Camarilla encouraged the practice of Humanity alone. Oddly enough, V5 Humanity is very similar to how Roads use to be practiced and developed.

        The Sabbat, who formed around the same time as the Camarilla, continued to practice the Roads, but also hypocritically altered them to be even more inhuman as to further separate vampires from humanity. From a meta perspective, we can see Paths become progressively more and more violent as the Editions went on after 2E.

        So to put it simply, they're similar because the Hecata's predecessors were the original inventors of the Path before the Sabbat twisted it to fit their own agenda.
        I think this might be the first thing about V5 I've ever heard that has made me genuinely angry.

                                Vilenecromancer the question was somewhat rhetorical, and directed more specifically at SetiteFriend since they had been responding to me in particular. But yeah, I agree that what you just described sounds like, at best, a "maybe" for the scenario I provided. That contrast in the Ethics pointed out really hits it: "hasten death when it seems unnaturally delayed" leaves a lot more room for nuance and interpretation than "hasten death, for everything, period."

                                One thing that I was reflecting on before posting initially was the PoDS ethic that, in my opinion, had the most potential to drive excessive bloodshed: "not killing when useful or necessary." Depending on how you view it, that could arguably be reason to kill literally everything that you come across, as who knows which deaths might reveal new secrets, unusual anatomy in an autopsy, etc. But that's part of where my discussion of "diminishing returns" and such came in above. After killing a bunch of people in horrible ways, getting a handle on anatomy by studying their corpses, the base level of knowledge will have been acquired, and most future killing will be determined by specific values. It leaves room for discretion, whether killing someone today or killing them another day might have the most potential for learning.

                                But "hasten death for everyone" is much more indiscriminate, and leaves less room for the kind of consideration described above. Going back to the terminal care home, there's maybe an argument that their death has been "unnaturally delayed." But if dealing death without qualification is your primary consideration, then you're probably just going to end up with a care home full of corpses after a single night.

                                To go back and address the OP very quickly, the difference between the Hecata and the Path of Death is that one is a clan, the other a philosophy. In fact, there are probably members of the Hecata who still follow the Road of Bones or the non-murderhobo version of the Path of Death, because the Cappadocians *invented* it. Maybe the V5 rules don't handle it very well, but there shouldn't be any reason you couldn't make a Hecata member of the Sabbat following their Path of Death, from a lore perspective anyways (ps. especially since, as noted above, many Hecata members view death and necromancy more as tools than subjects of study).
