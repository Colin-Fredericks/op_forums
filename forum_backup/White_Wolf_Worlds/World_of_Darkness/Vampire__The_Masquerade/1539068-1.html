                The core seven.

                I was originally going to do the old 'none, they're dangerous in different ways', but I think there's a difference between the original seven Clans and the subsequent additions, and that's the power sets. The lack of specialty disciplines gives them broad but practical abilities.

                The Lasombra are about projecting power, as a blunt hammer of necessary. The Venture are about projecting power you can't get rid of. While the Brujah are physical powerhouses their words are also ones which change the world. The Toreador's eyes let them build beauty, but also tear down both it and you.

                The original seven have three/four clans set up to find your every secret and hold them over you. As well as four that can break you down with their words, and three that'll mess you up physically. Then you have the Gangrel, who just won't die.

                Out of all of them the Tremere edge the others out just slightly, but only because you have no clue what powers they might pull out of their hat.

                There's a reason these seven were reworked into the Requiem Clans.

                    I agree. In general I find the common Disciplines more valuable than the unique ones, giving advantage to the Camarilla clans. I'm not sure about the Lasombra: Obtenebration is cool, but I'm not sure I would choose it over a common discipline.

                    That said, if rules and editions are taken into account, some things work out weaker or stronger than one might expect. Ex.: VDA20 Presence and Serpentis.
                    Serpentis is extremely strong for fighters, and Warriors of Glycon have Potence in-clan. I would fear a Setite enclave having mighty warriors and leaders (Warriors of Glycon), dangerous and effective intellectuals and preachers (Priests), and tricky Witches of Echidna with Thaumaturgy. They can threaten people from any direction (social, mental, physical, mystical) with a brutal effectiveness matched only maybe by Assamites, as they also have 3 castes. But Quietus Cruscitus is not as good as Serpentis for fighting in my opinion, and Quietus Hematus is not as useful as other disciplines. And Setites of all 3 VDA20 bloodlines are much more likely to have plenty of goons and cultists around them than Assamites.
                    Presence, on the other side, if one rules that spending 1 willpower point and passing a Willpower test at difficulty 8 is sufficient to be protected from it for one scene, is extremely ineffective, as anyone with Willpower 5+ on average will pass the test. That weakens all the clans with Presence, even if lore wise Presence works more consistently and actually allows one to control emotions enough to make vampires the seductive and frightening menace that they are.

                    This is mainly a lore vs rules thing. Had I written the lore based on these rules, the lore would have been different.

                            It depends on how you look at it, I guess. I mean, if your fear is being beaten up in the street or shot, the Brujah and Gangrel clans would be the most dangerous, and although I think the Gangrel are stronger individually, the Brujah probably have the advantage in numbers. Now, the Ventrue clan generally has well-connected members with greater purchasing power, and if money is power, then they would be the most powerful and most capable of pulling a trick out of a hat. Now, if information is power, then we turn our attention to the Nosferatu. In addition to all this, we have other factors such as magic, member qualities, disciplines, goals, and even the unpredictability of the clan. That said, I think the top 3 would be like this:

                            For common humans:
                            1. Brujah
                            2. Gangrel
                            3. Malkav

                            For wealthier humans:
                            1. Ventrue
                            2. Lasombra
                            3. Assamite

                            For other kindred:
                            1. Nosferatu
                            2. Ventrue or Lasombra
                            3. Assamite or Tremere

                            For Garou and other species as a general rule:
                            1. Ventrue
                            2. Tzimice
                            3. Brujah

                                                        Originally posted by TwoDSix View Post

                                                        It's easy to spin it the other way: sufficiently powerful social characters can outsource muscle, thinking, and anything else to other characters. Who needs to plan when you have a guy who can do that for you?

                                                        Yes in-play a player still has to come up with The Plan. But fiction wise it can verily easily become 'social guy decides on The Goal, convinces a couple of mental guys to come up with The Plan and The Equipment, and then the physical guys enact The Plan's.

                                                        Few characters can outsource like a social character.
                                                        Maybe I chose the wrong word with "planning".

                                                        The mental character provides both a vision for a worthy goal and a route towards it, and he does not need to humble himself to anyone else in the pursuit of his plan except as a pretense for convenience. A less smart character will pursue proportionately less worthwhile goals, easily made irrelevant even if attained, and will need to submit to the lead of the smart people to whom he outsources the planning. Human or vampire, nobody likes to admit they're not smart enough to decide what to aim for or how to reach it, and that they have to submit to another's lead and planning in order to reach their goal, becoming a pawn in their own game. And even if they're sufficiently self-aware to actually listen and obey those to whom they have outsourced the planning, nothing protects them from unknowingly being a pawn in their plans.

                                                        I am not talking about merely outsourcing some technical thing for which one lacks the specific skill or knowledge. That's normal. But you still have to be smart to play the game, above all. The smart person figures out easily how much social and physical might they need and where and how to source it from in the most effective and safest manner, while the social character outsources easily but doesn't see the full picture, and no amount of outsourcing can safely compensate that.

                                                        Even rules wise, what the mental character lacks in social traits they can compensate by researching allies and opponents' hopes and fears and using leverage accordingly, translated in additional dice or lower difficulties. And once they have secured in this way the services of enough social characters, they're set to win.
