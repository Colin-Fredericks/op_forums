    AFAIK:

    He studied under the Xue (the guy who founded the biggest 5 virtue Paths of Wan Kuei), but was proved to be imperfect under the sun and hated, banished from Xue’s company and then returned to the West (KotE, pg. 38)

    He stolen their secrets:
    1. Disciplines, he can use it, in an unhealthy way though (KotE, pg. 100), maybe only Dragon Tears (Sunset Empire, pg. 95), but I always think this refers to Obeah/Valern, not original Dragon Tears.

    He left the bloodline Wu Zao to deal with Wan Kuei

    He Embraced a jomon guy later called mokur and greatly pushed the foundation of Inconnu (Lair of the Hidden, pg. 16)

    He has killed many Wan Kuei (Gehenna, pg. 114, it’s from one chronicle so may not be canonical)

          Even more than normal, almost nothing in this is certain.

          You're dealing with the connecting point of two of the messiest pieces of lore in the entire setting, not because they're complex, nuanced or ambiguous, but because they're straight badly written for special reasons.

          1. Saulot is the most retconned character in the setting. 1st Edition Saulot is a good guy and that's it, and this is an important plot point that must remain possible. But many players and authors straight up abhor the notion, so we have recurring attempts to add uncertainty that haven't always been careful or thought through.

          2. The Wan Kuei hate for the Kindred is a result waiting for a cause. Both lines were designed independently, then it was decided they're in a nonnegotiable war for Doylist reasons and all explanations for why are post-hoc and not well integrated into any lore.

          For both you have the same underlying problem:

          A. The original lore follows a clear direction, is well integrated with the setting and necessary for some of its themes. Saulot must offer hope of transcendence and redemption. The KotE must be philosophers open to any seeker of Enlightenment.

          B. A later decision demands a result without a real change in lore. It's post-hoc and tackled on without much thought. Saulot must be dubious and the KotE must be completely hostile to the Cainites.

          C. The "solution" is throwing half-baked ideas, and since none can really solve the conflicted stance, you keep throwing more.

          D. Authors take the opportunity to work in their own preferences, since it doesn't have to really be coherent with the rest.

          E. The result is an incoherent stack of lore bits with no real connecting tissue. You pick what you want and join however you want, because they're not complete enough to make sense.

          Specifically for the question in the title: nothing anyone knows. Saying "Saulot did something and now the Kuei-Jin want all Cainites dead" is the whole point, you fill by yourself.

                                              For the Kuei-jin, the concept of a "Clan" in which you are Embraced is a very alien
                                              In their eyes the Kin-jin are all the same
                                              Some followers of the Song of the Shadow Dharma have taken up study of the various "Dharmas" that the western vampires seem to adhere - tho' they see those evebn less than the heretical Dharmas because no Cainite has ever progressed beyond the state of hin and their general ignorance of the spirit world, obvious marks of failure in the eyes of the Kuei-jin
                                              Because of their limited interractions, mostly based on field experiments, talking with ghosts and such sources they classified seven:
                                              The Hundred-Mask Clowns, referring to Clan Ravnos (tho' the Kuei-jin of Idia have a bit more experience with the servants of Zapatashura, had a run in with Kartarirya and his brood and possibly the Danava…)
                                              The Shameful Path of the Ghost Bully, referring to Clan Giovanni
                                              The Silent Tiger Brotherhood, likely referring to Clan Assamite
                                              The Rut of the Pretentious Geisha, likely referring to Clan Toreador
                                              The Way of the Insufferable Flesh Gardners, likely referring to Clan Tzimisce
                                              The Inauspicious Order of Blood Devils, likely referring to Clan Tremere
                                              The Thousand Charming Company Men, likely referring to the Clan Ventrue

                                              So they have a limited understanding and view things through a different lens

                                              When they see a Wu-Zao, they not really see a "Clan" or "Bloodline", a Wu is a "Blood Family", a group bound together by their Court or for some common purpose and by a totem spirit called a nushi
                                              Wu-Zao means the "Family of Zao"

                                              From their point of view there is this unsually large wu, all share the mark of the Chi'iu Muh (tho' they not really proficient with it, but passed the stages when the Dragon's tear is a gemstone to become an eye, but not reached the stage to become a great blazing, pear-like orb in the center of the forehead) and unusally resilient and/or fast - Black wind and Iron Mountain Demon Arts - suggesting a deep connection to their P'o with a parmament Yin Imbalance judging by their looks and still low on their Dharma, stucking at blood drinking to get chi. And their common goal is pretty clear, since they usually in pairs, a thief capable of obtaining the secrets of the Thousand Hells and a scholar who could decipher those secrets
                                              They are Zao's Family, they are named after Zao-lat

                                              The Kuei-jin never refer to Zao-Lat without some sort of epithet attached to the end of the name; the most common is "Zao-Lat the Traitor"
                                              They heard the stories of the student of Xue, a thief, a despicable creature who benefited from Xue's compassion, learned at the feet of the master and stole his wisdom and fled
                                              But this is is where the knowledge usually stops, he is a footnote

                                              You see, if you are not a Mandarin in your Court or higher you are technically forbidden to have a talk and business with the Kin-jin, if you suspects someone is one or have proof, accidentally talked one, you have to report it to your superiors and let the honored ones deal with this
                                              Young Kuei-jin are kept in the dark so to speak for their own good
                                              With the Way of the Lone Walker ritual a Kuei-jin willingly and willfully could turns her back on the systems of court and wu by declaring herself heimin and to be a heimin have some benfits, while not having a Wo and be a loner, dance on the edge of sociatey means they are looked upon with contempt and distrust the Courts pioneered the practice of using heimin as messengers and intermediaries between themselves and the western Kindred, in exchange for ensuring the heimin safe passage through their territory.
                                              So there is a way for a Young Kuei-jin "offically" have contact but in turn the Heimin's words are treated with suspicion and what they might say? With a grain of salt.
                                              We are again back to rumors

                                              At first glance a Wu Zao is not that different from a Kuai-jin, they are lockals and behave mostly like them, except the lack of acces to the Yin and Yang World and talking with the residents of these realms (with possible Demon/Wraith/Spirit/Hengeyoaki/Hsien enemies they made along the way) - but this last part is not that obvious unless a said resident rats them out...
                                              Sure the lack of knowledge with etiquette and such, how a Kuei-jin should behave usually gives them away, but they could keep up the act if they are learned enough

                                              The Kuei-Jin have a few powerhouses of their own right, but that is a side effect of walking the road to the Houndred Clouds and Bodhisattvas are usually removes themself from society
                                              In the whole Middle Kingdom only two individuals exist who actually met Xue and were his contemporaries and still around: Thousand Crane Mother, the founder of the Dance of the Thrashing Dragon Dharma and The Most Serene Black Pearl Mistress, Mother of Shadows, the only living Arhat of the Bone Flowers physically remaining on Earth
                                              These two ladies are the only two people in the Middle Kingdom who been there and know what really happened

                                              The whole Zao-lat "hatred" is basically passed down rumors and cautionary tales
                                              It is a bit like the story of the guarded flower in the Russian legends
                                              In the classic narrative, an Emperor (or a Czar like Catherine the Great) is strolling through the palace gardens when they notice a single lonely guard standing at attention in a remote corner of the courtyard. The ruler asks what the sentry is guarding, but the soldier has no idea and simply says he was following orders
                                              Confused, the ruler summons the captain of the guard, who in turn checks the official written regulations. The records show that a guard was assigned to that exact post two centuries ago to protect a tiny flower that had sprouted in the stone. A daughter of a past Emperor had placed a sentry there so no one would step on it. However, because the order was never officially rescinded, soldiers had continued to stand watch every single day in the exact same spot, long after the flower had died and the courtyard had changed
                                              The story is often used to illustrate the phrase "the way it's always done," symbolizing how organizations and institutions can blindly follow rules and traditions, wasting time and resources on things that are no longer relevant

                                              It is really like that, except those two ancient Kuei-jin no one around who been there, remember and can tell you the truth (and to be honest, even they not really know, back then Xue's students porbably were just mildy amusing to them)
                                              So it is really complicated to answer your question, we can puzzle together rumors, hints and even then we only would get a very rough picture what may happened
