                                Oh, you made a good point here. Let's adress it.

                                Magic follows universal rules and princibles.
                                When you are incepting, you are uncovering new magic, and working in accordance to it's rules.
                                When you are using the same disciplines others use, you are working with the same rules as they do.
                                If someone were to learn the discipline you incepted, they would be using the same rules as you.
                                Combo disciplines are the result of "lawyering" with rules. You aren't breaking any rules, you're just abusing them for the results you want.

                                Vampires, Werewolves, Changelings, Hedgewizards, wraiths and spirits all work according to rules. Mages still have rules, but they're a lot more lax, so they think of their magic as "dynamic" and everyone else's magic as "pattern magic". But when a mage tries to break rules they backlash hard.

                                So, basically, those guys you mentioned aren't making up whatever the hell they want, it's a lot closer to just finding new sets of rules that other people haven't discovered yet. They're still working within a "static" framework.

                                I get it. Change is scary.

                                    Yep, and I appreciate the info, not being familiar with Necroscope.

                                    I agree it’s useful to know why things were developed the way they were. It doesn’t mean we have to slavishly adhere to tradition, but knowing why things are the way they are helps to make an informed decision about what to keep and what to change.

                                    What gets me is how many people (this part is not not aimed at you) can’t handle things being changed in a new edition, like adjusting things is some kind of sin, even though the version they’re attached to was itself most often an adjustment of something previous. This is how these things go, especially in WoD, which is a conservative’s nightmare (in more ways than one).

                                    And in any case, there are several things that are pretty well certain: 1) This edition will not have unique disciplines. 2) The Tzimisce have already been confirmed to have Protean as of Chicago by Night. 3) Clan writeups are expected to be sect-agnostic, to emphasize that lineage and loyalty are not equivalent. 4) Following that, bloodlines will not be getting full clan writeups but are to be treated as the parent clan and represented with Flaws and Loresheets. 5) Blood Sorcery is now an umbrella term for a bunch of stuff that had different names in previous editions, thus not monopolized by a single clan. 6) This edition will not be presenting paths of enlightenment as a replacement for the Humanity system, though they can be represented by slightly tweaking Convictions.

                                    All of this gives us a pretty good idea of what to expect. For what it’s worth, I think these design principles make a lot of sense, and I’m eagerly awaiting the Tzimisces writeup. Still think they should put the S back...
