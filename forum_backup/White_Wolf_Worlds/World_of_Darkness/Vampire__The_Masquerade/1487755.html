    Divorcing clans from Geography/People

    07-24-2022, 02:53 PM
    The first seven clans in VTM were universal, making fun of class rather than race; they work whether you're in Birmingham, Brisbane, Benin, Beirut or Bushan. Western Ventrue have some Roman terms, but it's very easy for Ventrue elsewhere to go with -insert favourite local empire here- influence instead. The Tremere are a relatively modern clan (I would argue THE modern clan) and though they have clear european origin they are a globalist clan with a globalist agenda that's ready to take in the best from anywhere.

    The other six clans began as racist caricature, and although steps have been taken to move us away from the worst of this, the result is quite mixed. I've convinced myself the best thing to do is move further in the direction of culturally neutral.


    Small changers:
    The Followers of Set, Great stuff. A religion that appropriates itself across all cultures works. Egyptian myth works well as a common framework (It's myths are famous and the mythology is old)

    Giovanni, Incestuous families all across the globe... somehow makes them less incestuous, but vampires heading mortal dynasties fun trope and this strategy players and the ST Invent their own culturally-fitting family appropriate for the location. Given that they've become less "Other" they really aught to just joint the Camarilla and become a normal clan.

    Lasombra: They're pretty good by V20. A paranoid, aggressive clan that likes the sea and fears God. Could use more antitribu and perhaps de-emphasize the Latin America stuff a little more, though that's not entirely something to be excised. We'll not talk about later editions; I don't want to despair.


    Big changes.
    Assamites: Still too tied to the middle east. The Loyalist/Schismatic split works wonders in seperating "bloodthirsty fanatic" from real world religions, but there are still problems on both sides: The Loyalists are still bound to the middle east, the schismatics serve as refugee allegory and have a far-too optimistic, orientalist narrative to them. If we start with the Schismatics, they've left the loyalists for varying and sometimes incompatible reasons, and it's not going to be easy for them to assimilate into rightly fearful Anarch and Camarilla populations. If we discuss Loyalists, well, I would posit that the Assamite bloodline is really geared far more towards them: the caste system that encourages them to operate interdependently, the darkening that sets them apart from other Cainites, the poison discipline that encourages cruelty, the addiction to diableire, the infamy of it all: they're basically designed to be their own sect. But with such a cannibalistic, militaristic sect, is this likely their only separation? Of course not. In addition to the Assamites that went to Chorizan/The Sabbat, there should have been tens of Assamite cells deciding they don't need the mountain and going on to create their own secluded sects in the far flung regions of the world, setting up shop over towns and small cities, adopting new beliefs, producing brainwashed childer, and making forays into nearby cities. When they're finally located and retribution comes, it will still be hard to stamp them out. So long as one survives, they can rebuild: a persistent threat. After a few cycles, these sects are likely to lose touch with their Alamut origins and completely localize.
    Also like, Evil Martial Art Sects are a fun trope that work in any culture. (But if they're vampire hunters I guess they have strong antihero potential?)

    Ravnos: Probably the messiest transition: Gypsies aren't a group that shouldn't be touched, so WW went orientalist on India. As great as the Samsara path of paradox is and how fitting the -reality is an illusion- mindset works, I doubt Indians feel flattered by being given the Ravnos. The philosophical influence of India on the clan is great, at least the ideas on Fate and Illusion perfectly fit the clan and these ideas have been exported to other parts of the world. On the otherhand, the Caste system can die in a ditch: Historically clueless, sociologically and politically unreasonable. While it could be imagined that some parts of the world appreciate Ravnos more than others, especially areas in conflict which need mercenaries, they really are a bloodline pushed towards nomadic behaviours. They're tragic mirrors of most Gangrel: One tries to be humane in order to avoid getting cast out, the other can't help but get cast out and learns to avoid being humane. Ravnos work anywhere, with any human culture.

    Tzmisce: Kupala should just get retconned out of existence. The Tzmisce come in two flavours: We have Terrifying doctors that threaten the establishment with their transgressive art, and we have a backwards eastern european caricature that amounts to little more than a shitty country Ventrue. The former works anywhere, the latter belongs to the last century and needs to go. @ me



    Bloodlines are smaller, so, reasonably, they could be tied to specific places as a result of not having time to develop. But I think it's quite inevitable that successful bloodlines such as the Samedi will move well beyond their starting culture.

      Just stating, I don't think they should make a too big retcon to produce such changes unless specifically asked by the affected people. It may backfire. But I can no problem engage with the exercise of examining how it could have been, or where to go if things come to this.

      Followers of Set: agreed. I'd add that the prejudice against them is too overblown one you take out the Thulsa Doom vibes. I'd add then to the Camarilla with some frequency or as default.

      Giovanni: instead of Venice they should have their own presence within the Dark Umbra, making up for an "ancestral manor" without ties to any particular place, which would both help to globalize their idea and their actual disparate families while keeping the idea of it being an extended clan (small caps), which would more easily justify the practice being widespread and their studies in Necromancy.

      Lasombra: they really should explore the ties with deep waters more, maybe even take the whole Venice thing the Giovanni currently have with mysterious elders living beneath the waters. A full blown Innsmouth-style theme for havens and domains would be nice.

      Assamite: i actually think that their post-schism + Lore of the Clans take is already pretty good on its own. Sure, the whole refuge take is an obvious implication, although it isn't spelled out too frequently, but that could be corrected just by moving the schism to the past, maybe even ancient times. And while Alamut is still in the Middle East, currently it is more of an undisclosed desert location with no ties to any particular country or population, which I think works well. Better than that, only if they moved into the Near Umbra (which their V20 Dur-An-Ki depiction would even give some credence to).

      Ravnos: something could maybe be done with the actual Jati system instead of the castes. We could even have splits within the clan regarding what the British did with the castes, or something else regarding their relationship with the other clans within India. But yeah, in general this whole mess is in such a state that I fully think it is the case to just ask Indian authors to make a book on the subcontinent and let them run wild with it. I would personally in this case let them work and only touch the clan again after they're done. Otherwise it is really a simple premise to work with other cultures, but the real problem from the beginning was the whole relationship with gypsies.

      Tzimisce: I disagree that their particular take on "country lords" should be ditched. It isn't particularly hard to move to other cultures and really consider that the overall phenomenon of land-based lords isn't really that much of an Eastern Europe thing.

      Bloodlines: they're small and specific and I think it is ok for them to be culturally specific even when starting to move out of it.

                          I've recently come to a conclusion about the Ravnos. I think the reason the clan exists was to give the Gangrel a rival for the wilderness. Full stop. That avoided the issue of the PC Gangrel simply leaving their coterie in the city to live in peace in the countryside. Or the Gangrel in general simply avoiding other Kindred by staying in the wilderness. The Ravnos was the monkeywrench to be thrown into that idea. Now the Gangrel simply couldn't live in peace by themselves. They had a competitor. That is all that is needed for their clan concept.

                          The entire Gypsy thing was to make sure the Ravnos and Gangrel were to be enemies since originally the Gangrel were supposed to be protective of Gypsies. The Ravnos obviously violated that, putting these clans in opposition. But that can easily be replaced with any other story/justification, ideally something involving a dispute between the clan's antedeluvians that poisoned relations permanently. Even if individual members of either clan didn't want to react that way, they couldn't rely that it wouldn't become hostile when meeting a member of the other clan. So it is a self-perpetuating cycle. Something a little bit stronger than the Brujah-Ventrue rivalry, but not to the point it becomes impossible for a PC Ravnos to be in the same coterie as a PC Gangrel, or doom every encounter to always end in a fight.

                          You can keep the idea of Ravnos as wanderers, solitary tricksters, and enemies of the Gangrel while ditching any required ties to the Gypsies or India. Those just become options for character concepts like they would be any other clan.

                          Chimestry as a Discipline still needs a lot of work though.

                                              I once did some notes up for Healer Salubri that were able to survive in Africa, the Middle East, and Asia that the Tremere eventually had to realize that they were never going to be able to deal with for a "Global Camarilla" campaign I had. The rub being the Tremere discovered most of them gave absolutely no shits about the diablerie of Saulot and were powerful enough the Tremere had to make peace with.

                                              The interpretation I went with was admittedy much darker but not ditching the concept entirely. The idea of trying to make a Clan of Salubri that has the same basic concept but dark enough to fit with the other Clans as well as a game of personal horror.

                                              Here's my notes:

                                              * The Salubri Clan weakness remained being unable to feed from unwilling vessels. Unwilling being defined as those not entranced, involuntarily there, or otherwise resisting. They are masters of loophole abuse on this.
                                              * The Salubri Embrace doctors, morticians, hospice owners, religious leaders, and people involved in the medical trade.
                                              * Lots of people do die because of the Salubri influence but they try to minimize their "vampire footprint."
                                              * They are heavily involved in the local blood trades and frequently pass off spikes in deaths as the result of disease outbreaks.
                                              * Salubri have their own Roads that have survived since the Dark Ages that basically amount to "God said it was okay to feed on you" and "Feeding from the already dying and elderly is fine."
                                              * Some Salubri blood bond people they consider undesirable and keep herds that are left to lose ghouldom but not their devotion. These people are willing vessels they feed on over the course of years until death.
                                              * Other vampires consider Salubri less saints and more, "Hospital feeders."
                                              * Local variants of the Salubri included Animalism and Presence to go with Obeah because these allow a lot more feeding alternatives.

                                                  To be fair, the notion of the Ravnos as a clan of vagabonds does exist in some World of Darkess books. The idea was that this was a group of vampires who rebelled against authority not, in the manner of the Brujah, through some manner of principle, but merely through a selfish desire to have complete freedom. You get this portrayal of the clan in, for instance, the core rulebook of the first edition of Vampire: the Dark Ages. There is certainly space within vampire society for a group like this, with such a portrayal of the clan giving a bit of additional range for characters existing on the fringes of vampire society.

                                                  Yet there is a problem with the Ravnos, which is that they can't seem to maintain a single concept from book to book. So, we started out with "Gypsy vampires", then went briefly into "vagabond vampires", then into "Indian vampires", then into "kind-of-maybe-Indian-vampires-but-not-always". The result was incoherent, with each subsequent book making the clan harder to work with, just due to the accumulated mass of contradictions.

                                                  Symptomatic of the issues (and to an extent causitive of them) is how the Ravnos were treated in the clanbooks of the revised era. With the Assamites, one could see what happens when a single author with a relatively coherent vision was allowed to shape the notion of a clan. So Clayton Oliver trimmed most of the orientialism off the Assamites, created a focused notion of the clan of judges, and gave the Assamites a much needed depth. He introduced a lot of ideas to the clan, but these ideas were primarily to enrich and sharpen the focus of the clan. The end result is by no means perfect, and comes off, in some instances, as a work in progress, but does effectively deal with many of the problems of the clan's original incarnation.

                                                  The work on the Ravnos in the same era goes the other way. The clanbook, and the clan's entry in the Libellus Sanguinus series, throws a whole series of ideas at the clan without any coherence or depth. So you get an eastern and western clan, two Paths of Paradox, neither of which are terribly well considered, and a whole range of jati with no great relation to each other. The text draws upon India, but only in a very superficial way, and the writer seems to forget that India and the Mediterranean are actually linked by Iran and Syria. The ideas pull away from each other in different directions, and the seeming cause is the belief that the Ravnos are an ethnic clan (albeit Indian rather than Roma at this point) and that it would be worse to stereotype than to merely say the Ravnos can be anything. No wonder that, after this treatment, the Ravnos were unceremoniously bumped off.

                                                          Originally posted by James_Willoughby View Post
                                                          Yet there is a problem with the Ravnos, which is that they can't seem to maintain a single concept from book to book. So, we started out with "Gypsy vampires", then went briefly into "vagabond vampires", then into "Indian vampires", then into "kind-of-maybe-Indian-vampires-but-not-always". The result was incoherent, with each subsequent book making the clan harder to work with, just due to the accumulated mass of contradictions.
                                                          Yep, it is very inconsistent. The problem was that after it was realized that tying the Ravnos too strongly to Gypsies created various issues, they couldn't figure out what was to be its core concept. Everything they tried was a riff of the original. Going from Gypsies to vagabonds wasn't a stretch. Neither was Gypsies to India since they originated in the sub-continent. But those are two very different directions.

                                                          Furthermore, by themselves, those concepts didn't seem to give enough for STs and PCs. Changing from Romani to some greater Indian ethnos just made the group these vampires pulled from bigger, but kept it tied to human ethnic groups which was the same mistake. Trying to develop that aspect by the Path of Paradox and incorporate philosophical aspects associated with Hinduism wasn't productive in my opinion. It might have sounded good in theory, but in practice didn't help players or STs. "Vagabonds" by itself didn't do much either, since the Gangrel were already the wanderers.

                                                          There's nothing inherently wrong with "vagabonds" as a concept. However, I think it's stronger for the game to emphasize their rivalry, competition, and feud with the Gangrel. It's a bit meta, but honestly it's why I think the clan exists. That's the narrative device that should matter to the game developer and STs. Vagabonds are just one character type that can associated to that.

                                                          I think it is OK if both Gangrel and Ravnos are seen as "wanderers". Outside that aspect, the two do have obvious differences. Gangrel tend more to be survivalists, explorers, hunters, warrior scouts, and other hearty frontier types. Ravnos are more like tramps, drifters, vagrants, traders, and others who live among human communities but travel. Gangrel culture prizes members who can take care of themselves, and live off the land by using their physical attributes. Ravnos culture prizes people who can prosper and gain treasure, using their wits and social skills. A well traveled business consultant living mostly in hotels who dispenses advice and moves on to her next client would fit well with the Ravnos, but not with the Gangrel. So would people who are perpetual tourists, travelling entertainers (especially those that follow large crowds like carnies or Ren Fairs), or people who are in the RV culture. They could even be property developers who build small resorts and move on! In the Dark Ages setting, they could also be religious pilgrims, bandits, and merchants.

                                                          Gangrel are more like outdoorsmen who try to live in balance with their environment. Ravnos are nomads who exploit and deplete the area's scarce resources before moving on.

                                                          You can keep the links to the Romani. You can even keep the links to India by saying that after their feud, Ravnos headed east from Mesopotamia to the Indus while Gangrel headed north or west. But the main use of the clan by STs, in my opinion, should be to provide competition to the Gangrel over the scarce resources of the Outlands. Their different cultures and preferred solutions to problems means the clans don't cooperate well. The one prefers physical prowess and mastery of one's environment, while the other chooses social interaction and exploiting human communities. In theory, the two shouldn't conflict, but they always do. Whenever Ravnos enter an area, they just create too many problems for the Gangrel because they inevitably attract too much attention. Then they leave, and the Gangrel who remain in that area have to deal with the mess - whether that be riled Lupines, hunters searching an area, police investigations, people or animals leaving the area, etc. Then when things finally settle down, some other Ravnos might move in and start the cycle again.

                                                          As long as you keep in mind that the clan is supposed to be the Gangrel's rival, I think you can keep expanding concepts while retaining the core idea for the clan that the game needs.
