                                    I agree with your sentiment that the use of a ritual to restore humanity is problematic, but I think it's important to keep a few things in mind. It sounds like you actually know the rules for Bardo so you are using exaggeration for effect, but people who don't know the rules may think you are being serious, so I'll make some comments for them.

                                    First, the power that allows you to restore Humanity requires a Conscience roll (with the difficulty being the Humanity level trying to be regained), so in effect it is a "do over" roll for a character's failure. It's limited in how frequently you can do it (within one week of losing that level). So it's not like you are able to shotgun regaining humanity. It's a one time "do over" to make you feel guilty like you should have felt that first time but you rolled bad. At the cost of burning all your blood in V20 (but not the original 1e Hunters Hunted).

                                    Second, by "cow of forgiveness" I assume you mean the third level of Bardo, Gift of Apis. That just allows you to feed on animal blood as if it were as nutritious as human. So there's not actually any cow of forgiveness. But I find your terminology funny.

If you have the time, probably the best thing you can do is to recreate the entire thing as a system of rituals or perhaps a path of Blood Sorcery (or both). For Bardo, you are probably right - this is best seen as a series of Rituals. Which means the Children of Osiris should have at least one Path of blood magic that allows them to perform rituals. As such, I agree that looking at Akhu would be best, and the least morally problematic Path would be Ushabti. Which is a power also shared with Mummy's Hekau. So it makes sense thematically for the Children of Osiris.

                                        Does the "animal blood is useful" three-dot power that i forgot about make the blood more palletable?


                                        I think even if you're a "good" vampire and start using Bardo for the right reasons, eventually you'll realise you can do it for the wrong reasons. You're not going to be butchering people for fun on the regular, but "it's just one murder, I've got a ritual for it" is a very slippery slope. Once your faith affirms your evil indulgences, it really will drag you towards depravity. Bardo reinforces the idea that you're a good person even as you do bad things. You'll sin, you'll do some self suffering and that'll wipe away your sin. Congratulations; you've just abused a child for fun and then earned for yourself some forgiveness; That's a possitive feedback loop. That's something that puts the moral locus of control on you: You can sin, but you can also forgive yourself. For a lot of people, that's a fast route to crazy town.

                                        Bardo really is a bunch of rituals (banishing sign is more path like, but there are precedents for instant-rituals, like the vampire equivalent of jam technology.) and there are 6+ powers for it. But as to what primary path CoO will pick... I'm unsure. Part of me thinks they'll still go for the Path of Blood. Or, in true anti-settite fashion, they'll go for Lure of Flames (which Settites presumably avoid like the plague; the gods associated with fire are Set's enemies, and the brightness of the fire is going to upset their bloodline weakness) There are many good options. I don't think the CoO necessarily need to be paired with a "good" path, but they are unlikely to pick corruption.

                                                Originally posted by Elphilm View Post
                                                The Children of Osiris have never made an appearance in my games, but if they did, I would simply use them as written in Hunters Hunted. They were originally presented as a tiny sect (not a bloodline) of vampire hunters. That answers the question "What would make them useful to a game of personal horror?" to my satisfaction. They fit in a game of personal horror like any other Hunter group -- either as antagonists to the PCs, or as uneasy allies with the PCs against some Big Bad.

                                                The Children are not really redundant with the Salubri, because the latter are the hunted, not the hunters. If anything, the Children might be redundant with Mummies -- they fill the same narrative role as foils for the Followers of Set. However, I think that a group of vampiric Buddhist contemplatives is different enough from a group of immortal Egyptian sorcerers for the World of Darkness to have room for both, so the overlap is not a problem for me.

                                                I've never been keen on the fandom's apparent hostility toward genuine good-guy groups like the Children of Osiris and the Salubri. I see no reason to turn either into secret assholes, because they function perfectly alright as written -- as tiny rays of light in the World of Darkness that the player characters might stumble across on a rare occasion. A setting where absolutely everyone is some shade of hypocritical dickhead is boring and samey to me. You need rays of light to make the darkness deeper, too.
                                                ^This. Nothing is more boring than every faction in the setting being slight variations of the same asshole with different coats of paint.

                                                I LIKE the Children of Osiris and the Salubri being bright spots in an otherwise grimdark game. It keeps things interesting. If every faction is full of dishonest, craven bastards, the players will learn to just expect to be betrayed by everyone they deal with. And thus will stop dealing with them honestly, and instead always work to kill or betray or manipulate everyone first. Like a D&D party that learns to crawl over a dungeon with a ten foot pole, checking every inch for deathtraps, it gets tedious fast when they expect every social encounter to be a trap.

                                                As for how to use the Children of Osiris, I think the tendency to not know what to do with them is a result of being in an intellectual rut. Storytellers are set firmly in a certain VtM comfort zone. Where there are always wheels within wheels, everyone is looking for an advantage, and will stoop to whatever low to get what they want for petty reasons. That mortals and mortal institutions must be lowercase-d dominated, in order to get a leg up over the vampire's rivals. With Vampire: The Masquerade, every NPC or faction is expected to do these things, to some degree, because that's the mode we've come to expect from vampires.

                                                Consider, then, what kind of stories could be told when you factor in a faction that does not do these things. Not because they're naive or pure, but because they actively choose a different path. If a Child of Osiris gets involved in the Jyhad, it's because of motivations completely different from other vampires. Nor do they use many of the most common tools and tactics, because they are evil. They instead employ tactics a normal vampire never would, for reasons a normal vampire would never consider. Partly this is out of necessity, because many avenues to get what they want are closed off to them by choice. But it also means the Children of Osiris have reason to become very good at using alternative means, because they're the only ones left. Even if they are less convenient or close off opportunities other vampires would relish.

                                                Osirans do not accumulate human herds, and thus have no human herds to attack. (They find it distasteful, and besides seek to overcome their reliance on human blood through Bardo 3). They don't seek to acquire normal Influence, because temporal power means little to them, and they have no interest in gaining power over mortals or other vampires. Jockeying for social position within the local Kindred hierarchy is meaningless to a sect that stands firmly outside such social structures and avoids even being known of whenever possible. They willing to make unlife harder for every vampire in the area (ex: attracting vampire hunters, improving conditions in the Rack, bringing attention to institutional corruption, etc), because they're used to deprivation and going it the hard way. If presented with the opportunity to claim an artifact or knowledge of great power for themselves, they're more likely to destroy it or spread it around, depending on the circumstances, if it means no one (or everyone) can benefit from it.

                                                All of this makes the Children of Osiris unpredictable to other vampires. In many ways a high Humanity vampire is constrained by their own ethics, in a way other vampires are not. In other ways, though, they are more free. They can affect the world around them by "attacking" from angles no "sane" vampire would consider an option. Either because it seems counterproductive, or because more expedient methods are available, or because they don't see the benefits of doing it. Some vampires would tie themselves up in mental knots, trying to figure out what the "game" is in a situation, because they cannot conceive of a vampire not acting the way every vampire should.

                                                The Children of Osiris have the advantage of not "making sense", because despite them being on Humanity, same as most vampires by default, they have a radically different way of thinking. They go against everything other Kindred value in the Jyhad.

                                                So when examining the Children of Osiris and their potential role in the game, we shouldn't ask how we can change them to fit into pre-existing molds. Instead, we should ask ourselves "What can the Children of Osiris do that other groups cannot or would not? And how do we leverage that into unique plot hooks?"

The nobility of the Children of Osiris comes from the constant, active refusal of the Beast's urges, and the exercise of control. It is in doing good, and not doing evil. The latter being contrary to the vampire's nature. There is no "being good". It's a lifestyle.

                                                        True, any vampire could maintain a high Humanity without Bardo. But that is, as I've described it before, Vampire Hard Mode. Maintaining Humanity is very difficult, and losing it very easy. It's not fair to judge a character's goodness by them never erring. Circumstances sometimes make it impossible to avoid breaking some tenet of Humanity, or else making the price of not doing so unreasonably high (up to and including both Final Death and A Fate Worse Than Death).

                                                        I do not, therefore, consider it a mark against the Children of Osiris that they practice an ascetic discipline (or Discipline) to help them not spiral into moral decay, Wassail, and Wight-dom. If anything, I would say the willingness to become a Child of Osiris - with all that this entails - is proof of their sincerity.
