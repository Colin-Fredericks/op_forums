    To me, the Giovanni strive to hard to keep up appearances and work too much in the open to want to have Revenants. Yeah, they actually engage in some despicable and degenerate things, but that by itself does not fit the Revenant theme. There needs to be more. The only example of Revenants that appear "normal" would be the Grimaldi, and as pointed out by some here don't fit the vibe of the rest of the Revenants, and there are practical issues mixing with mortals because of the aging thing.

    But I do understand why someone feels the Giovanni would also have Revenant families, or association with some would be fitting.

    As for the Rossellini Revenants being canon, this is an example of canon changing. They weren't originally Revenants. Canon is very inspiring and fun to talk about, but at this point it's like one person is talking about Golden Age Batman, another about Silver Age Batman, one about post-Crisis Batman, and so on. While they all have certain elements in common, they are actually 3 (actually more now with even more reboots) different Batmans with no canon between them. It's not like the Sherlock Holmes stories where we can easily say the Doyle stories constitutes canon, and no matter how enjoyable some of the imitators may be, they are pastiches and not canon stories. That's how I feel between 1e/2e and Revised and V5. All are official, but they are different versions of the core setting.

                    Good ideas. Booby traps and places where dangerous animals can be used to most advantage both fit very well.

                    I had not thought about swamps and wetlands before as a fitting home for the Bratovitch, but it makes a lot of sense now that I think about it. You can get a lot more use of out gators, venomous snakes, constrictors, and the like since they are mostly not seen in a swamp and there can be a lot of them, than you can from wildlife in other desolate areas like mountains or deserts because there's a lot less pumas and other large, dangerous predators. They'd have to rely more on domesticated animals. Though now that I have seen Tiger King on Netflix, I'm open to the possibilities that the Bratovitch run an unregulated exotic animal "zoo"/"animal rescue"/breeding program that gives them access to a lot of dangerous animals they control in other areas.

                    So there's probably some Bratovitch estates on the Gulf Coast and Florida. Since Miami is supposed to have been contested domain for the Sabbat, it makes sense for the Bratovitch to be found in the nearby Everglades. They can easily support the Sabbat there. I don't think the Sabbat has traditionally been found near New Orleans or the Gulf Coast, but maybe some place far from New Orleans there is an estate that can be used for nomadic Sabbat packs to use as a safe stopping point in their travels.                         Late to the party but I had some stuff I wanted to add:

                        Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                        I'm curious how people describe or detail Revenant homesteads, particularly the Grimaldi and Obertus.

                        We're told that Zantosa and Bratovitch live in family estates. Bratovitches live on around 20 with 10-20 members each. So there's around 300 Bratovitch's total. Zantosa live on "at least" 17 estates between 5-20 members. So maybe around 200 plus members. Large, isolated family estates easily allow these Revenants to do whatever they want outside the eyes of mortals.
                        Honestly I think the numbers were meant to be kept low but as with most things population wise in WoD it can depend on what sources you listen to. Its very much a YMMV thing so there could be more. I mean its not as if they didn't keep adding more Revenant families after the fact (indeed Revenants have gone through some revisions through different editions I've noticed.)

                        But the Grimaldi and Obertus are different and present some challenges for the ST.

                        Grimaldi are supposed to live among humanity, but as individuals or presumably nuclear family units. The problem is that revenants age slowly. Not an issue for adults, but children are supposed to take 50 years to reach adulthood. It's possible for people to interact with an adult for a long time without noticing they don't age normally. You can probably spend one to decades in the same place before moving on. But if children don't age, it'll be noticed quickly. That does not present a problem for Zantosa and Bratovitch who live isolated from humans, and can do various things to limit their interaction with them. Much harder for the Grimaldi. How do you handle this in the setting as the ST?

                        Obertus have another issue. They do seem to live in isolation from humanity, but instead of living on family estates they gather in villages and even govern themselves "normally" like having municipal elections and such. The issue here isn't hiding among humanity. They probably keep outsiders out of their communities and create any false documents as needed like birth and death certificates. The issue is given the small number of Revenants, the Obertus likely number in the same range as Bratvotiches and Zantosa. Let's be generous and give them 500. How many villages can they possibly have? We're told they can be found in New England, the Midwest, and Central America. Does that mean all Obertus live in a total of three villages - one in each of those regions - of around 150-170 members each? It's possible to have villages this size, but they are very rare. There wouldn't be many places of business or public buildings. You could still theoretically elect a village president or mayor, and even a council, but they wouldn't have a whole lot to do. We can make it work, but the greater issue is should there really only be three villages total to account for the entirety of this revenant family - let's call them Dunwich, Gatlin, and Mayincateca for convenience. I imagine in addition to being remote, they are also isolated by geography in someway to prevent outsiders from stumbling upon them by accident and making the region around them unattractive for development. As home to around one-third of the whole revenant family (as opposed to just a clan) you can't just pick up and flee. You have to make yourself a hard target. Is that how you see it in your chronicles?
                        I never saw it as a problem honestly but I think it could depend on how your story is set up. The key thing (in my mind) to remember is that most Revenant families are only kept around (apart from habit) but because they have highly specialized roles. Grimaldi have their connections to the mortal world (And money) and the Obertus have alot of esoteric knowledge and research abilities. Grimaldi can be 'private' schooled or even privately tutored (Ghouls: Fatal addiction mentions Revenants are never in public school of any kind for more than a few years at a time, so you could imagine they get shuffled around and anyone asks says they got sent to boarding school or something) but even with that Revenants tend to be 'insular' and 'clannish'. There's lots of ways to be standoffish without drawing attention even for Obertuses ('those old religious recluses/crazy cultists several miles from town way up on the hill' for example would suit Obertuses) G:FA also mentions sometimes that fleshcrafting may be used to 'disguise' Revenants which can also help.

                        I'd also imagine that any Tzimisce or Sabbat who cared enough can arrange for special 'private' schools that can help conceal Revenant's true natures or otherwise take steps to isolate them. Hell some Revenants like the Grimaldis probably can do this for themselves (or others) already.

                        Plus I've always figured any Revenant family will have not inconsiderable amounts of land and wealth. Even the Obertus need lots of land to store their records, libraries and whatnot and you have to pay the upkeep (and acquisition of new stuff) somehow. And since the rise of online schooling (or more recently virtual schooling) to complement 'traditional' excuses like home schooling or boarding schools there's lots of ways you can conceal the truth. And this assumes any one Revenant gets enough contact with mortal society for a long enough period for anyone to notice, which I kinda doubt. If you own a amount of land outside the city (and its walled off) nobody is going to get close enough to notice anything. And even close to urban areas you can have gated communities or walled off houses (usually again the wealthy) so its not THAT out of place to have people who aren't in regular contact with people. And Revenants who aren't Grimaldi aren't supposed to be normal/sane enough to interact with people anyhow so secrecy would be necessary regardless of age.

                        Originally posted by adambeyoncelowe View Post
                        You could have Revenants age more like Dhampirs, in that the slowed ageing only kicks in at puberty. It makes things a little easier.
                        Actually while Revenants age slowly even through childhood and puberty, Dhampir powers don't kick in until Puberty as per Time of Thin Blood

                        Originally posted by Time of Thin Blood
                        A dhampir's occult legacy usually doesn't manifest until the onset of puberty; in addition to all the usual tribulations of growing up, the child must face a second layer of transformation, far darker and infinitely more mysterious.

                        The process varies from individual to individual. Most simply become aware, sometime during their teens, of a special reserve of physical strength that they can call upon - instinctively at first, in times of stress.
                        Other dhampirs bloom later. For them, mystical dabbling or a sudden encounter with a vampire might be what it takes to spark their hidden potential.

                        In any case, once a dhampir is fully awakened to her supernatural essence, she can never go back. The Blood forever alters her physically and mentally. Her aging slows to a crawl (which can be a real social disadvantage when it happens in early puberty).
                        In other words, as Dr. Netchurch so astutely notes in Chapter One, she becomes biologically indistinguishable from a revenant.

                        (Note: Biologically is the all-important qualifier. In all other respects, dhampirs could not be more diffrent from the freakish Bratovitches, Zantosas, et al. Revenants have a culture of their own, a skewed outlook that they have developed over many centuries. Dhampirs nominally belong to the human world, however imperfect the fit may be.)
                        And the reference to Netchurch from chapter one:

                        It appears, therefore, that two processes may generate revenants. They may be “bred up” from ghouls. Alternatively, revenants may be “bred down” from vampires. For reasons I shall explain below, I believe this can happen only among vampires of the 15th Generation.

                        To prevent confusion, such offspring ought to have a distinctive name of their own. The Gypsies of the Balkan region have legends of such creatures; they call them dhampirs. Although these legends are surely mere coincidence — perhaps even the result of old disinformation to protect the Masquerade — I propose using this name for the living offspring of vampires. It is a name we shall need: Michael may well be the first dhampir, but as the thin-blooded proliferate, he will not be the last.
                        I should note that in both mechanics and lore while Dhampir and Revenants are similar they're not identical. Revenants can (technically) learn true magic, whereas a Dhampir can't ever do that (or be any other kind of hybrid. They're pretty damn explicit about this even if they're 'technically' like Revenants.) They also don't have 'clan' disciplines (they're more like Revenant Caitiff in that regard).

                        But the biggest difference is in genesis. As Netchurch notes you can 'breed down' from Thin Bloods or 'breed up' from ghouls. Additionally the former is a 'natural' result of the Curse of Caine whereas the Revenants were an artificially created 'mutation' of the Curse so naturally there will be differences (Dhampir's don't 'activate' until they reach a certain trigger point, whereas Revenants are 'activated' from the start because of how they were created.)

                        Honestly I kinda wish they managed things better than 'Mostly Revenants' but you get what you get (as opposed to say, the Shade Walkers in KotE which are fundamentally the same concept.)

                            Originally posted by Mister_Dunpeal View Post
                            I never saw it as a problem honestly but I think it could depend on how your story is set up.
                            By problem I meant that it was something STs need to take into consideration when they are presenting those families. Unlike the other Revenant families, Grimaldi don't live by themselves, but among humans. That present challenges because certain aspects of Revenants would reveal them to be non-humans. In my experience, this is often overlooked by STs so I want to emphasize it. Or if you do things that isolate them, it goes against what the Grimaldi are supposed to be like. Some may be inevitable - like special boarding school they send their "children" off to before they decide to integrate them in human society by sending them off to college (more for the purpose of networking since they likely don't need the actual education at that point). But most attempts of the Grimaldi to self-isolate would be self-defeating.

                            But now that you figure out the Grimaldi need a special boarding school to keep the "kids" away from humans (given their small numbers, I think there should be only one), now you have something you can put in your game world and maybe it becomes a cool element in your chronicles. Maybe even the Zantosa sometimes send their kids to there, and that's one reason for these families rivalries. The Zantosa on their estates don't need such a school, but maybe it can provide some benefits to help them socialize and learn so they're better prepared when they do walk among the humans. It's ideas like that STs can use to improve their games as they start to inspire more things.

                            In terms of the Obertus, they don't have the problems of the Grimaldi since they are isolated. But their low numbers necessitates that there only be a small number of their towns/villages. You just can't place them anywhere if it seems there are only 3 such communities in the whole world. There are just certain things that don't seem obvious until you begin to figure out the details. There's a difference between "they can be found in New England" and "Haverhill, MA is one of only three Obertus communities in the world." As you said, there is an implied population increase in most supernatural creatures since once informed of any group, STs and PCs begin using those concepts and creating their own details. I don't think there's any problem deviating from canon, but I prefer such deviations to be an active design decision by the ST rather than them just being ignorant (which given the sheer number of sourcebooks now, some of which are contradictory can't be helped).

                            Originally posted by Mister_Dunpeal View Post
                            I think like most things, the 'design' of Revenants changed or evolved over time.
                            Definitely. I've said before on the forum that at this point, we have three separate canons of the WoD like the old Earth-1, Earth-2, etc. of DC Comics. Although all based on the same core material, the Greenberg 1e/2e VtM, the Revised metaplot of Achilli, and now V5 just have too many retcons and changes to be one continuity anymore. And the Revenants are not immune to this. That 2e statement about 4 extra families that entered the Sabbat and were destroyed seems to be a 2e element ignored, forgotten, or changed in the Dark Ages/Revised era. I'm sure when V5 delves more into the Sabbat, we will "learn" more things about the Revenant families that contradict earlier books.

                            In the end, every ST will be deciding what is to their taste and cobbling things together that best match that while cutting off the bits that don't. I do think a thread like this helps identify various aspects and helps people think what it is they do like.
