    What's your vision of Humanity?

    05-17-2023, 04:04 PM
    In the sense of how harsh are you with asking for Conscience checks to not lose Humanity. I know it varies a lot from one Storyteller to another, I know some that consider that at certain low levels of Humanity, not even diablerie counts as a sin, what I think is problematic. This thread originated mostly because of the book Lair of the Hidden. It's one of the books I like the most in Revised era and I have read it many times and always find myself coming back to it, there's one thing always bothers me. It's the fact that the leaders of the Inconnu, the Twelve in the case, all take part in a ritual that involves the sacrifice of humans and yet most of them have somewhat high levels of Humanity on their character sheets. Even though I usually refrain myself from the more manichaeistic visions of the world, both in real life and certainly even more so in the WoD, I think that the fact the Inconnu accepts the idea of practicing something that is regarded as one of the most monstrous things someone can do and still retain a somewhat high level of humanity is startling.

    So, I ask here again in search of perhaps finding some different views, how do you treat Humanity and Sin in your games?

      I generally find most players have a sweet spot of where they want their PC's Humanity to be (usually between 4-7). That is either based on their favored roleplay style, or what they think their Humanity needs to be in order to accomplish their goals (unscrupulous empire builders tend towards the lower Humanity ranks). Going below that rank matters to them. They don't care if they start higher and descend to it.

      So regardless of where their Humanity starts out as, I ask the players before the chronicle begins if they 1) want to roll their Conscience every time they violate things - knowing they're driving their character to a lower Humanity anyway - as they like roll playing out remorse, 2) if they want to concede the loss of Humanity (and thus make no dice rolls) after certain kinds of violations because such roleplay only gets in their way of the kind of character they want to play, or 3) do they simply want to start at a lower of Humanity they are more comfortable with?

      It is usually some 2 or 3 or some combination. Only those players who want to try playing a moral character are fine with going with Home

      In my experience, most players only want to make Conscience rolls and feel remorse for their actions if that is something they want to roleplay for that specific PC. That may be because they want to play a moral character struggling with their vampirism, or because it finally involves actions they want to be rare and want to prevent future Humanity loss. Players of unscrupulous businessmen may care about planned murder or even impassioned violence or killing in self-defense and want to roll when that happens, but don't care about theft, extortion, property damage, threats, etc. to build their financial empire. Rolling Conscience and having to roleplay out remorse in those conditions just isn't fun or worthwhile to them. They'd rather just be a ruthless bastard and take the hit, an reserve those rolls for when it matters to them.

      The issue is whether they think making rolls will disrupt or slow down the game, or if they feel their ruthless businessman feeling remorse for ruining a business rival would be false. They only want to make the rolls when it matters for them. So reducing the number of rolls until it reaches territory that really matters for them, has been an idea they have welcomed.

      So we try to only roll when it truly matters for the characters (and that is different for each PC), and reduce the total number of dice rolling in general.

      In terms of Humanity (or Path ratings) of NPCs, this has always been something that VtM sourcebooks struggle with. Character descriptions often don't match their Path ratings. Revised did seem to do a better job overall than 1e/2e (CbN's Humanity ranks are notoriously non-sensical), but they still have plenty of misfires. Either adjust accordingly, or figure out a way to justify it that makes sense to you.

          Humanity is one of those things that you almost have to go off of the individual character rather than a default mechanic.

          I have had games where one PC is more or less an anime vampire idol singer, whose idea of betrayal is being mean to her fans or cutting her loyal agent out of a bonus because he is blood bonded and wouldn't complain. On the other hand in the same group was a noire style detective character who was inspired by old Sam Spade bogart movies but leaned heavily on the max payne body count method of problem solving. Both were on the path of humanity, but they had wildly different priorities when it came to what was important when it came to what constituted a line they would not cross.

          Just looking at the V20 path charts, most of the players I gamed with didn't fit the humanity path IRL, their characters only went further off the beaten track as far as morality went.

          I usually sit down with the players and get a sense of the morality zone they are wanting the character to work within, during play I keep them inside that framework, but will work with them when they want to take the character in a different direction or feel the character needs an emotional arc of sorts. Yes this turns the game mechanic from something that is a dice roll to a Player/ST collaboration but that tended to work out better for my group's needs.

              To be fair, there's no technical or lore issue with the twelve having high humanity while sacrificing a human per year for centuries.

              Firstly because doing a sin against humanity doesn't immediatly lower it. You have to fail at feeling remorse.

              Plus, even when they would fail to feel remorse and hence lose some humanity, it can be regained. Which in universe means one seeks to be better. And as the twelve desires to walk saulot's path, they will rise their humanity.


              In game terms, this would manifest as the twelve generally succeeding their conscience roll each year, or they regain their lost humanity point during said year with obsessive introspection and study of saulot's way.


              I had a character like that once. An assamite whobwas a hitman in life. Desiring to leave behind his murderous ways, but trapped by his duties as assassin for his sire and clan.

              And yet, he was quasi perpetually at humanity 6 or above. Because he had a high conscience rating (which I rationalized as representing his deep desire to be better), so he often kept his humanity's ratting despite cold blood murders most of the time by feeling remorse, and when he did lose a point, I bought it back with XP, and justified it in game as him being horrified at his lack of feelings for a specific assassination target, hence going on an introspection.


              Part of the misunderstansding, I think, is that people sees humanity as a mesure of one's actions, while it's in fact a mesure of one's reaction to theirs actions.

                                  I mostly run Sabbat games, so I have very different view of Humanity. In my games, Humanity is effectively a disease that is ultimately fatal if not cured. Humanity makes one miserable, even suicidal, if it is effectively maintained. Moreover, it is almost impossible to maintain, and the doomed attempt will ultimately result in you becoming a wight. So Humanity is a lose-lose proposition. Maintain it and become suicidal, attempt to maintain it but fail and become a wight.

                                  I generally emphasize this by having Cammies look like late-stage cancer victims when my players infiltrate Camarilla cities. Camarilla neonates especially are miserable creatures. They are generally very close to becoming wights because their cowardly elders make them do all the dirty work, but don't teach them the Paths of Enlightenment needed to avoid wightdom when routinely doing that dirty work.

                                  This really drives home the importance of converting Cammy neonates to the Sabbat, and thereby saving them from the cancer of Humanity.

                                  In the context of your original question, that means I treat Humanity very harshly. It's largely implicit because the players either start without Humanity, or only have it during the period when they are training in a Path of Enlightenment, so Humanity is largely an NPC thing in my games. But the very fact that most Cammy NPCs are very close to becoming wights implicitly means Humanity is extremely hard to maintain while doing the sort of things a vampire needs to do to survive.

                                      Homebrew (without any sort of playtesting whatsoever)!

                                      No Humanity/Road/Path ratings. You instead have three Virtues rated 1 to 5; Courage, Prudence, Temperance. When you do something shitty that runs afoul of one of those, or basic human decency as your human self would’ve judged, roll a dice pool made of that Virtue plus another of your choice (for the latter, two of your best). Success means you walk away this time. Failure means that you end up with something like a Stain, or a Flaw, etc. That’s basic, default Humanity. A Road of Humanity empoweres one of your Virtues and gives you “Do” tenets that provide bonuses if your shitty act was made in their pursuit or otherwise reinforces them. A Path of Enlightenment erases one of your Virtues and saddles you with “Do Not” tenets (that you now only have two Virtues to roll against), but saves you from everything that don’t expressly and grossly go against those tenets, and upon initiation also sears away all prior accumulated Flaws/Stains/etc.

                                                  When I'm the one doing the storytelling Humanity is not about morality or acting like an actual human being. It is, like all paths, a philosophy of self-discipline meant to keep the Beast from consuming you. Humanity in particular does this through denial; the Beast prioritises preserving a vampire's existence with no regard for the wellbeing of others, so Humanity focusues on the opposite and prioritises the wellbeing of others over oneself.

                                                  Again, morality is not part of this. Dangerous criminals, people you consider evil or deserving of harm, other vampires, the absolute scum of the Earth; all will trigger a conscience roll for murdering and robbing them. One with mitigated difficulty, perhaps, but a roll nonetheless.

                                                  Nor must it be about humans. It's possible for a high humanity vampire to deem vampires more important than mortals - as mortals do with themselves and animals - and prioritise the wellbeing of their kindred over the kine, who they approach with more of an animal welfare mindset (animal cruelty can also trigger rolls). In this case the name humanity is thought to refer to emulating humans as cooperative, group-forming social animals rather than acting as the asocial solitary predator vampires are by nature.


                                                  The sins of "violation" refer to manipulating others to serve your wants and needs at the expense of theirs, as well as killing.
                                                  (So; ghouling, blackmail, gaslighting, embracing a childe without consent, feeding without knowledge and consent, most uses of disciplines such as dominate and presence and things like that)

                                                  Accidental violation can refer to performing these acts as the lesser evil, where you didn't come out here today intending to do this, but it ended up happening.

                                                  In play, humanity goes like this:
                                                  Step 1) Player violates the Path of Humanity

                                                  Step 2) I observe how the player responds in character, emotionally and in reaction. If they don't show remorse or make any attempts at amends then I just remove humanity automatically. If they do move in to the next step.

                                                  Step 3) Difficulty is judged by severity of damage done, the circumstances and their response to the violation. If the action taken was the lesser evil available then the roll is less harsh. Simply being upset but nothing more means a higher difficulty than actively trying to repair the damage done.*

                                                  Step 4) Roll conscience. I don't tell players the result or what their humanity is, I simply give them a feel for how close wassail is through narration and frenzy rolls, which became more frequent and harder to reign in as humanity drops**.

                                                  * If the player tries to make amends at a later point in the game I will reroll with appropriate difficulty. If they succeed I restore the lost humanity.

                                                  ** Homerule: the difficulty for frenzy rolls is your path rating. So at humanity 10 you'd only frenzy if you botched, at humanity 1 you need 10s to succeed.
                                                  Frequency is just a matter of judgement. If you light a cigarette in front of a humanity 10-6 vamp they'll just feel uncomfortable and avert their gaze. 5-4 are usually fine, but if it's been a really bad night I'll call for a roll. A vampire between 3-1 will flip their shit.
