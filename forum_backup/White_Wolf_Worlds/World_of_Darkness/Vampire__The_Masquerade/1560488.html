    How Did The Sabbat Exist?

    06-09-2026, 02:57 PM
    A thought has just occurred to me regarding the Sabbat's actions, and how they synergize with the Paths of Enlightenment.

    Upon retrospect, I realize that nearly all PoE's forbid the act casual murder, while the ones who actually do permit it demand that you must have a higher purpose beyond simple desire.

    So, how is it that the sect of blood crazed, vampiric berserkers, who bury people in mass graves and commit every sort of atrocity you can imagine are able to get away with these kinds of acts?

    Even among those who have adopted a PoE, shouldn't the whole sect consist of Wights by this point?

      So the thing is, most Sabbat vampires were just very low humanity. In a given pack, in fact probably less than 25% of Sabbat members were on a Path. Some clans were more likely to have members on Paths, for example Tzimisce, Lasombra, Followers of Skulls, were more likely to be on Paths, but then other clans like the Brujah, Gangrel, Nosferatu, Malkavians, etc, most of those members were just low humanity. Giving up your humanity and embracing a Path is actually really difficult.

      Still compared to the number of Camarilla members on Paths of Enlightenment, the Sabbat absolutely had a heck of a lot more. Ultimately though the Paths were notable but they weren't a defining aspect of Sabbat existence until 5E where it seems that most (all?) Sabbat vampires are now on a Path. It's also worth noting that for many Paths, killing is perfectly okay. The Path member may need a reason to do so, but for most Sabbat, killing as social activity to increase the bonds with their pact mates or sect would be reason enough.

      Still though, yeah, most Sabbat vampires were fairly low humanity or low Path rating and hovering close to wrightdom. It's just what happens when you have a sect whose members choose to frequently feed the Beast.

              Let's look at some of the more popular Sabbat Paths and see where they'd be. I'll be using the Revised era Paths of The Guide to the Sabbat. I know other editions have different Hierarchy of Sins.

              Path of Caine - I don't see any mention about not killing mortals. So that's an easy Path for the routine mass murdering Sabbat, and we know this is a popular Path for the sect.

              Path of Cathari - Prohibitions on "arbitrary killing" at level 2 (quite serious) and "impassioned killing" at level 6. If we excuse the killings done as part of the Auctoritas Ritae (like Blood Bath, Blood Beast, and Palla Grande) as not arbitrary, most followers can probably be at normal Path levels as Camarilla vampires on Humanity. They'd just need to avoid routine "needless" killing of mortals the rest of the time.

              Path of Death and the Soul - "Failing to kill when useful" (Useful meaning useful to one's study of death) is a level 6 sin. "Showing compassion" is a level 3 sin. "Killing without studying death afterward" is a level 2 sin. This is probably where the difficulty comes from as someone attempting to "study" at a Blood Feast or during a Sabbat murder hobo crusade is probably hard to do. "Needlessly preventing a death" is a level 1 sin. This path doesn't have a problem with killing mortals. The real complication is the need to "study" it afterwards. This will create some odd behavior, but should not present too much a trouble. It's just more likely a Path adherent will prefer isolated "intimate" murders where she can study the effects rather than killing sprees.

              Path of Evil Revelations - There's no conflicts here at all.

              Path of the Feral Heart - This Path is one that will have some trouble. Being "overly cruel" is a level 7 sin. "Killing without need" is a level 4 sin. "Killing a creature other than for survival" is a level 2 sin. Cainites on this Path will definitely have problems. This is the meaner version of the Path of Harmony of first edition. That edition made clear most Path adherents were Country Gangrel, many of whom were of Native American stock, who were Loyalists and nomad packs. These Sabbat were probably politically important in early Sabbat history when first arriving in North America, but were probably the big losers of the Sabbat Civil Wars as the Lasombra and Tzimisce (and others) became coven based packs in cities. In a Sabbat chronicle, the followers of this Path probably have little to do with most of the sect except for some old vestigial loyalties (probably due to the vaulderie). Instead, they probably just hang out with other members of their Path and interpret ritae like the Blood Feast very loosely. They are the Sabbat one could most easily justifying defecting from the Sabbat en masse, perhaps joining the anarchs or even the Camarilla. There's a reason Revised edition purged most of these when they eliminated much of the diversity within the sect in order to turn it into the Sword of Caine masturbatory fantasy of the Ultra-conservatives.

              Path of Honorable Accord - "Killing without reason" is a level 2 sin. Again, the Auctoritas Ritae probably provide a "reason" for killing so the knights can participate without lowering their Path ratings, but they are probably the least murderous Path outside of Feral Heart/Harmony. As long as they mostly hang out in their own packs, they can probably get by. Being in packs with the muder hobos would probably hurt their path ratings immensely.

              Path of Lilith - I'll note this is not actually a Sabbat path. The original version comes from Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand which is about the Tal'mahe'Ra. Lilith worshippers won't do well in a sect devoted to Caine as the followers of the Dark Mother despise Caine. "Killing a living or unliving being" is a level 4 sin. The Bahari will do well outside the Sabbat.

              Path of Power and the Inner Voice - A classic Sabbat path, and one that many (most?) of its leaders follow. No prohibitions against murder here. "Failing to kill when it's in your interests" is a level 5 sin. While individual unifiers may not kill a lot, there's not much problem if they do. They're just more likely to kill for a reason.

              Path of Metamorphosis - This is pulled from the Revised Vampire: the Masquerade Corebook. This is another dark Path. No prohibitions against killing. Instead "Refusing to kill when knowledge may be gained from it" is a level 6 sin. "Considering the needs of others" is a level 4 sin. "Exhibiying compassion for others" is a level 1 sin. These vampires are sick.

              So there's actually not much problem here.

              The Paths of the Feral Heart and Honorable Accord are the only ones who likely would a problem with most stereotypical Sabbat behavior. Followers of these paths probably do their best to stay away from most other Sabbat. If we assume Feral Heart/Harmony followers are those Native American Country Gangrel in wilderness nomad packs, they are effectively their own sect with limited ties to other Sabbat. Honorable Accord presents much more of a challenge. They'd probably have been destroyed long ago except for their fanatical loyalty to the Code of Milan. I imagine they mostly hang out with themselves and avoid other Sabbat except as part of the auctoritas ritae or their duties as paladins/templars, inquisitors, or their own packs. I imagine they recruit other newly Sabbat whenever they see a new one with potential and quickly try to recruit one.

              The Paths of Cathari and Power and the Inner Voice are probably only so-so as murder hobos. They likely won't engage in routine murder, but have plentiful of meaningful homicides. They probably work well with the above paths as they know they may need help at some time from the real sickos. However, they can usually fit in well with them.

              The Paths of Caine, Death and the Soul, Evil Revelation, and Metamorphosis are probably where most of the murder hobos come from. Nothing really stops them from killing at any time, and many will justify killing just in order to not sin against their Paths.

              I imagine early on, Sabbat who were on Paths were mostly those on more humane paths. But as the sect grew to identify themselves with radical notions of freedom, it encouraged the morally worst shitbags to justify their awful behavior in the guise of twisted Paths, and they gradually took over the sect.

                  I don't think you can seriously look at the Sabbat and it's functional viability and ignore the Viniculum's impact on its members. While the Sabbat likes to claim the Vaulderie frees its members from the tyranny of Blood Bonds... it does so simply by replacing the power of one vampire to exert radical control on another by forcing a weaker level of influence spread out among the rest of the local Sabbat. Unless a pack falls apart very quickly, holding a Vaulderie as often as the book indicates most packs do means the majority of members of a pack are going to be at 7+ Viniculum ratings towards the other pack members. So there is a lot of supernatural peer pressure to interpret Paths in a fashion that lets Sabbat members cooperate.

                  Since Feral Heart and Honorable Accord got singled out above me, Feral Heart doesn't negate the idea of "survival" being added by working with your pack and thus killing for survival can mean killing for the good of the pack instead of just for immediate sustenance. "For the good of my pack" is as viable as a reason for a Knight to do pretty much anything that might otherwise cause problems for their Path rating.

                  This is also a reason I don't like just looking at the sin lists. The Paths have deeper write-ups - and especially the Ethics lists - that help put those charts in context so STs and players can judge what should and shouldn't count. Feral Heart and Honorable Accord both heavily stress the importance of group cohesion (Feral Heart is more pack focused, Honorable Accord more institution focused) in how they're actually written. Adding that context in addition to the Viniculum means they're not nearly as hard to have in packs as they can seem if you just look at the sin lists. Both Paths also have a pretty big leeway between "You as a Path follower shouldn't do this," and, "You as a Path follower need to stop others from doing this." Beyond those two Paths, the Paths most associated with the Sabbat tend towards personal actions over demands on the group. There's very little that can't be solved by, "whatever floats your boat as long as you don't force me to participate," when it comes to balancing group dynamics when the Paths are involved.

                                      Part of the problem is that the Sabbat has never really been consistent in what it is. By Revised and thus in V20 it's taken on what's probably its most common iterating in use (because it's actually a functional organization at that point, even if still limited by avoiding too many additional retcons), but even in 1e the Sabbat was being adjusted in what it's purpose in the setting really was: from ultra-evil vampires who were both psychotic killers and somehow a functional group, to champions of the inhumane but still driven by a coherent and understandable if alien ideology. The later part of 1e, throughout the entire runs of both 2e and Rev, there was an ongoing process in trying to explain why the ultra-evil version of them could exist at least in some areas and times, but the organization was something that could rationally exist in the larger setting. Leading to contradictions and retcons to address them.

                                      You can see the influence of that development process on how Requiem instituted it's three many antagonists to its vampires: Belial's Brood (groups of seemingly young vampires punching way above their weight class doing lots of crazy evil things while somehow being cockroaches that keep coming back no matter how many you kill off and how little sense it makes for them to function beyond a small group level), the VII (purposefully vague ideological fanatics that have it in for the normal vampires for some reason), and the Strix (something deeply connected to vampires, but not truly vampires themselves, that represent a constant boogeyman to the kindred). One group becomes unwieldy when trying to embody so many setting functions at once, so Requiem went with the approach of dividing the narrative rules up between more antagonist factions.

                                      But VtM (well at least pre-V5) worked to keep all three concepts in one group. That's why trying to treat the Sabbat as a whole as filling one of those roles doesn't really work out; even if you're keeping to the early material. The Sabbat was iterated over time to be an institution that could have individual groups within it to be the specific Sabbat you want encountered in your game, even if they're not the whole group; letting the institution be more functional and coherent.
