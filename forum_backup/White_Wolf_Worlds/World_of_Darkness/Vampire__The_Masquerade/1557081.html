      Best you can do is have an open and honest conversation with your GM and fellow players about that you want to play a Salubri and would like it to not be a death sentence for doing so. But that's good policy for anything. Try to find a group you can have conversations like that about gameplay expectations and see what terms you can come to. There's nothing wrong with wanting to play one, or certainly at least exploring with your group if they would be cool with that and not instantly harsh on it once gameplay starts.

      You can also totally point to that the game, despite having made the clan drawbacks worse than previous editions and some other measures towards unplayability, narratively or otherwise, is now totally treating Salubri pcs like an entirely viable option. (The rock star book suggested with a straight face in a preview that going around with a scarf or hat essentially all the time will totally cover things in a fine and plausible way for the people around you, even to the point of maintaining an active mortal identity, so hey, there you are)

      I am being somewhat glib in the second paragraph only for having just spent a long post in another thread contrasting current V5 with earlier V5, just go with paragraph 1 there.

          Keep your team well fed - so they would not want to eat you
          Or try to convince your GM to let go that idiotic bane of flowing blood from your eye when using power...

          As for actualyl how to play them?
          Well even in V5 the two caste basically exist

          Healers - wise advisors, more often than not unwanted "saviors of the damned"
          Warriors - Avangers and Demon Hunters

          Take a pick and embrace it with an (un)healthy hollier than thou behavior or victimization
          Play into their tragic legacy, wisdom, and the struggle to prove the propanagda was lie, you are not a soul devourer monster but a very useful part of your team as a spiritual guide or a divine punisher, depending on your chosen path, using your powers to mend or break others.

                          Well, you can make any clan in any sect.
                          Just because they're not common or well loved within a sect doesn't mean they aren't there.
                          A Camarilla Salubri - you - could be under the protection of the local Prince, either because the Prince dislikes the Tremere or likes the Salubri or - of course - for politics.
                          This could save your ass for a while
                          Now on a street level?
                          The "Hunted" Bane is twofold - first, other Kindred find Salubri blood incredibly addictive, triggering a Hunger Frenzy if they drink too much and fail a test
                          Solution?
                          Don't let them taste you
                          The second part is that cursed 3rd Eye.... their third eye weeps vitae when using Disciplines, causing Hunger Frenzy tests for nearby vampires
                          Solution?
                          Keep them well fed
                          Really, you are their shepherd in a sense, care about them, always make sure their tummy is full so they not immediately jumps on you because ofthe hunger frenzy

                          Yes, you are a Salubri
                          Yes, you have a Bane that is pain in the ass
                          But no, not everyone wants to eat you, even if you sell your blood as a delicacy in little vials and they basically got addicted to your blood, they not automatically urged to eat you
                          The Bane has caviats

                          You have to carefully balance your usefulness to the Sect/Coterie with your Bane and Compulsion
                          But be a Healer or a Warrior a Salubri is very usefull to have around - you can heal mortal and kindred alike and even treat their souls not just their bodies

                          Now since you will be a fledgeling, a newly embraced Salubri this will be lessened usefullness, but you can balance it out with the right backgrounds, contact, inluence, herd
                          Regradless the sect and your coterie you could be the tie to the human world both matphisically and phisically

                          As a very emphatic doctor who became an Unicorn you would have the advantage of the background knwoledge you accumulated as a drcotor and your empathy could help to reach out and talk your ass off - be their therapist, or simply just someone who listen and understands them. - Don't under estimate the value of that!
                          Especially If the others will be young vampires you could easly establish yourself as their strong rock early on in the coterie , the one they can rely, the one they can count on and gather so many boons and favors or heck, build real friendships it would keep you alive

                          The "everyone wants to eat the Salubri" is a gross exaggeration and with a little roleplay and a little planing you can easly could mitigate the negative effects and only care about the potential masquarade breach because of your third eye and the fact you are... compassionate
                          But lucky for you, you are already and empathic doctor, so the Affective Empathy compulsion (Overwhelmed with empathy for a personal problem of someone else, any action not taken to help the person mitigate their suffering is at a two-dice penalty. This Compulsion continues until the sufferer's burden is eased, a more critical problem arises, or the scene ends) basically already built in your nature and you will not even notice it is there
                          Because you care , it is in your nature

                          Now if the Hunted Bane is that a problem for you Salubri can have a Variant Bane called: Asceticism (Whenever their Hunger is below three, the Salubri suffer a penalty equal to their Bane Severity to any Discipline dice pools. The bleeding third eye still remains - but as I said, that could be neatly kept under controll by providing food to your friends *cough* Herd *cough* and keeping them well fed)

                                  And it was actually a fitting one and paired well with the Salubri - their Weakness was after all was their incapability to feeding on an unwilling target.

                                  In one incarnation
                                  The Salubri had more version of their weakness than the times they tried to "fix" the Ravnos ... yet... all of them were thematically fitting

                                  The Players Guide (1991)
                                  Whenever one of the Salubri takes blood from someone who resist the Kiss, the Salubri loses one Health Level for every Blood Point taken. It must be healed normaly (with blood). The Vampire must know the target is not resisteing and is at peace in order to avoid the damage.
                                  Additionally, the character must continually persevere towards Golconda; and straying from this Path will lead to dire consequences (like the inability to regain Willpower). Whne Golconda is finally reached, an end must come to this exitence when a successor is ready.

                                  Vampire Storytellers Companion (1998)
                                  Salubri may take Blood only from those who give it willingly. If a Salubri's vessel resists her attempt to feed, the Salubri loses a point from her Willpower pool and may have to check for Humanity degeneration at the Storyteller's discretion
                                  All Salubri must take five dots worth of Generation

                                  Players Guide to the Sabbat
                                  The Furies clan weakness is slightly different from that of the main Salubri; they receive nourishment from blood taken either by force or during a Frenzy.
                                  In addition, Salubri antitribu cannot start lower than Tenth Generation or higher than Twelfth

                                  DA Companion:
                                  The status as a haunted Clan makes them dangerous companion. Many vampires refuse to shelter a Salubri in fear of the Tremere; the Usupers are proving to be dangerous enemies. A Warrior Salubri cannot refuse to aid someone in need; if she does not help someone in need, she will be down two dice in any rolls he makes untill he makes restitution. A Healer Salubri who injures another Will be down two dice in any rolls fro the rest of the night, to reflect how badly he been shaken by the experience

                                  DA Story Tellers Companion
                                  A Warrior Salubri cannot refuse to aid someone in need without paying a stiff price; if she does refuse, reduce all dice pools by two to refelect how badly the character been shaken by the experience. This penalty ends when she made some sort of restitution.
                                  The Healer's roll is to mend body and spirit, and he may not do harm to another. If he injures someone, even in self-defense, the player loses two dice from all die pools or the rest of the scene.

                                  No Salubri starts higher then 10th Generation, due to a desire to keep the blood Strong and "pure"; two points must be placed in the Generation Background. Most Salubri also starts with a dot in Linguistics fo reflect their knowledge of the language of the Second City (Enochian). which all Salubri learn in order to keep correspondance and conversations private.

                                  Nkulu Zao (Kindred of the Ebony Kingdom (2003))
                                  The Nkulu Zao may only take blood only from those who willingly give it to them. If the vessel resists the feeding, the Nkulu Zao loses a point from her Willpower pool in additon to drawing no sustenance from the vitate itself. Additionally, the character might need to chechk for degeneration at the Storyteller's discretion.
                                  Unlike their Western brethren, the Nkulu Zao have all but lost their distinctive, third eye. Some still posses a functional one, but many of the Laibon's have healed over. Whether that means they have lost sight of their purpose, no one knows for certain [as a side note, they have Obeah and the discipline Works perfectly fine for them even without a third eye]

                                  al-Amin (Veil of Night)
                                  A Warrior al-Amin cannot refuse to aid someone in genuine need. If she does, the players loses two dice from all her dice pools until she makes restitution for her negligence in some way.
                                  A Healer al-Amin's roll is to mend body and spirit, not to do harm. If he injures someone, even in self-defensem the player loses two dice from all die pools or the rest of the night​

                                  In Players Guide to the High Clans:
                                  Both warrior and healer Salubri are bound against doing harm, although in dif-ferent ways. A warrior Salubri who refuses to help someone is at –2 dice to all pools until restitution is made. A healer Salubri who injures anyone is at –2 dice for the rest of the scene.

                                  In V20:
                                  Salubri have difficulty feeding on unwill- ing vessels. If a Cyclops attempts it, she loses a point of Willpower.
                                  Or for the Antitribu
                                  Unlike their nonviolent brethren, Sabbat Salubri must consume blood taken by force, preferably in the heat of battle. Unless the vampire feeds on blood from a fallen foe or fights his target before feeding, the Vitae offers no nourishment. In addition, Salubri antitribu cannot start lower than Tenth Generation or higher than Twelfth, as their blood has yet to spread across a wider spectrum than that.

                                  In DAV20:
                                  Salubri Healers must succeed on a Willpower roll (difficulty 6) if they attempt to feed on the unwilling; if they fail, they must find a willing vessel or suffer a two-die penalty on all rolls for the remainder of the night. Victims enthralled by Presence count as willing vessels.

                                  Salubri Warriors must test their strength and establish martial dominance; they must succeed on a Willpower roll (difficulty 6) if they attempt to feed on someone whom they have not vanquished in a fight, or suffer a two-die penalty on all rolls for the rest of the night. This may be defined as a duel to the death, or a simple brawl to Incapacitated. A few Warrior Herds comprise of companions who’ve been beaten down in the training yard. (This one, again echoed in the Antitribu's flaw, fitting)


                                  Wu Zao Wind from the East
                                  Zao-lat’s ancient imperative to uncover and gather secret knowledge runs through the Wu Zao’s blood. Every player must choose an area of arcane lore with which her character is obsessed; this is the focus of her unlife. A Willpower roll (diffi- culty 6) is required to resist portunity - regardless of the risk. Possible areas of research include the teachings of the Ten Thousand Demons, the lands of the dead, the secrets of a specific mortal culture or a group of texts.

                                  Wu Zao V20
                                  The Wu Zao are scholars by nature, and each of them has a special focus. At character creation, the player must select an area of arcane study — the Wan Kuei, the culture of a specific mortal group, a group of mystical tomes lost to the ages, etc. Whenever the opportunity arises to learn something about this subject, the player must make a Willpower roll (dif- ficulty 6) to avoid pursuing that lead to the exclusion of all else.

                                  Wu Zao DAV20
                                  Ironically, Watchers can be remarkably short-sighted, having a tendency to fixate on their preferred small details and ignore the larger conceptual picture. Watchers must choose a field of study narrow enough for a Knowledge specialty, such as the Ten Thousand Demons, a specific mortal culture, or the esoterics of Necromancy. Watchers must succeed on a Willpower roll (difficulty 6) to pass up an opportunity (as defined by the Storyteller) to acquire or study knowledge of their field, or lose two dice from all pools for the remainder of the night.​​​
