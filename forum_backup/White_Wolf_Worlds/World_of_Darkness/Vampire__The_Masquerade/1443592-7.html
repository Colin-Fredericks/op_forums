                                        Something I like to use for that sort of thing: "fluff" vs. "lore."

                                        "Fluff" is extraneous. It might be interesting, it might be well written, it might be fun, but it doesn't add anything to either help you play the game, or expand your idea of what the game can do. Fluff is frequently associated with purple prose, but fluff can be tightly worded too... you just tend to notice it less when it's like that.

                                        "Lore" is in the range of useful to essential information about the setting that is vital for communicating what playing the game is about, and what the characters can do.

                                        There is a level of subjectivity to the assignments here, because there's a very big grey area around different people finding different things useful for getting them into the setting and playloop of the game.

                                        That said, it isn't too hard, usually, to check your biases on this. I don't particularly like city books. I have run two games in 25 years of GMing (not just WoD) where I used cities/etc. fleshed out in a book, adventure module, etc. Even for very well established settings, like Star Wars, where players might visit one of the places featured and highlighted in SW media, I tend to put the focus on new places (both to give the players something fresh to deal with, and to satisfy my preferences). I can still acknowledge that a lot of stuff in city books is still solidly in the "lore" category because lots of people enjoy using premade locations either straight from the box or as inspiration for how to create their own

                                        Fluff and lore are also usually mixed together, making it hard to tease out sometimes. The Ashirra in V5 books so far is a mix. There's lore there, but also there's a lot of writing that doesn't help you really understand how to play an Ashirra character, or how to include them as a ST outside of them being an exotic darker skin toned mostly the same but not exactly Camarilla..

                                                Originally posted by Perisa View Post
                                                How to integrate twelve touchstones without bogging down the story and leaving other players with nothing to do?
                                                I've been writing my own game system for a few years now (why so long? Dayjob and an insistence on playtesting to get things right) and Touchstones is like one of many concepts I started out with that sounded great on paper, but ran headfirst into reality like it was a brick wall once it was actually handed over to my play-testers.

                                                Its one thing if every PC had ONE touchstone and/or you could also pick things that aren't NPCs to be your touchstone (ex. your childhood home, an organization you belonged to in life) because then you could keep the numbers manageable. But three touchstone NPCs per PC, in addition to other merit/background NPCs a PC might take just creates a mass of NPCs over and above the ones the GM needs for the basic city power struggles (a prince, a sheriff, probably some primogen, a few higher up-and-comers looking to replace one of the above, etc.) just turns into a massive amount of juggling...

                                                ...and that's before you consider how absolutely lethal V5's combat engine is and that every time you introduce a replacement PC because a previous one was ganked the GM has to completely redo the relationship chart with three MORE touchstone NPCs and associated background/merit NPCs.

                                                Frankly, V5 doesn't feel like it was designed to be played with more than two (three at most) PCs with combat as something exceedingly rare so as to avoid regular PC deaths. The relationship charts just get too complex even at four PCs.

                                                I can't even conceive of trying to manage all the NPCs necessary for the largest table I ever ran... 15 players. It was enough that plots mostly became self-generating so I mostly just had to be witness when checks of serious consequence were required and run various NPCs here and there... but I can't imagine having to track 45 Touchstone NPCs on top of everything else.

                                                And that's the problem with a LOT of V5's mechanics. A lot of them sound great on paper... then you try to use them in a real campaign and you find yourself having to slap on duct tape and bailing wire by the roll just to keep it from falling apart in practice. Some of them may even be good mechanics, but they're just so poorly expressed that people end up house-ruling them to what they THINK the rule is trying to say and so you get a patchwork where you can barely even discuss the system with people because no can even agree on the basic rules (ex. I've read the rules on combat several times over for V5 and I'm still not certain if my interpretation of how they say to handle multiple attackers and making multiple attacks yourself actually interface with each other... and what the hell merging the dice of both attackers into a single conflict pool is supposed to even mean, you and your opponent's dice all get added together?).

                                                    I think part of the issue is there's an unconscious backlash to Requiem which shouldn't exist and it's unfortunately impacted the number of RPG books that should exist for Vampire: The Masquerade Fith Edition.

                                                    One of the benefits of Requiem was that it was very experienced player/ST friendly and strongly focused the DIY mechanics so that you could do almost anything you wanted to it with a very wide-opened sandbox of possibilities. The problem was that part of the appeal of V:TM was always that it was very much, "This is an easy set of things to use. 13 kinds of vampire. Easy rules. Go nuts."

                                                    Requiem put out books like NOMADS, COTERIES, and lots of stuff about how to roleplay the Hunger as well as feeding. These are things that made the gameline work for me and why I always liked the more obscure books of V:TM ala Elysium or the faith books. The absence of similar material for V5 is one of its larger weakness.

                                                    I've mentioned that I wasn't a fan of either THE ANARCH or THE CAMARILLA because both books really are just collections of in-universe fiction. The Anarch even more so than the latter that at least contains some interesting new ideas. But I feel like they lacked out of character information and roleplaying hints that would have made them great. But it goes beyond this with THE SABBAT and SECOND INQUISITION books being necessary supplements that never got made.

                                                    You know what is also a necessary supplement?

                                                    TOUCHSTONES and PREDATOR TYPES

                                                    I was really hoping this would be something that would be covered by the Player's Guide but the lack of its publication means that we'll never know. Still, I think we needed expanded rules for Touchstones and Predator types that explains how they can be made more organic additions to campaigns.
