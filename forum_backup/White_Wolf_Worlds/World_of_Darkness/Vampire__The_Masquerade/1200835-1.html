                                            Originally posted by Beriorn View Post
                                            So it does grant access to all Wraith abilities and powers, then?
                                            No, and the Merit doesn't say you do. The benefits are:

                                            - You only spend half the regular experience points for increasing Necromancy Paths after you've purchased the first dot on the Path.
                                            - You can move between Skinlands and Shadowlands by spending a Willpower point.
                                            - You have two auras: one for the vampire, one for the wraith.

                                            Kindred hunt you mercilessly if they find out you're not a true vampire for a few reasons:

                                            - You're an oddity ripe for study.
                                            - They want to know if you're one of many unusual vampires in Kindred society.
                                            - Necromancers see you as a threat.

                                                        Thread Necromancy on a thread about... Necromancy.




                                                        I have asked lots of questions about the V20 Necromancy merits and powers. But now I am seeing this thread for the first time.


                                                        Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post

                                                        Except to have Half-Life you need to be a Cainite who has spent a long time in the Shadowlands, and to be a Mortuario you need to have undergone a ritual only the premascine Giovanni (those who were embraced before the Cappadocian betrayal) have.

                                                        Yes it is entirely mechanically plausible to be a Mortuario Harbinger with Half-Life, but no ST is going to okay that. Furthermore, you've wasted the vast majority of your freebies, so you are EXTREMELY GOOD at the 1-3 dots of one path of Necromancy you have.

                                                        These two merits require a deeply contorted backstory which no one will okay. I died accidentally, and a Harbinger and a Premascine worked together to bring me back, and then kicked me into torpor in the shadowlands for 100 years... NO! I am NOT powergaming!

                                                        I have seen many merits in the "Lore of Bloodlines/Clans" that are Clan restrictive, but some are open for anyone or for large number of Kindred. Half-Life does not seem to imply that a Harbinger need to be involved, and the Mortuario merit itself provides good backstory for Half-Life (the ritual that brings you back is calling your wraith).


                                                        Some of the open merits/flaws are the Tzimisce "Bioluminescence", "Pain Tolerance", "Promethean Clay" (2 only require to have Vissicitude); Tremere "Arcane Curse" "Mage Blood"; Lasombra "Aura of Command". Nothing in those merits makes them Clan specific, unlike "Thaumaturgically Inept" (Only Tremere can take this Flaw) or "Blessed by St. Gustav" (Only Ventrue antitribu can take this Merit).

                                                            Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                                                            As stated earlier, this Merit doesn't imply in the least that the character can learn Arcanoi, have the benefits of Lifesight or Deathsight, or any other aspect of the Wraith condition. If that had been the intent, the designers would have spelled that out directly in the Merit itself, as they spelled out the mechanics being a Ghoul over in Mage.


                                                            This, however, is not why I'm here. I'm here to ask: If the Merit DID allow the Vampire to learn Arcanoi, would be actually be overpowered, as the original poster said?

                                                            I don't think so. Setting aside for a moment that many Arcanoi may not even be applicable for a Vampire (I'm not familiar enough with Wraith to say either way). Or how of all the powers available to the Vampires, I can't imagine any Arcanoi would be very much more powerful than some of the Disciplines VtM has created over the years. Let's not even mention these points.

                                                            Instead, here's this. There's a school of thought that says if you give one supernatural creature access to two different sets of powers, that also means the character must DIVIDE their experience points between the two powers. Even if the breadth of abilities a character could access is huge, their ability to learn and improve them is finite. If continuing to gain new and different powers is, in and of itself, inherently OP, then what makes a Vampire learning Arcanoi qualitatively different than learning out-of-clan Disciplines? Or blood sorcery, with its many Paths and Rituals?

                                                            All for powers that are, at least in theory, as good as powers they gain natively, if dot ranks are anything to go by?

                                                            The only possible exception to this tends to be Mage Spheres, since their applications are open-ended and flexible in a way other supernatural powers aren't. But even then, you're also investing large amounts of experience into those Spheres, AND into Arete, AND into any number of mundane Abilities that the character's Focus may rely upon to channel Effects. AND the use of those Sphere powers is subject to Paradox, which puts a ceiling on the kinds of things our hypothetical cross-trained supernatural creature can use "safely".

                                                            Not to mention, whether you're a different splat branching INTO Spheres or a Mage branching OUT, many of the powers gained (that you just spent a bunch of experience unlocking) will be redundant to powers you already have. But I digress.


                                                            My point is, while I'd personally be reluctant to allow different splats free rein to learn each others' powers, I'm skeptical that allowing it would really break the game. On paper, letting Vampires learn Arcanoi SOUNDS overpowered. But in practice, the character is, at best, edging out advantages at unexpected angles, at the cost of failing to refine the greatest of their own native powers. Not useless, no, but neither is it a path to overwhelming power.
                                                            Actually Risen has access to both Arcanoi and some Disciplines and nobody ever thought of them as broken.
