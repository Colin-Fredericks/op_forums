    Revisiting Disciplines - Serpentis (1-3) is really great.

    06-15-2021, 08:29 AM
    V20! My old friend! The first TTRPG I ever played!

    After traveling in foreign parts for some time (MTA, D&D 5E, Star Wars FFG), I've started playing VTM again. And having played other systems and learnt a bit more about mechanics in general, I find myself looking at Disciplines quite differently on my return.

    Might as well start with Serpentis!

    1: This power is better than I remembered! Flavour wise I like the hypnotic gaze vibe, and I like how it acts as a sort of single-person disable. This is a utility power for Vampires, a way to feed safely, a handy way to immobilize a single panicking mortal and give yourself time to think.

    I'll rate this one "If I were a Vampire I would want to have this power."

    The "Roll Willpower Difficulty 9 to use this on a Supernatural" feature is interesting, since if you succeed they cannot do anything at all unless you move to harm them in some way, or they are presented with danger. As a result it's a decent hail mary if you're happy to spend a point of Willpower to try and force a success. A sensible GM might put a time limit on this, "he's very strong, you can feel this won't hold him forever", but it's a neat power.

    Good flavour, good ability, great start.

    2: Honestly this is a bit too much. V20 and DAV20 both gave this power a lot of love, DAV20 especially. Biting without grappling already makes a Vampire's iconic weapon even stronger. Then add one extra die of damage, and drinking 2 more blood per turn! Just... a really good power.

    The tongue adds a neat new way of attacking people out of reach, and provides a fun and unique way of killing using your snakey vampire. The sensory bonus of flicking and tasting the air is fun too.

    Strong power, very monstrous, main downside is the Masquerade. Both the V20 and DAV20 versions are very strong in combat and provide a lot of utility.

    3: Arguably the "meta" of dangerous combat in V20 is smacking someone with so much Aggravated that they die in one or two hits. If you want to keep your precious snakey priest safe, Serpentis 3 is a godsend. Adding your Stamina, which you can blood buff if needed, to your soak against Aggravated (except for fire, magic and sunlight), and reducing the difficulty of all soak by 1, is amazing. Serpentis 3 on its own is like getting several ranks of RAW Fortitude, just with some terms and conditions. If you're an Apostate and you do in fact have Fortitude, all the better, since they synergize well. Serpentis 3 with high Stamina and a few ranks of Fortitude is very achievable EXP wise and provides a lot of protection.

    4: Snake Form is kind of weird. It takes multiple turns to assume and the main benefit seems to be becoming a snake. I'm reminded of the Evil Overlord Advice list.

    34. I will not turn into a snake. It never helps.
    5: Heart removal is a handy power, but it feels more like a Setite Sorcery ritual than anything else. Useful, but thematically weird for a Discipline which up until now has been snaky powers.

    I would suggest making heart removal a 5th level Akhu ritual, and replacing Serpentis 5 with another suitable power. Mother of Monsters from DAV20 is an interesting replacement, although it does feel like a weird jump in the abilities of the Discipline. So far we've been transforming our own bodies... giving birth to monsters feels like yet another power which is a high level blood magic effect.

    In conclusion, Serpentis is a potent, fun and thematically exciting Discipline which will spice up any party. The main problems are 4 (somewhat lackluster, the main advantage is turning into an enormous snake, something that will not blend in most of the world over, and the benefits it gives are already provided by other powers) and 5 (requires replacement).

    Ultimately I believe that the Discipline is perfect as is if you only seek to take levels 1-3, which is a great place for a Discipline to be, leaving you plenty of EXP to spend on other pursuits. 4 is mostly fun for having the ability to turn into a huge snake, which is its own reward. 5 should be turning into a swarm of snakes or something else sufficiently serpentine.

      Thematically and in terms of getting a balance of utility right, it's probably my second favorite of the "Protean knockoff clan disciplines". But that's the problem with it: until DAV20, it was a "Protean knockoff clan discipline" and writers/publishers never pushed away from that mold to give the discipline its own identity that it deserves. I've never had an issue with Serpentis taking the "Conan the Barbarian bad guy" theme rather than preserving fidelity with Egyptian myth, but given DAV20 manages to break that mold without sacrificing utility I'm strongly in favor of DAV20 Serpentis over practically any past version of it.

      My only issue with DAV20 Serpentis is they wrote it in the mold of DAV20 Vicissitude, where level 5 is "disposable minion creation power" opposed to "immunity/survival power".

      But honestly, level 1 is the most powerful level of Serpentis. Effectively hard countering every social discipline in the game is a hell of a power, especially for what a smart player can do in combination with practically any level of Presence.

                          Serpentis originally had two mummy-flavored powers, Mummify at level three and Heart of Darkness at level five. When the three-dot power was thrown out in Revised but the five-dot power was left untouched, the end result was that the latter now looked out of place among otherwise snake-themed powers.

                          The original version of Serpentis was written at a time when Thaumaturgical rituals were not the answer to everything. In 1991, Thaumaturgy was still a relatively small set of powers, and primarily within the purview of the Tremere clan. The suggestion that Heart of Darkness "should" have been a ritual makes little sense in the original context.

                          I feel like the criticism also mistakes the flavor text for the actual power. Cutting out your own heart is just how the Heart of Darkness is flavored; the actual power is "You become immune to staking." I can think of a dozen ways to rewrite the description without making it seem like a one-time ritual. Compare the power to the original six-dot Daimonion power, which makes the Baali immune to fire. I've never heard anyone suggest that Ignore the Searing Flames should have also been a ritual, because it's not written to seem like one. Both the Serpentis and Daimonion powers are simply meant to give the user a blanket immunity to a common vampire weakness.

                                  Originally posted by Elphilm View Post

                                  I agree. Original Serpentis was like two different Disciplines merged into one. The first was "Robert E. Howard style Stygian snake magic" and the second was "Egyptian Afterlife/Mummy magic". It only makes sense when you realize the intent was to create one power set that would give the Setites all the signature powers that you might expect from Ancient Egyptian snake worshipping cultists. Like you said in the next post, once they accomplished that they actually spent effort making the higher Discipline examples with a theme of "corruption" (which by nature were mainly an NPC thing as no PC could learn those Disciplines with any generation bought at character creation).

                                  So it never made too much sense from an in character or setting perspective as a combination of Egyptian funeral magic and snake transformation don't make sense outside of Howard's stories. It was for meta-narrative purposes to emulate a specific kind of pulp villain. As the setting began to distance itself from direct inspiration from Robert E. Howard in Revised, it undermined the very theme of Serpentis since the clan was no longer about emulating Howard's pulp villains. Simply put the Discipline is an artifact of the original "Earth-One" version of the WoD that doesn't make sense in the post-Revised setting but out of a sense of continuity it remains.

                                  This is why people now want to interpret either as an offshoot of Protean (it's just about shapechanging!), Blood Magic (it's obviously some kind of magical rituals!), or Obtenebration (Set is a God of Darkness!) because they want it to make sense in the setting. I don't think that's a wrong approach necessarily, but whatever style you choose, it is just a matter of taste. Just like if you want to be solely about corruption, or return it just to being Howard's cult of Stygian sorcerers.

                                  Personally I love REH's work and don't mind it being in the game. So I keep it like Stygian sorcerer, and just make the classic Serpentis a unique brand of Blood Magic. And I then have that and a few other Thaumaturgical Paths be proprietary to the Setites.

                                                      Thanks for the opinion piece, but I think the rest of us are fine without attempts to police the conversation. I've never understood the extremely online phenomenon where people have to loudly state their opinions about subject matters that they by their own admission are not even familiar with.

                                                      If the conversation is about how strange the latest iteration of a given discipline appears to someone unfamiliar with the history behind it, putting things in context is not a claim about the superiority of the earliest version compared to later ones. My approach to game design is that if you want to understand why a recent edition of a game does something unexpected, of course you should go back to the earliest possible versions of the rules to see them in their original context and to get a sense of how and why the rules have changed in the hands of new designers. Otherwise you're just inheriting assumptions, concepts, and unnecessary cruft without even realizing it, which is exactly how we ended up with both 20th Anniversary versions of Serpentis.
