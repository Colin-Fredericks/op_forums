        I don't think you understand; it's not that members of the Ashirra are presenting problems by being vampires (and Islam is more understanding than most religions on that particular point), it's that they are presenting problems by being members of the Ashirra.

        Roughly 85-90% of the Muslims in the world belong to the Ahl as-Sunnah wa'l-Jama'h, generally known as Sunni Islam, whilst the remaining 10-15% belong to the Shi'atu Ali, generally known as Shi'ism. If you actually pay attention to what has been written in various Vampire: the Masquerade supplements, you'll find that members of the Ashirra are Shi'ites for various reasons. I don't think the authors were thinking too deeply when they decided this, but it sets up a theological problem. Within orthodox (i.e. Sunni) Islam, the Shi'a are just plain wrong in a quite dangerous manner. People born as Shi'ites may be tolerated as People of the Book, but they are, at best, heretics to be persecuted, though the Sunni theological position is that they aren't Muslim at all. Whilst the media of the western world tended to miss this, the recent activities of ISIS in Iraq did involve a great deal of targeted violence against Shi'ites.

        It's a bigger issue if a Sunni Muslim actually converts to Shi'ism. This is apostacy, and punishable by death. But given that the majority of real Muslims are Sunni, it means that most would be converting when joining the sect, and opening themselves to accusations of apostacy. Remember when I said upthread about religion being treated as an aesthetic choice? Well, the sectarian issues are an example of this in practice. The writers who defined the Ashirra did not consider ideas about sectarianism and how it might have an impact upon ideas in the game. In the real world, religious sects can be a question of life and death, a fact that never seems to be under consideration.

Incidentally, I believe the second generation World of Darkness is the first reference of the Ashirra.
