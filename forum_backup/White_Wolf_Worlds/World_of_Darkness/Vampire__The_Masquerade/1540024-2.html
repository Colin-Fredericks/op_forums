    Originally posted by Matt the Bruins fan View Post
    I think VtM Revised Storyteller's Handbook had the best system for vampires countering mage spells (roll Thaumaturgy rating against difficulty equal to mage's Willpower; Thaumaturgical Countermagic adds one die per dot). One's ability to shrug off true magic depended on how much expertise and power one had at vampiric sorcery, rather than general occult scholarship and quick thinking in the hands of any vampire, even one with no practical ability in that arena.
    To be honest, I also think it's the best alternative in the sense that it's the one that makes more sense, I have used this one often in my tables, but then there are some who complain about "Tremere too op nerf pls". I think the passive countermagick should be only against things like turning a vampire into a lawn chair and the like, even though I also like the idea in M20 that any vampire with knowledge in Protean/Vicissitude and other powers that allow him to change form, should be able to revert the transformation with ease. Fortunately in my many years of crossover experience no mage tried to turn a vampire in anything like that, normally they prefer the "fireball approach" and then a Blood Sorcerer with enough knowledge of the magic arts should be able to try to counter it, but I don't think someone without such knowledges should have any other "naural defense" like "night-folk countermagick" in this case.

        I don't think any particular method is the best, it depends.

        At my current chronicle, which is actually Mage, I plan on using Occult, maybe with Blood Sorcery having an easier time.

        Putting aside whatever that may or may not make sense (Vampires are already magical beings that can still exist on Earth, I find it hard to believe they can’t have innate magical protection), I do that because I want Vampires to be relevant when they show up, and they’ll be central to two or three important side plots.

        Other than that, I see those systems together as dials to get the exact result you want. Without some form of countermagick, dealing with Vampires is trivial for most Mages. Having a default makes the vampires more relevant. Defaulting to Willpower is interesting as an option actually focused on dealing with other splats, a game about supernatural mystery where survival is not guaranteed. If you want to make Paradox a big theme in your game, it is even possible that the other splats have Permadox that don’t affect them, but affects Mages nearby.

        So, options. They make for different interactions that are good at different chronicles.

                    I don't think its a balance issue. Mages, in the Ascension books are always stated to be just utterly human. Yes they are human with god-like powers to shape reality and cause anything they can imagine to happen, happen, with the consequence of major undertakings causing reality to snap back with paradox, but they are, and will always be just human. This means they can die just like any other human their magic non withstanding. They're the glass cannons of the splats.

                    The other splat creatures, are all an accepted part of that reality (according to reality itself..) that the Mages are manipulating, so it should take more effort to effect a sentient being than say a pipe on a wall.

                    TBH there's a lot in the D10 system that WoD uses that is pretty unbalanced, so the best bet is to just take what you find and change it to suit your needs. Vamps having too big dice pools with Occult + Wits? Half it. Gives them some sort of resistance, and keeps it to more manageable levels. (So a theoretical 4th gen Methuselah would have a max pool of 9 instead of 18.)
