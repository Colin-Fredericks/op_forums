                            Originally posted by Grumpy RPG Reviews View Post
                            I am inclined to agree with her. But at the same time I think it would be a mistake, even a severe mistake, to simply dismiss the criticisms out of hand. If some behavior offends someone, including sexual behavior, we should respect that offense and take it seriously. The same is true for depictions of women and minorities. We should listen to the people making the complaints and protesting. We should consider those protests and complaints when making our own statements in the future.

                            A fair point, though I often wonder if we are getting bogged down in the responses to responses, rather than dealing with root causes. If a portion of the population is offended by sexualization of inanimate objects, in truth we should be looking into how best to help those people get over their hang ups rather than constantly hand wringing over if we might offend them.

                            Something like 1% of the population is epileptic and of those people 3% are photosensitive epileptics. But despite what a decent sized chunk of the population that is, emergency services won't give up their flashing lights, video game designers won't restrict development, anime studios won't quit making battle scenes, etc. At most the groups will avoid a specific frequency of light pulses, but not avoid flashing in general.

                            Instead the expectation is on the individuals who suffer from the condition to self regulate their problems with medication or actively avoid sources that would give them problems. Now seizures can produce all sorts of trauma to the body and occasionally cause fatalities under unfortunate circumstances, but the focus for dealing with it remains solely with the person who has the issue rather than the companies that make use of the technology. A warning label is considered ample enough effort to allow people to make informed decisions about such things.

                            Obviously Epilepsy is not an apples to apples comparison to personal views on sexualization, but it does provide an interesting counter point to the expectation of everyone else changing around the needs or trigger issues of the individual rather than the individual learning how to control such responses to stimuli.

                                    Originally posted by Thoth View Post
                                    A fair point, though I often wonder if we are getting bogged down in the responses to responses, rather than dealing with root causes.
                                    How do you get to the root causes without going through the responses though?

                                    A doctor can't just ignore symptoms to focus on the disease. Besides the harm symptoms can cause, diagnosing a disease is largely predicated on studying the symptoms of it.

                                    And societal problems can't be solved by giving people pills once you figure out which ones to give.

                                    There's also a fairly direct trend of need to amass evidence to sway the social opinion of people that currently have power in an institution/industry/etc. No matter how well reasoned an argument how something is sexist/racist/etc, and even coupled with a solid theory on the root causes, we don't see things actually change. There's a need for a mass of evidence until people unaffected directly recognize they need to help change things rather than ignore it as not their problem.

                                    It's not like the people of the colonies/US didn't know slavery and denying women's suffrage were moral ills until we finally got around to legally changing things. Abolitionists and women's rights movements existed before the founding the country and fought for generations against the status quo despite the obvious immorality of the status quo.

                                    It's not a far stretch to say that televised news broadcasts were hugely important to the Civil Rights movement, because lots of "good people doing nothing," had to confront their inaction when it wasn't stories, or text, but live video of black people being abused by the police for trying to have their rights respected. We needed to flood society with the truth to enact meaningful change, and even so many of the root causes remain unaddressed and when brought up solutions are deflected and dismissed because there isn't the weight to press the issue.

                                    Obviously Epilepsy is not an apples to apples comparison to personal views on sexualization, but it does provide an interesting counter point to the expectation of everyone else changing around the needs or trigger issues of the individual rather than the individual learning how to control such responses to stimuli.
                                    I don't see how it's interesting consider how far removed they are on two major points:

                                    1) Epilepsy is a medical condition that's a matter of bad luck to have. Being subject to discrimination by society is something other people do to you. Women don't get objectified because of bad luck on the genetic lottery, but because other human beings that have to learn how to control their own actions are objectifying them.

                                    2) The majority of people in US society have suffered discrimination of some sort, rather than extremely rare diseases. What's a reasonable adjustment for a company to make to a product for 0.03% of the population isn't the same as what something like ~60% of the population has to deal with.
