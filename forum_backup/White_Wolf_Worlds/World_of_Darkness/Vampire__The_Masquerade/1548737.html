      Yeah, I always questioned that.

      For me, he wanted a real far off place, with close to no vampire, and not much dense population. So he moved to an island full of "brittunculi" according to the Romans, to contemplate things in deep thought while still having a domain with blood. Far from the big centers of Cainite action at the time.
      Maybe there he desired to build a Cainite society styled after his own style and wants.

      I also think from time to time he goes on a tour of sorts, like a warrior poet of old, visiting places of his cult, or even going as far as his homeland. Although travel is super dangerous, he is a methuseleah of much skill, and has a network of loyal cults.
      Since just making him an arrogant selfish prick makes the legend of Mithras a bit too samey as all other oldies, I prefered to imagine him as the more "honor your words" "break no oaths" kind of religious Mithras that we got in history. Something like a king of judges, in a time where the were no sects to enforce favors being paid. Which would also put Mithras close to the Banu Haqim, doubling both as a reason to have Haqim himself visit Mithras, and a nice irony that he would end up possessing a Haquimite.

      So yeah, maybe he knew Britain would become an empire, or maybe he just thought it was a nice island with human herd and peace and quiet for him to use it as resort and hermitage.

      Strange... When coincidence seems too convenient, I prefer to call it fate.

                                                      I have to agree that the placing of Mithras in Britain really does work best on a Doyalist level where you get all sorts of juicy stuff to play with ranging from: the literal mithraeum, the association of the later British Empire with the Roman Empire (interesting because this association was invoked both as a positive and as a negative irl), the occult concept (descended from Theosophy iirc) that Britain contained one of the central nodes of occult energy on the planet (Kenneth Anger, a student of Crowley, identifies Glastonbury as the site of the earth’s pancreatic gland chakra center), etc.

                                                      Britain is of course interesting in general as a lot of ideas and lore about it of course remain high in the consciousness of anglophone societies, inevitably producing a tendency towards its importance. You’ve got the ancient and on-going fascination of writers with the Druids and Britain as one of the centers of the breakaway empires of the Crisis of the Third Century. You’ve got the Romano-Britains before one of the true collapses of the world of antiquity in Western Europe. This leads me to then wonder if in the WoD we should maybe play around a bit with some esoteric britain topics such as, say King Arthur having some sort of syncretism with a Roman cult figure such as Mithras could provide. I think it would be funny and within WW’s tradition to imply that Mithras may have been one aspect of the King Arthur legend.

                                                      Then in the Middle Ages you get to play around with stuff like the legends about the Holy Grail being in Glastonbury or that the Knights Templar (after gaining knowledge in the mystery schools of the east) fled to Scotland to avoid their persecution under King Phillip the Fair (which, of course, is why Freemasonry later emerged from Scotland in 1717). I’ve always thought that having the Ventrue be fascinated by freemasonry would be a perfect fit for them and it even works with their 1e/2e anxiety over the influence of ‘secret masters’.

                                                      Sinister and occult Britain works even better once you get to the Victorian Era and you can imply that somehow Mithras’ influence is directly connected with the expansion of the empire’s power. One can really go crazy with this. One can loot Alan Moore’s From Hell for inspiration on the arcane cruelty of the Occult British Empire or go back and look at Blavatsky & Bulwer-Lytton for inspiration (Blavatsky is fascinating here because of how her interest in Tibet and south eastern mysticism aligns with the British expansion into India and the rumors that Blavatsky may have somehow been involved in intelligence activities wrt the Great Game & Bulwer-Lytton, a man of some influence within the empire, whose dabbling in writing orientalist literature gives us the idea of a British ruling class secretly participating in a Cult of Isis, etc.)

                                                      From a Doyalist perspective, Mithras’ exoticism makes a lot of sense in retrospect, if we ground it by associating it with the flourishing Orientalism of the British empire at its height, with the character of Mithras as both a symbol of imperial vitality via his Roman connections and a symbol of a mysterious eastern religion synergyzing with the empire’s fascination with and intervention in Asia. Mithras’ proximity to power also works well as a parallel to anxieties over the empire’s condition dating back to the imperial era, with the reoccurring fear that somehow the east was going to ‘infect’ the empire through contact (see stories ranging from Dracula to Fu Manchu) taken to its logical extreme: that there is no purely ‘British’ empire to infect; the outside (Asia) has been on the inside (Britain, by extension, Europe) from the very beginning.

                                                      Cue the Lovecraftian break in the protagonist’s psyche as they realize to their horror that their entire notion of themselves has been a lie from the beginning and that they themselves are one of the very things which they had always defined themselves against.
