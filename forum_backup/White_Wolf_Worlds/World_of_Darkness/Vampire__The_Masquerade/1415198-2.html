    False Epiphany : An excellent post, you brought up several points that made me reconsider my position a bit, and you inspired me, too. The only thing that still bugs me is how methuselahs can have endless personal wars against each other for thousands of years, without anyone ever prevailing. Why hasn't Helena ever managed to kill Menele, if she's had thousands of years to do it?

    I agree completely about the Transylvania Chronicles/Giovanni Chronicles problem. The problem is mostly due to bad writing and a lack of modern post-indie-RPG theory at the time of the writing of those books, but it's also a systems issue, where my ideal for Vampire would be a system that gives player characters at least a slim chance to defeat a methuselah (with defeat I don't necessarily mean physically though), by stacking odds in their favor cleverly enough, or utilizing the methuselah's weaknesses against it. For example, the Swedish indie vampire RPG "Svart av kval, vit av lust" (Black from angst, white from lust) is kind of a love letter to Vampire, but it's mechanics is all about a kind of interplay between the reliable, fearsome powers of the elders, and the short bursts of beast-powered madness that can let young vampires beat an elder into the ground. Now, Vampire need not be just like that, but I did like the "self-destruct buttons" that the ancient elders in Lair of the Hidden had, that gave the player characters a way to defeat them. If I do decide to use a methuselah in my Mexico City chronicle (probably Shaitan then?) I'll think long and hard how it should be used, and how to make sure that the PCs are still the main characters in that story.

        Originally posted by Natsymir View Post
        False Epiphany : An excellent post, you brought up several points that made me reconsider my position a bit, and you inspired me, too. The only thing that still bugs me is how methuselahs can have endless personal wars against each other for thousands of years, without anyone ever prevailing. Why hasn't Helena ever managed to kill Menele, if she's had thousands of years to do it?
        Largely because they don't give each other many opportunities to.

        How many times over their millennia-long feud have they fought one another in personal combat? You had Pompeii in 67 CE and Fort Dearborn in 1812, and that's it. They primarily fight by pitting catspaws against catspaws, which is a very slow way to actually kill one another. If Menele's side had won the Council Wars by killing Lodin and installing Maldavis as prince, what would really happen? Menele would have more resources with which to search for Helena's resting place, but he and and his archrival would probably just continue to act out their feud through the primogen's battles. Eventually, Menele might manage to destroy or subvert most of Helena's pawns, at which point she'd probably decide to cut her losses and flee to somewhere else, and their Jyhad would get to play out all over again if Menele didn't manage to stop her flight. Theoretically, Menele is going to use his pawns once he's in a superior or lucky enough position to directly attack Helena's person, but the odds favor her in a confrontation against younger vampires. That's okay. Menele will try to hit her when she's most vulnerable and just keep throwing pawns at her until one of them finally manages to do the deed. But he probably won't get that many opportunities to directly attack her, because Helena will bring in more pawns after he neutralizes her previous ones, and he'll need to spend more time neutralizing these new ones. And since they're evenly matched as puppet-masters, Menele probably won't have an endless string of victories in these proxy wars, and Helena will manage to take out some of her archrival's pawns too. Menele will need to counter that. And so it'll continue to play out, over and over and over.

        This is a very slow and inefficient process for actually killing one's rivals, but it's also the most strategic one for an immortal being with unlimited time and whose foremost goal is to preserve their own existence. This is Methuselah 101. Don't risk your ass on the front lines when you can manipulate someone else into fighting your battles for you.

        I did like the "self-destruct buttons" that the ancient elders in Lair of the Hidden had, that gave the player characters a way to defeat them. If I do decide to use a methuselah in my Mexico City chronicle (probably Shaitan then?) I'll think long and hard how it should be used, and how to make sure that the PCs are still the main characters in that story.
        There are lots of ways for younger vampires to win fights against their elders. Canon has a ton of examples. Monty Coven kills Mithras after he's tired from a long torpor and exhausted from slaughtering a pack of Lupines. Lugoj kills Byelobog after he's driven into torpor by True Faith-wielding Teutonic Knights, and presumably with help from a large force of Anarchs. Gratiano attacks Lasombra while he's in torpor with another large force of Anarchs. Lair's self-destruct buttons were another neat point of vulnerability for PCs to exploit.

        Just as relevant is whether PCs actually need to fight the methuselah. If it's intended to be an antagonist, sure, have ways for them to kill it. But a methuselah can just as easily show up in a non-adversarial role. Japheth in Giovanni Chronicles is a dispenser of wisdom and insight to PCs. Valerianus in Vienna by Night hangs out with a neonate simply because he's lonely. Mahatma in Children of the Night guides his chosen protege towards Golconda, in hopes that doing so might allow him to obtain that same exalted state.

        The GM doesn't have to engineer a confrontation to give the PCs a fighting chance if the methuselah isn't actually interested in fighting, and a peaceful meeting between a PC and a methuselah can be a really fun and cool thing to play out. The PCs just have to be narratively consequential participants in the scene, whether that's through getting to influence the methuselah's actions, learning a secret or receiving some other reward as a result of their prior actions, or just enjoying a two-sided conversation with an interesting character. What's a waste of players' time is for the methuselah to show up and monologue at them or do cool things while their PCs sit and watch from the bleachers.

        Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.

                My standard take is to always have 1 or 2 of them somewhere in the chronicle's host as a kind of Easter Egg. The PCs may find them at some point and interact, and their roles vary a lot, but they may also never encounter the thing, just some signs of its passage. But ultimately they tend to be background for the main story, either tired of the Jyhad or involved in plans so convoluted that for all practical purposes are nonexistent.

                Caveat: the players' actions may affect any character in the host they decide to mess with, and the "Ancient Ones" are no exception. They usually do not involve themselves directly, but it doesn't mean they won't throw some weight for or against the PCs. Lair of the Hidden fits perfectly on my style of how the interactions effectively play out.

                But then, I have one player that really, really, really loves to interact with important NPCs in every setting. And I'm married to her, so I have to concede sometimes. Then I have my share of games with many Methuselah appearing. I also played once a long game that went really far into the dark-HIGH-fantasy area, where I could get my fangs on enough old necks to get to 3rd gen myself. It wasn't in any way a typical VtM game except for strictly following the rules and mostly the setting, but I can't say it wasn't very fun for a time

                I had most 6+ Discipline powers in this single character and I can confirm: they're mostly lacking.
