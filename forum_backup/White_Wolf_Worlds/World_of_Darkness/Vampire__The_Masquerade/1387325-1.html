The first murderer could've been a very archaic human, an african tribesman, a cro-magnon tribesman, or some neanderthal.

If Cain was just myth vampirism would start in a another locale where the first murder DID take place (or not have anything to do with the first "murder" a human has committed since that fiat of mythology came from the Book of Genesis altogether- but since it is a considerable part of VTM that a "first murder" has to take place for Vampirism to start, that by itself confirms Cain as the progenitor of Vampires through sheer Occam Razor because no one else fits the bill or precedent).

It hinges on one character that no matter what at some point is going to wreck the entire setting to cull the increasing high generation Kindred (a mechanic and plot device that slugs the setting) and not only cull the Vampires, but probably destroy the entire earth fighting his awakened Antediluvian childer as well. Its a character thats basically the main character, but shrouded entirely and is going only ever to really reveal himself fully when the VTM writers have decided that game has reached its waning era and its time to pull the plug (a thing I remind you they've done before). Its a drag!

                The World of Darkness is steeped in hermetic theory, which means that although parts of the Caine myth are true, it doesn't exclude the Cagn myth that the Liabon believe in. Althought there's a singular "God" it's more "The one above all" rather than something that excludes the Triat and discounts the whole WTA mythology. The Vampire, Werewolf, Mage, Demon, Wraith, Eastern mythologies can all simultaneously be true (but you've got to piece it together a bit)

                Similarly, I do like Generation from a religious standpoint. The "we can source outselves back to a blood god" is great. It gives us this feeling that ancient times must've been fantastical, we can look to an imagined past (something that fascists are so fond of). Generation creates a great deal of mystery, whilst not having generation or replacing it with blood potency just gives the impression that there's always been vampires and, although age might've brought them power, it doesn't really have the whole 'unknowable, unreachable blood-god' that generation brings. With generation in place, most vampires look to a 7th gen and realise "I will never be able to get as powerful as them, as their transcendant generation allows them to have more dots and powers beyond five, unless I eat my way up to remove my limits' whilst without generation, you've got something more like "In 300 years I'll be her pier, as percentage wise our ages won't be so different'

                            The way you put it does sound good, but Generation wouldn't be bad if didn't gimp each succession so badly until we get to the whole Thin-Blood thing. I wished they never came up with Thin-Bloods and just left it at higher generation makes you not stronger than the ones with a lower generation than yourself. It would be extremely hard to pull off from what I understand though, since how high can you take Generation without the Thin-Blood thematic until it mechanically doesn't make sense? Thin-Bloods make sense within the meta and mechanics because:

                            A) a generation system like we see in VTM would always have a downstream weakened state as you've mentioned, which will breed interesting conflict

                            B) you can't have continuously high gen Vampires without it reflecting someway ICly altogether

                            C) its a way for the story to not get bloated because sooner or later Gehenna is going to reset the entire vampire kind

                            I feel like this is bad though because the whole dialectic battle between "young and old" is thrown out the window once you get to Thin-Bloods/13+ Gen vampires. These type of vampires can never broach the whole continuous interesting conflict between young vs old because their just too flat-out supernaturally weak to be of any viability. Thats when the setting gets to be a drag when your stuck with these type of vampires that can't be interesting anymore.

                            EDIT: Also the whole "multi-layer reality" thing was just a cop-out because the writers of D:TF didn't want to explain the full oWoD creation of all the splats, just hamfist their old "Lucifer vs God" conflict into the oWoD universe. No matter how you spin it that splat breaks/retroactively retcons alot of lore from other splats. It was supposed to tie-in the entire setting as whole, but did the exact opposite entirely. I wonder how White Wolf even let D:TF be greenlight considering oWoD was coming to a close at the time.

                                            Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                                            If vampires didn't have generation, they'd just live alone, accumulate disciplines, and not bother their equally powerful neighbours all that much. Because you've got a big generational disparity, the older and more powerful vampires can order the younger ones around in a way that has a lot more real world parallels. If you were all equal, anyone could become prince, democracy wouldn't even seem like a bad idea, but being stuck in a facist monarcharchy/theocracy where the rulers take hundreds of years to age out? That's worth exploring.
                                            I think the idea isn't to flatten it out completely, but to make it depend on age instead of birth. With this proposed change (Blood Potency instead of Generation), Ur-Shulgi isn't insanely powerful because he was embraced by a third-gen, he's insanely powerful because he was embraced more than five thousand years ago, and has spent large parts of the intervening time becoming arguably the strongest blood-sorcerer in the world.

                                            In VtM, one of the important themes has always been powerlessness in the face of nigh-omnipotent elders. Even if you survive for five thousand years, you'll never be as strong as Ur-Shulgi is now. You don't have the potential for it. You'll always be limited by the circumstances of your creation, unless you're willing to consume souls for it. Which is great if that's what you're into! It's not a theme I've ever particularly liked, though. It seems to me like someone with five thousand years' worth of xp on their sheet should be insanely powerful just from that alone; they don't need an extra "oh btw I'm also just intrinsically better than you" on top of that.

                                            And if neonates do have the potential to become the next Helena or Menele—if they survive for millennia without getting killed—it explains nicely why so many of them are willing to buy into the Camarilla's propaganda. Sure, being at the bottom of the hierarchy sucks now, but look, you could be the one in a million that makes it to the top! In terms of real-world parallels, look at the classic American myth of the poor as temporarily disadvantaged millionaires. I don't see how that myth could exist if there's also an immutable, inviolable law in place saying someone of low birth can never rise above their station; the potential needs to be there, in theory, even though 99.999% of neonates will never achieve it.

                                                            I agree with the last three posts, its a headache. And Werewolf have hints of an Edenic holy spot in Ethiopia, but they don't necessarily acknowledge the story of the Garden of Eden.                                                             See, the point is Werewolf cosmology does NOT work well with the other splats' cosmology, not Vampire but also not Mage, Wraith or Demon. Note that Mage mentions the stone used by Caine to kill Abel as an extremely powerful magical item and Demon confirms Caine's story and its significance.

                                                            The way I found to make all oWoD splats' cosmologies work together is stealing a page from Mage:the Awakening. If we assume the war between angels and demons was a war where winning battles meant reshaping reality and time we can assume Enoch and the like existed in a "time before time" and the deluge was what happened when the angels won and "fixed" reality and time as they are now (some aspects of the previous realities still exist, the underworld for one). In my games the fera and spirits where pulled "out of time" before the same happened for vampires and the egyptian "gods" (basically mages with no paradox) and God turned himself into the Triat.
                                                            It's complicated but it actually makes the cosmology work smoothly.

                                                            IT: Generation was always a problem stat. It defines a character's potential power so at chargen, players who want powerful characters usually want to max it. The other problem is you can't lower generation unless you devour someone's soul and blood, which is a truly evil act. That's great for telling stories about power and how it corrupts but it also means unscrupulous players try their best to diablerize their way to "power", character development be damned.

                                                            On the other hand generation is an integral part of VtM: blood is power and the older the blood the more powerful its is, which makes it tempting for those who want power above all else and are willing to sacrifice their humanity to get it.
