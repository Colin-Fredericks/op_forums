    [V20] True Faith and Violence

    03-28-2024, 01:47 PM
    So, True Faith is always linked to an high humanity. I'm talking about the typical faith of a pius person. It's so deep this root that in the core manual the requirements is to have humanity 9 and you'll lose the benefits of the faith if this level drop. Of course this is only a point of view and I think there can be other options (mad monks? blind lawful inquisitors?), but mainly in this medium people with True Faith are depicted so pius that they feel bad for thinking about something evil (humanity 9-10) o zelous fighter of the dark forces (from the middle age Inquisition).

    How do you deal with this? Making a practical example from the canon: in Cairo there is a religious Nosferatu (Petra) with true faith that want to protect a girl (Afifa, the Herald) and in doing so, while escaping a group of Setite, she in frenzy slaughter her assailants in an alley. Slaughter. Humanity 9. So doing something bad against someone evil is still in line I think. (Ok, she was in frenzy but nothing caused it, maybe the stress, so is it not clear if it was her choice or not).

    This is of course only one example. If you think of the extreme violence of the inquisition you will find something similar.

    TLR - moral questions are always good in Vampire but since they tied a power (True Faith) to morale I see some problems in applying it. My conclusion is that there is no need to tied True Faith and Humanity. Being invested by divine power OR being a very good person that dislike doing bad things are different characteristics. A good person will have to choose to do something harmful at a certain point, otherwise is not doing anything about the evil in the world. Evil agains evil creatures? Mabybe ok. Evil against they controlled servants, in order to survive? Very bad for high levels of humanity.

    Edit: the last post speaking about this topic is 5 years old, so maybe something changed in your experience and there is something to add to this discourse.

                                      The way I handle it in the chronicles I ST is that low level True Faith is quite easy to have. You just have to sincerely believe. You don't need to be perfect. Those low level displays - able to hold a cross or utter a pray that turns away the forces of darkness, bolstering your willpower to defend you against supernatural evil or to keep on going when it is arduous, providing temporary immunity or defiance at mental control, and perhaps even sensing that something is off about a person who is in reality a supernatural creature - can apply to ordinary people - the regular church goer, an observant Jew, etc. That's how the grace of the divine works. And those people don't need to be moral exemplars. In the Dark Ages, I'd allow even the worst crusader or jihadist be protected by low levels of True Faith because there is probably sincere belief there. Those low level powers are just too great a trope to not use in Vampire and similar games.

                                      But higher levels of true faith - when people begin to seem like they are miracle workers - need to be on the path of living saints and mystics. For that level of divine power to go through you, you need to be acting in ways that reflect high humanity. The person needs to start transforming their life so that it reflects the highest spiritual ideals. Those people are abandoning temporal concerns for a spiritual life that makes them holy.

                                      People/players can easily accept the trope of an ordinary, but sincere believing, person warding off a vampire by holding up a cross. But if someone is an actual faith healer, can make vampires feel incredible self-loathing, totally immune to supernatural powers, or is seemingly protected by the hand of God, it stretches genre credibility unless the players think that character is an actual saint.

                                      Like many VtM concepts, True Faith works great as a narrative tool if the ST wants to explore those tropes in the setting. If you use it simply as a mechanical tool by itself without regard as to why it exists, it can lead to absurd conclusions at times.

                                          I don't think levels 9 and 10 are a problem at all. Aside any notion of how people work in real life, portraying a character that rarely does anything selfish is rather easy if this is what you want.

                                          What I actually think are problematic are levels 3, 5 and 8. Those are the sins pertaining to violence that may be required to uphold your faith and duties, specially 8.

                                          My own take on those sins, just short of taking a Road, is to add the line "unless unavoidable when preventing greater harm".

                                          This is still strict, I demand from such characters that they have both the creative and ethical thinking to avoid resorting to violence. But at least I don't punish them further when it fails, and I can have more heroic, if not so much knightly, behavior from "saints".

                                          For characters that really go proactively smite evil around, I consider either lesser Faith as Black Fox proposes, or I go for DA Roads.

                                              I think I treat vampire roads really as fiction. The way Humanity is framed in the game is a system of morality that barely exists in the real world as I conceive it. All religions I am familiar with are very different from it the more you go into them and explore the depths of their ethics. If one is not following a set of values defined by someone else in a religion, they're following their own, and the variety in there can also be at odds with Humanity as defined in game. And to present Humanity as the only genuinely good moral system and other paths as inherently problematici or flawed is a value judgement on the beliefs of people in the real world that I don't take even into consideration. So, pure fiction.

                                              If I wanted a system that makes more sense, I would take either the notion of Humanity used in VtR second edition (my favourite) or the Dark Ages Roads, because they're more varied and more easily adapted to real world systems of morality, also with the option of Paths and some leniency of the Narrator towards individual interpretations of certain sins. Or I would go for the idea, which I think is used in V5 (haven't read it yet), that each character just defines a few personal values, and that would replace Roads and Paths.

                                              As for True Faith, it is especially disjoined from Humanity, because no faith system I can think of lacks a self-defined system of ethics that diverges from Humanity on several counts. Even a person of faith who does not identify with a specific religion would, at least at my table, need to define how their particular beliefs define their moral system, and they would basically end up having their own Road of sorts.
                                              Now, in a fictional game like vampire I want to use a relativistic system where characters from different religions and philosophies can all access the game mechanic called True Faith. At the same time, I want this mechanic to be tied to their real religious beliefs, not to the artificial Path of Humanity, as if their True Faith did not depend on their religions or philosophies, but on their luck in unconsciously adhering to moral principles that are not even fully those of their chosen religion. At my table, it's more likely that True Faith be possessed by a character championing a corporal punishment or fighting prescribed in his/her holy book and within the religiously prescribed limits, than one who does not carry out some religious injunction on account of his/her own sense of mercy or other motivation. One who is, on the other side, cruel and abuses his religion's injunctions to justify a level of violence criticised by his own holy book and the more learned theologians is unlikely to get True Faith and/or a high Road rating.

                                                  One thing to remember is that humans are not on the Path of Humanity. They are not vampires and do not need a path. Their "Path" rating is just a shorthand to demonstrate approximately how moral they are, but humans are not in any way bound by its mechanics.

                                                  Vampires are on Paths because Paths are specifically designed as ways for them to control the Beast and therefore retain rational control ("the Man") over themselves. That's the purpose of a Path - to constrain the Beast. The Path of Humanity is just as "artificial" as any of the other Paths, but has the advantage of being based on something that vampires would recognize from their mortal days because it superficially fits human morality. They already "know" what they are supposed to do. They don't have to become philosophers of alien ideologies.

                                                  Most vampires on the Path of Humanity do not examine it in the same way as Sabbat do with their Paths because most vampires on Humanity don't realize they are on the Path. So while it is the most used Path, it probably has the least amount of study on it.

                                                  Well designed Paths must be internally consistent and work to restrain the Beast from taking over. It needs to be consistent because if there are obvious loopholes, the Beast will make sure to exploit that and the vampire will degenerate. All Paths are supposed to be designed to allow the vampire to tell the Beast "No, I will not allow you to push me to kill this person, attack this other person, not forcing me to feed right now at this very inconvenient moment" and so on. Because if they let the Beast make that determination for them (as opposed to the vampire consciously deciding what to do), they'll degenerate. (We all know many Paths are badly written because they don't actually provide a good basis at preventing the Beast from taking over, but that is a mechanical design issue with the writers often forgetting the purpose of the Path and instead just write up strange orange/blue moralities that actually don't provide any impediment to the Beast.)

                                                  So that can be one reason why the Path of Humanity at its highest levels can be overly strict. It's a function of the Path needing to be consistent and ordered in a way actual human morality cannot be because reality is messier than we'd like with lots of moral dilemmas.

                                                  In practice, most vampires are going to find it almost impossible to retain Path rating of 8,9 and 10 long term. Even Path ratings of 7 should be hard. Most vampires end up residing somewhere in the 4-6 range. That is just how things are. But NPC Path rankings rarely make sense. They are usually intended as a kind of power score rather than a realistic depiction of what their Path rating should be.

                                                  I don't care about vampire Path of Humanity rankings and True Faith in my chronicles because I would never allow a vampire (PC or NPC) to possess them. In terms of mortals and True Faith, I use the guidelines I mentioned in my earlier post.
