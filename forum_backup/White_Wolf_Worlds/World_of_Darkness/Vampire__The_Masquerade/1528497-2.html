                        The V5 supplements haven't done anything that particularly backs up the idea that V5 is going to continue to take ideas from VtR (in fact, it's more the opposite at this point), H5 and W5 even scaled back taking stuff from their CofD counterparts by a huge degree. The WoD5 books are still looking for ideas from other games to try to make games that seem more modern (H5's danger track for example, like Loresheets before it, was lifted from non-WoD related games), but the idea that the current teams at Paradox and Renegade are trying to further mash the WoD and CofD together doesn't hold up to a lick of scrutiny.

                        And so, they didn't kill CofD because it was redundant, they killed it because CofD fans liked it better than WoD5. Vigil 2e outsold H5 on DTRPG, as a data point on that. Despite how much they liberally lift game ideas from other games, Paradox thinks stopping the CofD is going to magically "reunify" the different fan groups instead of just pissing people off so people go elsewhere (or just the STV) for new CofD stuff.

                        And they have killed it in all but official cancellation: Paradox refuses to greenlight new CofD books. Calling it "editorial" redundancy is giving the decision makers far too much credit as business folks. The 20th books and the CofD 2e books coexisted just fine. They're not redundant. They're not redundant despite some cross-pollination that happened during that period as Onyx Path folks were working on both lines. V5 and VtR are only redundant on Paradox's financial spreadsheets.

                                                            Semantic hair splitting isn't the the argument you seem to think it is MrDrika. The idea of "blood potency" not the specific implementation, has been part of Vampire from the beginning: that's what Generation is. How potent your blood is. The idea that certain sources of blood are worth more than others has also been in the game from the beginning. V5 just added greater levels of variation within each Generation (something fans had been asking for since the 90s to decrease the incentives for everyone to start with five dots in Generation). It did so by clunkily meshing Generation and VtR's "Blood Potency" trait; but that doesn't mean VtR 1e was the first time "blood potency" actually was something the Vampire games cared about just because it was the first time one of the games used it as a name for a trait.

                                                            The same this is true of Touchstones. VtR just formalized something that was already narratively present and discussed in VtM. It gave a name to mechanics to something that was already supposed to be there. VtM had very early on established that maintaining close connections to humans was a key part of keeping your Humanity high/raising it. Did it call that "Touchstones" or have mechanics? No. But it was there from pretty much the the beginning when discussing the role-playing aspects of Humanity.

                                                            If you think some sort of "gotcha!" game where VtR was the first to use these as explicit terms with explicit mechanics means anything? I find it more likely you've never actually read a VtM book or played one, than the possibility that you have a serious point to make.
