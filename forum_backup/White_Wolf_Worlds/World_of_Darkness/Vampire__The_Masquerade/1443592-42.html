                        Yeah it works for one guy, but when you're all that guy, it doesn't fucking work does it?

                        A full frenzy is good for two reasons; First, it's your fault for getting into a situation where you might frenzy, because the Beast is predictable and hates certain things and you can keep it right by making sure those things it hates aren't around when a frenzy would be a big problem.                         Messy criticals are terrible because first, it's your fault for not hiding in your haven all night every night or murdering a guy, because they can happen at any time and any place unless you murder someone, and secondly every time the dice show you're going to fuck up the storyteller has to lean back and think of some bullshit way you have fucked things up; there isn't a clear divide between beast and man, so it's your character screwing up, and the ST is screwing him up for you.

                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                            Tommy may be the Brujah of the group but if you don't think that Lodin or other Ventrue have done the EXACT SAME THING then I feel like you've lost a lot of the central horror of vampire.
                            I see nothing in the game that suggests Lodin in specific, or Ventrue in general, regularly kill lower ranking members of their organization over minor mistakes that they blow out of proportion.

                            I don't see how every vampire being Tommy DeVito is at all demonstrating an understanding of the "central horror" of vampire. The central horror of vampire is supposed to be personal. Personal horror requires agency. The lessons for horror in Goodfellas are the compromises Tommy's friends make because of Tommy's instability, as their emotional bond to Tommy and the requirements of their existence come into conflict, and they have to make choices none of which are "good." Henry is the main character for a reason, as it's his story of how all of the choices he made ended up sabotaging who he wanted to be.

                            You will kill people you don't want to kill.
                            Tommy wanted to kill the guy though.

                            Yes, vampires invariably kill someone. But as I bang on about all the time here... execution of the rules matters. Killing people because you rolled bad, they happened to be there, and the Beast made you do it undercuts the horror of this (yes, even in pre-V5 VtM). The people your character kills because the world they live in puts them in a position where they feel killing someone (or at least putting themselves in a situation where they know it's likely that they'll kill someone) are the ones that leave real horror in their wake, because the character had agency and the consequences are on them. The Curse is that vampires are driven into an existence where they are constantly tempted to choose to devalue life, not that they're Cursed to randomly murder people; at least if you want to focus on the horror of "A Beast I am, Least a Beast I become," and all that.

                            As early as first Edition, the Prince of Milwaukee murdered his wife in a frenzy during a relatively normal fight.
                            Yes, we know, Frenzies have always been a thing.

                            But this is the difference between writing fiction and playing RPGs: The authors of a NPC's story can make the Frenzy appropriate and dramatic because they don't have to roll the dice to see what happens. It was possible, so it happened, because that's the story that was being told.

                            When you're playing the game, you want to be able to differentiate between places where this drama is good for the narrative, and places it isn't. If you want this moment to really work in a game, the PC has to come home in a bad state, so the fight with the NPC loved one is an obvious risk of the Beast coming out. So there's agency: they choose to stay and have the fight instead of leaving and doing something else, so that when that bad roll happens and the Beast slips in, the vampire can't blame the Beast honestly.

                                                            Yeah, as ST, I've never rolled a degeneration check for either humanity or path rating on NPCs.

                                                            Then again, I roleplay the NPC as behaving strictly according to their humanity or Path rating, so I wouldn't have to even if that were a thing in my games.

                                                            Humanity and Path-Rating are both blueprints when it comes to NPCs, who are there to fill certain roles: the compassionate mentor leading you towards golconda, the NPC who is ruthless but still has certain moral lines he won't cross to screw you over, the NPC with no moral lines whatsoever, the NPC Path Paragon who has strict moral lines, but those lines are alien and a minefield for players to interact with, etc, etc. Since the humanity or Path Rating is a blueprint for the ST to roleplay them rather than something they struggle with organically, there is no reason to have them violate that humanity level or path rating.

                                                            If anything, having them violate that would be rather odd, like having the wooden chair suddenly be made of marshmallow.
