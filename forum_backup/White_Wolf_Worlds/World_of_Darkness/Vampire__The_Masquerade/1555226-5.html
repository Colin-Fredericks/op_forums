            Dan Pinchbeck, one of the cofounders of The Chinese Room (who has left The Chinese Room in 2023), was on a podcast talking about the development of Bloodlines 2.

            From Rick Lane's article on PCGamer:

            As is so often the case, it appears Bloodlines 2's adoptive developers were highly aware of this potential problem. Speaking to Cat Burton of the Goth Boss Podcast (via PCGamesN) TCR cofounder Dan Pinchbeck explained how he urged Paradox Interactive not to call Bloodlines 2, well, that.

            Pinchbeck says that, from the start, The Chinese Room intended to build its own version of the game, rather than try to finish the work done by Hardsuit Labs up to that point. "When we first started talking to Paradox, I said—we'd even seen the Hardsuit build—I was like 'We're a studio that [has] built our reputation on storytelling.'

            "And I guess you want to do it justice. You want to take care of it. But taking over a project that had already been in development as well, that's such a tricky thing to take on. We are an original IP studio. The only way we're going to want to do this is if we are able to, basically, we'll take everything that's there and use as much as we can. But we don't just want to finish someone else's game."

            Paradox, apparently, agreed to this. But Pinchbeck says the "tricky question" was whether The Chinese Room's should herald its status as a sequel to Bloodlines 1, something he repeatedly discussed with one of Paradox's former producers. "We used to sit there and have these planning sessions of 'how do we get them to not call it Bloodlines 2?' You can't make Bloodlines 2. There's not enough time. There's not enough money."

            Pinchbeck points out that the original Bloodlines came out at a "really interesting period" when games like Stalker and Shenmue were shooting for the moon in terms of roleplaying simulation. "You could ship a really ambitious game that was full of bugs and holes, was totally flawed, but the ambition was really exciting."

            Pinchbeck says that "you couldn't get away with" that style of design now. "Trying to recreate that magic in a different environment felt wrongheaded. No one would be happy. You wouldn't make people who love Bloodlines 1 happy and you wouldn't make people who didn't know about Bloodlines 1 happy, because they'd never get Bloodlines 2 and they'd always get a flawed game that was built too fast and with not enough money."

            To briefly interject, I'd point out that making an ambitious but broken RPG wasn't acceptable in 2004 either—Bloodlines was a commercial failure that resulted in the death of Troika. Its reputation has been salvaged mainly through time and the absurd dedication of one German chemist.

            Nonetheless, Pinchbeck is correct that it's even less acceptable now than it was then. Hence, in a pitch that he says would make Bloodlines 1 fans elicit a "psychic scream", Pinchbeck said to Paradox: "We can't make Bloodlines 2, we can't make Skyrim, but we can make Dishonored. And if we look at something which is not an RPG and is not fully open world, but is really tightly focused and true to the mythos, and it's a good ride, we get a Bloodlines title out in the world.

            "And then we started talking about saying, then 'what would the next big Bloodlines game look like after that, if that happened'?" In other words, The Chinese Room tried to rework Bloodlines 2 as vampire Dishonored as a stepping stone to another Bloodlines game, perhaps Bloodlines 3 or, if The Chinese Room had its way, the true Bloodlines 2.

            Personally, I don't think The Chinese Room pulled off this plan of making vampire Dishonored either—though you can see that DNA more clearly than you can Bloodlines 1. And I think Pinchbeck recognises this too, as he says that "like every other game" Bloodlines 2's development became a process of "untangling an anaconda fuckball of competing priorities and what everybody wants and things like that".​
            The part I want to highlight here is that on one hand, Paradox desperately wanted to release something called Bloodlines 2 - but they had no realistic plan whatsoever to make that happen. Bloodlines 2 was supposed to be a bigger game and I can understand that any studio might be reluctant to agree to "simply finish the work" under those circumstances. There are all sorts of issues and challenges with making that happen.

            But then another baffling thing is that if Dan Pinchek is to be believed both TCR and well, I guess, some part of Paradox were aware that the way they were handling this would lead to issues if they were going to call the game Bloodlines 2... and nothing came of that. At least, I understand why Paradox leadership was so pessimistic in 2024. They had already been talking for over a year how calling the TCR-game "Bloodlines 2" is a bad idea.

            The way I think it makes sense is that ultimately the choices were between calling the TCR-game "Bloodlines 2" (obviously a bad idea) or telling your shareholders that Bloodlines 2 would be yet another canceled boondoggle of a game that Paradox had spent tens of millions of dollars on.

                    With Pinchbeck's comments in mind, I started to think about these bizarre, harsh comments from 2024 again. "Bloodlines 2 is a dead end", "If Bloodlines 3 will ever get made, it will be by someone else" etc. With the context Dan provides, this now makes a lot more sense. The whole point of that was to loudly reject the idea of this released TCR-Bloodlines-2 being a "stepping stone" for a Bloodlines 3 which would be a true sequel to Bloodlines 1.

                    I'm sure from TCR's perspective at the time that made sense. And I'm sure there were even people at Paradox that were happy to go along with that. Given the limitations for the TCR-version of Bloodlines 2 (both in terms of money and time), Bloodlines 2 can't be a true sequel. And if the goal is to make a true sequel to Bloodlines 1, why not make a Bloodlines 3? And maybe even rename this "Bloodlines 2" to make it clear that it's just a stepping stone.

                    That's the developers point-of-view, though... I'm sure, other people at Paradox were probably having a heart-attack at the notion of a Bloodlines 3. Because I also think the majority of the 60 million dollars went to Hardsuit Labs side of the project. And this proposal essentially meant investing in three games - at least, in terms of money. Of course, all the work and money poured into the Hardsuit Labs was wasted. But this proposal was calling to finance the unfinished Hardsuit Labs version (which was essentially wasted), the TCR-Dishonored pseudo-"Bloodlines 2" and the much bigger true sequel "Bloodlines 3".

                    (I also could envision that "If Bloodlines 3 will ever get made, it will be made by somebody else" meant Paradox might hope to find somebody in the future who takes the Hardsuit Labs stuff and simply "finishes the game" as a cheap way to make another Bloodlines... well, good luck to them if that's what they're dreaming about...)

                    But giving TCR neither enough money nor enough time to make a true sequel (which are both hallmarks of a bad publisher) while also being aware all along how much of a disappointment it would be to release this supposed "stepping stone" as just "Bloodlines 2" and while also being under a lot of pressure to release something called "Bloodlines 2"... I mean, this is like a perfect case-study of a publisher mismanaging a project. Paradox had managed to maneuver itself into a position where there were only bad options. Releasing this underfunded TCR-version called "Bloodlines 2" was simply the least bad option available to them.

                    And it goes back to them not having learnt a thing from this. I highly doubt Iceflake studios will produce a console edition for Cities Skylines 2 or will magically redeem the project in the eyes of older fans in a speedy fashion. More likely, at some point Iceflake studios will tell Paradox that they can't fulfill their expectations because of the lack of time and money they're given by Paradox. And then once again, just as TCR was telling Paradox they won't be able to make a true sequel to Bloodlines 1, Paradox will have to face the fact that Iceflake studios can't magically improve Cities Skylines 2. Then once again Paradox will have to confront how much they've already spent on Cities Skylines 2, and as a result, they'll end up settling for mediocrity or worse. Because they're now too far along on this path to change course. And what consumers will end up experiencing is just more disappointment and frustration.

                        I think the most depressing part of Bloodlines 2 and its handling is that it very much is a game that has strong bones and could be, in its present state, the perfect sort of game for expansion. Pouring more resources into it at this point must feel like a sunk cost fallacy writ large. However, I feel this is a game that feels like its never going to be able to reach its full potential. I like it for what it is but its the vanilla shake in its current form.
