    Originally posted by Black Fox View Post

    This is something I think every ST needs to determine for themselves because I always found this idea to be stupid. Princes just don't acknowledge whoever shows up in their domain. They have the power to refuse admission. Nor does acknowledgment simply require a vampire to give their name and clan, and the Prince accepts it at face value like a chump. It's more like an interview collecting lots of personal data that can be verified later. Who is their sire? How long have they been a vampire? What were there past domains? Who can vouch for their character? And there's likely a probationary period where a new vampire is allowed to stay, but not truly acknowledged while things get checked out. And during this time they are watched by ghouls, mortal servants, and the Prince's henchmen and allies.

    I think this gets overlooked by a lot of STs and PCs because people want to move on with the game, but that's actually what would happen. Different Princes and different domains will vary in terms of their strictness and leniency, but you can bet when it appears any threat rises, Princes become more strict. The Sabbat Crusades of the 1960s would have put everyone on alert for the next few decades.

    While all sects have their geniuses and fools, in general a vampire does not get to be Prince unless they're smart and cunning. So this idea the Sabbat can easily infiltrate is just dumb. No system is fool proof, so infiltration can and does happen. But I don't find the idea credible that Sabbat infiltrators always accomplish their mission. There's a lot of risk. Some fail. And to make a mission succeed, the Sabbat needs to spend time and resources to enable it to happen so even success has its cost. And if you burn a long time deep cover agent to accomplish something, there's extensive opportunity costs.

    In general, the game has often relied on the idea that NPCs are stupid in order to make the metaplot work. This was very true in Revised. But it makes for bad movies, and it makes for a bad RPG setting.
    The books are very clear that vampires are an extremely secretive and isolationist species. A lot of vampires don't know how many Clans there are or what their names are, let alone the detailed knowledge the Players have from reading the books. Certainly, there's no database of how many vampires or who they are about. A vampire introduces themselves as Fred and says he's the childe of Dave, there is no way to verify this. Any vampire who is stupid enough to try to assemble a name and description of every vampire in their domain, let alone for the rest of the Kindred race, will be set out for the sun.

    I always emphasized the Sabbat's infiltrators and covert agents in my games, though. I felt Sabbat undermining the stability of domains and converting people was a big part of their appeal. The shovelheads and vozhd never appealed to me versus the crafty shadow wars.

    Personally, I always felt it was a mistake to make the Lasombra so belligerent to their Antitribu and that the Lasombra should have always had a substantial presence in the organization. However, that was the depiction as presented in the previous books. I feel like the depiction in V5 is a course correction and moves canon in the right direction.

    But YMMV.

    I do find it funny a lot of people seem to have thought the numbers were always what V5 is making them.

        DAVE GETS DRAGGED OUT INTO THE SUN BY SOME GHOULS THE NEXT MORNING AND IS INCINERATED UNDER THE SUN.

        I can do lots of variations on that, emphasizing any number of things or how obstacles might be overcome.

        The idea that the Prince of a city, or some other vampire assisting him, doesn't know how to contact other important vampires is just ludicrous. Even if all he knows is the contact information of an Archon serving on the same continent, that Archon knows enough people to make calls and get responses to the Prince in a reasonable amount of time. Not all Archons need to be warriors. Some will specialize in Masquerade preserving activities. Others are glorified secretaries and bureaucrats for their Justicars who keeps tabs on people.

        Now there's going to be a fair amount of non-Sabbat vampires who can't answer all questions well. Maybe he's a Gangrel abandoned by his sire (who was subsequently destroyed), or a Brujah illegally embraced, a spurned Caitiff of uncertain ancestry, or something else that will cause that vampire trouble. Even in those cases, he'll likely have some kind of reputation where he's from, someone who knows him and can vouch for him. He may be in probationary status for a longer time, or harassed, or told to get out and he needs to wander somewhere else until he finds some craphole he can stay in and build a reputation. But he can likely give answers that won't immediately cause him to get kicked out, killed, or put under permanent suspicion. Or if he does, its tough luck for him. He'll eventually fall among Anarchs who are the ones who actually don't care. Or maybe gets killed by Lupines. Or runs afoul of the Sabbat or mortal hunters. But it's his bad luck, not that of the Camarilla or the local Prince. It also fits in historically with why certain clans always complained about the Camarilla harassing them.

        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
        Any vampire who is stupid enough to try to assemble a name and description of every vampire in their domain, let alone for the rest of the Kindred race, will be set out for the sun.
        This is literally the job of the Prince according to the Traditions of Domain and Hospitality. There will be lots of things a Prince doesn't know about in his domain, but any Kindred presenting himself for acceptance in the domain is going to get a lot of questions. And even if there are vampires who are never presented the Prince in the city, the Prince likely learns about them at some point (what he does about it is a different matter). Certainly in the smaller towns, this is completely doable. In larger cities, like canonical Chicago this is more difficult. But we know Lodin always struggled with this, but he never stopped trying.

        Vampires who show up and don't present themselves may be under the radar of a Prince for a long time. He can find the malcontents, discontented, and outright Anarchs. But he won't be showing up to Elysium or hanging out with respectable members of the Camarilla. And if or when his name and appearance gets reported to the Prince, the Prince will know this vampire entered his domain illegally.

        Information is power, and among vampires of the Camarilla even more so. I don't see this as being stupid or objectionable. This is expected and common.

        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

Its for long term deep cover agents who can be activated when absolutely needed. Most Sabbat spies aren't infiltrators, but the two man scout teams like Wendy and Philippe in CbN 1st who observe secretly and try to avoid interacting with other vampires.

        In general, I like how you describe how you run your games. But this is definitely one area I think is flawed.
