                            Originally posted by Lysander View Post
                            Your right that most ancients wouldn't really be concerned by the happenings of what is going in Chicago, New York, Toronto or Vancouver unless they reside there. Sometimes I found it hard to picture that even elders that Moncada would concern himself of what is going in the New World when he would have enough to deal with Madrid as he is more or less the Prince there.
                            It's not so much geographical concerns in my mind, as it is methuselahs -- true methuselahs -- have been there and done that for pretty much anything, and temporal politics -- mortal or vampire -- are just not on their radar.

                            I mean, I should take a step back on that one and work with some definitions. A methuselah is technically any vampire more than a thousand years old, generally of the fourth and fifth generations but some sixth or even seventh generation vampires should certainly be attributed with the label. But, there's still a huge difference between a vampire embraced in the wake of the collapse of the Roman empire, and those embraced prior to the rise of it, or in some cases the Bronze Age collapse or even the dawn of human civilization...even among vampires of the same nominal generation.

                            You rightly pointed out the "discovery" of the Americas as something of which methuselahs may not take interest, but it's in far excess of that. These are vampires that have seen wars that last centuries, the rise and fall of empires and dynasties that lasted a thousand years and spanned the world as it was "known" at the time, multiple "ends of the world" and consequent dark ages. If you witnessed -- hell, actively participated in or helped cause -- an event like the Bronze Age collapse, the threat of nuclear holocaust just won't hit the same. If anything, a methuselah that old will likely just laugh and say the sooner, the better. At least it would wipe the planet of excess human populations and all this problematic technology, and bring back "the good old days".

                            Even among vampire politics...Camarilla, Sabbat? they're fads, just two new factions of, by, and for no-account inferiors to elevate their own station, waging the same conflicts that have always been waged between vampires. The Ashirra predates either by nearly a millennium, and even that's a "new" sect based entirely around -- of all things -- a mortal religion. They're nothing compared to the Second City, Carthage and Rome, and whatever vampiric societies lost to time existed in between. And just like those, these new sects will pass into history in time -- and so will their celebrants and apologists, unless they wise up.

                                                Originally posted by Lysander View Post
                                                My definition of ancients are over 2,000+ and usually are around the 4th-6th generation and the ones that are 1,000 years old are still elders and haven't go through the profound emotional and mental changes that true ancients have gone through yet.

                                                But to be fair, that is how we do it our game that the most active elders are members of the 7th-9th generation with the 10th and 11th generations starting to come into there power.
                                                This is (indirectly) one of the reasons I prefer the concept of Gehenna as cyclical, and merely a supernatural event correspondent to regional, continental, or global crises if not outright civilizational collapse. There's a mass die-off among neonates and ancillae, elders close ranks or die off, methuselahs start calling audibles in response to rapid upheaval, maybe an Antediluvian rolls over and farts in their sleep; at the end, vampires repopulate and occupy new social niches as best-fits however mortal society reshapes itself, in accordance to whatever vampiric sects rise out of the ashes. Either way generations consolidate, and "age gaps" manifest between the vampires who survived the events and those embraced in their wake.

                                                From there you can categorize and sub-categorize vampires by how many "apocalypses" a vampire survived, and the psychological impact of each.
