        Here's another NPC from my TC game, which I just introduced during the last game session:

        Klauss Gottfried von Schaffhausen, childe of Malgorzata, Tremere conspirator envoy; embraced 1111 AD (intentionally; stupid Hermetic numerology)

        While most Tremere busy themselves waging the Omen War directly, or indirectly via outreach to other clans and playing prestation, Klauss Gottfried von Schaffhausen has methods and intent entirely his own. As childe of Malgorzata and member of Goratrix's conspirator faction, while his efforts benefit the Tremere as a whole, Klauss ensures the favor, influence, and boons he gains go to furthering his sire's and grandsire's interests in Hungary first and foremost. However, his methods and expertise are founded solely within the realm of logistics, an art largely lost since the fall of the Roman Empire, and in increasingly higher demand as the War of Princes looms.

        As a thaumaturge, he has mastered Transitus Velociter, and has secondary expertise in the newly-adapted Path of Mercury, as well as in Weather Control. He commands significant allies, contacts, merchant influence, as well as a spy network made of those very same merchants, caravans, and guides, as well as having a strong working knowledge of not just trade routes but back roads and hidden passageways that allow he and his companions to travel with a minimum of risk from lupines, hunters, bandits, and marauding Cainites. He busies himself as a courier and merchant guide, to see messages between cities carried, and other, more prominent, Cainites' pawns escorted safely.

        Klauss has no taste for, nor skill in, battle, and at first hint of trouble he will teleport away, care of his own knowledge of Path of Mercury, or by using Escape to a True Friend in the direst of emergencies. This has been of use to House Tremere on more than one occasion, as while neonate Tremere may fall to Tzimisce fangs, word of the attack and the best-possible measures for reprisal never fail to reach elders' ears. Luckily for Klauss, this fate has yet to befall customers outside the clan, but should it his prestige and influence will fall greatly, as he would be considered at best an untrustworthy guide, and at worst a coward and oathbreaker (not that either are necessarily false).

        Of late, he aided a group of Cainites contracted by Vencel Rikard to build a keep in Tihuta Pass to travel from Budapest to Mediasch well ahead of schedule. Choosing for his own reasons to meet with the Prince, Nova Arpad, later, he notices her unusual behavior and seeks to investigate. He will play a role in the rescue of the real Nova Arpad and ouster of her imposter, Ruxandra, and will secure a place for himself among the ruling coterie of Balgrad, which he will exploit to establish a chantry in the city in the dawning years of the 13th Century.

            #67 Muffled dialect and hazy images are the only clues Slate has about his past since the five years he was embraced. The last memory he has before rising from the dead includes horrified screaming, and that's not much of a surprise. Slate is a Gargoyle, a free Gargoyle who was naturally embraced, not the byproduct of some Tremere's magical experiment. His sire made sure to instill one vital lesson into him early on; do not trust the Tremere, ever!

            The Tremere wanted to shackle him, leash him, compel him, enslave him, command him, enthrall him, break him... destroy him! The Tremere were slave masters who eyed his kind with greedy ambition. Slate would not allow himself to fall under their spell, he would fight to the death, rather than be caged. And so with his sire's guidance, he joined the Anarchs, a collection of like minded kindred who seemed to hate the idea of slavery just as much as him, possibly even more so!

            As a certified amnesiac, Slate is the nickname given to him by his Anarch brethren, and references the stony appearance commonly associated with Gargoyles. Piecing together the memories of his past is one of his constant goals. During the last five years, Slate has made it his mission to recover his memory and learn about the man he used to be. But it's only been five years since that night, and he doesn't even know where to begin looking for answers. Regardless, he is convinced that a vigilant search will begin to reveal clues about his past.


            ----------------------------

            Role Playing: As a Gargoyle, Slate has the potential to bring some dry humor to the table. The mental image of a statuesque monster barely fitting into the back seat of a car while two other vampires are scrunched against the windows is sure to entertain players, as is his Drax the Destroyer style way of delivering unintended jokes. While his desire to find out about his past gives Slate something more to focus on than being the token bruiser of the group. Even better is if the ST decides to use his curiosity to lead him (and his coterie) into danger!

                            71) Every child inevitably goes through that angsty rebellious phase at some point in their life. They refuse to listen to anybody, because they think they've got the world figured out. For most people, this is simply a phase they grow out of, but others insist on clinging to their bitterness towards the world. So it was that the young and petulant Caleb Booth found himself a willing convert to the Baali. Originally embraced into the Bruja, Caleb's rebellious streak went beyond the standard hot bloodedness of the rest of the clan and bordered somewhere along the lines of asinine childishness and self destructive anger. Instead of the hardened biker gang banging style, he went with the more gothic demon worshiping outlook. At first, it was all bullshit, reciting false prayers and improvised rituals he found in a novelty bookshop. Caleb had no idea that somebody was watching him night after night. Eventually his own sire got sick of his shit and decided to cut him loose, leaving Caleb to bring about his own downfall, which is when Caleb's secret admirer decided to reveal herself to him. A few honeyed words and some revealed skin and Caleb was practically floating out the door with her in the lead.

                            She took him to the beach, to an inflatable life boat and told him to get in. When he did, she began to row them out to sea; neither one of them were seen again until the following night, at which point Caleb seemed a lot more focused than before.


                            Before his transformation into a Baali, Caleb already made an ass of himself when it came to demon worship and pagan rituals. Most people are used to it by now, so the occasional demonic activity doesn't draw as much attention as it perhaps should have when he's involved in the picture. Caleb has proven uncharacteristically skilled at using this to his advantage. He can act with more freedom than other infernalists; but even he is cautious about pushing his luck. He's only just started learning the powers his new existence grants him and revealing his cards too early would prove disastrous to say the least. Thankfully, he's got a smoking hot teacher to show him the do's and don't's of being the most hated clan / bloodline in kindred society!

                                A few I recently wrote out for a similar topic on another forum:

                                72) Wake-Up Man
                                An archaeologist that didn't learn the meaning of those shadowy late night consulting jobs until he was embraced into Clan Nosferatu. Often enough he was better at figuring out what happened in the past than those that were actually around at the time. With the help of his Clan's information network this allows him to perform a unique function in Kindred society. For a price he tracks down the whereabouts of vampires lost to torpor.

                                73) Serpent in Muslimah's Clothing
                                Her years as a mortal Setite cultist had left her with some practical problems. The same devotion that eventually earned her the embrace made it very difficult to function in polite society anymore. With tattoos everywhere from her shaved head to her feet, covering up was the only option. Since she's become one of the most pious appearing members of the local Muslim community. Actually trying to undermine it at every turn, dodging True Faith keeps her pretty busy though.

                                74) Camarilla Shovelhead
                                He's the lone survivor of a failed Sabbat raid. By the time he emerged from the earth the fighting had already ended. The only reason he wasn't executed on the spot was a familiar face. His uncle, who he had believed to be long dead was a Kindred with just enough clout to save his unlife. It's now up to him to make the best of it as a Lasombra Antitribu.

                                75) Rebel Economist
                                A Ventrue chose a brilliant economy student for the embrace. Not the sure way to make a new fortune that he hoped for. The Childe in question had Marxist ideals that are quite incompatible with proper Camarilla society. He's in fact now secretly looking to bring the local Kindred business interests crumbling down. And simultaneously he hopes to instigate an Anarch takeover.

                                76) Vengeful Widow
                                As a young woman her parents coerced her into a marriage with a man of wealth. He turned out to be a horrible husband. One day she just snapped and killed him. The police couldn't prove anything, but unfortunately for her a Nosferatu wasn't going to let this slide. He was her late husband's distant ancestor, guiding his family to prosperity over the generations. Her having killed his favorite living relative, mucking up all his plans, he gave her the worst punishment he could think of; the Nosferatu embrace. Horrific as this may be, it hasn't cowed the woman. She is still not one to keep tolerating abuse, murder plans are in the works again.

                                    #77: Orson Pratt was brilliant, talented and gifted. Everyone said so. His parents, Clem and LaDeane Pratt, realized as an infant he would babble along in tune to the radio. His seven brothers and sisters would put on a show every Christmas, and when Orson was three he played a flawless "Gloria In Excelsis Deo" on the family's electric keyboard. Each Monday, when Clem and LaDeane would turn off the television and declare a "Family Home Evening", the family would gather to play board games, read stories aloud, and sing along to Orson's playing the keyboard, from a songbook Grandma Young had given him for his birthday. When he was five he climbed up on the piano stool in the church social hall, and flawlessly played back, by ear, that morning's hymn, "As Zion's Youth in Latter Days".

                                    As soon as the ward choir director heard him play, he was entered into a musical kindergarten program run by the Salt Lake City School District. Orson excelled, and the ward's bishop led a fundraising appeal to pay for a special piano tutor, pointing out to the congregation that music was clearly Orson's special gift. The tutor, a music professor at BYU, and Orson met five nights a week, using the piano in the ward social hall. In seventh grade, Orson was playing well enough to win first prize at the Utah Symphony Orchestra's Lyceum Music Festival, entering an original composition.

                                    Orson was, frankly, bewildered why everyone made such a stir over his music. It's not like it was hard for him. He just sat at the piano, and his fingers did the rest. The pieces he wrote weren't so much a labor of creation, rather more like remembering something from long ago, and then writing it down. To be honest, he would rather do something actually challenging. Once a month, his dad would take all the brothers up to Bear Lake, where Uncle Nephi and Aunt Odonna lived. Dad would load up the passenger van on Friday afternoon, and they would drive until night-time. The next morning, before dawn, all the guys would spread out along the lake and just fish. Breathing the thin, cool air of the high desert, as the morning insects buzzed and landed along the rocky shore, like a mirage on some alien desert, planet. Orson let his mind wander, pretending he was fishing on Tattooine or Dune. The swaying of the lake surface, shaded by the long dark reaches of the circling mountains silhouetted by the rising dawn would lead Orson to a deeper music, an older music. This music was like the vibration in the ground during a thunderstorm or the cramping of your stomach when you were sick. It was the natural music, the music of the flesh and leaf and antler and fin. A music too pagan for his family to hear or understand.

                                    Those trips to Bear Lake were what Orson missed most when he went on his mission to Madagascar. Oh, he enjoyed serving the church, and he really loved hearing Malagasy music, like polyphonic a capella, tsapika, and jejy voatavo. This was music which understood the music of Bear Lake better than any Utah hymnal ever could. When he returned and went to Julliard, he worked to fuse Malagasy music and other world music traditions with the vernacular music of rural North America. Oh, he played proper, classical piano impeccably, his hands wide, sinewy and precise, like soaring birds, but firm and insistent as a flash flood in a desert gully. He mastered Tchaikovsky's Violin Concerto in D Major and the Ligeti Etudes, but his heart was always in the simple and direct, but never simplistic or reductionist traditional music of the developing world.

                                    Orson entered the MFA program in Musical Composition at Carnegie Mellon in Pittsburgh. His senior piece was a full orchestral symphony which blended traditional American church hymns with the vibrant and often plaintive sounds of Malagasy music. When it officially debuted, played by the Boston Symphony at Tanglewood, NPR called Orson, "one of America's most creative and fearless young composers. At turns sullen and vibrant, sadistically fast and lusciously maestoso. He is THE new voice to be followed. Expect to hear much more from him!"

                                    Two weeks later, as Orson was driving back to Pittsburgh from Massachusetts, he was embraced by a Toreador fan. They faked his death in a traffic crash, but Orson was not sure if that was a lie. He had lost so much, for an art that mattered so little to him. Sure, he was gifted at music, but so what? Had he been gifted with a wrench, he would have been a plumber. It was, for Orson, just a job. Now, it was a job he was expected to never, ever retire from. And the rest of his clan? Gol, they were a mixed up bunch! Always going on about how decadent they were, but really they were just sad, having lost their free agency to the blood at the embrace. He was toasted as a prodigy by his peers and elders alike, but was simultaneously revolted by them and pitied them. He survived on animal blood as best he could, but knew he had to find a better environment if he was to preserve his humanity and his morality.

                                    Orson fled his sire, but didn't know where to go. He tried Utah, but to his great horror, vampires don't fare well in the shadow of faith cast by Temple Square. He suffered constant day-mares of being cast into the outer darkness whenever he closed his eyes. His apartment in Pittsburgh had been packed up by his parents. Madagascar? He loved that place, and maybe someday... but, the logistics were beyond him. So, for tonight, he just kept driving.

                                    He travels the country, mostly the western US and Canadian Rockies, in a light-proofed, white panel van. He pulls in at National Parks and roadside fishing camps for a few weeks at a time. He has met several Gangrel on the road who marvel that he has never run into a Lupine. But, then, maybe he has and didn't know it. His missionary training taught how to keep an upbeat attitude and a ready smile for anyone he met, and how to maneuver out of dangerous spots when necessary. The Gangrel just sigh deeply, shake his hand, and say it was nice meeting him.

                                    He doesn't get why they seem so worried. So what if there are werewolves; he's a vampire, right? So, he plans to just fish the most beautiful lakes and streams he can find for however long Heavenly Father sees fit to keep him here for his (im)Mortal Probation. If he has to die the final death, he'd like it to happen doing what he loves: in rubber waders, waist deep in the cold, deep water of a mountain lake, swinging a hand-tied lure over his head.

                                                Every night, little Denni Lou Jackson snuck out of her bedroom and climbed the tomato trellis up onto the shed roof behind her house down a little dirt road in Manning, SC. She would just lay up there, with her stuffed panda bear, Mr. Pibbles, asking him questions about the sky. Why are some stars kinda reddish? Why is that part of the sky lighter? How high can the birds fly? To the moon? She would drift off to sleep, paying no mind at all to the mosquitoes or her hard, corrugated-metal bed.

                                                Then the town siren would sound which meant everybody had to go inside, or at least be back on their own side of town. Denise's Mama usually waited until then to carry her off to bed. Mama would pretend to be annoyed at having to climb up there every night, but, honestly, with five kids and a long day of raising other people's kids, she was not about to complain about her quiet little four-year-old angel. Denni Lou may be peculiar, but at least she was peculiar in a way that made life a little easier.

                                                Denise did so well at school, her Baptist church raised money to send her off to a Catholic school in Orangeburg, near where her grandmother's people were from. She lived with aunts and uncles and no one minded, because she was polite, dutiful, smart as a whip (especially at math), always did her chores, and followed all their house rules... except one. Every night (if the sky was clear), there she'd be staring up, into the heavens.

                                                Denise graduated from high school at 17, and took some classes at South Carolina State, fulfilling all the distribution credits for math and science majors in one year, but couldn't afford to continue.

                                                She figured, with the federal government and LBJ pushing through civil rights legislation, the military would be a good plan for a black girl who already knew more math than most of her professors. She joined the US Air Force at 18, and excelled, as usual, despite the childish antics and hazing of many of the men. She made officer in 1970, but knew she could only go so far. Women weren't allowed to fly (or even serve) in combat, and she was more interested in the engineering side of things anyway. The USAF paid for her dual undergraduate degree in aerospace engineering and physics, with honors, from Stanford. Then it was back south to Georgia Tech for a Master's in Aerospace Engineering. She did her doctorate work in Monterey, CA, at the Naval Post-Graduate School, studying the mathematics of fluid dynamics in high temperature aerospace applications. All the while, she worked on engineering the next generation of jet fighters, whatever the next generation was that year.

                                                And, wherever she was stationed, every single night, she took a few minutes to stare up at the stars in wide wonder.

                                                She was eventually posted to a teaching position at Annapolis, from which she retired to civilian life in 1990. Her military pension allowed her to pursue her original passion: astronomy. She moved to Princeton, teaching calculus and engineering to undergrads while earning a second Master's in Astronomy, with a focus on what most of her colleagues thought was a crackpot field: exoplanetology.

                                                Denise didn't care what they thought, even when they would leave little Marvin the Martian toys and little-green-men stickers in her office mail box. She knew there must be other planets out there, after all, we've got nine. And she was fixing to find one. All it would take would be one jump in the radial red shift, one tug on the path of a star, just one unexplained sine wave in the chromatophotography, and she would have found a whole new world!

                                                Funding was hard to come by, so astronomers would send her their old data from other projects, and she would analyze it looking for anomalies.

                                                She found nothing.

                                                It was about this time she met Hans at the Midway Diner. He was a lawyer from Philly, and seemed to like her, but never actually flirted. They would sit every Friday night, talking for hours. About this and that, sometimes science, sometimes law, sometimes, they would just sit. Just spending time with him made her feel younger, like she would forever be on the healthy and spry cusp of sixty.

                                                This continued for years. Hans exulted with her when the data on 51 Pegasi B passed peer review. With the Hubble Telescope, whose propulsion controls she had troubleshooted, there was a cascade of new planets. A hundred. A thousand. Two thousand. Soon, four thousand would be in sight, in the one little patch of sky Hubble examined.

                                                When 451b, an Earth-like rocky planet in a habitable zone was certified, she was jubilant, and Hans was jubilant for her. Oh, he didn't understand the math, of course, but then she was vague on his legal theories. She had long resigned herself to their relationship being intellectually stimulating, if platonic. He seemed to genuinely come alive each night as they sat, talking in that diner. She had noticed years ago that he never ate, and had no reflection. But, he really seemed to understand her bittersweet joy at knowing... really knowing... that there were whole new worlds to explore, even if she would never live long enough to explore them.

                                                She knew he was older than he looked, but not how old until he told her the story of riding the prow of a ship at night. A ship captained by Ponce de Leon. Hearing breakers ahead, Hans saw the fires of the natives on the shoreline. He was there, in 1513, when Europeans first set foot on Florida. In a way, it was a whole new world... well, new to the Europeans, anyway. So, he understood what it was to search the abyss for a single point of light in the distance.

                                                Hours ago, she explained about the discovery of the Trappist-1 system. Seven Earth-like planets in the habitable zone! And, now, as she pulls herself up out of her shallow grave, she looks up at the stars and cries bloody tears of joy... 100 billion stars in the Milky Way alone... multiply that by ten trillion galaxies. Out there, looking back from the vast black void of the universe even the Antedeluvians must seem as small and insubstantial as neutrinos. Somebody has to be the first vampire to set foot on a new world, and see from that perspective.

                                                And that vampire will be a Lasombra. You can bet on it, or her name isn't Dr. Denise Louise Jackson; BA, BEng, MS, MEng, PhD, Capt. USAF ret.

                                                She's come a long, long way from that little dirt road with the segregation siren in Manning, SC, and she's got a helluva lot further to go!
