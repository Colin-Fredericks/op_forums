                Originally posted by Val_Nir View Post
                I would love to hear your personal take on who or what and under what circumstances could be capable of taking Vienna chantry out.
                The question supposes something rather off from the perspective of what the WoD is supposed to be about.

                The important question to any such effort is less about "how," and more about, "at what cost." The setting certainly tiers costs such that there are threats that have costs far beyond PCs in anything but epic long games with huge piles of XP and plot gained goodies, but nonetheless the question of was the cost worth it is what permeates the WoD's themes.

                This is what the main issue with taking out the Vienna Chantry as written really comes down to: The Second Inquisition didn't pay a cost. They weren't exposed to the world. Their supposed weaknesses didn't get easier for vampires to exploit. They didn't sacrifice a plot device they could only use once. They didn't lose a vital irreplaceable Inquisitor that will take decades of their best to train in the hopes of recovering the faith and skills lost. Etc.

                The Second Inquisition didn't pay a cost commensurate with the narrative challenge the Vienna Chantry represented.

                This is also the biggest issue with trying to change things to the Imbued. The Imbued, as they stand with their core themes, aren't up to pay those sorts of costs. Advancing them to the point where they could even try is radically changing Reckoning from it's intended themes into something else.

                                If anything, this discussion also helped me understand the problem with both sides. The pre-V5 WoD, which I prefer slightly more over the new one, was too overcrowded with supernatural superpowers. Not only VtM splat, any of them - after reading on all splats, just the corebooks, I had a feeling of the world overstuffed with supernatural, and not too many of them were subtle enough to make their existence a believable part of a "darker reflection of our world".
                                Sub-cults inside cults inside sects inside armies inside factions inside supernatural species. Archmages, methuselahs, werewolf elders, incarna, bodhisattvas, spectres running all over the place and wrecking stuff making the world tremble were too much to believe the mortals would never notice or dismiss it and go back to everydays business. The feeling of the setting was departing from gothic novella to a grimdark comicbook. It all certainly needed an update, but a careful one, a believable one, involving build up and proper connections between crucial story point which could certainly help explain the shift in the mood.

                                And that's where the new guard failed, to my mind. They took the whole update and amplified it up to eleven in a very 'in your face' manner; with BJD being the only thing connecting all the dots. While BJD is an amazing read and easily one of my favorite WW books ever, it's not enough to justify such a drastic turnaround. After reading V5 corebook, I had a feeling of the new direction being taken with too much of a zeal, like the authors almost overtly telling me in my face "If you want your methuselahs, sect wars, city gangrel commandos and tremere orbital strikes then go back play v20/revised/2ed vampions; this is a game for grown-ups now! Feeding, hunger, beast, personal drama, everynight needs. Focus please, you are not some superhero/supervillain with fangs."

                                I honestly hope that the authors could find a middle ground between the two extremeties some day.
