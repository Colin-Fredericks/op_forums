    Originally posted by Rhywbeth View Post
    Ultimately, what I get from VtM
    I think this is the key thing that determines what a game line is or isn't. The personal thing you connect to makes or breaks the meaning of the game setting and system.

    If you are the sort of person who needs to be seen as good, needs validation of others or society at large, needs to right the wrongs of an imperfect system.... then yeah, vampire is the stuff of nightmares. Your existence is a blight upon the world, you can never be truly known because you must hide, and you have all eternity to contemplate how much everything sucks.

    If you are the sort of person who yearns for tragedy and loss, the feeling being innately doomed and cursed, the knowledge that everything good is transitory at best, but suffering is eternal, then VtM is your pity party drug of choice.

    If you are the kind of person that is more the adapt and overcome type, where violence and cruelty is neither embraced nor avoided, then VtM is just a continuation of your story. You feed on others yes, but you don't have to kill to eat. You have to fight to survive, but so does every other living thing on the planet. Yes you can be alone in the dark, but that doesn't mean you have to be uncomfortable with it.

    The reality is that the first two types of vampires, which seem to make up most of the trope PC character types would not survive as vampires for very long. The third type however would survive and indeed potentially thrive as a vampire. The annoying thing is that vampire society seems designed to cater to the first two types, rather than the third which would be the only ones around. But that is an entirely different discussion.

    I have run vampire games that were incredibly silly (vampire idol band on tour) and I have run vampire games that messed with people's heads (play as self, accidentally butcher family members). The game is only a canvas for the ST and players to project their own psyches onto the table. If you are comfortable with being dangerous or monstrous, then there is little "personal horror" to be had in the game.

                        I have noticed that many players confuse "horror" with "depressing". But that is not the case. Some horror, of course, can be nihilistic and lend itself to depressing. But horror, and especially gothic horror, is part of the romantic tradition. And the romantic can incorporate many things that elevate it from anything depressing. I admit that this is something that the Revised era often forgot as it reacted against certain notions that the earlier editions lend itself not to "horror" but the "super-hero" genre (the "Vampions" concept).

                        Another mistake is for some players to confuse tragedy with "depressing". I am firmly in the camp that vampires are monsters that should always be destroyed. But that does not mean that vampire characters that struggle with morality are nihilistic or depressing figures. They are instead tragic figures. And there can be many positive emotions that come from that tragedy.

                        I think the actual issue is that some players play up nihilistic themes with vampire, confusing that with the concept of horror. Or simply want their power fantasies associated with vampirism to be affirmed, that they are shocked to hear other players bring up the negative aspects of vampirism.

                        Horror is to frighten. But that is not the same as to depress. The two can coincide, but I think in good games there should be enough other things to prevent the chronicle into descending into that.

                                                I think for me it's important to emphasize that the horror of VTM is less about the vampire's feelings or connection to god and more about the impact they have on the people around them.

                                                Your ghoul gets a divorce and loses custody because he spends all his nights with you, and he's happy about that, at least till the blood bond wears off.

                                                You vigorously fight any anti-corruption attempts from mortal govermnets and do your best to interfere with the democratic processes all so you can have more money and a better time feeding.
                                                Your best friends are also slimeballs you'll support at the expense of the masses.
                                                If you're killed, the system will replace you.
                                                This take confuses me. None of what you listed is horror, at best it is just how the world works and at worst it's just banality.

                                                A General can order his conscripted troops to fight and die with no consideration for the families the troops leave behind. The tactics and strategies used really don't matter as long as you achieve victory and don't out pace your replacement rate. History is full of generals who chose battle plans based on their egos and a desire to be remembered rather than what has the lowest casualty rate. Your job is to break, destabilize, and disable a given enemy faction, nation, government or military. It is not your job to worry about post war clean up or how the civilian population will recover or when the local economy will get back on its feet. If you are killed in battle, someone else will be promoted or otherwise put in charge of your troops.

                                                Horror isn't being a Twitch ethot and knowing that your simps are emptying their bank accounts to the detriment of their family's wellbeing but still accepting the money anyways. Horror isn't knowing bad people, nor is horror representing non-democratic governmental processes.

                                                Horror is the emotional reaction produced when you are not strong enough to deal with a situation or the requirements thereof. The General doesn't feel horror at the prospect of war, but the soldier in the trenches praying for the shelling to stop just for a little while feels endless horror.

                                                The problem with VtM as a game is that it wants to have personal horror as a component and as such its design practically forces conflict and infighting. Yet when you consider from an objective point of view how to fix the problems of the vampire setting and its society, the answers break the game design.

                                                            And leaving my hyperbole about 99% of vampires being the most untrustworthy bastards on the planet aside; I compromise on it by making the vampire reputation for backstabbing villains be an in-universe thing that even vampires themselves kind of buy into, which is then reinforced by their instinctively cautious nature and the fact that politicking does actually happen with some frequency.
