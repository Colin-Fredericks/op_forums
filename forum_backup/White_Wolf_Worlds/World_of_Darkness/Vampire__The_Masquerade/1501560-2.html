    So we could construct a story where Pendragon is seen as a great Brujah hero in Britain, someone willing to defy Mithras and the three powerful clans in a brave, but ultimately doomed, attempt to promote Brujah interests. While Pendragon had to flee into exile, his name is still highly regarded by the clan in Britain. Thus his war is seen as yet another attempt at a creation of a Brujah utopia again cruelly crushed by the hated Ventrue and Tremere. It is not material whether Pendragon ever bothered to articulate any kind of just rule as opposed to his own pride and ambition. What matters was his brief period of glorious success and eventual escape to New England. He could be seen as a Napoleonic type figure except that his exile was not done by his foes, but engineered by himself as an escape.

    It's even likely that Pendragon and Jeremy MacNeil knew each other very well. MacNeil might even have been a follower of Pendragon at one point. They are both charismatic warriors, but Pendragon is much more an organized soldier than the highland warrior MacNeil could ever be. Plus Pendragon is still a product of the English aristocracy and could never accept someone like MacNeil as a true equal. Eventually their divergent personalities and outlooks would drive them apart sometime after the American Revolution. MacNeil considers Pendragon to be a traitor to the cause. Pendragon thinks MacNeil is a romantic fool.

    This allows us to have Pendragon have both an original coterie of devoted Brujah followers who came with him to America, and a source of new British Brujah replacements who "grew up" on stories about him. Of course, besides British Brujah, I would also have some American Brujah who are their childer. Eventually Pendragon's followers are going to create new vampires to replace their losses and expand power.

    I also want to make some other comments on Pendragon. First, while I like the idea that Pendragon was a British officer in the War of Spanish Succession, I think his embrace in 1703 is a little too early. The British had not fought many battles by that time. It was limited to several successful (and brief) sieges along the River Meuse from Benlo to Liege. Not much opportunity for a man to truly make his name like Roman supposedly did. And rather far from Zurich where he was embraced by Dark Selina. Instead, I prefer the idea that Roman accompanied the British Army down south and fought in the Battle of Blenheim, the most decisive battle in the War of Spanish Succession, in August 1704. That would allow Roman to have spent time learning how to fight wars under the Duke of Marlborough (considered to be one of the great military geniuses of the time - and an ancestor of Winston Churchill), win renown in a famous battle, and be much nearer to Zurich which he could have conceivably visited at the end of the campaign season in the winter of 1704/1705. Second, I am rather pedantically bothered by his name "Roman", which would be an unusual name for an Englishman for this time period. I would probably change is to the similarly sounding, but more English, name of "Rowland" should I ever run a chronicle here. But I'll continue to keep it as Roman for this thread so I don't confuse anybody.

    Pendragon's long term goal is to defeat the Sabbat of New England, crush the Anarchs, and set himself as Prince in a unified Dominion of New England by holding a Conclave that endorses his rule. Then he'll drive the Sabbat out of New York and add that to his domain, maybe even retake the entire Eastern Seaboard. Eventually use that prestige to become Brujah Justicar, and use that position to help him achieve his real ultimate goal - return to Britain and establish his rule there. Pendragon is extremely unlikely to achieve all his goals, but he could very well achieve some of them.

    These two posts are already long, so I'll continue my thoughts on the other Brujah of New England later.​

    EDIT: Stupid me. I just realized that the Rowland name is already used for the Ventrue dynasty of Upton Rowlands. It's probably why the name stuck in my head. It's likely too confusing to have a Rowland Pendragon and Upton Rowlands. I'll have to find another name I like.

        What about those Brujah not supporting Pendragon? If we say that Pendragon has the loyalty of around half the Brujah of New England, the other half is in opposition divided between Camarilla and Anarchs I'd probably make one of these groups larger than the other, the larger one being whichever the ST wants.

        While it is not clear in Dark Colony whether Pug Jackson is an Anarch, or just a Brujah with lots of connections to Anarch, I'd make him the de facto Anarch leader. He would lead the Anarchs in Boston. I'd also have a much smaller group of Brujah Anarchs in Connecticut, maybe just 2-3 total.

        Most of the anti-Pendragon Camarilla Brujah would also be in Boston/Worcester, but I'd still have at least 1-2 in Connecticut, maybe depending on how the other Clans fill out that domain. But given the Ventrue dominance there and the many Brujah elsewhere, this is clearly the spot where they are needed the least.

        I imagine various Brujah could trespass in Rhode Island, but given a presence of Tremere there (in my chronicles, but not canon) and a massive Presence of Nosferatu, they don't last long unless they secure an invite.

        I don't know if there are any prominent Brujah mentioned in Swanswong that I'd be tempted to steal for my version of the setting. But we definitely need more names and concepts. Those we can't steal, we'll have to invent on our own.

        I'd make sure that we'd have a wide variety of potential Brujah lineages for the Brujah here to showcase how many Brujah have been in and out of New England over the many years. Pug Jackson could obviously represent the lines of either MacNeil or Foccart, so I'd make sure the other one was represented as well. I'd also pick a few Brujah lines from vampires who did not come from Britain as they would not be so enamored by the exploits of someone like Pendragon. Foccart came from France so perhaps there were other French Brujah as well. We could also utilize the Dutch from New Amsterdam in colonial times. Or use the mass waves of emigration that arrived later that could act as cover for the arrival of Old World vampires first arriving. With New York in Sabbat hands, Boston might have been a much safer and preferred arrival port from the perspective of Camarilla vampires. Obviously there would be a huge number of Irish, but I'm not acquainted with a strong Brujah presence there. I think smaller, though still noticeable, immigrant groups came from Germany and Italy and Russia (Jews more than ethnic Russians). I think there's also been some noticeable immigrants from the Christian populations of the Ottoman Empire (Armenians, Lebanese, and Arabs). I also know that Boston had one of the larger Chinatowns in America in the 19th century, but that is a dubious origin for vampires in the WoD unless the ST has replaced the "Kindred of the East" with actualKindred. That could generate a good mix, even if it is only as someone's sire who has since moved on. (This could be used for other clans as well, but I think it'd fit the idea of Brujah coming and going, and providing an easy explanation for Brujah not interested in Pendragon is he is supposed to be a big icon among the British Brujah.)

        I'd also make sure to showcase the changing nature of "revolutionary" Boston that attracted this type of Brujah or that one as time went by. Boston has a lot of events that can contribute to its "radical" heritage - the Revolutionary era, the Abolitionist movement, anti-Catholic riots, the Anarchism of Sacco & Vanzetti, Vietnam era protests, etc. Placing vampires in the right historical local context is very important to ground a setting in its region. Have vampires who lived through and participated in these events when they were mortal. It's also a huge intellectual capital of the United States because of its famous universities, and those would attract a different sort of Brujah. Maybe there is a Brujah like Critias in Boston, but who represents Italian scholarship from its medieval universities or the Italian Renaissance.

                    I think I covered all the Camarilla clans. And while I will eventually get to the Sabbat, I want to use this post for ideas on "seasoning" the setting in general with colorful NPCs. This isn't necessarily clan based. It's just concepts in general that could be interpreted using various clans. I see this as having too components. The first is just local concepts that should be included in the chronicle simply because it is in New England. The second are the exotic vampires that shouldn't be there (like a Muslim ghazi, African witch doctor, or a Romanian child in Chicago), but provide interest.

                    So here are some character concepts I thought should have been included in Dark Colony, but which I don't remember seeing.
                    • An American soldier from the War of Independence or the French & Indian War
                    • A Hessian mercenary
                    • A New England lawyer-politician in the vein of John Adams or Daniel Webster
                    • A Transcendentalist writer/philosopher
                    • A Boston abolitionist
                    • Someone involved with the Hartford Convention near the end of the War of 1812
                    • Someone involved with the New England shipping/fishing/whaling industry
                    • Someone who can represent the Boston Irish, although perhaps not a stereotypical police officer or politician
                    • Someone representing the financial and insurance industry
                    • Someone representing the old industries of textiles, shoes, clocks, leather, flour, etc. that provided the basis for the early industrial economy
                    • Someone representing the new industries of medical research, computers, scientific instruments, robotics, pharmaceuticals and other high value/highly educated workforce

                    I know others will have lots of their own ideas. But characters like this would help ground the NPCs to the local setting. I find too many NPCs in vampire city books tend to be generic that could be anywhere.

                    While I like most of the NPCs to be native to the region (somewhere around 2/3 would be ideal), not all vampires can or should be native. But here I think it is important they add something to the setting. I am much more accepting of really old vampires not being native because 1) vampires had to come from somewhere else first, 2) it helps cement the idea that vampire elders on their own society distinct from the mortal world, and 3) provocative characters can hold the imagination of the players.

                    There are two ways of adding such characters. The first is simple - create your own. We all have our own ideas so I won't waste people's time describing my own. But the second is to look for underappreciated and minor canonical characters already introduce whose destinies are unknown. The Dark Ages By Night books are a good example. A lot of them have good characters who were easily overlooked because they were not the main drivers of the chronicle plot.

                    I wouldn't pick the most popular or metaplot-involved characters. Lucita, Anatole, and Mary the Black don't need to end up here. But what about someone like Nerea of Spain in Constantinople By Night? She's given no destiny. She's just a thirty year old Brujah Neonate who fled a blood hunt in her native Barcelona for killing her sire/lover in a fit of jealous anger, and regrets it very much. It's implied she senses doom coming to the city and intends to go down in flames with it as atonement for the murder she regrets. But at the same time, she also dreams of finding true love again, so her destruction is not assured. What happens if she is now a thousand year old elder living in Boston. She's survived several conquests of Constantinople, seen the creation of the Camarilla and Sabbat, and travelled the Mediterranean world. Sensing the coming of the Second World War, she made her way to Boston to survive. She is still searching for a new true love.

                    Or maybe the Lasombra Magdalena Castelucci Borcellino from Jerusalem By Night. The daughter of a Venetian shipping dynasty with great social skills and a natural at intrigue who was embraced by Narses. She eventually returns to Italy and becomes a successful player of politics, but disappears around the time Gratiano diablerizes the Antedeluvian. But there are rumors she has survived as one of the Lasombra anti-tribu. What happens should see be one of the Kindred in Connecticut? Maybe she decided she would like to pit her skills against a real challenge - the members of the Triad and especially the spymaster Warwick. Maybe she harbors resentment against the Giovanni because of their power in her native Venice, and she wants to pick apart their promising acquisition of the Milliners while thwarting the Putanesca of New York. Or perhaps she finds it delicious to help a prominent Ventrue dynasty (the Lasombra's old rival), and by helping them put them in her debt. What should happen if the Sabbat ever learn that one of the Lasombra anti-tribu is nearby? I think it could add a lot.

                    Of course, STs might not find my picks interesting and have their own candidates. And that's great. I just think there are some hidden gems out there in the older Dark Ages books that might make interesting characters if you transplant them to New England. Build on these interesting, but neglected characters.

                        One thing I liked in Chicago By Night, that is absent in Dark Colony, was the “layering” of the Jyhad. You had the obvious conflict of the Prince and the Anarchs. There was also the conflict between the Prince and Primogen. Then the manipulations of the two Methusalehs. But individual vampires also had their own concerns about the Antedeluvians, the Jyhad, worries about Gehenna, interests in Golconda or diablerie, and so on. Dark Colony lacks this complexity. Almost everything centers on the conflict between the “Redcoats” and the “Kindred of Liberty” with some additional high level conflict between the patrons of the Triad (who are not even in New England). There is very little else other than the struggle between the Sabbat and Camarilla, and even that is tied to the Methusalehs behind the Triad.

                        So I think we need to add these extra levels. One part of this is to consider the possible roles of the Gehenna Cults (from the Elysium sourcebook) in New England (though this is not the only way, there should be more). I had done a thread earlier on this, but only using possible examples from Chicago By Night. I’ll now consider it in the context of Dark Colony. The paucity of NPCs means we have less to go by, but let’s see what we could do.

                        First up is the “Eden” social club which hides the cults of the Royal Edenic Groundskeeper and the Imperial Order of the Master Edenic Groundskeeper. None of the NPCs in Dark Colony seem to make good members of the actual Gehenna cults themselves, but I could see elders of both Boston and Connecticut being members of “Eden”. I like the idea that potentially opposing Kindred like Quentin, Upton, and Elsa Linden might be members of the same social club that only hosts the social elite (high status and their court favorites) of the New England Camarilla. Perhaps they even “cross” borders so to speak to appeasr at alternating Elysium events, this one at Yale, next time at Harvard, afterwards the Boston Lyric Opera, then some Cape Cod resort, and so on. It would give opportunities for these Kindred in different domains to meet and interact. Maybe even Roman Pendragon shows up from time to time. And if an ST wants to introduce an actual member of one of the Gehenna cults (perhaps merely visiting to the region), then they could easily do so.

                        Likewise, I don’t see candidates for the Way of the Ancient Lawgivers. Nobody seems particularly interested in the First City, the Antedeluvians, or Gehenna. It is something to keep in mind for an NPC created by the ST. But I would also say that it is not necessary to try to include every Gehenna Cult in a chronicle. They should be selected judiciously so it is OK if this cult is ignored entirely.

                        I mostly feel the same about the Servitors of Irad, but with one possible exception. I think Pendragon himself might make an interesting member. Unlike the Lawgivers, the Servitors do not exclude Brujah. And many of Pendragon’s actions fit the profile of the Servitors. He wages war against the Sabbat and Anarchs just like the Servitors would. He is also an extremely divisive figure within the Camarilla, and the Servitors want to keep the elders divided. One can even argue his role in “destroying” Dark Selina would be something the Servitors would approve of, and it could have attracted their attention to him. Furthermore, even if Pendragon is a member, it is not necessary for him to be a sincere member of it. He might have just seen it beneficial as a way to garner support.

                        If this is to be more than just trivia though, and something that drives the setting and affects the PCs, we might want to expand the organization. Giovanni Chronicles IV introduced the Cappadocian/Giovanni vampire Marianna in Boston. Perhaps that gives Pendragon an interest in protecting her or acting revenge upon the Giovanni Clan. Or perhaps one of the pretexts the Triad joined together is that another one or all of them were members of the Servitors. Or at least one other (perhaps ST created) NPC in the region is also a member, and that makes Pendragon and her secret allies.

                        If we go with the earlier example of Magdalena Castelucci Borcellino being in the setting, perhaps she is a also a Servitor of Irad. Maybe she believes Lasombra is not destroyed, and he humored Gratiano to test him. Gratiano failed, and he found only an empty tomb. He and his ilk and therefore marked for destruction no matter how powerful the Sabbat Lasombra seem. Her intrigues against the Triad are really directed only against Warwick (and perhaps Biltmore). When she intrigues against Pendragon, it's more in the sense of harassing a friend, not trying to stop him. Pendragon tolerates it because he knows at some point he must turn against his erstwhile allies.

                        I don’t think the remaining three Gehenna cults - Twilight Cult, the Ninth Wave, or the Cult of Enlightenment have much to offer New England. They are either too obsessed about specifics (gypsies, magic, or the scrolls) that it makes them an ill fit, or would require an emphasis on things that relegates the local aspects of the setting to non-importance. But it’s entirely possible someone else could integrate them well into New England.

                                                            Before I get to the Sabbat itself, I want to comment on the Camarilla’s defenses. After pulling everything together, I think the New England Camarilla actually has a solid defense. Pendragon and his followers seem to be a fairly potent fighting force. Quentin and his Six Knights had their own successes back in the day. The Tremere chantry has some old and potent members who have been there for over two hundred years. Warwick has an outstanding regional intelligence network that goes beyond the typical Nosferatu. There should be some fairly potent Gangrel who act as a reliable scouts. And the seer Prudence Stone provides an additional unexpected intelligence advantage.

                                                            The frontlines have been fairly solid for around a hundred years. (Dark Colony is often too vague with history, we’re told of no noteworthy battles after the Battle of Bloods Brook in Lebanon, NH in 1896.) Furthermore, the Kindred here have had extensive combat experience against the Sabbat since the colonial era. To them, the Sabbat is not some mystery or unknown bogeyman. They know their tactics, have inflicted some major defeats on them, and know what to expect. They have overcome any fear and shock long ago. When the wave of Sabbat Crusades caused many American cities to fall in the sixties and seventies, New England was not impacted.

                                                            In other words, the New England Camarilla has been a major outlier. It is the one nut the Sabbat has not been able to crack despite notable successes elsewhere.

                                                            This should inform our decisions when we go about developing the local Sabbat.
