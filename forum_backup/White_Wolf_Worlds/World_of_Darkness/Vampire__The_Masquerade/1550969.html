    Thoughts on the Alternate Banes

    05-12-2025, 02:59 PM
    I've noticed that several work really well in what I think of as Rare Vampire games. That is, games in which the player characters are virtually the the only vampires in the game, and the conflict is almost entirely about small-scale personal horror.

    The Hecata alt bane doesn't really fit in a game where the Giovanni are making big economic/political moves. It's so punishing as to make them unplayable in such a game. But it works perfectly in a game that is all about how the player-character, virtually the only Hecata in the game, is making the home of the very family she loves fall down around them. Lends itself well to scenes of the player-character holding a family-photo from happier times and having it fall apart in her hands.

    The Nosferatu alt bane is similar. It's almost irrelevant in stories where the Nos clan is virtually sect unto itself, living in the sewers and making political power-plays. In a story in which a very low-level Nosferatu is virtually the only Nosferatu in the game, it works well that they are literally bringing pestilence on the human family home they love.

    The Brujah alt bane works well in this sort of game as well. Instead of risking frenzy, which is such an all-of-nothing thing that it ruins this sort of story, they instead do things like be subtly abusive to the people they love. You can have a character say something that causes agg willpower damage to their own human family members. People they are grateful to for not casting them out, and their subtle simmering rage makes the relationship poisonous despite their best efforts.

    The Gangrel alt bane works well to emphasize the vampire's outcast status from humanity. Every lit cigarette, every hobo trash fire, every stove instilling an animal fear that makes it impossible to approach humans doing mundane human things. You are truly alone in the dark as this version of the Gangrel.

    These are angsty, angsty, angsty banes, that are either overpowered or underpowered in games about political/economic power-moves. But in a story where vampires are rare, and the players are nearly caitiff dealing with humans, and trying not to hurt them while surviving themselves? Really good.

    Basically these banes work best if the game is practically Wraith.

      I'm currently running "Fall of London" and I'm about to change Hecata's clan bane. Decay is counterintuitive to me even though at first I liked the concept of Hecata as social outcasts and that it was imposed through game mechanic. But then I realised that Deacay as Bane is based in concept of entropy and it being tied to passage of time. Something vampires are exempted from (All that time catches up to you the moment you meet the final death). It becomes problematic in V5, as Path's are dead and Humanity becomes the only "moral resource" available. Previous edition's made you feel isolated and more "boxed in", so Decay would have definitely worked in 2E or V20, but V5 puts more emphasis of human connection and social interactions (or you'll find SI knocking on your door before you realise it. Also the reason why Sabbat became obsolete in V5).

      The bane of Painful Kiss was a Augustus Giovanni's doing and based on the fact that mortal Giovanni had connections to Euthanatoi Mages, I treat this bane not as a Lamia's last laugh after Augustus had diablerized her, but as a sign of Jhor.

      Now, in my game I introduced another alternative bane, which is Ministry's Cold Blood. As a clan of death, Hecata THEMSELF should be completely severed from the world of the living. It should be an individual struggle not an aura of "everything-is-dying-faster-around-me". Cold Blood gives you additional rouse check's to activate blush of life. Works nicely for elders with higher bane severity (completely isolate themselves, working through agen... sorry, extended family), as well as younger Kindred, who can interact with mortals (as long as they are not being touched) and this variant helps Hecata dive into politics (even mortal, as they are not screaming anymore either).
