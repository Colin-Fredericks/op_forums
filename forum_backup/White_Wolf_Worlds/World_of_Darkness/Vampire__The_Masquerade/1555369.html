    Great Leap Outwards Quesiton

    11-11-2025, 03:27 PM
    I was thinking about the whole situation and how plausible that sounded.
    Similarly I have heard arguments for either side, Kuei Jin and Kindred.

    I think socially Kindred possess more advantages with Presence, Dominate and Blood Bonds. They also have more resources at home front and in easier time feeding. Their more combat oriented disciplines are often more straightforward to use at the very least.
    Where Kuei Jin seem to dominate is seem to be magic and everything related. Like access to Umbra, diplomacy with supernatural creatures, etc. Also they can make Devil Children, dhampirs and Bakemono in quite some numbers. While these are better then Ghouls, they also attract more attention - first of all from werewolves and Pentex. Where they will not have much diplomatic pull.

    For ease sake let us assume a simple situation. A single Wu vs similar number of kindred. Let's say anarch Brujah with 2 in every clan discipline, 3 in combat abilities/traits, WP5-6 and armed with desert eagles and bowie knives. I think this is a reasonable straightforward estimate without getting too complicated.

    On the other side we have a Wu that had been mostly quickly thrown together, so nothing and no one important is lost if they die. I say, let's keep physical and combat stats the same, But add 1 more WP. Disciplines 3 or 4, because they are harder to learn. as they are aimed at combat, I think they would be taught combat Disciplines. I think something like Demon Shintai 2 (+dex,+str, any 2 mutaions), Yang Chi 1 and Ghost-flame 1 seem reasonable? In addition, they may throw XP together to get a Nushi/Totem. This is where it gets very interesting. Yuki allows to stake vampires with melee weapons at +2 dice, Crab or Shuang allow to step sideways with ease, while Byakko and the Archer give aggravated attacks. for more info see HERE.

    Besides that Kuei Jin all have death/life sight, can manifest demonic maw for 2 demon chi and deal +2 aggravated damage with it if I remember correctly. lvl1 ghost-flame also stacks with demon maw, so it would be something like +3 aggregated damage with fire qualities. Vampires can buff physical characteristics with blood, while Kuei Jin can buff it depending on disciplines they have (stamina and strength with listed, if I remember correctly?)

    While magical weaponry is available for Kuei Jin, I am not sure it would be spent on frontline fighters in that conflict. Magical items are still valuable. But, I think they could be given Awakened items. Sure it is annoying for the sorcerer to be casting that rite time after time, but they need no great investment. So something like unnaturally sharp blades, or guns that never miss.

    Do you think this is reasonable? and how do you think the fight would go? Let's keep it relatively simple and street level.

      Hey,

      This is all very interesting stuff and talks a great deal about the differences between the Kuei Jin and Kindred's power sets but I think it kind of ignores the two larger issues of whatever will happen with the Great Leap Outward (what a terrible name) and any war between the Wan Kuei and the Kindred.

      1. What is the actual goal of the Great Leap Outward?

      There's a not unconvincing argument that the Great Leap Outward is a scam. One thing that is notable in the modern era that was true in previous ones is the fact that the Elders of the Wan Kuei are no more actually concerned about their younger members than the Western Kindred. Many more modern Wan Kuei are being born that have very little of the spiritual devotions or cultural history that the ancients do but are still born because Hell spits them out.
      This "conspiracy theory" is that the Great Leap Outward was nothing more than an attempt to thin the ranks of the young by sending them on an in-universe racist war that got them out of Asia and used them as cannon fodder against (as Hamlet would say), a foreign quarrel. They're not meant to actually win any war here but die.

      2. The logistics of the Great Leap Outward favor the Western Kindred

      Part of this is due to the fact that I'm not 100% sure that Western Wan Kuei even exist. Whatever it is about the dragon nests or the Underworld in the area around China, Japan, India, and so on is not necessarily the case in America. Have there ever been any Kindred of the East born in California? I'm not sure. However, the simple fact is that taking the territory of the Western Kindred doesn't really do the Wan Kuei any favors. They can't make other Wan Kuei and Western Kindred don't have dragon nodes: that's something that is reserved for mages and werewolves.
      Every Western Kindred can make more of their kind and refill their power by draining the nearest mortal. The Wan Kuei? Well, they would have to expand their war against California shapeshifters and sorcerers.

      3. The two major powers don't actually care

      As with many wars throughout history, the people least important to the war are those fighting it. The younger Wan Kuei and Anarchs doing battle for the state of California have more in common with each other than they do their respective Elders. They're only being pushed into the actual battle for dominion by outside forces who wish the other would wipe them other. It NIGHTS OF PROPECHY, the Kindred of the Camarilla actually just decide to pay the Wan Kuei off and the latter are willing to do it. The Camarilla would love to have Los Angeles back but the Wan Kuei thinning the herd of the Anarchs is fine by them as well. Vannevar Thomas might want San Fransisco back but the police state as described in San Fransisco by Night is an untenable one to begin with. It's one run by racist Wan Kuei who don't realize they're incredibly expendable.

      4. Bloodlines may actually be the ending of the Great Leap Outward

      Say what you will about Ming Xiao but she was an actual Elder of the Wan Kuei and the head of the Los Angeles portion of the group. The Fledgling destroys her and a huge number of her military forces as well as the experiments they were running on Western Kindred. It certainly eradicates their forces there. Depending on how you view the importance of the war, it may actually have crippled the Kuei Jin's ability to fight in California.​ Among the Kuei Jin, the numbers wiped out by the Fledgling are not replaceable.

          Originally posted by Warpwind View Post
          I was thinking about the whole situation and how plausible that sounded.

          The problem is that the whole exercise is skewed and oversimplified.

          Comparing builds like that is only relevant if you stipulate a very narrow question and strict conditions for making them, like comparing two starting characters by the rules for a death battle at a white room scenario without hiding places. That's fun, but says absolutely nothing about how they compare as warring factions in a particular area.

          First things first, neither build is really likely. Starting characters by the rules are PCs, they're young, but above average, not representatives of what an average character of that age will be like. Or any age, because on top of that characters within the story are unlikely to follow any "combo", as they don't develop their stats to fill a PC role, they just live their lives. So characters on each side will vary greatly and very few, even if selected as warriors, will be anything like a combat-oriented PC.

          Second, it depends far more on their respective resources as factions than their individual powers. The struggle is not a sequence of direct skirmishes between individuals until one side is wiped, it is a complex mix of plots, political maneuvers, disputes for influence and money and much more. Even actual killings are more probably through traps, ambushes and other underhanded tactics than thugs in the streets hitting each other.

          Being able to achieve your goals without a fight is generally the best option in any conflict, and doubly so in the WoD, and being caught usually means retreating is more important than engaging interlopers, because your plans are probably already compromised. Actual fights are either for very specific strategic goals, result of massive failures, or completely unimportant. If winning a fight is actually relevant, each one will try to stack the chances in their favor as much as possible, so it won't be one-on-one. Fair fights are for sports and movies.

          Even if you ignore CTPhipps' points (and I think they're fair even if not necessarily the original intent of the authors), conflicts of this scale are not just a series of PC fights​. PCs get away doing things directly because they're the protagonists of a story, their actions are aggrandized by design, but the bulk of the conflict isn't like that. And I agree with Phipps that the big difference in such a situation, if it ever really boils down to beating the crap out of each other, won't be any combat power, but the simple fact that Kindred can Embrace at any time.

                  I understand the Great Leap Outward from a Doylist perspective, but don't agree with it. It's still a bad story, not just because of all the problematic stuff (and holy shit, they're problematic), but simply because presenting the new splat through a protracted war devoid of any dept is kinda dumb. You literally want people to engage those characters, and you'll do that by having them want to kill each other?

                  But I don't think this theory of it boiling down to an attempt at wiping out their young was ever canonical, at least not as a fact. It may have been raised once or twice as a theory, but nothing deeper AFAIK. And I don't think it would be the best, either, because it creates an obvious, big, problem: now all those young are away from your main propaganda machine and exposed to new ideas and easy access to freedom. The risk of desertion and possibly rebellion is too big and too obvious. The only reason it didn't happened and in numbers was that WW didn't thought about it. It wasn't really that important for them.

                  On the Fledgling and their antics, including against the Kuei-Jin, I mostly agree, I was just saying it has a downside. I'm on the fence if it will be truth in my games, but overall I'm good with Bloodlines being canon with everything that entails. I just wonder what the Fledgling would be doing now, IF they survived.

                  My personal opinion, and including the fact that I think both Kindred and Hungry Dead should have a greater presence in every continent, is that total war between the splats is unlikely and uninteresting. They would most probably keep tabs on each other and reach some kind of mutual understanding, if only for the same reason they each do so internally: they need the same resources and both risk depleting it if they go too far. Fighting is rarely to anyone's benefit. Sure, sometimes it happens, but not a global war.
