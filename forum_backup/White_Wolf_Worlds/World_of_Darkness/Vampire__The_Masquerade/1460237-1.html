                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                            I'm not sure the distinction is actually as fine cut as you might think. I mean in D&D and movies, you have people turned into vampires and they automatically are evil and want to kill their loved ones. That's silly unless they're possessed by demons ala Buffy. However, in V:TM, you might justifiably argue anyone who makes the selfish choice to continue existing as a vampire to be at least a little morally compromised.
                            I think even in those examples you see some nuance. Vampires are evil by default in D&D, but an evil alignment doesn't necessarily equate to "possessed by a demon". Van Richten's son gets turned into a vampire, and by the rules has an evil alignment, but retains enough of his human self to recognize he has become a monster and to allow his father to kill him. This was in Ravenloft, which takes the firmest "vampires are evil" stance out of any D&D setting.

                            I used to think it was stupid how D&D required all of its vampires to be evil, but in hindsight I think I was viewing alignment as more of a straitjacket than it needed to to be. One of my old GMs had a campaign where chromatic (evil) and metallic (gold) dragons worked together at the urgings of Bahamut and Tiamat (their respective good and evil deities) because dragonkind was threatened and the deities cared more about its survival than being "fully faithful" to their alignments. Gary Gygax liked to say two lawful good nations could go to war with one another. One of my favorite fan-made modules for Neverwinter Nights has a chaotic evil character who rapes the protagonist, but can eventually be made to feel regret for his actions and apologize for them. His larger alignment doesn't change, though. He's sorry he hurt the protagonist but remains an asshole to most everyone else. One of the other exceptions being his true neutral boss who he shows unwavering loyalty towards. All of this behavior is within the bounds of something an evil-aligned vampire could do. At least in a game I'd run, anyway.

                            D&D requiring its vampires to be evil-aligned was fairly reflective of how most vampires in "serious" vampire stories are. Most vampires in the WoD, if you were to give them D&D alignments, would probably be evil. There'd be some neutrals and a very small number of goods (depending on which edition you're using). The "always X alignment" entry in the Monster Manual isn't meant to cover the "once or twice in a campaign" exceptions like Angel and Spike. Planescape had the idea of risen fiends (demons and devils who've turned to good), so if literal embodiments of their alignments can turn against those, I always figured an exceptional enough vampire could do so too.

                            Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.

                                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                No, I'm saying how do vampires not become bad in your universe? I'm not contradicting you, I'm asking from a place of curiosity. Your preference is noted and I'm intrigued.

                                I think you have a very interesting take.
                                I'll try to make it brief and direct for ease of discussion, it's kind of a big question.

                                1 - Lower Humanity reduces your threshold for "sins", do not force you to commit them. A vampire may stick to a code of conduct higher than its current Humanity rating, so slipping in the code doesn't mean slipping in their actual Humanity;

                                2 - Redemption is a thing. It doesn't have to mean from 3 to 8. If you're plain 7 most of the time, but falls to 6 once in a while (like a decade), you can strive to stay behaving at 7, maybe even 8, and pay the experience for 7. Your behavior pretty much demonstrates that the "sin" is the outlier, but you're stable;

                                3 - No society really works through constant backstabbing and power struggles, esp. a society where becoming a secretary to the Prince in 50 years is considered a meteoric ascension. Most of the time, most of the Kindred are just going through routine and trying to enjoy themselves;

                                4 - By the same token, people in positions of power have to deal with dull administration most of the time and effectiveness and precision are more important in their nightly activities. Although it doesn't means being nice, it pays off more often than not;

                                5 - Doing terrible stuff for the lolz is usually a dumb idea in the long run, and while mortals frequently can escape consequences in the long run, Kindred not so much. Of course, they're still frequently bored and petty, but those two things kind of even themselves out;

                                6 - Vampires are simply still people. Some believe everyone secretly hates everyone else. Some cry watching Titanic. Some will do terrible things and become inured to them. Some will be horrified even more if they notice they're not feeling so bad as they think they should and will look for help;

                                7 - Age doesn't have to work the way we usually depict it as working for immortals, and I think it wouldn't. After some time mortals lose meaning? I've had several dogs and cats through my life, lost them, and my current pets are still like sons to me. Most of the time we ride the moment and barely remember how our life was 10 years ago, we lose loved ones and make new, why would it be different for immortals?

                                Not to say that any of this works for everyone, but that those are some of the things that effectively hold vampires from simply becoming a generic bunch of assholes. It is a tough life, full of horror and death, but it isn't really any tougher for nice people than normal life already is, just harder to learn how to cope. Are vampires more skewed towards being assholes? Probably, but not by that much.

                                For the opinion that the moral thing to do is to end one's unlife: first off, with which authority can you say that killing yourself is your right? This isn't a clear cut moral question for everyone. But above that, one thing is to say that suicide would be the right thing, but would you do it? Forget about the game, it is one of the most terrible decisions someone makes. Nothing is easy about taking your own life, it goes against every impulse we have and is an extremely desperate decision, eviscerating and terrible. Stop reading pseudo-philosophical crap about suicide and go read scientific papers on the matter and you'll see a world of horror, where no option is an easy option. This "option" shouldn't ever be on the table.

                                        I think it's also important to note the kind of major blowouts that involve multiple vampires committing sins in a rapid basis, only happen once every few decades at most. Shit may go down during a rivalry that heats up, during sieges or large power plays, but nightly unlife tends to be painfully dull. Being kind of an asshole to each other because you're stuck in a Sartrean waking nightmare, isn't a sin unless a vampire is ridiculously high on the minority of paths (Humanity included) that hold consideration of others and charitable behavior as a key precept.

                                        There's a wide gulf between Elysium snark and having your pawns fuck with another vampire's pawns and domain, which is the worst most vampires do on a nightly basis, and ordering your pawns to engage in open street warfare with another's, attacking another vampire directly, or joining the fray yourself.

                                        Also, do bear in mind that despite them being a minority, some paths do hold tenets that represent a higher behavioral and ethical standard than Humanity in many ways. Entelechy, Self-Focus, and Redemption come to mind; arguably, Honorable Accord, though how Honorable Accord fits into a potential spectrum of good versus evil depends heavily upon how the vampire interprets the tenets of that path for themselves. Alien they may be, they still defy simple classification along that spectrum.

                                        And for most of those paths, bear in mind the distinction between committing a sin and actually degenerating. A failed Conscience check represents the slippery slope of accepting and rationalizing the action; a failed Conviction check represents the inability to reconcile the sin with the path being walked and move forward. Most sins don't result in degeneration (at least in mechanical terms, with a reasonable virtue rating), indicating to some degree most vampires don't gleefully degenerate into complete depravity over the course of years or decades, and during the quieter times they seek contrition, or retreat into quiet contemplation, to overcome past transgressions to some degree.

                                                A person’s biology, circumstances, and opportunities certain can largely determine their moral trajectory. Probably a lot of people who committed terrible acts, like massacres, taking innocent people to death camps or gulags, would have lived relatively moral peaceful lives if they weren’t being given orders to do those bad things.

                                                Imagine for a moment a married man in a monogamous marriage. Cheating on his wife would be a betrayal of her, so an immoral act. Imagine the following scenarios:
                                                A: a beautiful woman flirts with the married man. He is extremely attracted to her and flirts back, leading to an affair.
                                                B: a woman who isn’t that attractive flirts with the man. He isn’t attracted to her and doesn’t flirt back. There is no affair.
                                                C. The married man had previously developed some health problem which lowers his testosterone and sex drive. The beautiful woman flirts with him and while he thinks she’s pretty, he doesn’t feel that strong desire and doesn’t flirt back, so no affair.
                                                D. The married man is less handsome than in scenario A, the beautiful woman passes by without flirting with him, so there is no affair.

                                                So we see that this man’s biology and his circumstances can determine whether or not he commits the immoral action.

                                                Becoming a vampire puts someone into scenario A in so many ways. They now have an incredibly powerful drive to commit potentially harmful acts that they never had before. Furthermore, they have far greater opportunities to commit evil acts - imagine suddenly getting the power to control other people’s minds, to make them your slave. That power would turn many people who think of themselves as nice into slave masters and rapists, which is basically what having a blood bound herd is. Then you’re surrounded by other vampires who are ruthless and will be expecting (or forcing) you to commit certain terrible acts.

                                                So, a vampire doesn’t have to be evil, they can be more moral than the average human, but most vampires are going to be evil. They are going to commit the occasional murder. They are going to commit acts that are very close to slavery and rape. They probably have a Humanity of 2.

                                                A 2 Humanity isn’t a murder hobo. Michael Corleone at the end of The Godfather probably had a Humanity of 2. A lot of mobsters, dictators, gang members, or even politicians might have a 2 Humanity. A lot of Game of Thrones characters have a 2 Humanity.
