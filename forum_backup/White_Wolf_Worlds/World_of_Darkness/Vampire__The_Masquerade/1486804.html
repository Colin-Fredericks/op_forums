    Blood Magic at this point is extremely common

    07-17-2022, 07:20 PM
    It seems almost every clan/obscure bloodline has Blood Magic at this point, and I think thats pretty cool.

    1) 4 different types of Setite Sorcery practiced by 4 different FoS bloodlines, with the most common being Akhu, and another lesser known type known as Nahuallotl which is most likely practiced exclusively by the Tlacique.

    2) Koldunic Sorcery practiced by the Kolduns of Old Clan Tzimisce.

    3) Assamite Sorcery/Dur-An-Ki practiced by the Assamite Sorcerer Caste, and a few Metheleusas. Becomes an in clan discipline for the Assamites in V5

    4) Sadhana practiced by the Indian Venture bloodline known as Danava, Daitya (FoS Bloodline), a few Indian vampires that come from other clans, and the Trimira (Indian Tremere) which accept other vampires from different clans.

    5) Different types of necromancy practiced by the Giovanni, Samedi, Nagraja, some Haitian Lassombra, and some Serpents of Light. Replace by Oblivion for the Hecate in V5.

    6) Abyss Mysticism practiced by the mystics of clan Lassombra.

    7) Thaumaturgy and Selinic Thaumaturgy practiced by the Tremere.

    8) Dark Thaumaturgy practiced by Baali, Angellis Ater, and practically every infernalist.

    9) Wanga practiced by a couple Samedi vampires, but used to a greater degree by the Serpents of Light.

    10) Anarch Sorcery that includes 4 different "Waves" (Think of them as different schools) practiced by the Anarchs.

    11) Sihr practiced by Islamic kindred.

    I see no issue with this, sure this kind of downplays the clans who have Blood Magic as a big part of their backstory, such as the Tremere, but I feel like this gives you a lot of options to create a story that is more heavily focused on the Supernatural side of VTM. I always felt the conflict between different blood magic clans and bloodlines was underdeveloped. It would have loved to see two different blood magicians battle over the ownership of a certain spirit, or something similar to that. It doesn't have to be a violent conflict either. They could just make a deal for shared ownership of it.

    It would also have been pretty interesting if we could have seen how the different Blood Magic Traditions view each other. Do the Assamite Sorcery suspect Kolduns of practicing infernalism? Do Kolduns think that Assamite Sorcery is outdated, and that Koldunic Sorcery is a newer, better version of Dur-An-Ki? How do different Clans and Bloodlines that are known for practicing Necromancy feel about Sadhana? How do the practitioners of Sadhana feel about Necromancy? I just find stuff like this really interesting and I feel it would really add more depth to the many Factions who are known for practicing Blood Magic.

    But maybe I'm wrong, maybe having so many Blood Magic Traditions harms the game as a whole.

    ​What do you think?

      I think it's worth looking at the difference between who has access to Blood Magic, and who has In-Clan Blood Magic. There are very few Disciplines that are hard to access (and most of those are because they're attached to a rare Bloodline). Even Discipline very associated with certain Clans (Protean, Obtenebration, Vicissitude, etc) can end up in other Clan hand even if not wildly taught. Toreador that want to learn the early levels of Vicissitude to do various kinds of "living" art on undead flesh can do that if they know the right Toreador rather than asking a Tzimisce for it.

      When a Clan or Bloodline has Blood Magic as In-Clan it not only impacts the XP value, but also the ease of learning as you don't to blood bond yourself to a tutor. The vast majority of Setites have access to Blood Magic, but would still have to learn it like any other Out of Clan Discipline even if they can negotiate to learn from another Setite.

      Access to Blood Magic grew a lot over the course of the original VtM run for sure, but also far more than In-Clan groups of Blood Sorcerers, which gives groups like the Tremere an edge.

          As a rule of thumb I think the in-lore growth of Blood Magic is a good thing. It makes sense and it is an easy way to expand both powers and culture of a clan without breaking usual Discipline "economy", so to speak (more than it was already broken).

          I also don't think it downplays anyone because Magic Traditions have the accompanying point of exploring styles, outlooks and theories behind the power as a means to explore the differences between groups. They don't play the same, don't have similar roles in the game and do not feel redundant even if they're all Magicians. Quite the opposite, Blood Magic arguably did a better job at making them shine as distinct clans than their own signature Disciplines for the Banu Haqim, Setites and maybe even Tzimisce (depending on who you ask this). I see no one claiming that Dur-An-Ki, Akhu and Koldunic Sorcery just made them seem too much like Tremere.

          There is a caveat, but it is not the concept of Blood Magic and, instead, its rules. While perception on this problem varies, there are many concerns with it for balance and the feel of the mechanics. But such is the problem that it remains in place even if you only have the Tremere.

                      Every WoD splat seems to have one area where the devs shoe horn all the extra ideas they have. In Exalted 2nd it was Martial Arts styles, in Werewolf it was endless charm lists, in Vampire it was the paths of magic/necromancy.

                      From a design standpoint, this is atrocious. It displays a lack of preplanning and too many cooks in the kitchen kind of scenario.

                      As for magic paths in Vampire, with each new clan/bloodline reveal, the history was retconned, which meant things made progressively less and less sense. Between the Tzimisce, the Setites, the Assamites, and the Cappadocians, is there any real reason for the Tremere to be considered special for their magic? Perhaps I am a bit jaded because when I started playing, none of the clans/bloodlines outside of the core 7 were named, let alone given backstories or disciplines.

                      All in all the situation reeks of every new thing contain the cool parts of previous things. While some may look at that and exclaim "build variety" I look at it and say that all that was achieved was making everything the same. Any clan can be anything, which results in a lack of clan and bloodline identity if the writers are not careful.

                      Think of it like playing an FPS, both sides have the same weapons, the same movement abilities, the same mechanics, and the same map goals. The only difference is a character skin or Team Color designation. Very much Red vs. Blue rather than anything deeper in game play or design. On the other hand, if you take something like AvP, which is an asymmetrical FPS game, each faction has different play styles, different gear, different goals.

                      So yeah, excessive magic bloat means the "blandification" of vampire in general and one hell of an XP sink for anyone who wants to "master magic" as a build.

                              CT Phipps Blood Sorcery House Rules

                              I also added these rules to spruce up the lore of Blood Sorcery in my games.

                              * No vampire can learn Blood Sorcery unless he had magical potential while alive, diablerizes a vampire with Blood Sorcery, or is one of the Tremere or Assamite Sorcerer Bloodlines.

                              * Necromancy and Blood Sorcery are fundamentally different disciplines. Learning one gives no advantage to learning the other.

                              * The exception to learning Blood Sorcery is if you make a demonic pact. Dark Thaumaturgy and Koldunism are really two sides of the same coin here as the latter requires a pact with Kupala or one of their minions. Setites also learn their magic by making pacts with Apophis, which Silent Striders claim is just straight up Wyrm magic.

                              * Infernalism is pretty damn rare but it is the easiest, quickest, and actually most common way for a "normal" vampire to learn magic. No natural spirit, angel, or Aeon will deal with vampires.

                              * Vampires cannot learn Hedge Magic from mortals. Any vampire who is Embraced loses both true and hedge magic but not psychic Numina. They may have a much easier time learning basic disciplines.

                              * A Embraced Awakened mage does have the potential to learn Blood Sorcery, small consolation as that may be. They also "attract" demons like a fly zapper and are a major source of Infernalists.

                              * Demons can visit vampires with magical potential in their dreams (which most vampires don't have). The Tremere generally tell you to ignore them.

                              * Aside from PCs breaking the rules for storytelling purposes, it requires a minimum of ten years of study to learn Blood Sorcery 1. Most Tremere Neonates have been studying thirty years and are fairly "old" Neonates by the time they're released.

                              * A Blood Sorcery 3 level is usually at least 50 years of study. Another reason the Tremere spend all of their time reading in their chantries. 5 is something that often takes a full century to reach and is a sign you are an Elder of the Clan.

                              * Even if a vampire were to acquire Tremere books of magic, they'd be useless due to the requirements above. It requires a massive grounding in philosophy, mindset, meditation, practice, and equipment to use magic.

                              * Despite how awesome Blood Sorcery is and can be, it is really a lifetime vocation and vampires aren't as impressed with it generally as the Tremere's players/Tremere themselves are. Few undead are able to make the commitment even if somehow offered.

                              * Lots of vampires have mysticism about their Disciplines so there's a mystical flare to studying everything from Dementation/Dominate to Oblivion to Vicissitude.

                                          I find it interesting to have this general kind of house-rules that help define what Magic is in yout game, but I disagree with almost every one of your options.                                           My own default (I'm never against changing my setting for a new chronicle) would be somewhat different:
                                          • Blood Magic is a property of Vitae, but need knowledge and effort to distill. No vampire has it as In-Clan (having only two In-Clans is the Tremere Weakness in my games currently);
                                          • Necromancy is special because affects things too close to the vampire's nature. It is still Blood Magic, but has distinct personal consequences;
                                          • Deriving Magic from the outside, like spiritual pacts, brings a host of its own consequences, but a wise Magician will take time to study and make benign pacts which the end result being effectively indistinguishable from regular Blood Magic or even be healthier, so to speak. Only a fool or someone who purposefully walks such a path will reek of the Wyrm;
                                          • Vampires can deal with all sorts of spirits if they know how to do so, which is tricky but doable. Those who chose the easier pacts, though, end up either as infernalists or under the power of entities that do not have their well-being in mind, from dangerous demons to spirits that demand constant and exacting service;
                                          • Hedge Magic is the basis for Blood Magic. While it doesn't work straight up for vampires, it is easy to convert into a new Blood Magic Path, as many magicians proved throughout time;
                                          • An Embraced Awakened has an Entrapped Avatar, unable to grant True Magic, but also to reincarnate. Yet this being still exists and can be contacted for knowledge and pacts with the right magic;
                                          • Contacting demons is rare, infernalism is a knowledge hunted and buried beneath ignorance, persecution and lies. But it is stupidly easy to actually perform and requires almost no deeper knowledge, even if the thing summoned praises you for your skill at research and ritual preparation. This is just hollow praise, it is almost impossible to truly botch the summoning. It isn't so hard to botch doing it safely, though;
                                          • Time of study depends on several factors, including magical style. Tremere Hermeticism is the most demanding style to master any level, serving as the upper bar. Under typical circumstances, which means about 2 or 3 hours/night dedicated to study and no greater talent than the interest in the topic, it takes Level² in decades to master any given level from 0. While there's no bottom line for learning times, it would take a true genius with everything stacked at their favor and almost total dedication to study to cut this time to a tenth without relying on any weird factor, but is possible;
                                          • With this in mind you can expect any active Tremere with more than 3 dots to be an Elder. Many active Neonates actually do not have even their first dot yet, and the clan has both active members that do not spend too much time studying (they still study magic a lot, to be honest, as they study 2 or 3 hours every single night), and shut-ins completely dedicated to master magic in a sensible time scale;
                                          • Studying Magic from zero from books is possible, but time-consuming and fraught with dangers. Yet the best source to do so would be a Tremere library if it is complete enough, as those are meant to have everything you need. Were the Tremere wiped-out at once, their whole brand of Magic would still be possible for any inheritor of their libraries to reconstruct;
                                          • It takes time, effort and/or risk to learn Magic, you can't have it without paying the toll in some way. It is also worth noting that this isn't really a path to greater understanding of the mysteries of the Blood or its supernatural powers, just a means to extract its innate magic and apply somewhere else;
                                          • You can derive a particular brand of magic from any Discipline in the same way the Lasombra did with Obtenebration.

                                              My own rules:
                                              -Thaumaturgy is a discipline of the soul, the blood is just a convenient power source: everyone can do it, unless there's something deeply wrong with you.
