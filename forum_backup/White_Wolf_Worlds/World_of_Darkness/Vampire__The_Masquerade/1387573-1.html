    Clanbook: Malkavian (part two)

    And once more into the breach.

    The Raving Lunatic
    Oh, for the love of .... Remember how I said I think I might actually hate the Freak? Well, I think I hate this character even more. I've also noticed that it seems that each Clanbook includes a template (the Rapper and the Biologist) that could be considered a refinement of that Clan's character from the Players Guide. Well this could be considered that, only refined in the worst way possible. What was this character like before the embrace? No one knows. We get one line that he had a happy and fulfilling life until he got attacked by a crazy woman in an alley. That's it. He flips back and forth between a hyperactive, gibbering, screaming loon and a broody, muttering, paranoid skulker. Usually for no rhyme or reason. (That's actually part of the suggested roleplaying hints.) He doesn't really have any motivation or goals. His equipment includes a straight jacket, steak knives, inflatable beach toys (WTF?), battered old taxi, lock picks, and autographed photos of TV stars (again, WTF?). He's got Brawl and Dodge at three dots, and Firearms and Melee at two each. Also Lockpicking and Escapology at one each. I repeat my original post comment about wondering why anyone would buy a single dot of Lockpicking instead of just getting Security. He's got Resources of one. There must be a bag of money in the trunk of that taxi cab or something. And he has a Conscience of five and Humanity of seven. I really, really dislike this character. Aside from being a walking stereotype of mental illness, it is almost 100% certain to disrupt and derail most games and player coteries. I'm not even sure this character would work well in an early edition Sabbat game. It's just bad all the way around.

    The Crazed Monster
    A life-long victim of abuse, in and out of youth homes and eventually jail, only to be gunned down during a robbery gone bad and her dying body dragged away by a sire who would subject her to several days of torture before the embrace. Now she just wants to make the world burn and inflict her own pain on anyone she comes across. This is not a terrible idea for a concept. It's just lacking in depth and room for growth or evolution into something more meaningful. It's sole roleplaying hint is "speak in morbid nursery rhymes." That's it. And not a lot to go on. The character image is interesting. It's got this early 90s morbid grunge-not-quite-goth feel to it that I don't dislike. Her Strength and Stamina are both at four dots, with Dexterity at two. Manipulation is at five dots, with the other social traits at one each. That feels off, maybe. I think a case could be made for a 1/3/2 split with an optional extra dot from freebies. Abilities are Alertness, Athletics, Intimitation, and Melee at three each, with Brawl, Firearms, Stealth, Investigation, and Occult at two each. Occult feels out of place for some reason. Given the character's focus, having those two dots in the secondary ability of Toxicology seems more fitting. Somehow the character has a Herd of three. I've no idea how she pulled this off. Conscience is at a one and Humanity a five, which feels fitting, if maybe hard to play. I find myself wanting to like this character, but it's flaws are holding me back. It's another of those that I think would be a lot stronger if a little more effort had been expended in the write up and the stats tweaked a little.

    The Moody Loner
    Another of those period stereotypes (and again, kind of more from the '70s), largely owing to both certain political assassinations and - especially in this case - the 1966 University of Texas shooting incident. The art is clearly inspired by Lee Harvey Oswald, right down to a shirt referencing him. If this hadn't been several years before Columbine High School, we'd probably have been subjected to an image of a teen in a trench coat. This is a guy who never fit in, but wanted to be one of those cool and heroic types. Secret agent, soldier, PI, whatever. Instead, he's ended up as a pawn in the Jihad (or whatever we're calling it now). The character is walled off from others mainly out of a fear of rejection, and is desperate for praise and affirmation, becoming devotedly loyal to any authority figure that seems to appreciate him. I have to give credit for this actually conforming to certain assessments of Oswald. This is actually a pretty good concept for a character and one that has some interesting potential. I just wish they hadn't leaned so heavily into the "water tower sniper" schtick. Seriously, his equipment list includes "map of local water towers". And his highest ability is a four in Firearms. But otherwise he's fairly competent as a character sent out to do work for his sire, as one of the Sheriff's flunkies, or as an Anarch agent. And like our previous entry, he's got two dots in Herd, as well as a one in Conscience and five in Humanity. I feel the first one needs a bit of an explanation, while the other two should be higher so as to differentiate him from just being yet another serial killer. This is another one of those that I don't dislike, but don't really love either. There's a solid idea here, but I think it needs to be better refined.

    That's all for this one. Next will be the final three Malkavians, and then Clanbook: Nosferatu.

        Clanbook: Malkavian (part three)

        The final three.

        The Detached Scientist
        This is hands down my absolute favorite concept of the book, and probably in my top ten of all Vampire templates. This wonderful character is effectively the first appearance of what would become Dr. Netchurch, the Malkavian who featured heavily in Ghouls: Fatal Addiction and Time of Thin Blood. He is one of my all time favorite Vampire characters and my favorite Malkavian (and I dearly wish he'd been the revised era signature character instead of Anatole), largely because he perfectly fits the clan's entire concept perfectly while also taking it as far away from "crazy = wacky" or raving lunatics as you can get. As for this initial incarnation, what we have is a man who is, at heart, deeply emotional and straight forward, but has struggled continuously to keep those emotions in check through logic, rationality, and later science. He's the stereotypical straight-laced, uptight, by the book, I-dotter and T-crosser intellectual. But he's also constantly teetering on a knife edge, about to completely lose it in a storm of unchecked emotion. And for a vampire, that probably means a blood-soaked Frenzy. It's almost terrifying to think about. Stat-wise, he has five dots in Intelligence, which doesn't really feel out of place here. I could easily see this as one of those young genius types who was too smart for his own good, his IQ rapidly dwarfing his EQ (emotional quotient). Strangely, his abilities all max out at three dots, including both Medicine and Science. However, he has one dot in Psychology, bought with freebies. I just fail to understand why you wouldn't just buy the fourth dot in Science and have a specialization in Psychology. He's also got three dots each in Firearms, Stealth and Security, which effectively qualifies him to be a professional hitman or secret agent. I also think this character could benefit from having a few dots in Herd, as being a doctor at a mental hospital gives one free reign to prey on the patients from time to time. That said, this is still my favorite of the bunch, and I think with a few points moved around, it'd be close to perfect.

        The Reality Buster
        So, if Gangrel's Lupine Impersonator was an effort to include a crossover character for Werewolf, I'm pretty sure this one is an attempt to do the same for Mage (the first edition of which had come out around the same time as this book). This character believes that Reality is subjective, but has likely been locked down into its fixed form by some conspiracy or other dangerous force in order to steal or harness its power. Young, hip, and full of youthful energy and exuberance, her equipment is basically a list of assorted high tech gadgets, measuring devices, and puzzle toys. In other words, the sort of stuff a lot of modern Mages - especially Orphans or hip early 90s technomancers - might use as foci. This really really feels like a first edition Mage character that someone statted up as a Vampire by mistake. And I have mixed feelings about it. This isn't a bad character. I think it would fit in fairly well with a coterie that skewed toward investigation and research, or maybe an LA based Anarch coterie that is more about just being who they are in the Free States instead of being the vanguard of the revolution. But at the same time, I feel like more could be done with the idea. This was also around the same time that the first English translation of Kult came out, and I could see the basic "reality is an illusion made to imprison us" idea leaning heavily into the Gnostic themes of archons and the Rex Mundi for a much darker and edgier take. Likewise if it leaned in to things like chaos magic or psychonautics and altered states of consciousness. Ability-wise, she's fairly solid, with a scattering of ones and twos across the sheet. Humanity is only six, though, which feels low for this character. I'm a little surprised she doesn't have the Meditation secondary ability. This is also a character who Cryptography actually wouldn't feel out of place with. Like I said, this isn't a bad character by any stretch. It just feels perhaps a bit lacking or maybe out of place.

        The Mad Prophet
        And another from the collection of Stock Character Crazy People. This is basically the guy who stands on the street corner raving to anyone who'll listen - or who can't get away fast enough - about whatever their fixation is, be it the end of the world, aliens among us, or the secret conspiratorial alliance of the dairy farmers and the homosexuals, and how listening to him will save us all. I don't really like it. But the fact that it's such a stereotype is only the lesser reason I dislike it. This guy was your average upper middle class success story, having earned success with his work, family, and community. Then an illness that hit him with a high fever for a week (it says 103, but looking up stuff online, it appears that's not actually high enough to result in brain damage), and something apparently just broke. He couldn't do his job or continue going through the motions of his mundane, mechanized former life. So he tried to commit suicide-by-auto-accident, only to be saved and embraced. Now he's trying to shake, startle, and shock other people out of the walking, waking death that is custom, habit, and routine. And that's actually an interesting idea for a Malkavian character. I just fail to see why he has to be a stereotypical disheveled street-ranter to do it. If this was some sort of motivational speaker/guru type, attracting a cult following while wearing a respectable casual suit and giving public talks, he'd be a really awesome character, and probably one of my favorites. Like some sort of weird cross between Dale Carnegie and Jim Jones. His starts are OK, but occasionally wonky. He's got five dots in Charisma, one dot in Manipulation, and four dots in Appearance. I'm wondering if those last two were put in backwards. Physicals are secondary and Mental tertiary, with a 3/2/1 spread. And a one in Wits feels too low for this character. His Empathy and Leadership are three dots each, and Firearms, Melee, and Stealth are all two each. I don't think that fits the concept all that well. All other abilities are one dot, including Public Speaking. He has five dots in Disciplines (Auspex 2, Dominate 3), meaning that's where all his freebies went. Over all, this is one of those ideas that needed a bit of fine tuning, both conceptually and statistically. There's a good potential character here, but it's lost in the clutter.


        So, those are the Malkavians. They are kind of a mixed bag, and in a way, indicative of the problems that have plagued the Clan for the entire run of the game. Three of them - the Mesmeric Manipulator, the Conspiracy Theorist, and the Detached Scientist - are very good, and a fourth (the Waking Dreamer) almost makes that grade. Three of them are fairly solid, but could use some improvement, and a fourth one (the Mad Prophet) could use a lot. And two of them - the Freak and the Raving Lunatic - I quite frankly dislike a lot.
        For representation, we have an even split of male and female, which is a first. For the most part, they are white. It's a little hard to tell with Timbrook's art style, but it's possible that the Conspiracy Theorist and Mad Prophet may be black, and the Reality Buster Asian (but this may just be an aspect of the way he tends to draw facial features). No one is really sexualized in any way, save maybe the Mesmeric Manipulator, who is more sort of that pre-1970s exotically attractive look than hyper sexualized.
        I think my original additional idea back in the day was some sort of obsessed stalker type, who serially fixated on a person and wanted to become them. Also the idea of a cult leader and the classic Cassandra oracle archetype (which I'm a little surprised wasn't one of the ones used here). And maybe a masked vigilante.
        I think if the Malkavians ever had an over arcing theme, it was that of Mystery. Many of the truths of the human brain and the psyche are potentially unknowable, including certain aspects of madness. The world itself is full of mysteries, and the World of Darkness only more so. And I think the better Malkavian concepts play into that sense of mystery. The four templates here that I like certainly do, as does the Reality Buster. And I think the Mad Prophet and Moody Loner sit on the edge of that, needing only some minor alterations to be pushed in the right direction. The Crazed Monster could be one of those also, once the details are more filled in. In contrast, the two I dislike are extremely shallow and one-dimensional, lacking any sense of mystery whatsoever. They are what they are and nothing more.
        And finally, I'll just note that this is the only one of the original seven Clanbooks to not have its character templates in alphabetical order.

            Clanbook: Nosferatu (part one)

            Our fourth entry is also from 1993, so the clanbooks were coming at a fairly brisk pace to start with. We get a striking cover that is clearly an homage to Max Shrek's Count Orlok in FW Murnau's 1922 classic Nosferatu. This book was written by Robert Hatch, who would later go on to be Vampire: The Masquerade Developer during the later part of 2nd ed and the creation of the Revised edition. Our character template art is once again by Josh Timbrook. I'm glad to see this continue, as I think it helps give a certain level of continuity to the whole thing and ensures that these characters seem like they all come from the same world.

            The Cleopatra
            Right away we start with an idea that plays against type - the Nosferatu with a social focus - while also being built off of the more common idea that the clan will often seem to embrace certain people purely out of a sense of spite. I generally like this idea, as it gives players a reason for playing the sort of character that doesn't seem to fit in with the clan and its image while not having to come up with an elaborate or even convoluted justification for why they are in that clan. In out case, we have an archetype of the time - perhaps of most times - with a woman who has always been able to go through life and get her way purely with her looks. And while this is a bit of a gender stereotype, I think this concept works just as well with your stereotypical hot guy hunk pretty boy. Prior to the embrace, this character was a corporate executive who'd made it a rung or three up the ladder. We don't really get any indication of what the character is doing after being embraced and her life ruined, other than to say that she's very bitter, but with her traits, I can easily see her managing to carve a place out for herself both continuing to make money (she has two dots each in Bureaucracy and Finance) and in the halls of Elysium, even as - dare I say it this early in the game's run when it was still a stereotypical Toreador role - a Harpy. Seriously, she's got five dots in Charisma and four in Manipulation and Wits, as well as four dots in Subterfuge and three each in Acting, Empathy, Etiquette, and Style. Her Willpower is a nine (!) and her Demeanor is Survivor. Her three dots in Obfuscate means she can still operate around mortals, and her backgrounds even include one dot in Status, which makes me think that she actually is meant to be a Harpy. If so, I rather wish that'd been spelled out more clearly in the write up. My only real quibbles about his character is that she has a Perception of one, which I think is too low, and a Conscience of one as well, which is more debatable. I think I would've liked to see her have a Retainer as well, again to play a bit against the clan stereotype (perhaps using the points spent on that single dot in the Body Alteration ability). But this is a very strong character and one that looks like it would be very interesting to play. I like it a lot. A very promising start for the book.

            The Derelict
            This one plays very heavily into the clan stereotype, in this case, that of homeless bag lady. Out character was your average suburban working mom who got laid off from her job during a recession. Her depression, combined with the severe shift in the family's financial situation, caused her husband to leave her and take their daughter with him. She became an alcoholic and eventually homeless. Giving up, she tried to commit suicide in a back alley, only for a Nosferatu to feed on her and end up embracing her out of some twisted sense of guilt and remorse. She hates her existence even more now, to the point where it has at least deadened her previous sorrow and regret. Trait-wise, she's Mental primary with a 3/3/4 spread, and two dots each in Bureaucracy, Computer and Finance. Also the "street survival" abilities of Stealth and Survival at three dots and Alertness and Streetwise at two dots. Also twos in Drive, Etiquette and Repair. I'd really love to know where that Drive and Repair came from. Generally speaking, this is an interesting character background, and a character who seems to have the traits to go in more than a few directions. The problem is that, as it stands, she's just sort of there, kind of spinning her wheels and not going anywhere. She was homeless - and if I had to guess, may have had issues with depression and possible alcoholism even before losing her job - and is now a vampire. And I guess is just going to stay a bag lady? Out of some sense of bitter cynicism or just to wallow in her own misfortune, maybe? Which I guess works, at least for a little while. But with her abilities and the fact that the Nosferatu kind of have their own separate underground - sometimes literally - subculture even within the subculture that is the Kindred, it feels like there should be multiple opportunities for her to do more. A street agent for one of the elders, or one of the people who helps a warren make sense of and exploit all the paperwork and notes they scavenge from corporate and government trash bins. This isn't a bad character. I just feel like there should maybe be a little more here.

            The Equalizer
            This is another of those late 80s/early 90s archetypes. Our character is a small town guy who grew up in the closet and was able to come out in college, especially when getting involved with various gay rights groups. Unfortunately, he and his boyfriend were beaten in an attack, the latter fatally. The character only survived because a Nosferatu embraced him. Now he's determined to use his newfound strength to protect the outcast and oppressed members of society, human and Kindred alike. He's actually a Mental primary character, which fits his background, with Physicals as secondary and augmented by an extra dot from freebies. Besides his mortal holdover abilities - Language at three and Politics at two, with a few ones scattered about - he's also got his newly acquired twos in Brawl, Dodge, and Melee. His Humanity is a nine, which I think is too high, but not too much as I think this is a character who could actually get away with having an eight. Strangely, he only has a single dot in each of the clan Disciplines. If any character was going to have a three in Potence, I'd have expected it to be this guy. I like this character a lot, and I recall him being one of my favorites even back when this book came out. Even beyond the desire to strike back against bullies - even if only vicariously through an RPG - the Nosferatu are at heart a bunch of outcasts, even in the eyes of the other Kindred. People see them as monsters purely because of their looks, and I can see how this can echo very strongly with the idea of people being treated as less than human simply because of their sexual orientation. This is a character that takes the Nosferatu and make them work as a direct metaphor for a social topic, and does so in a way that neither beats you over the head with it nor abandons anything about what makes the clan or the game itself what it is. Whether a vampire looks like a deformed monster or a beautiful angel, it's ultimately their Humanity and the actions related to it that define them, much in the way that how a human being treats others is a far better measure of character than their level of social acceptability. It's a theme I like, and it's a big part of why I love this character so much.

            That's all for part one. I'm going to try to get four of them in to the second part.

                Clanbook: Nosferatu (part two)

                More monsters.

                The Gross Childe
                This is one of those names with a double meaning, being both a Childe in the Kindred sense and a child in the literal "this is a kid turned into a vampire" sense. By the time of the Revised era, there was a move away from the idea of children being embraced. It was already kind of frowned upon, but Revised went and spelled out a number of reasons why. It's a very serious potential Masquerade weakness, though that is perhaps not as big an issue with the Nosferatu, and is also seen as an act of cruelty, condemning someone to eternal unlike in a child's body. I'm not really sure this character really cares about that. This is your basic elementary school gross out artist and class clown who never really outgrew that phase in middle school. But where most would've been lucky enough to mature and turn this into a future career as a special effects makeup artist, horror film maker, or what have you, this kid was unfortunate enough to be embraced by a Nosferatu who found his antics entertaining. And like I said, he doesn't really care. In fact, the idea of being a gross looking monster for all eternity sounds down right fun. This is one of those characters I have mixed feelings about. One the one hand, I like the idea of something that plays into the idea that the the Nosferatu are gross and disturbing and will sometimes even revel in it in a "shock the mundanes" kind of way, and this character plays into that in a kind of odd and unique way. On the other hand, there's not a tremendous amount of depth here. That's not necessarily a bad thing, as the character is essentially a child. But given his focus, I can easily see this character being an outlet for certain problem players, effectively being the Nosferatu equivalent of the FishMalks I mentioned at the start of that book. Statistically speaking, he's pretty good. A Wits of five, a Manipulation and Dexterity of four each. Also a Charisma of one, which might be a little crippling in the long run. Three dots each in Stealth, Subterfuge, Dodge, and Toxicology (which is quite frankly disturbing, but in a good way). He's got a pet snake that has apparently gotten bigger after being fed his blood, making him the only character in the book with an animal Retainer. (The book spent a bit of space talking about Nosferatu spawning pits, so I'm a little surprised by that.) He has a Willpower of ten. I find that maybe too high, but I will admit it fits a little with certain aspects of the character's dogged commitment to his interests and lifestyle even in the face of all opposition. All in all, I like this character, but I'm very wary of it being abused by problem players. It's kind of like the Malkavian Waking Dreamer in that I'd have to really trust a player before letting them do this. I also kind of wish it had gone in a little bit more of a darker slant that the Toxicology ability seems to hint at. But it's still a good one.

                The Leatherface
                Damn. This may be the most unsubtle in your face thing in the entire series of clanbooks. It is the huge hulking brute of a murder machine popularized by the 80s Slasher Movie genre. The artwork is strange, barely looking like anything recognizably human. I don't even think it's wearing clothes. The write up describes a childhood of unimaginable abuse from its parents, including some indication that the father may have actually been a more conventional real world type of serial killer. Naturally, this character had a total psychotic break, believing that the near by River was talking to it, and eventually murdering its parents. A Nosferatu found it before the authorities did. Now it exists only to inflict on others the pain it felt in life. And that's entirely what its traits revolve around. Fours in Strength, Stamina, Manipulation, and Perception, as well as Intimidation and Melee. Three each in Brawl and Stealth. And in spite of a total lack of education, one dot each in Police Procedure and Serial Killer Trivia. I'll have to go hunting for where the Trivia secondary ability first appeared, but this book leans into it strongly, with three characters having it for whatever subculture they're a part of. I'm really surprised it doesn't have a few dots in Taxidermy. This is the only character to have an out of clan Discipline, with a single dot in Fortitude, which does make sense on a certain level. Conscience is one, Humanity five, and Willpower at nine. This is one of those characters that is a cool idea, but is extremely difficult to integrate into a game and coterie. All it does is kill, in as brutal a fashion as possible. And, in addition to potential risk to the Masquerade, I don't see why such a character would associate with other vampires. At best, I can see it lurking among the edges of the Nosferatu's underground world near its beloved River, but not even they go near it. This is a really great NPC, but not so great of a player character, at least in my opinion. As much as I'm a fan of the 80s Slasher films, I I think this character would've benefited from hewing a little closer to the real world murderers like Ed Gein who initially inspired them and taking a slightly subtler route. Incidentally, I keep referring to this character as "it" because there is no indication of what its gender is/was. I've actually always wanted to see the slasher film Brute/Masked Murder Engine archetype done with a female character. (Like a lot of horror fans my age, I was disappointed there was never a follow up on the ending of Halloween 4 that clearly planned to go in that direction.) Overall, I have to classify this as a good idea that doesn't quite work right.

                The Luchador Profesional
                I won't lie, this is my favorite one in the book. (The Equalizer comes close, but my love for professional wrestling makes this the winner.) This is your archetypical masked Mexican wrestler. And much in the way that the Detached Scientist birthed Dr. Netchurch, this one birthed El Diablo Verde. At first glance, it might seem that a professional wrestler, even a masked one, is out of place in Vampire. However, Mexico has a long tradition through the 1950s, '60s and '70s of wrestling films in which masked heroes fought vampires, werewolves, mummies and other monsters (as well as gangsters and other enemies of the people), and I find this a fun nod to that. Our character was a champion selected to be part of a mass Sabbat embrace, only to be rescued by Camarilla vampires. Now he wrestles in the US, and I suspect still smashes the enemies of the Camarilla when necessary. He's got five dots in Strength and Charisma, and fours in Stamina and Brawl. I'm a little surprised his Athletics is only at a two. All his discipline points are in Potence. He's got Fame, Herd, and Resources all based around his career as a professional wrestler. (While it may seem odd for a Nosferatu, it's traditional for masked Mexican wresters to never remove their masks in public, and even in the presence of anyone.) As I said, I like this character a lot. He's a little over the top, but he's a really interesting subversion of the Nosferatu stereotype who can fill a number of roles in a chronicle or coterie.

                The Morbid Artsy Type Vindictively Embraced
                (I've always felt those last two words were somehow meant to be in parenthesis for some reason.) So, what we have here is essentially a somewhat mean spirited stereotype of the '90s suburban Goth, ie, the presumed target audience for the Vampire RPG. When I was graduating high school when this book originally came out, I found this character fairly amusing. But now, over 25 years later, I'm not longer very impressed when I see creators disparage - or even express outright hatred towards - their customers for not conforming to their ideal of what's cool or in fashion at the moment. While this character has something in common with Brujah's Fake Rapper, it differs in that the Fake Rapper takes the time to put its character in an interesting situation (having to continuously play an invented role), gives him some useful skills, and potential for growth. The Morbid Artsy Type lacks that. His artistic ability and occult knowledge aren't as impressive as he believes them to be, and it strait out says he's not good for much of anything. It's a character that exists for no reason other than to be made fun of. But adding to this is that the stats on the character sheet don't exactly match up with this. He's got five dots in both Manipulation and Perception, which is really impressive. Acting, Alertness, and Subterfuge are all at three dots, and Etiquette and Stealth are at two (as is Occult and Literature). These stats are for someone who'd fit in pretty well at Elysium, and actually be kind of dangerous as he lurks about largely unnoticed, able to eavesdrop on others and drop in certain comments to drive things in a certain direction. I wish the write up had reflected or acknowledged this. Or possibly have sown the seeds of the embrace having a sort of galvanizing effect for him to eventually evolve into a classic Byronic (anti)hero. So, over all, this is an OK character, but with some work and less of a fixation on mocking a certain segment of the fanbase would've been a lot stronger and more interesting.

                    Clanbook: Nosferatu (part three)

                    And our final three.

                    The Shock Rocker
                    This one is right up their with the Equalizer and the Luchador Professional as my favorites in the book, and again, this owes a little bit to my own interests and tastes. But it's also ties into the way it reflects the clan. What we have is the archetypical heavy metal rocker whose act is based more on spectacle than on musical genius. Effectively the likes of KISS, Alice Cooper, and certain stories about Ozzy Osborne taken in a heavy splatterpunk horror direction. This is just about the time Marylin Manson was first starting to become known outside of Florida, and that is probably why it plays more on late 80s metal tropes than 90s ones. The character was a young teen metalhead who dreamed of emulating his idols, but has a much better talent for eliciting shock reactions from people with his antics than in actually playing music. But he certainly put in the work to be the best shock spectacle he could be, and its always nice to see someone succeed at doing what they love. He attracted some non-human fans, and one of them decided to embrace him. And both he and his bandmates have taken the change pretty well. If anything, it's made him even better at his chosen career. His Charisma is maxed out at five dots, and his Manipulation isn't far behind at four. His two best abilities are Intimidation and Leadership. Music is only one dot, but Acting is two, which makes sense, but so are Melee and Drive for some reason. Everything else is a scattering of ones, including things like Finance, Politics, Style, and Body Alteration. (That last one is something that showed up in the Players Guide to the Sabbat - which I'll have to talk about soon - mainly as a skill to use with the Vicissitude discipline, but also slightly related to body piercing, tattooing, and plastic surgery.) He's got one dot in each clan Discipline, as well as an extra one in Presence, which makes complete sense even though it's usually the last power you'd expect a Nosferatu to cultivate. He's got a lot more background points than average, with threes each in. Allies, Contacts, and Herd, as well as two in Resources and one in Fame. Humanity is only six, which feels low. Willpower is five. As I said, I really like this character. He's fun in a way that is over the top, and while I might restrict this to a player I trusted not to FishMalk it up, it does at least come with a built in break in that it says if people start to ignore his antics, he tends to quietly sulk rather than up the ante. While I think a few trait adjustments would make some improvements, he's fairly solid, and I like the fact that he does not fall into the stereotype of Nosferatu being miserable at their appearance.

                    The Tunnel Crawler
                    This was a life-long quiet and studious nerd. Eventually he became a fairly brilliant engineer and architect. Too brilliant, as a warren of Nosferatu decided they needed his skill and genius. Now instead of erecting skyscrapers, he's digging catacombs into the earth. And he seems to be fairly OK with this. His Intelligence and Perception are both at four dots, as is his Stamina. Alertness, Repair, Computer, Science, and Security are all at three dots, with the secondary ability of Architecture at five dots. He's got the one dot per clan Discipline thing again, and with an additional dot in Auspex that, while interesting, I don't think is really needed. Humanity is at eight, which feels fitting given how sheltered he was prior to the embrace. Generally, this character is OK. There's nothing noticeably bad about it. However, I think it's biggest flaw is that this character lives totally underground, and has no reason at all to go above ground or interact with any non-Nosferatu vampires. If you're doing an all Nosferatu chronicle, that's probably OK, but for anything else, not so much. It also leans heavily into the idea that the Nosferatu seem to exist entirely underground, and I wish it had played a little more with the idea of various urban buildings having secret passages and byways that Nosferatu have snuck into the blueprints and then erased from the records as a way to move around the cities and places of power without being seen or other vampires knowing about it. So while this is not a bad character, I feel it could've been a lot stronger and more interesting.

                    The Vet
                    Another period archetype. And, once again, I'm pretty certain that this one is essentially just a refinement of the one presented in the Players Guide. The initial part of the prelude is a little off, as it describes the typical Vietnam era stereotype of the poor kid that couldn't go to college or get a draft deferment and ended up fighting in the jungles of ... Africa. Unless this guy was part of an air crew on one of the less than half a dozen transport planes that provided logistical support to Congolese troops during that nation's civil war - and the write up makes it clear that isn't the case - I'm left to conclude that either the history of the WoD is even more different from ours than I ever thought, or that Rob Hatch was sick the day they covered the difference between Asia and Africa in geography class. But that aside, this is the stereotypical Vietnam era vet coming back to a country that didn't really want to acknowledge his existence, could't really keep a job, and ended up homeless. And then a vampire. In spite of it probably being a decade or more since he saw combat, all of his abilities are geared around being a soldier. Alertness, Brawl, Dodge, Melee, Stealth, and Survival are all at three dots, with Firearms at four. Anything else, including Streetwise, is just a one dot afterthought. His backgrounds include a one dot Retainer, but no mention of who or what that is or how that came about. His equipment is an Army jacket, combat boots, aviator shades, a .45 revolver and an M-16. I'm not sure how a homeless guy walks around with an M-16 and no one notices. Or why this is more useful than the Rambo-style survival knife. On some level, I think maybe this character is supposed to be Rambo, at least as he was in the original First Blood film. And while that's not a terrible idea, I think that sort of thing would need some stat tweaking and more description of his personality in the write up. So, again, this isn't a bad character, but I think it could've been a little better with some more thought put into it.

                    And those are our Nosferatu. Overall, a fairly strong showing, I think. Three of them I really enjoyed, and two others, the Cleopatra and Gross Childe, I like a lot. Four of them I think are good, but could've been great with just a little more work and focus. Overall, I think the weakest one is the Leatherface, as it is the hardest to incorporate into a coterie or chronicle, and is less of a character than it is a plot device. So that's a good collection.
                    In terms of representation, only two of our characters are expressly female, and one of them was embraced to punish her for being a good looking but petty person. (The same could probably also be said for the Morbid Artsy Type.) However, as I mentioned, it's really impossible to guess what gender the Leatherface is/was. And by the same token, it's also impossible to guess what race or ethnicity any of the characters were in their living days, save for the Luchador Professional, who is Mexican. It would probably be a safe guess, though, that the Morbid Artsy Type and the Shock Rocker were probably white. And finally, the Equalizer is, to my knowledge, the first of our character templates to be explicitly LGBT+.
                    Given how much space the clanbook devotes to certain aspects of Nosferatu culture - including their archetypical role as spies and collectors of information, as well as their monstrous spawning pools and certain artistic endeavors - I'm a little surprised that none of those characters really deal with any of that. There is no Lorekeeper, or Spy Master, or Professional Blackmailer, or Garbage Collector (similar to what The Penguin did in Tim Burton's Batman Returns). No Phantom of the Opera style singer with the face of a monster and the voice of an angel. And, though this is probably before people had heard of the hobby, no urban explorer either. I'm also a little surprised we don't have a character who takes full advantage of the clan's affinity for Animalism. I think one of those Crazy Cat Lady types who lurks alone in a creepy house with two dozen cats would be an interesting idea.
                    I think the Nosferatu theme is basically that of the Outcast. They don't fit in. They are markedly different from other Kindred, and are kept apart from mundane humanity by their curse. And many of them were probably outcasts even before the embrace. Look at our collection of characters. The homeless, the unwanted veterans who reminded everyone of America's losing war, the homosexuals, the helpless victims of abuse, the socially awkward misfits and nerds, and those who are too self-involved to understand how isolated they are from others. Even El Diablo Verde has set himself apart from others the moment he decided to put on the mask and devote himself to the role of masked champion. And so do the alternate ideas I mentioned above. I think this sense of being an outcast and apart from others is part of what makes a great Nosferatu character, and all of these characters have that, even if some of the executions are flawed.

                    And so that concludes Clanbook: Nosferatu. Next, I will be looking at something that isn't a clanbook, the Players Guide to the Sabbat, which also came out in 1993.

                        The Players Guide to the Sabbat (part one)

                        So, 1993 also saw the release of our first look at the dreaded and feared Sabbat and made them playable. There's been debate in some quarters ever since if this was a good idea or not. This book was entirely written by Steven C. Brown, and the template art was once again by Josh Timbrook. I'm not sure how he got the gig as "guy who does all the template art", but it's a nice bit of continuity.
                        The Sabbat are a little different. For one, they encompass almost all the clans (meaning I'm going to be talking about a few of them before I really wanted to), including the all new in this book Lasombra and Tzimisce. In addition, the Gangrel are split in to two separate groups: the Country Gangrel, who are basically the same as normal Gangrel, and the City Gangrel, who get a different Discipline spread (Celerity and Obfuscate instead of Animalism and Fortitude) but keep the same weakness. The Venture also get Auspex in place of Presence, apparently with the reasoning that they're not allowed to be in charge because "reasons." (Basically, because they are associated with the Camarilla and being "The Man." This same sort of specious logic would also be behind the Ventrue being omitted from Veil of Night and Kindred of the Ebony Kingdom, on the basis of them somehow being so totally associated in the minds of the writers and developers with Europe that they just couldn't conceive of them in any other context, like say, Persia.) The Setites are the Serpents of Light, and are Caribbean instead of Egyptian, which somehow makes them more heroic or something. (I've honestly long wished that name had instead been used for a group that tied into the idea of the Serpent in the Garden and the Lightbringer to be some sort of Setite sect devoted to Luciferian Satanism. But I'm weird.) And we have the Panders, who are just Caitiff with a cool name and a sense of belonging.
                        The Sabbat get no background dots, but get an extra dot in Disciplines, because they are more focused on being vampires than pretending to be mortal. I'm not sure why they didn't just keep the existing character rules (or knock them down to three) and insist that Sabbat characters try to limit their backgrounds to things like Generation, Sabbat Status, Pack Recognition, Black Hand Membership, and Mentor. Instead of Humanity, all characters follow one of the new Paths of Enlightenment, devoted to certain inhuman and esoteric values. And to reflect that, the Conscience and Self-Control virtues are replaces with Callousness and Instinct.

                        The Assamite antitribu Assassin (aka Mercenary)
                        This one is both very alliterative and very low hanging fruit. Basically the very first idea anyone ever thought of with the 1st and 2nd ed Assamites and with their antitribu in this book. A street thug and leg breaker who ran off to join the Foreign Legion to avoid a murder rap. Then he came back to Alabama of all places and tried to open a survivalist camp, only to have a vampire hire him to kill someone as a test of his skills before embracing him. He's basically your stock Black Hand recruit, skilled at what he does, but kind of arrogant about it and a bit of a bully. The concept write up says it's unusual that he's not Middle Eastern. (I'd really forgotten how much I disliked that aspect of the pre-Clayton Oliver Assamites.) Naturally, all of his Abilities revolve around being a KillNinja, with three dots each in Brawl, Dodge, Intimidation, Firearms, and Melee, and two dots in Stealth. Everything else is a scattering of ones. Physicals are primary. For some odd reason he has a 3/2/1 spread in Socials. I guess it's to make him more intimidating. He's got two dots in Celerity and one each in Obfuscate and Quietus. Again, KillNinja. Weirdly, while the Assamite antitribu write up claims almost every member follows the Path of Caine, with rare exceptions on the Path of Honorable Accord, this one follows the Path of Power and the Inner Voice. This was often one of the Paths singled out for being "The Path of Whatever I Was Going To Do Anyway", which makes it oddly fitting for a KillNinja. I guess this character is OK. He's very one dimensional, and like I said, the most obvious character idea for this clan. Weirdly, when I started reading the write up, I kind of got the idea that he might be French for some reason, at least until it said he came back home to the States, and I don't know why but I find him more interesting as French. Generally, I just find this character kind of blah. (Although, if someone playing this character decided to go by the handle of M. Pierre KillNinja, I'd be highly amused.)

                        City Gangrel antitribu Animal Trainer
                        This is a middle aged widow who took up raising animals to sell as pets and meat (I'm guessing this is mainly rabbits, but there's no telling). Some mysterious woman who was also interested in animals struck up a friendship with her, and eventually inducted her into the Sabbat. She holds herself very much aloof from her pack, still living her her old home apart from them, and resenting when she sends her animals to help the pack and they get killed. (I have to presume she also raises dogs, but I can't help but imagine a Sabbat pack being aided by a herd of angry, blood thirsty rabbits and chickens.) She is a total Loner, with that as both Nature and Demeanor. Her highest abilities are Alertness, Animal Ken, and Science at three dots each. Alertness, Brawl, Dodge, Streetwise, and Survival are all at two. She's on the Path of Harmony, which is kind of the stereotypical "creature of nature and instinct" one that Gangrel antitribu prefer. Her Disciplines are one in Celerity, and two each in Obfuscate and Protean. Now, before I mention it, can you spot what is missing here? This is a character built around the idea of raising a bunch of animals and training them to aid the pack, and she lacks any ability with Animalism. I have this suspicion that this character was supposed to be a Country Gangrel, with the usual Disciplines, but someone decided that they needed to showcase the new City Gangrel variant, and just changed it, forgetting this little detail. (I'd prefer to believe this, as sometimes these things happen to writers, but also because the alternative is that someone actually sat down and wrote a Vampire template entirely based around a Discipline the character doesn't have.) All of that said, I actually rather like this character. I just think that she would be a lot better as a regular Gangrel character in the Camarilla, Anarchs or just unaligned than part of the Sabbat. The Sabbat originally drew a lot of inspiration from the film Near Dark, and that film has always influenced how I tended to see them. The pack should be the center of a Sabbat's world, and the focus of their night to night existence. And it isn't for this one. While I could possibly see a scenario where this character's story arc focusing on her leaving the Sabbat and maybe regaining her Humanity, the write up doesn't really set that up. So this isn't a bad character. I just think it's in the wrong book.

                            The Players Guide to the Sabbat (part two)

                            And continuing right along.

                            The Lasombra Manipulator (aka Leader Wannabe)
                            So, the Lasombra were presented as the Clan focused on leadership and intrigue, and this character leans into that idea pretty hard. Presented as a spoiled kid who grew into a manipulative corporate salesman, only to get embraced after taking advantage of what turned out to be a Sabbat pack. He's adapted fairly well, being a competent and brave leadership figure for his pack who takes the time to develop personal rapport with each member. The kind of guy you'd probably want to have in charge of your group, and his stats tend to back up this description. Which is why I'm a little puzzled at the character sheet listing his concept as "Leader Wannabe" instead of something like "Smooth Talking Politico" or the like. The term wannabe tends to imply that someone is trying to be something they clearly aren't. But that aside, his stats aren't bad. He's got a Manipulation of five, natural, with Charisma, Intelligence and Wits at three. Leadership, Subterfuge, and Finance (a hold over from his mortal life that still gets put to use from time to time) are all at three dots. Empathy, Drive, Streetwise, Etiquette, and Investigation are all at two dots, and there's a scattering of ones. His only combat related abilities are one dot each in Dodge and Firearms (and the write up spends a weird amount of detail on just what guns he carries and in which circumstances), which might be a bit of a disadvantage among the Sabbat. However, he does have that two in Drive, so I have this mental image of him being the driver when the Pack decides to do drive-by or other activities. He's got seven dots in backgrounds, which again raises my earlier question about why they didn't just give Sabbat characters limited background points, since everyone seems to end up having them. Willpower is at an eight, and he follows the Path of Power and the Inner Voice, which seems fitting. Overall, this is your textbook "the Lasombra are leaders of the Sabbat so this Lasombra is a pack leader" character, which isn't necessarily a bad thing. He is what he is and seems to do it fairly well. I would however, love to hear the story of how a young sales department type ended up looking like a member of a 90s Goth band wearing fishhooks as earrings.

                            The Malkavian antitribu Mime (aka Lunatic)
                            I approached this character with a great deal of trepidation. Anything involving Malkavians as clowns or clown-related, including mimes, is often a surefire sign of a FishMalk. Having the character sheet concept say "Lunatic" on top of that just makes it worse. So, our character was always "strange" and ended up in a home for wayward boys at the age of 10. Apparently having "violent impulses beyond [his] control," he killed another kid and ended up in a mental hospital. I'm left to conclude that "strange" here is a euphemism for "killed small animals and lit buildings on fires." Some Sabbat decided it would be amusing to break into the hospital and let all the patients out to roam free, and he took advantage of this to apparently murder several staff members. The Sabbat decided this meant he would make a perfect recruit. Most of the times, he's supposed to be fairly normal, kind and gentle, having insightful conversations with pack mates. And then every so often he gets a wild hair up his ass to dress up as a mime and brutally murder people. Apparently because a mime scared him as a child. Let that sink in for a minute. He's Mental primary and Physical secondary, and his abilities are about what you'd expect from a competent Sabbat pack member. Three dots each in Melee and Stealth, and twos in Brawl, Empathy, Intimidation, and Survival, with a few more ones to balance it out. And three dots in Acting for the Mime thing, I guess. Or maybe just to try to appear normal. His Disciplines are two each in Auspex and Demenetation, and one in Obfuscate. He's on the Path of Harmony, which generally fits his personality. Except for the mime thing, which violates several of its basic sins. I almost but not quite come close to hating this character. The mime thing is a blatant excuse to FishMalk. But I think what most irks me about this character is that, if you just ditched the stupid mime bit, this would actually be a pretty good character. Someone who has always been, in a sense, broken, and is now in a clan and a sect/cult that is only making him more broken, but who has ironically been thrown this lifeline in the form of the Path of Harmony. That path focuses on the idea that these violent impulses he's always struggled with aren't automatically "bad" or "evil", only that they must be tempered and only unleashed when necessary. And if he's around a pack that mainly follows this path, he has a peer support group who understand him in a way that family and doctors and the like never would have. That would, in my opinion at least, have been a really cool angle and a way of turning some of the perceptions of the Sabbat on its head. But they had to go and screw it up with the whole "look, a killer mime! LOL" thing. And that's why I come so close to hating this character. If you dropped the mime thing and made him someone akin to, say, Norman Bates, who is usually normal and personable but occasionally has these psychotic breaks, and was trying to struggle with how these outbursts interfere with his following the Path of Harmony, I think you'd have a really strong and potentially tragic character. Instead, we get a mime.

                            (I actually don't dislike the mime character art. If this was something other than a "look how kooky the Malkavians are" character, I might actually like it. I could see some sort of character who was just a street mime who worked in the parks, only to get snatched up as part of a mass embrace and is now part of the Sabbat without really wanting to be, perhaps using the mime make up as a way to disassociate themselves from the horrors they now commit. And I think such a thing would have made an interesting City Gangrel, Ravnos, Toreador, Ventrue or Pander concept.)

                                        The Players Guide to the Sabbat (part three)

                                        I'm not sure why, but for some reason I was thinking there were ten Sabbat templates, but there is actually only nine of them.

                                        The Pander Anarchist (aka Jack of All Trades)
                                        Basically a poor kid from a small town whose older brother had already been embraced into the Sabbat and decided to have his younger brother join them. And that's about it. He's not an anarchist, but dislikes authority and tends to question and oppose leadership as a knee jerk reaction. He's more of a devil's advocate than an anarchist. And that's reiterated more than once. He's got Stamina at five dots and three dots in each of his Mental traits. Brawl and Survival are at three dots each, and Athletics, Dodge, Streetwise, Drive, and Firearms are all at two. Then a broad collection of ones in a number of other abilities. So he does fit the jack of all trades descriptor. Panders have no in clan Disciplines, and he takes advantage of this fact to have one dot each in Auspex, Celerity, Dominate, Fortitude, and Vicissitude. He follows the Path of Cathari, which is partially derived from old Gnostic beliefs and focused on obtaining pleasure and power. It's another one that was often referred to as "The Path of Doing Whatever I Want". This is not a bad character, just a very shallow one. This could easily be any faceless member of any random Sabbat pack. Which isn't a bad thing. I just find myself wishing that the write up focused more on the devil's advocate aspect, as well as the brief mention of his mischief making and carousing with pack mates. So I generally like this one more than I thought I would, but I can see some room for improvement.

                                        The Serpent of Light Mystery Man (aka Ongun)
                                        While not spelled out, it's implied that this character is probably Haitian, and his back story is that he was drafted into a voodoo cult's secret war against the Followers of Set at a young age, eventually being chosen to join the Serpents of Light. He's well versed in certain aspects of the occult and from all indications has adjusted pretty well to existence in the Sabbat. He often tries to appear mysterious and enigmatic just to look wiser than he probably is. He also enjoys collecting spiders. He's Mental primary and Social secondary. Other than Subterfuge and Occult at three dots each, his abilities are mostly twos in things that allow him to serve the pack socially and as a scout. He's on the Path of Death and the Soul, which is one of the ones favored by the Serpents of Light. Much like the Pander, this isn't a bad character. He's essentially the archetypical Serpent of Light, and is competently built as such. And I swear that the character art reminds me of a specific rap/hip hop artist from the very late 80s/very early 90s, I just can't for the life of me remember who. Also, his equipment entry seems strangely fixated on details about his gun. So, another of those "out of central casting" characters like the Pander and the Assamite. Which, again, isn't necessarily a bad thing, as those certainly have their place. I just once against find myself wishing he was fleshed out a little more in the write up, especially the idea that he's slightly faking his persona in an effort to live up to others' expectations.

                                        The Toreador antitribu Sculptor (aka Torture(d) Artist)
                                        I kind of like this one for some reason. I suspect part of that is she reminds me a bit of the character of Jane from MTV's old Daria animated series, both in origin and how the character art looks (although this predates that show by a few years). She grew up in a family of fairly well off Bohemian artist types and was an extremely talented sculptor. But at some point, her art started becoming more and more morbid and macabre. (It's not said why this happened, but I can't help but wonder if this is some sort of reaction to turning 30 or 40, or else being diagnosed with some sort of terminal disease, either of which would be interesting, I think.) This attracted the attention of one of the Sabbat, and she was embraced. She's actually adapted to being in the sect fairly well, although still insists on keeping her own private haven and studio at what could very well be her family's mansion. Her Dexterity is at five dots, with threes in Stamina, Charisma, Manipulation, and Perception. However, she's got a one in Wits, which feels like it may be too low for some reason. Her main skills are Sculpting at four dots and Melee at three (which fits pretty well as she prefers using her art tools as weapons), with a bunch of twos in Drive, Etiquette, Stealth, Finance, Language, Medicine and Occult.                                             The Tzimisce Priest (aka Weird Lady)
                                            The Tzimisce were the other "leader clan" of the Sabbat, sort of like the Tremere to the Camarilla's Ventrue, in that they seem to take a more spiritual role than the political one of the Lasambra. This would change a little as the game went on, but that's the general impression their initial presentation left. This is also before the idea of flesh crafting freakishness and transhumanism became a thing with the clan, so this character is surprisingly low key and restrained for a Tzimisce, both in terms of concept and the artwork. And I find myself appreciating that. When every other Tzimisce is a Cenobite from Hellraiser, it's nice to see one that isn't. Our character was your typical middle class suburban housewife and mother who happened to get picked for the embrace. How she's sort of a mother hen type for her pack, offering leadership and spiritual guidance, while also occasionally reigning them in when necessary. Interestingly, she used to miss her family, but has apparently put that behind her. I say apparently, mainly just because I find that an interesting character angle of a Sabbat vampire who has never been able to fully divorce themselves from their previous life, and if playing this character, I'd probably have it be a reoccurring thing. Her Manipulation, Perception, and Intelligence are all at four dots. Her highest ability is a three in Occult, with twos in Politics, Investigation, Leadership, and Subterfuge. Everything else is only at one dot, including Dodge and Firearms, her only combat skills. Her Disciplines are one in Auspex and three in Vicissitude, making her the only character in the book to have a Discipline at higher than two dots. Oddly, given her focus on Vicissitude, she only has one dot in the Body Alteration secondary ability. If you remember me mentioning it when talking about Nosferatu, this ability primarily exists for use in Vicissitude power roles. While the first level power uses Intelligence + Body Alteration, meaning she can sort of "brute force" her way though it mentally, the second and third level powers use Dexterity and Strength, respectively, and she only has those at two. So the character is focused on a Discipline she's not actually that great at using. And this brings up something else I noticed when reading the character write up. It mentions her being taken from a Catholic Church by a man dressed as a Catholic priest, who used Dominate to make her come with him. The combination of Catholic tropes and the Dominate discipline sounds more like your archetypical Lasombra embrace. And when I went and looked at the rules for Obtenebration, I see that the first level power doesn't require roles, but the second and third level ones both use Manipulation + Occult. I can't help but wonder if this character was originally created to be the Lasombra template, but was changed to Tzimisce at the last minute. (Also, while the character has thirteen dots in Knowledges and five dots in Skills, she only has eight dots in Talents, and then the one dot of Body Alteration, which is supposed to be a Skill. Again, it feels like this character was remade at the last minute as a Tzimisce, and a Talent dot taken to make sure she had a token showing in Body Alteration.) Overall, I like this character. I think "mother hen" is an interesting role to have in a Sabbat pack, especially back during these early days when they were in the "run wild and act crazy" stage. But I think this character works better as a Lasombra. If I were to keep her as a Tzimisce, I'd probably shift her Discipline focus to Auspex and some Animalism, either a Auspex 2/Animalism 1/Vicissitude 1 set up, or 2/2 or 3/1 split between the first two. If I wasn't using Vicissitude, I'd take that Body Alternation dot and use it to raise her Alertness or Intimidation to two.

                                            The Ventrue antitribu Know-It-All (aka Whiner)
                                            I've always had sort of mixed feelings about the Ventrue antitribu. I think there was an issue in the early days of them coming across as Brujah wannabes. However, on rereading their entry in this book, I don't think that's entirely fair to them. I think there's a really cool idea here about individualism centered on the idea of reaching one's ultimate potential unfettered by shackles like tradition and politics, and it ties in really well with the Clan's overall themes. (And I think the Presence discipline honestly ties into that idea better than Auspex does, at least in my opinion.) But I think it gets a little muddled in the idea of them trying to be different than everyone else, especially the Camarilla Ventrue. And seeing "Whiner" listed under this character's concept certainly doesn't help their image. Also, it took me a minute to realize that the character pictured is wearing a trench coat of some sort rather than a Wiccan style robe or something. Anyway, this character is a natural born genius, always getting straight As and excelling academically, but lacking the charisma or ability to pick up on certain social cues to keep from coming across as pompous and unlikeable. She became a law student only for her to get taken for a mass embrace. (It mentions that she survived but her roommate, who was also taken, didn't. I kind of wish more had been done with that angle.) Now she's just an average member of the pack, save that she constantly annoys everyone by telling them what to do, pointing out when they're wrong, and saying "I told you so" when it turns out she was right all along, and being blissfully oblivious to just how irritating this make her to everyone else. I find this kind of amusing, and while I'd want to make sure it never became annoying to the point of making it un-fun for the players as opposed to the characters, it can make for a very interesting social dynamic in the pack. It says she complains when told what to do, but it feels like this is more one of those "I already know what to do" or "that's a dumb thing to do" type thing than actual whining. So I think the Concept should be different. (Like, maybe, the "Know-It-All" in the write up header.) Our character really is a genius, with an Intelligence of five. Weirdly, her Physicals are secondary, with a four in Stamina and three in Dexterity. (Also threes in Manipulation and Perception, with a one in Charisma.) I say this is weird because the write up goes out of the way to mention she's good looking, so I'm sort of surprised she doesn't have Socials as secondary in order to bump Appearance up higher. She's got three dots in Law, which makes sense because of her background, and then two each in Investigation, Language, Etiquette, and Drive. (There's that Drive skill again. What is it with Sabbat and cars? This is way, way before Fast & Furious was ever a thing.) Everything else are ones scattered all over the place. Given her personality, I'm a little surprised her Dominate is only one dot. She follows the Path of Honorable Accord. Overall, I like this character a lot more than I thought I would when I started rereading, and also like the Ventrue antitribu a bit more than I remember doing so originally. She's generally a fairly fun personality, and a bit different from what you'd expect from the Sabbat.

                                            And so that's the character templates from The Players Guide to the Sabbat. Overall, they aren't that bad. I'm rather disappointed that we didn't get a Ravnos, Tremere, or Nosferatu. I actually think that the Leatherface concept from their clanbook would've worked a lot better here using the Sabbat rules. There are a few oddities here. As I've mentioned, I think the Animal Trainer would've worked a lot better as a normal non-Sabbat Gangrel, especially give the City Gangrel's lack of Animalism. I think the Tzimisce Priest would make more sense from several angles as a Lasombra. And the Malkavian would be so much better without the &#!%ing mime thing. A third of our characters - the Assamite, Pander, and Serpent of Light - are very basic archetypes, the kind you'd expect to see in almost any pack. I think the Toreador and, strangely, the Ventrue, ended up being my favorites out of the bunch.
                                            As far as issues of representation go, four of the nine are female, although the Lasambra artwork has this androgynous quality to it and never really specifies the character being male or female. The Serpent of Light, obviously, is Afro-Caribbean, and I could actually see the Pander as being meant to be Native American or mixed-race. The Assamite looks like he could be read as Asian, if you wanted to, as could the Ventrue.
                                            While reading this, it occurred to me that, given how all Sabbat were automatically on a Path of Enlightenment at the time, and that we have six paths (not counting the Path of Evil Revelations, which was included in this book for some inexplicable reason), it would've been cool to see two templates for each Path, giving us twelve total, and allowing us to show almost all of the Sabbat clans. It also occurred to me that, in all honesty, the Sabbat should be kind of fractured along Path lines, with each pack all following the same Path of Enlightenment, lead by the pack priest, and being inducted into it as part of the creation rites. At least I think that would be an interesting idea to play around with.
                                            So, theme wise, the Sabbat are supposed to be monsters who've turned their backs on humanity and their old lives. Some of these - the Lasombra and the Tzimisce, especially - fit this idea really well. The Toreador and potentially the Pander also seem to have embraced this to a certain degree. Most of the others, not so much. As I mentioned in the Mime entry, I think it would've been really interesting to present this as a person who is potentially better off as a vampire. And the Gangrel Animal Trainer is the one who reflects this idea the least, which is probably why I keep thinking she'd be better as a regular Camarilla or unaligned Gangrel. So overall, I'd have to say that this is mostly successful, but not as big of a success as it could've been.

                                                    Interlude: Back to the Core Book

                                                    (Apologies ahead of time; This ended up being longer than I intended for a single post.)

                                                    As Nail Eater mentioned, the second edition core book included tiny little filled in character sheets for each clan. These were, apparently, meant to be the characters illustrated in the artwork that accompanied each clan's two page write up. (This art is, btw, completely different from the art used in the first edition's similar section, and these images would go a huge way toward influencing people's perception of each clan for a lot of the second edition period. This two page spread template would likewise go on to be the standard for almost all of White Wolf's future books, both for the World of Darkness and later Exalted. It's actually a pretty good marketing tactic, as these things are usually almost the first thing people would look at back in the days of going into your local game store and flipping through something before buying it.)

                                                    Artwork is, again, by Josh Timbrook.

                                                    As with the Players Guide, we have seven of them.

                                                    Out Brujah is, according to her concept, a Revolutionary. Her Nature is Cavalier, which isn't actually in the core book. Physicals are primary and Mental secondary, which does rather set the stage for how the clan would be presented in the clanbook. Abilities are spread fairly evenly, with three dots each in Brawl, Intimidation, and Firearms, making a character that's clearly expecting to see a lot of combat. Alertness, Leadership, Streetwise, Drive, Melee, and Stealth are all at two dots. Politics is only at one dot, which is a little ironic given how almost every character in the clanbook seemed to have it at three. She's also got one dot in Finance, which is interesting, if seeming a little random. Disciplines are spread evenly, with an extra one in Presence. Backgrounds are Allies, Contacts and Herd. Humanity is seven and Willpower is six. This all feels like it fits the character in the picture. She has this kind of female Billy Idol thing going on, which I don't dislike, and looks like she'd be the kind of character to have these stats, especially the Intimidation factor. (I think I want to repurpose her for use as a Redcap in Changeling.) A pretty striking image with good stats, even if maybe her Charisma could stand to be a little higher. I think this is partially why the Brujah were my favorite clan for the first few years of the game.

                                                    Next we have our Gangrel. He - at least I think it's a he; there's a certain androgynous quality to it - has the listed concept of ... Hippie. (you go to hell, game; you go to hell and die) Nature is Survivor; Demeanor is Loner, setting up the classic clan stereotype. Once again, Physicals are primary and Mental secondary. Though Charisma is a three at the expense of a Manipulation of one. We've got Alertness, Athletics, and Survival are at three dots, while we have twos in Brawl, Dodge, Empathy, Stealth, and Science. No Firearms or Melee, but with two dots in Brawl, I have to wonder if this is supposed to be one of those Billy Jack type "hippies." Disciplines are, again, spread evenly with an extra dot, this time in Protean. Backgrounds are Allies, Herd and Mentor. Humanity and Willpower are both at seven. This isn't a bad set of stats. However, they don't add up, as they appear to have spent four more freebie points than they should have. But I don't know where the hippie thing is coming from. Even the character image looks more like an extra from The Lost Boys or a member of a period Seattle grunge band. And that's not a bad look either. If I was to try to name this character's concept, I think I'd go more for something along the lines of "Loner" or "Drifter". Really, this isn't bad. The name just bugs me to no end.

                                                    Moving right along, we come to the Malkavian. This one is a Neurotic Comedian ... damn it. I haven't kept up with the stand up comedy scene for over a decade, but back in the 80s, there was a thing with "wacky crazy" or otherwise "oddball" comics. Robin Williams, especially when he was doing cocaine really heavily, and Bobcat Goldthwait, among others. And this is apparently what this character is going for. Nature is Jester and Demeanor is Deviant, because of course they are. Mental is primary and Social secondary, with most of that focused on the four in Manipulation. His best ability is Acting, at three dots. The rest are twos, including Alertness, Empathy, Intimidation, Subterfuge, Melee, Bureaucracy, and Linguistics, rounded out with several ones. Backgrounds are Herd, Influence, Resources, and Contacts. Disciplines are the same even spread, with an additional one in Dominate. Humanity and Willpower are both six. The character image is of a guy hanging upside down, hands on his head with fingers through disheveled hair, eyes wild and screaming. He's wearing a plaid shirt open to reveal a white t-shirt with "Now I have a Machine Gun Ho Ho Ho" written on it. (Yes, a Die Hard reference.) He also has a tie, that's just tied around his bare neck, because, you know, wacky. Just from a pure traits perspective (and again, these don't add up, apparently having spent two freebie points too many), this isn't a bad character. Fairly versatile, and I think it would work really well as, say, some sort of office drone or college student who just sort of snapped or was burning out only to get turned into a vampire. Maybe move the points in Acting a little, and change the Nature/Demeanor to something like Conformist, Director or Visionary. But I really dislike the art, and I suspect this largely torpedoed any chance of the Malkavians ever being taken seriously or seen as scary for much of the game's run.

                                                    Next is the Nosferatu and, looking at his sheet I see that ... it is a reprint of the Malkavian one. Yep, a layout error. Thankfully, the actually sheet was put in the Malkavian clanbook (along with "Page XX"), under the section talking about their views on other clans. The art is of a Nosferatu in very nice pinstripe suit and bowler hat, holding a pocket watch. This is a total 180 from the stereotypical clan image, and I wish it was used more often, to be honest. The sheet lists his concept as Corrupt Industrialist (and really, is there any other kind in early WoD books?). Mental is primary, with fours in Intelligence and Wits, and Social secondary, with a five in Manipulation. (He has a one listed under Appearance, but I suspect this is just an artifact of the basic character sheet.) Dexterity is only a one, with Stamina a three. He's got a four in Finance (making him the first of the bunch to have more than three in an ability), and threes in Bureaucracy and Etiquette. Empathy, Intimidation, Leadership, Subterfuge, Investigation, and Linguistics are all at two dots. (He's also the first - and it turns out only - to have any skill with Computers, with only one dot.) He's only got one dot in each Discipline, which is a change. Backgrounds are Resources (at five), Influence, Retainers, and Generation (another first). Humanity is seven and Willpower is nine. Once again, the stats don't add up. But even if you clean them up - say, drop that single dot in Streetwise, the single dot of Generation, one dot worth of Retainer, and one dot of Willpower - you are left with a character that is very competent and potentially dangerous in the world of the Kindred. Which I think is awesome. And I really love the character art. He rocks that suit so well you almost don't notice the face. I kind of want to play this character, which I think makes it a big success.

                                                    Next up is our Toreador. The character art is again kind of androgynous in that early 90s grunge way, but seems to be female (as is the clan stereotype). She's got some nice tattoo work, some of it pretty detailed, which is impressive. Her concept is Performance Artist, which is a little vague. I hope it involves more than doing cat's cradle like she is in the image. Nature is Architect and Demeanor Gallant, which is kind of stereotypical Toreador. Social are primary and Mental secondary, having a four in Manipulation and threes in Charisma, Appearance, Perception and Intelligence. Acting, Subterfuge, Etiquette, and Music are all at three dots. She also has twos in Politics and Firearms, giving our rose thorns, so to speak. And, strangely, there are four dots filled in down where Secondary Abilities would be, but no ability listed. I guess this was supposed to be some form of Expression (poetic, maybe?), but was deleted as it required something outside the core book. Disciplines are back to the even spread with additional dot, this time in Auspex. Backgrounds are Resources and Retainers. I'm a little surprised she doesn't have at least one dot of Fame. Humanity is seven, with Willpower at a seemingly low five. (Again, the points don't add up, being over by two.) This is a pretty good character. It feels like a solid out of the box Toreador character one could pic up and play, and the art is very striking. I have to give this one a win.

                                                    And on to the Tremere. Here was have a little girl pulling a rabbit out of a hat. And she's a Stage Magician. I'm not sure why. I have to be honest, if asked to name the first clan to spring to mind at the mention of the concept of Stage Magician, it'd be the Toreador, hands down, and Ravnos at number two. However, I can see the idea of the world of professional magicians, with their cliquishness and secrecy from outsiders, as well as their dedication to learning and mastering a very difficult set of skills, mapping to that of the Tremere. I'm just not exactly sure it would've been my first choice to show off the clan in the core book. (Though, to be fair, the Nosferatu one is totally against stereotype and I love the hell out of that one.) I'm also not sure I'd have gone with a sleepy Wednesday Addams for the signature image either. Nature is Conniver and Demeanor is Child, which I guess is supposed to fit the art. Mental is primary and Social is secondary, which is both the Tremere archetype as smart people who are also kind of manipulative, and works for a stage magician. Then we get to abilities and things start to fall down a little. She has three dots each in Subterfuge, Investigation, and Occult. (A crime solving child vampire magician? It would've made a cool 80s TV show.) Alertness, Intimidation, Leadership, Etiquette, Bureaucracy, and Politics are at two each. There's also ones in Acting, Drive, Firearms, and Finance. Disciplines are again the even spread, with an additional dot in Thaumaturgy. Backgrounds are Herd, Mentor, and Retainer (and I can't help but picture the rabbit as the retainer). Humanity is eight, Willpower is seven. Once again, the stats don't add up, being over by at least four points. However, if you get rid of the extra dot in Self Control which bumped the Humanity up to eight, and the dot in Acting, it works. Which brings me to what I think happened here. These stats are for a pretty decent character, specifically for your sort of archetypical up and coming Tremere who is well skilled in the clan's strong suits of arcana and politics, and is being groomed for a position of leadership. Which is a really cool character and potentially a lot of fun to play. Then, I think the art came in, and for some strange reason, Josh Timbrook gave them a picture of Wednesday Addams pulling a rabbit out of a hat. (Which, by it self, isn't a bad idea for a Tremere. With some tweaks, I could see this character sheet easily turning into, say, "Strange Little Girl" or something.) It's a lot easier, faster, and cheaper to alter text than it is art. So I think someone changed the sheet's Demeanor to Child, the Concept to Stage Magician, and added that dot of Acting (and maybe the extra Self Control). So we're left with a sheet that doesn't really match the concept or art very well. Which is a shame, because like I said, the stats are for a pretty cool sounding occult investigator/up and coming politico type and the art looks like it might actually make a potentially interesting character in its own right.

                                                    And, finally, the Ventrue. Out image is of a fairly cool looking guy in a nice, stylish suit, smoking a cigarette and looking pretty smug. Really the image of a dynamic up and coming young turk, as opposed to the idea of fossilized old tyrants or pompous blue blood elites the clan often gets stereotyped as. I rather like it. Our concept is Ward Boss. (This means he effectively runs the political party for a single ward or political unit of a city, pretty much picking the candidates for that ward's representative in the city council, as well as other politically appointed offices, and exercises a fair amount of influence within the party in the city, and possibly even on a state level.) He's Social primary, with four dots each in Charisma and Manipulation, and Mental secondary, with three dots each in Intelligence and Wits. Politics are at four dots, and Leadership and Subterfuge at three. This is rounded out with twos in Acting, Empathy, Drive, Etiquette, Bureaucracy, and Investigation, as well as a few ones including Finance, Law and Streetwise. Disciplines are one each. Backgrounds include Resources, Influence, and Contacts at three each, and a few more points in Allies and Herd. Humanity and Willpower are both at seven. Again, he's four points over. I'd probably drop one dot from Acting, the Allies background, and either one level of Contacts or Herd (or alternately one dot of WP). Over all, this is a really strong character, and I like it quite a bit. He's very archetypically Ventrue, but appears to be very competent and potentially dangerous in the more political realms of Kindred existence. Very much a win here.

                                                    And so, that was, for second edition players new to the game, their first introduction to a filled in character sheet representing a somewhat typical member of that clan. And, overall, I think it did an OK job of it. The single biggest issue here is that most of the sheet's don't add up, points wise. Most of them you can fix without much trouble, but the sloppiness is a little off putting (said the person who has probably made at least a hundred typos in this thread alone). There's also the issue with the Tremere one clearly being out of sync, and the fact that the Malkavian one is fairly bad. And the very minor detail of the Gangrel having a dumb concept name. It might also be arguable that the Nosferatu isn't archetypical enough. (This may have been another instance where the artwork submitted totally mismatched the character sheet, only to have the Malkavian one end up there by mistake, and the one you see in the Malkavian clanbook a totally after the fact invention.) But I think the Brujah, Toreador, and Ventrue are extremely strong, as is the Nosferatu, and the Gangrel isn't bad. So overall, I have to call this a fairly solid and mostly successful effort, though one with a asterisk next to it in the record books.
                                                    As far as representation goes, the Brujah, Toreador, and Tremere are all female, and the other four are male. (Though, as I mentioned, the Gangrel has a certain level of androgyny to it.) They seem to all be white, save possibly the Ventrue.

                                                    I will note here that there are, IIRC, similar character sheets with each clan write up in the 2nd ed Players Guide. However, my copy of that book has some binding damage (and has had this damage since the late 90s) which makes me extremely reluctant to go looking through it to the extent it would take to review them. So I may not do those, unless I can find some other place to look them up.

However, I think they should have Presence instead of Auspex, but that's just my personal opinion. I also think there's a place in the Sabbat for both this version of them and the monster hunters presented in the revised edition, and they may even compliment each other. One is moving forward toward a future of freedom, while the other is weirdly obsessed with trying to destroy what they see as chains from the past.

                                                            Clanbook: Toreador (part one)

                                                            So now we're moving in to 1994. The cover of Toreador is ... rather less interesting than its processors. Brujah and Malkavian were both very striking (even if I think the latter's might've been a little misguided), Nosferatu's was a nice homage to a classic vampire film, and even Gangrel, while pretty restrained, looked like some sort of urban predator. This cover image is very unremarkable. It's just a woman, hands together holding a cigarette, and looking at you with a mildly ... smug? ... look. I don't think it does much to convey anything about what's inside.
                                                            This book was written by Steven C. Brown, who wrote the Players Guide to the Sabbat. Artwork is again by Josh Timbrook. As I've said before, I rather like the bit of continuity. I think it helps that you could easily take several of these images and photoshop them together into a single picture of your coterie or Elysium gathering and it'd look like it was done by one person.

                                                            The Burnout
                                                            This appears to be the sort of person the Nosferatu Morbid Artsy Type was aspiring to be in life, complete with enough ennui to stun a large animal. The image is that of your archetypical vaguely Goth-like Bohemian artist type, with ruffled shirt sleeves, long hair and cigarette holder, and is so androgynous that the character's gender is anyone's guess (or choice). They were a top up and coming artist among the Toreador, and apparently managing to maintain certain ties to their mortal family (as Toreador are apparently want to do in this edition). The those evil Setites came along, killed the character's children and husband, and subjected them to torture. After escaping and going on a revenge spree, they found they couldn't create anymore. So they decided to try an unlife of hedonism, but that soon became dull. Now they are back in their home city, trying to start over, and aspiring to acquire a level of personal power that will ensure no one is ever able to hurt them again. Petty things like art, morality, or humans no longer concern the character; only power matters. They would rather continue to wallow in self-pity than actually try to heal the interior damage they suffered. I find myself rather liking this character, much more so than I remember from back in the day. It's very tragic in a way that seems appropriate for Vampire and its themes of personal horror. And anyone playing them has the option of either riding that bullet train to hell or actually trying to make that long hard crawl up from the darkness. Nature is Survivor, but I could maybe see Autist as a better fit. Socials are primary, with a five in Manipulation, offset by a two in Charisma. Artistic Expression is at a four, while Intimidation and Subterfuge are at three each. Politics, Etiquette, and Style are at two dots, as are Brawl, Firearms and Security, reflecting the character's backstory. Disciplines focus on Presence at two dots and Celerity at one. Given that the character seems to have certain traits in common with PTSD or mild paranoia, I'm a little surprised they don't have Auspex's Heightened Senses. Backgrounds focus on Resources at three, as well as some Generation, Retainer, Status and Fame. As the write up claims the character has "many beautiful ghouls" (and that the pet dogs are treated better), I'd have expected more than one dot in Retainer. Or maybe a dot or two in Herd. Humanity is at a very low five, representing how damaged the character is. Willpower is seven. Overall, I like it and think it does a good job of leaning into the whole "tragically beautiful" aspect of vampire fiction that the Toreador are there to represent.

                                                            The Comic Book Celebrity
                                                            Remember what I said about the Brujah Fake Rapper? Well, this is the same thing. In this case, it's making fun of comic book artist Rob Liefeld. I have, over the years, come to appreciate Liefeld's enthusiasm for comics and for the superhero genre. But his art is and always has been terrible, with most of his characters clearly derivative of preexisting ones. (I honestly think he missed his true calling being an editor.) And back in the very early 90s, at the start of that period's speculator bubble, he - even more than some of the other founders of Image Comics - was big on self promotion. Most famously for the Levi's jeans commercial he was in. So, you can clearly see why, in a book that staunchly divided the Toreador between true Artistes and pompous Poseurs, they'd do a character like this. The write up background/bio is pretty much that of the character's inspiration with the serial numbers filed off, even down to the dig at his inability to meet deadlines now. He's generally uncomfortable around other Kindred and continuously dismisses any and all criticism of being due to jealousy. It's not a particularly deep character. But there is a slight glimmer of potential here in the offhand mention that his creativity has improved since the embrace and the new challenges are actually providing inspiration. This is a Social character, with the usual 4/3/3 split and Mental secondary (Intelligence is two, but Perception and Wits are three). Artistic Expression is only at two dots, along with the Masquerade ability to pass for the living. However, Finance is at four dots, which is an interesting twist but perhaps a little out of left field. He's also got two dots each in Computer, Investigation, Drive, Intimidation, and Subterfuge. He's also got some interesting one dot abilities, such as Security, Politics, Stealth and Leadership. Disciplines are two in Celerity and one in Presence. He's got a lot of Background points, including four dots in Resources, two in Fame, and three in Herd. Humanity is at eight and Willpower at seven. From a traits perspective, this is a very respectable character. If you shifted a dot or two into Bureaucracy, he's be a very competent manager of his comic company. As it is, I kind of have this amusing mental image of him being forced by circumstance to help investigate a murder or other mystery, like one of those old novels that would set a mystery at a SciFi or writers' convention. (Which would probably make an interesting one shot for a convention game.) So, much like the Fake Rapper, I find myself reexamining a character I dismissed as a joke 25 years ago and actually liking it and seeing a lot of potential for it as a character. All in all, not bad.
