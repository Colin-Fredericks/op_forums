    That somewhat depends what you want out of a personal horror game.

    In terms of the general concepts of personal horror, V5 produces genre appropriate results. "I am horrified by the things I was forced to do under mind control," is a staple of personal horror stories, because the key aspect of personal horror is the negative emotional reaction of a character towards themselves; they don't have to be directly responsible.

    The problems that you're talking about are in the process of gamifying personal horror. In a game, agency is important to having a fulfilling experience; even in situations where the character might not have much agency, the player expects to be able to exercise decision making powers (otherwise they're just listening to the ST narrate what happens).

    So, it's a bit less the in-universe issues of the characters being able to say, "the Beast made me do it!" and having a point, or that Elders aren't there to be scary, but that the players have no real say in how this occurs. The inability to meaningfully engage withe the horror elements as a player makes it very hard to create a personal horror game, even if the system produces valid personal horror stories.

                                I find V5's rules harmful for streetlevel play.

                                Combat is all or nothing, so it's either really easy or you've lost. At a low level of power, that's not how combat should be. Combat should either be -I have a cunning plan- or -he took a few hits but he won out in the end-. If you're playing entry level vampires, your advantages are that nobody knows your coming, and freakish endurance. In V5, it doesn't matter how durable you are, you're going to lose if the guy has a bigger single combat pool than you, and win if your pool is best, and the impact and capability to pull off shenigans is greatly deminished. Your ability to prepare for conflict is peanuts because weapons are trivialized and armour doesn't exist for vampires, and also the retainer background was gutted so good luck playing a Ventrue commander.

                                Meanwhile the social game doesn't matter so much because with your coterie, if you assume everyone's playing the same rules as you, can totally wipe the city if elysium really displeases them so much. As stated before, willpower damage relies on public shame, which is a good way to get you killed and weirdly off for a game about vampires since the politics of parasites is going to be mostly backroom deals and friendship with benefits.

                                And, well, since I don't want to diablerize anybody, and the players, if they're half-smart, are going to start with a good domain beetween them, there's not really much reason to be an instigator. If I wanna get rich, the method to do so in V5 is to pay XP. If I want less garbage retainers, I spend more XP. Players become passive, waiting for something to happen to them so they can get XP, perhaps that's what touchstones are for, while in V20 the scramble for backgrounds is very real. The rules of V5 encourage the players and characters to move because of Touchstones or because messy criticals and the messy attempts to cover them are spiralling out of control, whilst in V20 the -oh fuck he frenzied/screwed the masquerade- is certainly prevailent in newbie games, but a good chunk of things are the players trying to get backgrounds and the consequences of their consciously chosen actions. (Of course, the storyteller can throw plot at them in both games, but if your players are instigators, it's generally means a lot less work and a lot more fun for the ST, at least from my experience)

                                        No, my preference would be, rather than making Frenzies and such akin to blackouts where the GM takes over, instead do stacking penalties.

                                        Say rank the Fight/Feed/Flight stressors from 1-5 and when they come up you roll your Self-Control (Fight/Feed) or Courage (Flight) to subtract from that. If you get it down to zero you remain in complete control. For each point you fail to remove you take a +1 difficulty penalty to all your actions unless the action is one that aligns with the Beast. If you do take an action in accord with the Beast then the penalty is reduced by one.

                                        So say the stressor is you’re being publicly humiliated and we’ll say it’s a 4. You roll Self-Control and get 2 successes. You now have a +2 difficultly penalty to any action that’s not lashing out in some way at those humiliating you. If you do lash out the penalty goes to +1, lash out a second time and it drops to +0... or you don’t and keep trying to interact with a +2 difficultly penalty to every non-violent attempt you make.

                                        Then you make it stack. So after several rounds of verbal abuse you might be at +5 or 6 difficultly and any attempts at trying to do anything remotely controlled are just sputtering failures and the only way out is to either give in to the Beast and do something violent or, say, spending willpower to drive the Beast back down.

                                        Maybe the Beast even offers bonuses to Fight, Feed or Flight actions to further incentivize giving in (say you get a -2 difficultly bonus and your built-up Fight reflex drops by 2 or more instead of 1 if you physically strike the person who triggering you).

                                        I’m still messing around with the idea in my head, but the gist would be instead of just ceding control to the GM, you just get progressively more penalties for trying to hold the Beast in check and progressively more benefits from just giving in (and having to deal with the fallout).

                                        The fact that it’s ALWAYS your choice to give in in order to remove the penalties I think would better reinforce the concept of personal horror because it’s never “the Beast MADE me do it” it’s “I GAVE IN to the Beast because it was easier than trying to keep fighting it.”

                                                        I don't think anyone's actually happy with V20's combat or social mechanics. Just that a lot of us will take the flaws in those over the flaws in V5's attempts.

                                                        The CofD approach is also to have multiple subsystems that can address social interactions, not just one. The big one is the Social Maneuvering system, which is the most important for vampires because it's all about trying to get other people to do favors for you (either by developing a relationship with them over time and improving their opinion of you aka Soft Leverage, or trying to force action immediately through threats/extortionetc. aka Hard Leverage). But the Investigation system has rules for using it to get dirt on people rather than actually solving crimes or mysteries, which can then be used to supplement social actions (either simple ones or ones as part of other systems), the Crafting rules covers non-physical concepts like building a plan for a group to follow (taking over constructive social systems like leadership), and the Chase system - while never really flushed out in how to do it - can be used for non-physical conflicts and draws heavily from the CofD 1e Debate system (making it good for anything that's two or more parties competing over the same social prize).

                                                        While somewhat disjointed compared to the "one social system to do everything" approach, it has a big advantage in that each one can be simpler by having less ground to cover, and therefore create better results for the specific focus you want to put on something.

                                                        Though, as I plugged in the porting from V5 to V20 or vice versa thread, the CofD 2e rules have a lot of good idea for working stuff into V20 or V5. That applies to things like Intimacies too. If you don't want to completely port Intimacies over to VtM, the CofD 2e social mechanics use existing Traits like Anchors (this covers things like Nature/Demeanor in V20, or Convictions, Touchstones, and Aspirations in V5) to fill in the same basic role in social systems. Trying to get a mortal investigator who has a strong sense of justice (Virtue: Justice) and is specifically trying to take down the head of a crime syndicate (Long Term Aspiration: Dismantle [name of gang]), social maneuvers against them can be really easy, or really hard depending on if you sell what your asking as moving towards those goals, or accidentally try to push against them.

                                                        Intimacies are one of the highlights of Exalted 3e, but they're integrated on a pretty broad level; not just the core of the social mechanics that they are. You can get a lot in VtM by taking advantage over the traits that are already there.
