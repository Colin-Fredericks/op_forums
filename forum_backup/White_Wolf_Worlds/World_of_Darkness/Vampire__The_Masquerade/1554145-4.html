                                    Originally posted by Shadeprowler View Post
                                    I would say if one thing this one is pretty universal
                                    Because you want to focus on the similarities and not on what I actually said.

                                    Languages alone are more than 6 thousands around the world right now, and we never had so few of them in recorded history.

                                    And the differences between those legends are far more relevant than their similarities, which are usually on very superficial and obvious aspects.

                                    The theme of humanity becoming unworthy and the gods punishing them, sometimes with the exception of a virtuous individual/family who is spared, is itself pretty common and link to many, many, many sorts of catastrophes. Fires, wars, plagues, droughts, meteors, predators, swarms, monsters, everything and anything. Floods are just a subclass on a very broad class of legends, with some cultures having several of those.

                                    So no, this is far from universal, very far. First in the sense that while there are cultures with such myths in every inhabited continent, in none of those they are all cultures. Quite the opposite, most cultures have no such myths. Second in the sense that those legends are quite different from each other past very, very broad strokes that aren't really specific to flood myths to begin with.

                                    We have a legend of "universal flood myths" because we have a history of Christian apologists and Christian Archaeologists that purposefully built a corpus of arguments in favor of a "global flood event" that would have been recorded by all cultures. And it was briefly reinforced by the many similarities between the biblical flood and the Epic of Gilgamesh.

                                    Recently, though, we confirmed that the similarities between the biblical and the Mesopotamian floods are due to it being the same myth. The Hebrews learned it in Babylon and then elaborated a version/response where it happens but their god is more powerful. Originally a reader of the story of Noah would also know the Babylonian version and it would have some additional layers of meaning because of that.

                                    Other than that, no, the flood myths of other cultures have nothing besides the obvious to do with the biblical flood, they refer to other events, and even if perceived by their culture as global (another thing that's fairly common in mythology, as every culture is the center of the world in their myths), they would have happened in different times and with different caracteristics.
