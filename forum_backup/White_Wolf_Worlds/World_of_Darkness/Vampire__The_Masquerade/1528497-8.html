                I think the big mistake of WOD5E has been to focus on redefining and narrowing down what the World of Darkness is. Rather than obsessing over making everything street-level only, personal horror etc., they should've taken an expansive perspective. 20th Anniversary, Chronicles of Darkness, introducing new lore like the Beckoning or just being a beginner-friendly blank slate and making the game more of a toolbox, boardgames, cardgames... It should've been embraced that there are many facets and aspects to the WOD-IP at this point. Besides even if one were to be concerned about divided attention between all these various lines of games under the WOD IP, it's not like if you cancel CofD, for example, then no Urban Fantasy personal horror game can appear on the market to compete with WOD. So in that sense, I think it would've gone further to corner that type of game on the market by having both WOD5E and CoD out there rather than just the former.

                After all, canceling the 20th-line and CofD hasn't led to V5 shooting up the charts. No fan of those lines was immediately like "Oh well, I guess I have to play WOD5E now.". Of course, it doesn't work like that. And the success of the Storytellervault shows that as well. Instead, you have all this content shoved into that digital corner when you could've released official products which even now still have an audience who are interested, as the success of the STV indicates.

                The climate on the community-side also would've benefited a lot from this as well. Sure, there will be people who like one thing more than the other. But you wouldn't have to go to them and tell them stuff like "If you like the old werewolf-editions, this new edition isn't for you.". By clinging to this narrowed-down and redefined version of WOD5E the pitch and tone of the conversation also became more exclusionary.

                Also, this narrowed-down version was hampered by inconsistency. You have a book like Chicago By Night on one hand with a lot of Loresheets and references to the past while the V5 Players Guide dismissively calls Loresheets "vampire arcana" that most people at the table probably don't even care about. And even now with another change in leadership, you get yet another slightly different take on this narrowed-down vision for WOD5E.

                At this point, I think the best thing to do would be for Paradox to just let Renegade manage the whole thing on their own. Because if there's one thing Paradox has shown over the last couple of years (both in terms of WOD5E and them as a videogame-publisher), it's that they have developed a real knack for pushing projects in the wrong direction somehow.

                        06-30-2024, 05:11 PM
                        Originally posted by Lysander View Post
                        I do hope that they add more lore sheets in future products as a nod to the old school fans.
                        They are adding more again but it's still nowhere near what we got with Onyx Path supplements. At least it's better than the snark and disdain we got during the H5 and V5 Players Guide era. The H5 core had a whole sidebar explaining why the book didn't have Loresheets. And now it feels like Renegade is acting there's some sort of unwritten law that H5 can't have Loresheets. I think, similar to how the H5 core was needlessly restrictive with hunter-orgs, there's no need for the unimaginative approach of just not figuring out what Loresheets are in the context of H5.

                        But that was par for the course for that era of WOD5E: It took elements from V5's game-design and erased them. That was the thrust of game-design in the last couple of years. That's another part where I think WOD5E just failed. It took V5 and instead of coming up with inventive new ways to adopt the system for mortal hunters and werewolves, the approach was more akin to thoughtless, wild swings with a sledgehammer. H5 is worse than W5 in that regard for sure but I never felt like the game-design direction for those two corebooks made a lot of sense in that regard. Instead of learning from how CofD worked in 2E, we're right back at the beginning where each splat is an island and it ranges from hard to nigh impossible to have crossovers between the systems described in these corebooks. And sure, I haven't met a lot of people who even want to do crossover with a WOD-game. But rather my thinking is that if you would want to make WOD more beginner-friendly, wouldn't it make sense to have a situation where it's super-easy for somebody who has played VtM in the past to easily shift to playing WtA? But thus far... take the dice... Hunger-dice, Desperation-dice, Rage-dice... It's such an obvious throughline, it's easy to imagine that an M5 might have something like Paradox-dice. And yet... They all work differently. Each splat so far handles these dice very differently. And that's a bit baffling because I think Messy Criticals and Bestial Failures could absolutely have been a template for WOD5E. Instead both H5 and W5 live in their own worlds. And sure, you can do that... but the whole point was to sacrifice the lore and complexity of past editions to make things more beginner-friendly. But as a franchise, WOD5E has only been pushing against that so far by making things more confusing and more complicated in how each splat works. You can't just take somebody who played V5 and have them play H5 or W5. Overreach/Despair and Brutal Outcomes are very distinct from Messy Criticals and Bestial Failures. It's the opposite of beginner-friendly.

                        Hell, if you have the dice with symbols on them from Renegade... it sucks to be you. Some smartass during the development of W5 decided that Rage-dice needed to be different from Desperation-dice and Hunger-dice. Or didn't care or realize that there was a difference. Bad stuff triggers on a 1 for Hunger- and Desperation-dice but for Rage-dice it's 1 and 2, so the Rage-dice with symbols are different. Talk about beginner-friendly... You might think your Hunger-dice you bought for V5 would also work for W5 (after all they also worked for H5) - but then you find out that this isn't the case.
