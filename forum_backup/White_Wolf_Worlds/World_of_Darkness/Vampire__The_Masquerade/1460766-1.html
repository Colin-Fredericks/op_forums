                Introduction

                Type: Introduction (Duh)

                Synopsis: A short description of how Kindred society has changed in regards to religion and how populous they are.

                Analysis: At the risk of repeating myself, it is a very different time to be a believer in Caine in 2020 than it is to be a believer in, say, 1999, or even 1992. Originally, the handling of religion in the Vampire: The Masquerade setting was that the Camarilla believed Caine was the first vampire and every "proper" vampire was supposed to be able to recite their lineage back to Caine. Presumably, the Tremere got a pass on this because it would be awkward to say, "Tremere>Random Tzimisce we experimented on>Saulot>Enoch>Caine."

                I think it was Revised that introduced the idea that not only was the Camarilla aggressively secular but that it actually was attempting to stamp out the idea of the Book of Nod as well as Cainite mythology. If I recall correctly, it began with Jan Pieterszoon who was apparently using his influence to support the idea of "Kindred evolution." Also, was almost unique in the fact he was an Elder who considered Noddism to be nonsense.

                Some players and STs, perhaps always uncomfortable with the Biblical overtones of the game, also embraced the idea that the Caine "myth" was just one story among many. Before, only the Followers of Set and Ravnos provided any alternatives to this idea. Either way, both were pretty much treated as being dead wrong. Which is fine, I don't think you have to cater to universalism in your fictional game about undead predators of the night.

                Still, I think they went overboard with the Camarilla's handling of it as we had the woman who printed up the Book of Nod, Ayisha Jocastian, put on the Red List in V20. That would have made my numerous die-hard Noddist PCs very nervous. Still, I get the rationale and makes an interesting contrast to the Black Hand who were always intense Noddist fanatics using that to justify their faith. It also made the Inner Council more EVIL to know explicitly the Antediluvians were real then claim they weren't to make themselves more powerful.

                This book changes not just the Camarilla and Anarchs but also the mood of the game considerably as Blood Cults are no longer purely the province of the Sabbat and Ministry/Followers of Set. While not giving any rules for Paths, they do give differing ethics that takes advantage of the V5 Humanity system and also presents a vast list of cults as well as a revised Hecata. This is cited as a reason the Sabbat are now, well, "EXTRA" if you want a word for it. A lot more extra than they were when they were previously the most extra group in the game. With creepy occult mysticism, Noddism, and fanatical dark religions then the Camarilla and Anarchs now have some of what the Sabbat had exclusively. Well, Sabbat and True Black Hand.

                The book states that only a handful of ANY cult will probably be in a city unless they rule said city like the Lancea Sanctum in Requiem, which is a good bit of data, but these groups all offer interesting new possibilities.

                And NOW WE SHALL DISCUSS THEM.

                    The Whys and Way of Worship

                    Type: Fiction

                    Synopsis: A letter by Aidtya Hay, Ventrue, Former Seneschal of Edinburgh

                    Analysis: I screwed up my order a bit and posted the Justicars' transcript first. It happens. In this case, we have a chapter that is full of nothing but fictional testimonials from various Blood Cults. No point in not handling them each individually. It will be a bunch of short entries that give my impression from the fiction. Here, we have Aditya Hay talk about how he converted to the worship of Mithras and handed the city of Edinburgh over to Roger de Camden. Basically, he comes off as a lunatic discussing all manner of Sabbat-esque rites of mutilation that he is deriving either enlightenment from or simply excitement due to the fact Elders tend to derive no emotion from life save when they torment or satiate their Beasts.

                    He does, however, describe something that shows the fundamental appeal of Blood Cults: they provide meaning. If you are a vampire and successfully manage to master nightly survival and even get your own little empire as Elders do then you will find unlife an unhappy dull affair. The only thing you will live for is blood, vengeance, more blood, and petty status among your fellow leaches that you hate. Younger Kindred can feel the same draw. Say what you will about mad religions worshiping 4th generation demigods but at least you feel useful.

                    This guy casts out his own childe for not converting at the end, which I also thought was a nice touch. Funny how converts often find themselves a lot more judgemental of the people they used to be like.

                    I also give props for the fact that the Seneschal and Ventrue is non-white.

                                            Time of Thin Blood

                                            Type: Fiction

                                            Synopsis: Some Thin Bloods join a cult to try to make themselves true vampires.

                                            Analysis: One of the things I liked about Revised was the introduction of the Thin Bloods. The Thin Bloods were what I felt was a "course correction" from Justin Achilli where he noticed that the Caitiff weren't really filling the role that they were meant to in the setting. Yes, they were hated and feared by the Camarilla but not really enough that it felt like a real danger. They also had flexible enough powers that they didn't feel VULNERABLE.

                                            The Thin Bloods were designed to be manifestly weaker than regular Kindred but more human than the average Cainite. If you wanted to play a "good" vampire then the ones who could feed on animals indefinitely and weren't dead were probably the best. I think V5 went overboard with them and should have kept more of the V20 versions rules but I think they did a decent job highlighting the differences with making them "living" undead.

                                            CT Phipps' House Rules for V5 Thin Bloods: I should note my house rules for V5 Thin Bloods are that I give them the Blush of Life for Free, reduced damage from sunlight, and the ability to drink blood that other Kindred would find rancid (dead blood, animal blood, and so on) but I allow them to be able to get 1 Point of three Disciplines chosen beforehand. They also don't frenzy save for extreme hunger.

                                            They can use a single rouse check per scene to increase their physical attributes too. Also make one healing per night of damage. Thin Blooded Alchemy is a thing they can get up to 5 points and gets them the option to throw around a lot of other Kindreds disciplines but this is still a rare skill in my setting. They also have to pay for each "recipe" too.

                                            I feel these Thin Bloods are useful enough to be weaker than "real" Kindred but still have a little bite. No pun intended.
                                            What I liked about Revised was that it did make the Camarilla seem more intolerant and "evil" with their Scourges and desire to commit genocide against the Thin Bloods. It also helped inform the Gehenna plotline as the Thin Bloods being slaughtered encouraged them to band together to diablerize or destroy regular kindred. My favorite Malkavian PC was a Gehenna cultist and Noddist scholar who was lovers with Jenna Cross and attempting to help the Thin Bloods in part because they were a sign of Gehenna.

                                            Here the Thin Bloods are not automatically hostile to "real" vampires but struggling for guidance. They want answers the same as much as everyone else and here we see how a cult can take advantage of that like homeless teens and RL cults. Here, they're recruited into the Children of Si that is one of the minor cults that seems related to the OPP concept of the Drowned.

                                                    Brother Sweetwater Speaks

                                                    Type: Fiction

                                                    Synopsis: Brother Sweetwater...speaks.

                                                    Analysis: Brother Sweetwater basically gives a short speech about how Gehenna is upon the world with the Beckoning, "Swords having been broken", and "We have failed." They must "make friendship with their oppressors" and how "only through worship may they be appeased." It doesn't take a rocket scientist to figure out that Brother Sweetwater is a former member of the Sabbat addressing a similar congregation of them. The sword being referred to is the Sword of Caine and their ideology is to change from attempting to resist the Camarilla and Anarchs to attempting to appease the Methuselah.

                                                    According to OPP's Gentleman Gamer, he said that the Church of Caine's original write-up was basically Sabbat-lite and they were told, "You can't have any Sabbat in this book. Please rewrite it as the Cainite Heresy." Which I think was a good call and gave us a non-Sabbat bunch of Caine worshipers. However, this feels like fiction leftover from the idea the Church of Caine would be made up of Sabbat or ex-Sabbat.

                                                    I think the idea of the Sabbat having a bunch of defectors is something that is both extremely hard to imagine given their level of indoctrination and also something extremely interesting as a character concept. That's not even bringing up the difficulties of the Vaulderie. I've certainly played multiple "ex-Sabbat" and have always found them to be great philosophical characters. However, I'm going to make a strange statement that Sabbat defection to the Camarilla and Anarch is actually one of the most consistent themes of the setting.

                                                    The mass defection of Sabbat to their enemies and the resulting changes is ALL OVER V5.

                                                    In addition to the controverisal Lasombra defection, both the Anarchs and Camarilla have both become a lot more Sabbat like. They even mention this in the defense of the SABBAT: THE BLACK HAND tweets. The Camarilla now having its weird occult and ancestor worship angle while the Anarchs are now much more vicious as well as religious themselves. The Sabbat book also confirms that a lot of Anarchs and Camarilla defections DID occut that have influenced both. The Sabbat becoming more radicalized as its moderates all leave to join their enemies and make THEM darker.

                                                    To quote Jason Blood, "Over the centuries, I became more like Etrigan."
                                                    "And Etrigan?"
                                                    "Etrigan, too, became more like Etrigan."

                                                    I've always disliked the monopoly the Sabbat had on Caine worship (have I mentioned that enough yet) so having the option of being Noddists who aren't on Paths or have drunk the Kool-Aid works well for me.
