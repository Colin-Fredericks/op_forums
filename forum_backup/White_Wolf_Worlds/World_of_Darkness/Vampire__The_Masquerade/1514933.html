    Does anyone else find VtM to be the most depressing gameline?

    06-14-2023, 06:52 AM
    Wraith and Changeling have that reputation, but changelings get to inject some wonder into the world and wraiths aren't told "fuck you" by the narrative itself every five seconds (despite arguably being just as fucked up). Wraith is about facing your troubles, finding catharsis and moving on. Vampire, as it's often portrayed, is about being damned and trying and failing to fight it.
    Over there, Mages and Werewolves fight for a better world. All you can do is poison it.

    Ultimately, what I get from VtM when it's portrayed in that manner is that you deserve nothing but suffering or death no matter what you do and your choices are meaningless (because you're a monster regardless), happiness is forbidden, even trying to be a positive influence on the world is a total waste of time, achieving Golconda is basically impossible (assuming it even exists), something as horrifying as suicide is considered a good thing, you're a bad person for getting dragged into a cycle of abuse as a victim in the first place, justice is a fiction, and the World of Darkness was created by and ruled over by a neglectful, sociopathic tyrant who actively wants you to be cursed because some asshole murdered his brother, and then thousands of years later your sire decided to murder somebody and somehow their crimes are also inherently your fault. The fact that you're innocent of that is irrelevant; screw you, enjoy your divine punishment. Should you find a way to undo it, say by getting a powerful mage to help, then apparently the resulting paradox backlash is god starting Gehenna early as a "fuck you".

    Oh, yeah, and the world is heading for a horrific apocalypse, so everything you're working for is meaningless and unlike, say, the Garou who are flawed heroes, even if you fight it you are an abomination that doesn't deserve to exist and can only taint everything you touch.

    Caring is a waste of time and the world ceasing to exist is probably going to be an improvement, frankly. Putting effort into being a saint is a waste of energy. All that effort you put into being a monster is meaningless, because you could never be anything else anyway. I mean, I tone it down at my table, but geez. The Setites may as well have it right; fuck Ra, fuck the rules, all glory to Sutekh.

    Prone to hyperbole

                      I don't know if I can pick a definitive choice about whose condition is worse between Wraiths and Vampires, from an IC perspective. While the suffering of a Wraith *can* be fleeting, it's worth remembering that plenty of them are centuries old, if not millennia. And the suffering of a Vampire can end any time the Vampire wants if annihilation is considered an acceptable outcome.

                      I think that Vampire tends to lean harder on the idea that their condition is a curse because that's pretty much the only thing keeping it from being *just* Comic Book Heroes and Villains With Fangs. To be clear, I'm not trying to bash that playstyle. But the writers were definitely leaning more towards Anne Rice than Near Dark with their original concept for the game, so making being a Vampire suck unless you *really* want to lean into it makes sense from that perspective.

                      Like, from the outside you can look at a Vampire and talk about their "pitiable" condition, and that wouldn't be wrong in many respects. But from the perspective of a Vampire that's decided to go all-in, it's probably a God Damn Blast. Whether they rapidly descend into Wightdom or manage to adopt a Path of Enlightenment, a Vampire that abandons Humanity and embraces their new condition may not feel very cursed at all.

                          Kharnov is right: vampires are set up to have a blast. It's a high cost, high reward kind of lifestyle.

                          Pros:
                          feeding is the most euphoric thing ever. It's not subjective, it's just the best (bar any spell specifically designed to cause pleasure, but those are pretty dangerous)
                          You'll improve rather than atrophy as you age. You might not be great now, but you sure will be amazing years from now.
                          If you play your cards right, immortality. you might even live long enough to see the release of half-life 3.
                          actually knowing what's going on in the world
                          You'll be able to create a cadre of people who love and worship you without much difficulty or even emotional investment.
                          You will never need a normal job to make ends meet.
                          The "I control animals" power
                          The "do as I say" power
                          The "I'm not here" power
                          The "I can jump six meters and swat you away like a beach ball" power.
                          The "literally magic" powers.
                          The "Bullet resistant" power

                          Cons:
                          You can only be up at night, that's most of every day gone! Being up when nobody else is can be quite a drag.
                          You need to lie to people on a daily basis and keep a secret.
                          Politics is shit, but it was when you were mortal, you're just closer to it now.
                          If you don't get your fix or surround yourself with problems, you might lose control and drain folks or worse, cause a scene.
                          If you're way too evil, you get uglier and lose control more.

                          All this damnation talk is just Toreador posturing/writers trying to keep player characters relatable and preventing players from building crazy (reverse) harems and living like hedonistic gods.
