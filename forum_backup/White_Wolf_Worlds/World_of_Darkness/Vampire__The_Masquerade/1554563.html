    Explaining Thin Blooded prejudice to newcomers

    10-22-2025, 05:29 PM
    I'm trying to have several older Kindred explain it and varying reasons. Also, how much of it should exist in your games.

    Here's my first answer:

    Racism.

    There's reasons for the racism like the fact that they're too human-like to really empathize with, endanger the Masquerade, and are too easily killed to really be useful. Some vampires even have religious reasons like they're portents of Gehenna.

    But at heart, most vampires despise Thin Bloods because they're weak and easy to bully around. Which ironically means most Thin Bloods have no problem selling out older vampires to the Second Inquisition or using their small powers to fuck up older Kindred.

    They also are near-universally Anarchs, which doesn't give the Anarchs much of a boost until you remember that they can still use guns and molotovs.

      I don't think this is the best answer.

      Vampires don't really empathize with other vampires, at least no more than they empathize with mortals. And as far as power and usefulness goes, they're better than Ghouls, but still less liked. If power and being easily bullied were the main points, clan vampires would actually like Thin-Bloods more than they like other vampires.

      The game always relied heavily on fear for this prejudice. Vampires fear Thin-Bloods, for a number of reasons:

      1- Strange powers. Thin-Bloods (and Caitiff) develop powers other vampires can't. For creatures that thrive in control and knowledge, this level of unpredictability is a horrible thing. You don't know what they're capable of, and that's worse than knowing they're capable of more potent tricks than you. You can prepare against potent tricks you know how to work and what they do;

      2- You don't know shit about them. Even if the origins of vampirism aren't really know despite legends, overall it's a predictable phenomenon. You know how things work and how they relate. And then comes a bunch of people that defy everything you though you know. Their nature, their powers, why they're so different, nothing about them really fits with how vampires work in general. And you never know when you'll find a new shocking difference between them and other vampires (like the development of TB Alchemy);

      3- Most have a degree of resistance to sunlight and holy things, the greatest limitations of vampires. Nothing strikes fear in a Kindred's heart more than the risk of being attacked during daysleep, when they're the most vulnerable, and Thin-Bloods are foes with powers and knowledge about the vampire condition other hunters don't have. Worse, most vampires get even more useless during the day as time passes, so this completely flips expectations as powerful elders will be the most diminished vampires if taken by day;

      4- Legend plays a huge role here. That has always been the greatest point in the lore: Camarilla elders fear the Thin-Blood because their dismissal of Gehenna is a facade. Thin-Bloods are actually welcomed in some domains, and the less a vampire believes in the Book of Nod, the more likely they are to welcome Thin-Bloods, to the point where in many domains persecuting them became a bad, unpopular policy. With 5th edition making the Camarilla turn 180º on the Noddist stance, now it's even more likely that in Cam domains the Thin-Bloods will be formally hunted.

      All that said, their ability to provide new services through TB Alchemy does means a lot of vampires may now reevaluate their stance on TBs as they're more directly useful. But ongoing Gehenna, as anti-climatic as it got to be, adds complexity to the problem. For some this is proof that the fear was warranted and they must be destroyed, or things will get worse. For others this is proof that they had nothing to do with that, or at least that they're not a problem anymore, we're already screwed, so let them be. Not to say that not everyone even believes Gehenna is ongoing, or will ever happen.

      Prejudice is a complicated thing. A lot of it doesn't have to do with perceived inferiority, but with perceived threat. Great Replacement Theory, innate savagery, bad omens, Conspiracy Theories. Jews, Blacks, Latinos, we're not victimized because people see us as inferior, despite stating this is a part of how we're victimized. We're victimized because people see us as a threat for completely ludicrous reasons. Of course there are far more complexity to RL prejudice (specially structural prejudice), but that's the gist of it.

          Adding to monteparnas's points

          -the masquerade. Yes I know, it's the main excuse of the Camarillo and others to exterminate the thin blood, but it's not utterly baseless.

          A large art of what allows the masquerade is the fact that due to their curses and their nature, vampires are constantly reminded of how they're no longer the same as humans.

          Thin blood have a far weaker version of that perceptual barrier. They can live easier in the daytime, some have relatively normal metabolism, some can even have children. For such thin blood. The separation from humans is far more of a belief than an absolute fact, hence they will be more tempted to be more represent with their friends and family, and worse, they might consider a world where they can reveal themselves to humans.

          -diablery. Devouring another kindred is something every single vampire might be tempted to do, but no one has more incentives to do so than thin blood. Even a 12 generation vampire might be content of his powers, and th k the risk of amarakth too much of a rice for he benefits, but ea thin blood is weak. Even in a society where they were not treated like vermin or second class citizen, their lack of strength would make them lacking compared to others. Worse, many will never be able tovemprzce, so the only way to bring their favored mortal into the night might be to first drain your ventrue neighbor.

          Furthermore on the last point, thin blood are a constant reminder that diablery is a necessity for the vampire race to exist. After all, your potential to create a perennial line is limited by your generation, there will I inevitably be a time where your descendant will be thin blooded and incapable of embracing. And as lower generations vampire can be killed, the only way for the species to endure is when the youngest east the oldest, ensuring their own chance to perpetuate kindred into the coming nights.

          So seeing thin bloods in your city is an eternal reminder that despite the apparence of eternity, your own species is degrading generation and generation, constantly perpetuating the tendancy tovdiaboery, and therefore the fear of the young. In many way, seeing one of them is telling you that the war of ages will never, ever stop

              An exemple of that in game happened in one of my campaigns.

              We were in a French city under a pseudo Camarilla regime (I was using the omega conspiracy plot line, with all PCs being of bloodlines rather than clans), the city had a population of thin blood which whose presence were tolerated and which were protected from direct discrimination (although of course, this being Vtm, this was not with good intentions).

              The players quickly interacted with them and became friends with Emilie and Patrocle, two thin blood acting as de facto leader/protective older siblings of the thin bloods. The first issues appeared quickly, the thin blooded were afforded the freedom to deal with their own stuff as long as they didn't cause trouble.
              ​​​​But vampire being vampires, they were some skirmishes with criminals, suspicions from detectives and vigilantes, and I'll timed feeding that resulted in corpses. But the thin blood had a far lower threshold of abilities than full blooded kindred, so they couldn't just memory wipe witnesses, they couldn't as easily physically outmatch the local brutes trying to impose their turf limits, and they couldn't just blood bond their local tenants to avoid weird questions on their nocturnal lives. Hence the interventions of the PCs to set things right.

              Howether, after a time, weird occurrences happened, thin bloods disappeared alongside other kindred, the former quickly accused the full bloods of eliminating them, then the blame was put on the mysterious threat looming over the city which is the current main plot of the campaign.

              In reality, as the PCs ended up discovering, the thin bloods were kidnapping kindred, then diablerised them. The newly ascended kindred then hid to avoid being known as diablerists, and the group was simply wondering whenether it would be best to leave the city in order to find new preys (which they cynically referred as "unwitting level ups") or whenether to wait until they're suffiently numerous and strong to take over the city at large.

                                      I think that in part it's tied to the reason why having children out of wedlock was such a taboo through out most of history and why "bastard" was a pretty dire insult and bastards were in fact mistreated.

                                      It has to do with important values that maintain social cohesion and the taboo against actions that undermine those values. Traditionally, in human cultures, marriage and monogamy was a large part of the glue that bound society together, not just the family unit but inheritance, gender roles, child care, and so one. People who had children who weren't married undermined that social order and so a huge amount of stigma was associated with that - for the children and their parents, especially mothers.

                                      For vampires and thin blooded vampires, including caitiffs, there is something similar. We get both thin blooded vampires and/or caitiffs when vampires are embracing too quickly and too frivolously. Vampires shouldn't be abandoning their progeny, they should be getting permission from the prince/elder to embrace, high generation vampires shouldn't get such permission. The more frivolous embraces that happen, the more likely we are to have more thin blooded vampires and caitiffs. Also, lots of embraces like this are likely to shift power in the direction of the young, which also threatens the stability of the elder based hierarchy.

                                      There are, of course, religious objections to thin blooded vampires - but the secular Camarilla has thin blooded vampires as do many largely secular elders.
