                    Well, if we are talking about "techno-thaumaturgy" then things are entirely different.

                    I thought we were only speaking about "mundane" Technology. If we give it a magical spin, things are totally different.

                    A robot animated by vitae ain't much different from a Golem.

                    If we add magic, then the "techno" part becomes more aesthetic than anything.

                    If we are talking about REAL "mundane" technologies (if we can call those magic mirrors we all carry in our pockets and that has the entire collection of human knowledge as "mundane"), then things are a lot less fantastic, but we can still take a few "cinematographic" liberties.

                    Drones, computers and smartphones are impressive things by themselves, double so when they are the top of the line military grade. If you "just" take what already is the best that is and make it just slightly better, then we are talking about some real powerful toys over here.

                    Take ChatGPT for instance, as impressive as it is; currently this is version 4.0.

                    Now, if you tell me that the "techno-leeches" are always at least 1 or even 2 versions ahead of the curve, a group of blood suckers playing with ChatGPT 5 or maybe 6+, is VERY impressive. That alone could be quasi Technocracy levels of capability.

                    Now for drones, have you guys watched the movie Sonic? Those Dr Robotinik's drones, that's not something too out of the realm of possibilities. Or the drones that Tony Stark gave to Peter Parker and that got used by Mysterio in the movie of Spider Man. Drones with military grade machine guns, perhaps with explosive ammunition, grenade launchers or flamethrowers, kamikase drones with napalm and so on.

                    For weapons and vehicles, here too there's room for improvement. Explosive ammunitions, incendiary ammunitions (white phosphorus maybe?). Vibroblade swords with a monowire cut (vibroblade swords have a battery that is used for the blade to slightly vibrate, causing the impacts to be much more devastating, while a monowire cut is obtained by sharpening the blade with a laser, making it to be so thin to the point of being sharpened at an atomic level, causing cuts almost at a molecular level).

                    With material sciences, your ultra tech blade could be made of high dense materials - say, depleted Uranium for instance - and highly resistant (imagine combining depleted Uranium and Kevlar or some such). This would be ideal for those Potence users, but even regular humans could pack a much harder punch with an extra heavy blade

                    On top of it, it could have a highly sophisticated system (controlled by AI maybe?) with small thrusters that increase the movement momentum, adding to the impact.

                    Combine all of the above for some "super tech sword" without a single drop of ocus pocus magic.

                    I recommend the book "Gurps Ultra Tech" for ideas on the "mundane" technologies of the tomorrow. This book is simply put the BEST possible for any one trying to introduce that little bit of cyberpunk into their games. It discusses everything you could possibly imagine - from medicine to energy (sources and storage), machines, vehicles, computers, computer programs and more.

                    Just as a quick explanation: GURPS divide the technologies in "Technological Levels". So, "TL" 0 is the Stone Age, TL 1 Bronze Age, TL 2 Iron Age, TL 3 Middle Ages, TL 4 is the Age of Sails. Those are described on "Gurps Low Tech", another incredible book, and if you guys are playing any DA game, I 101% recommend; seriously, there's stuff there that I could never even have imagined

                    Things start to get interesting at TL 5 - Steam Age. Early Industrial Revolution.
                    TL 6 is the age of oil
                    TL 7 is the atomic age
                    And finally, TL 8​ is the Age of Information, the Age we are in.

                    TL 5 to TL 8 are covered on GURPS High Tech, so if you want to play some Victorian Era, WW2 or Modern day, this is the book. Again, incredible source, and it enriched any game. Also, if you seek to just tweak a bit with what we already have, that's your toolbox

                    But, if you want the truly crazy stuff, you'll want GURPS Ultra Tech. This one sounds like a "Guide to the Technocracy" but without all of the more "exoteric" parts.

                    Here, Gurps creates 4 additional theoretical TLs, from 9 to TL 12.
                    TL 9 - Microtech Age
                    TL 10 - Robotic Age
                    TL 11 - Age of Exotic Matter
                    TL 12 - Age of Miracles

                    So, anything at TL 9 is acceptable. In fact, since the publishing of the book, many of the, at the time, theoretical technologies disposed at the TL 9 are either very close to being created NOW or are being prototyped already. Really, it is impressive.

                    Things at TL 10 sometimes are alright, sometimes are outlandish, so do consider them carefully.

                    Things at TL 11 are completely bonks, unless you truly want some silly "Techno-Vamp Star Trek" I wouldn't use those.

                    And TL 12 is basically the "Precursor Race" who is so Technologically advanced that can shape reality with their thoughts (or so it seems). TL 12 is basically the stage upon which technology becomes indistinguishable from magic, so don't bother with this

                    The book also makes a distinction between what is not just possible technologies but also PROBABLE ones, and some others that are deemed as "Super Science". So, just so you know, "Super Science" is any outlandish thing that contradicts what we CURRENTLY know about the Sciences. Therefore, any sort of communication and/or transportation that is FTL (faster than light) is automatically deemed as "Super Science". So, it is up to the GM to allow any of those or not, since those may well be in the field of fantasy.

                    On the other hand thou, the theory of Relativity would be considered as "Super Science" (aka Fantasy) for a 19th century scientist, so you may allow those based on that premisse that, perhaps, we just don't think those things are possible because we lack the knowledge of how they are possible to begin with.

                    Take a look into those 3 books, it is amazing.

                    And, if you guys love your little pet Franksteins, get also GURPS Bio Tech. Complete with everything from Artificial Selection (starting at TL 0 from the Stone Age) all the way up to "Bio-Buildings" and "Bio-Starships" at the TL 12 Age of Miracles, with everything in-between (cloning, DNA manipulation and many more).

                    By the way, the many advantages and Disadvantages in those books, particularly on Bio Tech, are super easy to adapt for WoD (heck, I would say that the ones on WoD were adapted from Gurps to begin with). Many of those in Bio Tech could be used even for vampires.

                        I'll give you guys 2 examples from Gurps Bio Tech (4ed):

                        "INSECT BIOWEAPONS (TL9)
                        Insect bioweapons are usually stored as vials of eggs and
                        released into still water; in a few hours, days or weeks, they
                        will hatch into tens of thousands of insects.
                        An area might be seeded with biting insects, such as
                        horseflies or blackflies. Multiple bites cause irritation (-1 on
                        DX and IQ until treated) and prevent anyone from getting
                        any sleep (resulting in fatigue). A sealed suit or the Sealed
                        advantage protects against this.
                        Other insect bioweapons might be given more subtle
                        targets. While sending genemod moths with a programmed
                        desire to eat the enemy’s uniforms is probably more an
                        exercise in whimsy than anything else, a plague of unsea-
                        sonal pests that eat crops or get into stored grain is no
                        laughing matter, especially if they’ve been altered to resist
                        the usual pesticides.
                        For greater lethality, insect pests could be infected to
                        carry genemod plagues dangerous to humans or other ani-
                        mals. Often, bloodsucking, winged insects are chosen, such
                        as mosquitoes (which can already carry several diseases).
                        In game terms, anyone traveling through an area infested​
                        with plague-carrying insects is treated as being in a disease-
                        ridden area (see Contagion, p. B443); roll at -2 if they lack
                        appropriate repellent, insect-resistant clothing or force
                        fields.
                        Genetic modification can be used to make an insect
                        bioweapon much more useful. The insects could be
                        designed so that they will not attack if they detect certain
                        chemical cues – which are often odors below the human
                        olfactory threshold. That way, you can protect your own
                        people with a specialized form of repellent.
                        For even greater precision, insect
                        weapons could be programmed to home in
                        on a specific chemical cue and only attack
                        targets that give it off. This is easy if the
                        enemy are another species, but if (for
                        instance) the enemy uses a particular food,
                        lubricant, or laundry detergent and none of
                        the local civilians or your own people do,
                        then you can gengineer your insects to go
                        after them and leave everyone else alone.
                        For the enemy to discover what was hap-
                        pening, they would have to first trace any
                        plague to the insects, dissect an insect to understand what
                        molecules it is reacting to, then correlate this with the
                        odors of all their equipment and supplies. Even with chem-
                        ical sensors and computer analysis, this could take weeks.
                        A more trial-and-error approach would be to leave out cer-
                        tain supplies to see if the insects are attracted to them in the
                        absence of humans.
                        Another modification that could make insect weapons
                        nastier is to redesign them to be resistant to certain com-
                        mon types of insect repellent, or to have pan-specific poi-
                        son resistance. A pesticide that kills any insect can be engi-
                        neered, but this may fatally damage the ecosystem (killing
                        beneficial insects or plants) and have toxic short- or long-
                        term effects on human health as well.
                        The best ways to deal with genemod insects may be
                        swarms of engineered defensive insects, target-seeking
                        viruses, tiny microbots, or nano-machines.​"



                        [B]That however was just TL 9 (the very next age after our current one). Now, let's see some of the outlandish stuff of the far future:[\b]



                        Sponge Computers (TL10)
                        Sponges have a homogeneous macroscopic structure
                        suitable for hosting neural networks, but the natural ani-
                        mals do not take advantage of this to perform complex
                        thinking. With some genetic tinkering, sponges can be
                        made to grow electrically active neurons in configurations
                        responsive to external stimuli. As these develop, they
                        become effectively “artificial brains,” that can be connected
                        to biosensors or other electronics. Sponge computers
                        incorporate memories and make logical connections much
                        like a real brain, with the advantages that the structure
                        allows the permeation of nutrients and oxygen and that
                        they can keep growing as long as these are supplied. They
                        are thus much more robust than vatbrains and easier to
                        grow.
                        At TL11, sponge computer implants can be made with
                        discriminatory growth patterns that let them grow into
                        small crevices and infiltrate biological material. Implanted
                        into a sinus cavity, it could grow into and either supple-
                        ment or take over the brain of a person. Use the rules for
                        vatbrains (p. 28) but halve the cost and weight. There is no
                        practical upper limit to how large a sponge computer can
                        be – they can be housed in the open ocean if necessary.
                        TL11 infiltrative sponge computer implants most often
                        suppress higher brain functions, giving the subject both the
                        AI and Automaton meta-traits (p. B263). They are about the
                        size of an apple seed and can be easily implanted in food,
                        etc. They take a day to grow; a HT-4 roll means the subject’s
                        immune system fended them off. Removal requires brain
                        surgery, much like removing a tumor; roll at -3 if they’re
                        half-grown or -6 if full-grown.​



                        Fungal Infonets (TL10)
                        Transgenic sequences can grow neural tissue within
                        fungal threads, allowing them to act as conduits for elec-
                        trochemical signals. This means fungi can build “wires”
                        and “circuits” on and within any medium in which they can
                        grow – which is just about anywhere. By planting such a
                        fungus, one can grow circuitry in places where building it
                        by more intrusive means would be too difficult or too
                        noticeable. Special nodes in the fungus allow connections
                        to be made to conventional electrical circuitry, or other
                        organisms that can send and interpret signals through the
                        fungal net.

                        Fungal Surveillance Net (TL11)
                        With their ability to grow on anything and their tenden-
                        cy to be ignored or overlooked by people, fungal infonets
                        make ideal infiltration organisms. Further gengineered
                        enhancements can add organs sensitive to light, sound, and
                        chemical stimuli, turning them into organic surveillance
                        systems that can be installed simply by spreading spores.
                        ​
                        Smart Fungi (TL11)
                        ​​​​​By concentrating neural tissue into distributed process- ing nodes, a fungal infonet can be modified to allow a more
                        coordinated reaction of the entire organism to local stim-
                        uli. Although a far cry from true intelligence, this gives the
                        fungus rudimentary problem-solving capability and allows
                        it to seek particular targets to grow into and digest. When
                        combined with acidic or parasitic properties, this produces
                        an insidious biological weapon.
                        ​​​​​​​
                        Intelligent Fungi (TL12)
                        Further enhancements give fungi neural processing cen-
                        ters and problem solving capabilities similar to sponge
                        computers (p. 29). Although sponge computers are likely to
                        be more generally useful, fungal versions may find use in
                        applications where other fungal properties are desirable.
                        They can grow almost anywhere there is sufficient mois-
                        ture and organic material, and can be combined with a
                        surveillance role, for example.

                                    Just for clarification, TL 9 will (would?) be when we are using nanobots on our daily lives. TL 10 is more or less "ALL HAIL THE MACHINE LORD!". Some Techs of TL 10 include self replicating factories that can terraform entire planets in a matter of months.

                                    Meanwhile, TL 11 is the pinnacle of any SciFi possibility, it's the truly outlandish stuff, because TL 12 is more like Fantasy than SciFi; TL 12 is basically magic

                                    Thus, when the book says that Sponge Computers or Neural Fungi are TL 10, it truly is because those are really wondrous stuff from crazy futuristic aliens.

                                    So, to answer your question... Undoubtedly, those "Sponge Brains" would be engineered (grown?) to repel predators.

                                    Or, maybe, would they actually be "smart organic glass"?

But then, TL 11 is TRULY insane. If you read on the book the stuff that TL 11 can do, it is truly ludicrous - as it should be.

                                    Most of those sponge computers would be used as... Computers. With the advantage that, rather than requiring a factory to manufacture, you just need a tube (acting as a "vatbrain"), nutrients and a sample of the sponge.

                                    Or the weaponized version that works as a "brain parasite", growing inside the head and replacing the brain of an individual.

                                    But yeah, a super sponge computer encompassing an entire world would be possible at TL 11. Maybe every colony on oceanic worlds begin with one

                                    And at TL 12 you could do really silly things like "intelligent electromagnetic waves" that live inside stars (and no, this is a SciFi idea actually), or you could make living metal and such.
