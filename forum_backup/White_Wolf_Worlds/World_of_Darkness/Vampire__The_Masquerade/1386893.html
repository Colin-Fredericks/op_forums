    [DAV20/V20] Hierarchy of Sin : Humanity (long)

    05-08-2020, 11:47 PM
    Me and my crew are currently playing a DA campaign that we hope to progress across the centuries to modern times. All the characters follow some other Road than Via Humanitas as it appears excruciatingly difficult one to uphold. However, I would like to make a social Malkavian who follows the Road/Path of Humanity – backup characters are nice to have. Even after using the search function and reading 40+ topics, I’m still at a loss with the Hierarchy of Sin.

    The character concept of the aforementioned Malkavian is to be a spy who, during his mortal days, worked for the Catholic Church. Officially he was a herald-diplomat for some lord but his true mission was to snoop out the secrets of the nobility for his true masters. Having to often bring ill news to an already hostile audience, a superb appliance of etiquette and empathy were required to keep breathing.

    I have difficulties determining where this characters Road rating might settle in. Moreover, people seem to have different views on how degeneration works. Some say it’s all about what the Beast wants. It doesn’t matter if you kill someone in self-defense, the Beast doesn’t care about your reasoning. Some say it’s more about the social mores of the time/place the character lived in. Slavery was a normal custom for the Romans so making a ghoul wouldn’t be a problem as long as they treated them in a humane way. I would much appreciate your take on these example Sins (and my reasoning) the Malkavian might face.

    Score 7 – Theft
    • He doesn’t pick pockets or steal horses. He buys the things he needs like a normal person. The Beast would be delighted if the character just took whatever he wanted and can at most understand stealing physical objects.
    • He eavesdrops and pages through personal diaries in search for secrets. In short, he steals information. The society does condemn spying but does the Beast understand such delicate matters? Would a spy with Humanity 7 risk losing a dot if he acquired knowledge the subject wouldn’t want to share?
    • Feeding and consent. This is a big one. Would it trigger this Sin if the vampire fed on someone sneakily in a non-violent and non-lethal way (during sex, sleeping target…)? He is very good at talking but would probably not be able to consistently convince medieval people to let a demon-spawn from Hell drink their blood. A single failure could easily lead to a manslaughter.

    Score 6 – Accidental violation of another
    • A nosy scribe needs to be silenced before the Elders order his execution. Dementation •• is used to occupy his mind with something else than the strange coincidences he took note of earlier. Unfortunately he gets himself killed by drowning while trying to save some hallucinated saint from a well. A degeneration roll seems appropriate for Humanity 6 character. Situation would be vastly different if he could convince a cloister to take in ”this troubled man” and keep him safe for long enough to recover (and for the coterie to destroy the evidence). In your opinion, can a humane character use Dementation as a tool even if he took precautions? Dementation • seems great for defusing volatile situations.

    Score 4 & 3 – Killing people
    • He loathes the use of violence and tries to avoid it. However, the coterie is ambushed and must fight. How high Humanity would have to be to warrant a degeneration roll in the following situations?
      1) He uses maneuvers such as Diversion or Tactics but doesn’t personally draw blood.
      2) He targets arms and legs, uses bashing damage and is prepared to give first aid to his opponents afterwards.
      3) He injures his opponent and sighs in relief when the opponent in question surrenders. Then a coterie mate tears the opponents head off and thanks for assisting in the killing.
    • The Prince calls a bloodhunt. Malkavian reluctantly uses his talents to discover the quarrys location but adamantly refuses take part of the actual killing. His coterie members happily waltz in and slaughter the fugitive like a lamb. What level of Sin did the character commit?
    • The Beast certainly understands personal application of violence. A sword through chest, the Beast approves. Plain murder is grade 3 Sin. How low ones Humanity would need to be to warrant a degeneration roll for a murder he technically doesn’t commit himself? Does the Beast feel anything if a vampire signs a death sentence but isn’t even present during the execution?
    • The society holds mafia bosses and ruthless generals accountable for the loss of lives their mobsters or soldiers inflict. How low a characters Humanity would need to be until (occasionally) giving orders to kill would not bring it any lower?

    Score ? – The Embrace
    • Turning a person into a death-dealing monster. Should be worse than stealing a purse, burning down an empty barn, or chopping off someones hand. Wouldn’t surprise me if most sires lost a dot of Humanity when Embracing. Chances are that a brand new vampire has a Humanity 4 sire showing them the ropes. Good luck surviving the first week when you don’t instantly have Herd •••• and you have never even tried to catch a rat.

    All in all, I fear holding on Humanity will be very difficult even before throwing in vampire politics, bloodhunts and what have you.

    In case I get shoehorned into choosing some other Road, how do you handle the alienness of Conviction when playing a social character? V20, p. 314: ”Conviction is completely inhuman; the character who has this Trait can no longer pass for human under anything but the most cursory of scrutiny.” Which alternative Road would you suggest for this character concept?

    Thank your time and sorry for the wall of text.

    TL;DR. Morality system is frustrating unless you want to play Louis.

      The Morality system really is reliant upon the specific needs of the players and the chronicle as both an overly literal reading of the system or under used system can lead to undercutting the thematics of a story.

      I'm not sure now is the right time or if I'm ready to get into a full essay but I'd advise talking to your storyteller and fellow players about how they want to treat the theme and moods of the chronicle because sometimes wrestling with these questions is what people want to do and it does sound interesting to me but it's not necessarily for everyone or every chronicle.

          Here’s how I tend to interpret violence and humanity...

          - Fighting back when attacked only needs a check if you’re at Humanity 8+ (injury).

          - Killing in self-defense needs a check at Humanity 6+ (accidental violation, because the death isn’t your intent; your survival is).

          - Killing someone when you started the fight needs a check at Humanity 4+ (impassioned violation, because you wanted to hurt them, but it went too far).

          - Coldblooded murder with purpose (i.e. you think they deserve to die for some reason) needs a check at Humanity 3+.

          - Killing others for your own enjoyment is a roll even at Humanity 2.

          - Torturing people to death for your own pleasure is a roll still made at Humanity 1 (Note: Mortals are incapable of losing their last point of humanity so this is the bottom of the barrel for humanity; guys like Jeffrey Dahmer).

          Now, to also put those checks into perspective, your average person with Humanity 7 and Conscience 3 who kills someone in self-defense has a 66% of making their diff 8 Conscience check and remaining at Humanity 7. They feel guilty despite it being self-defense and stay connected to their humanity.

          Even if they fail their conscience check, their Humanity only drops to 6 (slightly detached from humanity, as is commonly reported by those suffering PTSD after ending up in such a situation).

          Realistically, a normal person would have to be confronted by situations involving killing in self-defense at least twice, and more likely half a dozen times, before their Humanity would drop to 5 and even if they killed in self-defense a thousand more times, they’d never drop to Humanity 4 because they’ve become desensitized to that level of killing.

          Humanity 5 and even 4 has no effect on one’s bearing compared to Humanity 7. Also worth noting is that, if you’re a mortal, low humanity doesn’t really mean much mechanically. You’ll be more vulnerable to certain disciplines and humanity 3 or less will have higher difficulty on certain social tasks. But it doesn’t effect their sleep schedule or dice pools or anything.

          It’s the fact that you’re a vampire that makes the Hierarchy of Sin matter. Because you AREN’T human. The Path of Humanity is a mask and tool to keep yourself from becoming a mindless predator.

          Certain actions therefore matter MORE for maintaining a connection to humanity for a vampire than they do to an actual human.

              Thank you for your insights, @Gryffon15 and @Chris24601! Seeing how scarce in detail the Hierarchy of Sin is, ST rulings do seem necessary.

              After contemplating on the matter, I'm leaning towards the point of view where Sins are filtered through the Beast. Vampires know not to make overt threats in Elysiums. The Beast doesn't get riled up over subtle threats even though the bullied vampire knows what's going on. This suggests that the Beast minds its own business unless it sees a threat or an opportunity to tempt the "host" to indulge in the dark things it enjoys. Many have pointed out how Humanity is mostly a vampire "problem" as humans don't have the Beast within them. So, in the same vein as Elysium threats, some Sins might not make the Beasts radar beep as it doesn't take note of the event. I'd say that the Hierarchy of Sins represents violations so blatant that even the Beast can't miss them. This leaves a little wiggle room to play with.

              Theft is a grade 7 Sin.
              • Vampire strolls down the street and sees a trembling old lady. He walks to her, stretches out his hand and takes a firm grip of her purse. There's really no struggle. The lady doesn't have her purse anymore and the Beast is fully aware of what's happening. If the robber has Humanity 7+ a degeneration roll is required. It doesn't matter why the purse was robbed or how it makes the character feel. The Beast laughs at your sob stories.
              • A spy uses his tiny camera to quickly copy a stack of blueprints. An engineer spent years perfecting these designs in hopes of fetching a good price for them. Too bad mister Accardi thinks his offer was fair enough and paid the spy to procure the blueprints instead. The Beast can't understand why the Humanity 7 spy is feeling guilty. I'd say that no degeneration roll is needed in situations like these. The same goes for cheating on your taxes a bit or telling a white lie to an insurance detective. The Beast simply doesn't have a concept for these kind of things. However, I'd say Humanity 8 character would need a roll for grade 8 Sin in these examples.

              Manslaughter is a grade 4 Sin.
              • John has a gun. It's his constitutional right. When a robber broke into his house in the middle of the night, shots were fired. He didn't mean to kill the guy. In the movies a leg shot stops the bad guy and then the hero can have witty talk with him, right? Apparently there are arteries or some shit like that down there... The Beast has no opinion on those movies but it understands what happens when a trigger is pulled. A degeneration roll is needed if Humanity is 4+.
              • No killing. That was pretty much the only thing this quarrelsome coterie could agree upon. When the usually effective bribes & lies failed, a fight broke out. Now, this Malkavian here didn't know a thing about fighting but luckily he was an annoying twat (ah, another thing everyone agrees upon) who could create a Diversion when needed. This time he really outdid himself and a security guard took a knife to his chest and died.
                • The one swinging the knife needs to make a degeneration roll for grade 4 Sin since he was the one who did the deed (manslaughter) by the book.
                • Coterie members who physically softened the guard up (bashing damage, attempts to disarm him, grappling etc.) before the fatal swing would need to roll for grade 5 Sin. Their Beasts are a bit disappointed because someone else snuffed the life out of the victim but are happy with the outcome and for the thrill of a fight. Characters might get this transgression count as grade 6 if they genuinely try (but fail) to save the guards life. For instance, giving first aid and rushing him to a hospital or calling an ambulance would suffice.
                • The Malkavian didn't throw a single punch but would need to roll for a grade 7 Sin for his participation. The Beast was denied all physical contact but I'd say a normal human would feel bad for playing even a tiny part in an accidental death of a human being. That is the bottom line for me. Therefore I wouldn't allow any "discounts" for this. Instead, if he doesn't try his best to safe the guard, he get's to roll for grade 6 Sin instead.
                • I'd say a Humanity 7 character could plan a peaceful burglary gig into a warehouse and even pull it off without alerting his Beast (assuming nothing physical is stolen -- hacking an office computer for info is acceptable). He wouldn't need to roll for degeneration if he ran away or sat in Obfuscate when his friends decided to stand and fight. That wasn't the plan, his hands are clean (except for being covered in blood after giving CPR to the guard) and he bailed out when it came down to violence.
                • A Humanity 8+ character, however, would struggle hard here. If he participates in planning shady things that could reasonably result in a violent conflict and/or tags along with the coterie knowing this, he might have to make a degeneration roll even if he stands at the back Obfuscated and merely watches as the fatal fight unfolds. There has to be another way that doesn't impose a risk to human life. I'd call for a grade 8 Sin degeneration roll if someone ends up dead and a character had even a minuscule and indirect part in it. Again, the Beast cannot understand where all this guilt is coming from.

              Does my reasoning make any sense to you? I'd like to have Humanity limit the D&D nerds within my players but not let it cripple all the action
