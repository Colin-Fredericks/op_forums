    On the premise of how Presence functions

    10-15-2024, 12:45 AM
    Hello everyone!
    So, this is a hot topic, somewhat. At least in every table I've been STing since I was a kid. How should we consider many of the functions of Presence to work, Presence is a discipline that's always bound to create some discussion about its funcioning, because on one hand anyone can resist it with a spendure of a Willpower point followed by a Willpower roll (difficult 8) and a single success, what many people(including MANY of my players that love to play social characters in my chronicles "since forever") complain a lot about, because according to them, it makes it "trivial to resist one of the most iconic vampiric powers". Also there's the fact of whether it should work in combat or not, some argue that you cannot use it during combat, others that it only shouldn't work after you attack someone and many think you could still use Majesty and attack a target and he would be still subject to its effects somehow. Also the fact that one needs to keep spending Willpower to resist it for as long as in the presence of the cainite using Presence.

    Throughout my years, I've been changing one thing or two regarding its effect and use, particularly during combat, where it's more problematic. I used to rule that only Dread Gaze and Majesty were possible to be used during combat, and Majesty, only as long as the one using it doesn't attack their targets. But, then I was arguing again with a friend of mine and that made me curious to see how other ST approach Presence and its use in combat. I'd be glad to hear anything you have to tell mir about how you rule it in your games.

      I would treat Presence in the way that you would treat Chimerstry. The Clanbook: Ravnos revised has a great write up about unbelief vs Chimerstry.

      Lets make an example. Anna is a Toreador of 9th Generation. She has Presence at 5. She walks into a parlor and turns on her Majesty attempting to make a grand entrance. While some elder Kindred would know exactly whats going on here, and would either be unaffected (lower generation) or decide to spend the willpower (aged but same generation) the majority of the viewers would be affected normally as they have no reason to suspect they're being influenced, and even if they do perhaps they don't just care in that moment.

      Not everyone may know that:
      A. She is a Toreador.
      B. She has Presence.
      C. What being influenced by it feels like.
      D. How to resist being influenced by it.
      E. That she is even using it at all.

      So taking the above example, perhaps she walks into the room, and instead of using her Majesty to turn heads, the player rolls Manipulation + Expression, rolls really high and makes a grand entrance just the same. Does every character there just start burning Willpower because they think they might be entranced by a Presence power? Hilariously some might because the players shouldn't know what is actually trying to affect them. Was it an Intimidation roll that was really high or was it Dread Gaze?

      I think this is a problem of meta-gaming and too much information on the players part, and not separating character knowledge from player knowledge.

              Originally posted by Herr Meister View Post
              On the other side, I feel the need to say that the effects of Presence, especially someting like Majesty should be like "a hundred times" more powerful than a simple successful expression roll to impress people in a saloon. .
              While I agree with Majesty in this case, I disagree with Awe, Entrancement, and Dread Gaze. Presence is supposed to be subtle, and it can't be that if so much more powerful than an exceptional roll of the applicable abilities. Now don't get me wrong I'm not talking about getting 1 success, but someone walking into the saloon with 4-5 successes on a Manipulation + Expression (or whatever traits you want to use for this) should be equivalent to using Awe in effect IMO.

              So I wrote up a nice long write up about each of the powers of Presence 1-5 vs combat and the forum ate it. I don't feel like doing so again, so I'll try to summarize it. I'm using the write up in the V20 VtM core as the basis.

              Rank 1 Awe and Rank 3 Entrancement in combat should be nulled. Awe already has a screed about self preservation, and I think that Entrancement should have had one as well as it seems like a more powerful version of Awe.

              Rank 4 Summon isn't really applicable since you aren't going to be doing combat versus someone you are Summoning from far away, and the effect would end when they get to you anyway. (Cast Summon to make Bob come to you so you can punch him, well once he gets there he's no longer under the effect of Summon so it doesn't matter anyway...)

              Rank 2 Dread Gaze (Charisma + Intimidation vs diff Courage+ Wits, a possible of 2-10 dice vs a diff of 2-10) is meant for conflict, but the victim needs to first be affected by the power to have the chance to Roll the Willpower test then spend a point, which by that point he's already lost an action. Using Ana the 9th Gen Toreador from before vs John a 13th Gen Caitiff, if she goes first she can Dread Gaze him and avoid his first attack. After he will have to either win the Willpower test and start spending Willpower to be around her, or hope she fails and botches the rolls before she can accrue enough dice to Dread Gaze him into a stupor. Dread Gaze is an extended action, and the Willpower expenditure only stops him from running away or being cowed, not losing the action dice per round.

              Rank 5 Majesty is also meant for conflict, but puts the onus on the victim rather than the user with the resist test. (Courage of victim vs diff of Charisma + Intimidation of the user making this is a possible 1-5 dice vs 2-10 difficulty.) Again like Dread Gaze, if the user goes first the victim will have to fail the resist test before they can even think about using Willpower, which will have to happen on the next turn because that first turn requires them to resolve the effects of Majesty first. On the second turn nothing is stopping the user of Majesty from also deciding to start using Dread Gaze as well if they go first, and once again the victim will have to resolve the effects of that. Turn three being the first turn they could reasonably use Willpower to resist, and this is if they make all the rolls, and are still in the area where this is all going down. As stated in the Majesty screed "A subject who fails the roll aborts his intended action."

                  First things first, Majesty is a social nuke. It is one of the least subtle discipline powers in the entire game, is clearly and indisputably supernatural to the point of being a potential Masquerade violation, and its use is generally considered a hostile act in and of itself. Especially in Elysia, courts, and meetings, where disciplines are nominally prohibited in sum let alone hostile discipline use. Dropping Majesty in Elysium for any reason short of immediate and necessary self-defense, or breaking up fights, is a fantastic way to make an enemy of everyone in the city, let alone the Prince, Primogen, and Sheriff whose authority you just superseded.

                  For Toreador that's an even worse idea, considering to them discipline use for social events is a sign of weakness. Heads should be turning and jaws dropping because of fashion sense, swagger, entourage, and performance, not because of supernatural compulsion. Something like dropping Majesty in Elysium -- if the Toreador even survives the entirely-predictable consequences of it -- is going to get that Toreador put on the poseur list on the entire continent, if not planet, and they can kiss current or future status and clan prestige goodbye.

                  It's not a combat-only power, but to be frank, its best use is to defuse a hostile situation before violence breaks out. Regardless of its use, the vampire using it absolutely should be conscious violence may break out because of its use, not least of all against themselves if any vampire deems necessary and prudent to attempt resisting.

                  As far as Awe and Entrancement, there are select niche uses in conflict. Note I said "conflict", not "combat". For example, a scene in a Rack in which a feeding attempt goes awry, and threatens a bar fight or "catch me outside" scenario. Either power could be used to get a crowd's attention, influence third parties to intervene, or compel a hostile actor to instead de-escalate. Generally, once attacks start getting rolled the situation is beyond either power's capability to influence.

                              Like my old roommate said back in the 90's "Presence is a combat discipline".

                              But really, it's combat and social.

                              Vs Mortals, Presence wins friends and favors...and sometimes stalkers.

                              Vs Vamps and most supernatural, Presence is a WILLPOWER DRAIN.

                              It's gonna depend upon how a ST uses the WP economy and the harder it is to replenish WP, the better Presence gets.

                              Dread Gaze seems underwhelming at first. But I'm combat you hold your initiative until after the target spends a WP. Best used in group combat.

                              Ally attacks target, and lands a hard shot, target really wants to parry/dodge. ST may declare or conceal if target used WP, or require a Perception roll to determine. Tricky, but it is only a level 2 power

                              But if you are sure target greatly expended effort, the Gaze will deduct from future dice pools.

                              Even better after Target is Grappled.

                              Entrance is how you turn an enemies underlings against him. Great for DA campaigns and sieges.

                              Summoning, similar to Entrance but you can get Generals/Captains out of position. Or send targets on wild chases. Etc.

                              Majesty needs little explaining other than subject CAN expend WP and still attack the user.

                                      I do want to say, after my write up, I went through editions on all the powers. 2nd edition and Revised era Presence is only resisted with a Willpower Test + Point spent for a TURN, and the victim has to spend another point EVERY TURN. This is huge. Most characters realistically will only have 3-5 Willpower to spend. V20 says you Willpower Test and then Point spend to resist for a SCENE, but offers no rules on how to keep spending points. IMO V20 is in error, because of how concentration and Willpower expenditure checks work in all editions up to V20. For example to spend Willpower to resist a derangement you are taking a whole turn to concentrate on it, and IMO this matches up with how it should work to resist the effects of Presence or any mental Discipline that allows it. In combat this is huge because it forces your victim to first fail the Power test and be affected by it (Dread Gaze forces them to flee for example, so they lose that action and must use it to flee..) then they have to use ANOTHER action/turn to concentrate and shrug off the Power itself by spending Willpower. Earlier editions like Revised were very descriptive about how this was the point where the Victim could turn around and start ignoring the user to drop the effect of the Power. I think the Revised era has the better write up for how to deal with Presence in combat.
