        Back to the subject of Tom and Mark, Tom could do as suggested and have Mark apply gradually more make-up to appear older. Maybe dye his temples grey, to imply the passage of time.

        However, concurrent to that, Mark could begin introducing his frequent business contacts to "Mark Jr". Namely, Mark without the age make-up. Presenting "Junior" as his heir, who people will be reassured is being prepared to replace "Mark Sr" in his position. As time goes on, "Junior" will take a more and more active role in the operation of the business, while "Senior" shows up less and less. Until "Mark Sr's" "retirement", where he's allegedly gone off to the Bahamas or whatever, while "Mark Jr" (now firmly established as Just Mark) takes the position more or less full time. Buying him another few decades before he needs to start putting on his own age make-up, and create a fictitious grandson, "Mark III". Etc, etc.

        Obviously, this is the best case scenario, assuming nothing about society, the business, the economy, or Tom's place in the Jyhad ever changes.

        In reality, a single Ghoul's utility as a representative in the world of business is bound to alter, if not drop off. It depends entirely on what the business is, and what its place in the larger financial landscape is.

        For example, any business depending on specific technologies, barring those astoundingly simple and unchanging, may find old tech (and thus old knowledge) become obsolete over the decades. Either being outmoded entirely (no one needs a buggy-whip business now), or changing to the point where the know-how isn't applicable. (Although it would be interesting to suppose certain industries - namely American railroads - may have stagnated specifically because Vampires are behind the scenes, stopping them from changing. In reality, American rail is just full of conservative, greedy people, who are willing to sink the business rather than change or cede control. So, no difference to the Vampires, really.)

        A ghoul who is the executive of a business might become non-useful if that business gets bought out. In recent decades, we in Real Life have seen enormous, borderline monopolistic consolidation of all sectors of the economy. Larger corporations gobbling up smaller ones, and exerting such pressure on the remainder that getting bought out is the only way the business survives. (Or doesn't, as many businesses are bought and then liquidated for cheap cash and to remove a rival. Or get bought by venture capital and saddled with debt that WILL kill it.). If a ghoul survives in a business that got absorbed into a larger corporate structure, they're likely little more than a branch manager or someone's flunky. (And, of course, more likely to be within some older, more established Vampire's vast portfolio). Vulnerable to being replaced or demoted to irrelevance (and therefore a pittance of a salary) unless they really work to climb the ladder.

        Far removed from the dreams of "Passive Income" by way of being a Vampiric "sugar daddy".

        Finally, as many have pointed out, while a Ghoul's body remains as hale and hearty as ever (barring letting themselves go in other ways), their mind is a fragile egg. If the Regnant's treatment of the Ghoul, the influence of their blood, or the addiction to it doesn't compromise a Ghoul's effectiveness, the sheer eroding power of time just might. Even if they remain mostly sane, they're just as much a relic of an earlier age as their master. How well is a Ghoul found during the 1920s going to operate a smartphone, when Boomers two generations younger have difficulty with them? Much less grasp a global, digital economy? Many Ghouls may be retired from such service long before age becomes a problem, simply because they're not equipped to serve the master in the field they were chosen for, because that field no longer exists.

        So what does Tom do when Mark can no longer do the job? Aside from killing him, naturally. (Tom may stay his hand for sentimentality, practicality, pity, a desire to forestall Humanity loss, or simply because Mark is a familiar face that keeps Tom grounded or comfortable). Perhaps Tom pawns Mark off on another Vampire, or gives Mark as a gift to a Childer. Maybe Tom tries to retrain Mark in a new field, to keep up with changing times (imagine a Ghoul being sent back to school to learn coding or digital age finance). Maybe Mark is set up as the manager of a new business venture, one that's not meant to make that much money but needs to be done to service the master's needs (like a night club or freight hauling service).

        Or maybe, like in the recent Renfield movie, Mark becomes Tom's personal assistant. Taking his calls, managing his schedule, dry-cleaning his capes. Mark may not be making money anymore, but there's always a thousand little tasks that need doing around the Haven. (I think the main antagonist from Ashes to Ashes had "bitter Ghoul snaps after years of being relegated to menial work after being made obsolete for his master's interests" as his main motivation).

                            Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                            A frequent poster here, False Epiphany , runs a very detailed online game that incorporates a lot of ghouls into the greater cast. I think he has run lots of ideas of how ghouls are "disposed" when no longer wanted. Hopefully he'll post.
                            Man, it's been a while since I have. But speak of the devil and he shall appear.

                            A few ideas that occur:

                            One, sell or trade the unwanted ghoul/s to other vampires. Could be for other ghouls, could be for boons to cash in later. Most vampires will prefer to do this, since it lets them make a profit off of something they no longer have any use for anyways.

                            It's less than ideal for the buyer, since the ghoul is already blood bound to another domitor and it will take time for the bond to break and form around the new domitor--although with periodic draughts of their master's vitae, and time away from their old one, this probably happens inevitably. The advantage to getting a ghoul "secondhand" is that they are (probably) already trained, know how to behave around vampires, and are otherwise "broken in". The value of this should not be underestimated. I have had multiple PCs casually ghoul useful mortals, but never take sufficient time to "break them in" or properly instill that the relationship was one of master and servant. That mindset does not occur out thin air. These "untrained" ghouls frequently proved rebellious or less than dependable to their PC domitors, and many wound up dead. Getting your ghoul secondhand saves the time and trouble of training them if their last domitor has done a good job.

                            Also, the supernatural value of getting your ghoul secondhand can't be underestimated. Depending on how old the ghoul is, they may have developed Disciplines. If they belong to a lower-generation domitor, they may even have Disciplines that your own ghouls could never develop. "Hand-me-down" ghouls from elders can be highly prized commodities as a result.

                            As a sub-point of this idea, Kindred can also gift their unwanted ghouls to childer or other allied subordinate Kindred who will put them to better use. I once had a PC do this--she had a ghoul she felt like she'd outgrown and wanted to make room for other ghouls in her stable. So, her intent was to gift the unwanted ghoul to the leader of a neonate coterie that she was acting as a patron towards. (Something else wound up happening to the ghoul, but that was the PC's intent.)

                            Two, just kill them. Practical-minded Kindred will do this if the ghoul knows sensitive information their domitor does not want shared with other vampires. Ghouls can be in proximate positions to gather a great deal of information about their domitors--they are the often the "secretaries" who are entrusted with making sure their master's desires actually get carried out. As with secretaries, many vampires underestimate their ghouls and get accustomed to thinking of them as just "the help" or "part of the furniture" and may overlook just how much their servants actually know about them. So I think the number of Kindred that murder their unwanted ghouls may be in the minority.

                            More vicious-tempered or deranged vampires may kill unwanted ghouls out of emotional rather than pragmatic reasons. As with any terminated interpersonal relationship, there can easily be negative feelings involved. A ghoul that is no longer wanted by their domitor may have done something to disappoint or anger them. Some vampires might kill their ghouls unintentionally if the ghoul's offense was galling enough to provoke their domitor into frenzy.

                            Three, abandon them. This is bad practice for the Masquerade, for obvious reasons. This is bad practice for the vampire personally, since they're getting nothing in return for something they are losing anyway, while still leaving behind a loose end. However, I'd posit that quite a few ghouls get abandoned by more careless or fickle vampires. Toreador are probably especially notorious for this with ghouled artists.

                            Some vampires honestly might not see abandoning their ghouls problem, if the ghoul doesn't know anything about Kindred society--many will know too little to make nuisances of themselves (the vampire may or may be not be wrong in their assessment of the ghoul's knowledge), which in turn diminishes their value as assets to trade other vampires. If the ghoul isn't "broken in", and is just a blood-addicted human rather than trained servant, it is better to find another human who isn't already known and blood bound to another vampire.

                            Finally, some vampires (usually younger ones) may have consciences and not want to kill a no longer needed ghoul. They might not realize selling them to another vampire is an option, have ethical objections to doing so, or believe that no one will want the ghoul. A neonate might even regret taking a ghoul in the first place and not be prepared for the reality of the blood bond and vitae addiction. So, they simply disappear. Some neonates might also be lazy and not want to go through the hassle of trading the ghoul, while also not being ruthless enough to kill them. Cold-blooded murder of someone because they're no longer useful is a pretty callous act.

                            Either way, such ghouls will become independent and will probably try to find their way to other domitors, if not their original domitor. Even if they hate being a ghoul--possibly their domitor's reason for giving them up--the blood bond and vitae addiction are not easily overcome. Still, if the ghoul doesn't know any other vampires (or ghouls), it's possible they might return to some semblance of their old life.

                            I've had this happen in play with a ghoul PC. Her domitor was a Toreador fledgling who liked her artwork, got bored with her--the fact she was gay and not sexually interested in him contributed to that--and cut her loose. He wasn't ruthless enough to kill her, wasn't motivated enough to trade her to another vampire, and wasn't especially concerned about the Masquerade. This fact can be overlooked among Masquerade's roster of hyper-competent elder NPCs, but plenty of vampires can be just plain lazy and inattentive.

                            What I've seen more frequently than unwanted ghouls are ghouls orphaned after their domitor meets final death. Most vampires have at least one ghoul, and once they're dead, the ghouls are on their own. Other vampires may not know about them, or know about them all--the ghoul might not have been introduced to Kindred society, or if they were, other vampires might just consider them "the help" and honestly not remember them. (I make a frequent point of describing NPC ghouls to players in vague or generic terms, if they aren't doing anything notable, to feed into this bias.) These ghouls are often badly traumatized by the shattered blood bond and pose further dangers to the Masquerade.

                            Any significant conflict between non-Sabbat vampires will inevitably see large numbers of orphaned ghouls. Attentive princes and sheriffs will want to respond to this. So will opportunistic vampires. Orphaned ghouls can be added to one's stable or sold to other vampires. The broken blood bond may mean they're damaged goods, but unlike ghouls sold off by still-unliving domitors, orphaned ghouls can be immediately bound to new masters. Most princes and sheriffs will prefer to sell off (or hold on to) orphaned ghouls, since it turns a profit and fewer bodies to dispose of is less danger to the Masquerade.

                            An atypically ghoul-attentive Kindred may be able to tell if a vampire has met final death by observing the emotional state of their ghouls--although as many ghouls will mourn the disappearance of their domitors, this isn't a sure thing.

                            I always thought it was a shame how few By Night books detailed ghoul NPCs when in most cities, they will outnumber vampires, and probably by a significant margin. It's something I've endeavored to do differently to in my own games.

                            It's flattering to hear someone remembered that after how long I've been away!

                            Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.
