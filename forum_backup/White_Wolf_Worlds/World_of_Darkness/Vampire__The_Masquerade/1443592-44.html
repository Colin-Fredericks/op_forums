    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    Eh, I think of vampires as human. They're just CURSED humans.
    They're neither fully inhuman nor still entirely human; most of the game's dramatic tension comes from the conflict between the human and the inhuman elements. After all, their cognition is largely the same as a human being, they grow up in human cultures, mostly retain human morphology, mostly retain human psychology, learn the same way as humans, process their environment in largely the same way, experience all the same emotional responses, internalize memories the same way, etc. Even if you assume the Vampire eventually erases the Man, that process would take a long damn time, and you probably never see the last 10% or so ever being completely erased. No one starts out a vampire.

    A good portion of their perceived inhumanity is the result of a brutally dehumanizing physical and social condition; a lot of it is trauma shock, desensitization, hardening, learned callousness, rather than the result of a genuinely inhuman nature - they're not aliens, after all. Part of the game's horror is how much humanity is "lost" not from the Beast or any other element of vampirism, but the human side itself - all the politicking, occultism, dark religions, the lust for power, sadistic cruelty, the desire for dominance and subjugation, all that stuff is completely in keeping with human behavior under conditions of extreme fear, deprivation, and so forth. The Beast just wants a full belly and a safe place; it's the human side that seeks to avenge a hundred-year-old grudge.

            Vampires are humans given more power than they usually aught to have, and power reveals. If you want to see what a person's really like, give them the power to act like they want. The "curse" aspects aren't there to make the vampire directly miserable, but to push the vampire towards certain aspects of behaviour. Hell, A good enough understanding of human behaviour makes me conclude that the clan weaknesses were probably Deliberate Additions to the blood by the Clan Founders themselves, because shared weakness brings people together and helps them work alongside one another; without the weaknesses, every clan would see themselves as their chief competitors.

            In other words, the "curse" aspects of vampirism are brought about with someone with a keen understanding of human sociology. The weaknesses are designed to make vampires "stronger" as social units and as individuals. On a top down level, making the Nosferatu ugly as sin wins out on a cost-benefit analysis. Obviously I don't think that's the case for all curses; burned by sunlight just sucks and that's probably an outside power hating on vampires, but in the case of the Beast and messy criticals, that's just being fucking stupid. The Person doesn't benefit from it, the Beast doesn't benefit from it, the Vampire as a whole doesn't benefit from it, and the antideluvian puppet master doesn't benefit from it.

                Ok. So your average vampire is not on a path of enlightenment. From humanity 10 - 1, the vampire thinks as a human, and in a way, is.
                You have the mind of a social pack animal whose survival is geared around cooperation with other humans suddenly finding themselves with the instincts of a solitary predator, preying on their former "pack". Neglecting the human needs kills off the human and creates a wight, but neglecting the beast to cultivate your humanity may lead to mess when instinct takes over.

                Sunlight? Gone. (Can vampires get SAD?)
                Meaningful relationships? Gone. Kindred society is a paranoid clusterfuck and you can never be truly sure that your relationships are genuine. No human relationships can be safe, honest or lasting.
                And paranoia and isolation is so healthy, psychologically

                And the evil that vampires do to sate their hunger is always from the human. The beast simply wants food and safety. The human is the one that achieves it through stuff like human trafficking and murder. The beast doesn't care about your social games at Elysium, the human's doing all the backstabbing mind games. Do wights have governments and politics? Nope, vampire society being a poisonous clusterfuck is down to the human, again.

                Low humanity vampires become callous from cynicism, fear, depression, power, but they are still "human". The monster in your regular vampire is human.

                But for me, moving on to a path of enlightenment is a controlled destruction of the human psyche - killing off the human thought process without wassailing.
                A wight is pure vampire, a vampire on a path hit wight threshold and survived with their higher thought processes intact.
                This vampire is not human in instinct, in biology, in needs or in thought. Even if it may seem so at a cursory glance, their behaviour isn't human, observers are simply projecting their humanity onto them. There is nothing human left here, the person it grew out of is officially dead.
                (And there's a reason they aren't as common as vampires on humanity)
