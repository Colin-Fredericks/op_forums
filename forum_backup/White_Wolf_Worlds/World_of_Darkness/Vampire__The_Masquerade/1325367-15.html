                I think that Bioware's got a pretty mixed record on romances. To me, Baldur's Gate 2 had more interesting romances that were difficult to achieve and you could easily screw them up by saying the wrong thing. They were probably ridiculously hard in some cases, like Jaheera and Viconia's romances, and could have used some options to salvage the relationships if things were going wrong, but they felt more like how the characters would react. Their later romances felt kind of hollow. In Mass Effect it's pretty much anyone you want to romance you can, even if you're a crazy murder hobo and your partners a cop or sweet archaeologist. The Dragon Age games were pretty much give gifts until sex pops out, the second one you could even have romances with rivals that hated you.

                Something a little more balanced is probably the way to handle a romance. A way to actually fail needs to be in there (perhaps a chance to salvage things if you haven't done something they disagree with too much), and if you're doing things they absolutely hate, you shouldn't be able romance them. Doing things they like and making decisions in the game world needs to be a requirement for most of them.

                One night stands do happen, so if they were to put one in a game, it might be interesting if they ghosted you after, just to turn the tables on you. But that's just one example.

                                                I'd get rid of the roadmap design and the nice-guy syndrom that are so prevalent in bioware games and other games like life is strange, persona, stardew valley etc

                                                the one game I remember came close to my ideal romance was the last of us 2,
