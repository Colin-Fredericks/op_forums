    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    Except Salubri healing actually makes it significantly easier and encourages Salubri to seek out willing vessels as a mechanical incentive.
    Sorry, I’m just not seeing your argument.

    ANY vampire could heal a mortal just as efficiently by just feeding the mortal their blood (and if you have to heal the same mortal more than twice in year; you’re probably doing something wrong).

    V20 Salubri healing costs 1BP per health level restored; ergo there is ZERO mechanical advantage over just feeding them blood directly (just less physically gross because you’re laying hands instead of biting open your wrist so they can drink).

    “You can live forever as my ghoul in exchange for me getting to drink from your naturally replenishing mortal blood” is EVERY bit the same level of incentive to get mortals to be willing vessels as “I can spend the blood I take from you, minus what I need to live, to heal people” is.

    And again... in V20 a Salubri can feed on anyone unwilling by either spending a single willpower point or killing the person first and drinking after. It’s basically no different than the V5 Ventrue bane with a prey type of “willing mortals only.”

    Hell, a V20 Ventrue with its designated prey type as “freely consenting mortals” would be even MORE of a “friendly neighborhood vampire” than any Salubri as it couldn’t even spend a willpower or use its disciplines to get a meal and could heal human injuries as easily as any Salubri (and would have to be even more careful since a blood bound mortal can’t be freely consenting and ergo lost as a vessel).

    That’s ultimately why your argument falls flat; there’s nothing innate to the Salubri that actually makes it a “friendly neighborhood vampire” as compared to any random high humanity vampire of any other clan.

                                    The Good.
                                    Thin-Bloods: Well, I am actually running a campaign right now with one Thin-Blood and it's quite amazing. We don't see any issue, even though the two other PCד are full Vamps. VTM was never a game of power levels for me, more of a personal horror story. So far it's working great for us. The alchemy is cool and progresses slowly and through story events, recipes, and knowledge, which adds more things for the player to look for in the world. And we really like the discipline versatility with resonence.
                                    In other editions, they were just nerfed vampires. Here they are something else, the PC in our campaign unlike the rest of the coterie has some hope of a "normal" life, and it's something they fight for. And it's cool.

                                    Mixed.
                                    Oblivion: I don't know about this one. Really. The more books that come out the more it's clear that the Lasombra part doesn't really mix with the Necromancy staff. In my game, I just separated them. These powers belong to two different clans with a very different theme, and having them share this very spotlight-heavy power doesn't work for me.
                                    Clan-wide changes: Another thing I don't get. Something happens to a clan and suddenly their bane or their clan discipline changes all over the world for everyone. I don't buy it. One day your Giovanni woke up and Dominate and Potence just stopped being their clan disciplines. Amazing.

                                    The Bad:
                                    Nosferatu:                                     I don't like it that they went easy on their bane. They are my favorite clan, and their bane was the main thing that made them appealing in my eyes. There was a lot of creativity involved in customizing your own Nosferatu deformity. And now apparently they don't break the masquerade. They are just, somewhat ugly people.

                                    It was really hard for me to find things that I really don't like. I love V5 it's amazing and works really great for my group.

                                            Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                                            No
                                            -Brujah leaving the Cam. Hasn't been mentioned so far, so needs mentioning.
                                            The pigeonholing of the Clans to specific sects in general just feels so... whatever the clan equivalent of racist would be.

                                            Basically, V5 doesn’t seem to view identity as something that can exist independent of clan, at least not by default. If you’re a Brujah then you’re an Anarch, full stop. You can’t be in the Camarilla and be authentically Brujah. Your clan (which you have no more control over than the color of your skin) trumps any beliefs or experiences you have as an individual.

                                            And that’s before you consider that neither the Brujah nor the Gangrel are the sort of clans where “Well, some guy claiming to be our leader says we’re doing something so we’re all going to fall into lockstep” makes any sort of sense.

                                            I suspect some of this may come down to Paradox being more of a video game studio than a tabletop rpg studio and those sorts of binaries (ex. Horde vs. Alliance, Empire vs. Republic... Camarilla vs. Anarchs) with each faction having exclusive race/class options is just part of the initial perceptual blinders while the supplemental material produced outside Paradox walks some of this back or at least provides context.

                                            ex. the Camarilla supplement notes that the Brujah who remained loyal to the Cam rebranded themselves ‘Hellenes’ as part of distancing themselves from the members of the Anarch movement... thus, all the Brujah are Anarchs, but only because the ones in the Camarilla now identify as Hellenes.

                                            And THAT is something that feels both realistic and useful to add into a V20 game. The Brujah were not always called Brujah any more than any other current Clan name is the original one. Having a real time rebranding due to factionalism drives that home and plays into current events with various groups and movements rebranding due to scandals or segments of the group being disavowed.

                                            One sees it to an extent with the Banu Haqim (except that the generally playable ones are those who reject Haqim... so it’s a stupid name choice; Rafidi Haqim - “Those Who Reject Haqim” would be a much better name for the ones in the Camarilla), Ministry (way easier to sell others on than “Set Worshippers”) and Hecata (political rebranding to project unity)... but the Brujah/Hellenes split is the first that’s establishing that the factions of the clan wish to distinguish themselves.

                                            Personally, I’d like to see that extended across the Sects; ex. the Ventue who joined the Anarchs don’t think of themselves as Ventrue, but instead call themselves “Clan Reyes” (Spanish for Kings... perhaps a name initially localized to the Anarch Free States in California, but spreading to other Anarch cities) to emphasize their political break with their brethren in the Camarilla.

                                            That sort of in-universe branding would better sell the idea of the Sects as movements greater than one’s political party (i.e. as presented, Anarch vs. Camarilla is more on the level of Republicans vs. Democrats instead of, say, Americans vs. the British).

                                                            My thoughts on the Lasombra being part of the Camarilla are "It's a good idea with some caveats."

                                                            The previous Editions seemed dead set on making the Lasombra violently crazy about hunting down the Antitribu of their Clan and treating the idea of Lasombra in the sect as weird. Marcus Vitel was treated as a traitor to the Camarilla when his true Clan became known even though he was one of the most powerful vampires in the New World.

                                                            On the other hand, I've always felt that the Camarilla is a place where the Lasombra can shine. Being a sociopathic social Darwinist is pretty much how the Camarilla runs and the fact that they are there to make trouble for the Toreador, Tremere, and Ventrue just feels like it should naturally be a part of the system. Really, the Ventrue should have someone who is there undermining them and keeping them on their toes like the Shadow Lords do the Silver Fangs. The Tremere just aren't like that since they have been established as having almost no interest in mundane politics [which is a shame because "Evil Wizard" seems like it would be a great thing for a Seneschal role].

                                                            I also am of the mind that if you're going to make as radical a change as "The Lasombra join the Camarilla" that they probably did it about as best as they possibly could. I personally think that "Oh, btw, our Antediluvian is ALIVE" is something that should shake the Lasombra down to their foundation because all of their propaganda is based around this. It's the equivalent of Jesus arriving and whipping all of the GOP on the Senate floor before banishing them to Hell. You'll have people denying it but it's a thing that needs to be addressed.

                                                            OTOH, I don't think they needed to make it so overt. WIth as many casualties as the Gehenna War and Second Inquisition have inflicted, saying, "A few dozen to a couple of hundred Lasombra defected to the Camarilla, Blood Cults, or Anarchs" pretty much means the exact same thing for the Clan's power halving.

                                                            Plus, I really wish they'd come up with a Ritual or Maguffin to explain how the Vinculum was overcome even though I don't think it was ever as effective as some people believed.
