        Elders being terrifying is great. Paranoia and uphill battles, constantly struggling for your existence; That's what makes a great vampire game. I don't think Vampire did a particularly great job at writing powers at levels 7 though to 9, but it was great that powers 7 through to 9 existed, and the same goes for raising your attributes and skills beyond 5; it was a great motivator for trying to work up.

        Elders in V5... I'm not sure if they didn't think it through or knew what they were doing and decided to do it anyway. Blood Potency above 5* is a lot of trouble, to the point where you should absolutely not allow yourself to have that much potency. There are benefits, sure, but the drawbacks are suicidal. Diablerie in V5 is a quick and dirty route for XP, not a path to transcending your limitations and becoming less mundane and more divine, not a curse to keep the Cainite race at eachother's throats; it's literally just a method for which you trade humanity dots for discipline dots, discipline dots your character would get anyway if they'd just farmed resonances like you might be supposed to.

        Elders in V5 just aren't a force to be reckened with; half of them have been beckoned, the remainder are stronger than individual players but far weaker than a coterie or a coterie-sized bunch of hunters. They don't pressure PCs like they used to. Just shifting powers around doesn't really help them much. 'versatility' itself is not strength. Strength is strength. Elders in V5 become Jacks of all trades and they heal frighteningly fast but they don't become the dragons they were and you don't really have much incentive to conflict with them nor do they have much incentive to cause trouble with you, since you'd only try to eat your way towards caine in v5 if you were mentally deficient enough to not-be-embraced. Strong blood potency just lowers your quality of life as it puts you too much at the mercy of your beast and reduces the value of your favourite food sources. In V20 taking a particular elder down and getting away with it can be a chronicle, in V5 it's an episode. This in turn calls into question the motivations of the Sabbat, the Assamite loyalists, and any other group with an eye for generation.

        V20: Push, Push, Struggle. (80's!!)
        V5: Be happy with just the way you are, because trying to have more will just hurt you. Maybe fight some inept fascists (Camarilla, Church of Set, Assamite Loyalists..) for a power fantasy or fight hunters for a persecution fantasy.
        In V20 you're fighting up or down, in V5 you're fighting across the isle. I don't think what V5 is doing here is bad in a vacuum it's just not as good as what V20 is doing.

            I think there are ways to do Elder Disciplines without getting into the 6+ powers area, though it would still require changing V5's rules to accomplish.

            Something Vampire the Requiem does (though a bit of an edge case over there) to accomplish this are basically just having custom powers that follow the same basic formula as normal Devotions (aka Combo Disciplines aka Amalgams) but have Blood Potency requirements to create/learn. The end result isn't inherently all that different from Elder Disciplines (high BP characters have access to powers lesser vampires can't unless they fight their way up the ladder first, and they're more individual and variable than the normal Discipline progression), but the logic of designing and rating them is easier; resulting in a more consistent power level to them.

                        Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                        Calling this an "organizational" issue just doesn't hold water. It's not how the powers are organized, it's that you only get five of them, ever, and there's always more than five options, so you're always going to end up denying yourself access to some of the powers in a Discipline.
                        It is literally an issue of how each edition organizes its Disciplines, so whether you like it or not, it is an organizational issue. Like I say, if you rule it that you can buy powers at the cost of a Discipline dot, then there is no reason why you can’t have more than five powers. I guess that this is just a house rule but the spirit of V5 is that the game isn’t designed to be driven by a catalogue of powers - and it isn’t a problem for me personally - so it is just a solution to the issue outlined here and, as such it would need to be a house rule as it stands. Who knows, though, whenever V5 gets around to having an Elders sourcebook, they may end up ruling it themselves as it is seamless enough to implement.

                        This of course, as usual, skips the idea of play focused on the temptations of power.

                        The line between "superheroes with fangs" and "the power of vamprism is an insidious trap," is mostly in the the enforcement and execution of the downsides of being a vampire. But you can't make the temptations of being a vampire be a trap if the power fantasy aspect isn't there. It's very hard to do the whole, "young vampires think they're masters of the kine, and lords of the night, but those that survive quickly learn that the Curse of Caine takes far more than it gives," if you can't make characters that you can see feeling drunk on the power of being a vampire for a while, so when the flaws in the deal start to kick them in the teeth, it hits harder than vampires feeling completely cursed from night one, and their few powers never feeling like it was worth it.

                        So, again, as I said in my opening, a reason to go V20 is better support for multiple playstyles and approaches to the game. Because again, we're not here to argue why V5 is good, we're here to answer what about V20 would make someone consider trying it or adopting it after starting with V5.
                        Well 'temptations of power’ isn’t much of a theme if there are no downsides to it - it is just an excuse to play out a power fantasy. All fine in a game like D&D or Champions, or whathaveyou, but the Vampire game is billed as being a game of personal horror. If the powers outweigh the downside of vampiric existence in the game, it ceases to be a horror game. And again, this is just my view - but I fundamentally believe that losing any notion of ’superheroes with fangs’ in V:TM is a good thing. I flatly do not value it as a play style that Vampire needs to support - there are other games better adapted for that sort of thing.

                        Moreover, the PCs still get powers, but the Disciples system is organized in a different way. If people want to play that out as a theme - ’temptations of power’ - it is still a possibility. It just doesn’t work the way it used to. I know the players in my group are enthused by the powers their characters have at their disposal.

                            The thing with disciplines in V5 is that every dot, even if you took a level 1 power, increases the dice pool of the entire discipline and makes the whole package more powerful. A level 1 fortitude power taken as the 5th dot will increase your fortitude dice pool from 4 to 5 and will give you 5 boxes of health. This is quite amazing.                                 Another thing that V20 and prior excelled at was that the factions pulled you towards diverse, interesting and challenging philosophies. V5 has stripped it down to a dichotomy of order and freedom, with some heavy overtones of mortal politics; The Anarchs are champions of liberty, The Camarilla champion conservatism; The Ministry of Set Champion Freedom, The Church of Set Champion control; House Tremere champions tradition, House Carna celebrates deviancy. Factions are all yings and yangs, sometimes to the point of caricature: both church and ministry would be considered heretical extemes by the original Followers of Set which they stem from, but there's no middle-ground orthadox possition in line with what 90% of the followers believed before the split. It's very partisan.

                                V20 didn't do that; The Anarchs weren't the polar opposite of the Camarilla, and nor where the other major sect that went missing between editions, the Sabbat. Each side had their merits and flaws, they seemed natural, politically and sociologically viable*. They didn't feel tailor made to someone's viewpoint, and it was an interesting challenge to conform and wrap yourself around the ideals of your sect, where you are somewhat encouraged to play characters that don't have the same opinion as you rather than indulge in your comfort zone (playing a character with the same opinion as you is fine, but you gotta be smart about it, and that 'being smart about it' creates intriuge. Every major and minor sect has very interesting ideas that move beyond the scope of simply mortal politics run vicariously through vampires (Though I must admit V5's sections on feudal cities were pretty good)

                                *The Sabbat seem dysfunctional at first glance, but are evidently viable the more you read/understand the context in which they commit most of their crimes)

                                Speaking of philosophies, outside the camarilla (a few are inside) a vampire can choose to completely abandon the morality of Humanity and instead choose to follow a faith,creed or philosophy more amenable to their undead situation. Be it an outdated concept of chivalry, a dream of obtaining a state beyond vampirism, the idea that vampires are demons sent by god to test humanity and should act like it, an aim to become in tune with their beast, or dedicating their whole being to the god Set; Vampires can completely run up the wall with alien mindsets and strange ideas and can truly become monsters or at least interesting. This system of paths is also good in that they're set mechanically, so you're not doomed to haggling with your storyteller over convictions and chronicle tenets, though the concept is broad enough that you could adjust paths if you can agree to it.

                                            I actually enjoy V20's character creation, whilst V5's character creation is... cumbersome. But credit where it's due, the V5 character creation system is more balanced. The primary issue is v20 letting you allocate dots relatively freely while also charging incrementally higher XP rates as you go up a level. So with Attributes costing 4x current level in v20, but the ability to allocate 7/5/3 dots as you please, it's much better to do something like 5/4/1 , 4/3/1, 4/1/1 rather than something more balanced like 4/3/3, 3/3/2, 2/2/2. Let's take the 4/1/1 VS 2/2/2. If you wanted 4/2/2, it'd take the optimiser 8xp, and the balanced character 20xp, and this adds up significantly when you do it to the entire character sheet. The way V5 does things prevents such abuse, but it is a bit too regimented and difficult and plainly doesn't grant the freedom of V20. A more elegant sollution would be to cancel the incrimentally higher XP rates for attributes and abilities, but I must say I like incrementals for disciplines, and V5's system of 2/1/0 for disciplines is a very good idea (Though it gets screwed up by predator types offering an additional discipline dot, because 3/1/0, 2/2/0 and 2/1/1 are not equal in XP)
                                            While V5 has an interesting system in advantages (IE it's restrictive but that cuts down on the bullshit players pull) I do like v20's system of 15 freebies and merits/flaws, even if that's not very balanced and encourages the players to spend far too long working out how to best optimise merits and flaws. Also, quite plainly, some of the merits and flaws are broke, and I've had players tell me they always pick X because it's free points and it's not like it'd effect them anyway (I don't allow a lot of them)

                                                        My 'opinions' for a few things that make V20 worth picking up.
                                                        -Vampires are more powerful in V20; Ancient vampires are much stronger than young vampires, who are much stronger than mortals. There's a lot more verticality and it's great for playing plucky underdogs or indulging in the occasional power fantasy.
                                                        -Obfuscate and Dominate is really, really fun in V20. V5 clamped down on them in the name of balance, but here they're unhinged wonders ready to stomp all over your enemies. There's so much creativity you can pull off with these badboys in V20.
                                                        -Combat in V20 is less abstract and more simulationist. I like that a lot. It's a lot more tactical and if you're big into fighting IRL you can choose to get nitty-gritty with grapples and such. It's quite intuitive so that's a big plus, but slow without houserules
                                                        -A chill metaplot, crazy world shaking things aren't happening all the time, leaving you to focus on your local setting and whatever's cooked up inside it. V20 is pretty timeless and works great whether you want to play in 68, 1068, 1568, 1968, 2068 or 2168.
                                                        -More inter-sect politicing; V5 heavily emphasizes Red VS Blue, and while V20 certainly has that, a good chunk of your plots are going to be Camarilla Young VS Camarilla old, perhaps you'll join in on a plot to make someone the new prince or you'll uncover a cult which becomes a powder-keg. If you're in another sect, there's lots and lots of subfactions available for intrigue or infighting.
                                                        -The Sabbat is a cocaine blender for when you're bored of pretending to care about humanity, but also a deep and nuanced... they're interesting. Very interesting. You'll have people at your table arguing that they're the good guys, when they're clearly not; That kind of interesting
                                                        -I just like the way V20 does the beast better. I like control, messy criticals are not for me, and I think they make the setting/masquerade a bit suspect. V20 makes me more responsible.
