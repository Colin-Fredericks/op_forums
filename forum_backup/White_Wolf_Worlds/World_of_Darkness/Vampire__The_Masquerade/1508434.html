    Do your games have a singular truth (eg, Noddism) or do you prefer to mix it up?

    04-15-2023, 09:07 AM
    Not to be confused with canon, just what you like to use for your games regardless of what canon says.
    For example, some people prefer to leave it vague and unexplored (because whether the first vampire was Caine or Sutekh or made by a god or the Wyrm or science or whatever is utterly irrelevant to today's plot to assassinate the Prince and replace her with a figurehead), or else have the events of the Book of Nod as the 100% truth for the origin of vampires (which seems to be the default as far as I've found). I have yet to see somebody mention using an alternative vampire religion as The Truth.

    Personally, I usually go agnostic, but when I'm playing my more occult focused games (Sabbat, Setite, Bahari, etc) I tend to switch between truths as it serves the story. Sometimes the Book of Nod and the Revelations of the Dark Mother are more on point, and sometimes they're the propaganda of the Aeons. Sometimes it's more complicated than that, sometimes there is in fact a scientific reason, sometimes all vampires are limited by their lack of understanding of the scope of the universe and they're actually the prodigals the Fey say they are or something like that.
    Sometimes I think I need to get into Exalted and really mix things up, particularly in regards to games focusing on the Hecata bloodlines and the Neverborn.

    Prone to hyperbole

      One singular truth rarely ever matters in my games, we never got that deep into the cosmology. I usually assume a version of the Book of Nod is the truth, although no one in my games has access to a full version and even if they have excerpts they are transcripts who may (or may not) be altered with personal / cultural biases.

      My favorite clan are the Followers of Set but I never made their mythologie the truth, I also don't think it takes anything away from them if their beliefs are "false" from a player or ST standpoint.

      I have to point out though we never play games in the modern nights. We play in the Dark Ages or in Antiquity. With the latter I have to do a lot of Worldbuilding myself or I rely on people who are better at it than me (Thank you ST-Vault Athors!).

      As an ST I am a bit allergic to doing crossover with other splats so I never take other Origin Stories into account when I am writing my chronicles no matter which splat we are currently playing. Which is a problem sometimes because one of my players always wants to play characters who research other supernaturals. I usually try to accomodate him but I have my limits ^^' This might change a bit in my upcoming mesopotamian chronicle where I will try to be a bit more experimental.

                      I determined a specific truth on the origin of vampires for my chronicles simply so that, if needed, I knew how to present certain aspects of lore. It's not any one story as presented in the sourcebooks, but something that makes sense to me which could still explain why most of the myths developed as they were.

                      However, I would never present it as a single, incontrovertible truth for my players to discover. It's just a tool to help me as ST. I prefer to run more mundane oriented chronicles so the events of what happened 7000-12,000 years ago just cannot be settled. Even for my NPCs I have a rule that no one who has ever met one of the antedeluvians are even active. At the very best, you only have second hand accounts, and it is very unlikely for the PCs to meet even one vampire with actual second hand knowledge.

                      I think it is best to let players believe anything they want and to have their PCs believe they want without the ST going, "Nah, you're wrong!" Plenty of NPCs spend their entire immortal existence being wrong to no harm to them, so there isn't any reason why PCs cannot do the same. If they do find some piece of actual evidence, while it might give an important detail, by itself it won't validate or disprove any of the theories.

                      I think the only time it'd be relevant to the actual story is if I did a straight up end of the world, Gehenna chronicle, and it is extremely unlikely I'll ever do that.

                                          Every Kindred loves a good myth, in my take - at the very least to make sense of their existence as a bloodsucking monster. And most love telling the myth of themselves and their origin.

                                          Noddism was the big thing back in the middle-ages, and now remains as whispers among the Kindred of the Camarilla. The Camarilla has gone "secular", with Princes and Elders taking the seat of ancient beings that is the current generations distant Sires, the ones with power over life and death and the ones who secretly may eat young Kindred because kine blood will not satiate them.
                                          Before Caine, there were other myths. This is particularly true for all the "independent" Clans, the Ravnos, Banu-Haqim/Assiamites and Ministry/Followers still have their own legacies more fresh in mind.
                                          Another myth was probably that of Brujah and Carthago, tainted by the Roman Kindred's victory. (I do not like Baali nor do I find stories of infernalism interesting, so I tend to cut those things out entirely), and could by the sounds of it be the spiritual successor of the second city.

                                          As for the origin, I tend to leave it somewhat undisclosed at the table. I do not have any game where the players will ever meet someone who does know, and even if they do, their memories would hardly be something to be relied on. Something I have noticed myself with getting older is that memories tend to float together, and it is sometimes hard to tell what actually happened twenty years ago versus what we spoke about doing. I have seen photographs of myself in events I do not remember attending. Ancients will likely have created or recreated a memory several times over, perhaps even more if they have changed their views over the millenium to adopt to their new point of view.

                                          I also find the generation thing a bit nonsense. In my version, the blood thickens with age, and generation (and power) is more a function of when you were embraced, fairly much like we mortals do generations, but with much wider spans. There are some anomalies, like a very ancient vampire will likely create a childer more powerful, and a very young fledgling someone weaker. So, a typical 12th or 13th generation would Sire another 13th generation. This is more to recreate the model presented in 1st and 2nd edition where the text wrote generations as age, but then rules-wise allowed you to jump straight to elder by taking the generation background. It is also more inline with how V5 character generation works, but allows a 13th generation neonate to be the recent Childe of the Prince, without forcing the Prince to be of 12th generation. I only keep the numbers because it makes sense out of the already written context.
                                          The more powerful blood will probably cite their embrace having always been from a mythical founder, and with the pure prowess of creatures such as Mithras, Michael, Ur-Shulgi, Japeth, Montano, Baba Yaga, Yorak etc, few would dare to question it, at least to their face. Others, such as Menele and Helena may be more questionable. The truth is probably a lot more complex, with usurpation of Sires, murder and narcissistic rewriting of history about their person.
                                          I do tend to think that the idea of there being Antedeluvians, or 3rd generation, to be true, but that the original vampires are the 2nd generation. Exactly how vampirism came about is another question, but the modern take on an Abrahamic God is not one, just a later adoption with the spread of Christianity and justifying ones existence within that paradigm. Perhaps they were proto-mages, delving to deep into what was between life and death. Perhaps they were never among the Kindred themselves, but created the Antedeluvians to serve as their relentless enforcers/toys/experiements. Like the story goes, there was likely an uprising the "2nd generation" was slain. Perhaps one of them survived, and cursed them and later become the storied 'Caine'. Perhaps that is just later fiction. More likely, they were potent enough to influence their own bloodline, or perhaps it mutates of its own power depending on the will of everyone part of it.
                                          There are probably countless stories, all with a grain of truth to them going both before and from that point.

                                          There is no Calling. The truth is rather simple, I can easily add or remove Kindred that influence the type of story I want to tell. Its easy to create stories about characters that makes them seem more powerful than they are. A vampire from the 15th century is old. If they lie and claim they are from the Roman times, few can question them and may even believe in their story.

                                          "Anarch-revolts" are cyclic. The latest one is the biggest in memory, but its is not the only. The one before that was probably in the 6th century, which saw the Caine myth take hold as more and more embraced were now christians. The Elders that were the "victims" of the last revolt, were the winners of the one before that.

                                          While it has been my favorite faction, I am removing the Sabbat from V5 all together than permanent-Jyhad/Calling. The movement simply pewtered out after Thorns. While some die-hard cells remained, eventually they returned to the fold or remained independent, some even forming the hardcore backbone of the sleeping Anarch movement. A lot of the Noddists remains here, bitter about the Camarilla removing it's importance. So it comes that the rebels becomes the upholder of old values.

                                          As a sidenote, I've reversed vampiric blood and diablerie. Vitae is vile to the taste, much like ash. It is impossible to disguise it, but there are certainly individuals who have acquired a taste for it, in particular long-time ghouls and certain elders. As for diablerie, instead of giving power, it loosens the restrictions of the curse. For a short time period following the diablerie, the diablerist instead treats their blood potency as that of their victims instead of their own for the purpose of feeding and banes if they were more powerful than the victim. If the victim had no blood potency (ie thin-blooded), this even allows the elder to walk in sunlight. A younger Kindred slaying an Elder gains nothing but a price on their head.
                                          While this removes some of the intent with Elders being fearful of youngin' taking their power - the threat still remains. The only way to open up a position in management is the retirement of one of the current, and as they do not die of old age or generally move around, that means Elders have all rights for paranoia.
                                          These two points raises more questions about some clans than others, such as the Tremere, but once again there was probably a lot of Magick involved - and maybe not is what it seems even with the Warlocks. Despite their Elders millenium younger than other Clans, they still command the same respect. My take is that they were already used to a cutthroat environment and faking it until they made it in their new one. Elders resurfacing in the body of their slayer is a commonly perpetuated myth to keep Elder slayings down to minimal.

                                          It's a myth that all vampires are Sired. Many thin-bloods rise by their own violation, which is something the Camarilla refuses to accept as a fact, instead taking the opportunity to strike at their opponents power base, setting them up as having illegally sired if all things go well with the scheming. The only truth is that no one rises if there are not Kindred around, but as the subject is both taboo and contested, no actual research is done. Thin-blooded eventually cease to be thin-blooded, and can take on the traits of a Clan or more likely become Caitiff.
                                          Kindred of the East are simply vampires - call them a clan if you want - that are unable to Sire, but are able to help the transition from thin-blooded to full Kindred much easier.

                                          By the same token, it is a myth that Kindred need to feed on blood. They feed on life. A slowly traced mouthful of blood from an experienced Elder can provide the same nourishment as ait does a fledgling draining someone completely dry - and with the same lethal effect. Few Kindred bother or realizes they can develop other methods to feed, another separation from the Kindred of the East.

                                          Some myths based on clans/bloodlines:
                                          Banu Haqim/Assamites - Their story about being judges is probably made up on their own chamber when the warriors took charge. Their true origin is probably as seers and sorcerers.
                                          Brujah/True Brujah - There was no usurpation by Troile, the True Brujah has gotten it all wrong. There was a story in the Gehenna book about them claiming to be from an Antedeluvian that choose to never Sire, which I found amusing.
                                          Gangrel - Myths and legends mostly.
                                          Hecata - The Clan of Death has a history of purging itself, and making the most fancy stories about it. I'm just rolling with that the clan has always been Hecata, just under different management. Perhaps the founder is dead, and perhaps not. Perhaps they have been for a long time, and there simply been different factions taking turns at holding the reins.
                                          Lasombra - Their founder is presumed dead. Gratiano and Montano are considered its heirs, pending on points of view.
                                          Malkavian - Never been one for the "madness network", so removed that. The founder is likely to be presumed dead from an early "anarch-revolt".
                                          Ministry - Their myths point Set as one of the original vampires, and that they stand above all other Kindred. The Corruption theme is a myth brought on a few millenium ago, in truth Set is a warrior, and would likely purge their clan should they awaken.
                                          Nosferatu - Nictuku are probably a myth, or some other creatures all together. May even be two different bloodlines that went to war in the fog of ages. It is possible that the founder of the Nosferatu was slain by the creature commonly referred to Absimiliard, and the ancient Jyhad still rages on, while they myth states they are the same, because it is a better story.
                                          Ravnos - While I can not say that I am a fan of the week of nightmares, I am even less a fan of the Ravnos (mostly by the way they have been portrayed at the tables I have played at, to be fair). I would scrub the week of nightmares, and place the death of their founder somewhere in the 19th century, which in turn shattered the clan. Or perhaps they have awakened, and is slowly stalking their clan. Their bane is simply their blood telling them to never stay still, or they will be eaten.
                                          Toreador - Most myths about Arikel are just that. While the Clan nowadays are filled with a passion to surround themselves with beauty, they once took pride in themselves being the object of desire, like the story of Helena of Troy.
                                          Tremere - I hold it to my personal truth that they recreated ancient magick. It has not been done since. While they certainly experimented on Tzimisce vampires, that larger influence was that they became Kindred rather than their own type of undead, which was more likely to be the intent. They never killed the Salubri for their blood, but to take their position. Just like within the Order, there were just that many seats at the table, so they had to vacate one and set to their task with grim determination.
                                          Tzimisce - Their founder is presumed dead in the last anarch revolt.
                                          Ventrue - Many believe that the founder died along time ago. Perhaps they did.

                                          Salubri - Their paragon virtue is a recreation after they were gone. They were no better or worse than anyone else. They rubbed against Assamites in trying to be the judges of Kindred, they rubbed against the Hecata for trying to delve into death and they were scorned by the other High Clans because they tried to presume they where the righteous leadership of the Kindred. The Tremere had easy pickings. Much of the myth comes from individuals, regrets from influential individuals or simply enemies of Tremere.
                                          Baali - Just another name for Brujah.
                                          Most other bloodlines are simply mutations of the blood from a particularly potent ancient, and likely exaggerate their own origin and myths for self-importance.

                                              I like to do a bit of a regional-focus, where many of the myths are not only spoken of but have clues and shadows of proof. But outside of that area (country, continent, what have you), there may be some similarities here and there (like Babylonian, Egyptian and Greek myths echoes), but many radical differences. So little to none global truths.
                                              That is the same in my other settings. .... A bit differently, since I really mixed them up (in Mage I use Awakening origin, Werewolf have affiliation only to the Moon, not to Gaia... etc)

                                              But I go through the principle that I really like from Demon the Fallen. Where Reality once was many things (everything) at the same time (for example: daeva, devil, espirit, angel, azura, akuma, where all the same). Like, EVERYTHING was true, at the SAME TIME. But with the end of the era of divinity, be what it is, reality fragmented, and these thing became different, separeated, alienated. So, the world is kinda like a pastiche, of such a cosmically complexity as a whole, but understandable in smaller scale, that you think you are piecing a truth (Noddist, Setite, Laibon, Wan Kuei, Mummy, Drowned Legacy...) but you are seeing a mere angle of a shard.
                                              So yeah, kinda of a cheat.

                                              It is as has been said here in the thread. This is not something the PCs and Players interact with much. I never give concrete evidences. It is always second account, it is a mental or spiritual sighting, or blanketed through different lenses. Besides giving some different ideas that challenge hegemony (like, hey, it seems there were more than 13 Clans in Enoch, and not all current ones were there). The difficulty is keeping everything consistent, and letting Players piece the apocryphal and the canon in their heads.

                                              Strange... When coincidence seems too convenient, I prefer to call it fate.

                                              -Blood Omen: Legacy of Kain d=
