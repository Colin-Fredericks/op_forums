    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    From my perspective, the Brujah have always been primarily Anarchs in terms of numbers. It's just the Camarilla stupidly considered all Anarchs part of the Camarilla.
    On paper the Camarilla always claimed that they were the universal vampire organization and that every vampire in the world was part of the Camarilla. This made some sense since the only real goal for the Camarilla was to keep knowledge of vampires away from humanity. All of the Traditions and rules were simply there to reinforce the Masquerade and to prevent conflict between vampires that would eventually spill across to humanity.

    Of course in practice, you had vampires like the Sabbat who were opposed to the Camarilla, or groups like the Anarchs who rejected the Camarilla and you had vampires in other parts of the world that had never even heard of the Camarilla. Still, the idea was that any vampire from any clan or bloodline from anywhere in the world could show up in a Camarilla city, and they'd be treated like any other member of the Camarilla provided they followed the Traditions. Ultimately the Camarilla was formed to be an inclusive organization. So Anarchs were a part of it, but that wasn't stupid, that was intentional.

    Ultimately driving other vampires and geographies out of the sect just makes it harder for the Camarilla to quickly respond to Masquerade breaches. And again, the Camarilla was created under the concept that "vampires need to stand together or get individually burned to death by humanity" like they did during the Inquisition. It's really only been in 5th edition that the Camarilla has gone more from inclusive to exclusive, and that's kind of stupid because now it's just going to be that much harder to deal with a breach of the Masquerade when it occurs in territory or by vampires that the Camarilla has officially disavowed.

                            It's worth remembering that it comes off like a built in goal with the V5 Camarilla that it isn't to be some carefully thought out (by which I mean, the writers carefully thinking out how it would need to function to reconcile with detail x, y or z) tangible entity that functions within internal setting constraints. It's mostly to be a thematic antagonist punching bag for the Anarchs as a commentary on society's ills. So you can say "but logically" or even "but your own writing now says", it's just, even if pointed out, I doubt that would get anything but a reply of "yeah, see how short sighted and unthinking the Camarilla are now?" as an intended point. The individual details that seem glaring to you, like for instance a supposed sect of elites having multiple clans of wildly varying situations, come off as completely irrelevant to them.

                            If there's one thing you can say about V5, it's that serving the meta themes and images comes first, and thinking everything else out comes second.

                            Listen, it's not like taking this approach always fails. By which I mean, do you enjoy Baz Luhrman movies? (I absolutely do). That is a guy who is one million percent not thinking about how something sensibly comes together or reconciles with its own plot construction, even just from the details of his own writing. He is telling some story of broad, powerful themes and images with lush, lavish imagery and set pieces, and not one where he cares if you get hung up on whether the specifics made any damn sense. He gets away with this on sheer, impossible, incredible, grab you by the face, style. And if there has ever been a game that is style first, it is VtM.

                            Of course, to put it lightly, Vampire the Masquerade 5th edition books are not the movies of Baz Luhrman (also even his movies don't *always* work, a few of them just collapse under their own weight or can't quite paper over some baffling thing/terrible decision).

                                                I will talk some specifics that relate to my actual irl job, plus answer a direct request. Otherwise I think the matter was debated more than enough.

                                                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                Yes, they are fleeing from their territories but in a modernized economy, it's not like their bank accounts and investments will be effected.
                                                They will, specially if you consider how vampires own things (through proxies).

                                                First, most wealth in the world does not exist as money, it exists as a long list of assets, specially as property and real estate. While you can easily transfer their formal ownership in paper, the actual assets exist as physical objects, often immobile and too frequently hard to transport. That's true for any millionaire and even more so for those who operate outside the law, as such assets can have their actual control and ownership unbound from legal possession.

                                                Second, investments are... complex. You can't simply liquidate or transfer them at your leisure without loss, sometimes at all. Realizing loss is one of the worst things you can do, and you usually have to when liquidating pronto due to an unforeseen emergency. Some you can't withdraw from earlier than agreed upon. Some are extremely volatile and can cause actual loss (not only lose the whole invested money, but also end up with debt after that) if you can't follow through.

                                                But the biggest issue is that for influence and direct control, speciallythe kind of control the Lasombra like, nothing beats proximity. As a vampire your real asset isn't to nominally own something, but to be able to direct how it works (or not). Nominating and Dominating CEOs, collecting protection in money and blood, and so on. The eye of the master fattens the horse, and if this isn't completely true for modern legit businesses, it is completely so for most non-legit ones, and doubly for the likes of the undead as described in VtM.

                                                Add proxies to the equation and things get a lot worse. It's not just about accessing your assets anymore, but accessing intermediaries that nominally own those assets or directly manage them. If the proxy is cut off, then you have problem. If another vampire take hold of your proxy, then you also have problems. Those that stay behind can destroy, cripple, or achieve effective informal control, or steal the control from you, with little you can do from afar since you don't have direct legal ownership of anything.

                                                Now, this isn't to say that every situation is equal and it will never go well. As any complex situation, you can have a lot of outcomes between profit and total bankruptcy. Some Lasombra may have came out of that better than they were before, and a very few may be quite a lot better as they took unusual opportunities. But to address the group in general we have to fallback to averages, and it is unlikely that the clan as a whole didn't lost a lot.

                                                Just to be clear: my intent here isn't to "beat a dead horse" or anything like that, but to explain some of the nuances involved in the actual process of transferring wealth for whomever wants to use it and/or start from this to learn more. I wish I could have got a job in my actual original academic area, but since I got employed in a bank, I really learned the ins and outs of the financial system.

                                                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                How would you have handled the defection? Serious question.
                                                Let's get from the point they were in the actual setting, regardless of my opinion in this point: the Camarilla ditched two clans and is on the lookout for wealth and strength, the Lasombra defectors know they have to leave the Sabbat pronto and with protection for the time being.

                                                As much as they can boast and hide from each other during the exchanges and meetings, they know each other's basic needs at least, but also that they need to keep each other at arm's length. They need some guarantees against each other. But also need each other specifically even more so for the fact that they don't: the Lasombra going to the Anarchs is still plainly on the table, and while no one at it wants that option, if they can't reach an agreement it will be it (hence why asking for or accepting genocide isn't an option).

                                                Some back and forth and several nights later, the Lasombra offer the most prized possession: knowledge. They'll teach the Tremere the Sabbat Ritae, so they can together devise countermeasures for some of them, above all the Vinculum. They need a way to strip their own Vinculum and, they know it, be Bound to some Camarilla members. That's a guaranteed outcome for some, no reason to fight it. Other Ritae go as a bonus, even if the negotiation fail it is now in their best interest to teach the Camarilla the secrets of the Sabbat and let them fight each other.

                                                In exchange, the Lasombra demand some specific positions. Nothing too high, but positions meant to ease the process of defection. A Justicar of their own, an Archon under two or three other Justicars, a liaison to the Tremere to discuss matters of Abyss Mysticism and another to the Inner Council acting as military advisor against the Sabbat, then insist that at least some important cities get to have a member of the clan at any position the local Prince wants, preferably as enforcers. Those are all good positions to negotiate because they're politically dead, but key to ease things up and make the defection safer for the clan as a whole. Others will try later to acquire true power, they can't push too much now.

                                                Finally whatever comes from the Vinculum-Breaking ritual is prepared and the actual process of defection begin. The Camarilla will ease the transference or recuperation of assets, but will confiscate most of it whenever it can, this is the actual price to be paid. Here is where Princes will most exert their individual policies, some may demand only a token tax and offer ample help, others may go for it all. Some Princes may still add to the demands, and most will offer "tax exemptions" in exchange of staked Sabbat elders, enough to force the Lasombra to burn bridges, but usually not enough to jeopardize the whole process, a single important Sabbat elder buys out entry for several clan members (or only one really rich and powerful).

                                                Personally I would make the Vinculum-Breaking somewhat unreliable on the Bonding side, it makes the Vinculum break as intended, but sometimes the Bonding protection remains for a time. The Tremere is aware of that, but being unable to tell with certainty who's Bonded and who's not plays well for both sides, as otherwise they would have to debate who'll be subject to the Blood Bond. Now it is hard to betray each other as they don't know who'll ultimately play in which side, better to just get along. After a while I would have a similar process for the reclamation of abandoned Sabbat cities. The defection itself would have some skirmishes, but would mostly be as silent and quick as possible, without much time dedicated to hunting people and with many Lasombra being subject to the breaking of the Vinculum to join the defectors (or not).
