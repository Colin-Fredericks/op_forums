        Okay, I'm going to be obnoxiously self-indulgent here but discussing Chicago by Night characters IS MY JAM. This is like putting an unknown spellbook in front of a Tremere or your special blood type in front of a Ventrue.

        Dickie Fulcher

        I consider Dickie Fulcher just to be another delinquent thug with no aspirations other than it's fun to give the finger to the Man and get revenge on the people who killed his sire/lover. He was a troubled kid who was embraced at the age of 15 by another vampire who was 14, probably because she thought Dickie would be a good boyfriend as she thought he was cute and both liked punk music. This was not a guy who had any real ideas about political philosophy. He's a soccer hooligan without the soccer.
        Dickie is actually an interesting case of "read between the lines" from CbN1 to CBN2. The thing about Dickie is threefold:

        1. He is not a leader but he KNOWS that.
        2. Dickie is actually a monster.
        3. Dickie is best friends with a vampire saint.

        The story of Dickie Fulcher is that he's a Caitiff Anarch punk in the most authentic 1970s punk way that found Maldavis, taught her the ways of the Force, and proceeded to be exceeded by her in every way. He is someone who is fantastically loyal to her because he knows she's smarter, more charismatic, and just plain "better" than him. In the mortal world, Maldavis might have been a Kamala Harris or AOC or killed because it's the World of Darkness. Dickie is waiting for her to get her groove back and save the world.

        Except, there's the slight issue Dickie is a serial killer. He has Humanity 4 but it's probably dropping well past that because he shows up at suburban homes, murders everyone inside, and then stays there until presumably the bodies stink or someone notices. He's destined for wightdom but that's only if Maldavis doesn't find out that her strongest supporter is a mass murderer of middle class surbuban WASPS.

        That was an easy story hook if you were an Anarch. "Do you tell Maldavis her best friend is a monster or handle it yourself? Do you care?"

        Anita Wainwright

        I disagree heavily. While Anita was certainly an important Anarch, there is nothing in her background that associates her with actual anarchism which would be the point of Salvador's &quot;Brotherhood&quot; (or whatever you want to call it).

        I always imagined her as someone who would have been with the Weather Underground sending out mailbombs to blow up &quot;the pigs&quot; if she had not become a vampire. Those people were radical leftists, but weren't any kind of anarchists. Her biography shows her as someone who was becoming increasingly radical left wing. But she didn't want to eliminate hierarchy, just establish a new hierarchy that would be more &quot;just&quot; according to her political ideology, most of which would require some kind of strong state. She'd be closer to Leninism than anarchism.
        Well the thing is we don't actually have to think about what Anita's politics are as she's ideologically a committed Black Panther member. It's interesting how her politics have aged as that organization, so villified by Nixon and the FBI, has been re-examined in light of being less radical in many places and more radical in others. The fact Anita is a white woman is one of those funny "jokes" that you could bring up in game. Not just because, again, Anita being white while her biggest rival (Maldavis) is a black woman but also because the Black Panther Party has a great deal of traditionalist gender views.

        In my write-up of Evelyn Stephens for LET THE STREETS RUN RED, I actually stated that Evelyn Stephens kind of despised Anita for this and this was why the Gary Anarchs didn't ally with the Anarch Center.

        But one of the things I liked about C5 is that Anita is a High Humanity vampire that has the Brujah love of violence and hatred of authority that COULD have made her more radical but resulted in her ending up becoming the Den Mother to the Anarchs of Chicago. She's stepped up and become the kind of Anarch leader they SHOULD be and most want. A leader who looks after their needs, beats down the worst elements, and behaves as first among equals as opposed to a tyrant king. She's probably about the best Anarch leader you could get right now but she's not flawless. For one, she's completely misread Critias and he's likely to knock her head off if she ever gets him alone.

        Likewise, she burns with jealousy over Maldavis who is still a legend even if she's becoming increasingly more like Modius (a useless has been) than turning things around.

        Gengis

        I feel the need to bring up Gengis because C5 is pretty much perfect for showing the various trajectories of how the Anarch functions in V:TM society.

        1. Anita Wainwright illustrates vampires who rise to the occassion of being rabel rousing leaders and heroes (of the Greek sort). Your Salvador Garcias, Jeremy MacNeils, and the Nines Rodriguez.

        2. Maldavis and Modius (remember he was an Anarch) end up becoming broken by failures and revolutions before retreating into their own little worlds.

        3. Damien, Sheriff, and Cecily Bain illustrate the Anarchs who just finally say "Fuck It" and join the Camarilla to become what they most despise.

        4. Juggler, Callahan, Torque, and Gengis illustrate another part of Anarch vs. Camarilla society, which is, The Anarch Moderate or Anarch Puppet.
        Basically, there's a decent argument that some Anarch Barons and leadership exist as a double bind layer to the Camarilla's control. Juggler secretly answers to Tyler who is a Primogen member of the group. Gengis answers to Kevin Jackson who showers him with praise and resources while treating him as a peer if not an equal.

        A lot of Anarchs in a city will be nothing more than another tool for the Elders to keep the Anarchs under their thumb. They will get pointed at their enemies and also just harmless acts of defiance.

            Before I move on, I want to note some things that had not occurred to me before. Not about Anarch domains as such, but more details on some characters important to the Free States.

            We know Jeremy MacNeil fled to the Colonies in the late 18th century and participated in events during the American War of Independence. Louis Fortier would have been in New York City during this time as well. It's highly likely these two vampires knew of each other and perhaps even met before LA. It might explain why Fortier defended MacNeil before Don Sebastian and why he was willing to join MacNeil in an attack alongside the Anarchs. Was this truly just noblesse oblige? Or was this done because at one point they were friends during the American Revolution?

            Another possible reason for the two to know each other is that this was the time when the Sabbat was losing their "control" of North America to the Camarilla. They were in civil war, and the Sabbat was already incredibly deranged and becoming increasingly centralized- far removed from their role as Anarchs. It's possible these two might have cooperated in struggles against the Sabbat that cleared the way for more Kindred to arrive, or at least heard of each other's reputation as a result. Having some earlier tie - in any way - I think would have strengthened their relationship and explain why Fortier helped MacNeil and why MacNeil continues to support him. Plus... well the Scots and French are old allies, aren't they?

            It's also highly likely that MacNeil would have met the newly embraced Crispus Attucks at this time (as well as his sire, Marguerite Foccart who was also a neonate at the time) Marguerite's sire was Robin Leeland who is associated with northern England. It's highly likely Crispus knew Robin during his time as a Highland rebel. He might have served as a protector to Leeland's childe and her childe out of respect or paying back a favor he owed. Attucks' relationship with MacNeil might be very old and could very well have extended to Fortier. It makes sense that a natural rebel like MacNeil would arrive in Boston during this time as that city was the hotbed of discontent with the British.

            It is one of the failings of Dark Colony is that it doesn't mention these specific Brujah or any legacy characters associated with them. Sure, it makes generic mention of Brujah in Boston, but all three of these characters had already been detailed in Clanbook: Brujah published the year before. The authors' decision to place the crux of the book a "British invasion" in the late 19th century instead of the earlier colonial era with some established lore seems even more of a mistake now.

            I also find it a bit disappointing that Montreal By Night, published much later, did not make use of Fortier. Its new character of Ventrue Kyle Strathcona, formerly of the Camarilla and later joining the Sabbat, was not somehow tied to Louis Fortier. Per canon, there cannot be any relationship between the 8th generation Strathcona who did not arrive in New World until around 1760 after the French and Indian War (the Seven Years War to you non-Americans), and the 9th generation Louis Fortier (embraced in 1754 by a mysterious female Indian called Night Star). How many Ventrue could possibly be in New France in the colonial period? Having independent Ventrue bloodlines in French Canada this early was a mistake IMHO. It was a big mistake to not tie these characters together somehow. Maybe Strathcona could have arrived in the New World earlier and embraced Night Star, who in turn embraced Fortier? It's not necessary, but it seems like a lost opportunity to me. I'm actually not a big fan making connections to existing characters just to do so, but I do like connections/lineage that make sense. (In any case, Fortier would have never known his sire or her sire, as he was abandoned immediately after embrace.) An Indian vampire at this point of time is most likely, but not necessarily, of the Sabbat line. The Sabbat arrived first and embraced the first Indians. So regardless of Fortier's exact lineage, it means most likely Fortier is of the line of the Ventrue anti-tribu (unbeknownst to him or anyone else). Not actually relevant to a discussion on his character, but I find it interesting.

            What is even more interesting is that Strathcona was written as one of the few Scottish Ventrue, embraced in 1514 and fairly isolated among the Toreador in Edinburgh. This means that Strathcona was also present in Scotland around the time of MacNeil's embrace and living as a Highland rebel, hating the Ventrue. These characters MUST know each other. And they should have a personal history together, likely as each other's antagonists. Furthermore, Strathcona would have left for the New World BEFORE MacNeil. Strathcona would become Prince of Montreal in 1760. MacNeil seems to have arrived during a period of colonial agitation that only began after French Canada became British. Did this have anything to do with MacNeil's decision? Strathcona did not publicly become Sabbat and turn over Montreal to the Sabbat until 1839 or so. Knowing the character of MacNeil, these two should have clashed at some point even if MacNeil never actually visited Montreal while it was ruled by the Camarilla. And since Fortier was in New York at this time as a loyal member of the Camarilla, he would have seen Strathcona as his enemy after this time even if he did not before. We don't know about MacNeil's presence around the 1840s, but I find it interesting that MacNeil and Fortier likely have an enemy in common. And MacNeil being a personal enemy of a Sabbat Cardinal is an interesting aspect of his character. (If you compare their stats, I think MacNeil has the edge.)

            This would also push MacNeil's reputation even further as an enemy of both the Sabbat and Camarilla, and well before the Sabbat's failed attack on the Free States. Specifics like this make him a more compelling character than the rather generic information we've been given.

            Of course, none of this is intentional. It's not mentioned in anyone's biography. They were written independently with likely no thought about the implications. But it makes complete sense if you take each character's biography seriously.

                ​​As part of my pillaging and reinterpretation of LAbN for creating my own version of the Free States in my chronicles, I am on to the next thing.

                I've already covered the personal philosophy of Jeremy MacNeil, making La Hermandad/the Brotherhood an actual anarchist secret society, and the "Chartist" movement that Louis Fortier belongs to. Now I'm moving on to Smiling Jack.

                He's an important Anarch. He was a former pirate in the Caribbean. He participated in the final stages of the revolt of Los Angeles. And he has a greater reputation among the Anarchs than even Jeremy MacNeil, "the Liberator." While MacNeil has a Rebel nature, that is only Smiling Jack's demeanor. His nature is "Bravo" - meaning he is a bully at heart who torments those weaker than him. Smiling Jack was never a pirate captain per canon, just a second mate. And he has less intelligence and less leadership than MacNeil, but equal to him in charisma and almost as good at manipulation.

                I see Jack as mainly someone who likes to fight, and he's probably done it more than MacNeil. This is a guy who likes to pick fights, and is quite good at it. He's not a coward, but prefers to fight people much weaker than him. Which means most of his fights with the Camarilla is probably at far smaller cities. While he did participate in the revolt in LA, we're told he arrived "during the last bloody stages" meaning when the battle was almost over. I don't see him as someone choosing to fight unless the odds are in his favor. He likely was never in Chicago during Lodin's purge, or if he was he left quickly.

                We know he travels on a big motorcycle and dresses like a biker. I imagine he and whatever crew he hangs out is basically a motorcycle gang. That reminds me of the Wolfpack in Chicago By Night, the coterie of the Archon Tyrus. I imagine one of Tyrus's responsibilities is to patrol the Midwest looking for Jack, and help the smaller Camarilla domains get rid of him should he show up. They are also near contemporaries of one another in terms of physical age and embrace dates. Tyrus has better stats in general, but like all the "authority" figures in CbN have weaker Disciplines than expected (which I think was a design choice). Tyrus interestingly has Bravo as his demeanor, but a nature as Director. I imagine these two and their vampire biker gangs have fought before, but it's mostly been chases and narrow escapes.

                I think Smiling Jack has one main advantage that cements his reputation among the Anarchs. Unlike most of them, he actually lived in a state similar to what many Anarchs would want, or at least idealize. And that is the so-called pirate republics of the 17th century. Smiling Jack would have been a member of the "brethren of the coast" and operated under some kind of articles of agreement. He and his shipmates would have written up their own code of conduct, establishing rules for discipline, division of treasure, leadership, compensation for injury, and so on. All members would have signed the articles and swore an oath to each other.

                I think that is a system that would have a lot of appeal among many Anarchs. Not all of course, but many would like its egalitarian principles, relative simplicity, voluntary compliance, flexibility, and effective means of dispute resolution and punishment. I think this would be the means he would use with whatever crew he is currently riding it, and it is what he would apply to any short lived Free State he might have created in the Caribbean or North America.

                Smiling Jack doesn't need to be any kind of political theorist. He just needs to apply things he already knows about and is used to. Furthermore, such an arrangement doesn't mean the Anarchs have any kind of centralized leadership. Every single Anarch domain or group just needs to have their own articles. Different groups of Anarchs would just learn to live among each other like different pirate crews did in the haven. The "Pirate Republic" in Nassau provides the best model for us. In so far they need some to deal out justice, the different crews (let's not call them gangs) will hammer out another set of articles and elect someone as Magistrate to keep order and dispense justice. If someone doesn't like things, they can leave.

                When Smiling Jack and his crew are in the LA Free States, they treat it similarly like pirates did in Port Royal, Jamaica. They stay for so long and then head back out to loot and plunder. Smiling Jack would probably like it if one of the Free States was modeled this system, but he lives with what he has. But he operates under his own articles, and this is what he probably teaches to a lot of Anarchs as the way to go. He's not actually opposed to other systems - provided they are not forced on him. The more intellectual Anarchs probably disrespect such a simple philosophy, and the physically weaker Anarchs fear the bullying of those who buy into it, but Smiling Jack doesn't care about them. He attracts physically strong vampires who want to do what they want, and like the relatively simple and effective organization it has.

                Jack would hate Fortier, and he probably dislikes the political purist bent and manifestos of Salvador Garcia and his gang of anarchists. He'd see Mohammed (or some other ganglord like him) as a threat, but would respect him as a strong leader and fighter. He should see MacNeil as a personal rival, but probably likes him the best. Too idealistic to be sure, but he likes MacNeil's reputation as a proven fighter and that he doesn't see to push his politics on anyone else. The "MacNeil Boys" might be a touch snobby, but they can be trusted. I imagine when his crew stays in LA, he camps out on MacNeil's grounds, not that of other people.

                We'll call this movement within the Anarchs "the Free Brethren". The different crews who operate under this system, with their own articles of agreement, likely come up with colorful names for their crews like the pirates of old.

                    Let's talk about Mohammed and the Crypt's Sons.

                    I am going to keep my comments on this post mainly about how other Anarchs view him and mostly ignore that he is a Sabbat infiltrator, though that may make another post. I'll just say that I actually don't think most of his gang is actually Sabbat. Sabbat members would be limited to just him and whatever childer he may have made. Most of his gang are likely non-Sabbat. Though by the time LAbN was published, he had about 30 years there, I don't think he could simply spam childer and raise them Sabbat. Indoctrination takes some time, and that is VERY risky when you are undercover. He also can't risk converting Anarchs to Sabbat because if he misjudges the situation, his cover is blown. I'm sure by hook or crook, he's managed to get 5 or so Sabbat from his gang of 25. But the rest are probably typical Anarchs destined to be early canon fodder when he makes his move, but before the Sabbat actually invade. He'll make sure they'll get killed while killing other Anarchs while his real pack is kept safe.

                    Another question I need to address is what clan does he pretend to be. As far as I can read, LAbN never states it. We know he first helped the Brujah wipe out his old war party, and that is a strong option. Probably better to appear Brujah than be a Caitiff. And since Mohammed "Of the Darkness" is just a little bit too cute for a Lasombra, I think it's better if he's actually known as Mohammed X in LA.

                    Many think the gang's name and its association with a real human gang is in poor taste, and I agree. But it can easily be made into a fictitious gang and divested of anything an ST finds problematic.

                    So what does it offer us? Well, his best role is what it appears to be - a group representing that element of the Anarchs which comprises real criminals: killers, diablerists, thieves, and other scum that fill up a lot of the Anarch ranks. In other domains controlled by the Camarilla where the Anarchs are a minority, such Kindred may represent a necessary concession to the ranks. If you are on the Prince's hit list, you can't complain about your allies. But LA is different, and Mohammed's gang is an embarrassment to most of the other Anarchs. They'd get rid of him except they waited too long and now they can't. There is just too many of them, Mohammed is too good a leader, and they're dangerous. It'd be a very destructive war. So everyone is hoping Mohammed is actually more reasonable than they fear.

                    Now Smiling Jack is also a thug and criminal. But I feel Smiling Jack is legitimately attracted to the free life that piracy once represented to him. Mohammed isn't in it for freedom. He's in this for power. Everyone understands that.

                    I imagine Mohammed started relatively small. Maybe with just the coterie he fell into in 1965. But his skills and ruthlessness enabled him to take over or wipe out local rivals until he dominated South Central LA.

                    Most of the vampires in LA are young, and neonates tend to carry their mortal lives with them. So there is a racial element. In a city whose Anarch vampire movers and shakers are mostly white or Hispanic, he's really the only major vampire leader who is black. So much of his support are black vampires regardless of their clan - Gangrel, Malkavian, Toreador, whatever. They feel more comfortable with him. Another source are the criminal scum who come fleeing to LA. They won't be welcomed elsewhere, and some can find a home with Mohammed provided they can be of use to him. If Gordon Keaton from Chicago fled to LA, I'm sure he could probably be useful to Mohammed even though he doesn't fit the normal profile as a white yuppie. Of course Gordon couldn't pretend to be a member of the mortal gangs that Mohammed controls. But he doesn't need to be. He can be useful in other areas while supporting Mohammed as vampire warlord of South Central. In fact, he could be very useful precisely because he brings skills and connections Mohammed otherwise wouldn't have. Other examples of CbN NPCs that could fit as part of Mohammed's group would be Dickie Fulcher and Gengis (although Gengis as a natural follower could be justified to appear in most other camps - it all depends on who got to him first).

                    An Anarch criminal or gang lord is a perfectly fine concept. There's no reason why it shouldn't be represented in LA. Even though it means Mohammed and his gang really doesn't offer the Anarch Movement any real model to replace the Camarilla unlike the other LA factions. He's just a feature of the local LA Free State scene. Everyone know he's just a powerful gang lord. Feared and respected in southern California, but meaningless to the movement as a whole. And Mohammed is fine with that. Why? Because he's the Duke of LA. He's A #1.


                    Now he can provide something useful to the Anarchs. He controls an actual gang that is expanding from its base in LA. Camarilla vampires extend their influence in mortal organizations all the time, so there's no reason why an Anarch can't. As the mortal gang connected to him spreads to other cities, it provides a means for the LA Anarchs to smuggle contraband or Anarchs to other cities. They just need to pay Mohammed for it. Now this is compromised somewhat by the fact that everyone knows this. When the local Camarilla hears that this gang is now operating in their city, they know what it means. But it's still useful. If you are an Anarch in another city that has this gang in it, and you need help, you might be able to utilize it for a price if you know the right people.

                    Mohammed has no real allies among the other Anarchs in LA. Any he once had are now either dead or coopted. While he acts nice to most of the other Anarch leaders in the Free States, they are on to his divide and conquer scheme now. Everyone knows that if Jeremy MacNeil wasn't there, that Mohammed would be the most likely candidate to be the "Prince" of LA. He's ambitious to spread his domain further because that is what a warlord needs to do to reward his men. If his "gang" - and his followers should really be the only actual gang in LA in my opinion - is to expand its members, he needs new feeding territories. Everyone knows that the territory of La Hermandad makes the most sense. Jesus Ramirez is doing his best to prepare, but he'll need help. Thankfully he does have allies in LA. And there is a very real threat that Salvador Garcia could mobilize the other members of the Brotherhood in other cities to move to LA as reinforcements.

                    So there's nothing special about Mohammed for the Anarch movement as a whole. But he's good local flavor in LA. An example of how the Anarchs can go really wrong. He and his gang can provide a lot to the setting of the Free States even without the secret ties to the Sabbat. If he didn't exist, I think he would need to be invented.

                    Edit: OK, I'm going to rule in my chronicles that Mohammed is popularly known as the "Duke" (compare to Fortier being known as the Baron) and colloquially called "A # 1" ever since the movie Escape from New York came out in 1981.

                            Elphilm Thanks for the kind words.

                            In terms of inspirations for the Anarch movement as a whole, I think that is it for LAbN. Everything else in the book is more like "local flavor". It might add something to the Free States, but doesn't add anything for Anarchs elsewhere. That isn't a bad thing. It's fine. There should be unique things about the Free States that don't matter elsewhere just as there should be other Anarch groups and activities that don't affect Southern California. I'll probably post something in the future looking at the Anarchs in Chicago By Night to see what we can grab, as the Anarchs there are different from those in LA. So a comparison would be interesting, and we might find some interesting inspirations.

                            But I'll be keeping my comments to LAbN for now to analyze some of its local flavor, mostly the less powerful local barons. In general, my pronouncement is it isn't very good. More than the characters being bland or indistinguishable from one another (though that too is a problem), the major problem is the book never addresses WHY anyone would follow any of these barons, or WHO exactly make up their followers.

                            I'll start with Steve "The Main Man" Booth and David Geduld. These two characters are linked so it's good to discuss them together. First, I have to say that I like both of them as characters. A charismatic vampire surfer dude is perfectly OK. Steve could be a fun andn interesting character for PCs to interact with. And the comparison of his gang to vampires of the movie, The Lost Boys, quickly establishes what kind of vampires they are. Geduld has an interesting enough backstory that he's intriguing. I just don't like them as major barons. And they are major - the author explicitly lists them both as one of the large gangs alongside La Hermandad, the Crypt's Sons, and the baron of San Diego (Tara). But I don't understand who exactly would follow either of these two, or why they'd be able to recruit so many Anarchs (each is around the same size as La Hermandad) as the Free States are described. This is a big failing.

                            I can easily imagine Steve Booth as the leader of a coterie around him, most likely his old surfing friends and their girlfriends from the 1960s. Steve's embrace date is 1965 which is the same year of the Sabbat attack that destroyed more than half of the Anarch population. I imagine he was embraced to be a "replacement" for the numbers the Anarchs lost. He's called a "natural leader", but my impression is more that he is very charismatic, very physically attractive, very chill, naturally likable, and to your bullshit. People like him and want to hang out with him. But he obviously is not a leader. He has no goals, ambitions, or any insight into politics. He just wants to surf and have fun. If I would ever roleplay this NPC, I'd try to do it as a combination of Kiefer Sutherland's David in The Lost Boys and Jeff Bridges' Dude from The Big Lebowski, but with a lot more Beach Boys music. But I don't see him as a baron.


                            I would keep him as a vampire with a small coterie (maybe 3-6 members, heavily utilizing the dynamics of the vampires in The Lost Boys) with limited involvement in the politics of the Free States. The coterie would have "turf" that do protect. But it'd be small and no barony. Just a normal coterie turf. Instead, they'd ultimately rely on a larger protector, most likely Jeremy MacNeil, but it could be another baron. Steve is another childe of Marguerite Foccart, the sire of Crispus Attucks. I think Jeremy and Crispus would look out for him while shaking their head that all this guy wants to do is surf under the moonlight.

                            He's good local flavor, but some one who could easily be a one note character if people forget the inspirations.

                                David Geduld on the other hand is a natural leader. He's smart, ambitious, and organized. He made partner at a prestigious Chicago law firm (I assume because it is the law form originally created by Lawrence Ballard and thus part of Horatio Ballard's power base) while still in his twenties, and was selected as an agent to infiltrate the Free States as himself under a cover story. Lodin and Ballard would not have selected David for this unless he was highly capable. The cover story must have worked as David, despite know for being a Ventrue of Lodin's brood, was not killed. I assume as part of his defection he provided a lot of accurate (but incomplete or unimportant) information about Lodin's Chicago that would come in useful for the upcoming Anarch revolt there in 1968 along with a lot of misinformation. But he converted to the Anarch cause because the Free States were working (this was in 1963-1965 before the Sabbat attack, so we could assume the Free States were very different back then than how it was in 1994), and performed noted acts of heroism that established his reputation. Basically, he went native and is no longer a spy.

                                There's a lot to like about this character, especially as he is a Ventrue who is NOT going to betray the Free States (which would be an obvious and thus boring plot). The problem is determining WHO is following him and WHY? What is his appeal to other Anarchs? What distinguishes him from other barons? We have nothing to go on. We have no example of someone who is a member of his "gang". We're not given any reason why someone would want to follow him as opposed to someone else. The character may be interesting, but as a baron he's completely bland.

                                We're told that when he arrived in 1963 he quickly gathered a small coterie around him. Who was this coterie? Why would established Anarchs in the Free States want to incorporate this guy into their coterie? Or join him if he formed his own coterie? Even with a solid cover story that held up (I imagine Lodin would have called a Blood Hunt on him, and then used his influence with other nearby Princes to do the same, in order to give the cover story credibility), I can't imagine MacNeil, Garcia, or anyone else trusting him. They'd be suspicious for a long, long time. What kind of Anarchs would want to associate with him?

                                We could construct reasons for his followers. Maybe he took in those vampires who had lost out in earlier turf wars, and needed a leader. And he created his own childer in the aftermath of the Sabbat invasion. We can create plausible reasons. But in the end, what does this give us? Geduld can't provide any vision for the Anarchs as a whole. He's just a local factor. Nobody in other cities is going to care what he does (though it is amusing to speculate as an Anarch from Chicago, he might be one of Anita Wainwright's contacts).

                                But as local flavor, what exactly does Geduld provide the ST in running the Free States? The most obvious thing to do - have him act like a typical Ventrue but who is a loyal Anarch - just makes him even more Camarilla-lite than Louis Fortier. The answer in LAbN, portraying this lawyer as gang leader, is also unsatisfying. Geduld is no street fighter. He has mediocre Firearms and Melee, and no Brawl. This is not a guy who fights with his hands, and most characters likely to be his gang members probably aren't either. The plot set up in LAbN of Geduld leading his "gang" against Steve Booth's surf dudes just seems campy to me once I actually think about it.

                                I imagine their fight would go like this.


                                Now Geduld could offer a vision of an Anarch domain that actually works - mobilizing influence among mortals, equalizing feeding territory, providing some kind of order, and so on. No theory. Just practicality. But again, this is just Camarilla-lite. Certainly some Anarchs are tired of the violence and constant infighting and may join him. But this would be going over like a lead balloon to most of the population of the Free State. Geduld might be accepted, but he's not highly respected as written. His Status is only 2 compared to MacNeil's 5, Fortier's 3, Garcia's 3, Smiling Jack's 3, and Mohammed's 4. According to the book even Edward Vignes (an old fuddy duddy Ventrue from Pasadena) has Status 3! I interpret this to mean that while Geduld's leadership skills are appreciated, he's not seen as one of the leaders of the Free States. Fortier probably has no problem with him, and MacNeil tolerates him, but Geduld's vision would be rejected by any of the other leaders. Geduld may have talent and ambition, but he's out of place in the Free States.

                                Now a Camarilla-lite is OK, but LA already has that in Louis Fortier (or should, as written in the book Fortier's barony is just him and his three brides). It doesn't need another one. Geduld needs something ELSE as a character and faction leader to make him interesting enough to retain.

                                I think Geduld works best as the obvious Influence guy in LA. He, like the Toreador Victor Gerard, is the one who helps with the Masquerade. In addition, he's the guy who often handles other threats from the mortal authorities. He makes sure vampires get bailed out, aren't hassled by bureaucrats, and all the other typical Ventrue stuff. He just does it while working with MacNeil. And he is not one of the "MacNeil Boys", but is someone who makes himself useful to MacNeil and has some credibility as a result. Geduld does have a coterie of his own, and they do live in LA county's South Bay (where Torrance is located). Much of Geduld's direct influence is there. He's not considered one of the "Barons" - that term is associated with the Anarch warlords, but it's acknowledged that South Bay is his territory. He is tolerated as a stabilizing influence in LA. If he has a title or nickname, I imagine it is something like "the Governor". Something a bit more respectable than baron, but implying less physical power. If his domain needs a name, then something like the South Bay Commonwealth would do. I imagine many Anarchs actually call him the "Prince of South Bay". But it's somewhat in jest as they acknowledge his loyalty to the Free States.

                                Geduld does have followers - vampires who want to be left alone in a fairly safe area - and he obviously must have one or two people who can provide muscle. This is an area where creating additional NPCs is absolutely essential in order to not make him bland. Good place to put a Nosferatu or even Gangrel and balance out all the Brujah. Non-gangsters who would be appropriate for a relatively middle class area. But he'd be easily crushed by any of the larger factions. Mohammed doesn't pay him much attention. Geduld, of course, has higher ambitions. He respects MacNeil, but sees he is not providing effective leadership. He thinks Fortier is too decadent. He probably despises the Anarchs who despise him (but hides it better than they do), but tolerates others. He wants to have more territory, more followers, and more respect. He is just facing the limits of reality in the Free States for one like himself, but Geduld isn't letting that dissuade him. He has all the time in the world.

                                I imagine when something important is going down, and the main leaders of the Free States (the Barons) get together to discuss it, they invite Geduld as well. He's too useful, too smart, too organized. If I had to name a role model for him, it'd be him as Alexander Hamilton to MacNeil's George Washington, but without the surrogate son aspect. MacNeil doesn't use Geduld to bolster his own power or domain. He uses Geduld when the Free States collectively needs to get stuff done (particular involving the mortal world) because he's the only one reliable. In a city filled with anarchists, punks, and thugs who don't listen to anyone, Geduld willingly obeys the requests of MacNeil, and it makes him invaluable for that reason alone. He's not MacNeil's prime minister, or his seneschal. His remit isn't to convince the other Anarchs to do things because they wouldn't listen to him. It's to get things done himself using his own resources and those of his coterie on behalf of the Free States. This is a deliberate choice by Geduld. It builds his credibility among the Anarchs, and he also ensures his survival by making himself indispensable. Over time that increases his power. If there is ever another emergency situation like the Sabbat attack in 1965 and MacNeil needed to lead the Anarchs in war, Geduld would promptly return to work as his chief of staff coordinating that army in defense of the Free States.

                                Geduld likes the Free States very much (more in 1964 than in 1994 though). Living here is far better than being one of Lodin's brood, constantly doing whatever Lodin wanted immediately done, hemmed in by the Primogen and other powerful elders. Geduld is not an Anarch at heart, but he loves the freedom he has. He loves the freedom it gives him to empire build. He's loyal to the Free States, though not the Anarch movement itself, if that make sense to you. He willing does what MacNeil wants him to because 1) he respects MacNeil personally while acknowledging his weaknesses, 2) someone has to do these things if the Free States are to survive and thrive, and 3) there's no one else. It also helps that the Ventrue Fortier and Ramirez still act like other Ventrue to him, even though there technically isn't a Board. But if they meet from time to time to discuss matters pertinent to the Free States? Well, that's just understandable.

                                I think Geduld needs a stronger story that explains his acceptance by the Anarchs, and if you are going with my idea above, why MacNeil uses him. The easiest solution is to provide more details on his heroism during the Sabbat invasion. I think if he collected a group of Anarch survivors who were routed during the initial attack, and then lead them to save MacNeil's life when MacNeil and his boys were ambushed by a Sabbat war party, it would accomplish this. And since Geduld and his "gang" were actually in front of him, MacNeil would naturally send him out to rally the other Anarchs and pass messages. MacNeil would need a fighter like Crispus and his other friends with him. There was no one else to use in this more minor role than Geduld. Geduld did that effectively, and that's when his junior partnership to MacNeil began. Crispus and the others would be on the front lines fighting. Geduld would act as MacNeil's chief of staff and enable him to command an army. That's when MacNeil decided Geduld was someone he could rely on and use.

                                When new Anarchs arrive in the city and hear about Geduld and wonder aloud what this Ventrue is doing here, the answer is always the same, "That guy saved MacNeil's life." That ends most of those conversations.

                                While one might think Geduld's and Fortier's "gangs" would be similar, they are very different. Most of the Kindred who dwell in Fortier's barony are old LA types, vampires who were here when Don Sebastian was Prince plus some associates of Fortier who hold his Chartist beliefs. Some others have gone to him seeking protection. In all these cases, Fortier acts as a protective lord. Geduld's group is very different. The Kindred of the South Bay Commonwealth are all Anarchs with similar characteristics. Many were, like his cover story, not Anarchs by choice, but driven to that label because they had angered the local Prince or fled here. Others were idealistic sorts who believed in some generic "freedom" but not beholden to any sort of ideology, but became disillusioned of the violence that characterize the Free States. Geduld recruits the best of these - those whose skills he needs whom he personally likes- to join him in the Commonwealth. To the others he lends a sympathetic ear and occasionally some help, but does not invite to the Commonwealth. They might prove of use if they remain in LA in one of the other baronies (usually MacNeil's). His "gang" is basically a very large coterie who help him build power and influence. There all part of the same "team" that wants to live in the same kind of domain. He likes skilled Kindred who are ambitious, but not too ambitious. He doesn't want anyone who might eventually challenge his leadership. In exchange, he keeps out the criminals, punks, drifters, and riff raff. Geduld avoids performing the same tyranny of the elders simply because the rest of the Free States is his dumping ground. Again using the Anarchs of Chicago By Night as an example, someone like Modius or some of the other Socialists coterie might settle with Fortier. Kindred like Priscilla Biggs, Damien, and Uriah might be in the Commonwealth. Maldavis could also naturally fit here, but it's possible Geduld would not accept her because he feared she might want to take charge. He likely would not accept Hank Cave either, thinking he would not have anything to offer him. Neither Fortier or Geduld would like to see any of the other Chicago Anarchs in their territory.

                                Since we're using a Washington/Hamilton analogy, then it would make sense to use Crispus Attucks as the Thomas Jefferson of the three. These guys do not get along. Crispus sees him as too ambitious, someone with his own agenda, not a "true believer", and above all too... too Ventrue. MacNeil agrees, but reminds his old friend that since Crispus frequently travels so he has to rely on somebody. And Geduld is the most reliable. MacNeil is not friends with Geduld as he is with Crispus. He tolerates him as someone useful. He also acknowledges that Geduld is not a yes man or lackey, but his own man with his own dreams and ambitions. He is not obsequious like Victor Girard. And Geduld is devoted to building his own territory. MacNeil respects that and can work with such a person. That's a good little coterie chart of the three.


                                Geduld, in many respects, is the guy Victor Girard would want to be. Or at least Victor wants to be whom he thinks Geduld is to MacNeil, which is not actually the case. The difference is that Geduld is both more ambitious - he ultimately sees himself being the leader of Los Angeles - and more loyal. He won't betray MacNeil or tolerate the Camarilla returning, not even if they offered him the position of Prince. Victor wants personal power and the privileges that come with it. Geduld wants to build an empire, a legacy. Those are very different things.

                                Thinking about David Geduld was hard. I liked the character's backstory, but could not think of a good way to actually use the character as any kind of Anarch leader. I was tempted to just get rid of him entirely. But I think I figured out a way to keep him, still be true to his core concept, while providing some actual interesting flavor to the Free States.

                                Now, on to the other Anarch barons. I won't be as generous in my assessment of those characters.

I bothered with David Geduld because I actually liked his character profile. I just don't find either of these characters that interesting. There's nothing about their character background or non-existent coterie description that could serve the basis of an interesting faction. So it's just more generic Anarchs fighting.

It demonstrates the decline of the Free States, but won't usurp a chronicle like Mohammed beginning his war. I like that there's two female characters leading their coteries. So far LAbN has neglected them. However, instead of having a bland "baron of Anaheim" and "baron of Santa Clara", I'd probably just refer to the area as Orange County as a singular entity, and the squabbles be based on some kind of competing visions or interests.

                                                        The Anarchs were a major faction in both Chicago By Nights, and many historical events there (the Haymarket Riots and rise of Socialism in the late 19th century, the Nights of Rage and Democratic Convention riots of the late 1960s, and the mayoralty of Harold Washington and the Council Wars of the mid 1980s) involved the Anarchs. Looking back, is there anything the Anarchs there can teach us about Anarch domains or the Anarch Movement in general?

                                                        Chicago By Night 1e has three Anarch factions: the Nihilists, Hind's Socialists, and Juggler's Anarchs. Let's look at each one.

                                                        The Nihilists are a bunch of immature, emotionally stunted ne'er-do-wells, losers, and thugs. Quite typical for a large section of Anarchs. They don't contribute anything to the Anarch movement other than as cannon fodder - provided they can be convinced to join in when it matters. There's probably several coteries of such Anarchs in the Free States, mostly in MacNeil's barony as he's probably the only one who would tolerate them.

                                                        Hind's Socialists are more interesting from a historical perspective. Although described as has-beens in the 1990s, they were the leaders of the Anarchs in the 1890s. Although they can be described as the closest thing to thinks among the Chicago Anarchs, it's important to remember that these are essentially trade unionists. They believe in organizing and mobilizing. They aren't intellectuals. Modius is a member, which I used to think as strange. Modius is a Prince. He couldn't be an Anarch, right? Well, reading both CbN 1e and CbN 2e again I've come to a different conclusion. It's fine for Modius to be both a Prince and an Anarch. In Chicago, this isn't a contradiction. The position of Prince is not seen as inherently tyrannically. It's the person in that position and whether she serves "the Elders" or not. How Anarchs win is by putting one of their own as Prince (who will presumably run the domain more equitably). This becomes very explicit in CbN 2e when we have several Anarchs mentioned as candidates for Prince. It also makes it clear that in this era, the Anarchs really were part of the Camarilla and not any actual separate sect. Individuals among the Camarilla were an enemy, and the Inner Circle and the Justicars part of the elders. But the basic structure of Princes and domains, which is what most players think of as the Camarilla, is equally part of the Anarchs.

                                                        Before the establishment of the Free States, I think this was probably quite normal. That makes Jeremy MacNeil's refusal to become the next Prince truly revolutionary, and making him deserve the title of "Liberator". It’s like Washington not becoming king or retiring after only two terms. It established the Free States as an experimenter of different kinds of alternative governments. I think this is something that is important to point out as an ST, as it makes the Free States seem more attractive, and more revolutionary.

                                                        Going back to Hind's Socialists, I don't see them as something new to the factions already created on this thread. They'd simply be other members among the "Chartists," although among its more radical members. This would also make Fortier and his domain more acceptable in the Free States of 1944 then it would be in future eras. What he'd be doing would be much more inline with traditional Anarch thought back then.

                                                        Another interesting thing about this group is that they have mainly stopped worrying about Chicago, instead giving much more thought on longer term issues like the Jyhad and Gehenna. I think at the time, this was meant to show them divorced from reality and thinking about boogeymen. But now I think I'd run them much more seriously, giving good thought on how the Anarch Movement can survive in the long term and protect the members of the Movement. I don't know how exactly I'd portray that in game yet, but I would portray them as doing long term thinking, and not just how to seize power in a single domain. Perhaps they'd advocate for the need to expand the Conclave system, and have some sort of Grand Conclave replace the Inner Circle. Maybe even keep the Justicar system (although perhaps renamed), but make it more accountable.

                                                        This post is getting long, so I'll discuss Juggler in a future post.

                                                            Another interesting thing about this group is that they have mainly stopped worrying about Chicago, instead giving much more thought on longer term issues like the Jyhad and Gehenna. I think at the time, this was meant to show them divorced from reality and thinking about boogeymen. But now I think I'd run them much more seriously, giving good thought on how the Anarch Movement can survive in the long term and protect the members of the Movement. I don't know how exactly I'd portray that in game yet, but I would portray them as doing long term thinking, and not just how to seize power in a single domain. Perhaps they'd advocate for the need to expand the Conclave system, and have some sort of Grand Conclave replace the Inner Circle. Maybe even keep the Justicar system (although perhaps renamed), but make it more accountable.

                                                            This post is getting long, so I'll discuss Juggler in a future post.
                                                            Anarchs wanting to become Prince was definitely something that was implied to be perfectly reasonable for the Chicago Anarchs with Maldavis working with the Primogen to overthrow Lodin so she can become a better Prince. This is even given a nod in 5th Edition when Anita thinks how bizarre any Anarch would continue to think of Maldavis as a "legend" when she wanted to compromise with the Elders (White Anita attempts to recruit Critias).

                                                            The funny thing is that I do believe Modius is a "traitor Anarch" and he sold out to become a Prince (which didn't work out well for him). But I think this is actually implied to be common enough in the original CBN. Remember that Lodin, himself, used to be a "rebel" (not Anarch) that overthrow the Brujah Prince in order to install a Ventrue dictatorship.

                                                            But that is probably how it normally works. You rebel and then you sell out because punk was cynical about power structure like thatl.

                                                            Whether the Anarchs being part of the Camarilla was a good idea or a bad idea was debatable but I think they never really committed either way and it confused fans. Are you reformers, revolutanaries, or both.
