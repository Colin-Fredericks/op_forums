    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

    I agree but I think part of the issue here is that clearly different people have different ideas how the WOD work. Mine is a dark and Gothic exaggerated wold.
    Yes, this is an issue that keeps popping up.

    WoD is an extremely setting focused rpg. It's what it's about. But an inevitable consequence of that is every group makes their own version of their WoD. You find the game, you like it, you make it your own, you make the setting your own and then suddenly everyone have their WoD and nothing matches up with anyone elses. Don't think there's any real solution to that. People have to be able to make the setting their own, as it is a setting focused game.

    My WoD looks drab and grey on the surface, but underneath it all there's vast societies, almost like hidden countries. It is deeply political, deeply personal and quite, quite alive (even the vampires, hell especially the vampires).

    Also you mentioned something... this:
    3. They exist to remove the somewhat eye-rolling dismissal of mortals by vampire fans.

    I see this attitude pop up a lot of times and IMO it's entirely on its head.

    How should I put it

    WoD is build to be flexible. This allows people to discard boring setting elements and focus on what they want. From a design perspective, it is naive to force a user to use a tool for what you intended it to be used for. Rather you should build the tool to be what people use it for.

    It's like the whole 'People don't play the Personal Horror aspect' thing. Of course they don't. Personal horror can be cool, but it can also be a pretty niche genre only a few people will be interested in. And even then you get tired of it after one or two times. Only a very small set of people is going to want to keep playing it more than that. The reaction to people not playing personal horror shouldn't be "The game needs to cater to what the original tag line says it is about" it should be "that is amazing people are using our game to play political intrigue, character driven drama, and occasionally superhuman action. We should cater to that.", quickly followed up with "using setting elements, not rules, because it is setting focused"

    People dismiss normal mortals in WoD games because the splats are excellently made and so they are more interesting than "normal people". The only good way to solve that would be to make a mortal splat. (I think nWoD had that and that it was pretty popular? Do not have a good grip on the nWod.). Also the SI seems less interesting than normal mortals... so that's not good.

    ...I guess I'm totally off topic.

    Yeah the second inquisition.... I still don't see how it solves the masquerade in modern day thing. At best it feels like it stalls for a few years. I dunno, I'd probably just explicitly have the Camarilla cooperate with the technocracy and basically own youtube, facebook, google, reddit, every major media corporation. Maybe some operating systems as well. You use any of that congratulations, you have a low degree They Live style indoctrination to ignore the supernatural. Make modern information technologies sustain the masquerade (imperfectly) instead of threaten it.

    But even that is a copout. Really what should happen is the Masquerade should break. The end of the world occurs, the various supernaturals and mortals manages to break it off, aaaaand the mask is off. But it wouldn't be WoD after that, it'd be something else...

    Aside from the masquerade thing...

    Maybe it would work better if there where more avenues of conflict. With the SI they can show up and kill people, and occasionally they can try and get a vampire as an informant. With something like the sabbat I can run different types of conflict. And fine part of that is cause the sabbat has more lore, but part of it is the SI seems ultra focused on GENOCIDE ALL VAMPIRES.

    I don't understand why they jumped to "genocide the undead" from the start. Like the FBI finds out a number of US citizens appear to be members of a secret expansive society and also supernatural and their first reaction is 'let's commit ethnic cleansing and genocide them'. Like even if they found some atrocity, it feels the Second Inquisition should be more focused on arresting them. You can maybe rationalize it by saying the sects forced the issue when trying to stop initial masquerade breaches and stuff, but then you get back to it being focused on one limited interaction.

    And the sabbat remains more interesting because they represent an entirely different approach to the vampiric condition which ties right into the core of the camarilla (and now also the anarchs). Meanwhile the SI are like... government dudes? If you wanted mortals in vampire going organized crime seems to have a closer thematic connection.

    And it's somewhat like forcing an interaction between different splats, which tends to work poorly in the WoD.

    There's some other problems but I think they're easier to handle.

    Also aside from wider avenues of conflict, wider avenues of interaction? If they're supposed to be an extremely important part of V5 it feels weird pc's can't talk with them or hang out with them. Like with Mage and the Technocrats you had an entire strategy of defecting back and forth, occasionally team ups, interrogations, attempts at recruitment, stumbling into each other in the umbra, lot's of ways to interact. With the SI any interaction breaks the masquerade.

    Like one of the reasons vampires are evil is the masquerade rips away all safety nets aside from the sects. No hospitals, family, friends, employment or normal social context for you. But now, boom, SI. If they where a genuine alternative to the sects with a bunch of vampires allying and working with them, not as some allegory for an informant but just as y'know people they would be a little bit more interesting. 'My high humanity vampire can go with the sects that are somewhat pycho, or she can go to the SI. They'll try to give her a job she is suited for (accountant!) and also get her a therapist.

    I dunno that's the best I can do. Wider Avenues of conflict, easier time to remove them entirely from the setting if desired, wider avenue of interaction, themes tying better into Kindred thematics

    ...not sure it'd work even with that. Might be wasted effort.

                                                        The Union, despite some of them wishing otherwise, have factions with different ideas on things.

                                                        A lot of them think that most RDs will be eliminated once the Technocracy is in complete control of Consensus again; that's why other mages are such a bigger threat to them. Technocrats that know enough about the various RDs know that most of them are in various states of decline (at least as of 20th). Vampires have Thin-Bloods and the Withering, the Fera are dying off, the forces of Autumn are driving the changelings down, the strengthening of the walls between worlds keeps EDEs of all sorts away, etc. To the Union, that's proof that the Time Table will continually weaken them because they're being written out of reality. The only RDs that need to be directly addressed are ones that don't keep themselves secret because that slows the Time Table.

                                                        Now, how right they'd be to believe any of that is suspect to us, the audience, but in-universe they see a world where RDs are becoming thaumavores and bygones, waiting to eventually be easy pickings if that's even necessary as most of them are forced into the Umbra or cease to exist. One of the Technocracy's strengths is their long game.

                                                        That's what keeps the idealism part of the Union focused on other mages. Mages can change that course of events like nothing else, because they can alter reality like nothing else.
