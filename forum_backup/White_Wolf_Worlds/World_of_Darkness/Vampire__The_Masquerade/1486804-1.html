    Originally posted by Thoth View Post
    Every WoD splat seems to have one area where the devs shoe horn all the extra ideas they have. In Exalted 2nd it was Martial Arts styles, in Werewolf it was endless charm lists, in Vampire it was the paths of magic/necromancy.

    From a design standpoint, this is atrocious. It displays a lack of preplanning and too many cooks in the kitchen kind of scenario.

    As for magic paths in Vampire, with each new clan/bloodline reveal, the history was retconned, which meant things made progressively less and less sense. Between the Tzimisce, the Setites, the Assamites, and the Cappadocians, is there any real reason for the Tremere to be considered special for their magic? Perhaps I am a bit jaded because when I started playing, none of the clans/bloodlines outside of the core 7 were named, let alone given backstories or disciplines.

    All in all the situation reeks of every new thing contain the cool parts of previous things. While some may look at that and exclaim &quot;build variety&quot; I look at it and say that all that was achieved was making everything the same. Any clan can be anything, which results in a lack of clan and bloodline identity if the writers are not careful.

    Think of it like playing an FPS, both sides have the same weapons, the same movement abilities, the same mechanics, and the same map goals. The only difference is a character skin or Team Color designation. Very much Red vs. Blue rather than anything deeper in game play or design. On the other hand, if you take something like AvP, which is an asymmetrical FPS game, each faction has different play styles, different gear, different goals.

    So yeah, excessive magic bloat means the &quot;blandification&quot; of vampire in general and one hell of an XP sink for anyone who wants to &quot;master magic&quot; as a build.
    I absolutely HATE and utterly DESPISE Blood Magic in all forms, and more and more of it with each new supplement of "new bag of cool tricks" that is published. Just as much as I absolutely hate and despise all the bloodlines bag of endless weird. Oh, and the tons of nonsensical exclusive disciplines with powers that are each time more and more taken completely out of the ass. VtM just turned into a cartoonish carnival of nonsense because of those.

            Part of the issue can be traced back to the original Thaumaturgy paths in first edition. Basically, they were treated as an expansion of powers that could be added because no individual clan discipline set could cover all vampire powers in media at the time.

            Dracula may have originally been officially statted out as a Tzimisce, but you need Lure of Flames, Weather Control, and Movement of the Mind to complete the power set. But even if you convert those Paths to straight up disciplines, that would mean either giving every clan 6 disciplines or creating a system where there were always generic disciplines that were not associated by clans that could be picked up on the cheap.

            It might have worked better if WW kept Thaumturgy as a singular path with a ton of rituals, rather than adding on tons of paths. But I doubt that would have been any better than a stop gap measure of design.

                                        "The vampire live on, and cannot die by mere passing of the time; he can flourish when that he can fatten on the blood of the living. But he cannot flourish without this diet; he eat not as others. Even friend Jonathan, who lived with him for weeks, did never see him to eat, never! He come on moonlight rays as elemental dust—as again Jonathan saw those sisters in the castle of Dracula. He become so small—we ourselves saw Miss Lucy, ere she was at peace, slip through a hairbreadth space at the tomb door. If he be not at the place whither he is bound, he can only change himself at noon or at exact sunrise or sunset. These things are we told, and in this record of ours we have proof by inference.                                             I only partially disagree. The part about vampire magic being solely blood-themed is quite stupid.

                                            But to borrow now directly from Mage and shorten the explanation, my take is that Vitae is too powerful as a source of Quintessence, and that's what makes magic distinct for vampires, considering that the lore overtly states that magic is distinct for vampires, because while mortal magicians have a clear head start to practice they always had to adapt.

                                            Simply put, the presence of Vitae both somewhats hinder vampires from working magic as usual (possibly because their own Curse and Blood creates some kind of magical interference), but also offers them a readily available source of Tass. Whatever Paradigm they use, they have to adapt to this very specific Tass, hence they all have a cost in Blood. But you're not bloodily bloodying every bloody thing you put your bloodied hands on to perform magic, while you're constrained to always using this Tass, you have the advantage of this thing being a part of you and under your significant control, so this cost may very well be you meditating or whatever to distill the actual Quintessence you'll use out of the Blood to turn it into a form you can actually work with.

                                            Vampires are quite simply and directly the most effective Quintessence-producing Alchemical furnace in the setting, turning common blood into pure magic and then burning it at will for various tasks. That's why their magic come from their blood even when it is not blood-themed.

Actually it is possible that most are spells as far as the original novel goes. It has also Evil-Hogwarts and other witchcraft-related whatnot.

                                            The Tremere are as much a core vampire archetype as the other Seven Clans, and Thaumaturgy just as much a trope-power for vampires to wield, and that's actually one of my reasons to let any vampire learn it if they put the effort and pay the price.

                                                            I generally like the wide spread of Blood Magic as it provides a good way to add some flavor to Clans and individual Vampires. My main quibble with it is that, for the most part, Paths tend to fall into one of two categories: highly Clan specific powers that would probably have worked better as alternative levels of signature Disciplines, or Sorcery that aside from the power level is not really anything specifically 'cainite' and might just as well be listed in Sorcerer Revised.

                                                            In retrospect, a larger pool of Paths with different cultural flavors in a general supplement like 1e Sorcerer, with a unified system would probably have worked better for Revised than the patchwork approach. Dunno, maybe 5e is going to do that with Blood Sorcery and Sorcerer 5e, but I doubt it. But I like Blood Sorcery as a decidedly different thing than mortal Sorcery.

                                                            As for the Tremere, they are still the only Clan that is purposefully built around magick. They are Clan and House Tremere. Magick Inc. I can't see any other Clans threaten that position any time soon.
