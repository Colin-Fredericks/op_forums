    The economics of Necromancy

    08-15-2022, 05:26 PM
    The Giovanni Clan has built its power on the capacity to interact with the Shadowlands, to the point of enslaving thousands and thousands of Wraiths, actively plotting to undermine the barrier between worlds and accepting to refrain from active participation in the Jyhad in exchange for being left alone in their necromantic pursuits.

    So, this makes me think that, as the Giovanni are a collection of merchant families, they probably would have found countless ways of monetizing the Shadowlands, to the point of probably creating a whole economic network around them.

    Would the Giovanni create a "colonial economy" in the Shadowlands? And perhaps create "settler societies" of enterprising and alienated necromancers for whom the Shadowlands are the main centre of interest?

    Would they use undead labor in their more despicable factories? Would the Giovanni use their Paths to bring in this world the wraithly remains of lost treasures or powerful soulforged artifacts?

    What do you think? What form would the Giovanni shadow empire take ? How could their necromantic skills be leveraged to keep the Family powerful and affluent in Mortal, Cainite and Wraith society?

          The Giovanni clan is one of those concepts that I feel many people are portraying "wrong" in their chronicles. People make them far larger and more powerful than I think they should be. No problems if that is what you want in your chronicles, but I don't think it's the best design choice. And I don't portray the Giovanni clan like that in my own chronicles.

          First, as originally written, the Giovanni were a rather small clan. They were limited to making no more than 3 vampires every generation, which is about once a decade. Which means they have embraced only around 50 new vampires since the Promise of 1528 plus whatever number they had before then. As originally written, there is probably less than a hundred. I know subsequent mentions of the Giovanni dropped this and never mentioned it again, but a clan whose concept is "we only embraced within the family" has to be small. I blame the original Giovanni clanbook for this. It did a good job introducing some more concepts and additional families married into the clan to provide some needed diversity, but grossly overestimated how many vampires these sub-families could actually have. It's actually best to see most of these other families (especially the ones not from Italy who represent Giovanni interests elsewhere) as only having a few vampires at best, maybe half a dozen, with some of the other Italian families having a dozen or so.

          Second, the Giovanni are certainly rich and well off, especially for their small number. But they can't possibly have anywhere near the influence and resources of the Ventrue, and probably many other clans collectively like the Toreador and Tremere. Venice isn't Europe's leading economy and hasn't been for centuries. A clan based out of there and whose wealth is tried to the old Venetian trading networks simply can't surpass clans who operate in all of today's leading economic and financial powers. I view the Giovanni's wealth as being like a hornet's nest. Other vampires leave it alone because it is a hard target, and any benefit they would get from raiding it or attempting to destroy it just isn't worth their cost. The Giovanni can't compete with the influence of most of the Camarilla clans, but that is OK because they don't need to. They just need to enjoy enough resources and influences that they can go about their business and not have others meddle with them.

          Third, Vampire has always consistently failed to demonstrate how hostile the Underworld should be to the Giovanni. Vampire acts like there aren't any other competitors for them, or dangerous factions they would need to avoid. And that's completely false. The Hierarchy is dangerous. And even individual groups of Heretics and Renegades can also be dangerous in local conditions. The Giovanni simply can't enslave any ghosts they want. They need to tread carefully and not upset the other powers. Vampire has always treated the other spirit worlds fairly badly despite having several concepts that show there is a small, but active, group of vampires who do involve themselves in it. Because Vampire supports those kinds of games very poorly, an ST either needs to master an entire new game line, put tremendous individual effort into figuring things out in their own chronicles, or just leave it blank and let PCs and NPCs do whatever they like. Let's say you are an ST who wants to run a Giovanni centric game, or provide a lot of individual feedback to his gaming group, one of whom plays a Giovanni PC. What is more interesting? Just letting the Giovanni do whatever they like, or running a rich setting where they have to actually navigate obstacles, politics, and rivals? The Necromancy powers of the Giovanni should make them a hard target for Wraiths, and if they behave within reason they can get by and advance their plot. But they can't run roughshod over Wraiths. Again, I blame the clanbooks here. Because they are written in character, there is a lot of bluster about how powerful and feared the Giovanni are in the Shadowlands. But that has to be taken with a grain of salt.

          So I see the Giovanni as a relatively small clan with minor absolute power compared to the big sects. However, they do punch way above their weight. And in the relatively small areas where they do have interests, they are major players. Over all, they don't control the world economy. But you'd be a fool to try something in Venice or a dozen other cities where they have major interests.

          In the Shadowlands, their power is mostly contained. In terms of their influence there, they are at best like a large criminal gang or minor warlord in certain Necropoli, and absent almost everywhere else. They are just enough of a factor to make deals with certain other small, local political factions, but not too large that they'd attract attention as a potential threat and crushed like a bug. Their Necromancy can wield a lot of power over one or a few wraiths, but anything more and they'd be smacked down. So Giovanni have to be smart, keep a low profile in most circumstances, and avoid open conflicts. Most of their captured wraiths are from their own family, those stupid enough to deal with them, some smart trading of favors with the powers of the Underworld, and some discrete opportunistic captures. And ghostly souls are useful only to them as necromancers. You can't monetize them anywhere else other than the Shadowlands. They are just useless in the real world.

          At best, you might be able to find some lost real world treasures whose locations are forgotten, but which certain wraiths might know (where is the buried treasure of X that has been lost this past four centuries?) A more useful solution would be to use information gathered from wraiths that would help them in their business dealings - raising the spirit of a recently deceased competitor and forcing them to divulge trade secrets would provide them an edge. Or use enslaved wraiths to torment some of their rivals so they end up making mistakes.

          (I find the idea of the Giovanni capturing whatever ridiculous number of souls for their grand experiment to be something that only has value in a chronicle where Gehenna/Apocalypse/Doomsday is near. That was meant to be yet another sign that Gehenna was imminent. If that isn't the chronicle you are running, there is no reason for the Giovanni to be anywhere close to that. And if you follow the V5 line where the Giovanni were basically humbled and the Cappadocians have reclaimed control of their bloodline (albeit calling themselves Hecata now), it is even more superfluous. I don't put much stock in that as a setting concept.)

          In terms of an undead work force? The simple fact is that zombies can only provide mindless labor. They can't do anything value added. In an earlier time, they could use them on plantations and other unskilled tasks. The depiction of Legendre's sugar cane mill in White Zombie makes for a good, evocative image. No reason not to use it.


          That would be fine during the era of colonial plantations in the 16th to 18th centuries. It'd be very bad now. But something like that may still be done in a few isolated locations in the undeveloped world. But it doesn't generate anything like the old wealth it used to do.

          In my own chronicles, I portray the Giovanni divided between those who seek power in the mortal world, and those who concentrate on the occult. There are not that many Giovanni who crossover and do both because the one aspect takes them too far away from what they are really interested in. That is one of the tensions within the clan in order to make things more interesting from a roleplaying perspective. If you are trying to understand the mysteries of the Labyrinth, contact one of the Far Shores, negotiate with an Anacreon, and so on, you don't want to be bothered with stuff like increasing your bank account. And vice versa.

          That's how I run it at least.

              The weird thing about vampire clan/bloodline size and populations is that things get quite messed up with human population adjustments.

              In previous editions, the 1 to 100,000 mortal ratio included the Kuei-jin population in with the Cainite population for a total Vampire population. So, most Cainite numbers are technically lower than we think of them as. Furthermore, back in 1800 the human population of the world was considered to be only 1 billion strong.

              That would work out to a total vampire population in 1800 of about 10,000, with only 2/3rds of that being Cainite. So that works out to be about 6,666 Cainites in total who are active, in torpor, or in other dimensions. Now divide that by 13 Clans and you have about 500 members per clan and the leftovers covering the Bloodlines. So, for the Giovanni numbers would be only slightly smaller than other clans.

              Of course, with our current population which is supposed to hit 8 billion by November of this year, that would mean a vampire population cap of around 80,000 blood suckers, and a Cainite population of around 53,333 or so. Thus, you could take whatever Giovanni clan number you had in 1800 and multiply it by 9 and still be under pop-cap for the modern nights.

              This is of course not touching on the fact that the Giovanni, Cappadocians, Harbingers of Skulls, and the Samedi are all one big mess when it comes to categorization and family trees.

Focusing on Dominate until level three gets the best results for generating Background dots and rank five Resources can be taken at character generation quite cheaply if you want a head start on things.

                                  But then what do you do exactly?                                   Owning a private military company gives you access to military grade weaponry and mercenaries on command, but most of the time that is too blatant to be of use in first world countries.                                   So while I agree that Necromancy and ghostly minions can help generate Background dots and are useful, the weird thing is that once you get to a certain point you suffer from diminishing returns. Once you have maxed out the business-related Backgrounds, what exactly is the benefit? So far the only thing I can think of is the potential to have full access to your Backgrounds in more varied story scenarios, but that is an ST thing rather than a mechanical one.

                                                          I know how you feel and I find the whole idea of Official Prestation Bookkeeping equally laughable.

                                                          But the core of the system, while poor as is expected from a mechanic created almost overnight as a patch to the setting, does the job.                                                           Originally posted by Thoth View Post
                                                          I agree that a cross-splat system of favors could work quite nicely, I feel that is a role play thing rather than a mechanical thing because there is no aspect of oversight for said favors. There is a reason business/contract law is such a big thing IRL, because just saying someone has an IOU doesn't really mean much.
                                                          In a sense the Prestation mechanics are an attempt at creating a form of Contract Law within the setting. But regardless of its faults, I think that just brushing aside the ridiculous Harpies/Chancellor system it works well as a notation basis out-of-character. And it works across splats or within them.

                                                          Of course, in-character things are muddier. Spoken promises do work in some contexts, among some people, and actually work well most of the time. Of course, you only need one failure if the system doesn't have some sort of self-regulation between the parties. And an actual contract brings the need for external oversight, someone or something that can ensure it's biding powers.

                                                          But the rules as presented already have the tools to make something and put it to work for spoken agreements, and the supernatural splats have their own means to empower contracts if they want to, which most of the time would be the only way to ensure oversight.
