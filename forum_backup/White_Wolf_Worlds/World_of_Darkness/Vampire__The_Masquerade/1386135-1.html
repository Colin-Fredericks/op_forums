                Originally posted by Gubenyochi View Post
                SI is both too powerful and too weak as presented and that is the really the big problem with them. Schrodinger's Clinton* if will.
                My list is designed around trying to make a consistent presentation. What did you think?

                But yes, the conflict between the various groups is a definite weakness that will prevent the SI from actually destroying the vampire race. As presented in Chicago by Night, they haven't seen to have impacted the social structure of the city THAT much.

                To use your example:

                Schrodinger's Inquisition

                I think the real trick to the Second Inquisition is that it needs to be something that is threatening to the vampire world but not something that is going to utterly destroy the game world. Vampires still must be able to gather at Elysium to have their Eyes Wide Shut orgies. They have to gather at the Succubus Club in order to seduce pretty young things and drink their blood. They need to have sects and clan structures as well as meetings. These are the basis of the game and it's not going to be a gameworld where vampires live solitary lives and pretend to be human 24-7 rather than when only when they're in mixed or Kine company.

                The Second Inquisition does need to be threatening enough that the vampire race is afraid of them, that powerful vampires can be destroyed at seeming random, and they don't know when they are going to strike. To use a possibly tasteless example, the Second Inquisition should be the coronavirus. It's a new threat made from a pre-existing threat that has caused many diverse and disparate reactions among the Kindred race as well as killed a lot of people. There's much disagreement on how to handle it. It will be beaten and the world may (or may not) go back to normal because of it.

                So managing that balance between "this is deadly and dangerous and we need to pay attention" versus "doomsaying" is important. Player characters should be able to fight and kill SI agents, they should be able to evade them, they should be able to potentially hit and wipe out a SI base if they are doing the climax of a Chronicle. They might also get their hacker friend to create a worm that will wipe out all of their information in a cell. They are antagonists that are actually able to be fought and killed unlike Divis Mal or the Archwizards of Shade from Forgotten Realms.

                But they should be strong enough that the players aren't going, "Why doesn't Elminster just kill them?" Except replace Elminster with the Justicars.

                    Just throwing out my two cents as someone who likes both V20 and V5 as they are and that fans of both are valid as both has plenty of interesting things to offer, though I lean towards V20 for my personal tastes.

                    That disclaimer aside, I for one like CTPhipps list here and thing that it's a really interesting article. I personally wouldn't use V5's Second Inquisition as written for most of my chronicles but a lot of the ideas are interesting and I happily mine them for world building and adding my own personal twist. For most chronicles, except perhaps those focused on vampiric-hunter conflict, I wouldn't formally use the Second Inquisition.

                    That said many of the ideas of SI, particularly alongside the setting work we received from Hunters Hunted II is interesting. People have been complaining about (human) hunters lack of bite for years, if not decades, and you can't deny that the Second Inquisition has bite to spare.

                    For me, I use something of a middle ground between V20 and V5 as my go-to with adjustment as necessary. There are numerous human hunter organizations (some I take inspiration from HtV for) with the Society of Leopold, SAD, and Arcanum simply being some of the most prominent. These groups largely operate independently and for their own reason and are probably outnumbered by oft-short lived free radicals, hunters without an overarching organization.

                    Project Twilight exists within the United States government with many similar organizations worldwide. The Arcanum and other scientist and occultist collectives study the supernatural. The Society of Leopold and other religious organizations still carry the torch against the darkest night spawn. Sometimes rumors arise that loose alliances have formed between members of these organizations with chilling results, yet details remain scant.

                    Vampire and hunter society both have legends, rumors, and folklore. Some stories with more validity then others. The urban legend of a ruthless black ops inquisition is a good story for drug-dazed neinates fresh from the rack to share in hushed voices while coming down. Even if most of these stories are nonsense, it only takes one to make a credible threat...

                    Like many, if not most things, it comes down to personal taste. Not a particularly satisfying answer I know but one that I honestly think serves as the best possible given most people here and our experience with online debates and how futile they can seem.

                    That said, I'm not trying to stifle argument, I think it's interesting to spectate and fun to participate in. So, anyway, that's my two bits, thanks for reading this far y'all.

                            Some thoughts about how to potentially incorporate the Second Inquisition into a greater World of Darkness. All of this is non-canon of course.

                            CT Phipps ideas on the SI and other groups

                            The Garou and the Second Inquisition: The Garou, for the most part, are unaware of the Second Inquisition and wouldn't care if they were. Their short-sightedness doesn't apply to their Kinfolk, though, who have often been saddled with the thankless job of protecting the Veil. There are dozens of Kinfolk operating within the Second Inquisition and doing their best to prevent the discovery of the Garou's existence. This is a lost cause with the pre-existing hunters like the Society of Leopold, SAD, and Arcanum but many Kinfolk play the role of Scully-esque skeptics who try to paint reports of werewolves as just more Blankbodies (ignoring that they have infrared signatures). The existence of the Gangrel helps them but this is a flimsy protection as is. These Kinfolk are, by and large, supportive of the SI's efforts, though. While the Garou race would be safer if the SI was shut down, many of them actually feel like they're getting to fight the Wyrm for the first time.

                            Pentex and the Second Inquisition: Pentex is maintaining a neutral position on the Second Inquisition despite the fact that they have attacked their allies in the Sabbat to a ridiculous degree. Pentex could probably destroy the SI's government support with a few phone calls and maybe eradicate the entire organization if it turned its mammoth resources against it. It doesn't, however, because Pentex doesn't care. While the Sabbat has suffered thousands of losses to Hunters, the destruction of Camarilla elders means that the corporation has gained massive new areas of business to expand their influence into. Pentex has almost no fear for itself and few "valuable" vampire executives while a few less players on the gameboard only benefits it. Were the Masquerade to fall, the corporation would also be first in line to profit from the resulting chaos.

                            Hunters and the Second Inquisition: The Imbued are individuals that have been consulted by the Second Inquisition whenever they've been found. Many of these are in prisons or insane asylums after their actions got them captured by mundane authorities. The Second Inquisition is cautious about these people because of their overt supernatural powers as well as general instability. A few have been freed and serve as either full-time consultants or agents on the field. For the larger Imbued organization, they have noticed an uptick in vampire deaths but have yet to be contacted. If they were, they might end up just another arm being fed weapons and equipment or be deemed too much of a security threat.

                            The Technocracy and Second Inquisition: The Second Inquisition is something the Technocracy has infiltrated but does not have the time or resources right now to deal with effectively. Operation: Ragnarok resulted in many Technocrats becoming more interested in the threat of vampires but not enough to change policy post-Avatar Storm and dealing with things like Threat Null. As such, the Technocracy serves as an "angel investor" in the Second Inquisition. The perfect storm of funding, support from governments, magically disappearing handicaps to their efforts, and suspicious universality in the decision NOT to go public among its leadership are all the result of the Union helping them from behind the scenes. The Technocracy has also used the SI as a test bed for many low-level devices that would not otherwise work but the Hunters are incredulous enough to operate.

                            Despite this, the Technocracy is not actually a fan of the SI. While the die-hard anti-vampire Technocrats (called "The Helsings") are happy to support it, the organization as a whole considers the group to be a threat to the Timetable. Destroying vampires is something they don't want to leave to amateurs and the disruption of undead society risks exposing the undead to the Consensus. It doesn't help that the SI is allied with religious terrorists like the Society of Leopold and Hermetic affiliated groups like the Arcanum. As such there's a rising number of Unionists who think the SI should be made to go away, regardless of how many reality deviants they've killed.

                            The opposition to the SI would grow dramatically if it was known that in addition to the funding and covert support that they've been giving, the Helsings have actually broken protocol and actively launched Ascension War-style raids on powerful vampires. Men in Black, Victors, HIT Marks, and Non-Enlightened soldiers have done a dozen or more missions that they've passed off as the SI. Basically, if a vampire is attacked by what seems like an army and disposable soldiers are used in action movie or comic book fashion then it is almost certainly NOT the SI. The Union can always grow more after all.

                            The Traditions and the Second Inquisition: Surprisingly, the SI is ALSO receiving aid from the Traditions or at least two of them. The Celestial Choir and Order of Hermes have been assisting the SI covertly on the religious and occultist end of things without any real contact to the Technocratic assisted end. The Celestial Choir has had agents infiltrated into the Society of Leopold and Catholic Church for years. As such, its Acolytes have found themselves assisting the SI wherever possible with information or freakish strokes of good luck (if not the occasional outright miracle). A few Knights Templar and Celestial Choir paladins have also taken part in hunts. The Order of Hermes has never forgotten its two wars with the Tremere and while perhaps only a dozen Heremetics have been helping the organization, that's enough. Its influence may not be as powerful as the Union but it can pull strings with politicians, corporations, the media and also make sure that attempts to shut down the SI are flummoxed. The Tremere, in particular, doesn't know WHY the Inquisition is almost impossible to track down with magic. The Banu Haqim laughed...and has failed as well.
