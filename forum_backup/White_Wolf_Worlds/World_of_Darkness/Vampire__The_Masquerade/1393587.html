          I'm pretty sure DAV Revised is technically different as well.

          That said, I think DAV20 has the best version, though it's highly dependent on DAV20's implementation of multiple actions in general.

          Early VtM with 1 vitae for Celerity extra actions was just too much, and the spend for the next turn aspect of it acted as more of a binary "you have Celerity so spam it," or not than a real tactical control on the Discipline.

          The later moved to 1 vitae per extra action was good move in terms of making Celerity less dominating in a fight, and having some passive bonuses gave it some nice non-combat uses. However the Generation limit breaking aspect of it is clunky.

          DAV20 has a good mix here. It returns to 1 bp (two for both activated powers) to activate removing the need for the limit breaking spending, while keeping the passive bonuses, and not having it's effect on extra actions be overwhelmingly good.

                      Second favorite?

                      DAV Rev., with V20 being a close third.

                      DAV Rev. doesn't have the passive oomph of V20, but works in the Revised multiple action rules which are less punishing to non-magical extra actions. As well, DAV Rev. doesn't have the "all magical actions take places at the end of the round except defenses," thing, and instead everyone, magical or normal actions, cycles through one attack/misc. action, using up actions for defense as necessary, and around you go until everyone's out. This makes for more enjoyable fights in general rather than "take all your mundane actions, wait for everyone else to take all their mundane actions, then take all your Celerity actions, and wait while everyone else takes all theirs."

                      The utility of V20's passive boosts are nice (though also easily ported), but ultimately less fun than DAV Rev's overall package.

                      It's almost like the DA cores get to learn for their related core VtM edition's mistakes and be better....
