            Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post
            I do find the dread of unique disciplines funny, when the most powerful clan is plain old vanilla Ventrue. Dominate and Presence are insane.
            True, but people can feel reticent about certain disciplines for a number of reasons. For one, at least before V20 and DAV20 came out, a PC from a clan or bloodline with signature disciplines probably meant adding one or two more books to the ST's load, more time lost with doubts about powers that come up less frequently at the table, not to mention the potential for power abuse headaches if we are speaking of a "X-clan/bloodline fanboy or girl" that possibly knows that signature discipline like the back of their hand.

            Yes, it can be perfectly manageable depending on a ST's practice and style, but the fact it depends can on itself be a drag from other things the ST might have wished to focus on, like the other players, dangling some hook for a future subplot or story beat and more. That without going into the rest of the table turning idle and losing some steam every time you have to recheck some sourcebook again or debate the limits of an ill-defined version of a specific power because of that one specific character.

            Bonus potential annoyance if the aforementioned player requested that character choice even before you as ST spoke of independent clans or others, as that can be a very strong hint of either snowflake tendencies or indeed a "X-clan/bloodline fanboy or girl", players who can sometimes very easily double as "you're doing the setting wrong!!!" people when it comes to their one favorite thing and cause all kinds of passive-agressive trouble or worse, like the person from Gurkhal's Deathwatch game experience.

            (Doing the setting wrong? I played Dark Heresy with gangbusting Little Orphan Annie - "doing the setting right" can kiss my chainsword.... )

            And yes, being annoyed by how some clans and bloodlines can become sort of one-note due to the nearly inextricable connection of their character identity to a certain discipline is a thing too.

            So, while i'm anything but conservative when it comes to opening power options to my players - my current PC group has a "Caitiff" that just discovered himself to be Cappadocian, a Salubri warrior-nun (yes, both paths) just out of her torpor in the middle ages and a Gangrel ex-Gargoyle still getting over the "timeskip blues of the ages" in its ensemble of weirdoes - but i can understand that dread/dislike. Almost every ST and many players have seen some or all of those issues cropping up in their games to some degree through the years.

                    One thing I've been toying with is giving clans "Blood Gifts" more akin to changeling birthrights. That way, the "clan defining" stuff is just a free power they get -- and every clan now has one. The Disciplines themselves have been retooled to have Devotions (alternate and combo powers) and Devices (basically a standard power as expressed through your Blood Gift). Then I use the VTR2e Disciplines as the base.

                    So, for Protean, every clan or bloodline has a Protean Affinity, which determines how it manifests. Gangrel have an affinity with animals they hunt and live amongst. Tzimisce have an affinity for metamorphosis of the human form through flesh and blood, rather than animal forms. The various bloodlines of the Mesu Bedshet (Setites) have an affinity with sacred animals (Egyptian lineages might turn into ibises or crocodiles, West African lineages might turn into certain birds and snakes, the Tlacique might turn into jaguars, etc).

                    Unmarked Grave is the Protean 1 power shared by all, allowing you to meld with different substances to seek shelter from the sun. Different clans get a) one or more free materials they can meld with and b) a unique bonus from melding with their preferred substance.

                    Gangrel get Earth Meld, which allows them to meld with earth for free and feed on the animals within it -- approximately one vitae per week from worms, moles, and so on (mostly useful for healing in torpor). They slowly aspect themselves to the earth as they do this, however, granting them a bonus to dealing with/hunting animals who call that area home (after a week of sleeping there, they gain a +2 dice bonus for things like Animal Ken and Animalism that lasts until they sleep somewhere else).

                    City Gangrel get to meld with concrete and tarmac, and become aware of the throngs of the city. This gives them +2 to Streetwise and urban hunting rolls.

                    Gargoyles get Stone Warden, which allows them to meld with stone, concrete or brick, and monitor the things going on inside buildings, but they can also turn into immobile statues that don't burn in the sun. They wake up immediately if there's an intruder, but also the ST can pass on clues gained while they were sleeping. Their Blood Gift synergises with this, as when in statue form, they can become mobile for a number of rounds for the sole purpose of dealing with intruders. After that point, they have to worry about sunlight and sleep as normal.

                    Tzimisce get Lord of the Land, which allows them to restore Willpower while sleeping in their own domain. They may listen in and observe any "guests" (welcome or unwelcome) in their domain, and are able to use Auspex and other powers during daysleep in this area, provided they can sense the target.

                    The Mesu Bedshet (Setites) can sleep in "consecrated" ground (according to their beliefs), in which they are immune to blood magic, or they can become a desiccated corpse that is immune to injury as well, including sunlight and fire, at the cost of being completely inactive during the day and unable to wake. (You can get moved, though, if someone thinks you're a previously undiscovered mummy!) Some of their blood magic synergises with this to let them place a "mummy's curse" on their tomb as well.

                    ​​​​Chimerstry and Obtenebration have been folded into Rift: a Discipline of bringing other realms into this one, that borrows a lot from Requiem's Nightmare Discipline. Mytherceria has been divided between the Kiasyd's Blood Gift and a few Rift and Auspex Devices.

                    Thaumaturgy and Necromancy are the two very broad types of ritual Discipline, but they share some paths, which are differentiated via Sorcery Traditions. So Hermetic Thaumaturgy is the Tremere type of Thaumaturgy that works as we know it, but Dur-An-Ki has its own way of working.

                    ​​​To rebalance Sorcery, additional paths always cost the same as an OOC Discipline (only your primary path is in-clan).

                    Each Tradition has common, uncommon, rare and forbidden paths, and a rider effect which can be added to powers if desired (e.g., "the Mortuus Path"/Harbinger Necromancy allows you to wither living targets and drain wraithly ones of energy; Venetian Necromancy gives modifiers for subjugating and abusing wraiths; Eastern Necromancy gives bonuses for hiding among/acting as a ghost yourself).

                    Spirit Manipulation in the Spiritus Tradition (targeting animistic spirits) works very differently to Spirit Manipulation in Koldunism (the Way of Spirit), and so the same path essentially has different names and expressions based on that. Daimonion is a Tradition used by the Baali, replacing most of its 9 powers with access to a broader set of paths (Phobos/Prey on the Soul's Fear is usually primary, followed by Curses or Corruption).

                    Maleficia is Dark Sorcery that uses elements of Thaumaturgy and Necromancy. Daimonion arguably belongs in this category, too, although Maleficia is usually learned directly from demons and dark spirits, whereas Daimonion rises from the blood (and the dark voices the Baali hear in their souls, but ssshhh!). Molochim tend to learn Dur-An-Ki instead.

                                            I like the idea of Disciplines manifesting differently for different Clans and Bloodlines. What I could possible think would be an issue are two things.
