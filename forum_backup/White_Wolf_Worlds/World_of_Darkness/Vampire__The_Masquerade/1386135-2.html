    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    You have a weird idea anyone in the world of darkness likes vampires.
    To put it another way, vampires are what happens if terminal cancer could put on nice clothing and have dance parties and run your company. Normal people would, at best tolerate this because it was the social order and it provides a (meager) paycheck. Others might exploit it. But one one, who was not the vampire/cancer, would like it.

    Aside from that, in terms of money and wealth... what is to keep another group of splats (mages, werewolves, etc) from going full D&D on the vampires; i.e. why not kill the vampire and loot the lair for everything they can load into a truck? Nothing helps inter Tradition (or Tribe) bonding quite like an old fashioned monster hunt and then stealing all the monster's stuff.

    Vampires are monsters. That is the point of the game - deal with being a monster while living a secret life. They are not put upon fashionable types with solar urticaria (sun allergy). Vampires are, by definition, bad people. Bad people acquire enemies because they are bad people.

        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

        My list is designed around trying to make a consistent presentation. What did you think?

        But yes, the conflict between the various groups is a definite weakness that will prevent the SI from actually destroying the vampire race. As presented in Chicago by Night, they haven't seen to have impacted the social structure of the city THAT much.

        To use your example:

        Schrodinger's Inquisition

        I think the real trick to the Second Inquisition is that it needs to be something that is threatening to the vampire world but not something that is going to utterly destroy the game world. Vampires still must be able to gather at Elysium to have their Eyes Wide Shut orgies. They have to gather at the Succubus Club in order to seduce pretty young things and drink their blood. They need to have sects and clan structures as well as meetings. These are the basis of the game and it's not going to be a gameworld where vampires live solitary lives and pretend to be human 24-7 rather than when only when they're in mixed or Kine company.

        The Second Inquisition does need to be threatening enough that the vampire race is afraid of them, that powerful vampires can be destroyed at seeming random, and they don't know when they are going to strike. To use a possibly tasteless example, the Second Inquisition should be the coronavirus. It's a new threat made from a pre-existing threat that has caused many diverse and disparate reactions among the Kindred race as well as killed a lot of people. There's much disagreement on how to handle it. It will be beaten and the world may (or may not) go back to normal because of it.

        So managing that balance between "this is deadly and dangerous and we need to pay attention" versus "doomsaying" is important. Player characters should be able to fight and kill SI agents, they should be able to evade them, they should be able to potentially hit and wipe out a SI base if they are doing the climax of a Chronicle. They might also get their hacker friend to create a worm that will wipe out all of their information in a cell. They are antagonists that are actually able to be fought and killed unlike Divis Mal or the Archwizards of Shade from Forgotten Realms.

        But they should be strong enough that the players aren't going, "Why doesn't Elminster just kill them?" Except replace Elminster with the Justicars.
        Not a fan of your list, but your explanation here is exactly how they SHOULD have been presented and would have made the situation much more believable to me. Killing a few random named NPCs to show that they are scary but strike with precision and careful planning. Unfortunately the whole world setting now relies on them basically killing Elminster in his most defensible position as their introduction. This sets their base power level. Combining that with "Blankbody" detectors, it's only a matter of time that they wipe the civilized world clean. A few dual camera spy satellites shouldn't be a problem for people who can disguise a drone strike (which is about as subtle as crashing a plane into a building).

        It will always come down, for me, to what would it take game MECHANICS wise Pre-V5 to accomplish taking down the Tremere chantry? And that is the power level the SI has to be at to be believable and at that level they might as well be antediluvians as far as PCs are concerned. Personally I need my fluff supported by the mechanics for an RPG to be enjoyable, your preferences may differ.

                                            Originally posted by Damian May View Post

                                            Fear of losing one's privileges over an oppressed and abused minority is just a different way of saying the same thing.

                                            I used Fantastic Racism (See TVtropes.org) as an element in my United States of Monsters books and contrasted is to actual racism against minorities as a plot point. Peter Stone, the star of STRAIGHT OUTTA FANGTON, is a black man as well as a vampire while so is his creator. The character gets to discuss the fact that there's plenty of hate toward both but with vastly different reasons as well as what sort of differences that it means for a person who is both. The Maven of the Eventide discusses the issues brought up in her review of it.


                                            For me, hatred as a motivation of the Second Inquisition is an interesting way of depicting their motivations as they are less about the actual damage that vampires do or threats than a blind and unthinking revulsion at the idea of people who are fundamentally different than them. The existence of vampires challenges the status quo where the governments as well as majorities who make them up believe themselves to be the top of the world.

                                            There's a lot that can be done with the fact the vampire race challenges notions of superiority and dominance in their culture that is reasonably post-racial, post-sexism, and pansexual (hemosexual?). For a thoroughly unpleasant SI, you can have the decision to destroy the undead motivated by someone who fears that they upend the social order, especially when confronted by the fact vampires can wield substantial authority as minorities.

                                            Or it can all just be because they're bloodsucking corpses. Depends on how you want to process the themes of your campaign.

                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                    8. The Second Inquisition isn't listening to your phone (probably)

                                                    The Second Inquisition isn't using PRISM to monitor the entire internet for any mention of Camarilla, Anarchs, Brujah, or Beckett. Even if they could, they wouldn't be able to sort through all of the data or justify it to other government branches. The Kindred don't understand their enemy or its capabilities, so that's partially why the SI continues to bedevil them. They can hack the computers and phones of those Kindred they identify but it's usually a much more personal as well as slow-going process. The paranoia about one vampire potentially exposing many others is not entirely unjustified, though. How screwed are you if they have a bug in Elysium? The Succubus Club? Or more? Perhaps very or perhaps not at all as everyone uses an alias. It's all up to the Storyteller. It's also possible that the guy monitoring those places will stick his findings in a pile of papers that will never be read or be an agent of another power.
                                                    So your suggestion for running the SI is to nerf their one actual competitive advantage into the ground?

                                                    Carry a cellphone? Well you better be tossing it in the trash on a daily basis because that phones location data is being stored 24/7 and whenever a vamp is detected or captured, one of the first things done is to grab everything from their phone and get to work building out the contact map.

                                                    Where has this cellphone been? What other phones have been in the same locations at the same times? What numbers does the phone call, what numbers do those phones call?

                                                    All of the "possibles" are tagged, tapped, and traced. Every call and text from everyone of them is being reviewed in realtime.

                                                    Link a car to a vamp? Well then traffic cam footage is pulled to start backtracing its location and every bit of paperwork related to it gets put under the microscope.

                                                    The SI is a threat precisely because they are able to turn the full might of the NSA, CIA, Treasury, IRS, FBI, MI6, MI5, FSB, Mossad, and MSS against vamps in a coordinated manner and the vampires are absolutely not up to date on how to avoid modern surveillance capabilities.

                                                    So yes, when the SI goes hot and starts moving against vampires they already have most vamps IDed, localized, and being tracked in real time. They know who the active power brokers are, where everyone shelters, where Elysium is, what corporate and governmental resources are under vamp control, etc.

                                                    If you want to avoid the SI then what a vamp should do is immediately toss everything and go off grid as they rebuild from the ground up. At the same time they should be throwing lots of problems at the SI. The global economy is crashed, the Lasombra detonate a Russian nuke in the ME to sink a US carrier, scandal after political scandal hits the web & papers as centuries of dirty laundry is brought out and made public, local police are dominated into bad shoots, bioweapons are released, etc. The vamps throw the world into so much chaos and disorder that the structures of the mortal world and the infrastructure that the SI is reliant on ceases to function.
