    Also losing one (or two) point of humanity was never the end of the world... until V5.

    I don't understand where this mentality comes from (one should never lose syndrome?). Sure a Humanity 8 vampire set himself to very high and difficult standard... as he should! Because playing high humanity vampire should never be easy... otherwise what's even the point of the ladder? What differentiate a more Humane vampire than a human from a serial killer? A righteous ideology? Please... I thought this wasn't a superhero katana kind of game.

    That does not mean however that a Humanity 8 vampire can not fail or that he always was a good person. He may even have been a rather bad person before reaching an epiphany. That was a cool aspect of Vampire. That cool and affable elder? Well he may have some skeleton in the closet and he may still become again THAT dude if push come to shove.

    I feel like that add another layer of morality to the setting. First things are not always black and white. Yes the beast will rouse should you commit a wrong act and that may (and should) spurs some drama but nothing is final as long as the pc doesn't completely or partially give up to the beast. Which gives us the second layer: acceptance.

    So yes Humanity was hard. Because damn right it should be! A player that can maintain a humane vampire for as long as a chronicle (or more) should and is entitiled to feel some sort of achievement for taking the high road with all its bumps. And no, he does not get a game over for losing once. Only a new bonus stage. Which may or may not lead him to defeat.

    So yes I very much prefer the way Humanity was handled before V5. First because regaining Humanity was both easier to lose and regain. Stains are worse in that they only delay the inevitable... unless the player purposefully choose the "Tenets of what I wanted to do anyway" In which case Humanity is made completely useless.

        I thought loresheets were very... some of them leaned far too much into the realm of fanservice. The execution (and often the writing) felt poor.         Thaumaturgy having paths is something that makes more sense the more you read into it. Thaumaturgy isn't really distinct from hedgemagic and isn't part of the vampire condition. It's a discipline "for humans" (everyone), only as a vampire it works a little better because vitae is a much more convenient sacrifice than your health. Thaumaturgy is a product of the soul, and WoD runs with the idea that we all have amazing potential and can make miracles and shape reality as we please if we unlock that power (cue massive Tremere ego) Thus it makes sense that thaumaturgy has a myriad of powers because there's so many ways to shape reality, whilst vampire disciplines are rather limited because they're more... tied to your physical body/the beast.

        Literally everything about the way V5 does generation/Blood potency undermines the Jyhad. Why eat my way up when that's not a path to God or God-like status, but just a shortcut to more XP; you'll gain potency over time so you don't need to eat-up for it (and also Blood potency 5+ is really bad for you so you absolutely should not try to eat your way up because that'll increase the time you'll want to spend in torpor.
        1. My bias toward in-game lore is probably showing but I really appreciate Loresheets making a chance to intertwine your characters with the settings NPCs and major plot threads without being accused of being a Gary Stu. It also rewards players who do want to become involved with the lore and make their characters as part of the larger, richer tapestry of characters that is part of the setting. Mind you, I admit some of the rewards are crazy like being part of the new Council of Seven.

Part of the problem that presents is that Thaumaturgy/Blood Sorcery becomes an entirely different Discipline from the rest of them and encourages you to spend everything on Thaumaturgy paths versus your regular Disciplines. Mind you, I think it also underscores how stupid the Amalgam rules are right now. You can get all the Blood Sorcery rituals you want but not two 4th Dot Powers in Dominate.

        3. Because you don't have 200 years to become Prince and be powerful. You need power NOW.

                                I also recall V5 openly says something like " If you consider yourself alt-right go home and rethink your life, this game is not for you"

                                I would probably be considered "left" enough to be put on a list, but you simply can't have the kind of attitude to write something like that in something that's A: A commercial product that you hope will be successful and B: a satirical work of literature that you hope will bring people around to your point of view. That "my way or the highway" attitude is really significant enough that it's effects are felt on the rest of the work. Even if you don't mean to hurt "political horror" with "personal horror" the fact that you've taken such a strong stance with who can't play your game makes it feel like it's not such a stretch that you're taking a strong stance there too. Hell, the reduction of strong hierarchies and power disparities (IE Generation isn't that desirable and humans are a credible threat), which are important aspects of fascism, kinda gives rise to the idea that the previously exaggerated gaps in power have been reduced in order to make sure that such hierarchies aren't legitimized and everyone feels equal so that the game doesn't support fascist ideas (which is, to me at least, obviously counterproductive since those hierarchies were exaggerated for the purpose of satirical ridicule, but it's not unknown for supposedly liberal narratives to backfire and become counterproductive, like how the "strong women" in recent Star-Wars movies have been an object of some scorn. If someone's game enough to write "fascists stop reading" on a product you can expect little good)

                                In short, if you're dictating who is allowed to play it's not much of a stretch to believe you're also going to dictate how they should play. I'm not saying they are engaging in a 1TW approach, just that it's understandable that some people have suspicions.

                                                            "There's no such thing as bad publicity," is both questionable as a concept, and only really works on a corporate scale well above were most RPGs live. Bad publicity is bad because it has a cost. The bigger you are, the easier it is to absorb that cost and spin things to your benefit in the long run (ex: New Coke). Smaller companies frequently do not have that ability, let alone individuals.

                                                            As for Jason Carl's comment, it is both possible to feel it was sincere for him personally, but find it to be grandstanding by the company as they trot out someone that honestly feels strongly about it without necessarily demonstrating any corporate culture changes that would keep the same situation from happening again.
