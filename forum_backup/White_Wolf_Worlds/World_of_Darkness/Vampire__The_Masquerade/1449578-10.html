                                        Originally posted by Morbus View Post


                                        Yes it's what we call a retcon. At best. Not that this change anything to what I said about one true wayism. We got you loud and clear. V2 onwards was a mistake. A mistake that V5 strives to correct. Okay. 25 years of Vampire down the drain.

                                        People like to compare V5 to DND4 but frankly it's far more divisive than 4e ever was. Imagine if wizards deleted the Sorcerer or Bard class because "it was not in the first edition." Or "it was a mistake."

                                        Huh?

                                        My point is just that the creative vision is rooted in a dispute over this particular setting element that goes way, way back. Part of the reason Vampire: The Requiem even came into existence was creative dissatisfaction with some of the path-dependency built up in Masquerade over time; the setting kind of became stagnant, difficult to shake up, revise.

                                        Anyone remember the VtR book VII? It had three complete takes on what VII could be in your game. Part of the reason they did that was the toolkit approach the nWoD was taking at the time, but another reason was to preserve VII as a mysterious antagonist force - because back when the Sabbat was described in-full, that sense of mystery was lost, so they were attempting to split the difference and not make the same "mistake."

                                        If you want my personal opinion on what "way" they should have gone, I think attempting to put the Sabbat back in the mystery box is...well, I wouldn't call it a mistake, per se, since it's their vision to implement, so much as a lost opportunity. As pointed out, the vast majority of VtM's lifespan, the Sabbat has been the detailed organization we know and love. I would prefer V5 add a new antagonist group, one that they deliberately keep vague and/or toolkit in design approach, thereby creating a mysterious "off the map" antagonist.

                                        The Sabbat, on the other hand, I'd like to see go through the Gehenna War and the V5 metaplot changes and all that fun stuff. Evolve it over time. Take some of the dated and juvenile elements that survived to VtM revised and dial them back, work the Paths to be more playable and interesting, and so on. One of the things I like about V5 is the Camarilla's regression into neo-feudalism as a defense mechanism against the Second Inquisition, it would be interesting to see how the Sabbat evolved to survive it as well in a playable sense.

                                        They've been on the map so long I think it'll be difficult to slap a "Here be Dragons" sign on the Sabbat and have it stick for old-timers, and newcomers won't notice either way.

                                        EDIT: But then again, I'd take a hatchet to every single WoD game, mechanics and setting, so I'm probably not the right person to ask.
