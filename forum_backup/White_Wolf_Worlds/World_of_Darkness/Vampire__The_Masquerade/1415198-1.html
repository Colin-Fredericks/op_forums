    For establishing the setting, I need to know what are their intention and if those intentions will affect the PCs. After that, I used them if needed only.

    I'm running Vienna, so Etrius appeared for a brief moment to the Tremere PC (he captured an inquisitor with true faith, and useful for one of Etrius rituals), and for instruction of a lesser ritual via Oneiromancy as a reward to the PC.

    I used Mithras for an individual prelude scene for the Ventrue PC in '91, cellar beneath Centre Point office, London (right after his rise from torpor) and Kartarirya in Colombo, Sri Lanka. The reason for the PC to be around such significant kindred is because her sire is Bindusara and this was around the time before her release.

    In an older chronicle I used Lambach Ruthven when the LA coterie went to NYC to warn them to stop investigating events in the city (the dread worked perfectly, they wanted to leave the city as soon as possible)

    In my KotE chronicle i'm using Tremere/Saulot/Tzimisce as a focal point of one of the PCs (that has 0 idea of what's going on with him after he returned from Yomi from an apparent 3rd breath) because its the last part of the chronicle that will end in the Sixth Age. Saulot will use the PC to become the Demon Emperor, but Tzimisce/Tremere know of this and will interfere. I tease with visions, dreams, and instinctive knowledge on hermeticism and the on/off presence of the PC's P'o. One of the PCs from the wu read the Heaven Scream Book in Chongqing and knows how the sixth age will unravel upon them.

                                            I really like the "methuselah awakening under the city trope", however, it's absolutely ridiculously overused in Vampire, and often feels so silly in the New World, so I tend to avoid it, despite the fact that it appeals to me dramatically a lot.

                                            For me, it feels like stories involving very powerful vampires are much more suited to the cities of Europe and the Middle East, especially those that just breathe history. In my Cairo by night campaign, that unfortunately ended prematurely, I wouldn't have hesitated at all to use The Sleeping Lord, Angelique and other super-powerful vampires. And I do like stories set in such milieus, because the historicity of them, and the great contrasts between new and old, makes them very suited for the more mythological and epic aspects of Vampire's lore. And I do love those aspects...

                                            but I also love Americana, and we Swedes have such a powerful, albeit probably very fictionalized, image of America in our minds, thanks to decades of media portrayals. So I also very much enjoy stories set in the US, but there, I feel the focus should be on much younger vampires - an 800 year old Toreador Elder was by far the most dangerous vampire in my entire Las Vegas chronicle, for example (no one else in town was older than 300). I know lots of WoD purists will hate me now, but characters like Helena and Menele just don't work for me at all - it feels ridiculous to me that they would ever end up in the US. Why would they even risk the boat journey? But never mind the logic of it all - I don't think they fit in for thematic reasons. The US is so...new. Everything about it, to me as a Swede, feels new. The US, in Vampire, is for stories where you go deep into dishwasher drama with a supernatural twist, where you explore just what it means for someone to have lived for a century or two as a vampire - and that really, is sufficiently epic and thought-provoking.

                                            However, one can have the cake and eat it, perhaps. Mexico is a perfect setting for a merge between these two thematic opposites, because it feels much older than the US, and has a very compelling history, and more old architecture preserved, is more exotic than the US for my Swedish eyes, more full of mystery. Yet it's also a very modern country, with great contrast between old and new, a maze of dirty concrete houses scattered among old churches. And in Vampire, it was the capital of the Sabbat, so its easy to imagine that some very strange creatures ended up there. Yet it might also have traces of what was before, of those mysterious vampires, or their servants, whose lands the Sabbat conquered long ago. So in my current Mexico City chronicle, I've filled the city with vibrant, ambitious, very young vampires - but there's also a small but significant core of quite old elders trying to control them. And in this chronicle, I might use the sleeping methuselah plot despite my better judgement...for it just fits Mexico City's backstory in Mexico City by Night so well, and my plans for the chronicle too... so yeah, why not? And the players aren't that seasoned as Vampire players go, either, the trope might not feel as tired to them as it does for me. Let's just indulge in a classic methuselah plot. They're so cool after all.

                                                            I'd agree the "methuselah slumbering under the city" trope is more fitting for Old World cities, especially if the GM wants to use multiple methuselahs. But I think the rationales for "why are such old vampires in such young cities" worked pretty well in the published By Nights when we consider their methuselahs' backgrounds.

                                                            * Menele faked his death and fled to the Americas to escape Helena.
                                                            * Christopher Houghton fled to California after mortally embarrassing his sire.
                                                            * Derrick Zeel hangs out in San Francisco because he isn't particularly involved in the Jyhad. (In my headcanon, he also masquerades as an ancilla much younger than he truly is, because advertising he's an Antediluvian's childe is asking for trouble.)
                                                            * Marcus Vitel was sent to D.C. as a pawn of his sire.
                                                            * Shaitan is an indigenous (debatably) vampire in Mexico City who long predates the arrival of the Conquistadors.

                                                            By and large, we see that the methuselahs who wound up in American cities were losers in the Jyhad. The New World was a good place to run.

                                                            Montreal, New Orleans, San Francisco, Milwaukee, New York, and Mexico City also lacked methuselah puppet-masters manipulating the city's politics. You had Shaitan and Tzimisce in the last two books, but they were largely uninvolved with the city's Kindred, even to the point of lacking character profiles and biographies. I think White Wolf did a pretty good job at establishing the "methuselah slumbering under the city" trope as part of the game's lore without overusing it.

                                                            I thought Mexico City in particular hit upon an interesting point... just because there's a methuselah in the city doesn't mean that it's manipulating the younger Kindred as its puppets. Chicago established that trope with Helena's and Menele's Jyhad, but Helena was awake and Menele had an Auspex 6+ power that let him remain partly aware while in torpor. When Helena was asleep, she still had Prias. Both of them also went to a lot of trouble to call their various Dominated or blood-bound pawns and progeny to the city. But for a torpid methuselah who lacks Auspex 6+ and hasn't gone out of their way to preemptively establish control over the city's younger vampires, it's entirely possible for them to be non-players in the local Jyhad. There is only so much you can do from torpor. Diablerie: Britain and Diablerie: Mexico both offer good examples of this, with torpid methuselahs who aren't actually manipulating anyone and just want to be left alone.

                                                            Also tying into that is towards what end a methuselah is manipulating younger vampires. Helena and Menele take a very active hand in Chicago's politics because they're trying to use the local Kindred as catspaws to kill their archrival with. Shaitain and Christopher Houghton, in contrast, don't have an archrival in the same city and exercise a much lighter hand in local politics. Christopher props up Don Sebastian and kills him when he gets a bigger crush on Jeremy, then uses the equivalent of Star Wars battle meditation to help Jeremy drive out the Sabbat, but that's really "all" he does. Most vampires go about their nightly unlives free of his influence, even if his actions had a profound role in the shaping of the Free State. Your average Anarch is not likely to die in a war he's instigated.

                                                            Another neat example of three methuselahs engaged in an active feud was Dark Colony. You had three local elders who were pawns of methuselahs... still in Europe. Who lacked character profiles and biographies in Dark Colony, because they weren't in the region. I thought that was a really cool approach which made a lot of sense. There was no particular reason for these Roman-era methuselahs to make a dangerous journey across the Atlantic, so they didn't. None of them had actually lost their Jyhad to the same extent that Menele did against Helena. The fact those methuselahs could still exert control over their progeny from an ocean away also nicely established what old creatures and effective puppet-masters they were. I think that sort of setup should be more common in American cities than has been depicted, with princes and primogen acting out the feuds of slumbering grandsires still far away in Europe.


                                                            In my games, whether I use Antediluvians or methuselahs really depends on their scale. If I were running a globe-trotting chronicle whose focus was global plots, I'd probably use super-old elders a fair bit. I tend to believe less is more with such characters, though, especially Antediluvians. PCs shoudn't run into the same one more than once, maybe twice, in the same chronicle. Giovanni Chronicles, for example, doesn't need the PCs to have a private meeting with Cappadocius and later witness Augustus diablerize him. Having a meeting with "just" Japheth works perfectly fine. There's nothing I can remember Cappadocius revealing that an old-as-dirt methuselah also couldn't.

                                                            One of the classic problems with using high-powered NPCs in general is keeping the PCs' actions relevant. Transylvania Chronicles and Giovanni Chronicles were both horrible at this. I wouldn't be interested in playing either chronicle unless the GM told me they were rewriting the parts where Antediluvians showed up. It is just a waste of everyone's time to play out scenes where PCs amount to cheerleaders and bench-sitters for the GM's characters while they go off and do the cool things.

                                                            There are tons of ways to avoid that, too. Make Tremere's plot to take over Goratrix's body only able to succeed with the PCs' help (or let them sabotage it). Let the PCs determine whether Augustus is able to completely diablerize Cappadocius or remain "merely" of the fourth generation. One of my favorite tactics in elder vs. elder fights is to have the two NPCs be roughly evenly matched, and to make PCs the x-factor that tips the scales in either direction.

                                                            Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.
