    Regional Settings - New England

    01-28-2023, 03:37 PM
    Something I do on my free time is think about VtM and try to flesh out the setting in my head for better use in my games. I’m sure that I’m not alone in this and so I’m interested in getting an idea about what other people are thinking about things.

    Recently I’ve been thinking about regions of North America and trying to get an idea about how I’d handle them in my games and what a hypothetical source book for them would look like and how that could help explore options and flesh things out in the setting.

    So when I decided to take this to the forums, I figured I’d start with a region that actually did receive a sourcebook, New England. I know that Dark Colony is considered something of a mixed bag, so I figured that people probably had ideas about how they’d handle a revised version or an alternate version for that use.

    All that said, here’s some of the things that I’ve been thinking about re:New England VtM as a regional setting.

    •To the North of New England we have Montreal and the Quebec City-Windsor Corridor and to the South we have the Northeast Atlantic Megalopolis (New York City, Philadelphia, Baltimore, DC, etc.); so New England is a region that’s situated between two Sabbat strongholds in the WoD. I personally view Montreal as being rather civil and peaceful as Sabbat domains go, being the theological center of the Sabbat in North America, which I imagine seeps into some of the motifs that New England will have as a region and give us opportunities to have ‘quiet Sabbat’ influence around. Meanwhile imo the Sabbat is the Atlantic Northeast as the Camarilla is to the Great Lakes; the regions where the ‘default status quo’ of the sect on the continent most clearly exists. So naturally we can have any more conventional Sabbat antics come rambling up from New York or Philly.

    •New England as a region has become closely associated with Eldritch Horror/Lovecraftian Horror, particularly as far as the horror genre is concerned. For that reason it’s a motif I would want included. I’ve sometimes toyed with the notion of having the fictional city of Arkham, MA exist in WoD but I usually play it safe and have it be cribbed into Providence, RI. So that’s one element to play around with. Scholars of the occult, eldritch mysteries, and unknown powers circulating all around the region; I see this offering all sorts of potential inspiration or plot hooks for Malkavians, Nosferatu, Tremere, Tzimisce, Giovanni, Settites, & Ravnos.

    •I think most people in the US will read some novel set in Puritan New England at some point in their lives if they attended public schools, so I feel like that has to be a motif in play as well. The idea of there being centers of hunter activity, going back to the Puritans comes to mind, particularly as iirc that’s Sullivan Dane’s backstory. It makes me think about not only having Tremere and sorcerers in the area but also a Vigil style compact of Puritan-Christian hunters who have been in the trade since the old English Civil Wars.

    •Another motif I’d want to see explored is the various conspiratorial themes that pop up in New England. Things like Yankees popping up throughout the early Intelligence services in the US, what’s going on at Yale with Skull & Bones, and things of that sort. In the World of Darkness I think those veins are ripe for all sorts of inspirational material. iirc we already know that the Ventrue Upton Rowland is involved in such things, let’s explore that further. Let’s also get into things like Warwick the Nosferatu’s network and whatever vampiric secret societies definitely exist in the region. Maybe a Gehenna cult or two as well.

    •Boston naturally comes to mind as the central city of the region and as such an important vampiric domain to consider. The Giovanni Chronicles make it an important city for the Giovanni and I’m inclined to use some of that. IMO the Giovanni are good fits for the spookiness of New England, the idea of quasi-incestuous Boston Brahmins, the old history of piracy along the New England coast, and as a central node for the Milliner family. The Milliners in particular I think would be useful to use as a vessel for the image of the shadowy mid-20th century Yankees who may or may not have had their fingers on the pulse of the JFK assassination. Boston is also a useful city for the Anarchs imo. With Philadelphia as a Sabbat city, Boston is the city I most associate with old revolutionary history. This leads me to think of an idea which Children of the Revolution also plays with, a society of Anarchs in Boston who more resemble Fortier Chartists & Liberal Reformists then the punks out further west. I would probably have this society of Anarchs be a cooperative loyal opposition to the Prince of the City, whose of a Chartist-Reformist mold on account of the difficulties a hard-line Prince would have operating as such in a region with a prominent Clan Tremere, Clan Giovanni, Anarch Movement, & Sabbat on either side.
    -I personally think that William Biltmore & Co. can be salvaged as a Prince of Boston. Tone down the Arthurian & Revolutionary history elements somewhat (but don’t totally remove them) and imo it would be very interesting to play William & his brood as being in touch with the eldritch unconsciousness of the region and that being one of the reasons he’s able to maintain praxis and largely keep the peace.

    •New England is one of the parts of the US with some significant post-Columbian history and that should be taken into account; including the Revolutionary War certainly but not limited to it. I’d want to see Ventrue and Tzimisce who have centuries old power in the rural countryside because the colonies (particularly the Dutch ones) were originally founded as aristocratic holdings. I want to see Brujah & Lasombra pirates. I want to see Puritan and Quaker witch-hunting societies. I want to see trading magnates, both urban like the Boston Brahmins and rural like the family from Dark Shadows. I do want to see something about the American Revolution, particularly the old implications that the events of the Revolution were closely linked with the First Sabbat Civil War, and what the ramifications of that were. I also want to see what role New England played, if any, in the Crusades which would eventually see the Sabbat take power throughout the Atlantic Northeast.

    These are all the things that came to mind to me from my brainstorming about the region of New England for VtM. I hope that some of y’all found it interesting or enjoyable and I really hope that you’ll share any ideas of your own about what New England ought to look like in the World of Darkness.

                  I will be posting other things on this thread, but for now I will limit this post to commentary on just the Triad.

                  It was a major mistake of the original sourcebook to place the Triad as arriving in 1895 and bringing in nameless British vampire goons as a retread of the American Revolution. Nameless goons without motivations aren't good VtM characters - they are orcs. However, it is easy to fix this. Simply bring in the Triad prior to the American Revolution - early 17th century. Let them lead the rebellion against the Ventrue political domination of the New England colonies. They lead the rebellion and win.

                  Then in 1895, the Triad become overtly repressive as a result of them reacting against Sabbat incursions to their domains. They get scared. They succeed at repelling the Sabbat, but at a price - they become tyrannical. Their minions aren't nameless goons, but existing Kindred (which alas the ST will have to create) who decided strict repression to stop the Sabbat was worth it. But it alienated a lot of other Kindred, especially as time went by.

                  The Triad are still controversial figures. Some in the Camarilla laud them for being effective at repelling the Sabbat at a time when many other domains fall to them. But others complain that they turning back the clock and eliminating many Camarilla reforms that took the Kindred out of the Dark Ages setting. There is merit to both sides.

                  So you still have the Ventrue trying to win back influence. You still have some younger resistance to the Triad (though let's not call it the Kindred of Liberty). But it will feel more organic to the region instead of artificial. It builds on the setting's history instead of being an artificial repetition of it.

                  I will post more later on other subjects.

                          I’m excited to hear your thoughts Black Fox and looking forward to see your upcoming posts!

                          That said the mention of the Triad and their history also reminds me of something I’ve been chewing on wrt New England. That being trying to sketch out with the historical periods of vampiric power ought to look like from the early colonies through to the First Sabbat Civil War and the Northeast Atlantic Crusades.

                          One of the things in particular I’ve ended up thinking about is how the vampires of Britain ought to be sketched since it feels clear to me that they’ll naturally influence how the vampires of New England will develop.

                          Britain has generally been described as being somewhat apart from the mainland Camarilla of the Old World (if one imagines that the Great Lakes are the Camarilla status quo of North America then I have to figure that the French & German cores are the Camarilla status quo of Old Europe). So since in Vampire, the history of Mithras is analogous with the vampiric history of Britain, I have to figure what’s going on contemporaneously with Mithras in Britain will effect New England.

                          (please forgive any inaccuracies with either the history of Mithras or Britain in this post, it was made browsing over the wiki articles of both)

                          So Mithras arrives in Britain alongside the Romans, goes into torpor, and rises in time to make himself narratively connected with the post-Norman history of Britain. We know after that Mithras becomes the leading figure of Britain during the Middle Ages, via the Baronies of Avalon, and refuses to compromise his power by joining the nascent Camarilla even after the Anarch Revolts begin (although he does streamline things a bit). I wonder if we’re supposed to view the medieval Baronies of Avalon as a sort of vampiric Magna Carta in this period, based on the name and the idea that Mithras began to take a more active role in directly controlling affairs between the Anarch Revolts and the events of the Giovanni Chronicles in 1666. The mentions of the War of the Roses and Toreador strongholds in Scotland, plus some conflicts between England and Scotland, also makes me wonder if we’re supposed to imagine that the Toreador of Scotland are acting as a fifth column of the Camarilla against the Baronies during the early modern period while Mithras is still holding out. Then there’s the Tudors from up until the start of the period where the American colonies would be coming up and how to consider that factoring in. So from the period of the American colonies springing up you’ve got the Stuarts, the English Civil War, the Glorious Revolution, the Acts of Union, the Hanovers, and the Empire starting to come into its potential. Plus of course the French Revolution is a couple decades off.

                          That’s the kind of stuff that keeps coming around in my head when I try to think about how to bring together real history and vampiric history. Trying to have an idea of what cliques had power in what areas. For instance I can imagine one scenario where the Southern Lords & the New England Ventrue/Baronets self-consciously style themselves on the Baronies while the Big Atlantic Urban domains are nurturing larger vampiric populations with Chartists/Camarilla Reformists who seize the energy of the Revolution to further their own ends.

                          But this is admittedly all off the cuff. If I really want to weave in those elements of real life history I’d definitely need to do more chewing on them before having any certain ideas. That said however, I hope that it helps explain the sort of ideas that I’ve been chewing on and is the sort of thing which is also of interest to you all.

                              The next thing is to incorporate some actual lore into the setting that is already established, but which for some reason is absent in Dark Colony. You can't have Boston without some reference to canon characters like Jeremy MacNeil, Crispus Attucks, and Marguerite Foccart. Boston and the early days of the Revolution are supposed to be important in what will one day become the new Anarch movement and establishment of the Los Angeles Free State.

                              I would hold that originally the Triad were seen as part of this push for freedom. Roman Pendragon and William Biltmore being seen as agents of freedom (Warwick was there too, but is supposed to be unknown). We're told that the clans who challenged Ventrue dominance in New England were the Brujah, Gangrel, and Malkavians so this doesn't change too much. It's only later that MacNeil and others realize that Pendragon and Biltmore have their own reasons to overthrow the Ventrue, and it is not in the cause of reforming Camarilla society but their own ambitions. After 1895 that cannot be denied. In Pendragon's case, he effectively splits the Brujah as many Brujah still flock to him as a strong Brujah Prince looking out for clan interests.

                              So MacNeil and his friends become enemies of Brujah like Roman Pendragon who represented the old European feudal elite. While they are no longer present, there are plenty of Brujah who are their descendants in the region. They consider themselves the "real" Brujah against the elder Roman Pendragon and his faction, whom they believe betrayed the ideals of the Revolution by simply becoming another Prince.

                              I would change Pug Jackson, instead of being embraced in West Africa and then subsequently enslaved by white Ventrue and brought to the New World (!?!), to being a normal black slave brought to New England and subsequently freed and embraced. I don't understand the logistics or logic of enslaving an existing vampire and bringing him across the Atlantic (to Louisiana, not even anywhere near New England). Pug is canonically 9th generation and embraced in 1734 in Africa. So depending on what generation and age the ST wants him to be, he could be the childe of any number of Anarchs. If you want to keep the generation the same, MacNeil would be the sire though his embrace would be placed far later. Or if you want to change the generation, then either Marguerite or Crispus could be his sire. Or if you want to keep Pug older, you'll need some other Brujah to do the deed. You'll also need to work out the specifics of how he got to Boston (slavery was still legal in Massachusetts at the time of the Revolution). In any case, he should be a contemporary of these other Anarchs. He keeps in contact with his sire and a mentor and is an important Anarch leader in the region because of his personal connections. He should also be given some reason why he choose to stay in the area rather than leaving the others. I'd probably portray him as someone who code switches between a street smart gang leader and an educated, respectable person. Someone who can act both like Denzel Washington's and Andre Braugher's characters in the movie Glory as needed.

                              The Anarchs are rather neglected in the Dark Colony sourcebook. While mentioned, they don't have named NPCs in the setting and the Kindred of Liberty are not so much Anarchs as "American" vampires of the Camarilla struggling against the "British" vampires of the Camarilla. The canonical Pug isn't even an Anarch, but a member of the KOL. I think Pug would be a good figure to fix that with only a few changes to his background. You can then build an Anarch coterie around him. I'd probably limit it to some big umbrella coterie where all the Anarchs have banded together because between the Triad and Sabbat, they probably are more concerned with survival than with political disputes. That doesn't mean that individual members would not have differences, but that those are put aside because of the needs of survival. Though I could also see one or two other very small coteries of Anarchs (say 2-3 members) out there as well.​

                                  The Gangrel should be more prominent in the setting. In the history section of Dark Colony, generic talk about the Gangrel is quite interesting, but we only get one example - and he's not even originally from New England!

                                      Here are two other sources of inspiration I would use.

                                          And some notes on the Tremere.

                                          Since Brown University in Providence is the model for Miskatonic University in Arkham, the main Tremere chantry in the region is based there (not Hartford as per Dark Colony). They investigate a lot of "witch haunted" New England. Their presence in Providence, the stronghold of Warwick, presents some interesting conflict. I have it be the only Chantry in New England, although the chantry does have several safe houses set up around the region so the local Tremere have places to stay. The New Hartford safehouse is believed by non-Tremere to be the "home" of the clan, and that their true chantry in Providence is a secret known only to them and Warwick's Web.

                                          Evan Rhys (John Reiss) is basically the same Tremere, but instead of being some Austrian in colonial New England, I made him some Welsh colonist. I would change his sire to one of the two Tremere NPCs name dropped in the history section - Madeline Coventry or Baladin. Because why wouldn't you do that? One of the big flaws of Dark Colony is that so much of the detailed history that is provided does not show up in its actual NPCs. This would fix that. Other than the slight name change and some early history, it doesn’t change his function in the chronicle. Although I do have “John Reiss” be a mortal alias of his. He is one of the few Tremere who has intimate details of the feud between Coventry and Baladin, and his exact role during that struggle would be left up to the ST.
                                          ​
                                          ​Erich Weiss (Harry Houdini), the Rogue Tremere mentioned in one of the stories in Succubus Club, was originally one of the apprentices in the New England chantry. His unnamed sire is from there. There's no actual connection to New England mentioned in the Succubus Club, but the sire needs to be from somewhere, so why not? Since Erich Weiss went rogue, and it's possible his unnamed sire was punished as a result of an unauthorized embrace, this might just be trivia. Or it could be the springboard for something.

They investigate a lot of "witch haunted" New England. Their presence in Providence, the stronghold of Warwick, presents some interesting conflict.​
                                                  Something that this idea of having the Tremere and Nosferatu power centers in New England overlap reminds me of is that idea from the original Clanbook Nosferatu that there was some sort of ongoing antagonism between Clans Nosferatu and Tremere. I don’t think that plotthread was really carried out, aside from being used as an in-universe justification for the Chicago flood of 1992 but I do think it could be interesting to use that as a way of fleshing out the idea of a conflict.

                                                  We know that the Nosferatu, alongside the Gangrel & Tzimisce, were on the frontlines of early Tremere experiments with both their own vampirism and the creation of Gargoyles, alongside the early Tremere wars. That’s obviously one cause for conflict but it’s also a bit abstract. Unless you have elders of the victimized clans around to keep the animosity alive, it’s not going to come up very much. I do think there’s an interesting reason to keep that animosity around as a plot element however. The early books seemed to play around with having the Tremere be the actual designated-villains of vampire (or at least Camarilla) society. If you read the Brujah opinions on the other clans in the first two core books, it certainly seems that they reserve more animosity and suspicion for the Tremere then they do the Ventrue. If I had to guess this got lost overtime for a number of reasons, one being the development of Carthage and Rome as sticking points between Brujah and Ventrue as well as Chicago by Night establishing the former clan as the poster child of the Anarchs and the latter clan as the vanguard of Camarilla tyranny via Lodin and his brood. I think though you can get some interesting plots by keeping some of those older Tremere ideas around and that it could be used along the Nosferatu in particular. While the Gangrel can be written off as carrying long grudges because of their oral tradition and their somewhat implied honor culture as well as their general suspicion to anthro-centric magic due to their association with more ‘natural and wild spirituality’. The Nosferatu on the other hand are different imo. One of the Nosferatu’s whole schticks is that they’re the lead spymasters of vampiric society. I think that’s an interesting avenue to run with when thinking about them and the Tremere. The Nosferatu know things that the other clans don’t and they can tell that the Tremere are always up to something behind the scenes and that they’re not gonna like whatever it is - and the Tremere know that of all the clans, the Nosferatu have the most-accurate understanding of what they’re actually working on, which is certainly not something the Pyramid relishes the thought of.

                                                  The overlap of Nosferatu and Tremere with Providence could provide a good grounding for the basis of that rivalry-mutual suspicion in North American settings I think. It’s not simply that the Nosferatu, ever wary of persecution from outsiders, remember how their clan suffered at the hands of the early Tremere; they know for a fact that the Tremere are still up to something and that whatever it is, the Pyramid has long shown it’s willing to break a few sewer rats to get what it wants. What the Tremere are actually up to is an open question for STs though in my opinion and aside from whatever Vienna is scheming, the answer is probably different in every domain. That said however, of all the North American domains, imo is one of the more thematically fitting ones for the Tremere to be working on Gargoyles. I can also see the slavery motif complementing the US South or the Caribbean but the imagery of living Gargoyles suits the gothic sensibilities of New England & Montreal more then most other NA regions imo.

                                                      I’ve also been chewing on Black Fox’s posts about the Triad and I think it leads to some interesting ideas for New England as a setting. If I’m making any incorrect assumptions in your reasoning Black Fox, please correct me.

                                                      One of the most interesting parts of New England as a vampiric setting is, from a politics perspective, how surreal and archaic it would seem to most North American Camarilla of the modern era, even the residents of Chicago for example.

                                                      It seems that one of the core vampiric political debates of New England would be whether Mithras (the Ventrue Old Guard/Absolutists and the like-minded) or the Triad (The Leading Barons or the Quasi-Parliament of the Brujah, Malkavian, and Nosferatu) ought to be the final authority in inter-domain political disputes, with the Camarilla proper being something of an after thought/formality for both groups. The Old Guard, primarily represented by the Ventrue who first established domains in New England in the colonial period, arguing that Mithras (or his current representative as the case may be) is the best choice for an ultimate authority, an enlightened vampiric despot who can not only judge barons fairly but mediate disputes between them and the residents of their domains fairly, while preserving the security of all against outsiders. Then you have the Triad, primarily represented by the formal members of the Triad as well as the kindred (primarily but not exclusively of clans Brujah, Malkavian, & Nosferatu) who rejected the tyranny of Mithras and his Ventrue Baron puppets in favor of having the highest authority be a gathering of powerful barons (the Triad) who could not only do whatever Mithras could but also protect the rights of the smaller barons and petty ‘lords’ against the arbitrary rule of the Despot and their favored kindred.

                                                      Meanwhile, while these archaic distinctions are being squabbled over between the Old Guard and the Triad, you have the ‘Kindred of Liberty’ whose revolutionary cause isn’t some sort of Anarch Free State but just that the Camarilla as such establishes enough potency in the region that conclaves can be called and have their rulings enforced in regional affairs, rather then having it be known that practically speaking, either the Ventrue Old Guard or the Triad will be the ones determining how regional affairs will be run and that the Justicars are at worst a rubber stamp and at best a counter-weight forcing the locals to at least take the Camarilla into consideration before making its decision.

                                                      Then in the mix of all that, the actual Anarchs are those vampires who are too radical and too contemporary to be very energized by the Knights of Liberty’s calls to establish reforms that were progressive two centuries ago, preferring either some sort of Fortier-style Camarilla with Republican Characteristics or even west coast style Anarch Free States and Anarch Communes (Communes could be a good way to fold in the history of utopian socialism and utopian religious communes in New England as well imo).

                                                      All told this would give the kindred of New England a political landscape more analogous to a fusion of the English Civil Wars and American Revolution then decisively being the latter, but I think that makes things more interesting.

                                                      Particularly given the added twist that as of the late 20th century both Montreal to the North and the Atlantic Northeast to the South were all Sabbat.
