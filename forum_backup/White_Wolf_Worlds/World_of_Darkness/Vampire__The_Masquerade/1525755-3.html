V5 tried to take that and failed miserably because there are two different systems with two different premises. The paper a Methuselah is supposed to do in the Masquerade and the paper a high blood potency vampire is supposed to do in the Requiem are different. On the Masquerade, the low generation are an unavoidable threat, dealed differently between the highly hierarchical Camarilla and the way more "caothic" Sabbath. On the Requiem the threat is different, because you can voluntarily go in a torpor to drop your blood potency. You can't go from a lower to a higher generation because that would break the system. The fear of Gehenna is a vital part of the Masquerade. The fear of a 2k yo on Requiem is just not the same. It hit differently because he can be recently awakened of a volunteer torpor to be functional again. The blood thins with torpors, which is not supposed to happen in the Masquerade. Caine is a sword in the neck of every single vampire in Masquerade. There ain't a First Vampire on Requiem, despite of what the Lance of Longuinius and the Circle of the Crone may say. A vampire that knew the Christ that awakens in the Masquerade and the Requiem hit differently, because in the Masquerade is a blood hungry demigod that can wipe NY with a thought while in the Requiem may be a very cultivated low potency guy that can be lying through their fangs with bizarre memories that may or may not be real. The awakening of Ur-Shulgi remodeled the Banu Hakim formerly known as Assamites. The awakening of a vampire of same age that spent as long in torpor in the Requiem is just not the same. There are different games, which must be played differently, and throwing things as blood potency in the Masquerade is both ridiculous and counterproductive. Someone as Montano can't happen in the Requiem with very good reasons, because the scenario don't allow. A Gratiano in the Requiem wouldn't have the power to absolutely change, let's say, the Mekhet, because a Gratiano just can't happen in the Requiem. As I said, they're both good games with different premises, and trying to put mechanics of one in the other (blood potency, by chance) is not functional. That's the gist of what I'm trying to say. He might not even be a methusaleah and instead be an unusually powerful elder.
    • The Sighless Mother or La Llorona is a living discipline the incarnation of blood sorcery, she could wipe up the dust with Tzimisce, she does not even have flesh to manipulate, she is made out of ephemera. Her dominance of blood magic makes her a plot device.

                        V:tM has never quite conjured associations of approachable mechanics, so I'm simultaneously sympathetic to any calls for simplification (something V5 does not even aim to achieve by importing a totally unrelated system on top of V:tM's own system) and forgiving of failures to untagle the mess that is Masquerade's game design; what I find significantly less forgivable is the fact V5 didn't even have the decency to compensate for failures in game design by making itself a work of art, which has been the line's modus operandi since day one. The evocativeness and sincerity of classic V:tM justify investing in it even if one doesn't intend to make use of it as a TTRPG system; it is very hard for me to believe this level of artistic merit will be recognised in V5 three decades from now. Do people truly find more offence in misguided candor and unrepentant adolescent sincerity than in vapid self-referentiality and a pathological fear of vulnerability? Are katana-wielding vampires dressed in leather trench coats truly more distasteful than having a TTRPG in which you roleplay as a vampire in modern Milwaukee school you on the latest Drumpf tweet fail?

                        I'm not trying to "defend" the worst excesses of V:tM/cWoD (though I maintain tone-deafness is in basically all cases preferable to insincerity). I'm saying there's still a stable consumer base of overly sincere social rejects to whom an updated, streamlined version of classic Masquerade (that maintains the artistic trappings of the original) would appeal to, and who maintain a right to creative expression without having to justify their weirdness in reference to the latest asinine concern of mainstream culture. V5 succeeded to the extent it managed to engage those to whom the conceits of Masquerade appeal to; I would quite like for any future edition of Masquerade to abstain from appeals to mainstream sensibilities in the pursuit of a fresh coat of paint.

                                        This is going to sound potentially not too profound, but I sincerely wish to see high levels of play being more common or at last available. I cannot stress just how tedious and monotonous it is to constantly be chained to the standard character creation rules (whether V20 or V5) and having the standard sects in play. It is as if nothing changes and everyone is terrified to step out of the box of generic play.

                                        The most common opposing opinion I hear of this that it is potentially "broken" or "too OP", which is simply not the case. Anyone who has played Mage, Changeling, or even Werewolf knows how to properly adjust the scope and difficulty of the game to reflect a deeper form of play.

                                        I don't say this because I dislike more "street-level" games. I have had characters I absolutely love who are part of the street-level tier of play and have made stories involving such characters that I've been very invested in...but when I do it again for the, what, tenth time, I become so incredibly bored very quickly.

                                        I would love a book for VtM along the lines of Danse Macabre of VtR that delves into various levels of play and non-standard characters. A favorite trope of mine is having a low-generation character with an elder or even methuselah sire. I truly love the gothic atmosphere that that pairing evokes; being a young vampire so closely associated to an ancient being; being completely in over your head. Throw in even highly toxic/possessive romance, if you will, and you have quite an interesting character. I love playing elders especially becuase I love th existential crises, having a fully realized backstory to know where you stand in the world- there are just so many flavorful and interesting angles to play, if only these were actual options.

                                        I want to experience more sects as well. My favorite sect (besides the Camarilla) is the True Black Hand and, divisive as they are, are one of the most fascinating sects in the game for me. They cover so much of what I love about Vampire and yet, they are often shunned. Much of the same is said of the Inconnu as well, another favorite. Even the higher echolons of the Camarilla are often never touched upon. The only character I ever had that was even close to a higher tier Camarilla character was a Malkavian ancilla on the path of becoming an Archon for the Malkavian justicar.

                                        I do confess that I tend to play in play-by-post Discords and one forum, so those venues tend to be highly restrictive. I've had less than stellar experience with table games in that they have fallen apart rather quickly, so investing my time wisely is very important to me.

                                        The tedium got to me so much that I eventually made my own private server focused on such higher tier characters with the other sects available, though personal life and minimal support (though I'm still grateful for it) led to me putting it on hibernate.

                                        Disclaimer: I do know (and own) the particular VtM book that helps with this, Elysium: The Elder Wars, the V20 True Black Hand Book, and have copies of the Guide to the Camarilla, Dread Names, Red List, and Council of Primogen. Almost none of these are ever utilized, sadly.
