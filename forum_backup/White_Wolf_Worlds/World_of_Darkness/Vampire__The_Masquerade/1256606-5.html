    The idea that vampires can't see well in low light conditions

    1. Is not supported by decades of fluff.
    2. Fails to take into account how incredibly impractical, not to mention wildly dangerous unlife would have been for non protean/auspex having neonates in the period streching from the first city to early 20th century. Letters would not been written or read (unless you were standing close to that giant fire place) and the sheer, everyday amount of quickly escalatable danger that would befall someone who spent their every waking moment with vision based penalites whilst living a life of conspiracy, stealth, stalking and violence, sabotages any attempt to suspend disbelief.

    I also personally think that vampires with flashlights and IR-goggles are a huge moodbreaker. I want my vampires clad in cool, slightly impractical clothes, not riot gear. I want my vampires soaking damage with undead stamina and fortitude, not bullet proof vests. I want my vampires to fight with rending claws, flashing teeth and the occasional sword, not with M60s and phosphorous grenades. I want my vampires to see in the night.

                                    OK, so important bit on theme and mood? A core part of VtM is the struggle to remain human. Everything that pushes vampires to overlook how inhuman they've become is helping set the mood of the game.

                                    Having "blood senses" ala VtR 2e? It's all about that mood. It's not perfect dark-senses, but it's a nice boost. What you can sense when to comes to blood is the big boost... and wonderful for mood. Every vampire walking down the street is constantly reminded they're a predator walking past walking meals. The thrum of heart beats constantly reminding them of all that tasty blood flowing around. The warmth of human skin is carried by the blood. Those odd colored drops on asphalt everyone is ignoring? Blood. You can smell the misty blood on the air as some human is getting pummeled in the dark of an alley... it would be so easy to follow the trail and use the dark to sneak up on the mugger and be a hero... and snack on that asshole. Or just have it easy and sulk in the shadows, waiting for the violent blood soaked hands to leave you a gift wrapped meal laying in the shadow of a dumpster.

                                    You want mood? That's way more important than trying to make vampires uneasy because it's pitch black outside. Sure there's all sorts of practical uses to it, but it helps twist a vampire to see humans as kine. As meals carrying precious, precious blood... because just like drinking blood has taken over for hunger or other physical needs, sensing blood is now how they feel the world. They can never escape it.

                                    Want to make things scary? The vampires go into a place and all those blood senses? They pick up nothing. No humans... no dogs... no rats... no blood. Whatever lays in wait for them, whether the darkness conceals threats, or momentary scares, they know going forward means no way to slake their hunger, and every time they call on the blood, the Beast stirs a bit more, and there will be no respite coming soon.

                                    Darkness doesn't stop being a tool for mood if vampires have better night vision... it becomes a better one, but the mood is all about vampires struggling with the monsters they are, and the monsters they might become. The darkness isn't there to be a threat to their safety, but a threat to their humanity... because humans aren't at home in the dark, they don't see the world through blood tinted eyes, and they don't look at other humans and sense 'food' as their first thought.
