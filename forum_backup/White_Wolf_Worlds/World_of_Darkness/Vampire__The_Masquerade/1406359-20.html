                                        I am going to say this short little chapter, more like a vignette really, is probably my favorite part of the entire book. Part of this is just the nostalgia factor because I absolutely love Theo Bell. I think he's basically just Wesley Snipe's Blade with a thin coat of paint and working for the vampires instead of against them but that doesn't mean he's not awesome.

                                        I think this actually does a very good job of explaining a lot about the Anarchs and criticizing them at once. Their handling of Theo Bell and his "defection" is something that I was really fascinated by.

                                        Salvador, the Ancilla verging on Elder, is nursing decades-long grudges against Theo Bell who has probably personally murdered friends is someone who has no particular interest in welcoming him into the Movement. He cites the fact Theo Bell is a firm believer in the Traditions and just hates the corruption of the Camarilla versus hating the system itself. He also notes that Theo Bell may have lived a hellacious existence as a mortal but he became part of the ruling elite (while having a slave-owning master). He also cites that Theo Bell hasn't even been consistently anti-slavery but had a Darwinist view of his abolitionism.

                                        All of Salvador's arguments have a ring of truth to them but they're also very obviously motivated by the fact that he's been replaced as the Golden Boy of the AnarchsTM. Salvador killed the Prince of Los Angeles and probably some lesser cities' princes according to The Anarch Cookbook but none of that has the same impact as killing the FOUNDER of the Camarilla. It's a Tyler-level defining event (hehehe).

                                        Agata is, by contrast, charmed by the MYTHOLOGY of Theo Bell that is only tangentially related to the man himself. The idea of the slave who rises to the heights of the Camarilla, kills its tyrant, and then joins the Anarchs. It's something similar to GLADIATOR and really shows that style over substance is a powerful force in the Anarchs. It doesn't hurt that Theo Bell is a physically attractive and imposing man as she notes.

                                        I also loved the amusement that Theo Bell would almost certainly kill Agata Starek for being an unrepentant (or even repentant) diablerist but she doesn't care.

                                        I imagine Theo Bell will do his best to make the Anarchs as Camarilla-like as possible, right until they form their own breakaway sect like he and Jan did in the GEHENNA scenarios.

                                                Blood of Patriots

                                                This is middle section, in that there are better sections and worse sections.

                                                Here a vampire pretending to be a perennial student at Harvard is writing the minutes of a meeting with other vampires pretending to be perennial students at the old Ivy League school. Parts of the section have a real bro tone, in that it is one frat boy vampire writing to another frat boy vampire, and ridiculing the second one for getting too wasted at a party. It also has all the pretension of college student fans of Ayn Rand righteously discussing their plans and ethics. Those two tones do not work together very well. The frat boy parts have a tone of dark comedy. The writer might want the Ayn Rand fan club to be funny, but if so it lands with a thud (It’s not offensive, it’s just kind of there).

                                                That vampires are crawling all over colleges (hunting in college parties and eyeball deep in college groups and social life) is old news, but it is nice to see a reminder here. However, it probably would have been better by just making it about frat boy vampires, or just making it about sophomore level vampire objectivists. Trying to be both undermines the impact.
