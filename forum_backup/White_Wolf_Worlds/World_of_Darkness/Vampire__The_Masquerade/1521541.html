    Do you think Generation/blood potency undo aging effects?

    08-28-2023, 06:07 PM
    This was a notion that was coming to me after reading all posts from the effects of aging post. What is your take on such matters or house rules in general?

    To me I think members of the 4th-6th generation who were embraced at an older age with there strong blood would find such concerns meaningless as they are closer to supernatural then human. After that members of the 7th-11 generation would find the effects of being older much harder to offset and only could side step such weakness for a time as they would be closer to human.

    So what are your notions?

          I don't know if we ever got anything official, but in my games if you Embraced an elderly man with Alzheimer's you'd have an elderly looking vampire with Alzheimer's. The Embrace, even from low Generation vampires, isn't going to cure of fix mental issues like that. It's possible that the Tremere, Setites or other mystically inclined clans might have certain magical cures for those types of mental issues, and those cures would probably incorporate vampiric blood, but at base Embracing someone, in and of itself, wouldn't fix something like that.

                          In an old issue of the comic book Captain America (published in 1980), Cap is fighting the vampire Baron Blood. Baron Blood has returned to his ancestral family home to turn his hated older brother (the original World War One Union Jack whose house Cap lived in during his stay in England during WWII with the Invaders). The idea being that he would force his brother to live through eternity in a physically enfeebled condition as a punishment. I thought that was an interesting twist on the vampiric condition. So I'm tempted at having that be a thing in my setting.

                          In a roleplaying game that has XP, that is hard to do because even if the vampire character originally has only a "1" score in any Trait, it can always be raised by XP. Doesn't matter if it is Strength 1, Intelligence 1, Appearance 1, or whatever, it can be bought up to 5, possibly higher based on their generation. That is just how the game works. And I don't think an ST should prevent that simply because the mortal was old when turned rather than a much younger age. Besides, people still expect a vampire who looks like an old man or woman to still display the increased "vampire strength" we expect from the monster. So why can't a vampire who looks ninety years old have a Strength of 5 or even Potence 5 as well?

                          That is not exactly "reversing" the effects of old age, but it is also kind of irrelevant because de factor the vampire with the old body is becoming more capable through XP expenditures.

                          However, I still like the original idea in that issue of Captain America that someone is trapped in a feeble body because they were turned too late. But in order to do that, I would use Flaws that prevent XP from being spent to improve certain traits (especially attributes). There isn't one specific Flaw that easily matches to that. But the ST/Player could either select multiple Flaws to reflect the negatives of old age, or design a new Flaw of appropriate points that does the things she wants. Some example of such Flaws could include:

                          Hard of Hearing or Deaf
                          Bad Sight or Blind
                          Disfigured (not a true "disfigurement", but representing the effects of old age on appearance)
                          Deformity (not a true "deformity", but representing the physical decline of old age)
                          Lame
                          Weak Willed

                          Certain Derangements might also be appropriate to represent the mental decline some people go through (and not only the elderly, some people unfortunately suffer from early onset Alzheimer's Disease or other illness).

                          Flaws are harder to overcome because they cannot be bought off except by ST approval. And these Flaws either set hard limits by which certain Attributes cannot be raised, or apply permanant penalties to their die rolls.

                          So rather than make this a purely mechanical issue, this should be something an ST should decide how to handle. If the ST (or Player if it is a PC) wants their age related restriction to be more or less permanent, then select the appropriate Flaws. And if an ST wants to mitigate that in some way, then maybe one's Generation can eventually offset that (after some point in their unlife's existence, the ST allows certain characters who meet the requirement (say being of 4th-6th Generation and over a hundred, two hundred, a thousand years, etc.) to buy the Flaw off finally.

                          So if I wanted to create an old man in a wheelchair whose bones are weak from calcium loss and covered in lots of liver spots and other unpleasant skin conditions, I might give that character the Flaws of Lame (walking speed is one-quarter that of a normal human, and running is impossible​), Disfigured (difficulties of all die rolls relating to social interaction are increased by two, Appearance rating may never be greater than 2​), and Deformity (say lower both Stamina and Dexterity by two). That's eight points of flaws, so they'd only gain 7 freebie points if creating a PC, but that is irrelevant for NPC purposes. If I wanted it to be more or less severe, I'd just adjust. No matter how much XP could be pumped into that character, there is just going to be certain limitations they cannot overcome.

                          But an unusually active old person in excellent condition for her age? I'd not give any Flaws and just start them at perhaps lower than average Attributes to reflect their body's current condition. And they could just spend XP however they like just like any other character.

                          Just decide which scenario applies to whichever specific character.
