    Some simple ideas for Appearance houserules to make it useful? Revised/3e/V20

    07-19-2021, 10:34 AM
    We all know that looks can get you far in life even without education or high IQ (can get you even to hollywood even if you don't have talent). But by default Apperance in Vampire TM is useless and that's bad becasue many players love to play that pale, sexy vampires but that costs dots that do nothing. So I would like to hear your houserules for it. Simple ones that are easy to remember and use.

    I think of the following (forgive my English, I am not native speaker):

    "Appearance is not just a physical attribute and how good your face look. It's also how you present yourself on social events, parties, official meeting etc. You know the latest fashion trends and what suit to chose for meeting with Price and what for business meeting. You have different pair of shoes for every occasion and you know where you can allow yourself to wear tie and tied your hair and when you can allow yourself to wear mini-skirt and aggresive makeup. You also feel in formal cloths like in your own skin and you are not even slightly uncomfortable in evening dress on high heels.

    System: In every social situation where good look and presentation would give you advantage you can add your Apperance atribute value to your social dice pool of Charisma and Manipulation rolls (how many is up to Storyteller but minimum 1 extra dice). This does not work with powers that force your target to feel certain emotions or obey you, like Domination or some Presence powers like: Dreadful Gaze or Summon (but may work with Awe or Entrancement)".

    So for example if you want to talk your way as a male vampire through some male bodyguards that are straight you won't get Apperance bonus, becasue they don't care. They might even be slightly irritated by how good you look.

    But if you were a female vampire or met a homosexual bodyguard - suddenly your social dicepool for your Charisma + Emapthy rolls will increase becasue you are just so good looking that even a slight hope of chance to hit on you will carry you through a lot of dialogues. With Appearance 5 your Storyteller gives you 4 extra dice to add.

    But when you meet atractive female that is married that bonus may be reduced to 2 bonus dice. Yes, you are so handsome that she will flirt with you but she is devoted to her husband so she won't be as easy as that collage girl from club yesterday night.

    Same if your party got invited to talk about business with Ventrue CEO. You know that this calls for single-breasted jacket in techno strethch from Prada and so the Ventrue CEO is impressed by your taste and professionalism. 3 Extra dice.

    What do you think? Little bit Storyteller depending but I feel this could at least make Appearance "called for" in every social situation, giving it an important role and not to be forgotten.

      In my homebrew, I stole Heavy Arms' suggestions from another thread and combined it with Exalted 3e's rules. So it covers body language, poise, composure and unspoken communication as well as looks.

      You can add a bonus to positive social influence actions equal to the difference between your Appearance and your opponent's Wits, if appropriate, which makes it very useful. You roll it with Subterfuge to hide your intentions from casual observers (e.g., to blend into a crowd or hide your murderous intent at Elysium).

      Anyone can also take the 0-pt. Flaw Hideous to make your Appearance a marker of how horrific you are. It applies your Appearance as a penalty to seduction attempts (unless your target is into that), but adds to Intimidation attempts or similar in the same way standard Appearance adds to 'positive' social influence actions.

      Obviously, Nosferatu always have to take the Hideous Flaw, and they tend to have high Appearance rather than low, but it means something very different to a Toreador's high Appearance. I like that change to the rules, since Appearance is no longer a dump stat and Nosferatu ugliness is a weapon as much as a Flaw.

      So far it works great, and is better than the Composure stat of V5 and VTR because it's not as narrow; it feels more vampiric to have an Attribute specifically for seduction, intimidation and posing; and you can use it for performing or ritualistic behaviours too.

          I think appearance gets a bad wrap, honestly, but that's because how it is mechanically in default rules.

          I give everyone a speciality at appearance 1, and appearance specialities are very important because they dictate the specifics of how people respond to your good/bad looks. Appearance specialities can also change. The main thing here, is that there are high-risk/high-high reward specialities where some people may react exceptionally possitively but other people will be offput by your speciality, and more neutral, inoffensive specialities. Appearance specialities are relatively dynamic, and you can change them with effort.
          I basically make everyone judgmental. If you've got 1 appearance, people don't want to talk to you. If you've got 2 appearance, people don't have incentive to talk to you, but aren't particularly adverse. Perhaps if they're a little desperate at the club you'll get some attention. Three appearance is useful. 4 appearance veers into a realm where your good looks have a few drawbacks, as people are threatened by your good looks (the severity of course influenced by your speciality) but more often they will go out of their way to accommodate you. but at 5 you're an angel that makes 4 feel inferior.

                  Originally posted by Viben View Post
                  System: In every social situation where good look and presentation would give you advantage you can add your Apperance atribute value to your social dice pool of Charisma and Manipulation rolls (how many is up to Storyteller but minimum 1 extra dice).
                  This probably isn't a great solution. The system strains heavily once dice pools start going over 10. It's just not calibrated for characters that can get 15 dice for basic mundane actions as humans let alone supernaturals that can add even more on to that. A small bonus of 0 to 3 dice could work (ala the suggestion to use Exalted 3e's rule) because it's situational and doesn't run into:

                  So for example if you want to talk your way as a male vampire through some male bodyguards that are straight you won't get Apperance bonus, becasue they don't care. They might even be slightly irritated by how good you look.
                  This. These sorts of metrics are always a pain in play. One of the things I like to keep in mind when thinking about house rules is that a rule's job is to remove tension between the players and the GM by creating a neutral arbitrary baseline to refer to. When you put too much on GM fiat, esp. regarding cultural norms and customs with a lot of disagreements about how "real" or strong they are, you run the risk of subverting the very point of a rule by creating points where the players and GM are predictably going to argue (even though both are doing so for good reasons, not just one person being an ass).

                  Why doesn't Appearance matter to straight men talking to other straight men? Just because it's not a matter of seduction doesn't mean looks don't stop mattering.

                  A well dressed man in the latest style that carries himself surely and confidently is going to have a much easier time talking his way into a nightclub than a man that looks like he just crawled out of a dumpster and is stumbling around like he's already dangerously drunk. When you make the bonus dependent on this sort of rules call, you're both undermining the idea that Appearance isn't just good looks, and you're prompting the GM and players to get into game-distracting arguments over whether or not looks should matter in a given situation.

                  This is why the aforementioned Exalted 3e rule is a bonus of Appearance vs. another trait. It completely avoids any such arguments because it isn't about whether or not looks should matter for the bonus to apply, but how well a target of social influence resists their natural urge to judge based on looks.

                  Things like sexuality, dress codes, and so on can just be handled by difficulty modifiers to rolls instead of directly interfacing with Appearance's base rules.

                  Originally posted by adambeyoncelowe View Post
                  In my homebrew, I stole Heavy Arms' suggestions from another thread and combined it with Exalted 3e's rules.
                  Name check, woo! Also, the Ex3 add on is an interesting one, I might need to toy with that for myself at some point.

                  To restate what's being referenced:

                  In my WoD homebrew, I treat Appearance like any other Attribute. No "first impression" dice-cap non-sense. In order to make it valuable though, it's specifically the Attribute of non-verbal communication (which is really important because so much of normal human interaction relies on non-verbal cues even if the digital era is starting to pressure us to adapt to a reality where we lose a lot of those cues).

                  One of my go to examples is dancing. Dexterity + Performance is great for perfectly executing difficult routines when you're looking at something like an audition or competition. Appearance + Performance is what makes your dancing stir emotions in people even if it's not 100% perfect to some predetermined set of moves. Dancers (from high brow ballet to dirty strip joints) might need some high baseline Dexterity to be good at their job, but Appearance is what separates the average and the top. The prima ballerina doesn't have to be the best technical dancer in the ballet, but they have to be the one that knows how each of those moves tells a story, and captures the audience so they feel the emotional weight of the story without a single word spoken. The best tipped stripper isn't the one that has the best conventional looks off stage, or again the best technical dancer, but rather the one that's best able to perform on a thin line between showing too little and showing too much so that the audience is hooked on wanting to keep watching to see more.

                  Though as adambeyoncelowe noted, my homebrew makes sure to stress that all forms of non-verbal social interactions default to Appearance. Blending in with a crowd, threatening someone, being a good shoulder to cry on... all things that can be primarily accomplished by sending the right non-verbal signals.

                  Originally posted by Lynceus View Post
                  While I hate the first impression rule (limiting Social die pools to Appearance when you first meet someone), I do often make people roll Appearance for any Social rolls they make with someone they don't know. Even for Disciplines. THIS has gotten some players to feel like Appearance matters when they go to Dominate some random mortal and I'm like "now don't forget, it's Appearance + Leadership, not Manipulation".
                  I really don't like that "have to roll Appearance" for first impressions thing. It completely destroys a lot of archetypal characters who's words make up for not being great as presentation. It's also very... unreal... to how we perceive things. Since social situations aren't handled with the level of detail as things like combat, your first impression isn't just the first 3 seconds of seeing them, but also the first things that you speak, and lots of other factors.

                  It's also worth remembering that in the desire to fix perceived problems with one Attribute, you don't want to cause more. I usually point to Exalted 2e here because it tried to make Appearance matter, but in many ways over corrected and Appearance became the social Attribute that really mattered and felt more like Dexterity than a balance Attribute. Letting Appearance be rolled for lots and lots of Discipline activation just encourages high Appearance characters that "social-hobo" around because as long as they don't engage with long term social conflicts, they get to have great rolls on all their stuff.

                                                      For reference, here are my house rules:

                                                      Appearance
                                                      Appearance is a measure of body language, subtle expression, composure and physical presence. It is the Attribute most closely tied to one's Demeanor and Bearing, and hence is useful for disguising one's true intent or passing furtive messages without uttering a word. Appearance covers non-verbal communication, including appearing nondescript, blending in, or passing oneself off as someone of another class, rank or profession.

                                                      The most obvious examples of Appearance come from entertainment: a dancer might use Appearance to convey joy through his movements; an actor might use Appearance to embody her grief or inner conflict when playing a silent contract killer. But there are plenty of examples, too, in everyday life: a bouncer might use Appearance to exude a sense of threat; a police officer might use it to appear more authoritative; a politician might use it to portray sincerity or authenticity.

                                                      A vampire might find several of these uses pertinent. She might give off an aura of vulnerability to attract her prey. She might wish to present herself as trustworthy at Elysium, even as she plans to undermine the Prince. She might even use it to communicate silently with her pack mates when imprisoned behind enemy lines.

                                                      • Poor: You're an open book. You're clumsy, obvious and ungraceful.
                                                      •• Average: You can act sad at a funeral but you're not winning any Oscars.
                                                      ••• Good: You were good in drama class. You're good at giving off the right cues to get what you want.
                                                      •••• Exceptional: You're Al Pacino in The Godfather. You're Amy Winehouse before she even opens her mouth.
                                                      ••••• Outstanding: With but a look or a slight shift of your weight, hardened criminals break down weeping or rush to help you. By shifting your features, you become either unrecognisable or instantly mesmerising.

                                                      Hideous (0-pt. Flaw)
                                                      You are memorably ugly, as a result of some deformity, disfiguring scars, weeping sores, inhuman mutations, or some other curse of the Blood. Your Appearance can never measure how attractive you are; instead, the more dots of Appearance you have, the more intimidatingly horrid you look. You add your Appearance bonus to attempts to threaten, intimidate or unsettle, rather than to charm or persuade.

                                                      You must deduct your Appearance from most seduction attempts.

                                                      Clan Curse: Mark of the Hidden*
                                                      All Nosferatu gain the Hideous Flaw. In addition, you must subtract two dice from all social dice pools when interacting with those unused to your horrific visage (this does not stack with the seduction penalty; use the higher of the two). Almost all humans will be repulsed or distracted by the cursed looks of the Clan of the Hidden, though if a target is subject to a Blood Bond to you, this typically reduces the penalty by one for each step they are bound, to a minimum penalty of zero.
