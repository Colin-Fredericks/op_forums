              I share your dislike, although I actually think the opposite: it is ridiculously weak, despite the scary descriptions. It is a pretty bullshit Discipline, and while Malkavian is one of my faves, I never take Dementation Malk for a PC, but I do for NPCs sometimes. So, maybe I can help you reconsider some views, and that may come to improve your game.

              1 - Of course it has a weakness. Indeed, it has the worse weakness among the "mental powers", lack of user agency. Most powers are completely or almost completely in the hands of the ST for their effects. Running an NPC it's ok because you're the ST and can use this Discipline as justification to cause any effect you deem interesting for the story, even if it is not what the NPC would chose or benefit from. But as a PC you just get to ask the ST to decide the results, maybe make suggestions with no promises whatsoever;

              2 - Mental meltdowns of all kinds happen, they're more common than most people think (most people don't talk about them and end up thinking they're weirder than they actually are). So while yes, you may have lingering effects after the preternatural madness ends, specially if you're a recurrent victim, you'll also probably cope with that in some way soon after. Maybe it is a healthy way, maybe it isn't, but the point is that it won't be enough to be a Derangement and will get better with time;

              3 - Everything you can do with Dementation you can actually do better with Dominate or Presence. Mess with emotion and memory, induce the person to do things they never thought they would, and offer no explanation, a simple Command to cry or laugh out of context can be pretty disturbing if the victim doesn't know what's going on, Mesmerize can cause all sorts of mayhem in someone's life and replicate almost any Dementation effect on its own in some way, the few it can't you get with Forgetful Mind. It is scary to read what Dementation does directly to one's mind, but too easy to do the same and more with other Disciplines;

              4 - The power itself answers your second actual question within Total Insanity: every time the ST sees reason to allow it (like a therapy session or faced with evidence that a delusion is a delusion, both stated examples) the victim get to spend 1 WP. When you equals the vampire's successes you get over it, regardless of never noticing it was a supernatural power. Knowing that your symptoms may come from a power would reasonably be a way to hasten this process;

              5 - Following comes a quick breakdown of the powers and my comments on them:
              • Passion - On one hand you have some control over the effect, but just because you enhance a pre-existing emotion. To really control it the character would have to act in such a way as to cause the seeding emotion and be sure of it. I would call for an Extended Roll of Intelligence + Empathy for this process, although a simple Perception + Empathy would tell you right away what the current emotion to be affected is;
              • The Haunting - No control whatsoever, not even something that can hint to what the effect may come to be, except that it will be "unpleasant and obtrusive". Enough to maybe cause a dice penalty sometimes, and not even that the user has any control over. That's the power of being annoying, at a level when Dominate can turn you into a mass shooter if you're just a bit nervous and works in a bad environment;
              • Eyes of Chaos - The power of understanding people with Occult instead of Empathy because you're hasty, I guess, plus stare at random things when you want to ask the ST pretty please for a Plot Device information from heaven, that the ST only gives if they want to. The single best effect of this is reading secret messages with a static Dif 7 and a single turn, so it isn't completely shit, but it's also level 3, when Dominate lets you mess with memories and Presence completely captures someone's frigging devotion to you;
              • Voice of Madness - At least the user has to not choose from just two options, Frenzy or Rötschreck. No control over what the victims will do with it, either. And on top of that, you target yourself every time and may even end up being the only one affected after having a success. At this level Dominate can enslave a person for the rest of their life, Animalism can posses animals (and already take other vampires out of a frenzy), and Presence guarantees with a single success that a target anywhere in the world will reach you someday in the relatively near future;
              • Total Insanity - While 5 Derangements is a steep penalty, the result is strictly and completely in the ST's hands, and they're still just minor Flaws individually, nothing impossible to work around, they're more a role play challenge than a hindrance. Add to that the option to just burn WP to get rid of it with time and this power will hardly change the outcome of a scene in the way you'd expect from a level 5 effect.


              Your greatest challenge with Dementation as an ST is to keep your imagination fresh, because it is a real burden to have to come up with interesting results all on your own every time a player decides to use it. You also bear the whole burden of making it useful or useless, the reason why some players think it is an awesome power while other never got anything worth the effort out of it. I would forbid players getting it on those grounds alone, but otherwise it is really underwhelming. On the hands of an NPC it may work better, specially if you want to add a madness spice to a story but your hands are too full to go with a user of subtler powers creating it little by little.

                                      Originally posted by Cain Loup-Noir View Post
                                      Personaly i don't share this point, of course dementation isn't as strong as presence and domination at the same level, but a single use on the good person and you can change the entire story's end.
                                      Nothing that can't be said of any other action by a character, supernatural or mundane.

                                      Think about how much access the perpetrator need in your scenario to begin with, as you have to talk to the Prince to affect them. And you asked if it is subtle: you have to talk to the victim, it takes effect immediately, in a way you can't predict and vampires simply know this power exists already, it isn't subtle even if there's no other telltale for the victim. It's like you suddenly feeling awestruck by another vampire you were indifferent to just moments earlier, there's no Perception roll, but it doesn't take a genius to figure it must have been Presence even if you can't supernaturally prove. It has mundane non-subtlety.

                                      And you also play directly in the matter of power coming from the ST's interpretation. The Prince has paranoia, yes. But they also lived with it through centuries, past frenzies and trauma and every opportunity within the rules to develop Derangements anyway. And I will stress it, the user has no control over the outcome. Why a character that already deals with this weakness and had been for most of their life would succumb in this specific way? The effects will only play on this cord if the ST decides so, and the character will only succumb so thoroughly if the ST decides so, because otherwise the Discipline has no impact in their ability to make decisions about the symptoms. At the end of the day, the victim will only succumb if the ST so decides, even if the player succeeds on all rolls and has the perfect setup. You presented a compelling reason for the ST to think this result fitting, but if the ST doesn't goes along they simply have not to.

                                      Your complaint is about the power being disruptive and an "I Win" button. But it is only so if you as an ST decides to make it so, then it is not the power that is dangerous, it's your bias to grant it undue impact. It has a good description and it plays directly into a compelling theme that is madness, but it relies too much into the ST buying into the idea and translating excitement about the theme into tailoring the effects for the player. More importantly, it is a lazy way to dive into the theme, as instead of playing your target's psychology you just shout "go nuts" and they go nuts. You're feeling overburden with a weight you put there yourself by liking its description and hype.

                                      I do think it is disruptive, but just because of the amount of effort it demands from the ST. The player activates it and you're left with the burden of coming up with an interesting effect out of the blue with no guidance where to go, while interrupting the previous flow of the scene and role play. It is scary, but just because madness is scary (more than it should) and not because it delivers it in a meaningful way. It makes you want to give it gravitas and impact and let you do so freely, but actually has no tool beside the ST's volition and imagination to achieve this.

                                      On your scenario, a careful character could use other powers or simple guile to affect the Prince's underlings. Spread rumors about their allies betrayal. Make some trusted servants do things the Prince doesn't understand. Have their allies hold irrelevant secrets and then the Prince get wind that they're hiding something. Play the Prince, and the Prince will succumb to their paranoia. If instead you just get near to them and shout "be crazy", that's just a lazy plan and shouldn't have a major impact, no more than any other Discipline used with similar carelessness. It's dumb, not mad.
