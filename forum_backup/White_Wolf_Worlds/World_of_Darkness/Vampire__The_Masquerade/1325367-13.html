                                                            Ugh... we're reviving this thread?

                                                            Anyway:

                                                            I'll say it again. The issue with Bloodlines isn't that it has sexy female vampires as characters. It's how they're used. It doesn't matter if women like the game too; lots of people like entertainment that has problems with some form of bigotry. Everyone is allowed to decide for themselves how they want to deal with bigotry (aimed at them or others) involved with the media they consume; we all do it because there's always some. It doesn't matter if a sexy female character is designed by a woman either; women aren't inherently anti-sexist. Lots of women actively promote sexism, and hordes more are still struggling with institutional and systemic sexism's subtle influence on their decision making.

                                                            Ultimately what matters is execution.

                                                            The Hannah/Jezebel part of the Plague quest-line is actually well executed from this regard. With minimal hitting the audience over the head with things, it highlights how Hannah's life was negatively impacted by the prudish and nasty way sex workers are treated by society, how easily a group of bad actors can abuse her because she has to live in the shadows, and how that abuse spreads to uninvolved people because of it. It's not about Hannah being evil because she's in the sex trade; the game rewards you for recognizing her humanity and feelings. It's about how society turns its back on "undesirables" both visible and invisible and abandons them to horrific dangers; and only really starts to care when people society cares about start to be negatively impacted.

                                                            Jeannette is executed poorly. Her role in the story is to be a sex object for the player to either reject or accept. She exists to tell you that vampires can still have sex and annoy her sister. There's potential, as I said very early on, for something that could be a stronger statement and story, but what happens with Therese and Jeannette doesn't matter after you've handled them so you can get to Tung.

                                                            Of course, I'll throw out something for anyone that wants to say they don't see anything surface level sexist in the game?

                                                            Explain Poster Session, the side quest where you collect various random items, and give them to Gary, who rewards you with sexy posters for your Haven of female NPCs. There is literally no point to this fetch quest besides higher quality pictures of NPCs in little to no clothing.
