                    Originally posted by DarkKing View Post
                    That's the cool thing about Blood Points.
                    Nothing.

                    Hunger has only 50% chance of going up. It's mathematically being double the value it would be as Blood Points. Taking 3 turns to Slake 1 Hunger causes no damage. Taking a scene to Slake 2 also causes no damage. Drinking in Legacy causes 1 lethal per Blood Point.

                    So that means a Blood Potency 1 V5 Kindred has 10 Blood Points, but need to kill to go above 8, which is whatever, and can drink up to 4 Blood Points without causing any damage. Legacy always causes damage in a pretty brutal health system, if you remember the penalties and healing times for mortals.

                    As much as I like Blood Points, V5 is objectively easier on 13º Generation vampires. Logistics don't change much otherwise and don't matter, this is completely irrelevant as neither power actually uses Blood and it won't be the bigger problem.

                    The problem is that Aura Reading requires an action per target, a roll at Diff 8, does not read intentions, only emotions, and requires at least 2 successes to detect the main emotion, needing more to show enough complexity to identify anything remotely resembling hostility.

                    Sensing the Beast specifically detects hostility, though not at whom it's directed and requires a contested roll, so better for your intentions but still suboptimal.

                    Either of them will require that you glare at people like a weirdo for 3 seconds or more. Like a weirdo because you'll be darting from a target to another with sustained attention until you stare at everyone in the area.

                    You can read 20 people in a minute. Good. But in a busy street at night in a big city you'll see per hour about 200 passerbys, dozens to hundreds of windows and possible hiding spots, maybe more than a thousand depending on position, but let's simplify to 100 possible hiding spots, a number of them with active observers, and 1.000 to 4.000 cars. Best case scenario, 1.000 cars and no hiding place actually has anyone, it would take 65 minutes. It's literally impossible even if you somehow gets perfect success at every roll in 1.200 rolls (and 100 lost turns looking at hiding spots).

                    That's the scale of what you're proposing. And that would be every waking second of your character. The Flash and Captain America can "see faster" by their official descriptions, but no Auspex or Animalism power does that.

                    If your ST allows body language to mean anything, that's yet another action observing each suspect and it requires some minutes observing each target. It requires attention, not just a sweeping glance at the scene, and then a roll that can fail.

                    Double speak isn't some kind of universal code, either, and at best would require hearing them talking, the whole conversation, and roll something that can fail. If you speak their language, that is.

                    You literally can't live like that. Your character will have to limit themselves to manageable times and places just to be able to function, to have time for actually doing things. And would still have to deal with plenty of failures they can't roll again without penalty, sometimes getting botches that give dangerously erroneous information.

                    There's no such a thing as "my character can do that because is basic kindred survival". You csn do what your sheet says, and for this kind of feat you roll, being prone to fail.

                    And again you misunderstood the hunter part royally. You're right that a vampire won't loose instantly just because they blown out cover here and there. It's that being Sabbat makes no Kindred more resistant to hunters, and blowing your cover regularly and stupidly will attract enough hunters to give your luck a hard time, and enough information to make it easier for them to prepare better.

                    They use it in an extremely limited and specific setting and still fail regularly, as you'll see if you read the career of any "professional" poker player.

                    Poker players are not trying to find whom in the street has hostile intentions towards them. They're trying to figure out a bluff.
