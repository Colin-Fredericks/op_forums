            To elaborate a bit on what (I think) monteparnas is trying to get at, it seems like the problem is that you're thinking about this character more as a plot device than as a character.

            You seem to have a few basic ideas about their motivations and opinions, but most of what you have been telling us so far involves superficial details about her abilities and appearance. But to build an antagonist, those things are actually less important than their relationship with the PCs.

            *Why* does this 15-foot-tall monstrous muscle-mommy care about the PCs, or why do they care about *her*?

            It's hard to know what kinds of stats are particularly appropriate for an antagonist to have without knowing the nature of their conflict with the PCs. This is less important if you're just trying to make a cool NPC who just exists in the background of your setting, who your players may never even meet if they don't feel like doing so. They're just flavour. But if you specifically want someone to be an *antagonist*, you need to start by considering the source of their conflict with the PCs, and build out from there. Once you've figured out the nature of that conflict, then you can start determining what abilities (skills, Disciplines, etc.) are needed to fulfill that function.

            Looking to motivations a bit more generally, it's worth homing in a bit on the fact that the NPC is supposed to be a Metamorphosist. While pursuing Metamorphosis involves studying of the vampire condition, those studies have to be directed towards the "perfection" of the vampire condition. And it really helps to have some idea of what your Metamorphosist N/PC has in mind when they think of "perfection." This matters because there should presumably be an underlying rationale behind the abilities and Disciplines the characters will pursue, along with the modifications that she chooses to make to her body. In the Dark Ages, there was (in DAV20, at least) a split within the Road of Metamorphosis between the "Path of Flesh" and "Path of Spirit," which place different amounts of emphasis on physical versus spiritual transformation. The former is probably more likely to focus on physical Disciplines and stuff like Protean in addition to Vicissitude, while the latter would probably be more likely to supplement their Vicissitude with spiritual practices like Koldunism or other forms of blood magic. And then you have other practices like those of the Exsanguinists that, depending on how you look at it, could line up with either branch. All of which is to say that you have to spend some time thinking about your antagonist's approach to Metamorphosis before you can decide what Disciplines, etc., are going to be their top priorities.

                        On my phone, so just firing off a couple of points quickly:

                        1) Her being a member of the Sabbat does not guarantee a conflict with the PCs. For that to be the case, both her and the PCs would need to be on the "front lines" of some battle between the Sects. Where is it? How long has the fighting been going on? Where is muscle-mommy's place in all this? It's important to consider that most Metamorphosists don't really give a damn about Sabbat politics, so if she's getting involved, there has to be a good reason.

                        2) What is Azhi Dahaka? Ask ten Metamorphosists, and you'll probably get at least 10 answers, quite possibly more. This is why I spent a bunch of time rambling about the Path in my previous post. Without knowing what her particular notion of perfection or transcendance is, many of the skills or Disciplines you want to give her could be a pointless dead end.

                        Yorak is considered one of the greatest Metamorphosists to ever exist, and they (probably) didn't have any dots in Necromancy. So why does your Tzimisce think that it's worth the time and trouble? I can think of at least two answers to that question, but which one you pick is going to depend on the specific approach that your character follows in pursuing Azhi Dahaka. And you would choose different Necromancy Paths depending on that approach.

                                                        Originally posted by Samuelalpha86 View Post
                                                        Because I don't have a coterie I'm just putting out a concept for an NPC and want an opinion.
                                                        Aleph made a good point that a more structural and ongoing source of potential conflict might work better than a single inciting incident, especially if you don't have a specific group of PCs in mind that said incident could be tailored to. While I said earlier that you should keep in mind that Metamorphosists don't really care about Sect politics, that wasn't meant to say that's a bad idea, but just that you need to think through the reason why she would bother getting involved. If she's taking on the role of a war leader, it could provide the opportunity to raid for test subjects while shoring up her relationship with the Sect. If she's been given the job of defending an area under Sabbat control, it gives her the opportunity to take advantage of the Tzimisce predilection for controlling territory that monteparnas mentioned earlier, *especially* as a Koldun; the Way of Spirit is really good at helping to keep an eye on, and exert your will over, a large area.

                                                        The thing is, people are struggling to give you much feedback because this is still a very half-baked concept for a character. It's a stat block with bits and pieces of a character tacked on, and not a lot of connective tissue explaining how it's all really supposed to fit together. This is why everyone keeps pushing you to think more about the character's specific motivations and values, because that would help us understand what the complete picture could possibly look like. If you're just looking for feedback on how the stat block would work as a recurring antagonist, then I think monteparnas's advice from earlier is probably the best help you can realistically get under the circumstances: give her good defensive abilities and escape-buttons, and maybe be careful not to go overboard on her offensive abilities to make it less likely she's going to make a habit of murdering PCs when she shows up over and over.

                                                        And beyond that, there just isn't much else people can say until you're ready to dig deeper into her actual identity as a character. And to the extent that you seem to have "Metamorphosist" as the core of her identity and motivations, the next logical step is to want to know more about the particular details of her research. It doesn't even need to be the focus of her entire unlife, but there is presumably a particular aspect of the vampire condition that she is currently focused on studying. Achieving the perfection of the vampire condition involves maximizing its strengths and removing its weaknesses, so those are the natural places to start. Is she studying the need for vitae to sustain unlife? Is she studying the vulnerability to the sun, or the effects of being staked? Conversely, is she studying the limits of the Disciplines, or the effects of the blood bond? All of these possibilities could lead to different preferences in test subjects for vivisection, for different kinds of experiments.

                                                        Based on what you've mentioned up to now, it seems like a lot of focus is on the perfection of her physical form, seeing as how you have her focused on studying medicine and increasing her size and physical strength. But then you have her as a Koldun, who has also dabbled in Necromancy (I'm up to three possible uses in Metamorphosist research now). Unless you're tying this all together with some kind of theory of Metamorphosis that explains it all, it paints the picture of someone who doesn't really know *what* she's trying to achieve, just throwing everything at the wall to see what sticks. But really, it's seems like it's just a symptom of her stat block being a grab-bag of 'Rule of Cool' and Tzimisce stereotypes.

                                                        Describing a Metamorphosist as focused on "physical and spiritual transcendence," without any elaboration on what that means or how they are pursuing it, is a bit like asking a physicist what the subject of their research is and having them respond with "physics." Sure, that's technically true, but it's ignoring the really interesting details. Is it particle physics, astrophysics, string theory?

                                                        I think there's a lot of interesting possibilities in what you've laid out so far, but she's not really going to be interesting until you develop her identity as a character instead of thinking about her as an obstacle for the PCs to overcome. Giving her a complex personality and fascinating motivations will make her a better antagonist than any stat block you can devise. My advice is to spend more time thinking about her choice to subsume her former packmates, because there's some interesting potential there, in terms of her general motivations and desires, but it could also be an angle to consider when it comes to her pursuit of Metamorphosis.

                                                            Originally posted by Samuelalpha86 View Post
                                                            Because I don't have a coterie I'm just putting out a concept for an NPC and want an opinion.
                                                            You don't need a coterie and the basic idea is good, that's not the point.

                                                            The thing is, with the exception of specific considerations for the particular PCs (or if there'll be PCs at all), none of the other questions require you to have an ongoing chronicle, or even a chronicle at all.

                                                            Going back to the beginning of the thread: we can't evaluate the character stats without context. This context, though, may come from anything. You may be planning a city, you may be planning a novel, you may be telling stories in your head, you may be making a waifu for yourself, I've made all of those things myself, it doesn't matter.

                                                            What matter is just that we need to know in broad strokes the story you're telling with her, where and how she lives and what she does, to have any meaningful opinion on the stat block. Not on the concept, the overall concept is cool.

                                                            It's just that without knowing, for example, her approach to Azhi Dahaka in more detail, we can't say if anything in particular makes sense or not for her, or if a particular trait is explained by other aspect of her life, or if something calls for an alteration in her stats either up or down.

                                                            Overall that's the problem with stat blocks for NPCs. With starting PCs we can, at least, say if their stats fulfill a given concept within their limited resources. NPCs, since they don't have limited resources, their context become even more important to say anything meaningful about the stat block.

                                                            As far as addressing her without a story goes, I already did that in my first two posts: give her Strength 6 or 7, do the same with Perception and three or four Abilities, one Discipline at 7, for the rest you could distribute something like 5, 4, 4, 4, 3, 3, 3, 3, 2, 1. With Backgrounds, go wild. That would be a more organic starting point, although any particular decision would still go back to the need of context, a story to tell with her.
