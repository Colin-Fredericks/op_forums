                Originally posted by Iguazu View Post
                And, when I first started playing, I like the physical ones the most, Fort, Potence, Celerity, but, a vampire with only those disciplines is, essentially a pawn.
                It depends a bit on the exact mechanics one uses, and on the table's imagination.

                I once joked with a friend that Potence was a social discipline. If many Intimidation rolls are based on Strength RAW, including how to resist Intimidation, a vampire with high Potence and Willpower can hardly be turned into a pawn. And the attempt is risky on its own.

                Celerity is also very dangerous, since Dexterity (which gets extra dice from Celerity in VDA20) is a horribly broken attribute RAW. I once wrote down how many common roles depended on each of the 9 Attributes, and Dexterity was way ahead of all the other Attributes, closely followed by Strength. It does little to protect you from mental disciplines, but Initiative still helps and it's not like the other disciplines give you extra resistance to being someone's pawn. They give you tools to turn others into pawns, but not to resist. The game system heavily favours those who act first.
                I also one calculated that in VDA20 you could do without Obfuscate by maximising Dexterity, Celerity and Stealth instead. It would be more convenient in terms of uses, unless you specifically want to use Obfuscate 3 to change looks.

                Finally, physical disciplines are scaringly objective. The mechanics are straightforward and hard to argue with (though they could be improved). Being hard to kill and highly lethal is one of the key advantages of vampires over humans, everything else is built on top of that, and a vampire who cultivates this talent more than others is quite dangerous. And you don't really need more than 3 dots in Dominate and Presence to do most cool things, so investing in physical disciplines can be quite convenient.

                    My two(ish) favourite Disciplines very much reflect my personal biases when it comes to flavour and concept: Auspex and Blood Sorcery (pick your flavour pre-V5).

                    For Auspex, it really just comes down to me being the kind of person who loves knowledge for its own sake. If you're that sort, then the power that helps you expand your senses, see through illusions, etc., is just super valuable. I was glad to see other folks mentioning this as a favourite, despite all the other cool powers that you can choose from the list of Disciplines.

                    As for Blood Sorcery, that just comes down to always having been drawn to the idea of mage-type characters. Being able to turn a fondness for reading and a philosophical mindset into something that grants you access to a versatile list of abilities largely limited by your imagination is something that just appeals to me more than any narrow set of more intrinsic powers.

                    It's worth noting that my limited experience with crunch biases my opinions towards more theoretical assessments. I don't have enough actual playtime to assess my choices based on what has paid off for me in practice. But I feel like I would stick with my answers, and focus more on learning how to make the most of the abilities that fit with my preferences rather than focus on the ones with the most payoff from a metagame perspective.

                                        Originally posted by Iguazu View Post

                                        Yeah, the table had a few players that were into One-Upvampireship and I agree, initiative is key, for, encounters, but, not everything has to be an, "Encounter". They knew about acting first since Cyberpunk.

                                        I meant that, if you just focus on physical-stats, you're going to miss out on the other aspects of Vampire, which is about control, and if you can't do the controlling, then you character falls in the controlled-category.
                                        That's fair enough, though part of the issue is that you can do the controlling with a wise use of physical power. Ex.: the Warriors of Glycon. Lore-wise, they're the clan leaders of the Setites in VDA20. They are rulers, merchants, generals. Their phylosophy is focused on body and power, but their idea - and clan weakness - is to surpass others and dominate them in a certain arena or type of power. They have Potence, Obfuscate and Serpentis. How could they achieve this with that discipline spread? Combining Obfuscate's information gathering (through invisibility) and manipulation (through Mask of a Thousand Faces, improved by Serpentis 1 facilitating social rolls) advantages with the sheer intimidatory advantages of Potence and Serpentis 3 and 4 - talking about Disciplines alone, without considering other things they might do. And to some extent, depending on how the table plays and how the ST runs social interactions, those combinations can be more effective than Presence and Dominate, if one knows what they're doing.

                                        The issue is that the rules of social interactions, and also the use of backgrounds, are fuzzy and too open to interpretation. This I think undermines the social game a bit, which is paradoxical since it's a narrative game where acting and social maneuvering are highly emphasises. I think the game system ends up leaving all of that too much to how each table plays, opening the door for more squabbles between players and ST than for actual social maneuvering, except within bounds that the whole table is already comfortable with (which I think is a reduction, not an expansion, of the game potential, and I stopped playing with a local ST as a result and now hear other players are having the exact same problem with him. He thinks it's players who either don't think or are only focused on the rules, while multiple players have observed that he only recognises thoughts and ideas matching his own, which leads to either players giving up thinking and planning and being creative in how they act, or them trying to argue for their idea based on objectively written rules since he won't see our point otherwise).

                                        In theory most clans have a fair extent of social action, even if they don't have Dominate and Presence in their discipline spread. It's understood that they leverage other advantages to achieve pawn control. The rules being what they are, or STs applying them as they do, creates a bit of issues, leading to Presence and Dominate being overemphasized, in my opinion.

                                        Requiem 2E seems to do this part of the system better.
