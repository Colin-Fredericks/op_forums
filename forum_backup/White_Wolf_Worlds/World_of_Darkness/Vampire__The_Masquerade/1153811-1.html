                                    Originally posted by g3taso View Post
                                    No sense beating him/her into the ground BenjCano It is a groundless assertion, which is repeated regularly and applied to "banned disciplines" and other silliness also.
                                    The whole question is, do you want to have a strong power without caveats, or do you want something extra-grim and atmospheric? The two approaches lead to different game outcomes. There's no need to introduce 'banning' or any such heavy-handed approach. When you are presented with Abyss Mysticism, a set of secret powers and lore that's kept from even the majority of the Lasombra, and if you take up the offer, you are entering a secret society where nobody's adhered to Humanity for centuries. Sure, you could try to learn those powers while keeping your true self intact ... but it's pretty likely that you would be discovered, don't you think? The more common outcome would be a fanatical cultist, which is still playable if you're up to it, but it won't be a typical game of Elysia and running errands for the Bishop. There's much more to it than dots on a character sheet.

                                    The bigger question is, what makes the Lasombra special? They're not simply the Ventrue knockoffs of the Sabbat with cool shadow powers. Of all the clans, the Lasombra are distinguished by the sheer nihilism and hopelessness of the whole thing. The greatest aspiration of their rank and file is to make a name for themselves in order to be awarded diableries by their elders and rise through the ranks ... only so their souls can be meals for the next bunch of neonates. Their sorcerers and mystics have grandiose goals such as extinguishing the sun and bringing about a reign of darkness. Their methuselahs consider themselves to be merely extensions of an ancient will, his fingers in the physical world, with their souls and existence forfeit to his whim. The absence of light, and the absence of hope ... that is the Lasombra.

                                    ... or, you can just teach your PCs Abyss Mysticism as a set of cool Obtenebration powers, and have them level up in videogame fashion. It's your call, since I guess the alternative can be fun too.

                                                    Originally posted by BenjCano View Post

                                                    And that's a straw man. It's worse than a straw man, because I don't think that the insult I think you're going for, that video games are an inferior form of storytelling, is going to fly on a forum in which I'm willing to bet that 75%+ of the readership are avid video game players.

                                                    I have long since ceased to be surprised by encountering resistance when I try to advocate for the notion that PLAYERS should get to do cool things and have cool powers. But I never seem to stop finding it a relevant point to bring up that's what you're arguing against.
                                                    It is a straw man that you would assume I was using a straw man - who said anything about videogames being inferior? I can love Dragon Age: Origins - dark, fun action RPG - without wanting Vampire to turn into it. I wouldn't want to treat Abyss Mysticism like unlocking the Reaver specialization, I would want it to be the focus of a character and a chronicle.

                                                    I simply don't like casual treatment of blood magic, like it's so easy to get Path of X and unlock Ritual Y. It loses sight of what is a thaumaturge?

                                                    At bottom, a thaumaturge is a guy who squats in a basement and conducts unholy experiments, century after century. The Tremere get to fast-track that process and go out into the world to do things, because their elders went through and did most of the dirty work for them. For anybody else, learning sorcery involves joining a cult, making serious bargains with spirits, and essentially dedicating their unlife to improvement. Every discovery opens new vistas; where do you stop?

                                                    What is blood magic? Every Discipline results from and plays off the Curse of Caine. Blood magic results from and plays off of pride: the deliberate cultivation of one's ego. The thaumaturge feels superior to everything and everybody, wielding the forces of creation (or uncreation, as with Abyss Mysticism), and that feeling of superiority is what fuels their magic. That's why it's more of a calling than a set of powers.

                                                    Incidentally, that's one of the reasons why thaumaturges throughout the setting aren't exactly the best company, and why they mostly can't turn their powers to their advantage. Willpower 10 sounds awesome to have, but gloating is one of the things that get blood mages killed. Thaumaturges tend to resort to their exotic powers by default, enacting convoluted rituals and conspiracies when simple Discipline use would win the day. Is it in-character to have Willpower 10 powerhouses who don't exhibit any of these fatal flaws?

                                                    Why do the Ventrue, as vanilla a clan as it gets, end up being the strongest? It's because they don't cloister themselves, but go out into the mundane world and get things done.

                                                    As for there being Abyss Mystics who don't serve the Abyss, I can give you an example: Cardinal Moncada. He doesn't serve the Abyss, but he's a megalomaniacal nut who gives sermons on diablerie and how to cultivate inhumanity in the service of God. I wouldn't consider him to have 'gotten away' with using the power.

                                                    And as for PLAYERS not getting to do cool things and have cool powers, Abyss Mysticism is only a set of 'cool powers' if you assume you can get it without caveats. If you enter a secret Lasombra brotherhood and conduct experiments where you feed people's souls to Abyssal creatures, is it a 'cool power' at that point? You can say 'oh, you don't have to do that particular bit in order to be an Abyss Mystic', well guess what, you don't qualify for an invitation. What do you think Road of the Abyss types are up to on a nightly basis?

                                                    I'm not saying Abyss Mysticism, is difficult for PCs to get - after all, the requirement is only Obtenebration 3, and there are many neonates who are Mystics. The question is, are you willing to go there and shape your chronicle or concept around this pursuit? If those are merely dots on a sheet that let you do nifty things in combat, it's a cop-out.
