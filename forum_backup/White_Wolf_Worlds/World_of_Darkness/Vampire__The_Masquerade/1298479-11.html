                    I'm a bit confused by the 'I thought x weren't supposed to be sympathetic/goodie two shoes'. I always thought the point was all your options kind of sucked: Cam, Anarch, Sabbat - doesn't matter, you're gonna be played, used, and partake in shitty stuff that's bad for *your choice of Path*. Every Clan brought something to the table that was worth considering, but everyone also sucked in one way or another - Brujah I imagine are simply the easiest to use for a mainstream-ish storyline while closing an eye or two to ignore the (sometimes glaring) problems. (Disclaimer: I like Brujah, there was a time I was a sucker for their archetype, I am still fond of them.) Depending on your table and what you wanted out of the game, one of these would fit better than the others - from brooding and troubled teenager with a dark past and a big secret (what's the cheesiest you got? I think the cheesiest I came across was a 'new 2nd Generation, embraced by Cain himself to do.... something something salvation') to absolute repentless monster (powerless geek going batshit crazy over power - kinda like the protagonist of the movie Chronicles you could say). That was part of the charm, no?

                    Personally, I am thrilled about the trailer, it even got me into proper Masquerade mood again. The feeling was pretty much spot on for me. Won't preorder due to reasons, but will most likely buy it the second it is out if it's even approaching the first game's level, but with updated graphics and gameplay. Especially with mod support - that's a gift that keeps on giving.
                    The naming of Disciplines as Powers is a bit weird to me right now, but then, it's still a ways to go, that is a detail that might well be changed (or maybe by joining a clan you get access to real disciplines, or there might be some other in-game reasoning, who knows).
                    The Thinblood/Caitiff idea is interesting imo, curious to see it plays out.
                    To me, the most decisive factors are: "Does it capture the mood?" and "Is the writing on a similar level to Bloodlines? (If they manage the same cheek-in-tongue, I'd be delighted!)"
                    The rest would be common-place for any video game (balance, gameplay, possibilities of customization e.g. different approaches to the same problem, interactiveness/impact on the game world, graphics etc. etc.), and it of course won't be exempt from these criteria. Still, I appear to be far more optimistic than a lot of the people who've posted here.

                    Sidenote on the whole politics debate: I agree the games have always been political. I also see people's point on 'hope it's not too x'- basically, I agree with PMárk (assuming I got your point right): it shouldn't turn into preaching, same as Bloodlines didn't. Depict a variety of people, agendas, motivations etc., make sure none of these are totally without merit or at least relateable, and ideally none of these are obviously 'right' or 'good', and we should be good. Hell, a sympathetic 'leftist pandering' character's solution going sour while the unsympathetic 'evil rightist pandering' character had a good point would make for a fun quest, just to really strengthen the point that there are mostly shades of greys. Just make sure to include the inverse in some case, too.
                    For example: I've always felt Bloodlines' storytelling somewhat favoured the Anarchs. They were the most sympathetic faction, yes, but for me at least they managed to make a very poor first impression, and on the first playthrough, I went solo simply because they pissed me off just as much as LaCroix (had I known about the non-LaCroix Camarilla ending, I'd have gone with that). You will find different people arguing for what is the most reasonable choice here (mostly boils down to Strauß vs Anarchs vs Solitude, due to the nature of the other endings), and all of them will make good points. Same goes for how to interpret different characters and their actions: is Damsel sympathetic or not? What about Nines, Strauß, Jack? The heavy dose of cheek-in-tongue satire only made things even better.
                    I realize that 2004 was a very different time, and things need to be changed somewhat - I don't mind as long as I get a fun game. I really loved the Fishmalk- I also see how that can be problematic. Also also, though, I see the potential a more in-depth confrontation with mental illness could bring to the game. When it comes to the topics of inclusion, V:tM was quite progressive for it's time, I don't see why that trend shouldn't continue, and I don't expect it to somehow negatively impact my prefered style of playing, just as I didn't mind romance options in Dragon Age Inquisition that had nothing to do with my prefered style of playing. *snipped a bunch of stuff due to rambling*
                    So yeah, it won't be exactly like Bloodlines, because times have changed. That's okay, we have, too. I just hope it will be a worthy successor, and I choose to be optimistic about it; nothing I have seen so far suggests otherwise.
