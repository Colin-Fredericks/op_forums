    Originally posted by Loxosceles

    So throwing fireballs at vampires while riding a dragon is fine, but gender equality is too unrealistic, and ruins the veracity of the setting with post-modern conceits?
    As a female, yes, it can. In fact, it can be extremely bothersome as it basically shoehorns a female character into such a role because the person playing the character is female and for no other reason. That is not a good thing, because it basically says that 'hey, a girl playing, gotta change history so she can play the army commander she wants'. And that is putting the player's gender before the player's ability to play.

    Just because your gender has been oppressed and been seen as lesser through history, doesn't mean you cannot understand or respect historical accuracy of a setting and want it. Being limited to what you can play in a WW2 game because of your gender despite your favourite character type being mostly seen as male is bad, but it's even worse when the otherwise historically accurate setting is changed solely so you could play that character type is even worse. It's telling the female player that they are different from other players and things have to be changed to fit them in a game.

    There are female players who absolutely want to play a muscly macho man who might be misgyonistic simply to explore this kind of person, or just have fun and laugh at their own character. Because it is not real, it's just pretend and fun. And being told -your- pretend is not acceptable because of gender equality,well..that comes across as patronizing.

        As Loxo said in post #20.
        It depends on the group, levels of suspension of disbelief, the groups preferences, the setting, the group, verisimilitude, and the individuals in the group. Role-play, in any context is about expression, and everyone's expressive ability, personal expression styles, personal expression levels, and social comfort levels with various topics vary greatly.

        The Gentleman's Agreement comes into play where you're all there to have fun, and each person needs to respect that. If you can't have fun, because of differences in play-style, talk to your group, or else find a new group.

        More on-topic: The DM / GM / ST often has to go in-character as various genders (in WoD, Tzimisce make Genderqueer much more common). So why does it matter if the players do too? So long as it is done well, anyway.

        Personally, I am the DM / GM / ST often enough that it doesn't matter. Our group has a couple of people that can RP as either gender well (whether that means respectfully, or as an ironic caricature depends on game and story, playing opposite gender well means very different things when playing Mage versus Toon).
        That said, being Trans*, I play what I damned well please, setting permitting.

                    Originally posted by Loxosceles

                    It's a counterpoint to the frequent contention that gender bias is "realistic" for game settings. The argument that was made by Just John and Ana Mizuki to explain why they wanted female characters to face role restrictions that were not faced by male characters in their RPGs.
                    There is a difference between putting a restriction on what a female character can do in a RP, and putting restriction to what sort of RP a female player can be part of. It is the latter rather than the former which my post was about, and even then it was about it being a bad thing for the female player. About respecting what the female player wants to play, rather than assuming what they want out of a game.

                    I do not find gender bias a thing that absolutely has to exist in a game, especially in the fantasy game scenario that you used as an example. However, there a settings where addressing it can serve the female character well as a show how awesome they are for overcoming the bias. Or how they used it to their advantage to get things they want. Should it limit what a female character should and can do? Absolutely not. If a female or male player playing a female character wants to explore it and is comfortable with it, I see no problem having it in my games. And even then, it is not restricting what the female character can do, but using the -perceived- bias as a way for the female character to rise above what society thinks of them.

                    However, if a player is not OK with having the bias, well, then there is no bias in the game. Like many have here said, it is all about what the groups and players want in a game.

                    I hope this explains what I meant.

                        We have rather frequent cross gender play, from both sexes. I often play females, but I also feel as much female as male (not much more maybe slightly) and TTRPGs are a pretty good way to live that out, as I am IRL physically male and OK with that, but really enjoy playing females in video games and TTRPGs, especially ones with slice of life aspects to satisfy the other part of my psyche. I'd say I play ~2/3 female but not exclusively. Currently all 9 games but one have at least player cross gender playing, and about half include a female playing a male.

                        The main thing is it should be a character first, not a gender first. If their personality is just that they are a girl/guy there is a problem. I have had people who were problematic playing the opposite sex (or gay characters) but I generally dealt with it by dealing with them individually. Some players play opposite gender habitually, some rarely do. I know both women and men who run about 50/50. For us it is not a big deal; men and women are people, after all, and most of us have more in common with, say, a 21st century person of the opposite sex than a 10th century person of the same.

                        For me a major thing is if say a male is playing a female, is he doing it to have this sexy plaything, or is he identifying as her for purposes of the game (as much as he does other characters).

                        As for sex/romance, many of our games have a lot of it, but it does not tend to be X-rated (usually). We fade to black and such. But intercharacter relationships are a major thing, sex happens and is sometimes a major motivation for characters of either sex, but it tends to be more HBO Original Movie/Series than Cinimax After Dark. Most of us are married (or have been single for a long time) so there is not a lot of "doing stupid things for love" in our lives, so it can be kinda fun to have such in the games.

                        Gender bias depends on the wants and needs of the group, as does level of homophobia and/or transphobia and/or racism. In general, most games I run are real world set (currently 4/5 are within 3 years of present) and nominally ape real world levels of each, though in general it is not present if players do not want to have to deal with it, especially those who would be impacted IC. It is also more likely to come up in, say, Vampire or Geist than say Scion.

                        Pretty much all our groups games are mixed with at least two males and two females in the game (only one of 9 games currently going is all male, and only one other only has one female, and I am not personally in either of those though not for that reason). I think our comfort with romance and cross gender play may be partially for that reason.

                            Originally posted by Loxosceles
                            t's a counterpoint to the frequent contention that gender bias is "realistic" for game settings. The argument that was made by Just John and Ana Mizuki to explain why they wanted female characters to face role restrictions that were not faced by male characters in their RPGs.

                            It is something that is brought up fairly often and it is, frankly, a profoundly oblivious position to take up when discussing a game line that deviates sharply from reality in many ways (as the overwhelming majority do). Playing a game about cowboys fighting zombies, and then declaring that women can only be saloon girls and school marms, because otherwise it is not a realistic portrayal of the American frontier is pretty fucking stupid on about six different levels.
                            I don't think there is anything in your rhetoric to which I take exception, or with which I vehemently disagree. That is, I agree that we should run/play the games that most give rise to people having fun, do not make anyone uncomfortable and/or marginalize anyone, and ultimately fulfill the goals (thematic and otherwise) that the game participants (player and ST/DM/GM) have agreed upon.

                            That said, I would argue (and I don't think this necessarily run contrary to what you're saying) that certain settings militate that there be some sexism. Werewolf in particular (and especially if it is a historical setting) has an entire tribe of Garou, the existence of which, and whose philosophy, are largely informed by and are a reaction to the historical fact of sexism. The Black Furies are, in addition to protecting the Wyld places (which may or may not be the result of some kind of sexist, coded language stereotyping), a push-back against the pervasive patriarchy that has existed, probably, forever (if you believe Catharine A. MacKinnon).

                            With that in mind, I could see there being modern Garou septs, packs, Caerns, what have you, in which there is no sexism and everyone is a feminist and/or feminist ally (I added ally because there are those who believe that men cannot be feminists -- as a man, I will not opine as to that particular matter). However, and particularly in historical settings, it would seem disingenuous to have a Black Fury in a game without there being some kind of attempt by the setting characters (NPCs, what have you) to disadvantage women. My point is probably best illustrated by the fact of the existence of the Black Fury gift, "Man's Skin", that enables female Furies to appear male; this gift only exists as a reaction to the fact that women have been disadvantaged, and "other", in most societies of which we have a record, practically since time immemorial. This is an incredibly pragmatic gift, and while it appears in W20, it first appeared (I believe) in either the Dark Ages or Wild West game settings. While I am not prepared to say that anyone must run their game one way or another, I will say that having a Black Fury in a game without a patriarchy or any structural or systemic sexism, is roughly equivalent to portraying a setting with a "Resistance" faction, but no oppressive authoritarian proto-fascist regime to combat; like Rage without The Machine. Mind you, I'm not saying that there can be no Black Furies without sexism, so much as I'm saying that Black Furies without sexism are not really the Black Furies from the setting materials.

                            That said, and with regards to something you said earlier in the thread,

                            Originally posted by Loxosceles
                            The other thing... is that some of my players do experience the real world side of that on a regular basis. It is something that they have been hurt by (to varying degrees), something that impacts their daily life, something that they have trouble getting away from. They don't want to have it in their relaxing fun time game activity, realistic, hyper-realistic, or even just once in awhile. They live it on a daily basis, and no matter what the game is, no matter what other subjects it explores, even if it's a gritty, dark, dramatic, tense WoD setting, they don't actually need any more of it than they already get.
                            I would have to agree that, if a particular aspect of the game is going to be hurtful to, or detract from someone's overall enjoyment of the game, then there is absolutely no legitimate reason to include it in any game. And if the intellectual dishonesty (assuming there is any) of running a game without any sexism is entirely too much for the other players to handle, then I think you either play another game or find different players. None of this stuff (roleplaying games) is ever going to be worth hurting someone.

                                    This. Exactly this.

                                    I personally refuse to compromise my game settings for the sake of modern political correctness. I don't care if my players want to play opposite genders, sexual preferences or anything else as long as they do it well and believably, but I run a lot of historical games and I think it does a horrendous disservice to the game and world integrity if I am running a game in Georgian England and have women military commanders sailing off to fight the rebellious colonists, or women sitting in the House of Lords. It utterly compromises the setting integrity and makes me feel like I'm running a revisionist history joke.

                                    You ask why a game with fantastical elements needs to have racism/sexism/other prejudice involved? I'll counter by asking why anyone who is voluntarily playing a game of horror where they portray bloodsucking monsters who regularly do appalling things, or shapechanging beasts intent to fight the spread of human domination, or Awakened Godlings searching for a greater truth in the midst of the mundane really need to be protected from the bad things that exist in this world like prejudice? WoD is a horror setting, specifically focused on highlighting the ugliest aspects of the world, very much including prejudice. My personal policy is to have such things at the historically appropriate level for the game I'm running, but to bring them to the forefront when and if it serves the story.

                                    Now when running in fantasy settings it is a different story. When the setting is entirely fictional, I can set my own levels of such things without any concern for historical integrity (I say integrity rather than accuracy because I am NOT a history professor and make no claims to knowing THE TRUTH, but rather establish a truth that feels right for the setting based on our common understanding of the era in question). When running games in fictional settings they can have whatever levels of prejudice feel right and fit both the setting I want to create and the mood of the game in question. Prejudice is an inherently human trait, and I think ignoring it for the sake of a modern liberal sense of political correctness does a great disservice to my game and my players.
