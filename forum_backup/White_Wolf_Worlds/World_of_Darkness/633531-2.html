                                        First off : It has been mentioned that "nerfing" an effect with a long duration with more paradox would be unfair. I sincerly don't understand this notion. First off : The successes spend on the duration aren't spent to negate paradox in any fashion, that would need prime 5. They are spent for duration, that's it. A char who casts a shield spell with only 2 points spent on duration is set for the day, one who spends 5 or more is set for a long time. The one who spen only 2 may have a stronger shield, but he has to recast it completly the next time he wants to go into a dangerous situation. Especially annoying when the danger finds you suddenly unprepared, which could have been prevented with a longer duration.

                                        Duration successes are just that, they weren't underpowerd in the dark ages when you wouldn't get paradox and they aren't now. The notion that paradox is associated with the moment of casting the spell is absurd. Sure the rules may make it seem like that, but mostly paradox is about disbleif. So every time your spell does something impossible (vulgar), you get paradox. You are still changing reality, this is an ongoing process, it's just drawn out over a longer time period.

                                        A hardcore ST who wants to make paradox really hit home may even rule that every soak roll you make with your shield incurs paradox. I personally wouldn't, but it is not a completly crazy idea metaphysically speaking, just depends on the tone you want your setting to have.

                                        Getting paradox for each scene that each buff a mage uses in combat is too much. Mages needs stuff like soaking pools and damage/attack bonus to survive.
                                        They really don't. For once it depends on the kind of char you are playing of course. If you play a char who can't survive without fighting opponents of in vulgar fashion every day (or so) then you are in deep trouble. AS YOU SHOULD BE. I mean come on, that's like a chain smoker saying lung cancer is imbalanced. Though luck, try not to pick so many fights or learn fight them smarter.

                                        Mages have loads of ways to rig combat in their favour, all completly coincidental. Time 2, Entropy 1 let's you go full out "just as I planned" on everyone trying to ambush you. Mind 2 can easily keep people from attacking you. Entropy 2 can make you hit a perfect headshot with your eyes closed in panic, what are the chances? A combination of Life, Mind and Entropy can enhance your combat skills to truly amazing levels. Sure Werewolf may be fast, but it scant matters if you bring up your sword in just the right moment to go for his brains as he is trying to tackle you. Maybe you can even sidestep it too. Wow those master martial artists surly are scary, he probably trained his whole life to get such good combat instincts. All completly paradox free. Never even an enrgy shield needed. And what if you screw up even though you had those coincidental measures prepared? What if someone breaches them but once? Well nothing is keeping you from having an energy shield as a last ditch defense. Take some paradox, well too bad.

                                        If you look for a game where mages throw fireballs at each other in dark corners or fight with blows that could shatter mountains then MtA just isn't supposed to be that game. Well actually the latter part may happen from time to time in the hymalajas, the akashic paradigm dominates the concensus there. And there is allways the Umbra for such things. But on earth you don't fight flashy battles against vampires and evil warlocks. Because mage is not a game of physical, but of idiological conflict. You fight for ideas, for public perception. That is what the ascension war is a bout. Technomancers allways developing new stuff to release to the masses, to strengthen their faith that through technology everything is possible. While traditionalists try to keep humanities fading vestiges of belief in the supernatural alife, through various means.

                                        So yes there are mages that are deiberatly vulgar. Faith healers come to mind (though thanks to large enough sleeper cults they often even slip through the cracks of paradox' attention), activly trying to push the concensus back onto the "right" path. All other mages not so ideologically motivated? They just try to stay coincidental, because otherwise....

                                        Mage is all about being able to change reality, but finding reality loathe to be pushed around by you. Your potential is unlimited, but the narrowmindedness of the worlds population and (in the case of mystics) the machinations of the TU have made the world itself , the very fabric of reality toxic for you. You can rebel against the status quo and will have to face both reality and those who do not agree with you (what an epic struggle). Or you can choose keep out of the conflict and confine yourself to the limits set by paradox, allways chafing against them. Yes this is irritating as player when playing mage. "I could just fireblast that guy, but then I'd take paradox and I could just have solved this problem without it. Ah fuck, I'll talk him into giving me what I want. " This irritation however is not something bad. It simulates your chars feelings at being kept back when the world could be his to shape as he wants! It also pushes players to really think first because they just do something with magic. Which is good, because magic is not something to be taken lightly. If you do it just for shit and giggles you will be punished by the consequences. Doubly so in MtA.

                                        My Mage 2e Homebrew
