    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    Alertness and Awareness should be turned into one. They're both relevant enough for you to buy but, seriously, they are rather limited compared to other Abilities.
    I have to disagree on that particular point. I've played WoD since Day One (and slightly before, technically, due to a couple different play-testings) back in '91, and I think Alertness is as useful as most (not all, but most) other Abilities. Similarly, the distinction between Alertness and Awareness is a critical one, as one has to do with the mundane/physical while the other has to do with the supernatural/metaphysical (and before we go down the road of how various powers do "x, y and z", the Awareness trait represents a level of metaphysical intuition/astuteness that theoretically could be possessed by almost anyone *without* their having to have various powers that might not be as appropriate to their concept/implementation... not unlike how Arcane has a distinct flavor/purpose that is separate from Obfuscate, Blur of the Milky Eye/Blissful Ignorance, et al...). Now, as stated later on and in other threads, there is a validity to the argument that the two should be combined into Intuition, but then I think that conceptually overpowers the Ability: it would be similar to combining Medicine with Science (Biology/what-have-you) -- a doctor could be an excellent physician (Medicine:4) but only having functional biological knowledge outside of his applied practice vis-a-vis biological research/study (Biology:2); similarly, it would be like combining Computers with Technology/Repair -- there are plenty of highly competent (Computers:3+) programmers who only have functional knowledge of repairing/replacing the hardware involved (Tech:1 or 2), just as conversely there are plenty of engineers who may have Tech:4 but "only" have Computers:2 or (appropriate)Science: X = <Tech). It's a similar concept here. Alertness and Awareness both have to do with (P)erception, but in two very different ways. (That distinction has come up several times in the DA/Ravenloft hybrid I've been running again this summer for some friends, for example.)

        Originally posted by Just John, Forever... View Post
        Similarly, the distinction between Alertness and Awareness is a critical one,...
        The problem is if this distinction is actually critical on a mechanical level enough to justify multiple Abilities.

        There's a fairly significant difference between fiber arts (knitting, weaving, etc.) and carpentry. But they're both under Crafts. Which isn't without some problems either, but it's a lot easier on the players to have, "Crafts," and come up with ways to limit that broadness a bit, than forcing players to buy Crafts (X), Crafts (Y), Crafts (Z). Having lots of perception based Abilities is like having to buy lots of different instances of Crafts.

        Now, as stated later on and in other threads, there is a validity to the argument that the two should be combined into Intuition, but then I think that conceptually overpowers the Ability:
        Overpowers how?

        it would be similar to combining Medicine with Science (Biology/what-have-you) -- a doctor could be an excellent physician (Medicine:4) but only having functional biological knowledge outside of his applied practice vis-a-vis biological research/study (Biology:2);
        I think this is a bad analogy. Medicine is, in many ways, separate from Science to accommodate characters that aren't mainstream medical professionals. Whether we're talking volunteer EMTs, or traditional medicine practitioners, it makes sense to have a significant separation between, "I can heal people," and, "I know how the human body works." Since you don't need to know most of a Bachelors in Biology to actually perform medical tasks.

        Now, any modern educated doctor should have some Science to go along with their Medicine (and Academics, and Law, and Computers, and Technology...), but some concepts are more demanding on such things than others.

        similarly, it would be like combining Computers with Technology/Repair -- there are plenty of highly competent (Computers:3+) programmers who only have functional knowledge of repairing/replacing the hardware involved (Tech:1 or 2), just as conversely there are plenty of engineers who may have Tech:4 but "only" have Computers:2 or (appropriate)Science: X = <Tech). It's a similar concept here.
        This seems a fundamental misunderstanding of how people think Computers should be removed.

        The problem with Computers is that it isn't the niche skill it was 25 years ago. Anyone born in 2000 or later, is growing up in a world where everything is a computer. Phones are computers. TVs are computers. Cars are computers. Watches are computers. And not just in a highly technical sense, in a, "we use digital technology directly all the time."

        The idea of the "hacker" character that's just really good with computers is also extremely dated.

        Getting rid of Computers is a matter of changing the Ability list as times have changed. It's also not about shoving all of Computers into Technology. The physical aspects of Computers (assembly, repair, component invention) go into Technology like any other engineering field. The coding aspects go into Science.

        So the distinction between "coder" and "technician" remains solidly intact.

        I can more so agree with this, contextually. While most are fine for most cases, there are a few relics, here and there, that could use some tuning, though not to the "don't make sense at all..." degree, unless you can site some examples that may not be coming to my mind at this particular moment. (Brawl and Do, for example, have never been sufficiently delineated enough for me relative to each other.)
        Example:

        Alertness has no untrained penalty. So you can always just roll Perception at the base difficult to see if you notice something.

        Investigation can't be rolled without buying at least one dot. So, you can't look around a room to see if you spot anything in a systematic way (no matter how smart you are) unless you buy Investigation. You can only hope to spot something by happening to notice it (Alertness).

        Example:

        Lying has no untrained penalty (Subterfuge). Neither does trying to come up with thoughtful prose, and then deliver it (Expression)

        Acting does (Performance).

        More examples of the same line of comparison:

        Streetwise vs. Etiquette.

        Empathy vs. Animal Ken.

        Crafts or Drive vs. Technology (V20).

        --------

        Another way to look at it, if you don't like the "untrained penalties" thing but that includes grouping them by how "intuitive" they are supposed to be, is that there's no distinct grouping or theme to them.

        It's not like the nWOD that just does Mental/Social/Physical. Or Exalted that groups them by thematic roles (Dawns are warriors, so fighty stuff goes there. Twilights are the brains, so the being smart stuff goes there, etc.)

        While some concepts fall into obvious picks for primary category as-is (generally going to be Mental/Knowledge focused ones though), plenty of concepts clearly need to cross those lines to make any sense.

        Example:

        A medical doctor can stick pretty neatly into the Knowledges section as is, with Talents and Skills being flavor. Some specialties might imply ratings in a few things, but nothing core to being a good healer.

        A police detective has to be all over the map. Talents are important (Alertness, Brawl, Empathy/Subterfuge, Intimidation, Streetwise), Skills are important (Drive, Melee, Firearms, Stealth), and Knowledges are important (Academics, Computers, Investigation, Law, Technology). And that's not including any sort of specialty (K-9 needing Animal Ken, undercover needing Performance, etc.).

            First I was thinking my answer to Just John, them I saw this:
            Originally posted by Just John, Forever...
            the DA/Ravenloft hybrid I've been running again this summer for some friends
            That kept me hypnotized for quite some time...

            But, well, Heavy Arms already explained.

            We could have lots of Sciences, lots of Crafts, lots of a huge variety of things. To account the extreme variation on professionals you find IRL, you would have to make an equally huge variety of skills. That's what a simulator do, and WoD got stuck in an awkward middle ground for simulation, with Crafts accounting for an insane variety of skill sets (each of them complex itself, mind you) while knowing a culture (Academics or a Secondary Skill) is differentiated from knowing how to behave yourself in said culture (Etiquette, one of the silliest skills on the spread).

            So the question isn't if Alertness and Awareness are distinct things (they are), but if they need to be mechanically distinguished as standard(they do?). A good base system would have a simple set of basic skills equally usable on most chronicles, at least as far as possible, without dead or semi-dead weight, or skills so important that you take them anyway, but wonder why the game just forced you to buy this and pretended not to. And them modular subsystems like Specialties, Expertise, or maybe a better rule on Secondary Abilities (renamed to Optional Abilities) to help give it the tweaks you need for your particular chronicle (if you need to distinguish heavily Alertness and Awareness).

            And in the Ability Categories what's dated isn't the current distribution. Is the very dumb idea of distinguish categories as Talents, Skills and Knowledges. This doesn't make sense from the POV of how the human mind work and learn, it doesn't make sense in the POV of how literary character concepts work, except very specific concepts, and from a game design POV it is simply ludicrous.
