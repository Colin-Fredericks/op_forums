    I picked up Vampire and Werewolf, but haven't really had a chance to read them cover-to-cover. I had read that they basically just reprint the original games (as opposed to updating to the new rules), but that they included a few "tweaks".

    I'mjust wondering if anything was done to make crossovers between systems work a bit more smoothly, or if it's still wonky?

      The Anniversary Editions are whole new editions of the games, and mostly have brand new material. They don't use the newer World of Darkness system, but they do take some cues from it here and there. The biggest differences are that the metaplots aren't as intrinsic. The setting is more neutral and open. The background is still fairly in depth, but it doesn't whack you over the head with it (not nearly as much "The prince of Pittsburgh was killed by the Bishop of Philadelphia so now the Ventrue Archon...") . M20 may well end up being the odd one out, in a way, since the mechanics are getting a fair sized revision, with a lot of different options from the various editions. As for crossovers, there isn't much accounting for it. Each Anniversary Edition is its own beast, and that may or may not work for crossovers, depending on the table.

                  Originally posted by Yossarian View Post
                  The Anniversary Editions are whole new editions of the games, and mostly have brand new material.
                  In theory, that's true, but the fact of V20 included quite a bit of cut-and-paste from Revised Edition in the 20th book. I don't mention this as any kind of indictment, and I understand the reasons for it. White Wolf (at the time) saw the Vampire: the Masquerade 20th anniversary as a chance to put out a treat for those who loved the game. That treat was the V20, initially conceived as an omnibus of all the rules they could fit into one lovely volume. That grew into 'tweaking' some rules (most notable 'physical' Disciplines, Tremere clan weakness, fixes to some mechanics) and new art and fiction, but the book wasn't intended to be the 'new edition' that many of us currently see it as being. I'm not saying it does not deserve said designation, but when Onyx Path decided it was time that Werewolf: the Apocalypse got its own 20th anniversary book, we did receive a proper new edition. We should expect the same to be true for Mage: the Ascension, Dark Ages: Vampire, and Wraith: the Oblivion books.

                  The above only serves to show how imprecise terms can be. To say 'whole new edition' isn't exactly wrong, but it's misleading when dealing with V20 as the first of them. No doubt, I love my V20 to pieces, but I see it as more 'Revised Plus' than the full 'Fourth Edition' that W20 is. As ever, mileage varies, so don't feel that you gotta agree with me.
