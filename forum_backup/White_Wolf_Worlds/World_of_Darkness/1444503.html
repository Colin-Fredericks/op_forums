    Can someone explain to me the Syndicate paradigm of Primal Utility?

    04-18-2021, 10:48 AM
    And, in fact, start telling me how exactly the Technocracy and the Syndicate deals with most magic at all?

    Like, let's say that according to the M20 Book of Secrets rules that a person out in the field realizes that he's in dire need of a source of Agg damage for a bunch of super-fast healing combat homunculi made by an Etherite. He's got a good knife on him, and he's decided to use it on them. He's got Prime 3, and fully capable of making a Talisman...

    But here's the thing: What's the paradigmatic justification for being able to have an ordinary knife inflict Agg damage when previously it was a normal knife? What does he exactly do?

    Through sophisticated uses of instruments and principles... hypereconomist can... create something from nothing... use the normal prime 2 effects, although the character still needs to have some technological source to facilitate his conjurations.
    So what does he use? Does he buy a particularly expensive knife sharpener or special alloy and cover the knife with it?

    Actually, I've been looking at M20 and it's seemingly odder and odder. What's the paradigmatic justification for a lot of the stuff the Technocracy can do? I mean, Matter 2 lets you carry out transmutation. Not a lot, but it's there, even when it breaks the laws of atoms. What's the paradigm justification in the Technocracy that lets them carry out transmutation without a nuclear reactor, or is the Technocracy simply locked out of effects like this?

    Did the order of reason be able to transmute metals in the start? In Technocracy labs, do they have specialized machines that let them create near anything, but cannot be brought out of the lab due to sheer vulgarity of it?

          Most of the time, the Technocracy does indeed require a much larger amount of Instruments to be on hand in order to perform miraculous feats than a follower of a Mystic Paradigm might. It's explicitly built into their mechanics not allowing them to discard as many Instruments through raised Arete, at least according to M20. But you can do a surprising amount if you imagine your Instrument to be a super-tech wonder-gadget. I don't watch Doctor Who, but the "sonic screwdriver" is often held up as an example.

          To get a bit more extreme, the Technocratic Paradigm does leave some room for Psionics. Once you gather the Energy with Primal Utility, you use your psychic abilities to create a conduit that channels the Energy into your knife-blade, then a force-field of sorts to contain it there.

          Edit: that bit about accepting Psionics is also kind of how they handle "magic" in general. It's not "magic," it's Psionic powers that the Reality Deviants focus through ignorant superstition because they lack the Enlightenment necessary to truly understand the Rational Scientific Principles that *obviously* explain all of this.

              With a lot of what the Syndicate does, you can excuse a great deal just by appending a high pricetag to it. Syndicate reps believe - strongly - in the power of money, and in the principle that "you get what you pay for".

              If they pull out a knife and use a Prime 2 Effect to make it magickally charged, it could be as simple as the character dwelling on the fact they paid top dollar for that knife. It's probably a special knife, manufactured in a lab, at great expense. Regardless of whether the knife itself genuinely has any special properties, the Syndicate's belief in the efficacy of money will allow them to get more out of the knife than an unEnlightened person could.

              The descriptions of Sphere abilities are more the upper limit of what Spheres can do at a given level. The real determiner of a Mage's abilities is what they believe is possible within their Focus. This includes what Instruments are required, which can be as broad or as specific as the Player/Storyteller wants. This is why the Syndicate rep would need to have the specially manufactured knife to do the Prime 2 Effect, rather than any old knife they bought from a store. Or at the very least, they'd need to visit a high-end hardware, kitchen supply, or weapons shop to get a knife of sufficient quality (read: price). Rather than just picking up a box cutter or piece of cutlery from the dollar store.


              On the subject of making stuff from nothing, the Syndicate have another "cheat" they can employ that's part of their Paradigm: they can "find" items they require. The world around us can be full of hidden compartments, that a savvy Syndicate rep can "open" with the right knowledge, to reveal "caches" of useful equipment. If those things didn't otherwise exist, the Syndicate character won't call attention to that.

              Syndicate reps also tend to be well-connected, with networks of resources and people ready to do their bidding or make things happen. If they need a gun, for instance, they might ring someone up, ordering one to be delivered. If the item arrives through the mail, by courier, in a drop box, or arriving from the sky via a delivery drone or parachuting drop container (ala Metal Gear Solid V: The Phantom Pain). Is there a place where these items first entered the supply chain? Or did they appear within it when no one was looking, just in time to find its way into the Syndicate rep's waiting hands? Who can say.

              (Obviously, this is all very borderline Results Based Determinism, rather than Process Based Determinism. Although at least with sufficient Matter, you could argue part of the Effect is doing the material changes to the environment for as long as it takes to pick the item up. Although if you can't see the inside of a mailbox, what Hypothetical Average Witness could say if it didn't have a package arrive at some point before the Scene began? Reality doesn't seem to care as much about how long ago something was checked, so long as it wasn't observed shortly before. It's the same reason why Mages can teleport a gun into their pocket from elsewhere.)

                      Also, if it doesn't make sense in your paradigm, you can't do it. You're allowed to bend over backward trying to make a rationale for something, but if you aren't up to the task of doing so, then tough.

                      This is true for everyone, not just Technocrats.

                      Note, however, that the explanation doesn't have to make sense to Sleepers; that's only required if you want the Effect to be Coincidental. Most Technocrats can get away with “Hypertechnology” as a justification for what they do, of they're willing to pay the price in Paradox. Syndics are the exception: they pride themselves in avoiding Vulgar Magick whenever possible; and as such, you're far more likely to encounter situations where they'll “take the loss” rather than do something that would potentially trigger a Backlash. When they start breaking out the hypertech, that's when you know that they're getting desperate.

                      In addition, TR suggests that technocrats tend to rely much more heavily on Wonders than the Superstitionists do — mainly because they have a larger supply of them. There's a reason why Requisitions is Technocrat only: they're the only faction in the Ascension War that's got a large enough stockpile of Wonders for said Background to make any sense. The way I play them, what Instruments they have tend to be very complicated but multi-purpose; and working Procedures or Adjustments on the fly essentially boils down to calibrating these broadly useful tools for a specific purpose. Not quite as general purpose as a Sonic Screwdriver, but that's the sort of idea. It's only when you get into Coincidental Effects that the Technocrat starts to require a lot of special purpose Instruments.

                      Even there, though, I tend to run Technocrats using a variation of the Trinity Continuum's Dramatic Editing: the player of a Technocrat doesn't have to specify precisely what she's carrying, just a general idea of the sorts of Instruments (and Wonders, if she has Requisitions) on hand. If she needs something in the field that may not fit the profile of her general loadout, she can make a Coincidental Enlightenment roll to see if she has it or finds it lying around. (The type of Feat, i.e. number of required successes, depends on how absurd the request is.)

                      I generally don't allow Psionics unless the Technocrat in question has specified Psionics as part of his Focus.

                      My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
