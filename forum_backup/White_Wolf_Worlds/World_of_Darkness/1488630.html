    Invoking the Wyrd...in the Dreaming?

    07-31-2022, 08:57 PM
    Was playing in a friend's CtD20 game earlier tonight. We were in the Dreaming, in the city of Mistweir, and a fight broke out. Our Sidhe wanted to invoke Unearthly Beauty, which requires you to invoke the Wyrd. But invoking the Wyrd is done in the Autumn world, so..does the Birthright not function?

      The Wyrd is just the manifestation of the Dream inside of the material plane.

      It's like bringing a piece of the Dreaming and place inside the material world.

      If you're already in the Dreaming, it's like "the Wyrd" has "already been called".

      Thus the Birthright is already always on at full swing inside the Dreaming without requiring any rolls or glamour.

                  There are two ways to go about it.

                  One is to assume that it still only works when the character decides to activate and roll it, but it has otherwise no cost since the Wyrd is already your natural state in the Dreaming.

                  And the other is to assume that it is always on in the Dreaming, but the Sidhe would usually chose clothes that let them soften the effect most of the time. It would mostly work in the same way, only with the added possibility of an accident or sabotage activate the effect against the Sidhe's will.

                  I think the visual of someone that can adjust a hood or some other quick detail in their clothes and act more imposing, and that bringing the full brunt of their regal appearance to bear, is interesting and very fitting for the Sidhe.

                          Dreams & Nightmares had some rules for how Birthrights manifest in the Far and Deep Dreaming. The deeper you get, the stronger they become, too. This is called the Augmen.

                          There were also some general effects that changelings experience even in the Near Dreaming.

                          In the Near Dreaming, anything chimerical counts as if it were Wyrd, so any damage you take counts as real damage when you leave and all chimerical cantrips count as Wyrd (without the extra Glamour cost). You also gain the benefits of invoking the Wyrd for free. Using C20 rules for invoking the Wyrd, that's a significant boost.

                          The sidhe would gain the benefits of their Birthrights reflexively, as would any other kiths with Birthrights requiring them to call on the Wyrd or avoid human eyes.

                          That said, the sidhe's Birthrights in 2e were a bit different, so I'd modify those rules a bit, to take into account the C20 version.

                          The effect as described in C20 (stunning someone) should still require activating, though now as a reflexive action, as the sidhe would be reducing everyone to gibbering lackeys otherwise (which wouldn't be helpful to the sidhe in many cases).

                          In the same spirit, selkies can probably change form without water and pooka can transform for free, even if they're watched. Kiths with a Birthright that costs Glamour should probably ignore that cost.

                          Sidhe gain a new Frailty in the Far Dreaming, but I'd implement it in the Near Dreaming, too (as they're one of the few kiths who otherwise won't have a Frailty in the Near Dreaming).

                          The replacement Frailty is Empathic Blindness: +2 difficulty on rolls that require empathising with and understanding commoners.

                          According to RAW, cantrips would be at -1 difficulty, too, and you'd slowly bleed off temporary Banality (one point per day, until you have none left; permanent Banality takes years).

                          I'd also add a temporary dot of Remembrance to all fae (with the proviso that you don't remember much after you leave the Dreaming). This reflects the fluff, though isn't RAW.

                          When you get to the Far Dreaming, the benefits are even more intense: RAW, each Birthright doubles any bonuses it provides. So if you're a sidhe, your Appearance bonus increases to +4 instead of +2.

                          RAW, Frailties essentially double or become more intense. I'd just ignore that, as they're quite debilitating with that rule.

                          Cantrips would be at -2 difficulty and still count as Wyrd for free. I'd say you also gain two temporary dots of Remembrance.

                          You would also regain one Glamour per hour, and the food and drink of the Dreaming might replenish more (I'd say one point per day from eating the food of the Dreaming, plus an extra point if you engage in a huge feast).

                          Finally, in the Deep Dreaming, things double again and any Banality you have means you scorch the surrounding landscape when you make contact with it.

                          I'd just say that any dice pools related to your Birthright gain an automatic success, in addition to the increased bonuses you'd get from the Far Dreaming. Maybe rolling an exceptional success (5+ successes) restores a point of Glamour too.

                          At this point, it may be suitable to double penalties or the frequency of drawbacks from Frailties, but the "scorching" effect may be enough.

                          For the scorching, I'd maybe roll your permanent Banality dots as damage dice to the surroundings every scene, with each success triggering one significant injury to the Dreaming (a healing tree that withers, a river that dries up, etc) or throwing in an obstacle along the way (a gate that bars your way, a Nightmare creature that comes for you, etc).

                          Besides that, you'd regain one Glamour per hour (or every new scene), and gain a temporary dot of Glamour for the duration of your time there. I'd also give you three dots of Remembrance.

                          RAW, cantrips also work much better here, and even botched cantrips succeed. The description kind of sounds like unleashing, where the more successful you are, the more "wild" the magic is -- so I'd use that as a ballpark.

                          E.g., all cantrips would automatically count as being unleashed at no extra cost and without adding any Nightmare dice. Failed cantrips would still succeed but would net you a Nightmare die. Only botched cantrips would fail, and they'd also gain a Nightmare die.
