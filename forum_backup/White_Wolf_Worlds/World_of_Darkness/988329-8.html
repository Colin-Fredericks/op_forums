    Well first, why would the Tremere offer the Embrace to the Euthanatos instead of the Hermetic? Sure, Death Mages would be appropriate for vampirism - at least from a surface perspective (the Euthanatoi as a culture have a low opinion of the undead) - but the Hermetic would take to the Tremere's form of blood sorcery more easily than the Euthanatos.

    Come to think of it, why would the Tremere offer the Embrace to Mages at all? The Clan would need to see something worth having in the character. I'm not saying you can't go this route, but I am saying that Clan Tremere doesn't embrace just anyone. They have standards and needs.

    Plus, Mages that are Embraced lose their prior magical ability, and need to start from the beginning again, because blood sorcery is sufficiently different from Awakened magick or even unAwakened Sorcery. They'd only retain their understanding of metaphysical theory (Occult, Esoterica, etc.), and the Tremere could get that from any mundane occultist. And a mundane occultist wouldn't know intimately what they've lost, or have dangerous ties to Mage society. Often enough, a former Mage is more trouble than she's worth.

    Again, this doesn't preclude the embrace happening. It just means that the Clan needs to have a damn good reason for wanting the character brought into their ranks.

                    Plausible? This is Mage. Anything is plausible, so long as there's an internal logic to it.

                    And, really, a key like that can easily be seen as a Talisman, with an attached Correspondence 4 effect for opening a portal to a specific location, and maybe a Matter 3 effect to let the key fit any lock. It's hardly all that impressive in the long run, when any Mage who hits Arete 4 and Correspondence 4 can do the same thing on their own.

                    I've said this before on the forum, but many of us gamers become hyper-sensitive to things that could remotely be called "Overpowered". In a tabletop RPG, though, the players already have the freedom to do anything they can imagine (and the Storyteller allows). They could make one or two dots in Resources overpowered, if they are clever enough. Something that seems dramatic and game-breaking on paper might appear far less so when taken in the context of the larger world the characters inhabit.

                    For one, the key is a key. Keys can be stolen or misplaced. If they lose the key, they don't just lose access to the Node, but all the crap they stored in the location. If they more or less "moved in" to the Sanctum, they might lose a lot of valuable stuff there (especially if the physical location is difficult or impossible to get to otherwise). For another, this key requires a door with a lock in it to function. No doors, no access. A big deal if the characters are stranded in the middle of the wilderness.

                    If the group is separated, only the individual(s) with the key on them can effect a quick escape. This necessitates a greater degree of coordination among the cabal, as some or most could be left without an easy trip "home" if the group scatters.

                    On another note, the key was pillaged off the corpse of a Batini. It stands to reason, then, that the Sanctum they gained access to is "formatted" to that Batini's Paradigm. Ergo, Ahl-I-Batin magick is Coincidental there, while all other magicks are Vulgar. This can create a whole host of headaches for the cabal, if they think they can get away with anything in their new crib. Make the process of "deprogramming" the space as long, arduous, and time-consuming as you want. Sancta aren't easy to create, let alone convert to one's personal use.

                    Finally, while we're on the subject of Paradox, remember that opening a random door to a different physical location entirely is Vulgar, in most places in the world. The Ahl-I-Batin are masters of subtlety and stealth, so they would know how to mask the transition and avoid the Paradox. Does the player cabal know how? Probably not. If they've been using it a fair bit so far, start keeping a tally of the number of times, with a steadily increasing pool of Paradox points stored on the key. It's a Talisman, so it could store Paradox without the user knowing. Once it fills up with as much 'Dox as it has Quintessence storage space, roll a backlash and have it apply to the user.

                        Don't worry whether something is "over powered" or not. As Bluecho says, OP stuff is par the course in a Mage game, and starts to fall apart if examined.

                        Instead, just pay close attention to whether they players rely on a certain item of technique a little too often. Then adapt the game slightly to see how they deal when said item or technique starts giving them trouble.

                        For example, if you think they are beginning to use this key too much, see what happens when a rival mage starts to fight them for it.

                                Undoing the original owner's work to make a space into a Sanctum is a matter of roleplaying, less about mechanics. They're going to be consecrating the Sanctum to their own Paradigms, which can be a long and intricate process, informed by those same Paradigms. As such, it will be highly dependent on their Focus.

                                As a start, ask the players, from the characters' perspectives, how they would go about making the space theirs. A Chorister might go from room to room, splashing holy water and loudly praying for God's blessing. A Hermetic might draw complicated warding sigils on the floors, walls, and around doors and windows. A Virtual Adept might work to remove every hint of dust (particles that small wreak havoc on electronics), and lay out extensive electrical wires and nerdy posters. More or less, you're trying to cast an Effect without actually casting an Effect, and as much thought should go into creating a Sanctum as one would cast a spell.

                                Just, you know, taking much longer. M20 doesn't specify how long it takes to make a Sanctum, but earlier editions had it somewhere in the ballpark of 5 years per dot in the Sanctum background. Feel free to not go quite that far, if it doesn't serve the game. Still, carving out a place in the Consensus where one's personal magick reigns supreme should not be a quick task. It takes commitment.
