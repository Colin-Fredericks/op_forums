                  A lot of the material written primarily for Vampire features a headache-inducing assumption of "highest level wins!" without worry or care about what said Traits represent. Even when it deigns to go with, say, Rank instead of power level, it's still ignoring that Rank does not equal dots in a power. It's far closer to a Garou or Fera's Self-Control rating. It also ignores the fact that a fair number of higher-level Discipline effects seem to be rather underwhelming, for example Potence letting you eventually do what The Falling Touch does when said Discipline reaches six dots as an optional and very expensive power, or the Serpentis 9 effect in the DAV20 open development, which basically just gives you, stat-wise, Crinos form with an additional special effect. Not really worthy of an automatic success when opposed, that.

                      I tend to run very crossover-heavy games when I make an OWoD game. However, every OWoD game I've run has been Mage, which has a bit more crossover potential. Also, because it's a Mage game, the point of introducing other supernaturals is to explore other philosophical interpretations of the world and to uncover the greater Truth behind reality, instead of playing a circle-jerk with ever-increasing levels of supernatural power. That's not completely unavoidable, but things flow a bit better when you aren't arguing over who wins the rock-paper-scissors war. Besides, it leads to absolutely hilarious moments, like when the Bone Gnawers come over to visit the Mage chantry/node and drink a few beers, or the Mages wind up buddy-buddy with the city's Prince and develop a habit of calling him at random for conversations and favors.

                      An example of the confusions caused by mixing an ancient and out-of-touch Kindred with a couple of younger Archmages:

                      -----------

                      The Prince has only just settled into his desk for another night of politics and paper-pushing, when the telephone on his desk rings. He carefully picks it up and places it against his face, ensuring the corded end of the device is closer to his mouth, as his assistants had pointed out to him. "Ahoy-hoy?" He felt the shadow of a thrill of pride at his successful use of the speaking telegraph.

                      "LaCroix!" The man on the other end of the line was animate in his voice, and there was the sound of screaming, fire, and an automobile in the background. "We need fifty gallons of mustard!"

                      The Prince paused, even blinking once -- half out of old habit, half out of genuine surprise. "I'm sorry, come again?"

                      "Mustard! We need fifty gallons of mustard!" There was another voice yelling in the background, and the original spoke once more. "YELLOW mustard! Very important, he says, it can't be BROWN mustard! Dijon could work too, I think!"

                      The Prince paused again. He inhaled to speak, and then stopped, letting it out in a sigh. He took another breath. There was the sound of rubbing against asphalt over the phone. "And why, might I inquire, do you require fifty gallons of yellow mustard?"

                      "To deal with the DEMON, of course!"

                      The Prince slowly shook his head, placing a finger against his temple. "You know... you say these things, and I know you're trying to make things clearer, but you always just wind up confusing me even further. I'll... I'll see what I can do."

                          I've ran my fair share of crossovers over the years. All lines have their pro's and cons concerning crossovers, but the easiest to work with without any greater hitches are, in my opinion, WtA/MtA/KotE crossovers. KotE is nice in a way since it functions well with Mage, Werewolf and Wraith having some common ground with each including somewhat easy access to any realm the plot might need. Still most common crossovers have been VtM/MtA since there are always some players who would want to play Vampire no mater what and others who love Mage, the biggest problem in these cases is generally VtM and it's limitation to operating times, events pretty much need to happen at night or the Vampire players will be left out, this is somewhat of a problem with KotE as well but there those Permanently imbalanced towards Yang may actually do some leg work even when the sun is up which eases the matter. I've also had a fair share of CtD/VtM crossovers which tend to work pretty well too atleast up and until the Vampires piss the Changelings of as the latter tend to have pretty much control over how things will go in the end, the Vampires will need the Changelings to upkeep their Enchantments or they will simply forget all that has transpired and be left out.

                          Wraith is one of the most problematic to crossover unless the Wraith players are willing to go Risen, because they reside in another dimension which tends to divide ST attention over two places at the same time as well as making it a rather one sided affair as the non-Wraith players are either Necromancers, in which case they have pretty strong cards over the Wraiths, or not, in which case the shoes in the other foot.

                          I should note that by crossover I mean a game where players play different splats not a game where there are NPC's of different splats, even though I do run such NPC's by their own lines rules not any placeholders like having disciplines as stand in's for True Magick or Gifts and Shapechanging etc. Just noting this to make the distinction clear as any of my games can feature, should the plot demand it or should the players do things that should attract attention from such things, the other supernaturals apart from those being played.

                          Also another problematic game is DtF, it works well with Vampire and somewhat with Wraith but since it's writers apparently forgot there was an Umbra it kind of falls apart with Werewolf and Mage though CtD might work considering that the Fae are likely not something that the Angels or their creator actually created so they can easily represent an alien threat or an unexpected ally. Though in my opinion Devil's Due has the superior rules set for DtF that can easily be adopted to have Arcana that allows for Umbral interaction as well neatly patching up those holes DtF left, including that DtF rather dully makes pact making with Vampires for example impossible, despite Vampires being known enough for selling their souls that the Sabbat has it''s own inquisition, DD makes pacting work cross splat without problems.

                          So all in all I have never understood the claims that crossovers are somehow any more difficult than normal one line games to run, apart from the few hitches I mentioned like existing on different plain of existence (WtO with anything really unless RIsen) and Vampires forced curfew. Naturally crossovers mean that the ST needs to be familiar with all the lines that are featured as player characters.

                          As for the ready made adventures, I really cannot comment as I do not run ready made adventures and I very rarely if ever use ready made NPC's.

                              I have seen how lots of people detest crossover games, I don't share that opinion. When I played online, any kind of dynamic setting with enough players to keep games running had to be crossover flexible. Things were handled best in a Semi-mod status for my tastes. A few reliable STs and a lot of communication. Being online whenever some ST got stuck because of crossover rules outside their experience, they can call on another ST with more experience in the system the game was touching on to take over events for a while. Whenever a character showed up from a different system, there were some allowances made for their world, but it didn't skew the whole game from once system to the next. However if enough people stopped playing from one system and others from a different system turned up, the story could continue on.

                              On TT it's much much harder to handle multisystem characters and making sure all the rules mesh without the characters and their abilities imbalancing each other. I had a Mage group bring in a Werewolf for help because one was a kinfolk. The situation was meant for infiltration and knocking out the power so they could sneak inside of a Technocratic base in order to steal some sensitive information. A single grizzled werewolf GW Athro knocked out the power temporarily, knocked out the guards, unlocked the doors, defeated the security system and hacked the Technocratic database all without having to go much of anywhere past the security fence. Mostly gifts and spirit favors and some really good rolls. Even if the Werewolf were a Cliath, it still would have been a mega Tank in that situation.

                              Another problem with Crossover on TT is the sheer amount of information from all the systems the ST has to work with. An Experienced ST will have all the pertinent information and resources on Cheat Sheets, but for those still getting the hang of crossovers, it's a mess. And if a Player knows more than the ST about their character's particular system there's going to be arguments and the player will probably be able to pull some fast ones over on the ST convincing them their character has abilities that the ST doesn't have the time to look up to refute.

                              Possessed: I find your description of a Vampire Changeling crossover very intriguing. That's one I've never encountered before except in theories thrown about between players talking about potential characters and storylines. (Like the Nosferatu falling for the creepy cemetery girl that turns out to be a Sluagh) I would love to have been a fly on the wall for those games to see just how it was handled.
