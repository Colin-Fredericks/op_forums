                                  The Unaging Merit permits cheap access to what is, in-universe, a very rare condition, because that state is ultimately not actually that valuable or important to the timeline of an individual game.

                                  Life can just extend someone's lifespan, though usually this is presented as requiring greater and greater amounts of rarefied reagents or Tass to maintain.

                                  Mind and Matter can invest the mage's consciousness in a more durable vessel, such as a marble or brass body.

                                  Mind and Life could allow a mage to be "reborn" through one of their descendants. I've had an NPC antagonist in our game use a method akin to this - they used Life, Mind, and Prime to create a very complicated living Wonder, a child with incredible physical and mental aptitudes, and suspended a Time/Entropy/Mind effect to possess the body after the kid had grown to adulthood.

                                  What if the mage isn't immortal, but is using a very sophisticated Time effect? They don't live forever, exactly, but they're gliding across the surface of their own timeline - the mage only really exists when he's doing something significant, and so only really ages a couple of hours each year.

                                  More esoteric, imagine a Mind/Entropy/Time/Prime effect tied to the Destiny Merit. The mage no longer exists as a distinct individual, but has a potent Destiny attached to their Avatar and an effect which causes their subsequent incarnations to exhibit the mage's personality and memories when they're involved in that Destiny. In other words, the mage "Steve" has technically died, but anyone with Steve's Avatar temporarily turns into Steve when they run into one of Steve's old acquaintances or enemies.

                                      A good justification for Unaging could be some cosmic curse. Not Caine level, but some powerful god or spirit cursed the character (via an intermediary, some angel or lesser spirit) so that they'd be stuck with something bad until they repented for the sin. However a weird side-effect of that, which doesn't seem like a big deal to the immortal spirits, is that their aging is put on hold until the curse resolves itself. Naturally this means you take some flaws to represent the downside of the curse that they need to live with.

                                      Alternatively, they could be unaging because they found one of the old Hermetic Youth Formulae, and actually managed to get it to work. People agree that it shouldn't have worked, since powers like that have faded away with the end of the mystic era, but it did work in your case. It's just a shame that you can't replicate that success, no matter how hard you try, since if you could you'd be one of the most powerful Hermetics. Many powerful mages fear death, and would do a lot to get their hands on a safe agelessness potion.
