    About the Sekhem

    08-18-2015, 11:31 AM
    According to the core book, Sekhem starting rate is equal to Balance rate. Since the starting Balance is 1 then the characters begins the game with just one point of Sekhem. But I noticed that many rites and spells (even those in level 1) need a bunch of Sekhem to work. There's a way to deal with this unpleasant situation?

      Originally posted by Ipergigio View Post
      According to the core book, Sekhem starting rate is equal to Balance rate. Since the starting Balance is 1 then the characters begins the game with just one point of Sekhem. But I noticed that many rites and spells (even those in level 1) need a bunch of Sekhem to work. There's a way to deal with this unpleasant situation?
      Potions, Relics that provide the mummy with additional Sekhem, are just two examples, and there are others. However, for the most part, a "beginning mummy", per the RAW, *isn't* capable of very much (note, further, that few mummies actually start out with a Balance of just 1, as most players will spend Freebies to increase it to 2 or 3). You have to work the characters Balance up per the RAW in order to begin properly channeling Sekhem effectively. (Note, further, how a mummy with a Balance of 1 cannot, effectively, use his Sekhem *at all* because unless he is within the Web of Faith he would then be in Semektet and in sorry shape if he spent his one and only Sekhem... a conceptual flaw relative to how Balanc/Sekhem is applied/related... and speaking of which...)

      This is one of the *THE* biggest metaphysical/meta-conceptual errors in Mummy (the linking of Sekhem to Balance, and the idea of how Balance is presented relative to the game mechanics). A mummy with a Balance:1 is *barely* connected to Ma'at; while this may seem appropriate for a beginning mummy, from a simplistic POV, it actually is almost egregiously metaphysically incorrect. The soul of the Second Life which the Completing Spirit bonds with may have been flawed (and perhaps downright evil in some cases), but the fact that the First Life spirit *does* become one with the Second Life means that said flaws are essentially (in most cases) eradicated. On top of that, even many flawed souls will still have a cross-comparative rating (using Humanity as a guideline vs. Balance, but understanding that a few aspects have to be "cut-in-half" numerically because Balance is in many ways more stringent than Humanity) that is *already* at least equal to Balance:2-3; contextual to that, and even taking into account a Second Life that equaled only a Balance:1, the new mummy should metaconceptually have a starting Balance of between 3-5. Using Balance as a direct mirror to Arete -- vis-a-vis the maximum rating in Hekau that a mummy can have vs. the same for Spheres with mages -- is flawed cross-comparatively because the thematics behind the two are quite different, and that is reflected in what the two "staring values" *should* be. A mummy should *not* have a Balance of 1 at the beginning, as that indicates a mummy who only *barely* "passed the test", as it were, which is really only the case with Udja-Sen; other mummies "should" having ratings somewhere between 3-5 to start, as that would better represent the "completeness" of soul/purpose that they now have via the Completing Spirit. It's most emphatically *not* the same as a newly (A)wakened mage who may only have a dot in Arete (though, again, per the RAW, very few starting mages have only one dot in it, as the initial Awakening often results in a starting Arete of 1-3, per the use of Freebies and as indicated in the Storyteller's Guide/other books).

      M:tR had a number of good ideas, and was an improvement over the previous editions; however, this and a few other things (don't *even* get me started on some of the Purposes, mechanically, or the Udja-Sen, specifically -- we'll be here all day...) were so miscalculated it's a wonder they made it past review/play-testing with a book that was published in the Revised era (that is, unlike with the 1st Ed. books back in the early 90's, there had already been enough years and editions that such meta-conceptual/metaphysical oversights such as these should really have not still been occurring).

      But anywho....

      I have been around here for waaaayyyy too fucking long...

              That's the limitation involved in trying to resurrect too many times within a century -- you can only expend so much will (Willpower) or raw spiritual might (permanent Sekhem) before your soul/essence is utterly depleted. Your mummy has to have real time to recoup their strength (spend XP to recover Willpower/permanent Sekhem), which can involve years, decades or even (in the case of many resurrections within a relatively short period of time) centuries.
