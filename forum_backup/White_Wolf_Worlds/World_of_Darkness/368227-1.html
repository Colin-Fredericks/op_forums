                The lines had a lot more interaction very early on. However, the writing approach changed dramatically between editions, and there have been plenty of retcons to distance each line from each other. The first editions might have been taking place in the same world, or not, but the Revised and Anniversary editions certainly aren't tying your hands to a single world.
                Maybe this is part of my disconnect. I tend to think of 1st and 2nd edition as the meat and potatoes of the World of Darkness. It may just be how it seemed to me, but it felt like more books were published for 2nd edition than every other edition combined, and that 1993 to 1998 was the golden age of White Wolf (in terms of publishing volume, number of players, and influence on the gaming industry - revised had better writing and book quality on the whole). If your impression of the classic World of Darkness is based on Revised edition and later and you place less emphasis on the hundred of books already published to that point, I can see where you would get a different feel from the game.

                                                        Originally posted by thedonnie View Post
                                                        TBH, I would like to point out that the writers pretty much painted themselves into a corner. They set every game as "one week away from the end of it all" and this meant that they had to do the endgame scenario.
                                                        I feel compelled to point out, whenever this comes up, that the notion that White Wolf had to end the game lines is a bit of carefully crafted revisionism perpetrated by the developers and continued by the more devout members of the fan base.

                                                        When they announced the end of the Classic World of Darkness lines, the developers at White Wolf spun the decision as something they had promised the fans since day one. Essentially, they were saying "We've been talking about the end of the world in our games since the first edition of Vampire, so we're finally giving you what you've been asking for! Isn't that great!"

                                                        Except no one was asking for the game lines to end. While people were certainly excited to see what books like Gehenna and Apocalypse would be like, no one was really out there demanding these books be written.

                                                        For that matter they had never promised these end of the world books. Quite the contrary - at least one of the Vampire developers had gone on record during an interview in the mid to late 90's saying that they would never write a Gehenna book. Of course, management changes, markets change, and if there's one thing I learned as a fan of White Wolf's games, it's that any time they said publicly they weren't going to do something, you could bet they'd do it at some point or other.

                                                        There was no pressing, narrative need to end the cWoD game lines, as evidenced by the existence and success of the 20th Anniversary editions and the accompanying new material that has been released. Gehenna still works just fine as a narrative tool in V20 as it did in 1st Edition - each troupe is capable of making up their own minds about the truth of the Gehenna Prophecies or what have you.

                                                        In the end, the decision to create the nWoD games came down to money and company politics.
