            If you’re willing to consider things in terms of results instead of motivations, please compare the following options:

            • 1s cancel successes; you botch if you have more 1s than successes.
            • 1s cancel successes; you botch if you get no successes and any 1s.
            • you botch if you get more 1s than successes and no 10s. You can botch and succeed simultaneously.
            • you botch if you get more 1s than the lowest trait in the pool. You can botch and succeed simultaneously.

            The first is the RAW in 2e; the second is the RAW in Revised; the third is my “boostless” option; and the fourth is a paraphrase of your option. What are the actual results of each of these rules?

            • the first two options tend to suppress the number of successes achieved, with the resulting statistics for success resembling what would happen if you didn't have 1s cancel successes and instead jacked up all difficulties by 1.
            • the third and fourth options have no effect on the number of successes achieved; that's determined solely by the difficulty rating and the size of the dice pool. They also both share the concept of being able to botch (i.e., “things get complicated”) and succeed at the same time.
            • the first option results in the likelihood of a botch increasing as the size of the dice pool increases.
            • the second option results in the likelihood of a botch becoming vanishingly small unless the difficulty is very high and the dice pool is very small.
            • the third option results in the likelihood of a botch going down as the size of the dice pool increases, but at a more modest rate than the second option.
            • the fourth option results in the likelihood of a botch going up with the number of traits that comprise the dice pool*. The effect of the size of the pool on the likelihood of a botch also depends on the number of traits contributing to it, with the overall trend either holding steady at zero (for one trait) or increasing as the number of chances to get a 1 increase faster than the number of 1s you need to get in order to botch.

            * if you're rolling a seven-dice Humanity or Willpower pool, you won't get a botch even if you roll all 1s; but if you're rolling a seven-dice Intelligence + Occult + Path pool, three 1s will definitely suffice for a botch and two 1s might even be enough.

            Is there anything I missed in my analysis?
