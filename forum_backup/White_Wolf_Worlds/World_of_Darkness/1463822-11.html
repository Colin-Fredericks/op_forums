    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
    Sorry; the Buffy bit was over the line.
    Why is it over the line?

    The problem is that it's what Tier 3 mixed splat HtV looks like. The Slayers have an Endowment that grants them superhuman physicals. The Watchers and Witches have an Endowment that lets them access supernatural Merits. A few full supernaturals decided to ally with the group. Xander has a weird unique Endowment that drastically increases his Skill dots as the plot demands (this isn't even some meta thing that keeps him relevant, one episode he got turned into a GI Joe style soldier and even after that ended, he's shown to have retained and refined skills he had access do from that experience). The Initiative is a joint operation between TFV and the Cheiron group practically on the nose.

    Buffy is HH with a bunch of Numina users and loner supernaturals from different backgrounds coming together. Buffy is high level HtV.

    Buffy, despite Joss Whedon, was popular, and people want to play that sort of thing.

    HtR wasn't that though.

    [quote...it's not really my thing.[/quote]

    Nobody's asking for you to change your mind on this. Just respect that the people for whom HtR is their thing don't want their thing being removed from a new edition of HtR because Paradox doesn't seem to want to actually make a new edition of HtR, but wants the HtR branding.

    When HtR was first announced, that's what a lot of us were looking for: regular people who were made aware of the supernatural threats around them and decided to do something about it. The Imbued, being supernaturally empowered to the last of them, were not what we were looking for.
    And what does that have to do with a new edition of HtR not being what HtR turned out to be?

    The fact that H5 is more your speed doesn't address why it should be called HtR if it's not going to be what HtR was about.

    The rest of it was, as I see it, merely an attempt to help sell the then-brand-new Exalted game by planting hints that the Imbued were somehow the former Solar Exalted reborn into the modern world.
    People that actually wrote for the books have repeatedly refuted this nonsense. The Exalted-WoD connections were (1) not just in HtR and (2) turned into Easter Eggs before anything besides a single ad campaign went to print.

    If you believe this, then you must believe that all of Revised was a giant ad for Exalted: VtM Revised was about selling Abyssals, WtA Revised Lunars, etc. because there's just as many Easter eggs in all the other game lines as their are in HtR, and they matter just as much to being able to enjoy those games independent of Exalted.

    If you think it sounds crazy that Mage Revised was merely an attempt to sell Sidereal Exalted by planting hints that the Awakened were somehow the former Sidereal Exalted reborn through the ages (as they were never imprisoned like the Solars were)? Guess what. There's as much evidence of that being the case as the Imbued were supposed to be the Solars revived.

    it required them to be supernatural beings themselves.
    Why is it required that so many of the HH/YotH splats are supernatural beings themselves? They're practically all Numina users of some stripe (assuming their not full supernatural splats like the Children of Osiris).

    Why is it required that there's a whole group of HtV splats that are defined by being having access to supernatural powers instead of hunting with mundane weapons and smarts?

    Because they need to actually stand a chance against what's out there in their settings. Just like the Imbued have powers that give them a chance against the supernatural monsters they fight.

    One of the seeming never ending frustrations with ever talking about HH vs. HtR is this insipid idea that HH/YotH is about "mundane mortals that totally aren't supernaturals," but HtR is about the Imbued, who are "true supernaturals." Even though it's complete bullshit.

    HH/YotH/etc. Where the Vatican's D&D-Cleric special ops teams, Psychic power used government agents, sorcery wielding members of a mysterious organization with dark secrets that studies the supernatural, Qi powered anime kung-fu monster hunters, and cyborgs all take on the nasties of the WoD are the books that aren't about "supernatural beings themselves."

    That said, I would not object if each Creed were given an optional Edge that brings the HtR Edges into H5 — optional, so that those of us who prefer something more like the HH style don't end up having to house-rule the game to get rid of them.
    What does that even mean?

    "It's fine if H5 has characters that can study mystic martial arts to punch supernaturals for aggravated damage, but if the ability to channel conviction to the hunt into mundane weapons so they do more damage isn't regulated to optional rules, the game will be ruined."

    That seems a very odd hill to fight on.

    That, combined with the Bystander traits, would allow for the Imbued style of gameplay for those who want it.
    No. HtR with a proper clean up would allow the Imbued style of play I want.

    Or there's another option: do True Faith as the H5 version of the Imbued..
    No. That's not the Imbued, that's a half measure that lets people that don't like the Imbued feel like they're through the fans of the Imbued a bone while they forget that we're humans and bones don't actually do us much good.

    For all that you seem to say that you understand what the Imbued playstyle is... you really don't do a good job of showing it.
