    Mage it's a game where you really need to read all the rules. If you stay with what's written in the Spheres, without understanding the difficulty to pull it off, you inevitably get to think: OH MY GOSH, I'M OMNIPOTENT. The devil it's in the details. Balancing rules can't help if nobody knows/uses them. Plus, you need to understand what's unbalanced and what's balanced to make these rules (and that means understanding the rules).

    My problem was always with penalizing the (already weak) improvized magick, without adressing the real problem that it's the Ritual abuse. That's why I applaud the harsher rules for Rituals found in M20, while hating the newer rules of Counterspelling.


    The trick to balance mages, I think, it's to adjust the successes needed. Anything worth more than 4 sucesses it's outside the realm of posibility for a starting mage to do in a turn. Anything worth more than 5 might require a long ritual, specialy with the new rules for rituals.
    Turning things into silver seems too easy at Matter 2 for our werebeasts?. Well, Silver it's a precious metal, precious metals are harder to do than normal metals. So, let's say, 3 successes for a single bullet seems fair (let's give mages an attack, at least). 4 may be required to turn things that aren't already metal. 5-6 to transmute the floor (it's an area of effect, it shouldn't be easier than that). Really sadistic and want to turn the very air into silver?. Fine, 8 successes-it's an area casting where you try to turn a gaseous element into a solid, that happens to be a precious metal, and I just don't fell compelled to make it easy...
    As you can see, a lot depends on the storyteller. By doing that, I can kill any pretence of omnipotence at the reach of a casting roll. If you cast during various turns (how did you managed to not get killed?), you may end casting the Air into Silver Rote...or not, if I decide that such amount requires a ritual casting (spanning minutes or hours, not turns). If you add extra counter-rules to regular casting, either mages will end being unable to do anything without rituals, or the Storyteller it's going to counter-balance those rules by making magick cost less successes...and rituals would be as OP as ever.
    Also, too much balancing ends souring the taste of the games. For instance, I see no one arguing to weaken werewolves in order to make them more compatible with starting characters of other splats...In fact, I think that would anger many. However, they are way more unbalanced than Mages as far as PvP goes. They can steamroll any combat oriented build, and if a mage can do anything about it, it's not without a difficult/long ritual.

        I'm all for a "One World of Darkness" splat, but only within reason.

        What I'd want from such a book is two-fold:

        1) Better adjudication of powers, as the OP pointed out. Too many abilities rely on splat-specific abilites to determine things like difficulty. CoD does this better, with its Resistance abilities and the fact the core supernatural stat is pretty much a "resist other powers" boost. While adding the former is a bit harder in WoD (*glares at Appearance*), it only takes a little tinkering to do the same with the latter. It's part of the reason I ported Blood Potency into Masquerade in my House Rules and tweaked it to play nicely with Generation.

        2) How the various subspecies interact with one another. Yes, yes, I get it that everyone likes to keep to themselves, and I have no problem with that, but it's not always possible. Nor is it particularly realistic. Sure, Garou hate Kindred and pretty much have a Kill-on-Sight policy (boring, imo), but Mages and Garou share living space as it were, with Caerns/Nodes and the Umbra. Wouldn't Dreamspeakers at least have some contacts? Or what if a cabal of mages gets Technocrats on their tail and wind up getting themselves indebted to the local Prince in return for protection? I'm not saying every supernatural group needs to have a weekly luncheon where they all get together and trade legends, life stories, and phone numbers. But the groups should brush up against one another from time to time, and it'd be nice to have some guidelines on that beyond "X doesn't care about Y so they don't know anything about them at all" which is pretty much the official line since Day One.

                Not necessarily less antagonism, but less "kill on sight"/"avoid at all costs" reactions. As written, this is how all supernatural communities treat each other, and quite frankly, I find it stupid. I've got no problem with werewolves and vampires being at war, or changelings avoiding the banality and stagnation of the undead. But Mage and Wraith have almost no excuse for not having some degree of interaction with damn near every other splat (yes, wraiths are dead, but damn near every subspecies has their take on necromancers and ghost whisperers). Demon and Vampire would also have little problem interacting. I personally loathe Apocalypse and prefer Forsaken for Werewolves as far as lore and purpose goes.

                I've always broken down the various WoD factions thus:

                Camarilla Vampires: Rulers of the City. Who you run afoul of when dealing with things on a local level.

                Anarchs/Caitiff: the rank and file just trying to get by. The guys you'd run into in biker bars and seedy clubs looking for a quick snack. Unless in a Free State.

                Unaligned Clans: The mercenaries of the night.

                Sabbat Vampires: The Monstrous Undead. For when you want just want vampire antagonists in your setting, unless running.

                "Forsaken" Garou: (in my cosmology, the tribes that try to get along with humanity), the hunters of the "civilized" wilds, close to and even within cities.

                "Pure" Garou: (the Garou who want to purge humanity and generally hate everyone not them): Antagonists unless running a pure Werewolf game.

                Tradition Mages: Secretive, stand-off-ish, but a few will trade favors with those who need a working done.

                Technocratic Mages: The weird Men in Black guys and Cyborg Zombies who occassional show up when the Masquerade/Veil gets REALLY trashed. Most rank-in-file only think they're some kind of government agency. Those who've been around for a while fear these guys more than the Inquisition. The Ultimate Autumn People.

                Orphans: These are the ones most likely to wind up in debt to some vampire elder or sidhe noble, working as their own personal "court mage". Of course, that usually gets the mage a drastically reduced life expectancy.

                Seelie Fae: Rarely encountered by any but mages, some childlings are nearly recruited as the Awakened before it's discovered how differently their gifts manifest. The more nature-oriented ones might make friends in the Were communities.

                Unseelie Fae: In areas where this Court holds power, there's usually some kind of truce (or state of cold war) with the local Kindred community. Most Fae of either court avoid vampires, except areas where Toreador or Malkavians hold domain.

                Wraiths: If you're a necromancer or ghost hunter, you pretty much run into these. Doesn't mean you know jack squat about what's going on on the other side of Veil, though.

                Fallen: Mages and Garou respond badly to these creatures, thinking them common demons. Vampires and even Changelings can find kinship with them, however. Though honestly, they're most likely looking for a new chesspiece in their schemes.

                            “Resisted by game-specific trait” is something that needs to go, for the most part. When you say “the subject rolls Humanity” and the subject is a werewolf or mage — or, arguably, even a human — you're forced to make up a solution. If resistance is always based on common traits such as attributes, you can always have a game-specific trait supplement the resistance; but you'll never have to question what trait to use.

                            The exception to this is when the thing you're resisting is specific to your splat's nature. Only mages suffer from Paradox; so it's OK if Mage-specific traits are used to cope with it. Likewise, a vampire's Virtues are all abiut resisting urges that only vampires experience; so any powers that are resisted by vampiric Virtues are powers that don't work on non-vampires.

                            That's the sort of thing I'm hoping to see in One World of Darkness and its sequel lines: not so much a setting that assumes that, say, vampires and mages will bump elbows all the time; but rather a rules system where when they do bump elbows, it will be blatantly obvious how to handle it. I also want the One World of Darkness core book to be about the mortals in the World of Darkness with some sort of generic Horrors system that gives you antagonists and plot fodder even if you don't own any other book.

                                                    It's simple, we need a comparable set of traits for powers, since this seems to be the common theme. Let's steal from CoD and make them a trinity of power/finesse/resistance. They can be tacked onto the back of any existing character sheet without faffing with unique traits.

                                                    We'll call them what they are: Power, Finesse and Resistance. All we need then is a set of formulae to translate disparate unique traits into a set of three universal ones.

                                                    Any suggestions? I'm thinking, for example, that Power for Vampires would take into consideration Generation and age (maybe add +1 for every 1,000 years of undeath). Finesse would be calculated from highest Discipline and, say, the number of high level Disciplines possessed. Resistance would be calculated from Self-control and the higher of Occult or Thaumaturgical Countermagic.

                                                    Let's say every one of these traits is on a 1-10 scale and is rolled as a dice pool like Willpower. Use a combat-style system of opposed rolls. Simple. Alternatively, you could have them on a 1-5 scale (halve the numbers after adding them) and pair them with an appropriate Discipline (e.g., Resistance + Dominate to resist Mind).

                                                        What I'd like to have in a 4th Edition
                                                        • A CoD-style book for World Description and basic rules.
                                                        • Attributes akin to CoD
                                                        • The integrity system and the condition should make a transition from CoD
                                                        • Difficulties should, after all modifiers are calculated in, be between 5 and 9. Every modifier above 9 should be a success threshold, every modifier under 5 should be an automatic success.
                                                        • Powers (disciplines, gifts, etc) should grant additional dice, ususally and difficulty adjustment rarely.
                                                        • Damage, should be a fixed value - reducing the number of rolls for each combat action to: Attack and Soak (defensive action, if targeted character spends an action for that).
                                                        • Merit and Flaws being characteristics that are unlikely to change (gained, or lost). Make foreign languages learned a background, for Christ's sake.
                                                        • Anchors and Aspirations, or a similar system should be carried over from CoD, or Wraith's Pathos system
                                                        • Social Combat (and possibly mental combat) as seen in ASOIAF-RPG (or CoD, as I read in the wika)


                                                        What I don't want to have in a 4th edition
                                                        • Rules and mechanics to have crossover. Being a mummy amongst Vampires or a Gurahl amongst Garou isn't a merit. I can see how this is suitable to regulate 100 people LARP organisations, but it has nothing to offer in a tabletop game.
                                                        • Every game should be it's own Setting. There is really no need to unify the games to make them more cross over friendly. And there is no need to regulate how the sewers are shared. Mutual agreement, uneasy stalemate, enforced decision by powerful individuals should all be okay, should not be defined any where.
                                                        • No streamlining of powers. Certainly there should be some clean-up regarding powers of disciplines/gifts that are somewhat similar, just because authors had similar ideas and created powers that are kind-of alike. But gifts should be their own thing, and not be redesigned to match disciplines of certain levels and so on.
                                                        • EDIT: Additional actions in a turn as a power. V20 Dark Ages, that made Celerity affect the normal multiple actions rules, seems to be the most balanced approach to Celerity so far. It should be ported to all game lines. Acton more than once in any given turn is unbalanced in a way that just can't be corrected in any easy way. So, just don't to it.

                                                            The problem with the crossover is that not every splat uses power statistics. Garou have Gnosis, Changelings have Glamour, Mages have Arete, etc, but Wraiths and Vampires have nothing. I think that when the powers of two splats interact, it should be a reflexive contested roll, level of attacker power against level of defender power, with a difficulty equal to (level of opponent's power/2, rounded up, plus 5), with the power with the most successes gaining dominance (a tie would alert both participants that something is happening and would require a full reflexive contested roll of applicable Attribute plus applicable Ability). The exception would be powers that have specific rules governing interactions with the powers of other splats (see Mind Shields of Psychic Numina and Counterspells of Sorcery). In the case of the suggested rules, a vampire with Dominate 5 trying to attack a Garou with a specific level 3 Gift that gives immunity to mind control, would gain 5 dice and roll against difficulty 7 while the Garou would gain 3 dice and roll against difficulty 8. While the vampire will probably win, the Garou at least has a chance of resisting.
