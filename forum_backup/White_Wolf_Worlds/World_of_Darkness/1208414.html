    Mortals in the Umbra

    04-18-2018, 09:18 AM
    In a story i am currently planning out that is set in a all-boys summer camp there are certain scenes where the main characters, who happen to be mortal campers, end up in the Umbra due to a Shallowing that manifests in and around the camp. My question is how would mortal adolescants percive the Umbra and how could they survive their incursions into It?

      In a Mage story? .

      They would perceive it the same way mages do (which it's a whole can of worms on it's own)

      They risk to become spirits if they stay 3 months, but they probably won't be able to survive there that long.

      Keep in mind that the Spirit World is full of dangers. The nature of dangers depends to what Umbra leads the Shallowing, but none it's safe . For instance, if they go to the Penumbra, even without giant spiders wanting to calcify you and Banes that want to murder you, what the campers *think* it's edible fruit will belong to a plant spirit that may get angry if taken witout asking permison, animals are smart and some hate humanity, even water may fight back if the campers are unlucky. Also, to get lost in the spirit world it's just a matter of turning on the wrong tree. You were near the camp a second ago, and now you're in another Realm or even on a whole other level of the Umbra - good luck getting back.

      To make it simple, if the campers don't want to die horribly, they need a spirit friend (a Spirit saavy Mage or a Garou work too). A friendly local spirit can show the campers how to avoid the worst hazzards, how to act when facing other local spirits, and may be able to find a missing camper. You really want to have a spirit friend or equivalent to go there.

          Depending on if you want this to be dark, consider having them enter the Shadowlands. They are befriended by a rather helpless ghost who seems very intent on keeping them alive. You could have the reveal be that the ghost is a spectre, looking to drive the group towards oblivion, or a renegade seeking to keep a perfect source of pathos nearby. Perhaps the ghost is a member of the hierarchy seeking to prevent the group from returning in order to preserve the dictum mortem.
