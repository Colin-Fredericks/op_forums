            Actually the Technocracy are by no means servants of the Weaver. The original 1st edition Technocrats were faceless agents of static reality with zero humanity and no goals beyond perfect order. This is not what the late 2nd edtion/Revised/Onyx Path Technocracy is about. The later Technocracy are agents of change with a goal of a transhumanist Utopia with absolute freedom to all. They are tool users, and they use technology for the masses, but they are people. They have emotions and they will fight tooth and nail to keep the people safe. They may have to do some hard decisions and do questionable choices, but in the long run they are fighting for humanity's betterment... and one day everyone will have supertech that will allow them to truly master the cosmos in a brave new world.

            The Weaver does not seek this wonder and progress. If it were up to the weaver it would stop at any possible level of technology if it was enough to control Reality. The Weaver seeks absolute peace, tranquility and lack of movement.

            On the other hand Changelings are about dreams, wonder and Chaos in the vein of tricksters and whimsical Fae. Wyld is not like that. Wyld is about sprouting cancer out of nowhere, about water flowing backwards, about ice growing from flowers and about horrors that seek to break down reality into a soup that can create endless new vistas.

            It's not a good idea to apply the concepts of Werewolf to any other game, as the cosmology of Werewolf doesn't really play well with others.

                                                Originally posted by Quiet Storm View Post

                                                As for not mixing Werewolf to other games -- I personally feel any chance to cross systems together is a great benefit to every game, as it allows for exposure to those casual cWoD WW fans, who gravitate towards a singular setting, maybe leaning on a secondary genre, who wouldn't generally explore the other denizens without a given excuse.

                                                Sometimes, the best word of mouth is to put it directly inside someone's comfort zone.
                                                They may just say -- "Oh, I get it now".
                                                While there are definitely some fun ideas in combining Werewolf and Mage (or any CWoD games for that matter) there are several very problematic issues in doing so. Werewolf is a game of completely absolute ideology. They are RIGHT. They know the way the universe WORKS. If you don't get their way, then you are deluded and/or wrong.

                                                Mage is game of perishingly few absolutes. And when you find them, many, if not most, of them are arguable. In Mage, "Being right" is very relative. In a multi-Tradition or multi-Faction group, "right" varies from individual to individual. The universe doesn't work the same for you as it does for me. It doesn't even work the same for all of them THEM (insert whatever you want here - different groups of humans, humans and other supernaturals, different types of supernaturals, groups within specific types of supernatural). You may believe the other person is wrong, but that really has no effect on their ability to do their thing. The universe works in an almost infinite variety of different ways with a loose "democracy of agreement" governing the relative weight of them.

                                                So you have to try to harmonize those two and that's NOT easy. You can try to handwave it or ignore it, but that will lead to difficulties down the line. Werewolf being the absolutist view, it is the easiest one to cleave to and thereby, make Mage's fundamentals WRONG. They are completely incorrect. It's all about the Weaver/Wyrm/Wild. No way around it. Mage suffers as you try to shoehorn all the variety into boxes that were never meant to hold such things. Alternatively, you can say Mage is correct and Werewolf is correct for only the subset of Reality(-ies) that they interact with. Which sounds good on paper, but it does undermine Werewolf. Now Werewolf's ideology is WRONG. The Wyrm/Weaver/Wild/Gaia are all just their thing. Cool for them. Also a bit limiting don't you think? Technocrats aren't paws or allies of the Weaver. They may see the Weaver as manifestation of a fundamental aspect of the Tellurian in the way the Werewolves see it, but, in the end to the Union, it's another EDE (albeit a weakly-godlike one, at the very least) which it means to be contained/destroyed/used/guarded against.

                                                Or you can TRY to find a way to harmonize them, but every way I've seen so far ends up with people having to pick one game or the other and going with it. And, unfortunately, end up having to do a disservice to the other game. If you come at things from Werewolf, you will slam down the Triat and MAKE IT MAKE SENSE for Mages by making them pawns of this or that or some combination. Or you pick Mage and go very relativistic and pluralistic and say "Werewolves think X" and then follow from that in how they interact with the various types of Mages.
