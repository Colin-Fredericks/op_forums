                So first of all, if you look at the EXTENSIVE examples that I provided in my previous post, it should be clear that I'm referring to the "deeper way," at least when we're talking about the relationship between Paradigm and Ascension.

                Secondly, you're ignoring the fact that, while there is variation within them, each of the Traditions is defined by a broad philosophical framework that binds them together regardless of any particular differences that might arise from local/cultural perspectives or interpretations. That's what makes each of them a *Tradition* that can be bound together across the entire world.

                And third...

                Um... Yeah?

                The fact that the path to Ascension is a very personal thing that will vary even among Mages with similar Paradigm beliefs is a pretty good explanation for why there isn't a neat little list of "10 Easy Steps That *Anyone* Can Follow To Achieve Ascension *Today*!"

                If Ascension being vague and ineffable is a problem for you, then it's a problem for *you*, not the text. If it bothers you that much, then come up with your own concrete list of requirements like they give for Transcendence in Wraith. Again, half the reason it's written so vaguely is so that Storytellers can determine the meaning and requirements for themselves. The Roleplay Police will not bust down your door and throw you in Roleplay Jail for doing so.

                And, again, the other reason it's so vague is that we're talking about something that's supposed to be equivalent to achieving Nirvana in Buddhism, while *also* leaving room for it to fit into a dozen other radically different spiritual (or secular) worldviews.

                I can't really think of anything else to say that isn't just more of me repeating myself. If you're still just not getting it, I think I've just got to peace out and wish you good luck, at this point.

                    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                    For example, the Book of Ether speaks of the Walls of Troy, a metaphor for reality. In this metaphor, everything is possible, even contradictory things. The Walls of Troy contain those things that have been accepted as possible, with everything outside the Walls being considered impossible. In this view, Ascension consists of tearing down the walls so that the Scientist truly accepts the infinite possibilities of reality. That's how an Etherite views Ascension: developing such a profound understanding of Ether Science that all things become possible.
                    From my own reading of the Kitab al-Alacir passage found in the Sons of Ether Revised Tradition Book, I wouldn't say that the Kitab says that "Walls of Troy" (i.e. the Consensus) should be "torn down" per say (the passage even states that the "Walls" are necessary for normal human life, as without it we'd all go insane as our limited minds try and fail to grasp the whole of infinity) but more that they must be progressively "expanded", until they eventually encompass all.

                    And partially unrelated to the thread, but which M20 Paradigms (or combinations of them) would you say get closest to accurately reproducing this philosophy? M20 core book lists A Mechanistic Cosmos, Tech Holds All Answers, Everything is Data, Everything’s an Illusion and Might is Right as options, and I’d personally add A Holographic Reality from Book of Secrets as well. Opinions?

                                « “Matter” is energy, shaped by our perceptions into the illusion of solidity. What we perceive as reality is in fact a holographic illusion of a greater Reality that exists at the edge of human awareness. Every element of this illusion is preserved in every other one, resulting in an infinite replication from which nothing is truly lost or destroyed. Although we catch glimpses of the truth in fleeting phenomena (déjà vu, precognition, telepathic contact, synchronicity, and other related “impossibilities”), human existence as we know it demands limitations on what we can perceive. Awakening, however, removes the need for such binding and arbitrary limits. Ascension, then, involves opening one’s self to the infinite truth and realizing that everything we “know” is ultimately without substance, for All is One.
                                A scientific perspective of the Everything’s an Illusion and Creation’s Divine and Alive paradigms, the holographic reality concept draws from quantum physics experiments within the last few decades. According to neurophysiologist Karl Pribram, the world as we know it is a “world of appearances,” refined by human consciousness from a vast spectrum of interacting wave-forms. Although this world of appearances is “real” in most senses – inflicting boundaries upon what we can and cannot do within that world – it’s essentially a projection of a boundless interplay of energies that we set into “form” through our consciousness. Within this paradigm, “magick” (by any name) involves a recognition of, and an ability to manipulate, our world of appearances. Such feats, of course, are not supernaturalism, but science! Sure, ancient mystics may have first conceived of the truth, but it took science to prove its validity.
                                Whether or not you consider this concept to be a paradigm in its own right, or simply a new spin on an old idea, is really up to the player and her Storyteller. Even so, the holographic reality concept – detailed in Michael Talbot’s now-classic book The Holographic Universe – features a growing body of scientific research behind it. A character who embraces this paradigm (and who’s familiar with that research) can spin dazzling webs of implacable techno-logic to support her work… and since the theory has at least one example of conclusive proof in the existence of the Digital Web, this paradigm enjoys surging popularity among twenty-first-century mages – mystic and technomancer alike! »
