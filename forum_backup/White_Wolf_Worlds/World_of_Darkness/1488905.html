    Ludo narrative dissonances and other old lore incoherences in the wod

    08-02-2022, 08:00 PM
    That was quite a mouthful of a title, I'll admit.

    It's been quite a lot of years since I became a fan of the world of darkness now, long before I had the chance of actually playing the games. And if I'm honest, it's in my top five of all fictional universe.
    Howether, after having probably read every book there is in the gameline, watched many interviews of writters and observed/participated in many discussions amongs the fanbase, I noticed, like many people,incoherences both in the lore itself and with it's interaction with the themes and rules of the games.

    Incoherence that were not merely present in the books, but seemingly, for some, ingrained in the collective vision of what the games are about. So since I've not seen yet a topic trying to gather them all, I'll give it a try.

    Just a few words before starting

    -Before anyone ask why I complain rather than appreciate the games, well, I can do both, and it's more about looking critically at them for the sake of it rather than ranting. I'll still enjoy the games, even when there's flaws.

    -I'll only talk about the big five. I was never entranced by mummy, kuei-jin, hunter and demon.

    -I'll restrict myself to the games as they are till 20th edition, It's no secret that I don't like at all V5, but that's not the point here.

    -This is in no way an attack on past and present writters



    Let's start with Vampire, which is, with wraith, one of the most consistent game according to me.



    "There's no way this'll work out as presented"

    - The night. Seriously, there's no way vampires did anything in history while being, in their basic state, as clueless in the dark as humans, especially when you always risk going nuts when you have a torch close to you. Yes, you can see a lot under the moonlight, but that's not going to help any nosferatu in their tunnels, anyone in a building. And while the idea of a vampire stepping on an unseen lego while trying to bite a mortal in their sleep can be fun once, it pretty mess up with the idea that vampires could ever survive. Let's be honest, how many of us storytellers had to houserule that vampires see better at night than humans?

    For the exemple, my houserule is basic vampire see better than most humans, with auspex 1, see as clear as day under any form of light, only bothered by absolute darkness, with protean 1, see perfectly in the dark, only exception for obtenebration based shadows, with abyss mysticism 1, see perfectly.

    - Following previous point, there's no way vampires ever achieved the kind of social influence attributed to them when they live at night and can hardly function, if ever, during the day. In most eras, people were particularly close to each others, there's no way no one would remark the one never doing anything with people, or the noble that only appear at night. Even in isolated communities, like monasteries, where capadocians were said to live, you could never pass as a monk by being constantly absent of daylight activities and mass. Mind control and blood bond and ghouls help, but in many places in fictions, characters talk with mortals as if they seeing them only at night is normal. The inquisition truly didn't need the records showing that the lord of the castle has the same name since 6 generations, they merely had to look to people that never appear during the day.

    -There's no way thin blood are a recent phenomenom, sure, it might be the period where there's the most of them, but across all history, all it takes is a runaway neonates in an relatively isolated place embracing and not ruling over their progeny to achieve thin blood vampires. You'd need extremely mobile elders with auspex 5 or more wholly dedicated to hunt them down to control their numbers.

    -Looking at the state of the garou nation, vampires should not have that much issue settling in relatively rural zones.

    Theme/Lore contradictions

    - The theme of slow moral degradation and of the fight to maintain your humanity against the merciless grind of century doesn't match well with the pletora of methuselahs that are just fine with it.

    - Since the game is gothic punk, rebellion is a big part of the setting, howether, the reasons to rebel for kindred are, in the end, pretty few. You need very little to survive, a regular source of blood, a roof, and occupation. The game make multiple time this statement "imagine needing a job to survive, and your tyrannical superior will never leave his position". But in the lore, when you live in a camarilla controlled zone, you don't actually need a political position to survive. The only reason to rebel against the Man is if an elder is specifically targetting you, or if you want power or to embrace who you wish.

    Theme/Rules contradictions

    -Ok, I'll allow myself one little barb toward the writters on that one. If you didn't wanted trenchcoat with katanas in vampire, don't writes rules and adventures that call for that.

    -Humanity as written does not match the desired them of slow degenerescence of morality. Most PCs would start between 5 to 7 points. The sins needed to go below are so extreme that nightly unlife wouldn't lead to such loss most of the time. Also, mirroring the arguments of an article of a blog I sadly forgot the name of, contrarily to call of cthulhu sanity, the fact that it's harder and harder to lose humanity doesn't create the desired feeling. I won't fear attaining the dreaded 0 humanity if I don't go exterminating the population of a village for fun.

    -The blood bond isn't as absolute as presented. Even without taking account of the merit unbondable, after some time, it will lose strengh if you actually will to break it. The rebellion of Carna and other tremere feeling like slave of the pyramid isn't that hard, even after the cup, all you have to do is wait and canalise your will on your desire for independance.

    Lore/Rules contradictions

    - The power relation between humans and vampires. While there's 1001 reasons for the inquisition's effectiveness, many groups of vampires are simply too powerfull RAW to ever be threatened by humanity

    -The fear of werewolves. While yes, neonates will have issues with a pack of garous, most garous aren't that though compared to many ancillae and elders, especially on the mental power part and there's no way garous can hide their caern from kindred's intelligence, and protect it from eventual constant sabotage. Also, due to dominate, the blood bond, presence, etc... There's should be many, many manchurian agents among the garous controlled by vampiric masters.

    -The fear of antediluvians eating you does not match the rules of the methuselah's thirst. Yes, they'll be hungry, but they only need to drink until they go above their "famished" rate of blood. So they might kill the vampires directly around them when waking up, but that's casulaties within one dozen vampires at most, less if the victims are of low generation themselves

    -Okay, I didn't really knew in which category to put this one. Dark powers and morality. There's multiple times, when dark thaumaturgy is mentioned, a proposed rule to make it's use the cause of a degeneration's roll for humanity or the less evil paths of enlightement. Howether, that sounds pretty arbitrary, while obtaining this power from the source is clearly not morally healthy, most of it's powers are not worse than other disciplines. Is using dark thaumaturgy to make someone suffer really worse than using the path of blood to boil someone from the inside?

    Strangely impactless lore

    - Joseph Panders has a ritual that makes you clanless, litterally. It's even precised that it can cut up to the madness network connection of the malkavians. How is it not world breaking for vampires around the globe? Why isn't there masses of converts from the nosferatus, samedi and so on desesperate to escape their curse?

    -It was mentioned in another topic, but seriously, ghost exist, there's an afterlife, there's actual demons and other spirits, and no one bat an eye? You might be spied upon by a ghost, a demon and a vampire doing astral projection right now, how does that not impact massivelly the kindred's society? Your sire that you murdered may come back to whispers your secrets to your enemies, the raging anarchs of the city may well end up bargaining with a devil for the power to eliminate you, and there's probably the entire population of vampires with Auspex 5 astrally present in your room when things finally becomes intimate between Mithra and Kemintiri...

    -Powers in general. Seriously, when 8 out of 13 clans have one mind/emotion controlling power, no vampire would meet ventrue, lasombra or tremere outside of elysium (and even then) without endlessly wondering if *that sentence* was a dominate order that you might carry. Or with 4 clans having auspex, how could you feel ever not paranoid, even at elysium? after all, it's basically impossible to prove that someone used auspex on another. These clans would have basically been banished from proper vampire society ala low clans.

    -The assamite sorcerers countered the tremere curse by creating a ritual by which a large portion of low generation vitae can be transmutted in a generation lowering potion. Now that the sorcerers are part of the camarilla, this would have massive impact, even if only elders knows it. Kindred offered lower generation for accomplishing dangerous missions, without the moral issues of diablerie, and the risk of creating addiction!

    -The caitiff are litterally creating new disciplines. Kindred of all clans would gather inceptors like precious ressources.

    -The tremere (and I suppose other blood magicians like the assamite) have a 5 level ritual allowing the sorcerer to open a gate to any spiritual realm they know the name of. They can litterally go anywhere in the umbra if they only know the name of a place (which, with dominate and auspex, they should have no issue obtaining from garous or mages).



    Commonly accepted tropes

    - Despite the many times it is repeated in books, by writters and in the fanbase, there's nothing in the writing and rules about the beast that validate the idea that it leads vampires to cruelty and is acting toward destroying a vampire's humanity. The beast is basically the id of vampires. It react to exterior stimuli. When hungry, it want blood, when angered, it want to rage, when scared, it want to run.
    Even humanity/path degeneration has nothing to do with a desire for cruelty from the beast (especially when looking at some paths that are extremely cruel, looking at you, path of night).
    When you look at how degeneration works, the beast is never the leading cause. Vampire do a terrible thing, they fail to feel remorse, hence, the beast is more prevalent. But it's not the beast that does that, at no point in the rules does the beast ever push the vampire toward immorality, it is purely content with survival.

    That is doubly damning (pun not intended) when looking at the golconda. A vampire who achieved that stated has total mastery, or at least atained peace with their beast. The beast cannot frenzy ever. It is powerless.

    Yet a vampire in golconda can suffer degeneration and lose this state that way, if the beast was the leading cause of that phenomenom, such vampire could never, ever lose humanity, no matter how cruel they are, since the beast is leashed.

    I said earlier that I dislike V5, but on this point, I'll give it that the edition helped resolved the issue by implanting compulsions, some of them are clearly beast caused impulse to be cruel.


    -"Caine's existence is not an absolute, it depends on the storyteller" and variants about the book of nod not being necessarilly true.
    I understand the idea, but seriously, there's too much published material contradicting that. It would have been better to insist more on variant origin stories of vampires if the intention was to keep things in the fog, lore bits hinting at set's story being the right one next to another pointing toward another conclusion, etc... And caine showing himself in most official end of the world scenarios. Also, there's litterally texts written by his hands in Enoch. we have the book of nod/Erciyes fragments, that accuratly prophecise, well, anything it claims to prophecise from clans being destroyed by usurpers to modern day civilisation.

    -A more recent one "the antediluvians may not be those nearly omnipotent things of legends, it might be all a plot to make them *seem* that powerful"
    Too much contradicting evidence. Saulot, according to BJD, may be close to being able to drown the world in blood, Tzimisce, at least in some books, achieved metamorphosis and became something far more than vampire, Set, Saulot and Malkav were endless fountains of accurate prophecies, Capadocius had a magical text that litterally gives one unlimited divine power and could very much make one a god, Ennoia is now more an unliving landmass than a vampire. Even Tremere, a "mere" usurper, was able to devise a massively powerful ritual protecting a vampire from sunlight.




    Missing lore where there should be

    -Seriously, what are the dark, endgame plots of the tremere? We know what are the apocalyptic plans of the giovanni, why the setites do all their stuff, the ideal that the tzimisce follows, but while there's tiny hints for the tremere, that's all. Which is weird considering how much the tremere plotting for the very long term and planning for changing everything with thaumaturgy is supposed to be part of the clan. We know that a tiny group is trying to get the true name of humanity, and that others are working to reverse the tremere embrace, but outside of that? Nada. I doubt the world shattering conspiracies of a clan of sectarian and ambitious wizards could be resumed by two secret projects.

    -The ventrue obsession with the daevas and the secret masters. Regardless on whenether they actually exist, ventrue should have been actually depicted outside of their clanbooks as following leads on them, and spying other supernaturals for traces of these elusive ennemies.

    -Metamorphosists' plans and variations. Tzimisces are always depicted as playing with vicissitude to transform themselves, yet rarely are their actual effort toward metamorphosis depicted, nor are they depicted seeking other disciplines to transcend, the "path of mind" mentioned in dark ages, is barely touched upon.

    -Auspex, yes, again. With it, you can do astral projection and enter the spirit world, or at least a part of it, that should have a huge influence on the clans that use it, they should know far more on reality than other kindred. Also, they can fly up to the moon's orbit. That would have made massive impacts on their plans. Imagine a group of cainites in the classical era or the middle ages knowing the existence and position of america, australia and so on.

    - One of my favorite blood magic bit of lore and, in my opinion cruelly underused is the staircase of heaven. A spiritual realm you can enter mentally with proper technique. You ascend it's stations (based on classical depiction of planets) by walking stairs that make you face inner challenges, meet spirits with which you must negociate to obtain blood magic, and each stations (only two revealed on eight supposed to exist) is a place of influence, such as the station of mercury, which allow the ashipu to connect with the world wide web, and at the end, the gate of the stars, which is supposed to offer something great to those that achieve it.

    -In the same way, the diverses plots to escape vampirism are pretty vagues or lacking details, we have a tremere alchemist making clones mentioned in V20, the desire of Kemintiri to make kindred into, basically, the undying, or the plot of Lazarus to transform the very nature of vampirism. But it's generally a few sentences only. I think this is especially a missed opportunity since the conflict with one's vampiric nature is one of the theme of the game.

    - More of something I'd like to see than something really missing, The opinions of clans or sects on specific disciplines, especially those not their own. Would the nosferatus push their neonates to learn auspex? Have the tremere tried to use celerity to accellerate rituals? Did the salubri considered obtenebration an evil thing? Maybe brujahs mocking those learning fortitude as not being able to take a punch by themselves, or setites despising dominate as a tool of the aeons to chain all souls?

    Aaaaaand this is way longuer than expected. Oh well, I'll do werewolf tommorow
