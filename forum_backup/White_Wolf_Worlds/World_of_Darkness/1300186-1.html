            So your average Syndicate guy can just buy all the tools he needs, requisition them from Q Branch, purchase surplus etc. But that can be boring for a PC who wants to do things themselves.

            With that said, an inventor Syndicate mage is 100% a good concept! There is cross-convention training after all.

            You could play as a former Q Branch engineer who became an business mogul, for example.

            I'd say that the "normal" answer is that a Syndicate Mage with Prime 2 Forces 3, for example, gets a team together. A group of Extraordinary Citizens or staff of some kind, fueled by his mastery of Prime 2 and Forces 3, make a set of plasma grenades for him to use. It's his magick at work, he's just working via the Management/HR instrument with a Hypereconomic practice.

            If you wanted to make that more engaging, you could make your Syndicate guy an engineer himself, and have him get directly involved in a hands-on manner.

            So if I were making a PC with this concept, I'd give them Crafts (Design), Technology, Hypertech, Leadership. Hands of Daedalus merit. 5 points in the Cult background, but not an actual Cult of course; this is just to represent his team of highly trained extraordinary citizens. Make Prime (or Primal Utility) the Affinity sphere, and make Wonders of all kinds their main focus as a Mage. So with Arete 3 and his team helping him, in his Sanctum, he's able to make all sorts of crazy gadgets. He works for the R&D wing of a successful corporation, which is where he gets his funding, and how he fits into the Syndicate concept. He's the one meeting with boards to discuss funding and profits, their next line of products etc. etc.

                I think you'll find that your understanding is flawed. Not going to entertain this discussion beyond this last reply by the way; make the most of it.

                Mystics are, RAW, as stated in M20, more comfortable with working in ways that push the boundaries of belief. The Hermetic KNOWS that in an ideal world he'd be able to summon fire just by wanting it to happen; the words/chants/writings all exist inside his head, writing them IRL is a crutch. So it's easy enough for the player to say "I'm going to work without focus, happy to take the +3 difficulty", or "this isn't how he LIKES doing magick, but he can chant the words instead of inscribing them lovingly... is that +1 difficulty, since he isn't used to it?" This is absolutely in the spirit of Mage, and it isn't just saying "I can do whatever because Magic I don't need to explain it". The explanation is right there; he knows in his heart that it's possible to summon fire by chanting. He's seen people doing it, he accepts it as part of his belief. It isn't how he likes to do it, but he knows that he COULD. So the naked Mystic needs to have their mouth gagged and their limbs restrained.

                Things are very different for our strict technomage, especially if they're a technocrat. They simply don't accept humans creating fire out of thin air, short of rare exceptions like Psionic powers. This is why M20 literally says what I'm saying:

                The second part is a bit trickier: Does your mage BELIEVE that he can do such things? Sure, maybe you’ve got Forces 3 and Prime 2 on your character sheet. A Black Suit can’t just snap her fingers and make fireballs appear, however. Such feats don’t fit her focus – they violate her beliefs. She can’t conjure fire using someone else’s focus, either; if you gave her a rune-carved staff, she’d try to hit someone with it, not summon a firestorm. That sort of nonsense is Reality Deviance, and so – Spheres be damned – the Black Suit could no more conjure fire with a staff than she could fart unicorns and shoot them into space.

                So that’s where roleplaying comes in.

                If the answer is No, as with the Black Suit mentioned above, then think of another way to make things happen. Okay, the rune-carved staff won’t work… but a lighter and some hairspray would! In the story, the agent rushes to the bathroom cabinet, grabs the Aqua Net, pulls a lighter out of her pocket, and there you go. Now your Black Suit can conjure a fireball with a Forces 3/ Prime 2 Effect, using the lighter and the spray can as a focus instrument.

                I recommend that you reread M20 thoroughly. Apologies if I'm coming off as patronizing, but if you're going to come for me and state that I have a flawed understanding, you're going to have to accept it when I correct you.

                Yes, black suits have some "naked" foci. That's why I... listed those foci, and spoke about how they could perform some effects even with no physical tools. But while a Mystic trapped in a room with only their own body can meditate until they feel like they're floating off into the distance (stepping sideways with Spirit 3, or teleporting to their sanctum with Correspondence 3, assuming the room isn't warded in some way) a black suit SIMPLY DOES NOT BELIEVE THAT THEY CAN TELEPORT BY MEDITATING. To them, meditation is a way to deepen their understanding of their own mind and body; it's what they'd use to boost their Willpower, Mental Attributes, or figure out an escape route via Entropy, or even predict likely outcomes of their imprisonment with Time 2. Black suits do not make themselves Reality Deviants. They're capable of performing Vulgar magick, but it HAS to be Vulgar magick that they believe in. And even if they have seen a Reality Deviant teleport away while meditating, he doesn't believe he can do that, since he knows he isn't a reality deviant. Agents of the NWO are committed to removing things like self-willed teleportation from reality, and replacing them with teleportation devices and similar, as per the Timetable.

                The black suit is not robbed of all options, they're not powerless, they're not completely stranded, but they are put in a position where they need to jury-rig a solution. Perhaps they can tear out the wiring in the walls and use Technology to craft a device that they can use as a focus, grab a guard's gun after distracting them with a Mind procedure via eye contact, use Mind procedures and meditation to make themselves immune to torture, or use Mind/Life and Martial Arts to force their body to temporarily mimic death in the hopes that they'll dispose of his body in a way where he can escape.

                Anyways I'm sure Likes/Replies will demonstrate that most of the people on this forum, who are very well versed in Mage mechanics, will agree with me in general. I get the impression that you feel kind of salty and really hate Mystics being able to "make shit up", but it isn't really making shit up if you believe that people can do "impossible" things just by willing it. There are huge difficulty modifiers associated with this sort of thing. This sort of thing is very hard. Stepping sideways out of a moving truck while tied up is not easy. But Mystics can ATTEMPT it if nothing else. But a Void Engineer in the same situation simply doesn't BELIEVE that they can cross over into another dimension with sheer will alone. They think they need rare and specialized equipment, protective suits, lots of electricity to power the equipment... a Technocrat who thinks "I know that mystics can do this, and those shapeshifting Deviants, so I should be able to do it with Enlightened Reasoning and Psionic Disruption" might be able to do it, at the usual +3 difficulty, BUT such a Technocrat is unusual, unmutual and the Union will frown upon such activity if they become aware of it.

                Anyways, again, read M20 thoroughly, and try to be more polite to people who know more than you

                And, to reiterate, you might benefit from reading this:

                It's sometimes worth simply boiling it down to Vulgar or Coincidental when it comes to this sort of thing; one of the features listed as something to consider with Vulgar/Coincidental is Resources. Are you working with something that already exists (the taxi down the street) or something that didn't exist (a taxi you create out of thin air).

                If a Technocrat's explanation for blowing up a lightbulb to inflict a huge amount of damage to a building is "well it has secret technological features", that's bullshit. It's just made up. That's Vulgar, simply because the internal explanation doesn't even make full sense within the Mage's paradigm; a lightbulb shattering could be coincidental, but the Mage's own internal justifications have to make sense. Just in the way that someone could just happen to develop a cold, but an Akashayana couldn't infect them by sending them an email, since that's not how their magick works, and they don't believe in it. Instead the Technocrat should actually use their Paradigm (worth remembering that while the whole union has highly standardized and formatted Practice, which makes it very easy to work together, personal Paradigm will vary, from Tech Holds All Answers to Might Makes Right and Everything is Data) to determine how they make the lightbulb explode.

                "Well by tweaking the wavelengths traveling through space around me using my personal disruption gear, I can concentrate a lattice of electromagnetic energy around the lightbulb to blow it up". Simple. Much better than pretending something always was a certain way.

                This problem only really seems to come up with Mages who aren't proper scientists in and of themselves; black suits who are the agents of the technocracy. In this case you simply have to remember that NOT ALL MAGES CAN DO EVERYTHING. Sometimes Technocrats are at a significant disadvantage because they simply can't justify blowing something up or conjuring fire, they are limited to scaring people with suit/sunglasses and Mind, blasting people with a gun with forces, finding the perfect part of a building to destroy to bring the whole thing down with Matter/Entropy... you get the idea. Some Mages are limited. Some are stuck at their desk with every effect taking 5 hours because to them alchemy is this slow process. And that's fine. If you don't want your mage to be like that, design a Mage with a paradigm/practice that allows improvisation. There's a reason that Techgnosi / Scientific Mystic is an expensive merit. There are huge benefits and drawbacks to both technomancy and mysticism. Having the benefits of both is powerful.

                                                            Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post
                                                            Mystics are, RAW, as stated in M20, more comfortable with working in ways that push the boundaries of belief. The Hermetic KNOWS that in an ideal world he'd be able to summon fire just by wanting it to happen; the words/chants/writings all exist inside his head, writing them IRL is a crutch. So it's easy enough for the player to say "I'm going to work without focus, happy to take the +3 difficulty", or "this isn't how he LIKES doing magick, but he can chant the words instead of inscribing them lovingly... is that +1 difficulty, since he isn't used to it?" This is absolutely in the spirit of Mage, and it isn't just saying "I can do whatever because Magic I don't need to explain it". The explanation is right there; he knows in his heart that it's possible to summon fire by chanting. He's seen people doing it, he accepts it as part of his belief. It isn't how he likes to do it, but he knows that he COULD. So the naked Mystic needs to have their mouth gagged and their limbs restrained.
                                                            Heck, I've thought of how a Hermetic could cast spells - with Instruments - even tied up and gagged. He uses his Mind Palace (the use of which can be either Meditation or Thought Forms).

                                                            The Hermetic visualizes his Mind Palace, and specifically a chamber within that conceptual space dedicated to ritual work. I mean Dedication in the mystical sense. When he was designing his Mind Palace, long before he ever got into this situation, he took time to set aside space for a room that is built from the ground up to mimic a ritual space he had in reality.

                                                            When he casts a spell with it, he visualizes the space, and all the ritual implements, and acts out the intricate series of steps needed to cast an effect. Not with any tools he might have dedicated beforehand to a task so he could employ it quickly, but in the "slow way", going back to first principles of Hermetic High Ritual. All the physical tools he made are shortcuts, so he could cast specific Effects more quickly when he needed them. The core of Hermeticism - the groundwork upon which the whole Art is based - is the slow, methodical work he must now return to, but in his imagination.

                                                            Casting an Effect with a Mind Palace is by no means fast, easy, or convenient.

                                                            Even standing in his actual ritual room would be faster, and less mentally draining. Because the Hermetic must keep track of the space, the ritual implements, the altar on which these implements sit, the intricate designs on the floor, and the litany of chants he must mentally recite. Even elementary rituals might take significantly longer to pull off in a Mind Palace, because he does not have the benefit of space and implements and designs and chanted words that exist independent of himself. He must keep track of it all, or he'll not only fail, but potentially fail spectacularly. Imagine if a wizard tried to summon a spirit, but didn't draw the protective circles correctly. This is the stakes a Hermetic faces when trying to visualize their way to magick. To do so requires concentration, memorization, the ability to multitask, and freedom from distraction.

                                                            It's not easy. It's not fast. In every way, it's less convenient than having any number of other Instruments. And it requires the Hermetic to be trained in that kind of deeply mental exercise. But it can be done, when necessary. If it were me, I'd count that as "casting with Instruments".
