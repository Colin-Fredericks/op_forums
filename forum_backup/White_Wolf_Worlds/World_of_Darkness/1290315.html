    How does consensual reality work if people disbelieve even science?

    02-21-2019, 09:12 AM
    Greetings,


    I am a player of Mage the Awakening and just found out Ascension, and I got confused about the Consensual Reality and how it works, I will copy (and edit) texts from other people which exposes my doubts really well:
    People in the world are religious and therefore, you should be able to use magic without a problem if you disguise it as miracles. Tons of people believe in ghosts, psychics, paranormal phenomena or outright believe in magic and witchcraft. Distrust in science is so high that a large part of people disbelieve in evolution, vaccines, and the moon landing.

    In other words, consensus simply does not work as presented in Mage books. It's a broken idea. People simply aren't that logical and rational. We're a superstitious, religious lot that believes in magic, miracles and the paranormal.

    If people don't really believe in science, then there should be nothing "backing" the scientific consensus. Because this is all about belief. Either people do believe (in which case you've got a majority of religious people - shouldn't be too hard to convince them that there's a new breed of holy men), they don't believe (but then the scientific paradigm is not backed up by anything anyway, so why should it inflict Paradox?) or people do believe in the scientific paradigm as a majority (and that is not the state of the world).

    The thing about evolution is about just how much people distrust science. This isn't about laws of reality. Remember, there aren't any laws of reality - there's just the Consensus. And here's the deal: people just aren't that enthusiastic about it. And this means that there's just not that much belief behind it. People need to believe, truly believe, to make a paradigm the dominant one. Science just isn't backed by this sort of belief and public trust. Whereas, there's tons of belief behind religions, which means that if you adjust your magic to fit the religious paradigm or claim to be psychic, the majority of people should buy that explanation.
    Unless I am missing something, nothing should work in the Mage cosmology, if this has been answered somewhere please point me in that direction.

    Thanks for the time and attention,

      Originally posted by Ibeaumanior
      If people don't really believe in anything, then there should be nothing "backing" the scientific consensus.
      There's not a scientific consensus, there's a Technocratic consensus. It may sound similar, but it's very different and, I think, it's the key of making sense to Mage.

      Mage doesn't have to make sense, but it can make sense. I'm not postulating that Mage, as the Authors tought about it, makes sense. This vision isn't necessarily the same that the Authors have and it's not canon (but it's 100% compatible with the canon, as far as I know), it's just how I see this stuff.

      Having said that:

      "Belief" it's not just how you explain the world, it's something deeper that has to do with how you "act" upon the world (this is very well represented in M20 by the tripartite division between Paradigm, Practice and Tools). At a deeper level all humans make jumps of faith. It's usefull to think about this in David Hume's terms: People doesn't have any proof that the sun will raise tomorrow - most people just can't grasp the physics needed to demonstrate this even if we accepted causality (which Hume doesn't) - yet people believe that the sun raises every day just because it always has done so. This is not a logical reason, but a falacy. Hence people believe in this w/o proof aka Faith. People always pay lip service to a lot of beliefs, but those beliefs they act upon, I think, run deeper.

      So, I would say that the Technocratic Consensus it's there because people, in the deepest sense that there is, believe in technology:

      Devout Christians, Buddhists and Umbanda practicioners alike would use a car to travel than ask for milacurous teleportation (even when the latter would be much more practical !). A devout christian may not understand or believe in the internal working of the machine, but they do believe cars are the most convinient way to travel and asking an angel to take them to the church would be totaly out of the question unless there's the dirests of needs (and even then probably they will pray for a taxi rather than teleportation - and that's probably Coincident ).
      This may be not becuase they believe milacres don't exist but because they believe they're rare, difficult, that God's name shouldn't be invoked in vain, or that they're not holly enough to deserve it...so be it, the Traditions dug their own graves when they decided to make their milacres "special".

      Our practicioners of Santeria believe in guns so much that they have a saint that you can pray to protect you against bullets...and, really, that's nonsense if guns weren't effective and fearsome to begin with.

      All you need to have the Consensus up and running it's the majority of the populace of a given area to believe that your mojo works. The Consensus doesn't care about the why and hows as long as the people of the Reality Zone can rationalize it somehow.
      Obviously, the Technocracy does care about the why and how becuase they doesn't want merely to be accepted by the Consensus but also to exlude everyone else's - and this they haven't achieved, as M20 clearly states - but as long as there's more people fighting with guns than people fighting with inchantations, technology remains on top (even if they believe that guns work trough incantation).

      The Technocracy burried the idea that guns, planes, and cars function by using them when they were still Vulgar. They accepted (not always voluntarily, though) to blow themselves and their minions up in the face of unbelievers until "guns are effective" was hammered in the minds of enough people as to constitute a bulkward against Paradox.

      People believe in (most of) technology because technology just works. In our world this is because what science says holds water (most of the time). In Mage this is because the Technocracy and normal humans pushed it 'til it worked. And even then, failures and nasty consequences of technology are sometimes atributed to a form of Paradox in the canon - and in M20 the concept of Reality Zone allows to create places where technology it's unreliable (often these are remote enough that failure can be atributed to technological necesities like "having signal").

      The examples you mention can be explained: I doubt most people disbelieve in the moon landing, but even if they did most people aren't in the position of traveling there (and those who are work in an environment where nobody doubts it). Evolution it's simply a theory, a theory can never be Vulgar - the Consensus enters in scene when you do stuff. If the anty-vaxers won the populace, in Mage vaccines would start having problems and eventually cease to function, but since medicine it's still a major part of life in advanced cities (even if most people would thank God, they usually do what the medic says), I would wage that these places aren't that well connected, don't have a lot of people (Consensus counts people, not area) and thus can be either superseeded by the global Consensus or made into Reality Zones (the Technocracy's job hasn't ended there).

                  We should also circle around the assumption that dissent from actual scientific fact is representative of a disbelief in science. Modernist argumentation values scientific evidence, rationality, etc. - this is the cornerstone of the Technocratic paradigm. But if it turns out we were 100% dead wrong about, say, photons, that would not render prior statements about them "unscientific" - a paradigm has to do with what we consider valuable argumentation, interpretation, and evidence, and not the factual outcome of investigation.

                  Remember that Intelligent Design proponents make assertions from geology, the fossil record, ergonomics (see the infamous 'banana' example) - we may believe that they are inwardly insincere, but their disagreement agrees with the (paradigmatic) rules of engagement. They're wrong about the facts, sure, but they are approaching their assertions by positing "scientific" facts to contradict the evolutionary model, and argue that the theory of evolution is not in accord with reason. They agree with the underlying paradigm of argumentation, but dispute the facts. Many of them think that we are the ones being irrational and unscientific, in fact.

                  Most of the people who buy magnetic bracelets wouldn't buy them if you said they were magic. They've been bamboozled by an argument in a paradigm they find convincing - the assertion of "rational scientific principles" which do not accord with the facts, but do accord with the paradigm of valid assertions.

                  Paradigms are models of interpretation, not bundles of facts - you can be a proponent of Intelligent Design or a faked moon landing or vaccines causing autism or UFOs and still be in accord with the Technocratic paradigm.

                      Getting back to your points, when I think of something like vaccines breaking down I think the first signs would be something more like what we saw two years ago, when the flu vaccines had an abyssmal success rate because they predicted the wrong strain for that year. Obviously lack of belief has nothing to do with that in the real world, but the two pair together quite well in Mage if that's the way you want to go. Strangely enough, belief in evolution or the moon landing don't affect things much in Mage. Evolution because it normally operates on a longer time scale than the vast majority of games, and the moon landing because it happened in the past. If the disbelief were more focused on genetics or rocketry that would likely have a bigger impact.

                      As for how the current consensus became the consensus, I attribute it to several things. First, it takes a lot of 'belief' to make something an everyday part of life, but not that much to make it happen occasionally. That sets up a situation where evidence for something can start forming before it is widely believed. Second, as others have said, people only have to believe that something works and that there is some kind of reason for it, they don't have to know or understand the reason. Third, people actually want to have explanations for the world (even if they don't want to have to learn the details) and to believe they have an element of control. In regards to that the modern paradigm offers the most bang for the least buck.

                                              Reality is not 100% the Consensus and vice versa. There are baselines, this is how Sorcerers, Vampires, Werewolves etc can exist without Paradox. This is what they call "Static" magic, sometimes its more powerful than what a mage may be able to produce at a given time, sometimes it's not. These form the rules that are built into Reality of which science/technology (small s) is one small collective method of those rules as it passes from a Dynamic state into a Stasis state (eventually all things fall to Entropy), or if you prefer this is part of the Weaver's direct influence on the world via humanity.

                                              These small collectives of rules that allow for static magic are where we get the beginnings of a Mage. This is where the pre-initiates of the Order of Hermes come from, the non-awakened Druids and so forth. The Awakening isn't of course necessarily linked to these things or people doing these things but what it does do is provide the Mage with a sudden understanding of Reality or The Tapestry and that some of these collective rules can actually be used beyond their limited scope to literally re-write the Tapestry to the mage's desires. True Magick / Awakened Magick. Now the Tapestry can endure minor tweaks but doesn't like being unraveled and rewoven and this provides Scourge which would later become / be replaced by Paradox. Now this is where the Consensus comes in. The little rules collectively called sciences are a part of Reality as we know, and some Mages Awakened not to spells and spirits and so on, but to the functional Possibilities of what the rules of science could achieve. This becomes Enlightened Science as inspired by Genius and the Order of Reason worked on the public perception enough that they provided access to science to the masses at the exclusion of the other small rules. The Consensus was always present in some way, shape or form with what the masses in an area functionally believed. However the Order of Reason got people with a focused true belief on an as yet unseen scale and (given that every human alive is a potential mage, plus the way the umbrae work) this changed what were locally effective consensuses into the Consensus, built off of what everyone experiences every day on a fundamental level and then taking it up from there. With this achieved (and while ti was being worked on) they could then start thrusting a wedge into the Tapestry and its rules so that acts of Enlightened Science would gradually become part of the Static Rules while everything else becomes rarely seen and forgotten / disbelieved in.

                                              What you end up with after all this is a Tapestry of Reality that has been slowly rewoven into something entirely different. One that is readily accepting of minor tweaks only now those tweaks take a certain theme and form consistently & globally more often (but not exclusively) than regionally or locally. However despite this the Static Rules / magic exploited by supernatural creatures and sorcerers and the existence of the umbrae and so on are still there and the rules and methods of magick are also still there. This is where the tagline of "Reality is a Lie" comes from and is the very core nature of the Ascension War because the Consensus is an artificial construct that spans the physical world created, maintained and enforced by the Technocratic Union which provides a world they can more readily control [We. Are. Control] and a battleground against those they cannot control <Control is a Technocratic illusion, data is supposed to be free 00100010111011000222> of their own choosing. The truth is that the Consensus is massively ingrained in people and the Tapestry now with things they simply do not want to let go of making it rather powerful. But it's also fragile and not because science (or even Science) is going to stop working, but because magick even after 2+ centuries of Consensus hasn't gone away. It is possible that it can work its way back in and not necessarily by replacing science in the rules but by providing options beyond what science can do and outside of the Technocratic Paradigm. It creates people who challenge and question and seek challenges and questions and maybe in that sparks more Avatars into Awakening or simply shifts the Consensus just a little bit and some of that Enlightened Science becomes a little less reliable because it's no longer a tweak in a carefully constructed Tapestry but a re-weave of a less predictable one. Again here is the nature of the Ascension War.
