                    Possibly an unpopular take, but I think this whole Edition concept needs to die in a fire.

                    I didn't always think this, I remember in the 90s reading Dragon Magazine in the library, wondering why D&D seemed to have all these rules about which Race can be which Class and which Class can use which weaponry. I remember being excited for 3rd Edition, with the Race/Class restrictions finally being scrapped... And then I got into Warhammer, which seemed to go through a new edition every five years.

                    Maybe it's capitalism burnout or maybe I'm just getting old, but I don't get why games "need" new editions every few years. The best arguments I ever saw for new editions were either the D&D 3rd Edition, which pruned their Byzantine Race/Class rules, or when White Wolf (Or Onyx Press, or Paradox) finally sat down to reckon with the problematic (mostly racist) baggage of the earlier World of Darkness.

                    Also, I'm very biased, I admit, for 90's sourcebooks and rulebooks. Being mostly softcover, they were obviously cheaper, but I also liked the black and white art better (partly because I like to trace pictures and tracing color pictures is just hellish).

                        I'm not sure it's unpopular as much as unrealistic. Even if you try to minimize the capitalist issues, working on something like an RPG is going to be a compromise if you ever want something finished other people can play. At some point you have to accept that a given rule is good enough, or that you're going to have to trust your gut with the way certain rules interact will work well far more often then a potential oddball result will crop up.

                        When you release it, you'll find your own personal assessments need to be reevaluated based on what happens "in the wild" instead of in your head or in your curated play-testing. Things you never thought of as being important will come up Things you thought we great will get meh reactions, and stuff you were worried was busted will turn out fine. You'll slowly amass a pile of notes of improvements that you want to make. Eventually you end up with a new edition when you get around to implementing all that (or at least an X.5 edition). And it's going to keep happening until you mentally stop caring about making the game better; as it will never be truly perfect.

                        There are PWYW/free RPGs out there that have multiple editions because the designer(s) just got to the point of feeling that they had enough changes to implement that it justified a full new edition rather than a bit of errata or optional rules suggestions.

                                                        To play the devil's advocate, part of the problem with KotE was whether or not it was just one culture. The sourcebook was very Chinese (according to a podcast I watched, the frequently used term Middle Kingdom was a term used for China), and the lore clearly had the Kuei Jin starting out in what is now China. But, and this is a big but, the book insisted the Kuei Jin were not just "the Kindred" of China, but almost all of Asia.

                                                        You lump the United Kingdom in as part of France in your fiction and you're going to, at least, annoy some British and French people (this would have happened even before Brexit). KotE lumped in Japan, and Korea, and Mongolia, Nepal, Burma, Singapore, Thailand, Taiwan, Vietnam, Laos, Cambodia and India, all in as part of China's Undead community. That's like saying that not only are the Kindred of the UK just like the Kindred of France, but so are the Kindred of Ireland, Poland, Germany, Belgium, Hungary, Switzerland, Spain, Portugal, Italy, Albania and Romania.

                                                        Also, the blowback from the KotE was NOT all just from white liberals "being offended on someone else's behalf." Here is that previously mentioned podcast, hosted by three Asian Canadians who tried to go through the original Kindred of the East sourcebook. I say they tried to go through it, because the podcast is two hours long, and I can confirm that at more than an hour into it, they had made it to page SEVEN.
