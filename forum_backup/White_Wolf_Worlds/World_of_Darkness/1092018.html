    M20 - How to Create a Temporarily Bulletproof Leather Coat?

    05-13-2017, 02:39 PM
    The M20 rules say that with Prime 2 you can enchant objects with quintessence to protect against harm:

    Weapons or attacks infused with Quintessence through
    Prime 2 Effects may hurt spirits or inflict aggravated damage,
    and substances infused the same way may protect against such
    harm - M20 p520

    and in How Do You Do That it says:
    Using Prime 2, the mage can “energize” the reality of an
    object so that it can inflict or soak aggravated damage. Working
    that Effect into weapons allows them to harm spirits or dish
    out serious hurt against vampires, werewolves, and other rough
    beasts. The “holy weapons” and “sacred armor” of religious
    mages and their favored allies comes from this Effect, and
    although that power tends to fade after the spell’s duration
    ends (unless the player enchants that item permanently), that
    power gives Awakened characters a much-needed edge when
    they’re dealing with the Night-Folk.

    Plus this vital item:
    • At Prime 2, the mage can create Trinkets and simple
    Charms

    The 'Rules for Wonders' in M20 give some rough ideas on how to create a Trinket and a sample 'Bulletproof Hoodie' that has a Matter 3 effect that provides four soak dice versus certain types of damage.

    Given all that, and only using rules from M20 and subsequent products how do I, as a mage with the relevant spheres, go about enchanting a leather coat to make it only temporarily (or permanently) bulletproof?

                                      Agreed. Forces 2 bullet shields can be flexible in this regard. Do you want it to deflect shots? Just stop them? Curve their trajectories away from oneself? Any of these are possible, and any of them can be coincidental, given the right setup. If the mage was wearing a large metal plate on their chest - Clint Eastwood style - it would be easy to justify shots just bouncing off. The hypothetical average person knows that bullets ricochet off hard surfaces, so it's plausible.

                                      A large, bulky coat could easily make a diverting shield coincidental. The shots get lost amid the fabric, making it difficult to prove it wasn't just a miss.
