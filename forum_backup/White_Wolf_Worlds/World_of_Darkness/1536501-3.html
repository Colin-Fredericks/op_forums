    Originally posted by Saikou View Post

    There is no such thing as room temperature liquid nitrogen, not unless you change the boiling point of nitrogen which is a matter 5 effect. And the books are very clear Matter 2 cannot change the temperature of the target.

    And this has been the case since second edition. In fact liquid nitrogen is specifically discussed in the write up of Matter in the core book

    Going by the same write up of 2e, you need Prime and Forces to heat up or cool down along with Matter 3 to change the state of an object. While Revised allows a combination of Forces and Matter 2 to change an object’s state.

    Mrev pg172
    M2e Pg206

    To clarify I am not arguing how the game should be played at your table, just explaining that these rules are in fact written up quite clearly. If you don’t think they make sense, do it the way that makes sense to you. I am only trying to explain why the rules provided make sense to me.
    The very presence of Prime in that example limits how much credence I’m going to give 2nd edition when it comes to interpreting M20. It’s clearly using a different line of reasoning. M20 doesn’t need Prime for temperature changes.

    State changes are the explicit power of Matter 3 in M20 Whether or not that includes temperature changes is a matter of interpretation, but if we are getting far enough into the details to consider state diagrams I consider that a strong sign that it does. It means we are deep enough to be considering the enthalpies of fusion and vaporization and that state changes, even without temperature changes, take a surprising amount of energy.

    If Matter 3 can ignore that heat/energy requirement to change one physical property of Matter (state), we can easily ignore the same requirement to change another property (temperature).

                                                    Neither one is scientifically feasible at all. Which is fine because Mage doesn't really have to be scientifically feasible. By rank 3, most spheres are breaking hard from what we know of the natural laws.

                                                    We're not supposed to need this level of scientific rigor to interpret the rules.                                                     In short, if you are going to get strict with impossible substances you will rapidly find most effects migrating to Matter 5. If we were seriously going to alter the abilities of the spheres to conform to natural law we would have to gut the entire system.
