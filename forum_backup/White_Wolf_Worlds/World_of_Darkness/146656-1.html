                I prefer the one-world interpretation, with pretty much every human and supernatural very ignorant or misinformed about a lot of things outside their immediate experience.

                I like Werewolf best and know only a little about most other gamelines, so I see the overall cosmology as more Werewolf-slanted in the places of contraditions. But I like to view each splat's origin story as true as possible, and try to see how they can exist together. I see a few "consensual" traits but mostly only in the Umbra, not the physical world. So for example, to the shapeshifters the Triat is three intelligent, self-aware mega-spirits with minds and desires and servants, while to the Traditions the Metaphysic Trinity is three flavors of impersonal essence that color and influence supernatural entitites. Both are right, but the three forces do exist and are metaphysically very important in the cosmos.

                One of the ways this works for me is that when a splat claims that their God or Goddess or whoever created them and the world and everything in it, I accept that said deity created that splat, but isn't necessarily the top of the heap and doesn't resemble their interpretation of it. There is a major god who cursed the Cainites, and a major earth goddess who created the werewolves, and an august personage of jade who cursed the Wan Xian, and a major god against whom the Demons rebelled... but none is THE Supreme Being who created or rules the whole universe.

                I also tend to throw Exalted in as the prehistory, because I always liked that concept.

                She/Her. I am literal-minded and write literally. If I don't say something explicitly, please never assume I implied it. The only exception is if I try to make a joke.
                My point of view may be different from yours but is equally valid.

                                I've done prettymuch what Katsura and others have done. One world with some editing and adjustments. I try and stick with the 5 original main game lines, but I'm not against allowing in a Hunter or Demon player into my game as long as they are aware the perspective of the game is going to be heavily influenced by the other systems. I try and stick with one system and let it touch on the other 4. I blame having played online in White-Wolf's original chats for this perspective. I feel old for admitting I remember when there were only about 7 or 8 rooms. Two Vampire rooms, one for each of the other four systems, Shadow's Bar for everything and the OOC room. There was really no avoiding that the 5 systems all existed in one world and it made for some pretty crazy games. As soon as there were more rooms to expand into then every system seemed more isolated and more its own universe that didn't have to acknowledge any other world's circumstances. This still made for good games, but it's definitely a different feeling. More like a clique. It has its merits and flaws and I've carried the online gaming experience with me to my own TT games. It's given me an appreciation for unpredictable diversity to keep the game fresh. As long as the ST can keep the game systems working together, the players can enjoy it. When too many world's collide at once it can be a disaster. So it all comes down to what the ST can handle and where the dividing lines get drawn.

                                    I tend to run with 1-world interpretations. I've never found the differences in the settings especially fundamental, given how little the various factions understand about the things they do interact with (and due to how much White Wolf always put into emphasising that the underlying aspects of the world tended to warp to the observer's expectations). It requires tweaking a few things (the nature of Caine's Judge, a few terminological tweaks around the Umbra, re-emphasise just how unclear the Fallen's memories are), but very little that really impacts typical play.

                                        Originally posted by lustral_star View Post
                                        Big Brother, that's the standard go-to for most WoD gamers I know. It makes sense, it's easy, and it can cause some interesting RP opportunities when the various entities encounter each other ("You do what? Oh, wow, all this time I thought that vampirism was a disease!")

                                        Unfortunately, for the metaplot of my game, I had to dig a bit deeper and answer questions with more detail than just "This group was right, this group was wrong". I wanted to cultivate a sense of something big happening in the world, and encourage the idea that the literal fate of reality itself is going to be hanging in the balance. I believe the official blurb for the "Fractured Cosmos" idea is toward the back in one of the later Mage sourcebooks... unfortunately, at the moment, I don't remember which one it's in. Probably Ascension, or one of the Storyteller Handbooks.
                                        It was in the supplement that dealt with the fate of reality itself: Ascension.

                                        ​I also prefer the Many Worlds Interpretation, in part because it means that I don’t have to be consistent from one chronicle to the next: when I’m in a Mage game, mages warp reality through focused belief. When I’m running Werewolf, their powers are spiritual in nature, and many are unwitting pawns of the Weaver (Technocrats, mostly), the Wyrm (Nephandi, mostly), or the Wyld (Marauders, mostly), up to and including having been empowered by something in the Triat. In a Changeling game, they’re humans who have tapped into the power of the Dreaming. In a Hunter game, they’re the Spellbound. In a Demon game, they’re humans who have actualized their Faith Potential. And so on. It’s like lustral_star’s “bubbles” concept, with the added fact that the various bubbles are generally synchronized with each other: events tend to play out in mutually consistent ways for the most part.

                                        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
