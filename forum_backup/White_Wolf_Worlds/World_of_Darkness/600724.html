      Dharma Book: Devil-Tiger. While it might be surrounded with the onerous baggage of KotE, it is what you want in a splat book, as it lays out exactly what the flavor of the character type is and a number of ways in which you can pursue this theme, and it makes all of them sound incredibly interesting things to do.

      Get of Fenris Revised might not be the best, but it gets honorable mention for managing to clean out the Augean Stables that consisted of the tribe's prior Deutschland Uber Alles presentation, as well as painting a picture of why a Garou tribe might not be on the favorable side of ancient legendry.

      Possessed is a comprehensive guide to four different character types that have very versatile use, and makes many of the struggles that Garou have with their Triatic enemies more nuanced while pointing out "best uses" for fomori and such. It also introduces a very well done mechanical tradeoff system with Autonomy; its only flaw is an awkward Willpower cap. That aside, for the Triatic possessed, have a nice flavor flourish in getting five points of powers with no demands. The first hit, after all, is always free.

                  This is probably weird, but I really liked the ending books: Gehenna, Ascension, Apocalypse and Time of Judgment. At the time, of course, I was really upset that the WOD I loved was ending and getting replaced, but I ended up finding so many interesting ideas in these books.

                  I've always been fascinated with the idea of running a game set after one of the really apocalyptic end game scenarios, like when the Earthbound or Nephandi win. Something where heroes emerge from the downtrodden and enslaved to run, hide, unite and fight back. (And maybe get to time travel back to change things.) I just think that would be so much fun.

                  I've also thought it would be really interesting to run a (slightly modified to suit my particular taste) version of Wormwood for Vampire. That seemed like it could be really intense.

                          For me, here are my picks...

                          Chicago By Night 1st Edition: This is the book that started it all in terms of setting books, and it did it the best.

                          Vampire: The Masquerade 1st Edition: Things are always best when you go back to the beginning

                          The Hunters Hunted: The first book for playing mortals and arguably the best one. Also introduced the Children of Osiris, a bloodline I loved and wished they could have done more with, instead of killing them off in Revised Edition

                          Demon Hunter X: Generally I don't like Kindred of the East, but this book was really good. Especially if you're like me and you love anime

                          Street Fighter: The Storytelling Game: Okay, while technically not a oWoD book, it's from the same era and I thought I'd give it an honorable mention

                          World of Darkness: Mafia: I know a lot of people hated this book, but I loved it. Mainly because I am a sucker for Mafia movies and shows like Goodfellas or The Sopranos

                          Clanbook: Ravnos Revised: This book was really refreshing because it removed the unfortunate implications that the Ravnos suffered from in 2e. This book is why the Ravnos are one of my favorite clans.

                          Ashes to Ashes: A classic early oWoD adventure module

                          Dust to Dust: A modern V20 module that is a throwback to the bygone classic era of 1e

                          Mage: The Ascension 2nd Edition: While Vampire is the game I have the most experience with, Mage is my favorite overall in terms of concept and this book is the reason why

                          Elysium: The Elder Wars: Great for Elder games

                          Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand: I know what you are thinking, but bear with me. This book was not that great overall, but does not deserve the vitriolic hatred it gets from both White Wolf and the fanbase. If you take the book for what it is and the kind of chronicles it's intended for, it actually works quite well. For the type of games I enjoy, this book is really good. Not as good as Elysium: The Elder Wars, but I liked it for what it was. And it's still better than WoD: Gypsies.

                          Montreal By Night: Yes, this book is fucked up, but in a brilliant way. It is an excellent resource for Sabbat players and Storytellers by showing how the Sabbat actually control their domains. A bit crass to say the least, but an excellent resource for Sabbat fans nonetheless.

                                  Traditionbook: Euthantos 1/2e was a great read. That book, more than anything else, proved to me that the Euthanatos were more than just emo, gothy assassin types that the original Mage: the Ascension presented them as. After reading that book, I realized that the so-called "death mages" never kill for shits and giggles (unless they're utterly tainted by Jhor) and always make sure that their assassination targets are people for whom the world would be better off without.

                                  Elysium: the Elder Wars, for the same reason Camilla mentioned: to be able to play elder characters.

                                  Guide to the Camarilla: I had been waiting for this book or something like it for years when it finally came out. Finally a book that took a look at the entire Camarilla sect as a whole rather than from the perspective of a single clan. tGuide to the Sabbat and Guide to the Anarchs were both useful, but it was Guide to the Camarilla that I really enjoyed reading.

                                  DC by Night: Yes, Chicago By Night, both editions, were very good setting books and set the mold for the rest. But DC By Night holds a special place in my heart for being a city setting book that is almost as good as the Chicago books and also because I live in DC.

                                      Most of my reads are core books. Since living in Brazil meant rare opportunities to see sourcebooks, I mostly had access to the entirety of cWoD through the internet, so second-hand information was my primary source. And I had the dubious opportunity to read Gypsies, so I didn't wanted to look too deep at first. But from what I read, what I can say...

                                      Vampire: The Masquerade 1st Edition - This was the book that defined Punk-Gothic. Doesn't matter if you don't like Vampire, the World of Darkness started here, and this book still have the best mood for the game of a world shrouded in darkness and horror.

                                      V20 - As Vysha said, a wealth of information without methaplot. Its almost like a toolbox, with everything you need rules-wise to play or thinker with the setting as you see fit. I wished W20 to be so complete.

                                      The Hunters Hunted and Street Fighter, as Camilla and for the same reasons. Our similarities end here, tough.

                                      Mage: The Ascension Revised - Since I learned Mage by friends, I wouldn't put the previous books in the list. I DO worry with mechanical issues, tough, and Mage allways had a lot of them (even to cWoD standards). Revised presented the best set of rules, even if a little tough on Paradox.

                                      Changeling: The Dreaming 2nd Edition - I love this setting as much as I hate it, and its a lot. Just wish it wheren't so repelent to most players of the other lines. I have only TWO friends who play Changeling, among more than a dozen of cWoD players.

                                      Demon: The Fallen - Beautiful. Touching. At the same time darker than Vampire and lighter than Changeling, for they are the only splat that can realy have hope in the future. I love to play as a Fallen, be in a Demon game or with other splats, as hard as can be to conquer their thrust.

                                      I can add that I liked most of the End Times books. Gehenna only have some issues with too little on the hands of the players on the scenarios as writen, but nothing the ST can't change. Ascension was the sad exception, I found it barely good and quite frustrating compared to even Time of Judgement.

                                          Let's see..

                                          Get of Fenris revised- As Saur Ops said, this book took a tribe that had gotten the short straw in the 1st ed tribebooks by a mile with its nazi theme, and changed it into a very complex tribe. Kudos.

                                          Silent Striders revised-Basically the best tribebook in my opinion. Makes the tribe complex, explains away the Curse they suffer and makes it into something REALLY fun to play, and discusses it all in a solid tone.

                                          Book of the Wyrm 20th ed&Tribebook: White Howlers- I lump these together, because together they basically remade the BSD tribe. As much as I loved the 2nd ed Wyrm book and its BSDs, eventually I got tired of the 'BSDs are only trying to rebuild the world' stuff. Not only are the new BSDs far from stupid and notedly clever, their morality and viewpoints are far sinister and far easier to fall into now.

                                          As for the Howler book on its own, Iron Age WtA and a look at a rarely spoken about group of people. And, most importantly, excising the old Conan the Barbarian take on the picts.

                                          The Skinner- Skin Dancers are my favourite foe in WtA bar none. This adventure book gives us motivations why kin become Skin Dancers, a way a pack would function. And it uses Sam Haight as a foe VERY well.

                                          Dark Ages Werewolf- Not Werewolf the Dark Ages. This book details life of the garou during the middle ages, noting how they acted and survived and how the world reacted to them.

                                          Wild West Companion- Solid book detailing foes and friends in the savage west, even offering two new ones, Los Infelizo and Abominations.

                                          Rage across the World 20th- A book that kinda got forgotten in the W20 frenzy (heh), but still a very solid one. Detailing sept life, garou in various areas and answering questions that fans had been asking for a while.

                                          Fool's Luck: Way of the Commoner- Changeling the Dreaming is a troubled game that has a lot of aspects that conflict with one another. Fool's Luck takes the aspect of the Accordance War and makes the setting into an awesome tale of war and loss. It also lessens the Banality-scare a lot.

                                          My gallery.

                                              Wraith's Shadowplayer guide is still my best. Developping the game world, guiding on the most iconic aspect of the game, essais that are usefull, funny and insightful.. for any game (not just Wraith).

                                              I've a a lot of love for Blood sacrifice: the Thaumatugy Companion. Now, I don't care about the Thaumaturgical Path, but the book sold me on the idea of the vampire as a cultural parasite. Vampire (Kindred or Cainite), don't have any original ideas, they steal ideas form a culture (or, if we extrapolate, a society), twist them for they own benefit. This book explained this process quite well and have a great impact on my games. And the legend about a vampire teleporting itself on the Moon, only to realise that if we can see the Moon, it's because it reflect sunlight is still my favorite in-game vignette ever.

                                              Killing Street, for KOTE, is one of the most striking despiction of the criminal world and of the urban slums. It is set in Asia, but most of the description can be set anywhere. I think it is the most underated book of the cWOD.
