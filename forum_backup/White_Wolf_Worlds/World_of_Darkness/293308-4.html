                                    To take from Star Trek, there is an idea: IDIC = Infinite Diversity in Infinite Combinations. What exactly is Camp, by definition: v. tr. To give a deliberately artificial, vulgar, or banal quality to: camped up their cowboy costumes with chaps, tin stars, and ten-gallon hats. (The American Heritage Dictionary). Growing up in a multicultural family (Cajun, Celtic, German, Indigenous Peoples, and Hispanic) I got the outsider-in view on a lot of what diversity is allowed and not allowed to mean. For instance, is John Wayne the hero who rides in to take care of those who need a hero, or the white man who interferes because the downtrodden minority in the film couldn't possibly be able to handle their own situation? I have heard arguments in both directions, so this is an academic and rhetorical question.

                                    The idea of camp seems to come from the question of perspective. One person's hero is another's stereotype. There is a food regularly served on the Pow-wow circuit called fry bread. Nothing about fry bread is indigenous. Some look on it as a symbol of oppression. Some see it as a symbol of surviving oppression. Many just see it as food or income. All these perspectives are correct in that they exist.

                                    Where I always get amused is that when one subgroup of society works to maintain the purity of their heritage as Indigenous Peoples, members of the Nations (i.e. followers of Santa Ria, Candomble, Lucumi), or maintain insular social communities such as Chinatown, etc. then it is Multicultural. But when a Werewolf tribal subgroup wants to do the same for their Germanic ancestral traditions it is being Nazi-esque.

                                    The long and the short of it is that when a group is making up the stories that entertain them - they should express those elements they find entertaining. However, keep in mind that each stereo-type that is deliberately omitted is equally an expression of bias against someone else's hero. Infinite Diversity in Infinite Combination will provide a place for those they cannot stand as equally valuable to those whom they exalt. Besides, where is the real drama in only role-playing to that which we find comfortable and self-reinforcing?

                                    So, if your cowboy wants a ten-gallon hat, will they be allowed to wear one or have their dreams dashed because it is too campy?
