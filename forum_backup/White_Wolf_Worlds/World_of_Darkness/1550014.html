    Reflexion about armor protection

    04-10-2025, 06:43 PM
    According to V20, W20 and M20 an armor give the character extra soaking dice when suffering damage. When the character isn't supernaturally tough those dice are the only one they can use to soak lethal from guns, rifles and other similar threats

    Let's focus on firearms and use a mere humans with no power at all. A vile murderer shoot him with a gun (a classic 9mm). Considering how a body armor such a skevlar work, it should prevent the bullet from penetrating the body, leaving only the impact kick the poor guy. Since the bullet didn't go through then there's no hole in the skin, no muscle and soft tissues torn appart and no bone shattered, meaning that if the victim suffer damage it's going to be bashing and not lethal, allowing him to soak them with it's stamina

    Problem: after some research it seems that if soft armor can protect against pistols, when it come to rifle it's another story. From my reading most of those rifles round use hard material to get through armor, wich is why most bulletproof vest used in army are built with hard plate that can stop these kind of bullet.
    It means that a soft body armor protect you from most small caliber (mostly pistols) while hard body armor protect you from bigger caliber such as rifles. And since there's 6 levels of protection in body armor it's even more complex that just soft and hard

    If you were to change something in armor functioning, what would you do?

      I don't think you can really fix armor without overhauling the entire combat system.

      Though a quick fix is to copy the CofD 2e combat rules: damage and "soak" is unrolled, weapon damage adds to final damage, armor has two potential effects. Armor can reduce final damage or downgrade some lethal damage to bashing damage.

      So, in CofD2e, a Kevlar vest is rated 1/3 (general/ballistic). The first three lethal damage from guns is reduced to bashing damage, and the total damage (starting with the worst damage) is reduced by 1. So if you get 5 damage from a gun, you end up taking 1 lethal and 3 bashing.

      It's far from realistic, but it has a better feel towards armor being significant without making combat a slog by making characters too tanky as armor might make gunfights less deadly but normal humans are still going to fall quickly to all the bashing damage (and can just be executed while unconscious if the enemy wants to) to make sure violence is still very much a threat (though the ST doesn't have to make the enemy so ruthless).

                              To just put in a bit of real world thing into all of that... guns aren't about being accurate and saving ammunition when it comes to being weapons of war. For hunting or sport shooting that might be the case. In battle, guns are the dominant weapon because they don't require nearly as much skill or physical conditioning as older weapons to provide the same level of lethality. You can carry far more ammunition, that both wrecks hit targets and punctures armor with more ease than bows or cross bows, and can be fired fast enough that they remain functional even in relatively close quarters. Most estimates of modern (WWI and later) warfare put it around ~10K bullets fired for every enemy causality. Largely because machine guns are a thing, and machine guns have killed multiple orders of magnitude more people than snipers have. Bullets are, ultimately, cheap. Unloading 20 to kill one target quickly is more efficient than ensuring one shot one kill.

                              This is why shrapnel spewing weapons are so brutal (and so many are banned by the Geneva conventions), and the last century has literally given people lead poisoning because so many bullets are in the soil from major battles and are slowly leaching lead into the ground water.

                              Whether or not you want your RPG to reflect that is a different matter, but if you're looking at the real world and trying to make a game that has some sense of "real" that's the real you have to consider. The default way guns are used in violence against other humans is shooting a lot of bullets and hoping one hits.

                                      As I said from the start, "better" for me would take a much larger and holistic approach to the combat engine of the game.

                                      A very specific issue that I already brought up, is that buffing armor while keeping the ability to target unarmored areas is going to negatively impact game play. This is exactly what happened in the nWoD/CofD 1e rules. Called shots and armors imparted the same kind of penalties, so it was very simple for players to figure out that if you can shoot someone wearing a Kevlar vest in the arm for a -2 penalty, or just shoot them normally for a -3 penalty... you always go for the arm/leg/etc. There was zero downside (and if you rolled well shooting someone in the arms or legs gets you extra impact too).

                                      The WoD math is more opaque and harder to know exactly when aiming for unarmored body parts is mathematically superior to not doing so, but if you do something like jack up the number of soak dice the armor provides, then you're increasing the net positives to just aiming around armor; which I pointed out isn't how fights really play out in a gun totting violent scenario.

                                      That isn't necessarily bad. In lots of genres the protagonists rarely bother with armor in stark defiance of real world combat logic, while heavily armored foes are easily dispatched by the protagonists unrealistic combat tactics that work because the intent is to make the protagonists look cool as they pull off wild combat stunts.

                                      So "better" is subjective to intent. If your intent is to get players to invest in armor, you need to make sure armor is valuable. Value isn't just the direct benefits, but how things slot into the total equation. If armor makes a character nigh invincible against direct attacks, but can be circumvented with a +1 difficulty to the attack roll, than the value of armor is really +1 difficultly to attacks instead of insane damage reduction; minus any penalties you might get for wearing armor (both mechanical and narrative).

                                                      OK, so, for me, the "best" way to deal with that as phrased is to get away from NPCs and PCs operating on the same rules. Most designers focus on the benefits of simplifying NPCs for STs(/GM/DM/etc.), but it also lets you pull tricks that make the enemies easier to tweak to match the level of threat you want without mucking with the combat rules as much.

                                                      For example, if you have NPCs not roll damage or soak, how you calculate their static damage and soak values is something you can play around with that doesn't change the math as much as if you broadly change the combat system.

                                                      So, things like damage type reductions (lethal to bashing) really doesn't matter to making human hunters more threatening. 8 Bashing vs. 8 Lethal is about if they survive not if they can keep fighting in the moment.

                                                      If you set the calculation of NPC automatic soak value to: Stamina/2 + Armor (if applicable for either if you want to mirror the damage type divide), a Stamina 4 hunter decked out in high end armed police (SWAT, etc.) gear rated at 4 soaks 6 damage against instead of 4ish against Bashing and 2ish against Lethal/Agg. Not enough to completely tank what a vampire or werewolf can do, but enough to take a few hits without being out of the fight before they can do anything. You can do similar with NPC weapons to make high end gear more impactful and stress that getting into fights with people bringing assault rifles is dangerous even for supernaturals.
