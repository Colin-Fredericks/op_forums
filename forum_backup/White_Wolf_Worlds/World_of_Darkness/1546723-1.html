            It's not terribly different from Revised's section on the same topic (though Time-Triggers aren't discussed there in Revised). Concentration has been part of magic in Mage, but always vague and ST-call based in what it actually means for when the increased difficulties will hit or not. "Concentration" isn't a category of duration in M20 (or previous editions); as in the effect ends when your concentration breaks.

            Rather, if a Forces mage is actively controlling fire to create a show of flame-sculptures, it's a single spell with whatever duration you got on your roll, that requires concentration. A break in your mage's concentration will cause your current fire-art to lose cohesion, but as long as you have duration left, you can have your mage recreate it by focusing again.

                        I absolutely abhor the notion of setting effects based on "I feel that..."

                        This approach of "going on feelings" is what have caused the innumerable examples of poorly designed effects that have infected Mage ever since the first edition, rather than analytical adherence to what each lvl is supposed to do.

                        What Entropy 2 does is exactly to deal with conditions; Entropy 3 deals with probabilities of the matter and Entropy 4 deals with probabilities of life patterns; the "I feel is too little" is a poor argument to me, and simply turns the lvls of Spheres completely meaningless, thus turning all effects arbitrary which causes the exact problem I mentioned of infecting the game with nonsensical poorly designed official effects, which culminated in the abomination that is HDYDT.

                        ​​​​​I wouldn't mind if the requirement were Entropy 4 or Forces 5 and Spirit 4 and Prime 3, if any of those were coherent with what they do, but such isn't the case. Likewise, there are things that may "feel too much" - as in, require too high lvls for seemingly "simple tasks" but alas, the system must show some coherence, otherwise we should just tell STs "just go with your guts".


                        I dont buy the "the spell fails till trigger". It makes no sense to me therefore i operate on the asumption that the spell is like a program with an when, for or if loop on certain elements of its effect.
                        But "the spell fails till trigger" is exactly what a trigger is. Indeed, if we are talking about computational algorithms, that's exactly what a conditional do - if executes a command or don't execute a command unless a set condition is met. "If Else, Or Else, If Not", all of those are conditions that causes the algorithm to "fail" in a sense, unless said condition is met.

                        The Time 4 variant (which still needs Entropy 2 on top of it mind you), is just the same but in reverse, the spell is only activate unless pre established condition is met. So instead of "fails till trigger" is "only activates when trigger", but is essentially the same concept, so I don't see why you would be against the concept if that's the exact idea of "triggers"
