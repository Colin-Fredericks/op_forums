              I once wrote this in my moment of random inspiration that might be in the spirit of what you're looking for: Fallout VtM. Hope it's to your liking, feel free to take any of it and use or discard it however you like with my blessing.

              The Clans
              Assamite:
              There weren't too many Assamites in the US Pre-War and there are next to none in the Post-War one. The Assassins naturally thrive as much as anyone on their particular business, but their business is one where you don't want to be known as anything else than "someone who can fix a problem." In the Post-War America where communications are generally on the level they were before phones and public postal services in many parts of the US, the few remaining Assamites on the continent tend to act much like the Hashashyyn of the Middle Ages and travel around the US discretely and eventually return to a set destination for training. Many of the Clan's tradition remain and are practised regularly by all Assassins with dedication and knowledge of the main Clan and the ways of their rare few peers.

              The Warrior Caste has changed very little since the time before the War, they remain the Assassins they once were and take their jobs with the same dedication they always did.

              The Sorcerer Caste typically have two roles: Master their magic for the good of the Clan and maintain the strongholds the Clan have. It's their role to essentially act as Keepers of Elysiums for their peers in the Clan.

              Finally the Vizier Caste have two tasks: Information gatherers and contractors. Typically they are sent across the wasteland to infiltrate the various Princedoms and establish contacts within, usually undercover. When the opportunity to arrange a job presents itself, it's they who get into contact with their Warrior brethren, whether via courtiers, in person or via Auspex. Like the Warriors they wander and eventually return to their stronghold every so often, but their role is one much more pacifistic in nature.

              All in all, the Assamites are usually seen as boogeymen for younger Kindred who came after the war and not a few recently embraced have never heard the Assamites exist at all. Should one be exposed it's generally fear that's felt towards them due to their fearsome reputation and ways of life, for better or worse. Most importantly, their taste for diablerie has never once subsided, and with the Camarilla largely gone there is little that can stop an Assassin on the prowl.

              Brujah:
              As the bombs fall and the cities are put to the torch, the Brujah of the US suffer heavy casualties as the majority of their membership vanish into ashes; few can hope to survive above ground in the cities whether they are inside or out. Those who aren't torched by the bombs will likely be buried by rubble; the ones who aren't or struggle out with their impressive strength must worry about the prospect of the sun with so few shelthers now remaining. And moreover, in the post-War setting there are very few people left to feed from in the rubbles of the cities. And the ones there are highly radioactive.

              These aside however, in the post-War America you need to be tough in order to survive, and out of all the Clans no one has as much raw pack to their punches as the Brujah do. The Rabble that does survive the bombs are in a very strong position to make the wastelands theirs, especially in times like this when people are easily rounded and gathered up into tribes and groups for mutual protection or just to have fun in an ended world. Moreover with the bombs having destroyed society, the Camarilla nolonger has the power and influence it once did; nolonger can the Masquerade be enforced through strength much like in the Dark Ages. It's rarely been easier for individual Brujah charismatic or intimidating enough to gather people under them and become warlords of the wasteland to whatever ends they wish; and rest assured, the Brujah has one of the easiest times doing just that, making them still quite a threat and rivals to the remaining Ventrue. For the Anarchs, the bombs became a necessary evil to win the freedom they've struggled so hard to gain. Now that they have it, only time can tell what they, and others, will do with it.

              Followers of Set:
              Although the Setites took a great deal of losses due to their increased intolorance for fire and sunlight, thus seeing a good deal of their temples turn to ash-filled graveyards, very little has changed with how the remaining Setites do business. The surviving Snakes have for the most part focused on recollecting themselves, re-gather their numbers via embraces and use the opportunity of the power vacuum to get themselves up on their feet again and claim the power the Camarilla had.
              This of course puts them at odds with anyone trying to do the same thing, as well as those who don't wish to submit. That said, a great many wastelanders nowadays indulges chems (drugs) and have little ethical qualms about the likes of prostitution and in some cases forced labor; and whatever sells the Snakes peddle. They have a good deal of influence in Nevada where their goods and methods are most appreciated amongst a tribe known as "Caesar's Legion" and they always seek to expand further and further across the US. Besides the remaining Camarilla trying to bring themselves back on their feet again, the Anarchs have no greater foe than the Post-War Followers of Set.

              Gangrel:
              Though the cities burned and the majority of Gangrel within perished with everyone else, Kindred and Kine, the Gangrel stand in a good position in the Post-War setting. Many of the Gangrel could flee into the ground when the bombs fell and shield themselves from the fire using the earth. True; it was not always enough, rubble still kept many from rising again and the need to feed remains ever present. But for those whose refuge into the earth paid off can proudly take credit for the Gangrel's rumored tenacity and ability to navigate the rural wilds and rugged landscapes.
              Although none could predict it at the time, what saved the Gangrel more than anything was their preference for the wilds over the cities. Although little shelther can be found in the wilds for those who cannot sink into the earth, the undeveloped landscape made poor notice for the Chinese when they began launching their bombs against the US; thus the Gangrel hiding amongst the woods were often affected due to proximity at worst and not at all at best depending on their distance from the cities. And now with the Old World ended and the new one taking its place, few Clans stand as strongly in Post-War America as the Gangrel do; and for better or worse for the ones living there, with the cities nolonger the urban jungles they once were and becoming increasingly reclaimed by the wilds as they are, more and more Gangrel are beginning to wander there to see for themselves what they can find in the remainders of the Old World.
              And if they claim territory amongs the ruins, your options lie between respecting it and fighting them for it.

              Giovanni:
              The Giovanni of the Post-War US have a very mixed bag of blessings and curses in the modern world. On one hand they lost a good deal of their family's members in the bombings and their financial investments and influences lost all the sway they once had leaving the family's interests crippled in their ruined mansions in the living world.
              In the lands of the dead however the Giovanni are the greatest Necromancers still existing out of all the supernatural beings in the world, none other have had as much time and effort put into the Underworld as they have and in the wake of the Old World's fall, the Giovanni have more freedom than they ever did in the past with the fall of the Camarilla and Sabbat.
              Many of the Giovanni sought refuge beyond the Veil when the bombs first fell, giving them a unique advantage of dodging bombs even as they fell. Consequentually large portions of the Giovanni population survived the War and are one of the most well-off Clans currently existing. Their efforts to replenish the wealth they once had in the mortal world is still ongoing, and they remain xenophobic and careful about outside influences; but they nonetheless have among the most stable pieces of land wherever their mansions still exist, and if that fail there is always the Underworld were a great many refugees remain for just such occasions they are needed. And moreover, the Giovanni have quite a great advantage when it comes to keeping contact via their Ghost allies, making communcation between the Clan very simple.

              Needless to say, the wake of the war has seen a sharp increase in Ghosts on and about and as said no one is a greater expert on death than the Giovanni. They know every trick they need to manipulate ghosts, bargain with those needing exorcisms and build themselves right back up from where they left off after the War.

              Lasombra:
              Although they are not quite as concerned with staying in high places as the Ventrue are, the Lasombra nonetheless generally prefer places of more luxurious natures where they have a good view on everything. The direct result of this saw to the demise of a great deal of US-stationed Lasombra to the nuclear flames of the bombs. In the aftermaths the surviving Keepers saw the dissolution of the vast majority of Sabbat as the loss of the Sabbat's ability to organize itself into anything more coherent than a few Packs working together over similar goals.

              As a result of this loss the Lasombra for the most part- the ones who weren't in the roving Packs anyway -resorted to the same tactics their rivals in the Ventrue did minus the lipservice to their old Sect and began various Princedoms across the US where they could establish themselves ruling their territories ruthlessly and with a strict sense of order. Sometimes openly, other times not so much. Out of all the Clans, no one can better incorporate the old mindset and systems that were in place during the Dark Ages than the Lasombra can, and for those strong enough to hold power the method is tried, tested and very, very efficient. Sufficed to say, the Lasombra took quite a loss during the war, but they are picking themselves up quicker than most other Clans are; putting them in a good position in the Post-War America. Given time, it's not too unlikely to think they will stand at the top of the "High Clans" of the new world, should such a system once again rise, much to the dread of their hated rivals and to the delightful amusement of the Keepers themselves.

              Malkavian:
              Although many lunatics perished with the bombs, a surprising 50% of the Clan proper actually endured the falling of the bombs relatively unscathed. Few can properly explain how or why, but no one can deny that Clan Malkavian is one of the largest Clans currently occupying the remnants of Post-War America.
              Although since the bombs fell many Malkavians have begun striking out on their own again with whatever interest they happen to have, quite a lot of neonate coteries consist exclusively or majorly of Malkavian ones. When questioned about the large numbers of Malkavians you typically gain varying responses ranging between "Luck", "lots of embraces Post-War", "Planned ahead of time" and sometimes even "I just happened to be somewhere safe." However most Pre-War Lunatics often speak about an increasing feeling of dread and doom in the days leading up to the war which became an increasingly potent distraction blankening out anything else in favor of trying to dig themselves as deeply beneath ground as possible; whether with help of the Nosferatu, Gangrel, by seeking the Vaults or literal digging. Whatever means and method, the lunatics who did not (or could not) resist the urge to flee quite obviously endured all the way to the present Post-War world they now live in.

              In many cases Malkavians make a heavy presence in any court existing giving quite a lot of say in what goes on in any given Princedom due to sheer numbers alone; and a vast majority of Princes- of the non-Malkavian variety -have acknowledged that one of the worst things you can do now is to piss off a Clan with high numbers and frightening amounts of united purpose once they put their mind to it. To this end most holds have at least one lunatic along acting as a "Seer" of sorts whose main role is comparable, if not entwined, to the Primogen of the Clan. Sufficed to say, whenever you see roaming bands of Vampires in the wastes there is a significant chance it's a Malkavian one.

              Nosferatu:
              Although it's not to say quite a few Warrens didn't fall when the bombs hit, the Nosferatu's preference for the sewers and tunnels became their salvation more often than not as society was burnt into a big radioactive crisp. Prior to the bombs some Nosferatu made good business offering underground shelthers to certain members of other Clans, away from the main Warrens of course, and gathered quite the wealth and favor currying from it. Sadly, they overestimated the worth of paper money in a world where society has fallen. Dollars hardly meant what it once did and most of the survivors who eventually began using currency preferred to turn to the more sturdy and easy-to-find (bottle) caps.
              With the cities barely standing and most migrating to all sides, the Nosferatu nolonger have the information network they once did, simply because information isn't as centralized as it once was. Much like the Camarilla, the Nosferatu have gone back in method to the Dark Ages where instead of frequenting select locations and circling around important individuals, they instead keep their Warrens as homely caves beneath the grounds to return to when their trips out into the world to learn as much as possible of Post-War America and reestablish contact between the Warrens and other members of the Camarilla still enduring.

              Members of the Warrens who do not focus their efforts on establishing databases and full electricity in their caves are often expected to depart the nights and wander the wastelands to scavenge, establish contacts and collect as much information as they can and possibly also direct outsider-Nosferatu to the local Warrens. In times like these, Clan Unity means more than it ever has since the Dark Ages. Even in a world were they nolonger need to be as mindful of the Masquerade as when society stood tall, they remain loathed for their clearly inhuman appearence and even the Ghouls can tell them off from one of their own. At least, more often than not.

              Ravnos:
              Before the War, no Clan was as low in numbers as the Ravnos, threatened by the brink of extinction since the beginning of the 21st century, disliked by everyone of their peers with next to no Elders of their own to rely upon. Even their old tactics of standing by their peers in numbers could never again be relied upon.
              As the War commenced, the Ravnos had become a full on Clan-in-name-only who travelled in groups according to where their wanderlust took them, usually by travelling between city to city. This had a mixed result when the bombs hit; about half of the remaining Gypsies fell with the bombs much like their other Vampiric peers with only the most enduring ones standing any hope of survival. But at the same time, the other half of the Clan were not currently in a city when the bombs fell, making their situation very similar to that of their supposed cousins, the Gangrel.
              Naturally they aren't nearly as well off as the Gangrel are, even Post-War, but because of the survivors fortunate sense of time and place, a lot of Ravnos managed to survive the bombings into the Post-War America. And with so many others of their peers gone with the bombs, the Ravnos are perhaps in the strongest position they've ever been since the Week of Nightmares. That's to say, they're not very strong at all and still mostly consist of roving bands of wanderers moving from place to place, with little sense of communications between themselves. But hey, if they do gather up maybe they can actually use the time-honored tradition of 'The Treatment' if someone pisses them off! And if any can make themselves out to be trustworthy enough to be entrusted with messages, few make as great couriers as the Ravnos do.

              Tremere:
              In the wake of the bombs the landscape of the US saw next to no large city still standing without being pillaged clean, and few other Clans were as heavily concentrated in the cities as the Tremere were. Consequentially the Tremere, an already underwhelming Clan as far as numbers are concerned, suffered among the greatest casualties of all in terms of raw comparison between percentage and numbers. In the entirety of the United States of the Post-War world, it's safe to say that the Tremere are fortunate if a handful of Chantries still remain standing with an actual population remaining.

              With the Camarilla and society in shatters, the Tremere are now left to themselves in many cases and as a result they have become extremely introverted and xenophobic, much akin to the more conservative aspects of the Brotherhood of Steel with a focus for Thaumaturgical Rituals rather than Pre-War technology.
              They are aware just how many enemies they still have, especially in the Tzimisce, and with the Ventrue in many cases nolonger able to protect them they must now look to their own defenses with whatever tools at their disposal. With the bombings they lost a great deal of contact between themselves and many texts on Thaumaturgy burnt with the Kindred who scribbled them. However the Tremere remain strong with their practice of Blood Magic and no one can deny that the remaining Tremere are individually powerful and even moreso in a group. Their Chantries are tightly knit and no member within are risked or much exposed to the outside world. Although many dangers exist in the horizon for the Warlocks, few places in the wastelands are as safe as the inside of a Chantry if you're of the right Clan.

              Although the Camarilla for the most part has ceased to be in the conventional Old World sense, most Tremere still adheer to the rules of Vienna including the Vinculum between themselves and the tight regulations on embraces. However, with the inability to transport new blood from the Inner Council and the unwillingness to risk the outside world, many Elders of the remaining Chantries bind new neonates to themselves instead and rarely send anyone to pursue fleeing Warlocks without complete certainty that they can be brought back or killed. Every member of the Usurper Clan are utmost important and the Elders play their cards accordingly.

              Toreador:
              Much like the other city-dwelling Kindred, the Toreador took some of the strongest punches to their numbers when the bombs fell and as a result of the massive depopulation they are now one of the least populated Clans in Post-War America. Their Disciplines of Auspex and Presence gives them a good chance at carving out desired positions among the surviving population of Kindred and Kine alike, and one or two members of the Clan of Roses have even made grabs for power in the vacuum. Next to the Ventrue, the Toreador are the most devoted to seeing the Camarilla rise again from the ashes and become the bastion of stability it once was for the Vampiric population of the world. A lot of Toreador remain as invested in human interests as they ever were and not a few seek to rebuild the United States and see the surviving towns replenished to their old glory.
              While it's not to say there are no Toreador ambitious enough to seek to carve out their own particular slice of power somewhere in the wastelands, most are more concerned with finding a safe haven and outfitting it for a comfortable and luxurious stay. The second goal is typically with gathering a herd so they can indulge their old pleasures admist the crowds with a vested interest in obtaining contact with their Kindred peers and get themselves admitted and acknowledged into whatever Kindred Society they find and help molding it into something worthy of both the US, the world and most importantly: the Clan of Roses.

              Tzimisce:
              There are primarily two types of Fiends Post-War: The raving monsters terrorizing the wastelands bringing very real boogeymen to the unfortunate survivors of the devastated United States with their various Packs that now constitute the Sabbat, and the older more collected Fiends who, after the war, calmly gathered what possessions they had and rebuild themselves into a new castle where they set down to isolate themselves from the world and continue whatever effort they were working on before the little bomb distraction.
              Few Clans can compete with a focused and rational Tzimisce in terms of rebuilding themselves, for the Fiends are very ethically flexible and can easily simply snatch away survivors from whatever camp they hole up in to create their servants, war-ghouls and childers from, not to mention material with which they can rebuild their havens with in any shape and form they desire. With the Camarilla largely gone as well, few Fiends need to dread the possibility of being targeted by their enemies, and even the rare few can usually be bargained with or made examples of.
              Though the Sabbat is dissolved, many of the older Fiends still maintain their bargains and, in some cases, alliances with the Keepers; making their union a strong one in the wastelands so long as their interests do not conflict. Moreover, Vampires are very patient and long-term creatures, and some Fiends have seen fit to begin their first step in the Post-War world towards regathering their old allies in the Gangrel and Nosferatu to once again begin their campaign against the last remaining Tremere Chantries that are left.

              The rest? Well those who aren't busy moving around and terrorizing the wastelands are generally up to whatever goal they used to have before the War, give or take. A few of the Fiends even strike out for power in the Post-War Americas, setting up their own Princedoms, and they are good at keeping order. Moreover, the Kolduns of the Fiends have more potential now than ever to gather their forces as their brand of Blood Magic is both unique and very useful for people around, giving the Fiends a solid card to play with gathering servants.

              Ventrue:
              Out of all the Clans, save perhaps the Lasombra, none suffered as highly from the falling bombs as the Clan of Kings did. Fancying their high towers and being so heavily invested in the status quoes and what influences they'd built over the centuries as they were, few were willing to leave it all behind over the unlikely chance that maybe the Chinese would go the extra mile and bomb the US at the cost of their own country. The numbers simply spoke in high favor of the Ventrue. Unfortunately, a low chance is not a zero chance and the bombs very much fell. During the day. When the bombs hit and the irradiated fire spread accross every facet of the cities, not even Fortitude could save the majority of the Ventrue who had stubbornly avoided seeking refuge away from their towers and even if the fire failed to burn them out, the sun certainly finished the job.
              Some of the Blue-Bloods did, perhaps fortunately, consider the possibility of bombs falling likely enough a prospect- or too risky to leave to chance -and used their money and influence to either seek refuge beneath the ground by curtesy of the Nosferatu or got themselves admitted into the Vault programs. Obviously, these individuals became the majority of the Clan who managed to survive the fall of the bombs and walk into the Post-War wastelands of the United States.

              These indiviuals among Clan Ventrue had just as much potential for growth here as they had disadvantages to overcome in a world where the statue quoe had fallen and all past accomplishments wiped clean. Money nolonger held sway, society was nolonger there to be manipulated and although the mortals suffered just as much as the Kindred did in terms of percentage, the Vampires were also far fewer in numbers than the Humans were, and now the Vampiric population is lower than it had ever been at any point of recorded history.
              The worst obstacle for the remaining Ventrue to overcome was their need to feed, for out of all the Vampires the Ventrue suffered the most on this front due to their limited tastes. A Ventrue who could not endure the radiation lingering amongst the surviving non-Vault populace (a populace that became lower by the day as they got into contact with the outside world) would've been better off killed in the final days of the War.
              The ones who could overcome it however have among the greatest potential for rebuilding their lost influences stronger than ever before. Although the Ventrue were the biggest bulwark the Camarilla had left, they too realized the Pre-War Camarilla as they knew it could not endure in a world were all slates were blank, society had collapsed and the Old World was ended. Accordingly they adapted and became much akin to the Dark Ages Princes of old where individual Princes were responsible for upholding order and enforce his own claim on any territory spoken for. And indeed, with both the Disciplines of Dominate and Presence the Ventrue has more potential than anyone to gather the remaining elements of the surviving population, Kine and Kindred alike, around them and reestablish new bastions of Camarilla survival. Or just personal power, depending on what each Ventrue values the most.

              The Sects
              The Camarilla:
              The end of the Old World, as marked by the dropping of bombs that devastated the land, meant the end of the Camarilla as a powerful Sect that had nation-wide control and influence over the United States of America. When the bombs fell it didn't matter whether you were Kindred or Kine, young or old, those who failed to seek adequite shelther perished.
              As a result of this devastating blow to their membership and Elders who had maintained control, the Camarilla finds itself nolonger able to enforce its rules and traditions across the US and in many cases not even locally. While many Vampires formerly belonging to the Camarilla endured and set up their own Princedoms, the adherence to its old ways is strictly dependent on the individual Prince and their peers. The Princes of the Post-War United States are closer in meaning to what it meant to be a Prince in the Dark Ages prior to the Camarilla's formation: namely a full-fledged ruler who kept power through strength and force of will rather than formal declaration of magistratedom by their Elders. A Prince then, and now, is the local Kindred of an area who can both claim a land and hold onto it both against competitors and their own peers.

              The Sabbat:
              Though undoubtedly eager to see the Camarilla in such fractured state whose only surviving aspects float on the rafts of the traditional Kindred who once held membership in it, the truth is the Sabbat are no better off in and of itself. Though it very much still exist as an idea, it's closer to a gang of raiders today than a full-fledged Sect with its own rules and influences. Essentially anyone who claims to be a member of the Sabbat in effect is one whether or not they are Kindred and live up to the old standards the Sabbat held.
              The older membership, the ones who didn't burn anyway, have largely abandoned the younger survivors who now roam around trying to rule the wastelands, subdue the mortal population and strike against anything calling itself Camarilla anymore and even the Packs that still remain have little or no contact between each other, running fully independently for as long as their candle can burn before expiring whether from making too many enemies, being targeted by sufficiently prepared people looking to end their terror or if they simply grow up and begin reaching for more in life than running from place to place and yell at people with a fanged maw until they run away in tears.
              All in all, the Sabbat is a shared but fractured identity that nolonger is worthy to be called "The Sword of Caine."

              The Anarchs:
              With the Camarilla and Sabbat, along with society at large, both gone, much of what united the Anarchs in their quest to topple the powers that be is nolonger present. Not all Anarchs set out on their own looking for whatever entertainment of the week, but whether or not they stayed with their peers there is little sense of purpose left among them. They won- sort of -and now the people are free to do as they please. Now what?
              Some stick around their holes and indulge the company of their peers, others move nomadically from place to place either looking for oppression or to indulge in raiding depending on their tastes. Some even try to set up their own societies based on whatever values they advocate, usually with very mixed results (and progress) from their peers depending on how they go about it. Sufficed to say there is nolonger an over-arching idea unifying the Anarchs due to the lack of an enemy to point to and most are completely reliant on their kinship with their peers or a common foe in the local area to stay united and prosper together. That said though, the Anarchs have the greatest population of Post-War Kindred amongst their ranks than either the Camarilla or the Sabbat, simply because they indulge embraces more than either (though not by a lot in the case of the Sabbat) and because they tend to draw less heat to themselves than the Sabbat shovelheads do on average.
