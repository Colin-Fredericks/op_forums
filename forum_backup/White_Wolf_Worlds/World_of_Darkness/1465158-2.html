                                                    Originally posted by Karthak View Post
                                                    The Technocracy's ideals also include mass murder. The fifth Precept of Damian is a call to genocide, as is the Declaration of the White Tower, the founding document of the Order of Reason.
                                                    Yes with a caveat and a lot of context.

                                                    The caveat being that most of the terms used on both the Declaration and the Precept are open to interpretation. It doesn't means they didn't meant it when they were first defined, but it does mean that individual members and groups in the Union may have less murderous interpretations and even the Union as a whole may change its instance on them, so they may not have such a strict interpretation today unless the ST wants to.

                                                    After the first batch of Convention books, material on the Technocracy have a tendency to hint that such genocidal readings aren't universal and may not even be standard anymore.

                                                    Context being that the Union know of supernatural beings from their own perspective, and due to everyone's tendency to hold to their secrets, they have no reason to change such perspectives. They are hardly the only group, even if you count on Traditionalists, that see no reason to accept the continued existence of shapeshifting monstrosities that come out of nowhere and murder humans indiscriminately and without reason, for example.

                                                    Many Traditions and Crafts gave them no more reason as a group to think otherwise. What is your proposal on how to deal with a Taftani that fully knows the risks of Paradox to the people around them and yet don't give a shit? Again, just an example. Mage's history is full of moments when those supernatural willworkers were locked on wars of all kinds and just threw their magics around with apparently little regard for others' safety. And that was precisely the kind of conflicts they were in when those rules got made.

                                                    Does any of that means the Union is just right? Of course not. It does mean, though, that the situation is too nuanced to be fair to just say they're monsters, either, and ignore all the other things happening and the other ways they may behave.

                                                            Personally, I'm not terribly fond of the Nephandic infiltration narrative, as it can be seen as absolving the Union of its own failures: “we're not to blame; it's the Fallen who took over Control who have made such a mess of things.” (though to be fair, the Nephandi wouldn't be able to do half the damage that they do if their victims weren't so corruptible.)

                                                            I am, however, fond of the idea that the Technocracy has a problem with Denial, brought on by their reliance on Conditioning. This can range from the Rose-colored Mirrorshades that Guide to the Technocracy mentioned to the occasional cases of “magic isn't real!” — with the latter occasionally going into full-blown Negation Men territory. And all too many of the Technocrats who aren't in Denial don't see a problem with this — at least, not one severe enough to do anything about. Heck, there are Technocrats who know about the Negation Men in their ranks and view them as a sort of asset, a weapon that can be used against the Superstitionists.

                                                            That's not so much a matter of the Technocracy being “dark” (as in, corrupt) as it is about them being… not entirely in their right minds.
