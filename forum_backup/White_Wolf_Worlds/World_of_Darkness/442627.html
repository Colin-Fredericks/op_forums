      When I heard about Changeling, I was playing my very first tabletop rpg, at summer of 2008. I've graduated school, played Sabbat Assamite, talked with folks six or more years older than me, and enjoyed it very much. Such words, as 'Faerie' or 'Glamour' didn't made me flinch at the time. Until 2009-2010, when I created that Malkavian primogen of San-Francisco, who was deluded, that he's Oberon. He was half-shakespearean type, half-fishmalk type, not my best character, but fun, though. Then, I've become acquainted with fan translation of second ed. Changeling core book (thank you, A., wherever you are now)!

      Since then I had only two Changeling games (and two, as ST, so four in summary). That's how easy to find die hard fan of Changeling here. First campaign was set in San-Francisco and were loosely based on Toybox adventure (without any of NPC's stats!). It was a story about pair of sidhe court wizard's apprentices. My character was named Lysander aep Eiluned - orphan of war, who was raised by his teacher, baron Falstaff aep Eiluned (court wizard, himself) in his own wizard's tower, which was hidden inside of pipe of very old steel plant. Second apprentice was glome, named Rowta/Rauta (I don't know, how to spell it), who fled ranks of Glome military to become alien in court of changelings. There was also chimeric butterfly of mindshattering beauty and undying love for puzzles and riddles - Adonis, and I've played it too, for the most part.

      It was quite a stretch to allow my character to have Metamorphosis Art at 5. Let's face it - some Arts was severely underpowered, and some others was overpovered like hell and Metamorphosis was latter. And, besides, it was sooo wizard'ey, that I practically begged ST to allow it. This was his first face to face table top game, so he agreed for his sorrow. And this addition changed a lot about the story. We haven't killed any of chimeric creatures from the trunk. Under protection of mighty Rauta, Lysander turned dragon into giant sheep, to put it into taxi's baggage compartment, turned bear into cute hamster-like mini bear, and some unfriendly chinese dragons - into turtles, three meter sized ones. Among other things, Rauta met and inspired urban fantasy author, struggling against writer's block, Lyzander fought really tough battle with agents of banality (two lecturers from [Church of Habologists], if you know, what I mean) at esoteriс center "Chorus Eye", where he taught "History of mythology and folklore". Then, we overdid containment measures for captive red cap - actually, there isn't much you can do to stop cantrip casting, if captive has appropriate Arts and Realms: bunk could be anything, we mused, so... some duct tape was used. Poor Slique...

      And after that was immortal scene of three dozens Shadow Court's changelings attacking count Aeon's freehold. With us in it. "Big mistake." Storyteller said, he didn't expected from us to side with count, when we learned about heartbraking betrayal of our teacher. He didn't expected from us to save count at all. And also, he didn't expect me to burn 2 dots of permanent Glamour to turn into mythical creature ("Dragon Enters", you shadow scum!), effectively destroying half of Aeon's palace, and physically killing four changelings - at least one of those wasn't accidental and quite brutal. That's how we play Paladin's Legacy! That game was changed a lot about what I expect from good roleplaying game overall and really awesome game mastering in particular.

      There was also another game, but this is another story...

      Thanks for reading!

                      It is hard to know where to begin. Our work-in-progress timeline (from May 2002 to October 2014, so far) is over 50 pages long in 10 font. We have played many different Changeling games, but all in the same world. Interestingly, in every game we have played (except the current one I'm running) an evil redcap super genius named Black Beret has appeared. He has been captured, frozen for years, but he always finds a way to escape and renew his unending campaign of evil. One of the characters in our games - a troll named Ulf - has become High King, but way back in 2002 he took an oath never to harm Black Beret. This oath - made in an early game - has vexed High King Ulf for over a decade. If any my players are reading this, rest assured Black Beret shall return!

                          Back in the long-ago, 1st Ed. Changeling came out. One of my long term players desperately wanted to play it as a respite from my grim Vampire games and grim-ish, intense Mage games. I obliged her.

                          We set the game in the local area, using local landmarks, businesses and the occasional person (my MA advisor was cast as a satyr... he had a certain fondness for his female graduate students that had resulted in a series of marriages). The game began in media res, with all the players having apparently gone through Chrysalis at the same time in the same place. Unfortunately for them, that place was a museum, there was a prominent display case that had obviously been broken into and the alarm was going off...

                          We managed to play a game with a lot of flashback scenes. As the characters discovered the extent of their amnesia, we ran scenes from their mortal lives that led up to that moment in the museum where someone opened a box and they all became changelings.

                          I definitely ran the game more from being inspired by the Grimm's version of the Grimm's Fairy Tales, so.... not so "fun" really. The requestor played a sidhe and she ended up pledging herself to the Baroness of the local court. They discovered that she was "under the spell" of a vampire (they just knew him as "the Dark Man") and that the way to disenchant her was to put someone's Glamour into the magic box that had gone missing from the museum back in Act I, Scene 1. The teenage crow pooka voluntarily made himself "undone", contributing ALL his Glamour into the box (and forgetting everything about the others and the changeling world). The sidhe then had to trick the Baroness into opening the box. She was successful. The Baroness opened the box and was disenchanted, but tricking her liege definitely violated her oath of fealty. So she went blind.

                          After the game was over, she said "That wasn't fun." I asked if she meant "funny" as in light-hearted and amusing. She said, "Yes." I asked if she thought it was a good story and if SHE had fun. She admitted that she did. And from that point forward it was well established that I just don't seem to be able to do "funny" for the sake of funny.

                          But it was a good game. It ended up a part of the backstory of other, later games we played. (We're currently playing a Vampire game, based on a Mage game, which has that Changeling game as one of it's backdrops.)

                                  And lo, a wild mistypaw appears. This seems a good a thread as any to say "Hi, I just found this and made an account!" ::waves:: It's been a long strange trip since I last played, which has been longer than I'd like. But it is what it is.
                                  Stories... well, you can thank me for the blurb in Kithbook: Pooka about pig latin. Back in the day people gave me trouble with whether that was a valid expression of the frailty or not. So I emailed to Jackie and Nicky and was amazed to actually be answered by the fine ladies.

                                  My first character, and probably best known one was Mistypaw, whose name I still use online. She was a pig latin speaking cat pooka who had a quirk that when she chrysalised and remembered her fae self, she forgot her mundane self. So she was a bit flighty and wandered around in and out of various stories and lovers. As I was young and a new gamer, she was comparatively a pretty simple character. But in my headcannon, she eventually stayed with her troll girlfriend Einmyria, whose plight and devotion inspired and impressed her. Misty made Ein feel lighthearted, happy, and free... except she had already sworn betrothal to another troll and he would not release her. The fallout hurt her quite a bit and it was odd, but Misty had to be the one to take care of someone else for once. Someone who paid a dear price to be true to her feelings for her. Though she had fleed previous engagements herself, Ein needed her and made her feel like more than just a stray.

                                  My other main character was Melitha, a sluagh childling who had been in lives past a rather dark character and she wonders if it was nature or circumstance. If this life might turn out better. Remembering so much of the past, she's rather mature in her head and has instincts and desires that are not age appropriate. Running from those who knew her in the past to give herself a chance to see if things could change, she travelled the childling underground railroad and wound up in St. Augustine, FL. That town might well be the home in her heart, but she had to go to a boarding school for childlings... for protection, they said. She used her skills to play the role of a vengance spirit in the school to manipulate and gather information from the students, and used that to get professors to teach her rarer arts not on the curriculum. And she dated a Fir-bholg that integrated himself into the school. Things were interesting, but the game went on hiatus and never finished.

                                  I wonder if someone started a game today, if I would ever find her an ending... or who I might make up next?

                                          Mistypaw! It's good to see you posting again! (says the forumite formerly known as Julius_Patel).

                                          Let's see, my Changeling: The Dreaming origin story. At about the time that Vampire: The Masquerade revised came out, a friend of a friend invited me to play in their World of Darkness game. I had an absolute blast! So much so, I got a copy of Vampire and started up a World of Darkness game for some close friends. Everyone started as mortals with the possibility of becoming some sort of supernatural through play. All the players listed their top three supernaturals they wanted their character to become. One of the players listed her top choice as Changeling. At the time, I had never heard of the game, but she was super curious about. I picked up the core book to see what it was about. I couldn't put it down. The themes, the writing, the world, the kiths; I was in love! I created a pooka NPC to be her character's faerie mentor - Julia Patel. I loved playing the NPC, and she was a huge hit with the players. Later, when the player offered to run a solely Changeling game, I really wanted reprise the NPC, but I didn't feel comfortable playing an opposite gender PC at the time. One of the other players suggested playing her identical twin brother, Julius. So I did.

                                          The Patel Twins were born.

                                          Rough and as new as we all were, those still rank as some of my all time favorite chronicles.

                                                          The only Changeling I've had the privilege to play was a moth-winged Sluaugh childling runaway. A well-meaning (but ultimately enabling) social worker kept him supplied with the anti-depressant drug that she was convinced was essential to his mental well-being (and to which he was addicted), even as he refused to stay in any foster home in which he was placed. When he underwent Chrysalis, his Irish-red hair started coming in dark at the roots; about an inch or two was visible in his mortal Seeming. His Faerie Mein had only the very tips of his hair still anything but black, but those tips almost burned with a coppery radiance in any ambient light.

                                                          As with all his kith, he was quiet and curious. He tended to find his way into all sorts of places he may not have been wanted, and when a local Sidhe lord asked for volunteers for a quest which could range all over the land, he signed up. It's not like he had anything tying him down, and maybe travelling with older Fae would let him get away from all the authority figures that seemed to think kids should be in the banality-laden child services system.
