      - Technocrazy not so united. Confrotantions between agents from different powers, for example West vs Comunism in the Cold War, or French vs British empires in the XIX centurry (and everybody againts Spanish empire in the explorers age).

(Disclaimer: in the real life Church doesn't allow any magic because supernatural phenomenons are by only two sources, or God or Devil, if you try "good magic" really are you opening your door to infernal forces.       - Multiverse, uchrony or parallel worlds, for example a post-apocalipse land ruled by Bone Gnawer garous, or a wild Pangea-(James Cameron's) Avatar where feras fight againt alien invaders.

      - A not real world setting, but a mixture of Ravenloft, Kult: lost divinty, Exalted and Scarrend Lands (and nations from 7th Sea?). This could be a out-of-canon sourcebook.

          I always modify my WoD from canon. The biggest change is that I don't acknowledge or follow the metaplot. My games can best be summed up as "Revised Edition never happened"

          ​Also, the fan-game Senshi: The Merchandising is canon along with Street Fighter: The Storytelling Game as well. Meanwhile, Kindred of the East and Demon: The Fallen are explicitly non-canon and I'm considering giving Wraith the axe as well, but I'm unsure.

          ​So, basically take early 1e and 2e WoD, move it from 1991 to 2016, remove Revised in its entirety and add in Sailor Moon and Street Fighter. There you go, you have a WoD game the way I would run it.

          It's not conventional, but I like it.

              Replacing the Camarilla with the Maquerade Traditions, a group of multiple ideologies united by a shared set of principles, rules and a need to hide from humanity. Rather than one homogeneous block the 'Camarilla' is now made up of:

              - The Hermetic Order (The Tremere's power center, one of the strictest regions)
              - The Mithraic Tradition (The British Isles and N. France as controlled by a recently returned Mithras.)
              - The Camarilla (Vanilla Ventrue)
              - The Caliphates (Assamite and some other bloodlines and new factions)
              - The Cult of Kali (Hindi vampires)
              - The Lancea Sanctum (Lasombra Antitribu and Catholic Cainites)

              As well as a few smaller ones. This allows for easier decentralisation of immediate power without weakening the sect and makes the Masquerade somewhat more sustainable IMHO. This also allows for the Camarilla to obfuscate Noddism by throwing the door open to a dozen or more faiths and requiring that their members show them respect. It also allows them to parlay with foreign nations without it stinking of some huge, unsustainable, inexplicable empire of German Agnostics.

              Oh, and removing hunters with magical powers (save True Faith). It just never sat well with my groups that these guys definitively had their own set of powers and rather threw the power balance out of the window.

                                                          I only use the original five game lines. I generally accept the corebooks as is, but everything else in supplements might be included, removed, or altered as I like. I ignore metaplot completely so the game is essentially second edition status quo.

                                                          I really have only ever played Vampire and Werewolf, so my changes are basically to those lines. If I were to summarize the major changes, they'd be:

                                                          Vampire:
                                                          • I downplay all the Independent Clans. None are anywhere close to power or numbers of any of the Camarilla Seven although the Setites are the largest. The Giovanni and Assamites are particularly few in number.
                                                          • Many Followers of Set are in the Camarilla or at least claim membership of it, and therefore accept the authority of the Inner Circle and Justicars.
                                                          • I ignore bloodlines entirely unless I am running a plot where one is prominent. Some bloodlines aren't related to the existing vampire genealogies at all, but have their own, modern origin due to curses or other bad juju. This is suspected by the dominant Cainite line which explains why they are persecuted.
                                                          • Camarilla is what is was described in the original books - a worldwide conspiracy of "all" vampires. Vampires don't have different sects based on modern version of multiculturalism. Elders don't care about that, and the idea that the Camarilla are "European vampires" is ridiculous.
                                                          • If the subject is ever brought up, the Elders believe that the rising of Cappadocious from torpor was a historical event and was the origin of the Black Death. This explains why the Camarilla was OK with Cappadocious's destruction by the Giovanni. They believe more pandemics would have happened if he hadn't been destroyed. They don't talk about this much as it contradicts their "antedeluvians are all dead" propaganda. Likewise the Sabbat takes this as more proof they have to destroy the antedeluvians.
                                                          • The Tremere have many enemies outside of the vampires, and this occupies much of their energy. House Tytalus is still trying to destroy them and is major antagonist.

                                                          Werewolf:
                                                          • I reduce the number of werewolves out there considerably from canon. I've come to the conclusion they work best in very low populations. Most cities even lack Glass Walkers and Bone Gnawers.
                                                          • I use a more Lovecraftian interpretation of the Wyrm as I am often unimpressed with canonical fomori types, Wyrm cults, and PENTEX.
                                                          • "PENTEX" is basically the aliens from They Live trying to anti-terraform the world for their alien masters. Any other elements of canonical PENTEX I like are simply separate corporate conspiracies.
                                                          • I do my best to make werewolves more like they are depicted in traditional media (movies, folklore) rather than the fantasy D&D with ecoterrorism I think canon and many fans treat them as
                                                          • I try to keep the dominant politics in the Garou Nation as being sept based (although since many septs are mono-tribal or with set number of few tribes, tribal politics often works through the septs).
                                                          • I try to make the spirit totems more like they are portrayed in myth and legend (see some of my "heretical thoughts" posts on the werewolf forum)
