    Originally posted by adambeyoncelowe View Post

    I take the argument for alternate powers in general (as with Arcanoi, for example), but the V5 implementation was awful. If they fix that, most of my complaints evaporate. But they can't try to jam everything into a small set of Disciplines and shouldn't publish a slew of new powers with every book. Well curated lists of themed power sets is fine.

    One advantage of fixed powers, however, is simplicity—you don't need to agonise over your picks and plan your builds. If you have Devotions, you can add an optional rule to let players take a Devotion in place of the main power (provided it's a single-power Devotion or you meet the prerequisites), but you can't really do the opposite without huge sidebars to explain it.

    Rather than lots of narrow, specific powers, however, you could also just focus on broader, more flexible powers. E.g., Protean 2 might let you change a body part such as a head, a single limb, a pair of hands or feet, etc. Wanna grow claws? Done. Wanna grow bug eyes? Ditto. Wanna make your head look like someone else's? That's covered now.

    Or—and this might be sacrilege—we could just remove the linear nature and let people buy whatever powers they want in any order, perhaps with a few prerequisites if it absolutely makes sense. It'll require a bit of reworking, but it's shown to be feasible with Loresheets. Don't want Protean 3? Fine. Skip it.
    As I said, VtR2nd is the best approach to power to date, so imagine something in those lines: you have the structure from V5 (which already has more Disciplines than VtR2nd), but VtR2nd powers both as options and the standard to design against. And totally keeping versatility.

    But with V5's take is easier to do what they should have done from start: port Legacy Disciplines. Several of them are mixed bags from the start, precisely because they had to fit the 5 dots ladder. With V5's take you can adjust powers to any level they make sense, remove or add effects that fit better, and ditch things that were never that great to begin with.

    Serpentis? Not all iconic powers are snakey Protean. Quietus? You can mix its powers with something else to finally get what you want without 2/3 redundancies in the middle. Mytherceria, Visceratika, a lot of Disciplines could be improved doing exactly the "jam everything here" thing. Of course, after getting rid of the stupid 1-dot-1-power rule and every power reworked into VtR2nd design.

    Simplicity is attainable with suggested kits that show how a certain group buys that Discipline and why. Whatever the requisites and whatnot, the group comes with at least one kit with all of them digested for you and explaining their synergy.

                        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

                        Maybe, but that's besides the point. What's relevant is that we expand what you can do for a Bloodline without the need to stay with a 5-dot structure.
                        If that's the aim, then broader Disciplines would mostly avoid the need for this and are less messy. I think the glut of choice in V5 is mostly an illusion, since it comes from making powers more granular, not less. More choice is not a suitable alternative to better options in the first place. My main problem with V5 Disciplines is that the increased choice just disguises the weaker Discipline design ethos overall, and I'm not convinced that they'll rectify this if they focus on giving people lots of powers over giving people more useful powers.

                        You don't need alternate powers if Disciplines become more Sphere-like (but obviously with more limitations). Instead of alternate powers, you could just present a selection of example effects. Or you can have something like Unleashing at greater risk to the Beast.

                        Alternatively, maybe everyone gets broad powers but clans and bloodlines also get a rider effect that interacts with those broader powers? E.g., Hecata can use Auspex and Dominate on ghosts (you still need Necromancy to summon them), and Ahrimanes can use Animalism and Auspex on animist spirits.

                        Or you could go the "regalia"/"modes" route, as mentioned, which is a more specific rider for each power. Modes essentially add to/build on powers rather than being complete alternates, but like in ExEss, they sometimes might appear a little differently to better capture a clan theme. Buying or creating additional modes should be fairly cheap.

                        E.g., Setites get to use sacred animal forms for Protean (this could be snakes for Damballans, lions if you follow Sukhmet, etc.), while Gangrel base theirs on local animal species. Sacrifice or hunt an animal to gain a new form, respectively.

                        Let's say Unmarked Grave becomes the core "Earth Meld" power, letting you merge with any material for a variable cost. But Gangrel can choose to take Earth Meld instead, making it free to use on bare earth and maybe also letting them heal an extra level of aggravated damage (or Willpower damage?) per night without a Willpower point.

                        Gargoyles can choose to take Bond with the Mountain instead, which also lets them choose to turn into a statue while in contact with stone, in which form they're immune to sunlight. Tzimisce get One with the Land, allowing them to use Auspex, Animalism, etc., throughout their domain so long as they're melded with it.

                        These would require extra room, like additional powers, but things are much easier to navigate and there's less decision paralysis. You don't need to juggle prerequisites and you can retain a clan's "niche protection". The layout would be something like:

                        Protean 1: Unmarked Grave
                        [Core power description]
                        Example modes:
                        • [Bond with the Mountain]
                        • [Earth Meld]
                        • [One with the Land]
                        In most cases, you can limit the example modes to the clans who treat those Disciplines as in-Clan. Modes are always optional, meaning you can choose to take the core power or your clan's mode.

                        My preference is obviously for this last route, which preserves meaningful choice while retaining visual simplicity and reducing bloat.

                        I think you're greatly overthinking what such a kit looks like. It would be simple to integrate into a group's description. "Usually this clan selects the following powers for Clan Disciplines: ... This reinforces the theme of ..."

                        A section of common builds is already common and, in my opinion, overused for small details. Take some of its space for a kit and done.
                        I think the clan write-ups were better when they were double-page spreads, but I'm not averse to this replacing the paragraphs on how each clan uses its Disciplines that appear in V5. But the longer, less efficient clan write-ups of V5 become harder to justify if nu-WW want a tighter CRB that covers more ground (i.e., all 13 clans). It's essentially 13 extra paragraphs of material to include.

                        That said, you lose the immediate benefit of giving players a simple list to choose from at chargen. Doing it the other way around means you don't have to first choose to reduce choices, if that makes sense? IME, people often prefer fewer choices (e.g., choosing between streamers is easier than choosing between hundreds of cable channels) so long as each of those options is more useful for them. Lots of bad options (modern Netflix, APPLE TV, Amazon Prime, etc.) is still worse than one good one (old Netflix, when it was a one-stop shop).

                        This doesn't impede other takes on their unique traits, just add options. I'm struggling to see how this structure would be a problem in itself, and making a rule to let Devotions being taken in substitution to a "main" power would require way more words to achieve the exact same goal.
                        You only need something like this:

                        At the Storyteller's discretion, a player may choose a Devotion in place of a core power where it better fits her character concept. She must still meet any prerequisites when she does so, but the Devotion itself counts as the level of the relevant Discipline it replaces for this purpose.

                        That one paragraph gives you carte blanche to have DIY Discipline lists without confronting people with a huge list of options to navigate first. They can choose to take the core powers and never look at the Devotions, if they don't want to.

                        Ultimately, this may be something we just aren't going to agree on, and maybe I'm in the minority here. But IME, busy players just want things to be straightforward and accessible. V5 chargen was a hot mess compared to V20, and Disciplines were an aggravating factor that also caused problems in play as well. The edition just seemed a model of excess and chaotic design, with everything thrown at the wall to see what sticks. I'd love something far more refined.

                                First, let's recognize everyone is speculating a lot on very little.

                                Second... I think there's something missing in all this talk about how to approach Disciplines: it still needs to be VtM in the end.

                                While I agree that the core idea of V5's Discipline structure could be executed well and doesn't inherently lead to the problems that arose... I think not repeating those problems runs into the fact of wanting to have something recognizable as VtM in the end. V5's physical Disciplines suffer a lot simply because "one Physical Attribute to supernatural extremes" doesn't parse out well to a set of optional powers to pick and chose a "style" from. If you weren't tying yourself to VtM conceits, you could make it much better by merging all three into one kit of vampiric physicality; but then you reduce three iconic VtM powers to one and diminish the uniqueness of the Clans significantly without importing something else. We saw how this hampered V5's Blood Sorcery by trying to merge Banu Haqium having a sorcery sub-splat and Quietus with Tremere Thaumaturgy, or how merging Necromancy and Obtenebration into Oblivion really didn't work for all the concepts involved.

                                It's possible for a design to have abstract potential that just doesn't fit in the 6th edition of a game. V5's general power structure is functional. but it's held back by needing to capture VtM... not just by biases against V5.
