    Family and Magick, Risk and Personal Horror

    10-02-2021, 04:43 AM
    I previously did a post, before Technocracy Reloaded dropped, about the possibility of Elite Boarding Schools for Technomancers' Neglected Kids: http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...neglected-kids

    This is mainly focused on the Traditions who I imagine as having a closer connection to their sleeper families than career driven Technomancers in a high demand bureaucracy. Some basic questions:

    - What is marriage on a metaphysical and spiritual level?

    - Is there Magick that you can do if you are married that you couldn't do if you weren't?

    - Would Mages always prefer to have the companionship of another Mage who understands Magick over a sleeper who doesn't?

    - Do children raised by Mages have an increased chance to Awaken?

    I have given some thought to the possibility of Tradition Mages with sleeper families using their close connection to them as a conduit for Magick intended to influence the local community and larger society. You would obviously want to strengthen and empower family members before using them as conduits. In game terms, they might be considered Acolytes or Assistants who could aide in High Ritual. They might also act as messengers, communicating the effects of Mind Magick by proxy, with perhaps a little Correspondence or Life. They may be unknowing participants.

    There are two firm stances that someone could take on the choice of whether or not to involve family members in casting Magick and spreading one's influence:

    A) Using family to facilitate Magick in the context of the Ascension War is wrong, because it endangers them by making them potential targets.

    B) The mere fact that you are a Tradition Mage makes your family members targets, regardless of the choice. By involving them, you can empower them with Magick and help them prepare for what they might face. If they are unaware of their role in the diffusion of Magick, the Magick is already subtle enough to avoid enemy detection.

    Things stay interesting if we assume that some PCs and NPCs will chose B:

    - What are some effects that you would cast on or using members of your family?

    - What kind of businesses could a Mage's family get involved in that best suit the goals of the Mage or their Tradition?

    - What is the ideal way for this to turn out?

    - What is the worst that could happen to a family member as a result of their involvement, knowing or unknowing? - Curses, death, abduction, imprisonment? - A miscast spell that was intended to be beneficial?

    - And, of course, the personal horror aspect - How is the Mage impacted by harm to their family as a result of their choice?

                  Be attentive that I didn't said a Mage wouldn't marry a Sleeper. I said they would marry a Sleeper in the know. I don't think at all that most Mages marry among themselves.

                  The thing is how marriages work. Relationships in general work on some sort of common ground. You don't just know the person, but you two have some common context and experiences. Things you talk about, things you have either a common understanding or a clear disagreement, there are things that affect both of you. On any relationship there are common elements between your lives. And the less such elements a relationship have, the more you grow apart.

                  Familial relationships have a lot of those naturally, as the family itself is unavoidable common ground for many of its members, but there is an exception: the couple. Their common ground isn't innate, they can grow apart. They can part ways or have a strained relationship, or even become so distant from each other that this can barely be called a marriage anymore.

                  What it means to be married to someone you have nothing in common, you don't even understand their life and possibly know next to nothing about?

                  If the Mage makes their spouse into an Acolyte, then no problem. It is even possible that many Mages would prefer this to a relationship to another Mage even if this Acolyte isn't actually on to their Paradigm, but at least understand the world the Mage lives in. If the Mage spends more time with mundane activities and work, so the bulk of their lives is mundane and can be shared with their spouse, also no problem, but this Mage is distancing from Mage society.

                  A dedicated Mage, though, that marries to a common Sleeper, will have a spouse with whom they can't talk about anything of a big chunk of their life. To whom they can't show their greatest achievements. With whom they can't commemorate important victories by what they are. They can build pretenses and false explanations, but what does this lack of fellowship does for their feelings towards that person?

                  Marriage is a big thing. It is partnership and compromise. People coming together to share their lives and destinies. It may be out of love or any other reason, but they are still in that together like it or not. When you're not in that together anymore, can you call this a marriage?

                  Just to be clear: a character keeps secrets but still spends a lot of time with their family still have this. The trope of the spouse that is secretly whatever else holds because this character is still present most of the time and emotionally invested in their family. OR precisely getting stranded.

                      Something else to consider: Mages are willful. It is inherent in what they do. On average, sleepers aren't quite so willful. There are exceptions, of course; but as a rule, Mages will be willful while sleepers won't be.One effect of this is that an ideological class between two Mages is going to be a much bigger deal than an ideological class between a Mage and a Sleeper. So the success of a marriage between two Mages is far more likely to be dependent on the compatibility of their Foci then a marriage between a Mage and a Sleeper.

                      But I would hesitate to generalize about who Mages are likely to marry. Marriage is an intensely personal thing, and will probably depend on factors other than stuff about Magick. I suspect that Mages are likely to choose marriage partners in much the same way that everyone else does.

                                                  Originally posted by Aleph View Post
                                                  Also, a spell that could be benefitial has the potential to be problematic if the mage didn't read the situation well enough or wasn't careful. Like, maybe you place so many Wards against everything arround their house that it ends blipping a bit too hard in NWO magick radars, and thus you end placing your family in more danger then they needed to be.
                                                  This is something I thought about when it comes to using magick to protect a loved one. The very act of doing so makes them potentially a target for those with the ability to sense the interference. Whether it's Prime mages who can sense spells themselves, or any mage whose understanding of Spheres allows them to know when those Spheres have been twisted around someone. Anyone with Entropy could see if someone has been blessed with good luck.

                                                  Now, what that party does with this knowledge can vary. Any Sorcerer with dots in Fortune can make someone luckier. How often does any given mage investigate random people who might just have a little magical help or a good luck charm? But at the very least, if someone is walking around with enchantments on them, that might get someone's attention. Which could lead to all sorts of trouble.

                                                  Sometimes, the best thing a mage can do for their loved ones is to keep them indistinguishable from every other random person.

                                                  Not that mages are above falling to temptation. When you have the power to warp reality, it can be so easy to try to make life easier or safer for the ones you love, through the most direct means available.


                                                  Another thing is that using magick to interfere can gain the attention of anyone, even if they can't sense magic, if the effects of that interference affects someone else. If a person gets a promotion because their Awakened spouse nudged things in their favor, it might create resentment among those who got passed over for promotion. Granted, this could happen regardless of the mage's influence, as being envious or resentful of someone else's good fortune is hardly unique to the supernatural world. But if the professional rival thinks the spouse didn't "earn" their place - or even has reason to suspect the promotion was unusual - they might become suspicious and nosy.

                                                  Their involvement might even be a fluke, the rival reading into things rather than having real evidence (in essence, being right for the wrong reasons). What matters is that manipulating events as the mage did created unintended side effects. The mage is unlikely to stop interfering if they believe their spouse is under threat, even from a mundane enemy. Especially if the rival making a big deal about the matter draws in other parties who are even more dangerous. There are a lot of ways a seemingly small situation could spiral out of a control and become very large, when magick is involved.
