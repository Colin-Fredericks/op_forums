            All great suggestions! Mine are less appropriate, and more than a little obvious, but they have their uses.

            While a bit more Changeling: The Lost than Changeling: The Dreaming, I do have to point to Hellboy 2: The Golden Army. While a bit campy, in that distinctly Hellboy way, there are some segments (the tomb of the Golden Army, the Goblin Market, and the elemental creature) that make good inspirations for locations in the Near Dreaming and chimera, particularly in a modern setting. Plus, Prince Nuada kind of exemplifies what a Sidhe knight who goes Unseelie out of sheer bitterness and rage might look like.

            While also more The Lost (and, I'm half-convinced, probably a case of the tail wagging the dog to use it as an inspiration for a Changeling game at all), Lost Girl also has some value. Particularly, the way their Seelie and Unseelie equivalents interact, and the way those courts aren't purely good or evil in stereotypical ways, can come in handy for more social games. The character of Kenzi also makes a great example of an Enchanted mortal ally. Some of the monsters they fight can also make decent inspiration for Gallain, rogue chimera, or adhene. As a warning, the show is a bit adult, and some episodes can definitely be a bit monster-of-the-week, but it's a fun fae-focused romp if you don't take it too seriously.

                        We can also add Life Is Strange. A unique newer point-and-click adventure game, the entire game is a metaphor for growing up, albeit in a different way than Changeling. While the supernatural elements are a bit more subtle, the game's use of symbolism are pure Dreaming.                             Sucker Punch (trailer) looks like a textbook Changeling movie. I haven't had a chance to watch it (yet), but apparently its about a supposedly mad girl fighting across multiple fantasy worlds for a chance at freedom.

                            The original edition of Changeling cited Mythago Wood as a key inspiration. The dynamic isn't quite the same, but the story of a man chasing his dream girl (and evil brother) through a forest populated by beings out of myth and legend could easily be adapted to C:tD. And "Hellfire Harry" provides an excellent example of how new fae come about.

                            The mystery of Ryhope Wood, Britain's last fragment of primeval forest, consumed George Huxley's entire long life. Now, after his death, his sons have taken up his work. But what they discover is numinous and perilous beyond all expectation.

                            For the Wood, larger inside than out, is a labyrinth full of myths come to life, "mythagos" that can change you forever. A labyrinth where love and beauty haunt your dreams. . .and may drive you insane.
                            Oh, and Susan Cooper's "Dark is Rising" quintet works very, very well as Changeling story. Particularly after you get past the first book (Over Sea, Under Stone), and the series introduces Bran and Will. Its all Arthurian (while a Changeling campaign can be more . . . mixed), but still very, very good.

                            Will Stanton’s ordinary life is shattered with the dreadful revelation that the Dark—the source of all evil—is rising in its last and greatest bid to control the world. He finds that he is no ordinary boy, but the last-born of the Old Ones, immortals dedicated to keeping mankind free from the Dark. Soon Will is swept up in the great battle, along with his ageless master, Merriman; the three Drew children, who are mortal but have their own vital part to play; and a strange boy named Bran. These six fight fear and death in a quest through time and space interwoven with the most ancient myths of the islands of Britain—until, at last, Will and Bran find the weapon that will ultimately vanquish the Dark.
                            Or if you want something a bit more obscure, try the live action Witchblade (trailer) series. Its not hard to see the titular artifact as an Inanimae (a la a Selkie's coat), and the show blurs the lines between reality and fantasy, madness and sanity, in much the same way Changeling does. Take special note of the fight scenes - there are some really wild ideas that you can borrow for your game.

                                    The Fall, Ink, and the Imaginarium of Doctor Parnassus are all very much Changeling to me both Stylistically and in terms of Story.

                                    The Anime Puella Magi Madoka Magica while not properly the same thing in terms of story is very Much Changeling in terms of Visual Elements. The Abilities of the Magical Girls seem almost like Cantrips at times, they Live under a Dual Identity that begins to pull them apart from their Mortal Lives as time goes on, and The Witches...Oh The Witches. They remind me of Very Powerful and Dangerous Chimera as well as Changelings that have become Entirely Freehold Bound in the most terrifying cases of Bedlam, Their Labyrinths are very Surreal as well, taking an entirely different animation style from the main show creating very Unique and Disturbing Visuals that reflect the Inner Psyche of the Witch.

                                        In the vein of Millennium Actress I would suggest the predecessor, Perfect Blue, which shows a very Unseelie side of Japanese Stardom and often blurs the line between reality and fantasy. Her sister is very much a Dauntain who had her dreams shattered only to end up in Bedlam after (without spoiling much) QUICKLY shedding Banality.

                                        I'd also suggest the Tecmo's Deception video games, well, #2 (Kagero: Deception) and the interestingly named Nightmare Princess (technically a remix of the 6th game of the series). Ignore the demonic aspects and instead replace Hell/Darkworld with Arcadia and imagine the Princesses as Fae being forced to work for their Unseelie nature (I like to imagine them as Leanhaun). Without spoiling more (check out some let's plays, if you don't like the series it isn't worth buying) the Velguirie route is PERFECT for playing or portraying a Typhoid Dauntain dragging people into their infectious glamour.

                                        And, while not perfect, The Babadook is a lot, to me, like a Childling coming out of their Chrysalis and forcing their parent into the role of a Grump or reluctant Kinain.

                                        Perhaps, depending on your taste for camp and kitsch, check out RuPaul's Drag Race. The transformation from male to glamourous or shockingly female can be pretty amazing and like the mortal vs. the fae. Plus seeing how meek some of the men are as men compared to how together and animated they are in drag is pretty interesting.
