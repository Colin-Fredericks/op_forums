                          Well, the basic rules for each game line, once you get to the 20th anniversary editions are the same. Roll x dice against difficulty y for ability use and damage is similar. The combat capabilities of characters and how the special powers of each group interact are quite different.

                          Mages, although very powerful if they can use Spheres effectively and have a strong Arete and Avatar so that they can pull off substantial effects, are normal humans for purposes of damage soaking. Mages need armor or magick to soak lethal damage and rarely have anything that works against aggravated damage. Vampires can be effective at combat, but since a lot of their disciplines require blood points, a vampire isn't going to last long in a fight if they run low on blood. As for Werewolf, some of the werewolves aren't combat monster, but anything in Crinos is a lot more dangerous than the base form. Vampire and Werewolf powers are much more static than Mage magick, so it's a bit easier to see what fight is fair before it happens. A Mage, well that's only limited by their imagination.

                          Since each line also has its own lore, in general, the more you mix, the more difficult managing the World of Darkness will be. That being said, one of the best games I was ever in as a player was a Vampire/Mage game. However, everyone in that group was a hardcore gamer who committed to the game and our co-GMs spent hours planning each weekly session. If I were to have any crossover elements, I'd carefully consider which ones work best for the players in question.

                                  My experience is that the problems with baseline power levels are more due to the Storyteller not communicating a coherent theme to the players than to the rules. Its not really any worse than when one guy builds a Brujah with physical primary, five dots in brawl and their starting dots in celerity/potence while another one builds a Toreador with social primary (physical tertiary), no dots in any combat abilities at all and their starting dots in auspex/presence.

                                  Yeah, a combat focused Crinos can rip things up. So can a combat focused vampire with some dots in celerity, fortitude and/or potence (plus some ghouled retainers as backup) or an Akashic with Forces 2, Life 3, Mind 1.

                                  By contrast, a werewolf theurge with tertiary physical stats and couple dots in brawl might only have Str 5, Dex 4, Stam 5 and a dice pool of 6 for their attacks in Crinos and only 2 rage to take extra actions or heal with. It'd cost a lot of blood, but even a non-combat vampire could hit those stat levels for long enough to escape.

                                  The problem is when the guy with the Werewolf builds a Get of Fenris Ahroun with physical primary attributes and high Rage, the one with the Vampire builds a Toreador with Auspex, Presence and tertiary physical attributes (because they want primary social and secondary mental) and the Mage builds a Virtual Adept with Correspondence, Entropy and just enough Mind to project into the Digital Web. Not only are they wildly disparate in combat ability, their existences are so removed from each other that just putting them in the same room would be problematic.

                                  This is mostly a communication problem.

                                  If the Storyteller let the players know that the planned campaign was going to be dealing with say, dealing with the Unquiet Dead in the wake of the Sixth Great Maelstrom then my group would probably be looking at something like a Silent Strider Theurge, a Giovanni Necromancer and a Euthanatos (w. entropy 3 and spirit 3) so they'd all have an interest in the subject and a means of contributing as well. Similarly, if the Storyteller says they want to run a combat heavy war against the forces of darkness (say a Baali/Nephandi/Black Spiral Dancer team up) then that Ahroun having the company of that aforementioned Akashic and a Vampire sniper with Celerity and his five ex-military ghoul retainers would be a pretty kick-ass crew where everyone's got something to contribute too.

                                  The biggest hangup once everyone is on the same storytelling page though is just vampires absolute inability to operate during daylight hours. An ordinary human can be active 16-18 hours a day with ease and can get by on just a few hours sleep or even skip a night entirely in a crisis situation. Werewolves with their superior endurance and mages with life magic could go days without sleep if they had to and not have it impact their ability to function.

                                  By contrast, vampires all but shut down when the sun is up, needing 5 successes on a difficulty 8 humanity check to stay awake for a single scene (after making a difficulty 8 Perception+Auspex check to notice the need to wake up first)... less than that and they're up just 1-4 turns before they collapse into sleep again. During the summer months this means maybe 9 hours a day of activity in someplace like Chicago (and only about 15 hours there even in the dead of winter) and they have to spend part of that time hunting and feeding too.

                                  Even adopting a nocturnal lifestyle the humans/mages and werewolves are going to have 50-100% more time during a day to actually get stuff done (particularly since their hunt for sustenance involves only the half-dozen steps between the fridge and the microwave) while the vampires are laying around in a coma-like sleep. Their utter combustibility from even indirect sunlight is a trivial mechanical nuisance by comparison (though it is excessive as well... a vampire without at least 3 dots in fortitude will die in less than 30 seconds even at twilight).

                                  My suggestion for crossovers is to...

                                  A) Use Mage's underlying cosmology as both the Werewolve's cosmology AND the Vampire cosmology can both be 100% correct at the same time in the Mage cosmology. If a Mage needs an explanation for why the others don't get paradox, its because Vampires are thaumivores that gains the Quintessence they need by draining the life essence of those it feeds on while Werewolves evade paradox because the Delirium they produce causes 98% of the population who even sees them in action to just forget or rationalize it away.

                                  B) Remove the "must sleep during daylight" limitation (keep the dice pools limited by humanity during daylight, but they don't have to roll anything just to be conscious), have only direct sunlight damage vampires (difficulty 8 to soak with fortitude; difficulty 6 if you can still make out the sun through the clouds) and let them burn blood points off in place of taking sunlight damage (one blood point per health level they'd take; not restricted by generation spending limits). This puts vampires on more even footing with the other characters, though they can still burn to a crisp pretty quickly in direct sun, but running from shadow to shadow and burning off a couple blood points to make it through a sunlit area in an emergency (provided they can make the courage check) is now an option.

                                                          That's my point. A werewolf, a mage and a vampire on the same page won't be too far apart. That's been my experience since long before VWM20 and remains so now. In our latest crossover there's a bigger disparity in ability between the vamps in the group than between the combat focused mage and the combat focused vamp and between the garou theurge bass guitarist and the ravnos singer (both social focused).

                                                          Yeah, Crinos is nice, but only if you're building for combat. If you're starting from tertiary physical and no brawl/melee then you're probably looking at about STR 6, DEX 3, STA 5 with an attack of just 3 dice. A vampire starting with the same could get straight 5's in all three scores (so an attack of 6 dice with fangs) for a scene if they're willing to spend the blood to do so (depending on generation it would take time to get up to full and they'd be hungry afterwards but its on par with what Crinos gives the werewolf non-combatant, can choose which stats they want to bump and don't have to look like a 9' tall death machine to get them). A mage with Life 3 could get to all 5's and be able to soak agg and still be coincidental.

                                                          Yeah that werewolf probably has spirit allies to help, but the vampire probably has presence, dominate or some other discipline to balance the scales with and the mage, even without life has six spheres worth of crazy to evade or confound their enemies.

                                                          And a combat focused werewolf is a beast with probably STR 7, DEX 5, STA 6 in Crinos with brawl 5. But a combat focused vamp could start with STR 3, DEX 4, STA 3, Celerity 3, Firearms 5 and use its resources an AR-15, magazines of silver and incendiary bullets and military combat armor). Even with the armor penalty its got a ten dice attack and can soak 8 lethal or 5 agg that isn't fire/sun. A combat focused mage with Forces 2, Life 3 and Mind 1 can buff their stamina to the human limit and soak agg for a scene with just 4-5 successes. Throw in reinforced clothing and they can have seven dice of soak against agg. Plus they need just one success on their attacks to use forces 2 with "violence as focus" to make their attacks deal 6-8 levels (not dice) of damage per hit, possibly as unsoakable agg if they're using silver or incendiary bullets.

Werewolves in essence "pay" for Crinos with weaker options elsewhere. In D&D terms they're stuck with 2-3 levels of fighter before they can multiclass into anything else and they either have to devote enough other selections (physical attributes and ability picks) to make those fighter levels useful or end up with the D&D equivalent of wasted levels.
