They don't get the big picture, or don't know enough, or can't convince enough other people, or just choose to deal with other problems which seemed to be more imminent from their viewpoint. People are stupid, short-sighted, narrow-minded, factious things. Think about how much must happened during the second WW before the Allied forces amassed something that resembled cooperation.

It seems so that they and their wars are everywhere (because these are games about them and those, duh...), but in reality, according to the books, even a full-blown Sabbat siege could be covered as a turf war. And there are much more vampires than mages, or werewolves. That puts it in perspective, I think. The big conspiracies, they're hiding themselves really well, even from their subordinates and especially from each other. A Giovanni might notice that there is some organization behind the groups she's dealing with on the stock market, but she would need to do a lot, lot of research and in very obscure ways to find out any real evidence. It's not like the leaders of the Pentex, NWO, Syndicate, Camarilla and Giovanni holding an Evil V5 Conference each year.

And even then, those ages-old creatures saw just a part of the things, not the entirety of any event much less history.
            I see two problems with this line of thought. The first is that inter-generational storytelling changes accounts into myth... but this is not as much of an issue for creatures like Mokole or Rokea. The Rokea in particular can theoretically live forever, therefore their oral history isn't quite as likely to be obscured by the ages. Information isn't passed down through the eons via dozens or hundreds of storytellers, it can be received from 1st, 2nd, or 3rd hand sources.

            Second, our current modern western perception of needing rigorous scientific accuracy regarding history is quite new. The chroniclers and historians of old were never so precise; usually they wrote with specific narrative motive, and 100% verifiable truth wasn't it. Myth is not necessarily bad or even wrong. When an elder says 'this story is true,' they mean it, but not necessarily in a way that would satisfy Wikipedia. The moral or message of the story is what is important, not the precise gritty details that we currently value so much.
