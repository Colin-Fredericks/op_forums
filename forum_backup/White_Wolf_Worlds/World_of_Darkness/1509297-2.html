    Honestly, now that I've come back to think about it again, I can't help but feel bitter about what's going on with oWoD nowadays.

    I tried not to not be... but I can't, because oWoD was so unique and just awesome; even despite some of the "choicier" material found within it. Parawolf overcorrecting and then stuffing it with even worse material that is completely bland and doesn't match any theme or internal logic that the previous edition of a certain game had, is just utterly horrible.

    All the things they're doing to WtA SHOULD not the happening.. and I'm shocked that they're doing this "reimaging" thing (see: reboot) with WtA to begin with, as it was one of the "Big Three" of White Wolf splats. Hell, Parawolf doesn't even give WtA the courtesy of at least being a "soft-reboot" like what they did with V5... just trash the entire setting and make it more like another game series, because you got mad the former wasn't as popular as the classic predecessor.

    To be completely honest, Parawolf should have just stuck with developing CofD games and left it at that, to let oWoD die peacefully. It's clear that they don't genuinely care about the majority of aspects that made oWoD.... oWoD. But again, to beat a dead bush, they've done this strategy for pure branding power, and nothing else.

    All the changes they make, when it's not something to make oWoD to be more like CofD, is to either remove something that they think modern sensibilities and nu-young adult audiences cannot "handle" for some reason (even though WoD in general is supposed to explore mature themes, with urban fantasy mixed-in; i.e Metis and Kinfolk for the Garou being removed, thus eliminating any context to how Garou integrated with humans throughout the ages or formed their own subcultures within already existing human ones... this was a deliberate design choice); or something that the nu-writers at Parawolf have a clear bias for or against:

    VTM

    - Anarchs being a sudden uber faction, when they were always the peripheral third faction (clearly someone at Parawolf is an Anarch fan of somesort)

    - A dumb Beckoning plot devise to remove all the Elders from the game, thus enforcing more street level and angsty disorganized Vampire society, instead of the high-stakes supernatural political maneuvering and intrigue that was the old feel of the game

    - The Sabbat being completely removed from the game as playable characters and lorewise basically all migrating to the Middle-East due to this "Beckoning" plot device nonsense (the most egregious thing Parawolf has done to show their bias against certain elements in the oWoD franchise)

    - Tremere being broken up in the dumbest way possible

    - Lasombra mass defecting to the Camarilla despite the Vinculum and the centuries of fighting against the Camarilla, a sect traditionally associated with their greatest rivals, the Venture (no doubt a meta-bias by someone at Parawolf who leaned toward the "Camarilla Lasombra" camp of yore.. that now has become a cringe-worthy reality)

    - Making the Followers of Set join the Anarchs, when they always were being on the sidelines away from the other sects and corrupting everyone equally. Then afterwards, evangelize (literally in this case) their name to "The Ministry" as way to unsubtly harken-align them to basically be "the Christian megachurch guiss who're actually corrupt from the inside and manipulate people xDDDD"

    - Unique bloodlines being soft deleted by the new "Loresheet" institution introduced by Parawolf, due to the bigotry of low expectation that new people cannot go back to learn about these interesting bloodlines by themselves; so now let's just completely make them one-note (literally) and be something that a newcomer to oWoD will have zero interest in playing them to begin with...

    - All the unique Disciplines have now been relegated to the stupid Amalgam system (the principal casualty of this being Vicissitude) because Parawolf thought that all of these powers would be too much for newcomers to stomach... then they turn around by releasing at least 150~+? Amalgam powers that surely will be more easily consumer to the average person.. oh, and no more Discipline dots above 5; because it was too much "bloat"... but again, the whole 150+ Amalgam powers thing...

    - Whatever the Hecata is supposed to be

    HtR

    - The IMBUED.. the key element characters of that entire splat.. being COMPLETELY remove from their own game.. and as far as I know (correct me if I'm wrong) not even mentioned in H5. This is one of the most RIDICULOUS things Parawolf as done.

    WtA

    Most of the grievances against the upcoming W5 and the glaring problems that have already arrived with the release of some information of it, can basically be found in the WtA forum. To me, however, the worst thing Parawolf basically did with W5, is making the Umbra DE-EMPHASIZED / relegating to a more "uncharted" status.. no, just no.. It's clear to me they didn't want to deal with the unique cosmology of the Umbra and all of its unique locations, so now they want to make it more like Hisil from WtF (which is clear as day given the attitude of the former design lead).

    Also, something that I touched base with before in this post, is the fact on how the Garou basically.. are nothings now. They have no real culture or are now not supposed to be connected with any context to human society in an alt-history sense at all (except for the Fianna.. because apparently Fianna is just a "word"? As if it has nothing to do with the Celtic peoples? But nah, apparently it is just a word, so it can safely fit in with the idiotic "verb" naming scheme thing Parawolf came up with)

    The only audience W5 is going to have is V5 fans.

    Beyond that, I don't expect W5 to do particularly well at all; especially more-so with how the WtA community has reacted to it. I wonder what Parawolf will do if W5 ends up a disaster like H5 was, or if they'll even entertain doing a M5.. which will be the mother-of-all-disasters with how Paradox-Parawolf is handling this franchise.

                        "Realistic" is a strange word to through around to describe the power of fictional monsters at Embrace, and what they can achieve over time.

                        The problem is that by trying to have both Generation and Blood Potency the way V5 does, is you really just have a more complicated take on Generation. A 12th Gen vampire in V20 rules has a hard cap on how power they can get no matter how long they endure. A 12th Gen vampire in V5 also has a hard cap of the same sort. The only difference is V20 just has on "level" where V5 has some growth from 1 to 3 before hitting that cap. 7th Gen and lower vampires will always start at where 12th Gens stop, and only grow from there.

                        Having a more fluid system isn't inherently bad, but it has to be weighed in whether the increased complexity is really adding to the themes, play experience, and setting of the game. Since V5 wants to keep VtM's "unfair" Generations where power dilutes over time, it shackled Blood Potency to Generation. This undermines a lot of what Blood Potency was meant to do differently for VtR.

                            There's this phenomenon you can see with history where a rare and horrible crime occurred that sparked public outrage is wrongfully misconstrued by the popular imagination much later to be the norm. For example, people emptying their chamberpots out of the window onto a public street happened, but it certainly wasn't normal and anyone caught doing that would get their head kicked in and delivered to the authorities, but somehow we have it in our minds that it was normal. Similarly people have the idea that the ancient Greeks were conspicuous homosexuals who enjoyed teenage boys, when in reality pederasty was a mentor-student relationship with the occasional scandal: Sensational authors, political slander and Translation issues with the greeks frequently using love for strong friendships and brotherly/paternal type love didn't help that case.

                            I think a lot of V5 changes are extensions of this principle
                            There are Gehenna cults in the Camarilla, which are persecuted by the establishment: The Camarilla loves Gehenna cults
                            A few Settites are reactionary purists, despite the ideology of Set being against that kinda thing: Many Settites leave in a schism because there are too many reactionaries.
                            A lot of Brujah, which is a heavily divided clan internally, don't like the Cam: The Brujah leave the cam.
                            The princes in many publications are incompetent so we can have a dramatic story: The entire Camarilla is incompetent.
                            Some Tremere struggle in the pyramid: The Pyramid's divisions were so deep it could split into three big ideologically distinct factions upon leadership loss.

                                    I think an important difference between misconceptions about actual history, and how developers look back at RPG settings and where to go forward with them, is that RPG settings are interactive and contemporary. It's entirely possible to have playing the WoD games for years, and come away with the idea that say, the Brujah have no business being a "Cam" Clan and should be predominately Anarchs with a few Cam and Sabbat ones out there, without it being a misconception of the game. It can just be how your play experience has been, and how the play experience of people you talk to has been. Having the personal experience of playing in some differing groups that never really interacted much with each other, I've seen takes on factions/splats get entrenched that aren't in violation of what the books portray, but are more narrow than what the books actually promote at the same time.

                                    There's also play-style changes that happen when you change things like standard 3-6 player TTRPG to LARPs. When it's just you and your friends, and the ST is tasked with playing the powers of status quo in the setting, everyone being an odd-ball iconoclast is fine. When every member of a city's court but the Prince is a PC, it falls apart. You need the players handling the sheriff, primogen, etc. to all play more to type to keep the game coherent, and there's also just a stronger negative attitude towards "special snowflake" PCs as being disruptive.

                                    People that have only played TTRPG style with their friends are going to have very different ideas of what the games need to change compared to someone that primarily comes from a large multiple group organized LARP background. Different experiences lead to different expectations of what changes will push play in the direction the new developers actually have as a goal.

                                    While I bring this up to give an alternative explanation than impact of tunnel-vision on perceptions of history, it's also not a neutral example. I can certainly see a case for having more people with a LARP background bringing in new ideas for a new edition of the WoD, I think a lot of the problems that have divided the community around WoD5 are because WoD5 feels - at least to me - more like LARP folks trying to make a TTRPG without listening to the TTRPG folks. What can be very successful events for major LARP stories, to not necessarily translate well into TTRPG metaplot events.

                                    Besides divides in the community, I think this extends further because it's not like LARPing or TTRPG styles are actually better for the idea of rebuilding the WoD brand when trying to come up with products in other media spaces. How I would run a WtA LARP vs. how I would run a TTRPG WtA game, is basically orthogonal to what would make a good WtA video game; or what would best update the games for 2020.
