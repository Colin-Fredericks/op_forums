    Yeah, I interpret that as more of an innate group counterspell ability rather than anything related to the Consensus. I assign one die of counterspell for every one hundred direct mortal witnesses if the direct mortal witnesses have not been explicitly warned to expect magical effects (which I also apply to Changeling Arts, Fera Gifts, Kindred Sorcery, Mage Spheres, etc).                     The books case for mages not being able to learn sorcery is largely a game concern. The authors have been clear that even if a Path might look tempting, it is largely an XP trap for mages. Mastering a Path costs a huge amount of XP, for a few tricks that are Paradox free, compared to what the mage could get with Spheres.

                    The in-character explanation is not terribly strong for it. But it is sufficient: once you awake up, you can't go back to sleep. Once a mage has awakened, they have to interact with the world through the lens of a mage. They understand too much of how things really work to try to "dumb it down" and perform Numina magic (except True Faith in most incarnations of it, but TF is weird even for Numina); their enlightened knowledge just has to butt in. The closest a mage can do is pull on the mystic threads that allow Numina, making their magic easier by following the same in-character rules sorcery uses, but it's still dynamic magic in disguise.

                    As for "should mages be able to learn other supernatural powers" thing, metaphysically it's questionable once they're in the category of having spiritual/etc. connections like that. But really, the out-of-character point that the amount of expense in learning a Gift for a Kin-mage is so high, it's stupid to even bother.

                        The main reason I'd think mages wouldn't go with paths is because the paths have a sort of stigma to them. For Hermetics, the paths are not real magic at all, but a tool to help one gain the right frame of mind to proceed to real magic. Returning to the paths after awakening would be like a cyclist opting to put training wheels back on their bike. It looks silly, and makes them seem less able in the eyes of other mages.

                        This view is echoed by many other traditions, though in different ways.
                        The Choristers that awaken are closer to The One than the others, and as such have a duty to revere The One's power. Returning to Static Magic then may be seen as a rejecting the wisdom of The One in order to stay with what was once familiar, a step backwards.
                        Ecstatics would not be able to achieve the same high they once did, and would grow less and less satisfied with hedge magic. They may be able to, but it doesn't really do anything for them anymore. Kinda like how once you've watched a HD Television, going back to SD is rather disappointing.
                        Virtual Adepts would avoid wanting to seem Lame by sticking with standard Bleater style coding. Rep is everything with these guys.
                        and the Akashics (last example, I promise) would also feel as though they are relying on a crutch if they go back to static magic. Theirs is a solitary journey of constant examining of ones ways and lifestyles. If one is unable to let go of what they hold on to, then they are trapped. Once you've achieved a level of freedom, returning is almost like you've given up trying.

                        Also, mechanically speaking, Static Magic is actually pretty annoying to use. You can do barely anything, and it costs WP to use. WP is one of the most often drained resource of all the stats in the games I run. It means you're limited to about five or six spells a week before you just don't have the energy anymore. And for every WP lost, that's another point that can't be used later for those oh so critical automatic successes.
