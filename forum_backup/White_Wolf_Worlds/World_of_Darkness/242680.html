                      Back in university, we sometimes had two games going at a time of different systems, and alternated them by week. They generally lasted 1-2 years, and were often 2pm-11pm or so. These days we're lucky if we can game once a month, and it's more like 4pm-10pm with an hour or two of that just socializing.

                      Then there's MUSHes. I've been on one werewolf MUSH that's run for nearly twenty years now, and still has some of its original players. I've also seen a lot of WoD places that pop up and run for somewhere between six months and two years. Fingers crossed... Anyway, the successful ones have something going most nights, and the most active players are often active many nights per week. Short scenes 1-2 hours, big events 12+ hours, but as the crowd gets older everyone tries to avoid that happening. I spent about forty hours one week in a rite of passage plotline, while also working fulltime... good times, except not. I don't think that's the norm though.

                                          Many of our games last maybe 6 sessions, if we're lucky. Some last just one, and others have been an ongoing setting for 20 years (with multiple characters, chronicles, and stories using the same setting and timeline.)
                                          For instance: We have a Marvel game that has been ongoing since '96-ish, where we may not play the same characters, but it's the same universe and what we did years ago still affects what happens now. The same for a game that started in '94 with the TSR module "Masque of the Red Death" that ran for several years with our original characters, then came to a conclusion and started again in the 1940s (with the Nazis raising Dracula and the Red Death), and then a third game (Masque 2K) that took place in the 2000s using the Spycraft system... where we played super-spies/agents of "good-guy agents" vs. "bad guy vampires/undead agents". To this day, that game still crops up. At one time we had 3 GMs in 2 different states running the same setting.

                                          When it comes to WoD games, most tend to only last a few sessions, unless we REALLY are attached to the game. Our latest W:tA game started in 2008, and though we've had some years in between sessions, and a few splittings of the game group, we've continued to play it when we can get enough people around.

                                          And that seems to be the issue we've ALWAYS had: Our group has always had a hard time getting the same 5-7 people in the same place, each week, at the same time. Our TSR Marvel game has over 57 "active" characters over the last 20 years because each time we wanted to play, we never had the same people around. I have folders full of characters from "one-shots" that never went anywhere because the same group didn't show up, or we didn't have the books, or just didn't feel like playing that game that week.

                                          So, yeah... a great number of our games last 1-2 sessions. A good number of them last 8 or so sessions, but that may take a year or so to get through and we never really come to an end of a story as it just peters out, and a few rare games last many many years, but often don't follow the same characters in the same setting.

                                                      When my friends and I were in our later years of High School (mid 1990s), we had a "Summer of Fun", where we gamed every day and night for 3 months... At the time, Domino's would award you a free order every 100th order... we hit that twice that Summer. Once school/college/jobs picked back up, that was it... and we were back to once a week/month again. We would often do a game once a week, on a Sunday, or a day after school where we would meet at Taco Bell, or Burger King, or something, and game for many hours (getting kicked out and moving from fast-food restaurant after restaurant as Managers didn't like us "loitering", even though we continued to buy food.)

                                                      College had a "gaming club night", where we would often play one game a week, but swap games every other week (L5R one week, Deadlands the next, etc.)

                                                      As folks moved away and jobs/families occupied more and more time, or put people on difficult schedules, trying to get ANYONE on the same repetitive night was impossible. To this day we have to work damned hard to get the same group of 5 together for the same game. Having a gaming day/night is an actual event, either requiring driving 60+ minutes to the location, and/or planning a week+ in advance to see who can attend and figuring out what the game will look like for the GM that night.
                                                      Some happen semi-spontaneously when a few of us are hanging out at someone's house, and we have someone to run a game, two others there, and a phonecall made to drag another into a game ("Hey, we're playing Star Wars tonight! Come on over!")

                                                      As for character advancement... shit... we don't really keep track of our XP that way (not with a log, or anything) and few of our games last long enough for us to advance. Hell, to be brutally honest, up until a friend's recent game, we've never played a Werewolf: the Apocalypse game long enough to even deal with Rank Challenges, or going up in Rank (and we played that a LOT back in the day!)
