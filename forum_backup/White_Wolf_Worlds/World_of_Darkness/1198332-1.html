                                Demon: The Fallen is easily my favorite oWoD game. Even with its flaws (the lore is sometimes a bit odd, the wonky rules here and there and the art that's nothing special) I really like the ideas it brings to the front, and the Fallen can get very powerful. Quick overview of the books:

                                Must Have
                                - The core book, of course. The first four chapters are all lore told in stories instead of matter of factly (which I've been told was pretty common in the late oWoD books).
                                - Demon Players Guide. The second half of the core book. Has a trove of information for character creation, info on Virtues and Lores, the merits and flaws, DIY Apocalypse Forms as well as rules for relics and rituals.
                                - Earthbound. Everything you'll need to know about the Earthbound, their origins, their rise and fall as well as rules for creating them and their servants. Also has rules for fighting them.

                                Should Have
                                - Houses of the Fallen. The splatbook for all seven Houses containing lore and has rules for using the Houses. Also has rules for relics and rituals, making it work best with with Players Guide.
                                - Storytellers Companion. Has lore on the Earthbound (you can ignore this if you have the Earthbound book), the Factions, Greater Demons (who are not really that great tbh), the realm of the dead and rules for interacting with vampires, werewolves, mages, wraiths and hunters.
                                - Damned and Deceived. The book on Thralls. The first three chapters are all stories from the pov of Thralls (about half the book's length), with the other half being rules for how to make Thrall characters and how to play them, as well as how to find potential Thralls for the Fallen.

                                Could Have
                                - Demon Translation Guide. The go-to book if you want to integrate Fallen and Descent somehow. Has some interesting rules if you want to put Fallen into CofD (1-3 dot Lores become Embeds while 4-5 ones become Exploits) or the other way around, but sadly lack options for the Possessed or its demons.
                                - City of Angels. Los Angeles is the home city for Demon: The Fallen. It goes into detail about the city, the main movers and shakers as well as the rules for Infernal Courts, which is pretty fun if you really like dynamics along the lines of Camarilla games in Vampire. If neither of those are your groove, you can skip this one.
                                - Saviours and Destroyers. Like Damned and Deceived but with an even greater perecentage of the book being stories and even less being actual ways to use Exorcists, aka those who hunt Demons. Barely any rules in here and no simple step-by-step character creation with some extra goodies. You might as well skip this, or instead get Hunters Hunted and replace all references to vampires with demons.

                                Would Have
                                - Fear to Tread. A chronicle set in LA. Never run it myself, but as per it nature it's only interesting if you want to run it.
                                - Days of Fire. What The Book of Nod is to Vampire Days of Fire is to Demon. First 80% is something of a retelling of the story of Creation and the end of the world, as well as references to the other lines. Final 20% is the investigation of the theft of the Dies Ignis parchments, of which Days of Fire is a translation of. Things start to nod add up culmulating in a seemingly impossible double murder. All of this is intercut with signs of the end of the world, including observations of the Red Star.
                                - World of Darkness: Time of Judgement. The Time of Judgement book for Changeling, Hunter, KotE, Mummy and of course Demon. More or less the sequel for Days of Fire, so make sure you get that as well. Prologue and Epilogue are narrated by Lucifer Morningstar himself, giving some interesting insights and a line near the end that will uproot everything you thought you knew about the game. The three scenarios for Demon can be summed up as Fallen VS Earthbound, Fallen & Greater Demons VS Earthbound and Fallen & Lucifer VS Earthbound, the latter of which being the most interesting and playing to the central theme of Demon the most. I'd almost say get this just for the Lucifer parts, but most of the book you'll never use for Demon.
                                - Trilogy of the Fallen. A fiction trilogy written by Greg Stolze. From what I've heard the first is pretty good, but the second less so. Get it if you want to read some fiction on Demon.
                                - Lucifer's Shadow. An anthology of ten short stories. No idea about the quality.

                                And finally, there's Dark Ages - Devil's Due. It's a Dark Ages book on pacts between mortals and demons, and while it's not related to Demon: The Fallen it shares a lot of ideas and mechanics (some of which it even does better). I haven't read all of it yet but from what I've seen it's pretty neat.

                                        What I found interesting after reading Demon the Fallen when it came out was the idea that the Fallen's personality can be unpredictable to their peers. The evilest most desperate and hateful Demon could possess a serial killer and wind up with a Fallen who is jarred by the mortality of even someone so despicable and combined with eons of loneliness in the Abyss you could have the resulting character be someone realizing humanity for the first time and seek redemption and compassion. Conversely you could have one of the more sympathetic and merciful fallen possess a kind selfless person and have it result in a psychotic nihilistic Fallen due to some strange interaction experiencing human memories and the hate and loathing and hypocrisy could just erupt. In the Novels Fallen seemed really careful around other Demons particularly ones they knew from before the Abyss. It was like they had no idea that possessing a human body and mind and countless ages in isolation could reshuffle someones priorities and personality.

                                            Generally I can say that those people who play demon tend to like it alot. But it usually takes a bit of houseruling to cope with the system. But the setting is great and very evocative, combining many things from different wod gamelines (the fight for the future or mage, the reconciliating with what you are from vampire, the fight for existence itself from werewolf, etc).

                                            That said here is what, in my mind, seperates DtF from other gamelines. Other splats hide from humanity, wether out of self interest (most) or due to metaphysical complications (mages, werewolfs). Demons don't. While they can in minor ways be fucked over by disbelief (though very very minorly compared to mages and easily overcome by burning a bit of fuel unless they've been trained to negate lores... which is part of one of their apocalypse scenarios but I'm getting off track) they generally don't care if people know about them. They are in fact empowered by it. One of their ways of gaining Faith (their fuel) is to reveal themselves to mortals. They build cults around themselves. Sure so far no one has gone on national television, but that's more because it'd place a target on their chest. Demons will reveal themselves (and everything else along with them) to humanity sooner or later. The big metaplot event of demon was Lucifer himself showing up over LA, visible for miles and making hundreds of thousands of people aware of his existence. And all the bigshots in hell when they come out will want to do similar things, to build big cults, to get much faith etc. pp. This is also excerbated by the fact that humans (and in fact most beings) cant really lastingly hurt demons. Loosing your host really sucks, but the demon usually survives, unless another demons eats them (or a mage if you want them to exist in your demon games does some high level rote I guess).

                                            So demon is an apocalyptic game. You're on earth. Millions and millons of more demons will come to earth soon, some of them very very potent (not so much mechanically actually, but thats easily fixed by giving them the Mastery trait Earthbounds have). They will tear the earth apart, some of them because that's their goals, others because they clash in their objectives. Earth will never be the same again. Maybe it'll take just a year, maybe ten. Where is Lucifer? Can he stop this? Can we prevent this? Do we even want to? And if we do what do we want to build from the ashes of civilization?

                                            Demon is one of the most high powered WoD games. While you can do slice of life stuff with demons just trying to adjust, the setting is set up in a away that very much has the apocalypse impending and cosmic consequences could very well be the end game of your chronicle. This is a game of angels and demons clashing in the sky over cities, the old gods awakening, not bloodsuckers trying to hide from the herd they tell themselves is inferior.
