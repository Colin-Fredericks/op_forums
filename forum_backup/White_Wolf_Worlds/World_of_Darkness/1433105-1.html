"Consensual Reality" is just the vague thing that a bunch of blind people can agree "is there" while they grope an elephant, but each has different beliefs about the actual truth of the matter, based on what part of the elephant they are feeling.

            Consensual Reality is not meant to be a cohesive Theory of Everything because Mage has no cohesive Theory of Everything beyond what an individual Storyteller decides for their Mage game. Whether or not Everything is Chaos or the Choristers are right depends on the Storyteller ultimately, the game will never give you a solid answer.

                    Originally posted by ryu238 View Post
                    Now these obviously need to be logically consistent to the Mage in order to work.

                    Actually, they don't. Paradigms have to be internally consistent, not logically consistent, because they're just a framework for a mage to approach the infinite possibilities of magic in a way they can make sense of. The Traditions and the Technocracy tend to have more logically sound (within the assumptions of the setting) paradigms because of centuries or more of refinement and cultural exchange, but Orphans (mages that awaken on their own and figure out magic largely by trial and error) have very ad hoc paradigms. Marauders are, of course, even less bound by logic.

                    An important part of magical Focus (paradigm + style + instruments) is that they're subject to refinement. A more functional one will supplant a less functional one because refinement gets better results. "Wacky" paradigms can exist, but they don't practical last because they're unrefined and get abandoned for more functional ones (or a Marauder happens).

                    However in the light of Consensual Reality and Paradox, that reality seems to be subjective, on what grounds could their logic in particular, let alone any sort, rely on?
                    Consensus is stable enough that it functions like an objective reality most of the time. It's like Newtonian physics. Newtonian physics has been supplanted as the main model of physics, but we still teach and use Newton's stuff. Why? Because it's how things work on a human scale, and simplifying out all the relativity stuff and later advances saves time and energy for people that don't feel like studying quantum theory.

                    More importantly though, the metaphysics of Mage is all about mages superimposing their personal reality over the current reality of their location (Consensus or not). In order to do that, mages need to have a reality to overlay on top of the local reality. Overlaying "reality is subjective, anything could happen," is in theory possible, but in practice unusable because it means you can't actually use it to accomplish consistent results. Why go through all the training and effort to be a mage if exerting yourself is as likely to create a rubber ducky as it is to toss a fireball at your opponent? Mages study more consistent and structure paradigms because it means that when they exert their reality over the current reality then can produce a reliable effect. Normally, you can't chant words and then a fireball flies at your enemies. A Hermetic overlays a reality that says you can do that, and then does it.

                    Paradox is the push back based on the difference between current reality and overlay reality. If you overlay a reality that's very slightly different, there's less feedback than if you overlay one that's drastically different.

                    Would 2+2=Fish if the Consensus believed it fully, rendering mathematics meaningless?
                    It wouldn't render mathematics meaningless, it would render it different. Mathematics is just an abstract group of symbols we all agree mean something and then the logical extrapolation of what those abstractions do when they interact based on the rules we decided to set. It wouldn't cause someone that puts two apples into a basket with two other apples to sudden have a single fish. They'd have a fish worth of apples as fish is now also the concept that we current call four.

                    Altering Consensus is extremely difficult because it's a nigh infinite number of interlocking and frequently subconscious beliefs. If you try to change one, the rest will resist. If your change creates a negative feedback of dissonance, it will be resisted as well. That's why the Order of Reason and then the Technocracy pushed an agenda of Enlightenment based humanistic technological values. Technology provided a simpler way to add things to the Consensus despite its reactionary nature. Once you've embedded the idea that humans can do anything with the right tools, all you need to do is introduce the tools in digestible steps and people will accept that you can build a flying machine with science and technology, instead of spells and magic that only mages can do.


                    Would Last Thursdayism be a thing?
                    In what sense? No non-Maurader mage would actually follow it, but mages study all sorts of philosophic things.

                    I ask because I am taking the implications of the Consensus to their logical conclusion, and wonder if any mage ever considered this.
                    You're not taking the implications to their logical conclusions because you don't know what those are.

                    Heavy Arms said that the nature of Consensual Reality makes a Mage's Structure all the more important...but as we see with the Traditions and other mages that makes any idea of consistent internal logic more absurd since any worldview is valid!
                    Any world view being a path toward valid magic doesn't mean they're equal in their validity. See above about wacky paradigms.

                    The Marauders seem to the logical conclusion to this.
                    They aren't. They're a possible conclusion to this for those that can't or refuse to refine their paradigms to a functional state.

                    What then is the point of the different factiins proclaiming their Paradigm to be "THE TRUTH" if it is technically determined by shared belief?
                    See the overlay of realities analogy. The factions embrace their taught realities strongly because that makes it easier to overlay onto of the shared Consensus.

                    The Earthly plane, which extends beyond Earth itself. The Earthly plane is the part of the mage universe that Sleepers can observe.                         I'll add that the notion that every mage is doing the same thing in different ways is an innovation of the Traditions, and is mostly not believed by mages outside of that faction. The earlier editions of the game were written largely from the Traditions' perspective, so it's easy to make the mistake of thinking that everyone believes this, except for a handful of ignoramuses and the Technocrats. Indeed, earlier editions of the game framed the rise in Arete and the discarding of foci as a mage growing beyond his paradigm, which is also a central feature of the Traditions and their teachings. M20 has changed this so that the discarding of instruments in a mystical Focus comes from a greater gut-level understanding that Magick comes from within and that instruments are merely aids in the process of exercising that innate power; but the Paradigm and Practice remain firmly in place all the way through, no matter how high your Arete reaches.

                        Also: the meta-OOC concept of consensual reality has so little impact on the actual game that is entirely possible to excise it entirely as a fundamental truth of the setting with virtually no impact on the setting. In my games, the question of the ultimate nature of reality is left as a mystery, even to me. I accept that the Traditions believe in some sort of “it's all the same, fundamentally” philosophy, and that there's evidence supporting their views; but I also accept that the Technocracy believes that there's something fundamentally different between what it does and what the Superstitionists do, and that there's evidence supporting their views. And as I mentioned to Penelope, it hasn't really affected my games.

Normally, mages escape a Quiet, having learned some humility about letting their powers get out of control. Marauders are in a permanent Quiet that there's no known escape from. On top of this, the bubble protects them from Paradox (they have no control or skill at this) and deflects it to the nearest mages instead.

Within our more logically-consistent world, it makes sense for us to assume that quantum mechanics five hundred years ago worked the same way they do today.
