                  If its too late for the Embrace, then its too late for most methods. As far as I know, only one character has ever (canonically) been brought back, after their corpse had cooled - and that was Osiris. It took a pair of highly skilled mages working together to pull that off . . . and arguably he didn't come back right. Plus, the secret of how they did that is lost - there's one ancient mummy who might have witnessed the ritual, but that's about it.

                  Really, their only hope is that the deceased had strong passions, and unfinished business. That would allow them to hang around as a wraith, and maybe someday become a Risen (although that's more zombie than resurrection). It might take some retconning into your story to pull it off, but since most rookie wraiths can't affect anything on the living side of the Shroud, that should be doable.

                  Or, if they're Asian, they could come back as a kuei-jin (Kindred of the East) . . . although that's not really a fate most people would want for a friend or loved one. It means that they've been the "guest" of the demon lords of the East, and subject to all the torments of Yomi. And now they're a fugitive from hell, desperately trying to find a way to avoid going back.

                  But . . .that's kind of it. Even in the World of Darkness, the dead usually stay dead. You might be able to tell some good stories, though, about the PC learning to cope with his loss. Or about him going mad with grief, turning to increasingly dark and unholy figures in desperation, and eventually degenerating into a (real) monster (hey, it is still the World of Darkness).

                      There are a number of ways it could happen across the official game lines but they're all difficult, dangerous, and stand a fair chance of going horribly wrong. Success could provide for good drama as well as failure and increasingly desperate attempts.

                      You could also borrow from outside sources, like the posthumous Embrace in Vampire: The Requiem which can work up to a week if the victim had ever tasted a vampire's blood and is almost without unusual side effects. Bringing that into Masquerade I'd suggest including some issues, like a need for a ritual (and a big favour to the Giovanni) and/or some odd Flaws that result like the late-Embraced character needing more blood to rise.

                      I suppose the question, best discussed with the player, is what would be most interesting? Failure and despair, a hard-won success like a descent into the underworld, or a relatively easy revival with consequences to follow, for the vampire or the subject or both? (See Jonathan Strange & Mr. Norrell for a great current example of the latter.)

                                          The NWoD Promethean game suggests other possibilities for bringing someone back to... life-ish via science, alchemy, and what have you. I'd say using Frankenstein as a guidebook could lead to some interesting stories. There's also the option of going the delusional route, and having the PC only think the loved one has been brought back.

                                          Generally speaking, I'd say that any method used to resurrect the dead other than actual divine intervention should lead to lots of unpleasantness for the person raised, the person doing the raising, and innocent bystanders in their lives. Whether the result is a zombie, a patchwork being with no true identity, an exceedingly solid ghost, another kind of vampire, or a demonically-animated doppelganger, it should be at least as tortuous for the person breaking all the laws of nature as dealing with his grief would be.

                                              I agree with Craig - this is really something you need to discuss with the player (possibly the entire group). Decide (at least vaguely) what direction you want to go in, and how big a part of your game this should be. That way you can work up some interesting (side) adventures & npcs, he gets to play out the character's obsession, and the other players aren't crowded out. Do it right, and this'll transcend a mere dungeon crawl - this could end up a work of art.

                                              Plus, you have a decision to make. Unless the dead person's soul is still hanging around, as a wraith/spectre, or in one of the pseudo-afterlives in an Umbral realm. . . .its not going to be possible to bring them back. Not without the direct intervention of God, or possibly one of the Oracles (who have magic that transcend the Mage game). So if (s)he's truly "moved on", nothing is going to be able to bring them back.

                                              Originally posted by Coridan View Post
                                              The Player Character in question is not particularly a scholar of the occult and I am certain anything he does is going to lead to a walking corpse with murderous intent. Just wanted to know where to start reading up depending on which direction he takes. (Studying Egyptian occult, demon pacts, mortis, wraiths, mage magic)
                                              Good - that's very World of Darkness

                                              Reading Mage - unless you're planning a full blown crossover (which can be cool) - isn't really going to be productive. The Mage rules get very "meta", very quickly, which gets away from what you're looking for.

                                              Instead, if your group wants to pursue the mage angle, you should probably pick a couple of (real world) styles, and read up on them. Each mage has a sort of "theme" to their magic: super scientist, voodoo priest, faith healer . . . and they can use their "Quintessence" (read "Blood Pool") to achieve "impossible" effects - in that style. A mad scientist probably can't summon an angel, but might be able to pull a Frankenstein. Or the holy man on the mountain - he isn't going to have robot legions, but might be able to talk directly to God.

                                              So if you're thinking of bringing in a mage or two (for a while), its really more important to get the "feel" of their style of magic down, and worry less about the actual mechanics. Mage magic (even the "scientific" type) depends heavily on atmosphere and belief, so you really want to get the trappings of the style right (which, incidentally, will help make it more memorable for the group, too).

                                                  Oh, and if you're looking for a canon example of how badly a resurrection can go wrong, take a look at the Bane Mummies (from Mummy, obvioiusly). Back when Set and Horus were feuding over Egypt, Set decided his vampiric Followers weren't enough - he wanted his own mummies, too. So he brought in some dark mages . . .

                                                  And the result was horrific. Seven loyal minions died . . . seven abominations came back. Things that had the memories of the dead, but not their souls. Oh, sure the personality was sort of right - but ultimately it was only a cruel parody. These "Bane Mummies" were loyal only to hell, and bound to the cause of universal destruction.

                                                  And yet, they still had the signature power of mummies - the ability to resurrect themselves. Stabbed, shot, burned, devoured by werecrocodiles . . . sooner or later, they would always rise again.

                                                  So if you want to really torture the PC, you might consider having their loved one "return" as a Bane Mummy. Their friend, but not - and a horrific monster, to boot. And no matter what they do, the PC will never be able to make it right. The damage is done, and this creature, which looks and talks just like the deceased, is loose on the world. And yet it makes a mockery of everything the lost loved one stood for. Even if the PC breaks down, and kills it, it just won't stay dead.

                                                  Could be an interesting way to bring a recurring villain into the series, if you're interested. I'd suggest drawing that out a bit, though, to get the most out of the "pure" grief, first. It'll make the new Bane Mummy more horrific




                                                  Edit Incidentally, not all mummies (true or Bane) are Egyptian. Horus "mass produced' them (creating maybe 100) to fight Set, resulting in a large cultural block of Egyptian ones, but the "Great Rite" has been independently developed in most cultures that practice mummification. So there are Incan mummies, Chinese mummies . . . many of whom don't like their Egyptian "cousins"

                                                      I had something similar happen in a Mage chronicle a decade and change ago... But it was the entire cabal. A close associate was killed horribly right in front of them during a battle when all but one of them was helpless to do anything about it. The player of the one character dropped out of the game shortly afterwards and the other players really really wanted Edward back...

                                                      It didn't completely dominate the game, but that desire did become major focus. Trying to figure out what to do become a passion. Followed shortly thereafter by collecting all the relevant "plot coupons" needed to make it happen. (Which I started placing here and there. But they had to figure them out. There was some backtracking and substitution.) It also involved them having to learn a lot about how each others' magic worked, how Edward's had worked, and how they could overlap.

                                                      Ir was a lot of fun. They had to overcome a lot of problems to make it happen. For example. since the body was gone-gone, they had to acquire a new one... They ended up using the body of an opponent whose soul had almost entirely hollowed out during possession by a specter. They had to put the body into suspended animation and popped it into a Han Dynasty suit of jade burial armor to "marinate" it appropriately. (They had to make a fairly unpleasant deal with a vampire Prince to acquire the jade suit). It ended up working out that the body still had a little bit of soul left in it, which ended up not being as bad a thing as it could have been, as Edward's soul was somewhat damaged from being caught in a perpetual loop of reliving the horrific moment of his death in a little tiny Atrocity Realm. However, it did mean the Edward they got back was not entirely the Edward they had lost... E.g. Now Edward, who had been raised in a Euthanatos monastery with a pretty austere and terrible childhood, became a fan of watching (American) football games, during which he ate classic bar food. All of which was completely out of character. (That's a cute example. Not all of the results were cute at all.)

                                                      One of the most interesting complications was that one of the PC's was pregnant and Edward was the father. That was, pre-death, different body Edward. That lead to some... adjustments... in the relationship after he was reincarnated/resurrected/(re)assembled.

                                                          There are a few European (Greek?) mummies running around too. So in theory, it is possible.

                                                          However, the person in question should be a wraith at least, because otherwise their soul would have passed on. Reviving someone without a soul is bound to cause all kinds of nastiness (although this is the World of Darkness, so this is probably exactly what you're intending, right?). But reanimating the dead flesh is probably the least difficult part. Even a Giovanni could do that (if he had the right paths of Necromancy). Reviving the body and restoring its corpse to an undecayed state is even possible. You could even summon *something* to dwell inside that body.

                                                          In fact, you could have them bargaining with various different supernaturals to have various parts of the process performed one at a time. Step 1: reset the decay and preserve the body. Step 2: revive the body. Step 3: summon a spirit. Step 4: all hell breaks loose.
