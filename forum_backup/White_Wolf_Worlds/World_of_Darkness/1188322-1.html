        I think it highly depends on the circumstances in which it is being presented.
        Some guy walking down the street with a sabretooth tiger or a dinosaur? Yeeeah, I think that would hit the familiar with the disbelief/paradox effect.

        However, there have been so many actual RL stories about scientists having the concrete idea of cloning these old critters from DNA found in fossils and frozen mammoths and what have you, that if a technomancer (or a mage simply pretending to be one) invited members of the public for a BIG REVEAL, and then showed off the critters along with explanations of cloning, they actually could become coincidental - even for further mages following up with the idea.

        Naturally the Technocracy would either send out the NWO to either suddenly 'debunk' the 'hoax' the media fell for, putting the coincidence back towards vulgarity....or the Progenitors might actually go 'Oh well, it's a slight, uncontrolled timetable acceleration. Put down regulations for the tech and we can make the best of this situation.' Slight timetable acceleration because, realistically, sleeper tech these days can pull off the whole feat already. Just nobody is crazy enough to clone HUGE PREHISTORIC BEINGS yet. Looking at you, Etherites.

                    Originally posted by The Nilbog View Post
                    Wow. Looks like you guys think way more highly of sleepers than I do. I tend to think of them as, by default, boring sods that reject anything that interrupts their boring sod life. (Though, given the interpretation that they will accept things that they think are merely possible, rather than likely, I stand by the idea that the Choristers, Templars, Batini and other major religion based Mages should be in a more favorable position than they are in the canon.)
                    That depends how far on the sliding scale of sinicism you wish to go.

                    For me, a sleeper is just people, the people you meet on you’re normal walk to work, the guy at the bus station, the girl standing by the atm, waiting their turn. I reject the overtly consumerist portrayal that the original books used to describe sleepers as rather unfair. I also find it a bit reductionist to just say “only mages, or at the very least sorcerors are capable of abstract thinking and emotive reasoning. Sleepers only do as their told, buy stuff, and watch tv.”
                    I don’t mind that being a stereotype in game, especially one held by overtly snobbish mages, but to make that a reality of the setting in many ways cheapens it.

                    For me, the only thing that separates the sleeper from the mage is that the sleeper does not believe themselves capable of magic. They are still people with their own stories and lives, every bit as complex as any mage. They may be rebellious, intellectual, imaginative, curious, and even politically charged, with their own goals and ambitions every bit as varied as a mages. Perhaps one wishes to be an astronaut, perhaps another is working up the nerve to ask the barista out, and maybe yet another is trying to get their novel published.

                    They are simply unaware of their influence on reality, they see themselves as predominantly reactionary. Unable to change what the universe gives them, or at the very most, only able to change things with expert advice, be it spiritual, scientific, financial or otherwise.

                    Let’s not forget though, that while the technocracy might have a strong hold over the consensus, it is the sleepers that actually create the consensus. If enough of them believed that pigs can genuinely fly, then they would. If enough thought they could walk through walls, then that too would be possible. The problem is that sleepers are so varied in their beliefs, that any one individual belief that differs from the main consensus is not strong enough to bring about change, that’s what mages do. However, if enough people believe something, then it changes, gradually, but certainly. That is how the technocracy operates, gradual change in the guise of “development” in order to change the consensus little by little to something iterative enough to make individual will working unnecessary. Why awaken when everyone can do powers?
