    As adambeyoncelowe pointed out, you use bonus content for that, not promotional content meant to get people interested in your product in the first place. If signing up got people a free Loresheet or monster write-up for Hunter that's only going to be given away via a Paradox account? That's how you get people to give away an email address.

    There's no appeal to:

    "Hey, we've got a new game."
    "What's it about?"
    "Create an account and find out!"

    There's no way you don't lose more people because it twinges their Internet red flags than you'd get by tempting people by putting an advertisement behind an account sign up.

    "Hey, we've got a new game, check out this preview pdf!"
    "That's neat."
    "Cool, if you sign up for an account, you'll get updates and a free monster write-up exclusive to people with accounts."

    That? That's going to get more people to make accounts than didn't, and if used well should generate more than enough sales to cover the costs of a two-page monster write-up and a 1/8 page art piece a few times over.

    La Celestina looked interesting enough, that I'd consider converting her to HtR 1e or HtV. If there's a freebie monster I could get with just an email? If people said it was as cool as La Celestina I could see myself making an account to get it legit instead of waiting to pause a YouTube video to read the text as it flashes by because the reviewer was showing off the art. I might never play H5, but a good bestiary that has lots of neat antagonists is something that would actually have value to me.

                                        I'm umming and ahhhing. I like the visuals. I don't like the generic terminology as much (the Driven sounds more passive than the Imbued), but it's not a deal-breaker. If they explicitly and easily allowed Imbued to be a part of the game, instead of just retconning them, I'd be happy.

                                        One thing that has weirdly rubbed me the wrong way is the sidebox in the new preview that likens 'the Reckoning' to terms used for modern social justice movements. I think it's because I always find WW lexicons full of frivolous (if fun) language that's deliciously style over substance. But it seems cynical to link 'the Reckoning' to #MeToo or #BLM. It's trivialising and commercialising.

                                        'The Reckoning' doesn't need to be an IC term. And it definitely doesn't need to create an equivalence between fantastical vampire killing and civil rights movements.
