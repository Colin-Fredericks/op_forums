    I think people are overlooking Caine's most powerful asset: God cursed him so that anyone who tries to hurt him (or does hurt him, depending on the interpretation) gets back what they give seven times. The only person who could hurt Caine (other than God) was Lilith, because she is effectively God's equal/other half. Everyone else would be obliterated before they could destroy him or shortly afterwards.

    Lilith, being a proto-mage, would likely destroy everyone in the world and be the sole survivor. She has no particular loyalty to mages or vampires, because she's a different order of being now. She might save her own 'children', though, depending on which aspect of her personality is ascendant at the time.

    I imagine she'd then go for her revenge on Yahweh. That might be a stalemate (in Revelations of the Dark Mother, it's indicated both he and she are mere manifestations of a greater, non-personified Godhead, which has a range of avatars known as The Shining Ones).

    If the Neverborn are indeed dead Primordials (beings who existed before the gods/celestines), they might be earlier manifestations of the Godhead which might stand with their still-living counterparts in Malfeas (or outside the universe, depending on what you believe) to defeat the gods.

    Then there wouldn't be much left for anyone to claim victory, since the Neverborn want the destruction of all reality. Except maybe Grandmother (and she's more a symbol of the Godhead's destructive side, rather than a distinct, conscious entity). The Godhead (perhaps itself a duality of Prime/Oblivion) wins in the end.

                    I think the problem with the scenario is that, if kept to their general characters, none of the groups would allow it to get to a battle royale.

                    Garou are used to being out-numbered by equal or stronger forces, and thus their tactics should be more guerilla warfare and ambushes than outright battles. They're wolves, after all, hunters and predators, not soldiers.

                    Vampires generally have infiltrated human society to a scary extent. They generally know how to manipulate not only their own resources, but that of the humans around them, as well as their underlings. Vampires are ideally the masters of long term planning. A Prince or Elder ought to be buying up land, having woods and libraries cleared away for other useful things, siccing the police and other mortal institutions onto their enemies, sending the lesser vampires out in packs, and so on.

                    Mages ought to be using their own resources, their chantries and alliances, gathering information and using carefully executed rituals to steal away or peck away at the Vampires' power bases and the Werewolves's caerns and resources.

                    Ultimately, I think the Mages would probably win, if only because Vampires have fairly severe liabilities once you get them cornered (or if you find their lairs during the day), and Werewolves generally lack the capacity to manipulate the human world and weapons that the other two groups do. But it'd be a long time before they get to the battle royale stage, if ever.

                    If somehow they did, I think my money would be on the Garou. Assuming that the Mages did not have time to prepare. If the Mages do have advanced warning, then Mages. But it wouldn't be pretty.

                                How many of each splat there are running around the WoD is really worth looking at as well. At the very least it gives an idea of starting numbers. And I know plenty of people disagree with canon populations and just add more of each supernatural type as necessary to make their chronicles interesting. That being said, what I remember from canon is (and these numbers I seem to remember being from various ST handbooks):

                                One of the Garou books placed their total numbers somewhere around 13,000. This shouldn't include Spirals which number about 3 tribes combined so another 3000 more at least. 16,000 total.
                                Mages are 1 in a million so about 6000 back then, closer to 7000 now (due to human population increasing by a billion).
                                Vampires are 1 in 100,000. 1/3 of which are kue-jin. So 60,000 total back then (40,000 cainites) and 70,000 now. That means about 46,000 vampires to start.

                                So Vampires are way, way ahead in numbers to start off a conflict like this. Not saying this will be a deciding factor but it will certainly help.

                                I can look through my books when I get home to cite sources for the numbers, should anyone really deem it necessary. Like I said, they are from memory, so if anyone have references for different numbers for the populations, by all means throw them up.

                                Also, at least according to one of the scenarios, just what's in the Revised books should count, and so I don't think Arch-Mages are even considered since I don't think they are covered in the book.
                                Another thought: if you do include the other Changing Breeds alongside the Garou, the outcome changes a lot I think (as does their numbers). As was stated, the Garou were only ever meant to be soldiers. Having the support Gaia meant for them makes all the difference.
