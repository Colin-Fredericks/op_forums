    Modern Age adapted gamelines

    01-02-2026, 04:51 AM
    T​​he idea I want to explore here is an alternate present for the World of Darkness, where the various Dark Ages gamelines end up persisting into there present with much less in the way of changes than what exists in the published modern World of Darkness.

    Dark Ages: Vampire and Dark Ages: Werewolf are, as far as I know, similar enough to Vampire: the Masquerade and Werewolf: the Apocalypse that I suspect that nothing needs to be done here.

    Dark Ages: Mage is where bigger changes start happening. In this alternate Modern Age: Mage setting, there wasn't an Ascension War so much as an Ascension Skirmish; and a detente was reached between the emerging technomantic factions and the mystic Fellowships before it escalated to a Sorcerer’s Crusade. As a result, the mages of the Modern Age are still members of Fellowships, and utilize Foundations and Pillars instead of Arete and Spheres. This requires coming up with many new Fellowships: at least one per Tradition that lacks a Fellowship in published material: Akashayana, Children of Knowledge, Hollow Ones, Ngoma, Sahajiya, Sisters of Hypollyta, Taftani, Templars, and Wu Lung, for starters. Fellowships needn't be limited to four Pillars each in the Modern Age; the high water mark would be the Order of Hermes with nine Forma (i.e., Pillars); but I figure that every extant Fellowship will have developed at least one of two new Pillars of their own over the centuries.
    Modern Age: Mage doesn't have a Technocracy; just an Order of Reason. At least seven technomantic Fellowships would be needed: five that roughly correspond to the default setting's Technocratic Conventions, plus counterparts for the Etherites and Virtual Adepts. I would also deprecate the Old Faith and the Spirit Talkers, and instead replace each with multiple cultural Fellowships like the Valdaermen.

    The trick to making this work would be assembling a toolkit for easily making more Foundations and Pillars, to go with the additional and expanded Fellowships.

    There was no Shattering, and Dark Ages: Fae has become Modern Age: Fae. For this, I've been considering borrowing a page from Changeling: the Lost 2e, and allowing for the Fae to organize themselves in ways other than Seasonal Courts, with different Court structures having different sets of Dominions.

    Changes like the Protestant Reformation still happen, and Dark Ages: Inquisitor gets replaced by Modern Age: the Faithful. This would be a hybrid of the aforementioned Dark Ages book and Hunter: the Reckoning, I have mechanics worked out for this; and I'll share them when I'm not quite so sleepy.

    Devil's Due evolves into Modern Age: Demon, otherwise largely unchanged. There might be something about integrating Fallen Houses and maybe some Lores into the book.

    That leaves only Wraith and Mummy without Modern Age versions, and maybe the possibility of adding other lines to the lineup. I have thoughts for both; though again, I'll have to come back to it once I'm awake. My thoughts for Wraith include integrating elements from Orpheus into it without either listing its identity (if possible); and my thoughts for Mummy would involve something more like 2e, but with a few elements from Resurrection and a bit of inspiration from Curse.

    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

          Take another look at my suggestion. Dark Ages: Inquisitor was set in an era in which there's no way that the Imbued would have existed. But it borrowed heavily from HtR concepts and mechanics to present an alternate take on the notion of the Faithful. That's what I'm aiming to update to a Modern Age setting.

          Start with Vampire-style mortals: that is, they track the Virtues of Conscience, Self-Control, and Courage as well as a Humanity-like trait (called Piety). Add to that three Superior Virtues: Faith (which extends Conscience), Wisdom (which extends Self-Control), and Zeal (which extends Courage). These are analogous to the HtR Virtues of Mercy, Vision, and Zeal, respectively. What I mean by "extends" is that they can't exceed the underlying Virtue, can always be added to dice pools based on the underlying Virtue, and deal with analogous concepts.

          The Inquisitor is able to use a Courage+Zeal pool to resist supernatural fear, and Self-Control+Wisdom to resist supernatural mental influence.

          Inquisitors also have a "fuel stat" called Conviction which they can generate by pursuing their Superior Virtues. They have a "Risking Conviction" mechanic where they can spend a point of Conviction to add to a dice pool, which they lose if they fall the roll but get back along with another point of Conviction if they succeed. If their Conviction ever exceeds their Piety, they enter a state of Callousness, where they become self-righteous in a manner in keeping with their Nature, and must roll a Conscience+Faith pool to resist their Callousness. Callousness is a sin and can cost you Piety; and loss of Piety can come with a Curse that can only be removed by an act of repentance.

          Pushing Conviction all the way up to 10 lets them trade it all in for a Blessing, a supernatural power that they can use thereafter by spending a point of Conviction to activate it.

          That, in a nutshell, is how Dark Ages: Inquisitor works. I trust you can see the parallels to HtR. For its Modern Age counterpart, I'd make a few changes, such as replacing DAI's Blessings with HtR's Edges, using Conscience+Faith in place of Mercy for the purpose of accessing Edges that require Mercy, Self-Control*+Wisdom in place of Vision for access to Edges that require Vision, and Courage+Zeal in place of Zeal for access to Edges that require Zeal. I've got an asterisk next to Self-Control above because I would replace Self-Control with Reason in the Modern Age counterpart, as a nod to the Bystanders rules in the Hunter Player's Guide, making it a Reason+Wisdom roll. (Note that that's not the same as using the Bystanders Virtues. I don't know why, but Bystanders use Reason in place of Conscience, not Self-Control.) I'd also rename Conviction as Devotion, as a nod to the Religious Devotion Merit found in Hunter Player's Guide and possibly allowing for Conviction to be used as a substitute for Conscience as it sometimes is with Vampiric Paths of Enlightenment. (I don't know if I actually want to go there; but I'd rather leave the door open.)

          And rather than having a single Piety score, I'm thinking of using something similar to Vampire's Roads/Paths of Enlightenment for this, with each Faithful having a track that is built around a particular faith instead of assuming that everyone follows the Catholic faith. Look at GURPS Monster Hunters 6: Holy Hunters for some ideas as to various Pillars of Faith that might be available to a Modern Day Faithful character.

          Finally, I'd supplement the Edges that can be acquired by buying them with Conviction with a version of the Freeform Edges system found in Hunter Player's Guide: if you want to use an Edge that you haven't purchased, but that you have the requisite Virtues for, and it's appropriate to the narrative, you can spend a point of Conviction and roll an appropriate Virtue+Superior Virtue pool to get access to that Edge for the rest of the current scene.

          This wouldn't be Hunter: the Reckoning any more than Modern Age Fae would be Changeling the Dreaming. But it would be roughly analogous.

              Vampire: I do think it would have serious differences. A lot of the current setting has to do with the aftermath of the Anarch Revolt and the Convention of Thorns. Significant changes to these events can and should lead to extreme changes in the larger, world-wide political landscape of vampires, and a complete overhaul of the Sects. In fact it's possible there would be no Sects, Princes would have more skirmishers for expanding Domains (probably with a lot more vassalage between cities), clan distribution would probably be quite different, and Roads would still exist in place of Paths. A default to Humanity could still be in place as a result from Enlightenment and other societal changes and movements, but there would be a lot more variety. The Salubri would still be around as a clan, and the Tremere would have a very different history, probably less powerful and possibly less tightly bound, maybe with more links back to Mages.

              Werewolf: can't say much, really, but I think it wouldn't change much. Most of the significant events in shaping the splat's society in the line are far older than the Dark Ages, even the White Howlers' fall. Some Gifts and Fetishes may change or just be added, some Camps will change, the Glass Walkers may have another name (Warders or something else), the numbers of some Fera may change along with their contact with the Garou. The biggest changes I can think of wouldn't actually affect the Garou directly: we could fiat the Ceilican back into existence, and the Rokea may have been spared the Bikini Atol Detonation in some way.

              Mage: I agree, and would also add that Paradox wouldn't necessarily exist, at least not as we know it.

              Fae: to be honest, this is a very, very complex point. As a fan of Changeling, the idea of playing by the rules of DA Fae isn't something I like, and the very canonicity of DA Fae is up in the air, so much did this book break with CtD. I may be wrong as I can only state from limited personal perspective, but as far as I could see, while DA Fae has a very supportive fanbase, most CtD fans just assume similar rules in place in the past to what we have today, just with less Banality in the world. That said, the setting would be quite different if the Shattering and the Interregnum didn't happen regardless of which rules you use. And without them, there would be no Resurgence and the associated war.

              Demon & Mummy: those two lines have something important in common, that is their defining event happening quite recently, already by the time of Revised. For all intents and purposes the rules for 1st and 2nd edition Mummies were already in place by the Dark Ages, as were what little society they had, and this was the state since ancient times and all the way through to the end of the 1990's. The same is true about Demons, with only one caveat: Devil's Due is based on VtM rules, so the differences are less about demons working differently back then and more about the rules assuming you're using it with Vampire, while with DtF you can simply use the core rules with very few changes. And just like Mummy, it assumes a situations similar to Devil's Due all the way to the Week of Nightmares in 1999. So those are lines that don't really get a lot of change besides a "change in edition" of sorts. Their societies during the 90's are already established and very similar to their DA counterparts.​

                  Vampire and Werewolf aren't really my thing, which is probably why I just skimmed over them. But you're right: a Modern Age Vampire where Paths of Enlightenment are commonplace, the Salubri are still around, and the Camarilla basically isn't, would very much be appropriate for this. And I was aware of this, too, as I've mentioned Roads/Paths of Enlightenment in both of my previous posts. Oops.

                  For Modern Age Mage, I'd probably borrow the idea from the latest Black Hand book and say that Backlashes are handled as counterproductive Magickal Effects determined by the Storyteller, rather than introducing an extensive set of rules specifically about how to resolve Backlash. And yeah; Paradox likely wouldn't be a thing, just like Avatars wouldn't be.

                  For Modern Age Fae, keep in mind that this is intended to be an alternate setting, one which is probably as similar to the World of Darkness as the Chronicles of Darkness is. I'm not meaning for any of this to be a knock against the World of Darkness gamelines. In particular, I'm also a fan of Changeling the Dreaming; but I've got the World of Darkness for that. The "Modern Age of Darkness" setting I'm thinking of is a "What if the modern age more closely resemble the Dark Ages setting, only more modern?" Not "let's improve on the World of Darkness by upgrading the gamelines to match the superior Dark Ages lines". Just different; no judgment intended on which is better.

                  With that said, one of the motivating factors here was exactly what you're commenting on with respect to Mummy and Demon, though it also applies to Hunter: these "latter-day" gamelines featured newcomers and/or radical changes to existing types as part of the "the end times are going into overdrive" notion that you mentioned — which makes them inappropriate to use in Chronicles where the end times aren't going into overdrive. Since Dark Ages had counterparts that didn't depend on the "latter days" plot…

                  You're right that Devil's Due was written in the context of Dark Ages Vampire; but then, all of the Dark Ages line did that: technically, every last one of them was written as a supplement for DAV, in that you were expected to own that book in order to use the others. For what it's worth, my focus here is specifically on the part about demons as player characters, not the sections on the Baali or the Red Circle.

                  But I'll disagree about Devil's Due being how demons worked right up until the time of Demon the Fallen: the Dark Ages book has a sidebar in it that makes the point that the Rite of Renunciation that allowed for the demons as described in that book only worked during the Dark Ages; it stopped working long before we get anywhere close to the present, preserving the "the Fallen are effectively newcomers" aspect of Demon the Fallen. That's why I mentioned that just keeping the Rite functional all the way up to the Modern Age should be enough. There are also at least as many differences between Devil's Due and Demon the Fallen as there are between Dark Ages Vampire and Vampire the Masquerade; that is, there's little that's drastically different on a conceptual level, but there are a lot of crunch changes: DD demons have Resolve rather than Faith, Arcana instead of Lores, and Sins instead of Houses, just to name three differences that pop out at me right away. The question I have is how much of the DtF stuff do I want to use in Modern Age Demon?

                  And upon reflection, my thoughts about Wraith have largely been the opposite of my thoughts on everything else, probably because there was no Dark Ages Wraith book. There's a thread over on the Wraith forum about how such a book ought to have looked like; and I could see extrapolating from that to a Modern Age Wraith where Chiron never went missing, the Guilds were never banned, the Fishers never turned out to be corrupt, and (possibly) the Dictum Mortuum either isn't a thing or isn't as heavily enforced. I'd still like to incorporate Orpheus-style elements into it (such as Projectors being a thing and Orpheus-style Shades being available to ghosts that have an uncommonly strong connection to the living); but that's harder to justify in the parameters for this thought experiment.

                  And yeah; Modern Age Mummy as I see it would basically be closer to Mummy 2e than to MtR. But I would like to borrow elements of the latter that can be separated from the "latter days" metaplot. And I noticed that the way the Ba rating works is not unlike how Sekhem works in Mummy the Curse, in that both are ticking clocks counting down toward a point where the Mummy will have to go away. Thus my musings about maybe borrowing some other ideas from MtC.

                  My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                      On Modern Age Fae I understood the idea, but my point is that many STs prefer to use modern rules and a different plot derived from CtD in the DA setting and thoroughly ignore DA Fae. It's just too different a game compared to any other DA line.

                      The point being, you have two distinct options here: you may figure out a DA Changeling to turn into Modern Age Changeling, or really keep to DA Fae, but then end up with the single most unique splat compared to regular WoD. For Changeling specifically, and considering how CtD later talked about the past basically ignoring that DA Fae ever existed, I don't think either is a more fitting choice for your exercise. You can even do both, treating each as a distinct Gallain.

                      I don't really remember Devil's Due well enough, can't say it's a book a like that much, but I'm not sure if the Rite of Renunciation is a big difference. The bigger changes in rules in this case are the ones on the Apocalyptic Form and the benefits of Pacts, IIRC. I'm open to hear about the differences I may be missing, but overall I see the distinct rules in Demon more like the differences in Vampire, in which they're more about being a distinct game/ruleset than their situation being too different from now (except for leaving Hell in vastly greater numbers). Personally I would use mostly DtF rules, with the relevant changes from DA, but otherwise I think it's more of a matter of taste. Of course, except for the whole political side and the Factions.

                      Mummy has an interesting distinction if you want to partially implement MtR without retconning Mummy 2nd Ed: while Demon happens because the Avatar Storm/Maelstrom/What-have-you broke the walls of Hell, MtR happens because the Spell of Life was restored and improved. And while this was done by Osiris in the metaplot, it's an old plot point that Kemintiri does have the complete original spell somewhere in her memory, so you don't really need an End Times plot to just have it appear at some point.
