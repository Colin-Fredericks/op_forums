                                                    Granted, I doubt WoD will get cancelled, if only because the fanbase is so small nobody actually cares.
                                                    I feel like that's assessment falls short-I can reliably get a lot of response about World of Darkness in just about any group that has a passing knowledge of roleplaying games. There're a lot of reasons why Paradox was convinced to get into World of Darkness as a property that could provide returns for them, and it's the fact that it still has a ubiquity amongst people interested in this sort of thing is one of those reasons. Hell, going back to anecdotal, not only do people know about World, but I keep hearing a lot of healthy traffic for playing the game.

                                                    What people usually aren't aware of is the history of the franchise after the 3 key points of 2004(the switch to Chronicles), 2009 (the end of the Chronicles Golden Age), and 2011 (The 20th Anniversary of Vampire: the Masquerade), with a general understanding of World being a past-tense production. A lot of people have heard buzz about 5E, but never got around to checking it out, probably because of all the other times something was going to happen with World but never manifested (most notable of which is the CCP MMO), and because a lot of what is new doesn't exactly have the glowing recommendation to get it competing with a now flooded market (comparatively). For most people, World of Darkness is at best something that people are still trying to get back, like any number of movies or games in development hell, and at worst still isn't producing anything.

                                                    In terms of cancellation, the size of the fanbase is probably one of the less relevant factors to it-the relevance of the property is probably the biggest thing, because basically nothing about recent attempts to get into everyone's attention have basically been so much wheel spinning. You can actually see this in how the backlash against the....let's be polite and call it negligence, the negligence behind the marketing and development of Vampire: the Masquerade 5E went. People got pissed off about a lot of what was done, on a lot of different points, but the response turned out to be more "well, this isn't going anywhere, back to what I was already doing" rather than "We need to kill this thing in the crib before it can get big", because basically no one who was against it thought it was going to be big.

                                                    A few other things also probably keep World of Darkness, on the whole, hard to summon the feeling and effort to cancel, one of the big ones being that World of Darkness has a lot of strong branding as an adult/mature game. Unlike a lot of other cirucmstances where something objectionable can turn people off and get them inflamed to find it, a lot of people know World handles dark and extreme themes well in advance, and doesn't try to sell itself for a younger audience-in fact, World has often been ridiculed for how seriously it takes itself for what can be understood as a teenage edgelord game, but with that it gets a lot of leeway in terms of what it can do. It doesn't hurt that, for some of their bigger flops, you have pieces like Charnel Houses of Shoal, and lot of their more extreme products were clearly marked as being No, Really, Not For Kids (I even remember tags on the wrap that detailed what was alarming about it). A lot of cancel culture stuff doesn't really come up for things that are aimed at their audiences appropriately (not that it doesn't happen, just not as much). It's hard to be mad about a game including a scene of rape if you know in advance you're going to see rape in it.
