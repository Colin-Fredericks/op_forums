    Ultimately though, you're just arguing from your own definitions. How can anyone argue if you're setting the terms with a definition that presupposes your position is correct? You're not defining "religion" and "magic" in a fashion that tries to describe what those are, you're defining them to get to the conclusion you want to get to; so of course your conclusion seems the best.

    A crucial issue is that religion is a belief system, while magic is an action based on a type of belief system. Religions are something you believe in, magic is something you do. When you start to say what acting in accordance with a religion means, or what belief systems result in people performing the act of magic, the line doesn't just get blurry, it disappears completely. You can talk all you want about how people don't focus on what True Faith demands of the faithful... but that's neglecting the demands of Paradigm in Mage just as much for how Awakened Magic works.

    It's really simple to try to say philosophy and computer programming as distinct and separate things, but it's making the same category error where the distinctions all come down to looking at different parts of the whole. Computer programming is doing something based on the principles of symbolic logic that derive from philosophy and the formulation of logical thought systems. They're not the same, in a sense, but "Christianity," and "having a baptism," aren't the same either in functionally identical fashion; and thus are parts of the same whole.

            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
            I got those definitions from Google; I didn't make them up myself.
            And that's a very bad place to take serious definitions from, as it will poorly summarize an extremely complex academic discussion, ignore the several conflicts between the views that are being mixed in this summary as if they were the same thing, all that in the best days, when it isn't hallucinating shit. By got from Google I bet you mean you got from Gemini (the AI doing those summaries), and both as someone who studied those concepts academically and as someone who studies LLMs now, I have to say that trusting Gemini/Google is a very problematic move.

            I know this distinction between Faith and Magic you described, and it is terribly out of context and severely butchered by conflations. More to the point, though, it isn't meant to apply to anything resembling what we're talking about even in this form. Long story short, this is about the distinction between what you believe in and how you apply this belief in your life, and by definition it's talking about two sides of the same system every time. It's basically saying that going to church is the religion side, praying to get a job is the magic side of this religion. There's a little more to it in the form of the work of Versnel, but that's the terribly out of context part (Wikipedia cites his definition for didactic purposes, but isn't done very well and ends up taking far more focus than it should given the very limited context in which it was used and what Versnel was really writing about).

            So it actually isn't a distinction between True Faith and Awakened Magic, because both are about believing in something and then deriving power from this belief. Paradigm and Faith are both "religion", spell effects and True Faith effects are both magic.

            There is a difference between them, but it isn't anything about what you believe in or how you believe in it. Instead, the difference is literally in the very nature of the person: Awakened Magic comes from someone capable at forcing reality to conform to their belief, while True Faith is an element of reality already. And that's also why Heavy Arms' defining it simply as "Numina" is precisely on point. True Faith is, like every Numina, a sort of power that normal people can have under the natural rules of the World of Darkness without ceasing to be normal people as far as it goes.

            Does True Faith requires morality? Arguably yes, but so does most Paradigms (arguably all of them, in a sense), it's just that Mages that change their minds also change their Magic, while normal people don't get this benefit. Anything else we say about them is either semantics or speculation.

            You shouldn't be so fast at stipulating the presence of gods as a defining trait, either. What even is a god? Many forms of Buddhism, Spiritism, some Shinto, Religious Taoism and Confucianism, are exactly deity-less religions. And while WoD's Order of Hermes is seriously agnostic/atheistic as it is based on Telema, most "hermetic" mysticism and magical practices through history is heavily based on the assumption that gods and their servants exist, including Christian hermeticism through the last two thousand years of European occultism. The very distinction between magic and religion as we understand it today only started to take form in the 18th century and really took it by the late 19th century, early 20th, only to be criticized by Anthropology already in the 30's.

            When we talk about True Faith and Awakened Magic in the context of the game, we're talking about very specific mechanics, and the metaphysics that fuel those mechanics pertain to the game, not to strict definitions on morality or the distinction between religion and magic. Awakened Magic comes from an Awakened person, the end. The nature of Awakening is definitely relevant, but isn't defined as exploring it and finding out is one of the elements of the game, yet we can say for certain that they're not normal people. True Faith comes from a perfectly normal person, with a quite abnormal level of trust in a given belief, that for unknown reasons resonate with the background supernatural forces of the universe, and lives accordingly, the end. Any further explanation is beyond the scope of the game except as a possible mystery to investigate.

            Not to say, as Heavy Arms already said, that it is a very bad rule in which it gives a set of requirements and effects meant to cover what it means to be faithful in any "religion", but it's actually only really fitting for a sample of Christian denominations, going from "kinda wonky" to "completely misses the point" when you go outside of this narrow set of religions. And even within it, it's still only fitting for some stories, mostly Vampire stories and a bit of exorcism stories, plus random gamefied effects tackled on to complete the power set.

                        To be fair, I didn't say that True Faith requires a god or pantheon; M20 did. Also, I never said that the definition I gave is the currently accepted one; only that I didn't make it up, contrary to Heavy Arms' claim, and that that definition does appear to be closer to the one that M20 is using.

                        Likewise, Book of Secrets doesn't appear to be using the religion/magic distinction that you're referring to in its writeup of Faithless: whether or not it's technically accurate, it's clearly assuming a distinction between religion and magic that's more like what I outlined. Because if it wasn't — if, in effect, religion is whatever your Paradigm is — it would be meaningless to say that the Faithless Flaw only comes into play when the mage's paradigm and practice is religious, like saying "this only applies if water is wet". And it explicitly calls out atheists as people who aren't subject to the Flaw, and speaks of needing a deep belief in a higher power before the Flaw can apply. It doesn't say that the gods or pantheons actually need to exist; only that the mage has to believe in them.

                        And both of these build on MRev's definitions of magic and True Faith, as described in the Revised Celestial Chorus Tradition Book:
                        The crux of a great divide … lies in the nature of True Faith versus magic. A Chorister performs miracles through the power of will, using tools that consecrate or create a bond with divinity. The Faithful perform miracles by becoming channels of some greater power. The lines do blur. A devout Chorister sees her will as simply an extension of the One. The major difference is that True Faith relies on the intervention of some outside agency, even if the faith itself springs from within. A Chorister could, conversely, be ultimately faithless and yet still work magic. Such a mage would be a sad creature indeed, since she would be hobbled by her belief that she can’t do magic until she regains her faith.

                        In game terms, both True Faith and magic allow for some phenomenal miracles, but True Faith is the less structured of the two systems. How, then, do you justify having both in play? What do you do if you have a character with both? Can a Chorister serve a greater power through True Faith and yet also have personal power?

                        And it then offers the following options:
                        • True Faith and magic co-exist: The usual World of Darkness style. True Faith is a channeling of some greater power through profound personal surrender. Magic is a channeling of personal power through personal will.
                        • True Faith and magic co-exist, but not in thesame person: You could argue that a Chorister cannot have True Faith because she imposes her will on Creation, while the Faithful instead are nothing more than agents of Creation who subsume their will in the Divine. In this model, no Chorister has True Faith. They have Awakened to personal power, and their service to the One is always a matter of using their own devices. The Faithful, in the end, are vessels who are directed where they must be.
                        • True Faith is Awakened magic: The Faithful are just Orphans! That is, if you decide to use this option. Doing so solves some neat little loopholes with metaphysics and makes the game a little more internally contiguous. On the other hand, this option also eliminates a wonderful little stumper — the real world doesn’t always make perfect sense, and neither does the game world. In this model, the Faithful just have some sort of proto-Awakened magic that allows them to perform limited feats. A faith healer, for instance, is really an Orphan with Life magic.
                        • There is no True Faith: God is dead, and so are His servants in this variant, so nobody has True Faith. Perhaps humans can’t know the Divine, and the Chorus is doomed. Perhaps Awakened magic is the only route to real human cosmic power. Perhaps the One does exist, but only in a distant form that does not interact with humanity.
                        • Choristers aren’t mages at all: This option is pretty far-flung, but you can rule that Choristers aren’t really mages. Instead, they are legions of the Faithful, with theurgists (sorcerers of holy magic) backing them up. In this variant, you still have to deal with the metaphysical nit-picking of Faith and magic in the same setting, but suddenly you make the Chorus take a very different direction in mage society. Can you train someone to have True Faith, even though you can’t train an Awakening? What are the limits of True Faith, and how does it compare to magic? What happens if anyone ever figures out that the Septarians are right and that the Traditions really are just heathen wizards?
                        Note that the first option explicitly describes the dichotomy that I mentioned: Awakened Magick is the channeling of personal power through personal will, and True Faith is channeling a greater power through profound personal surrender.

                        Not to say, as Heavy Arms already said, that it is a very bad rule in which it gives a set of requirements and effects meant to cover what it means to be faithful in any "religion", but it's actually only really fitting for a sample of Christian denominations, going from "kinda wonky" to "completely misses the point" when you go outside of this narrow set of religions.
                        Also from the Celestial Chorus Tradition Book:
                        Remember, exercises of Faith often follow the established sorts of miracles for the individual’s religion. A Christian might walk on water, turn water to wine or heal the sick. A Buddhist might float in the air, meditate without food for a year or become invisible. A Mithraite might breathe fire or make his skin as impermeable as iron.
                        In other words, True Faith isn't restricted to Christianity and Christian-adjacent religions. (And yes, I'm aware that Buddhism is an agnostic religion.) M20 goes so far as to say that a Nephandus can have True Faith in the monstrous beings that some Nephandi worship. That ranges from directly opposed (for those Nephandi that are also infernalists) to wildly askew (for the Malfeans and K'llasha).

                        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
