                            Originally posted by MarkK View Post
                            Where is that in C20?
                            In a lot of the write-ups of the Houses and the descriptions of the various kingdoms, duchies, etc. Yes, the overall broad-view of the modern era is that it's characterised by sidhe increasingly closing their freeholds to all because of increasing banality. It broadly describes them as jackasses, the same way their behaviour was described during the Accordance War in all editions of this game. However, I think the nuance you're looking for is present when you look at explanations and descriptions of specific areas, individuals and institutions - again, as it always was. If I recall a lot of the canon you cited in support of a view that allowed for more sympathetic Arcadian sidhe was supplemental material based on the histories of individual Houses. That's material that in many cases is still there in C20.

                            Examples:

                            House Danaan is described as having an Eshu as High Lord, and as being welcoming to commoners.

                            House Daireann are stated to retain their strict laws of hospitality and their freeholds (or cauldronhouses) are required to remain open to all as a result.

                            House Eiluned foresaw the Shattering and left members behind to watch over their holdings. Most of those members became Unseelie as a result, but this is a clear indication that there is a contingent of Autumn fae in House Eiluned, and that they did not wholesale abandon their charges.

                            House Fiona's entry notes that many of that house chose to stay behind during the Interregnum (including Fiona herself) and that they have one of the highest levels of ennobled commoners.

                            House Gwydion is still stated to have retreated on the orders of their High Lord and against the will of many individuals.

                            House Leanhaun's High Lord is said to be beloved by commoners because of her populist views in the Parliament of Dreams.

                            House Liam, of course, is now one of the houses that stayed behind en masse but in addition, it's entry notes that while they do not have as many holdings as other houses, sidhe swearing fealty to House Liam are among the most widespread - a commoner-friendly house is apparently one of the ones you are most likely to meet.

                            And, of course, House Scathach are still noted to have stayed in the Autumn world and are still said to welcome all kiths into their ranks.

                            To move on to specific geographical areas, again there are a lot of places where there are either sidhe supported by commoners, welcoming commoners to their freeholds, ennobled commoner rulers successfully working within the existing sidhe-centric framework, or just sidhe generally not being jackasses to everyone around them:

                            New York is ruled by a troll from House Fiona.

                            Connecticut is currently ruled by an elected council.

                            South Carolina is ruled by a Liam sidhe who challenges noble expectations and ideas about parentage, being born to Vietnamese immigrants, and he does not challenge the autonomous rule of the nearby Gullah fae.

                            Some of the rulers of Appalachia are described as being in active alliance with the local Nunnehi.

                            Duke Cormac of Virginia is described as having modern political ideals, which presumably implies a pro-commoner bent.

                            It's said that Queen Mary-Elizabeth of the Kingdom of Grass welcomes all changelings to her freehold as long as they forswear violence and abide by the rules of Concordia.

                            Chief Greyhawk (problematic descriptive language aside), ruler of the Kingdom of the Burning Sun seems to be moving away from traditional sidhe rule entirely in favour of following traditions aligned with the Nunnehi.

                            In Phoenix, Arizona there's a Baron who has built a think-tank of many different houses and kiths in an attempt to study emerging patterns in Kithain politics and work out the best way forward for the fae in the face of the oncoming winter - a more flexible and inclusive response than shutting down the freehold to all and hoarding glamour.

                            Tallahassee is ruled by a clurichaun duchess.

                            Queen Aeron of Pacifica is expressly stated to keep her halls open to all fae, commoner, noble or gallain, regardless of court affiliation.

                            And that's just Concordia. But it certainly seems to me like the book supports the notion that there is space for a variety of opinion and action on the part of the sidhe, and presumably also the commoners living under their rule.

                            Anyway, that's why I agree with zenten. C20 presents the sidhe as - broadly - closing ranks, panicking and shutting down their freeholds in the 2010s due to the fear that winter is imminent. However, there is obviously a wide range of individual behaviour and the house and geographical descriptions bear this out.

                                        But I'm not trying to buck the overall trend, I'm trying to show the setting makes it clear the trend is not monolithic; that it's perfectly in keeping with C20 to state that there's a measurable minority who disapprove - that's what the conversation was referring to when you made the "where does it say that?" comment.

                                        I raised the historical references because you referenced Tamlin the Fiddler keeping the gates open and Gwydion's forcibly ordered retreat as 2e examples of nuance. In that vein I felt Fiona staying and Gwydion's orders remaining canon were absolutely relevant, as is establishing that Autumn sidhe exist in houses other than Liam or Scathach, since that's a whole new type of sidhe with different relationships to commoners, and a different set of ethical concerns.

                                        The references to the courts of Aeron and Mary-Elizabeth are absolutely current. I'm not particularly interested in defending Chief Greyhawk as I agree that both the concept and the language around it are problematic, but he exists in the setting and whether he proves the terribleness of House Gwydion or not, it's a third kingdom that's ruled by someone who, presumably, won't be shutting doors in the face of anyone without noble blood.

                                        Similarly, you may have excellent reasons for being leery of Dice and House Danaan, but the setting provides a noble house led by a commoner, so that's probably one more group of sidhe with better than average relationships with commoners.

                                        Again, with all this, and with the geographical list generally, my main point is simply variety. The new Connecticut Commonwealth may be new but this is a setting where it can exist! As can the other examples.

                                        Finally the Accordance War may have happened 25 years before 2e, but a lot of the sidhe participants were still around thanks to time spent in freeholds and the Dreaming. War criminals were still in charge, painting it as ancient history isn't quite accurate. Even setting that aside, Fool's Luck saw commoners and nobles on the verge of war. This conflict isn't new to the setting, nor is portraying the sidhe as an invading, occupying force.

                                        I don't think 2e painted the sidhe as blanket amtagonists, nor do I think C20 does. Like redcaps or the Unseelie Court, it's easy to make them antagonists, but I would not assume a redcap or Unseelie courtier I met in play was a villain, and the same goes for the sidhe. The setting, as written, provides plenty of options if you want to play a heroic sidhe unloathed by commoners. It's just also honest that a good portion of them see no moral issue with forcing people to adhere to the feudal system, violently if necessary - and it has always been honest about this.
