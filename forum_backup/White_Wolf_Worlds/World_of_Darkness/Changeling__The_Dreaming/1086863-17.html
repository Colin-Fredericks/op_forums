                                        Originally posted by CameraObscura83 View Post
                                        see i am not getting the unseelie = bad seelie = good vibe... maybe because im hyper fixated on the Sidhe issue

                                        The sidhe issue is one that I'm focused on, as well. Maybe it is a sign of our current climate, but more than ever they seem like The Establishment. The commoners have a very nuanced relationship and place within the Dreaming, and I was hoping that the sidhe would get a more explicit nuanced presentation (because we can infer or extract interpretation based on our preferences all day long). For instance, I've always loved the idea that sidhe are dreams of power and grandeur made flesh; they are literally the fairy princes and princesses little boys and girls dream of growing up to be. The Dreaming created them to rule, so if they do not rule, they are betraying the Dreaming. I think it creates a more complex relationship with the Dreaming and with the commoners. Sure, I wholly accept that there are sidhe out there who are merely entitled brats with megalomania, but considering that the idea of nobility is literally written into the fabric of the Dreaming (supported by mechanics e.g. Fae 2, Sovereign and its relationship to Title) I would have loved more representation of Noblesse Oblige and the burden of leadership and the responsibilities of service to those you rule.

                                                    This is a good time to bring up something that's bothered me about authority and leadership in RPGs in general.

                                                    Being in charge often means you have no choice but to give a certain order, or that you cannot give a certain order. Magic or not, you need to think about how you keep charge of your followers. "Never give an order you know won't be obeyed" and its corollaries just aren't portrayed well enough.

                                                    If you're leading troops through hostile territory, get ambushed and your troops scatter and run, one of your best choices is to shout "Take Cover!" - sure, you had a complete breakdown of discipline, but after the encounter you can restore order among troops who jumped the gun a lot easier than among troops who outright betrayed you en masse.

                                                    I'm not sure how you'd represent this in an RPG but I'm sure someone has an idea how to tie this in to fae nobility.
