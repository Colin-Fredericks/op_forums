    'Fixing' changeling

    09-29-2014, 03:12 PM
    I love changeling. I really do. But as written, it's quite difficult to explain to new people (What do you mean two souls?), the powers are kinda flat (So I get a facelift and forget I'm a fairy?), gathering glamour is really difficult (JUST BE INSPIRED) and the dreaming is impossible to explain (What do you mean I see both?). With that in mind, what ideas have people got for streamlining changeling, making glamour less difficult to get (not even easy, just less painful), making cantrips more equal to gifts/disciplines and making it easier on first timers?

    -Failure is always an option

      I don't play Changeling so without knowing the exact rules I offer doing something like a hybrid of Gnosis and Willpower. To regain Glamour have them choose a craft at character creation. It should be appropriate to their character concept and require ST approval.

          I kinda feel like Changeling, more than the other lines, needs more reading to get into. I had less of a problem with the mechanics, and more trouble just getting myself into the setting. The (1st edition) core book I have puts a huge emphasis on establishing tone, but spends less time showing you what the world is actually like. The time spent on tone is lovely! It's a huge draw, and it really sets the line apart from others...but I think it just needs more time spent talking about what everyday life is like for PCs. Everyday life is key! You can extrapolate grand quests from everyday life, but not vice versa. Writing about epic showdowns and glorious battles is all well and good but that's not what seems lacking.

          I didn't have too bad of a time with some of the concepts you mention, like the dual natures...the bigger problem I had is that, whenever we tried getting a game together, all the players were real excited to play their characters, but the GMs didn't have much idea what the world was going to look like around them. PCs were well established but they didn't know how to act within the world.

          I'd say just more stories. Pages and pages of them even, and not "example of gameplay" with dice rolling examples. Whenever there was a big story in a Changeling book I came out of it with a better sense of both setting and mechanics and what I was supposed to "do". Actually, I think the prose did help with the existing-in-both-worlds thing, and probably stories dealing with inspiring glamour, or things like how that "freeholds are entirely in the Dreaming even though you can just walk in from the street" thing works, would help a lot.

          Lines like Werewolf are easy to get into without knowing what you're doing. Okay I don't know how a sept is really put together well let's throw you at an evil oil refinery and there's ugly things attacking you. Even then, understanding the Umbra just isn't possible without solid fiction written in it, not just gameplay examples. It helps that Werewolf is kinda "pop culture werewolves plus a spirit world". Changeling on the other hand feels like... "okay, watch Neverwhere, except you live in the real world and the Dreaming at the same time, and there's Glamour, and it's usually less terrifying and more wondrous, and in any case you usually have different thing you're afraid of and you spend your time completely differently"--it's more steps apart from what we are already used to.

          So cram more prose into the book, and if people complain "Changeling has too much writing and not enough crunch," just nod your head and say "well yeah it's Changeling, that's what it's like".

              I don't think CtD needs more prose. It needs better prose. It definitely suffered worse than the other game lines for having purple prose over useful prose.

              It is is, frankly, a game. If it needs more prose to communicate itself (and it has plenty) something is kind of wrong with it. Games are meant to be played, not just read and put on a shelf. It is there for us to tell stories with, not to be passive about.

              And no, the mechanical problems with Changeling are not fixed by trying to explain them more. There are definitely concepts which need better writing (Banality and the two-worlds thing), but it is still a case of quality not quantity. If the concepts cannot be adequately communicated in the book the size of CtD's core book (either) than the concepts need to be reexamined to see if they're too much concept and not enough RPG tool. Changeling should not be defended ala Chinatown. For example M20 did actually address tightening up the prose on core concepts (the new Focus model vs. the old paradigm/foci model) and getting them across more effectively. They didn't leave it as just, "well that's how Mage is," and M20 seems to be better it (at least from the Quickstart).

              There are also mechanics that need work. There can be (and has been) debate on what they should be based on what different people want out of the game, but that doesn't change the fact that the current rules have lots of areas where the results are not what the books aim for.

                  I think the big problem is that it's not that intuitive because of a disconnect between the faerie tales people know and how it relates to the society and character creation in the setting. Another thing is that while vampire, werewolf, and mage can allow people to live vicariously through their cool characters with powers beyond mortal ken (even if at a price), it's much harder for people to see how being a changeling can do that. I can explain the other lines in one or two sentences, and people will want to play. Changeling seems to require an hour long discussion which cause most people to just bail out and never try it.

                  1) Make it easier for people to mirror actual myth and legend. Too often, Changeling made it hard for people to determine how to duplicate faerie tale character X in the game if they were inspired by it; a related problem is that instead of using the Kiths as broad archetypes, they increasingly started assigning very specific Kiths to do it. The game would have benefited by giving actual examples of real faerie tales and what Kith would fit. One can figure that out on one's own, but it should be much easier if you want to bring in new players into the game. I still have trouble figuring out what the hell an Eshu is supposed to be in terms of real life faerie tales. There is no reason why so many partial animal faeries have their own splat, Pooka should be enough except they defined it too strictly as "trickster" so all the other animal type faeries (huldras, selkies, swanmays) are excluded.

                  2) It is hard for new players to know what the hell their character is supposed to do. It is much more apparent in other game lines because people know how vampires, werewolves, etc. work as protagonists in fiction, and they can easily imagine "What would I do if were Lestat, Larry Talbot, Jim Dresden, etc.". On the other hand, faerie tales weren't written with the idea people would identify with the faeries. Instead, we have Narnia or some of the works of Neil Gaiman or Mervyn Peake. However, while people can understand that you are playing the role of Lucy, Peter, Edmund, etc. in Narnia, it's much harder to figure why they should do so rather than just play D&D. CtD needs to do a much better job leading new players to the water.

                  3) The corebook lacked much of the setting conflict that was established in supplemental material. So once again it is harder for new players (and STs) to decide how a chronicle should look, what hooks they could use, etc.

                  4) Do a better job integrating the real world and real people with the fantastic of the setting. Too much essentially takes players out of the real world into its own corner rather than integrating it. Once things become too divorced from normality, a lot of players will decide they'd rather just play an outright fantasy campaign.

                  I actually think that along the entire game line, these elements are in there. They just need to presented in their entirety and integrated much better in any new corebook.

                  ​I speak as someone who has looked at the line repeatedly, but always decided to not play it.

                              I think Heavy Arms and Black Fox have good points. It's not that C:tD needs more prose, it's that it needs better prose, As well as the rules not being very intuitive.

                              When Wendell_Burke and I had a chance to discuss some of the mechanics, it became clear that Changeling does seem to have everything one would need to play the game well in the main core book (2nd ed), however it's just written very haphazardly. All the gems to explain certain abilities or mechanics lie buried in other supplements. What the Changeling: the Dreaming core book really needs is for those refinements and exposition that explains gameplay best, to be consolidated into one source. I still have trouble understanding Changeling. Apparently that clears up according to other long time changeling players> I've been told time and again that if I don't "get it" I just haven't read the entire series of books enough times to figure out how it all fits together.

                              There's lots of other threads debating ways changeling could be improved and things we hope Changeling the 20th Anniversary edition will grant us. However I think the best we can do to make those changes happen, is to discuss aspects of Changeling's system in depth in as many different ways as possible, so that the developers can read these threads and see just where the issues lie. It shouldn't be a lambasting of the system, but a thorough dissection and critique of what mechanic works, what writing style needs to explain better, where the contradictions in the rules sit forgotten. It isn't enough to say Changeling needs Fixing, we'll have to roll up our sleeves and start examining every word, getting down to the nitty gritty so the way to 'fixing' Changeling can be made clear to those doing the research and the writing can see what we, the community think makes the most sense.

                                  I, for one, found C:tD to be my first love in the World of Darkness. It sparked me being a huge fan of the Fair Folk in Exalted, just due to the tenuous link in backstory.

                                  However, mechanically, not only is it clunky and poorly organized, but honestly, I don't think the people who wrote the character creation rules read the rules for how to actually use the powers. Glamour is too hard to gather, the realms require far too much investment, and the arts are...well, kind-of narrow. That last is more an artifact of how most WoD splats were handled, and wouldn't be a problem on its own. But the Realms... it was nearly impossible to have enough to be able to use your powers on much of anything.

                                  Strangely (for those who know me), I don't have a broad proposal for fixing the problems like how hard Glamour-management is. Realms...I like the concept. Honestly, that might be as easy as just giving more dots of them.

                                  Tone-wise, it's very cool. There was a time I was unsure whether you were really playing anything but a guy who shared his hallucinations with others, because I couldn't tell if the Changelings could really do much supernatural in the real world. But re-examination said they theoretically could. Just, you know, not with the resources given as written.

                                  I think C20 would be stronger than its predecessor if the Dreaming had more impact on the real world, and had a surreal, haunting influence that reaches out to touch the other supernatural creatures. Make them somehow...feel that a Changeling is bringing a lot of invisible power to bear. So using Auspex or Mage Sight or werewolf senses to perceive the Dreaming, when appropriate, is both chilling (suddenly, how much threat Changelings pose might be visible), and mildly necessary (so the threat isn't invisible as well as present).

                                  That said, Changelinges have always been the most fragile of the splats, and I'm not sure I'd like to see that change. But it'd be nice to have a solid indication that the dual worlds are there for more than just them, and that their ability to draw on the Dreaming makes them serious players in the supernatural world. Tying the mundane world to the Dreaming's reflections is how you render them vulnerable in both: they're just mortals, in the World of Darkness, but the World of Darkness impacts the Dreaming in strange and terrible ways.

                                  Sorry, mostly rambling. I love the splat, and it is the anniversary edition I am looking forward to the most. I hope it's fully playable, this time!

                                                      Dark Ages: Fae is basically NWoD Changling before it came out. DA:Fae inspired a lot of the NWoD and Exalted stuff so I'm told. It's a very well done set of rules that are way more fun to play than Changling and fit the World of Darkness far more.

                                                      Basically, Fae in it are monsters of old and are utterly inhuman. They are weird and strange, and make contracts with humans. The humans are slowly eroding their world with their static concepts and the Fae are being driven into hiding. Fae are immortal and some predate time, and time itself is fickle to a Fae. So very fickle. They don't perceive it at a constant rate as a human does. Your character is always at constant threat of gaining new weaknesses too. So yeah, it's pretty fun and way more WoD-y than CtD. I always recommend it to ST's for making Fae over CtD.

                                                      Good luck finding a hard copy, the book was a really small batch when released and as Ian W will tell you, it was rare as all hell afterwards ($70 for the $20 book was a deal after launch). As always, the Dark Ages line makes the way better rules and fluff than the original modern nights stuff, but they require patching for a Modern game if you want to use them.

                                                          While the DA Revised is, to me, still a better base rule set than even the 20th books so far, I'm not sure the individual supernatural power mechanics are entirely good. The DAF rules being better than CtD isn't saying that much, and there are some pretty serious issues you can run into. Something that DA Mage and DA Fae did was compress things down to extremely small number of traits (in fact, both use four 1-5 traits to cover pretty much everything) which can be a bit of a pain to run. Both games have some very strong points in them. The whole Echoes deal where the Fae are weak to various folklore wards against them is one of the best weakness setups in the cWOD... but at the same time it relies a large amount on the ST and players to define them; and the guidance could be better.

                                                          DA Mage and DA Fae both suffer a lot from not being "new user" friendly. Despite smooth rules, they rely a bit too heavily on people used to pre-existing games (esp. Mage) to work their best.
