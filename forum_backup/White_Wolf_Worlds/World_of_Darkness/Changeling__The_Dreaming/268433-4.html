        Originally posted by ganonso View Post

        Would that counts as "fixing" at least a bag of issues with Dreaming?
        At that point, you're turning Banality into Changeling: The Lost's Clarity. The primary theme of Changeling: The Dreaming isn't holding on to your sanity. That's Lost's territory. Changeling: The Dreaming is about facing the death of dreams and wonder - the slow erosion of your vital spark.

        Changeling's biggest problem with how it presented Banality was inconsistency. It shows in this thread. It all depends on which sections of which books you read to come away thinking that science somehow equals Banality. In other places, the core book stresses there is nothing inherently banal about science. That's certainly the take I run with, and the game's backstory backs that up.

        Woodstock and Apollo 11's moon landing happened less than a month apart. Woodstock, despite its place in popular culture, is rarely talked about in Changeling. Apollo 11, one of the greatest scientific and engineering achievements of the 20th century, is explicitly the catalyst causing such a massive upsurge in glamour in the world that even the gates to Arcadia opened briefly once more.

        This isn't to say science can't be banal, but so can painting or music composition. It's all about the approach taken not the activity itself.

                                One thing that always bugged me was the association of Banality with "growing up." Sure I get where it's coming from, but it needs to be developed more. The burdens of adulthood absolutely have the potential to crush someone's spirit, but holding down a job shouldn't be inherently banal. Living in the basement, smoking pot and playing video games all day carries just as much danger of Banality as working in a cubicle. To me Banality represents the point at which something has stopped growing, stopped being dynamic, and has started decaying. Banality is defeat and stagnation.

                                With that in mind I would propose reworking how the Seemings operate. Decouple them somewhat from a hard chronological age and link them to one's ability to regain Glamour and withstand the press of Banality.

                                As a Childling everything carries with it a certain sense of wonder, Glamour is easy to find but you find yourself easily hurt and frustrated by the cold realities of life, making you vulnerable to Banality.

                                As a Wilder you're trapped in a half-step. You've tasted bitterness and compromise but the struggle is what you live for. Glamour takes a little more work, but that just makes it sweeter. Banality still stings, but what doesn't kill you only makes your stranger.

                                Becoming a Grump is hard. Some have even likened it to a second Chrysalis. Those that survive though are stronger for it. You've learned how to balance the responsibilities of your "normal" life and your Changeling life. You've accepted what you cannot change and your skin is thicker against the ravages of Banality. Unfortunately this cynicism often makes it harder to feel the pure rush of the Dreaming, and Glamour is more difficult to come by.

                                        I'm not keen on the suggestions to 'fix' the game which ultimately boil down to 'turn it into DA:F20' or 'make it CtL'. If the game were vastly overhauled, I don't think it would be in the *20 spirit. V20, W20 and M20 are all 'love letters' to their fans. Making sweeping changes to CtD that would make it another game wouldn't be honouring its fans, but instead trying to please people who didn't like the game in the first place. So I really wouldn't buy C20 if it was a vastly different game. I currently ST one game of CtD and play in another, and while it needed a Revised Edition, it wasn't as hugely broken as some make out. It requires house rules and a few tweaks, but it does work.

                                        I like that you can play urban fantasy of the sort that Charles De Lint writes, but also steampunk adventure and 'D&D in the City', if that's what you really want. It's great for the kind of metro-mythic setting of China Mieville's King Rat, Jeff Noon's Vurt (if the trips were journeys into the Dreaming and the posthuman stuff were merely chimerical reality), Pan's Labyrinth, and Gaiman's Neverwhere and The Sandman.

                                        That said, there are some recurring issues that should be addressed:

                                        1. Banality should be dealt with more consistently, and in a more nuanced way. It needs to be more than 'science' or 'mundanity'. We also need some explanation as to its exact nature if, as with the Dauntain, it actually seems to be a kind of 'dark magic' as well as the crushing weight of shattered dreams. It's not Paradox. Perhaps it's just another kind of magic--a paradigm antithetical to that of the Dreaming. That would be more interesting, because it allows for Banality to be both of the facets we've seen so far. But then, we need to learn more about its origins and limitations.

                                        2. Linked to the above, we probably should de-couple age and Banality. The Seemings should be more metaphysical phases than fixed age ranges. It doesn't make sense that someone hits 25 and then suddenly becomes a Grump. But an 80-year old, hardened changeling who's battled Banality and been scarred by it? Yeah, okay.

                                        3. Realms can be limiting, although I like the concept of them. There are a few solutions. You could provide more dots for starting characters (even starting with 7 instead of 5 would make a difference--although 10 would be too many). But I think it's actually the Arts that are a bigger problem. It's the combination of a limited power (Art) and a further limiting Realm that makes cantrips too narrow. The system is otherwise not as difficult as it seems. I'd probably broaden the powers more, or make them cheaper and offer more alternate level powers. An even simpler option would be to bring in an optional rule to have 'Arts' as freeform powers limited only by Realms. They'd be like Spheres, but with much narrower focus (Wayfare is travel, Sovereign is command, Primal is enhancing the body with nature). You'd have to bump up their cost, though, or maybe require Glamour for all cantrips.

                                        4. In my own experience, Glamour's about as easy to regain as Pathos. It can be more or less scarce depending on how much dross the ST brings into the game and how many freeholds there are in a city. Unlike other critters, changelings can cast chimerical cantrips without spending any Glamour at all, if they want. This means you can get away with casting lots of cantrips in all-fae company without spending any Glamour at all. I very rarely spend Glamour when playing a changeling character. But making it easier isn't a bad idea, either. It allows for characters without easy access to a freehold to regain Glamour much more easily. I'd just let them draw directly from people's dreams like in the BFG.

                                        5. Mood. I expect vast changes would just see something similar to the Edition Wars that plagued Mage. But showing the darker side of the setting wouldn't go amiss. The fluff and artwork didn't help, since everything featured dragons and bunny rabbits and balloons. There's no reason why chimerae wouldn't take more urban forms--giant king rats, concrete golems, dragons made of car parts. There's no reason why the pervasive ambience of the World of Darkness shouldn't be expressed even in the Near Dreaming as whirling, delirious storms and shuddering swarms of chittering flies. It's always made sense to me. Dreams aren't always candyfloss and unicorns; they come from the id and are full of perversion, repression and neurosis.

                                        Not mentioned before, but also something of an issue, is healing. You need Primal 4 just to heal damage using magic. This means that many players just take the Regeneration Merit, which is conversely quite powerful. Meanwhile, wraiths and vampires just spend a point of Pathos/blood to heal a level of normal damage. I feel like the Holly Strike and Heather Balm powers should be split, putting the healing power at a lower level and keeping the attack as Primal 4. Alternatively, they could just allow changelings to spend Glamour to heal themselves (or at least their fae miens).

                                        In terms of the 'dual vision' issue some people have had (it's never really been an issue for me), it's always occurred to me that you can go the route Nancy A. Collins uses for her Pretenders in the Sonja Blue series: changelings have to 'shift' their vision into the chimerical spectrum, as it were, but they can do this without spending Glamour. It would bring their supernatural vision in line with the Deathsight/Lifesight powers of wraiths, and prevent things getting too confusing for players who just can't envisage two realities at once (which, if you think about it, would give most changelings a really bad headache). In-game this could be an effect of the Reckoning/the thickening of the Gauntlet/the increased presence of Banality.

                                        I don't want a radically different CtD for C20. I just want one with better rules and more suggestions for how we can play the game in a variety of ways.

                                            I've been considering reworking the realms myself. the descriptions in 2E feel rather arbitrary and random to me. I've been toying with the idea that each dot covers a level of familiarity and intimacy with the phenomenon in question. in regards

                                            *- intimately familiar you see and interact with the target everyday and know it well. like a loved one you live with, a favored tool, a tree you climb all the time, a mortal enemy or your own magic spells.
                                            **-familiar- you see and interact on a fairly regular basis, you know its name but don't know it intimately. a co worker, a good friend, a chimera who lives in your neighborhood.
                                            ***-passer by: you see it often but rarely interact with it beyond the most superficial ways. the guy you occasionally make small talk with in the elevator, the office building that you run errands to on a semi regular basis but don't work in. the fast food place you stop by on occasion.
                                            ****-the familiar stranger- people and things you have some familiarity with but don't really know beyond the most superficial level. the guy you see on your walks but don't know his name, the news anchor you watch every night but never met. the lake you've passed on your way to work but never dipped your feet into. an enemy's loyal henchmen you've punched a few times.
                                            *****site unseen. you've never seen or interacted with this person or phenomenon in any shape or form before.

                                            thoughts?

                                                        Step 1 - Solve the game's Tone problem - Changelings are happy, bright creatures fading away from existence. The book's colorful and happy, but the game's themes are dark as hell. Either pick a direction, or give the game a better focus ON THIS DICHOTOMY.

                                                        Currently, Dreaming mumbles quietly to itself what its' theme should be. Instead, the game's whole book should scream "Do Not Go Gently Into The Quiet of the Night. Rage, Rage, Against the Dying of the Light."

                                                        Step 2 - banality is the mechanic by which the fae are doomed. If it's a gaeas preventing them from changing, that's on thing, but it would work much better with one change - Instead of things that are tied to the mundanity of the world, tie it to what it should have been to begin with - The Opposite of hope. Banality should be the chains that hold you to the mundane life, that keep you from making dreams reality. IT should NOT be something that penalizes you for using technology to make your dreams happen, or from touching vampires (especially when you're slaying them.) Giving in to despair, being held down by the world around you - these are the things that should give banality, not Virtual Adepts' constructed realms or a trip through the underworld (a magical experience, even if it is depressing).

                                                        Step 3 - Include the dreams of the modern world. Rocket ships, Aliens, superheroes, Hollywood Glamour and Bollywood madness, Cowboys and Indians, Spy games and Heist thrillers. If you're going to feed off dreams, have some dreams that are more common for the common man than Kings and Crowns.

                                                        Step 4 - Cut the giggly cartoon aspect. IT has its' place, and it's a great way to get people to underestimate you. People forget you have the ability to violate physics if you make them laugh. Rewrite the powers to strip out some of the Merry Melodies antics.

                                                        Step 5 -Solve the Chimerical problem - Don't Let Changelings hide in the emperor's new clothes. Changelings that are physically different need to be physically different. your "Seeming" should be a physical change visible to all. A Fae form is the true form, a human shape the banal mask that you hide behind. You can hide mystical artifacts as mundane crap, but doing so locks away their power. By removing the chimerical aspect of these things, you solve the problem of the Troll in the Doorway - In troll form, he ducks. In Mortal form, he's already taken the banality hit. Illusions are one thing. "IT's This, but it's really this, But it's this to humans, But itt's THIS to mages." is needless complexity.

                                                        Step 6 - Make the Seelie/Unseelie thing something less of a binary and more of a pair of lines you have to cross. This isn't DnD, this is the world of darkness. Why is there no shade of Grey?

                                                        Step 7 - More Alien-ness, more Other-worldlyness. I'm not talking about chimerical garbage, I'm talking about the fact that you're the soul of an N-dimensional being grafted onto a human body and restricted to existing in 4.5 dimensions (you can only move in one direction in Time).

                                                        Step 8 - Find out what happened to the soul you replaced, since you're a changeling.

                                                        Step 9 - Relegate fae courts' current structure to a minority and/or the past. For a game about creatures of dynamism, it's static as hell. Feudalism is for Vampires.
