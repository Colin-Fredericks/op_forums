                                                Nighthawks


                                                There have always been those who lived out of step with society. For some, this has been literal, or rather, chronological: people who walked at night and slept during the day, when others were active. Historically, these were usually people fulfilling a role: guards, servants, parents of newborns. Some few were the odd and unusual, the nightowls and dreamers, star gazers and shamans. In the World of Darkness there would have been more of these, particularly among certain groups such as shifters, mages, and vampires.

                                                The modern era has seen a marked rise in such people, though. The advent of electricity has allowed more and more people to stay up all night, and lead to all manner of sleeping issues. At present as many as one in eight people work the third shift in some countries, and then there are those who are up all night partying, gaming, reading, binge-watching...

                                                The Nighthawks arise from their dreams, from those who walk a world others do not get to see, who sleep when other are awake or stagger through life in a drowsy daze. They were once chimera, named by an observant Sluagh for the famous Edward Hopper 1942 painting depicting a handful of people in an all-night cafe in an otherwise abandoned cityscape. They have been called many other things, too: Nightwalkers, Red-Eyes, Dreamweavers, the Sleepless. Like the Wolpertinger, they gained a whole new life during the Resurgence, turning from chimera to a full-fledged Kith. At the time they were still fairly rare, but over time they've grown in number, particuarly in nations and areas where third-shift workers have become far more common. This can be as much the third world as the first world, thanks to the arrival of large factories, mines, and other businesses that operate at night.

                                                For their part, the Nighthawks find all of the world to be a fascinating place. Their mindset is in ways both alien and familiar, as they often look at things around them with new eyes, in the way that the night time world looks so very different to those new to it, or the way that the daytime world can look strange to those who are. They've become particularly associated with the Chicanery cantrip of Dream Logic, often stating that it feels to them very familiar to their own existence.

                                                Nighthawks tend to favor being active when others are not; night time, early mornings, weekends, holidays. They are natural contrarians on a certain level. It is not that they are anti-social, but more that they feel a distinctive affinity for these between times; some find them to be comforting, others a thrilling taboo, others marvelous secret to share only those a select few. They likewise are often drawn to places others avoid, whether all of the time or at certain times. One infamous Nighthawk lived in the subway stations in New York City. He would vanish into bathrooms and storage rooms and other niches when they are busy, venturing out between trains, during the off hours, when he would gladly talk to the few who might be about. He had a habit of bestowing small blessings and aid to those he found, particularly regulars such as street musicians or janitors.

                                                They often take jobs that allow them to wander such places, or similiar work that lets them go to places and times between. Jobs with odd hours and shifts are the most obvious options, but there are others. Some find that traveling lets them do so, as they race between time zones and cities, facing new experiences that those more bound to one location won't understand. Jobs that allow for long distance travel swiftly, such as trucker, train operator, or flying or even working on airlines have often held appeal to them. Interestingly, places like oil rigs, deep sea trawlers, light houses, and weather stations have been known to attract them for similar reasons. Ultimately, they like the between times and places.

                                                Appearance: The fae mien of the Nighthawks does not appear to be all there; some say this is because they started out as that moving shadow or strange reflection in the corner of your eye. They're just a little translucent, as is anything they wear or carry for very long, though, thankfully, anything ingested remains hidden. Their skin tends to be an odd hue, just a step off their human form: olive might become gold, dark brown might become dark red. Most notable are their eyes: solidly one color save for pupils, the most frequent hue being, unsurprisingly, red. Dark shades of purple, blue, and green have also been seen, but even among these their eyes turn red when particularly emotional.

                                                Lifestyles: Nighthawks tend to not like crowds; they are not anti-social, but they prefer to deal with people in smaller numbers, and find a certain beauty to a world less cluttered. Their unique perspectives tend to endear them more to misfits and outsiders, who may not understand them any better but at least appreciate them usually. Others may find them anywhere from quirky to strange to unsettling, depending. As noted, they tend to take jobs that allow them to live on the fringe of society, either physically or chronologically, and sometimes both. They tend not to be afraid of approaching total strangers with their stray observations, though. They often come across as a bit naive and dreamy, and many have been mistaken as drunk or mentally unsound or, in this more modern age, potheads or addicts. The difference between Seelie and Unseelie among Nighthawks is subtle; Seelie are prone to more traditional jobs at odd hours or places, while the Unseelie are more likely to take stranger jobs that let them work more outside the norm, frequently with no set hours. Jobs as artists and writers and photographers might especially appeal, often rooted in childhood or teenage hobbies.

                                                Childling: These are the kids who stay up late, reading, sneaking out to play with friends or downstairs to watch TV or play video games. They wander the great and mysterious world of Night, which is so very magical and frightening at once to children. Even before they awaken, they are apt to do this.

                                                Wilder: Wilders are still sneaking out, still cheating on curfews. As they grow older, they start to truly discover the beauty of strange places and times. Some become prone to a particular artform that they may pursue into a career that lets them follow their unusual proclivities: photography, writing, painting. For others, this remains just a hobby, part of their bizarre charm.

                                                Grump: A grump Nighthawk is a truly strange bird to many. They tend to become more withdrawn or less coherent, and wind up viewed as the neighborhood hermit or oddball. Those prone to solitary jobs often become even more of a loner, while those used to working with others become that weird guy who pops out of nowhere and nobody can understand. Many are so lost in their own skewed view of the world that they hardly understand others. Sadly, quite a few are believed to have dimentia or other mental illnesses and wind up 'treated' for them if not protected by fellow Changelings.

                                                Affinity: Scene

                                                Revelry: Nighthawks find revelry in discovering beauty in strange places and things. In stalking an empty mall or quiet cafeteria. In wandering a forgotten church or famous tourist destination during the off hours. And, most of all, in sharing their perspective with the few whose paths they might cross, whether they are understood or not.

                                                Unleashing: Strange shadows or reflections spotted just out of the corner of one's eye lend creedence to the theory that this is how Nighthawks started out. Sounds seem to echo as through an empty hall, and lights to dim. Odors are muted, just the faint remnants of scents many hours old, faded. Sometimes images become fuzzy, as if seen through a heat haze or blurry eyes, and the urge to yawn strikes many.

                                                Birthrights
                                                Untiring - Nighthawks never get tired. Never. Not only do they not need to sleep, but they never get winded, exhausted, or weary at all. Others sometimes find this unnerving.

                                                Dream Daze - Those who don't get enough sleep often start to hallucinate, moving through a world colored by their half-dreaming mind. Nighthawks have learned to enter this world themselves, but to their benefit. For 1 Glamour, they gain a bonus of -4 diff. to a single simple task. While his could be used to make a normal task ridiculously easy, this is more frequently used to make nearly impossible tasks possible, like picking a lock with a screwdriver. For 2 Glamour this can be a more extensive task, such as driving a getaway car, but the bonus is only -2 difficulty. Either way, for the duration of Dream Daze the Nighthawk becomes very difficult to understand, as they have entered the world of Dream Logic; all social skills suffer +3 difficulty during this time, as are any mental skills not specifically related to the task at hand, as the Nighthawk is effectively both hallucinating and hyperfocusing on that one task. Though this power can be used during combat to perform a task, it can't be used FOR combat; Nighthawks have found that the Dream Daze mindset simply doesn't work for this.

                                                Frailty
                                                Guileless - Nighthawks have a strange way of looking at the world. They tend to make connections others do not, and miss connections others find commonplace. This causes them problems at times in communicating with others, particularly in matters of subterfuge. It's not that Nighthawks don't understand lies, or what a lie is; it is more that they are prone to missing the cue or taking it the wrong way, and their own efforts are often obscure and disjointed. Nighthawks are at a +3 difficulty for understanding or employing any form of subterfuge; at the ST's discretion this is not necessarily limited to the Subterfuge ability, as efforts by others to be discrete, sarcastic, or clever may also go right over the Nighthawk's head.

                                                Stereotypes
                                                Dirk Skyggen, Unseelie P.I., opines:

                                                on Boggans - They're nice to talk to; always listen, very polite, and some of them keep interesting hours like I do. Know one who looks after this concert hall, he never minds if I hang around and chat while he cleans.

                                                on Clurichauns - Angry little things. Half the time I meet one they say something strange to me and then look confused; by me or their own words I am not certain.

                                                on Eshu - Fellow wanderers on forgotten paths, they tell us tales and we share with them what we see before they move on. I feel like their ancient relatives of ours.

                                                on Nockers - They make the most amazing things. They look at machines and things the way we look at the rest of the world, only I don't get nearly so angry when

                                                on Piskies - I love these guys, so happy and easy-going and, ah, hey, where's my hat?

                                                on Pooka - Real friendly types, but any time I talk to one after a while they tell me I'm giving them a headache.

                                                on Redcaps - They're supposed to be so very mean, but I gotta friend works at a all-night cafe says one's his best customer. Must have poor circulation, though, he's always showing up wearing a ski mask.

                                                on Satyrs - We get along with about half of these guys. Or maybe it's only half the time, I don't know which. One moment they're being all nice and poetic and brooding in a dark corner, and the next they're jogging off to some horrible party.

                                                on Selkies - I once spent a very lovely evening sitting on a dock with one staring up at the moon. We didn't say a word. When I finally did turn to say something, he was gone. Lovely evening.

                                                on Arcadian Sidhe - Our paths seldom cross; I have no interest in their courts, and they have no interest in mine.

                                                on Autumn Sidhe - It might surprise you but I get along with this bunch better than their cousins. They often have an interesting appreciation for our ways.

                                                on Sluagh - I like these guys. They know the value of secret places, and quiet. My partner, Andy, is one, and he's a great guy. Always telling me to let him do the talking, though. Strange thing for a guy who always whispers to say, huh?

                                                on Trolls - Big, dark, and quiet. I got a cop friend who's a troll. Straight arrow guy, always looks at me kind of funny, but he never says anything, so I figure we're square.


                                                NOTE: I still might make some adjustments to the Birthrights, I am trying these out yet.
