    Originally posted by adambeyoncelowe View Post
    I read all of the Unseelie code very differently, I guess. It strikes me more as an individualistic, non-hierarchical code.

    'Glamour is free' means, to me, that Glamour should belong to everyone. Glamour shouldn't be rationed and hoarded. It should be shared and spent and used. However, I agree that Ravaging needs to be less objectively evil in the setting. It should be possible to Ravage someone but for them to recover and dream again. I'd perhaps make it more like Changeling: The Lost, where you can inspire an emotion in someone and feed off that, as a baser form of Glamour reaping. It's how wraiths feed (sort of), so it wouldn't be completely out of place in the setting. The side-effects for the victim could range from temporary madness to inertia to a deep, nightmarish sleep, so that there were still consequences, but ones which don't kill the Dreaming.
    One thing I liked about LARP changeling was how they described ravaging thresholds. It was less about destroying someone's ability to dream and more about instilling in them more negative emotions, you break someone out of their comfortable life by filling them with rage, or fear or whatever. THis lets you have more traditionally heroic Unseelie playing Batman or being political activists

    I would also change the focus abit from Glamour is free to Glamour is meant to be Free. Then play up the value of spreading it around like an investment or planting seeds.


    'Honour is a lie' - well, it's kind of true, isn't it? Yes, oaths have an objective existence as magical ties to the Dreaming, but they're also a kind of voluntary servitude or even enslavement. Following an oath becomes less about honour, for me, and more about lack of freedom. You're coerced into following oaths, because there are serious metaphysical penalties for not doing so. True honour would be following a vow to someone or following a cause without supernatural forces pushing you to do so. I'd propose, instead, a system of vows to mirror the system of oaths. These would be agreements freely entered into, but which have no mechanical or metaphysical component. They're things the Unseelie want to do, rather than things they have to do. I'd have them be roleplaying concepts only (like Nature and Demeanour), but you could always allow them to provide social bonuses - especially if you port in the Social Combat rules from Dead Man's Party.
    Tell a Troll Honor is a Lie, but ignoring the oaths thing this tenent is abit annoying because its not really a drive to be weird alien morality but rather "be a cynical jerk". The Seelie comments on honor aren't just "play chivaliric knight' but also between death before dishonor and never forget a debt plays the fairy stories where they do crazy alien things to Assuage their perception of honor.



    'Passion before duty' is also a pretty Romantic, anti-authoritarian and highly individualistic concept. It reminds me of Crowley's tenet 'Do as thou wilt shall be the whole of the law'. In other words, do a thing because you feel passionately about it, and because you want to do it, not because of guilt, shame or moral obligation. That might seem selfish, and it probably is by most counts, but it's also being truer to yourself. There's a different kind of honour, subjectively, in being true to yourself and your own ideals, rather than to the status quo. For that reason, I'd recommend that Unseelie characters note down passions that they have. When following these passions, I'd give them the opportunity to regain Glamour or Willpower (to balance it out, I'd also reward the following of oaths with the same for Seelie characters). You could port in the Passions system from Wraith if you really wanted to get crunchy, otherwise you can just list three core passions and issue Glamour or Willpower as feels appropriate. (Another option might instead be to allow a reroll on any action that directly feeds or follows a passion.)
    I might go so far as to give out Kith based passions, the HUNGER of a Redcap, the Tricksyness of a Pooka.


    I'll say in general the problem with the Unseelie is they are.. well banal in litteral sense of the word, these are not statements of grand nightmares with alien thoughts and desires but rather simply understandable cynical acts. the Unseelie should be inspiring and beautiful in a horrific fashion not a cynical commentary and that's really all I get. Nothing alien nothing fae just sort of a vague parody of Republican politics...

                                                    Originally posted by Lian View Post


                                                    Its not the Seelie who claim that Glamor is dwindling. Its an explicit part of the game. "Do whatever you want wahooo freedom!" is well just trite advice and seems to merely be a reaction to the values of honor and duty placed on the Seelie. It comes off not as profound but as teenage cynicism. It doesn't come off to me as advice to be grand but rather small and petty.
                                                    I agree that the fluff could be better (another instance of CTD not being clear about what kind of game it it), but, from the Unseelie perspective, if the fae accept that Glamour is scarce and that's the way it should be, that's admitting defeat. 'Glamour is Free' is more a statement of defiance, a reflection on ownership and the sharing of resources, and a rallying call than a statement of objective fact. Besides which, even if Glamour is scarce, it can still be free--it belongs to everyone, and to no one; it's for sharing and using, rather than hoarding. Yes, the setting makes that hard, but real-life rebellion is hard too.

                                                    The mindframe behind the Unseelie is rather anarchist in many ways (and perhaps libertarian too, in the right light). For the Unseelie, accepting the status quo perpetuates the status quo. Refusing to accept it might lead to change.

                                                    This may be idealistic or even naive, but that's what activists and campaigners and dreamers and poets do every day. That's a perfectly acceptable mindframe for a changeling. It doesn't matter if it's in the setting, OOC. IC, it's perfectly fine for changeling characters to treat Glamour as if it's something they can seed and share and spread if only they imagine they can. This is a game about imagining more and defying what's 'real' anyway. It makes sense that the Unseelie are wilfully denying the 'real' to create their own 'truth' (see: Mage, the Consensus, etc).

                                                    Likewise with Passion Before Honour, I don't see it as immature (even though it would be considered such according to most contemporary Western perceptions). I mentioned Aleister Crowley earlier, because the root of his philosophy was: 'Do as thou wilt shall be the whole of the law.' A very similar tenet. You or I may not agree with that philosophy, but it's a detailed and nuanced one that underpins a lot of contemporary magickal theory. It makes sense for a game about faeries to draw upon ideologies like these, rather than trying to make them understandable by everyday human standards.

                                                    Passion Before Honour isn't about being irresponsible, per se. It can be, yes, but it can also mean, 'Trust your own judgement to do what you feel is the right thing to do (even if "right" here means "evil"), rather than blindly following the rules because The Boss knows best.' I just think the setting needs to elucidate these things a bit better, rather than leaving us to debate them because they're not clear.

                                                            I'm not sure I should be wading into this conversation, so I'll leave the rest of it alone. I do want to say something about one thing though.

                                                            Originally posted by Lian View Post
                                                            There's no psychological drive for duty
                                                            Humans are incredibly social creatures. We are pretty much hardwired to form groups. Part of that is empathy and supporting people outside of the individual - the basis of duty.

                                                            Glamour comes from the higher order human thoughts and emotions, both positive and negative. Honor, duty, and standing among one's peers is part of glamour, so is rebellion, shirking responsibility, and forging one's own path.

                                                            The two courts embody the extremes of human psychology, so while death before dishonor and passion before duty are polar opposites and almost no individual will ever always embody one to the exclusion of the other, they are both valid psychologically and are equally glamorous.
