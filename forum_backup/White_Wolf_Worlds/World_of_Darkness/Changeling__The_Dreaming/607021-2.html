    Oh, it sure is...

    But it wasn't just in CtD, it was all across the cWoD. Mage included.

    The thing is, there where two kinds of science in the cWoD. One is the symbol of modern society, and since the setting was hugely inspired by a view of modern society as decadent and hollowed, it WAS inherently evil. Is the science represented in early editions as the Technocracy.

    Yet other was the science of books of fiction. The science of Julius Verne, Isaac Asimov and George Lucas. Sometimes it comes close to actual science, but the sense of wonder is more important, and a certain literary feel. It was "science" only as far as it used technological trappings, but all the sense behind it made it clear it was something different from that "other" science.

    Certainly things got more blurred than that, but not because science wheren't evil. What happened was that the setting evolved with time, the themes transformed, and things sometimes got confused. The very concept of science as evil was, in fact, diminishing with time. Guide to the Technocracy tried to make amends for this and start the path to integrate science in the setting along the "good" guys and find better sources for the maladies of the world. Maybe too late.

    Each and every line before Revised, though, followed this reasoning. Science is evil. It can be redeemed under some circunstances, mixed with magic, but by default it is evil. It is the main tool of the Wyrm, the main source of Banality, the weapon of the Men in Black, and even if it can be a source of some goodness, it is only to deceive the naive or be snatched by someone who can truly turn it to good mixing with magic. Without it, science will lead humanity to its doom.

                            Aging in itself isn't banal. No rule in Changeling enforces a gain of banality when you age (as far as I know). No, what rules enforce it's a higher banality for older starting characters. Because it's common for adults to be Banal.

                            This it's the explanation:

                            Precisely because when you age you're more attuned to the realities of the world, you lose the ability to live with fantasy.

                            Even the most imaginative writer fantasies ain't as intense as the ones of the child that genuinely believes in Santa, to the point of even bothering to argue how improbable it's for a guy like Santa to exist (like if it was something that could, or has to, be explained). Or those of the child that it's genuinely scared of monsters.

                            No, adults are too attuned to the wonders and horrors of the real world, and that's Banality. Banality it's when the real world can more than your imagination, or when you have to adapt that imagination to the limits of the real world. Or, better, the limits that society thinks that the real world has. The landing in the moon shattered those limits, allowing Fae to enter the world

                            I, personally, think that's why Technocrats are (and have to be) Banal. They're actively enforcing one true reality, they're agents of the "real world". Even Void Engineers are striving to make the universe as "clean" as possible. When Technocrats are over reality will be the only truth, and imagination will be just that, fantasies (preferably, fantasies about the real world, not that fantasy nonsense). In enforcing one world, despite the overwhelming evidence that they have about the existence of magick and other worlds, Technocrats are blinding everyone to other posibilities, bringing forth Banality with their Enligthened Will.

                            Oh, And Technocracy isn't "science". Enforcing one view of Reality over all others isn't science, even if that view happens to be healthy to objective science.
                            Science it's about discovering truth. Technocracy it's a secret society that happens to have a lot of scientist in their ranks, but it's main purpose isn't to discover new things, but to expel from reality any truth that seems dangerous to objective science. I invite you all to read the countless flame wars that have been fought about that theme in the Mage subforum, and to leave the issue of banal Technocrats as a poor crossover ruling.

                                        And about science being paved on the real world. That's true, but to be a scientist you need to defy the preconceptions that build up that real world.

                                        Paving these preconceptions (faeries don't exist, monsters don't exist, you dad has cancer and it's going to die because miracles don't exist) it's Banal for sure (and should be so). Being skeptic it's Banal, paving the pragmatic "real world" of the adults it's Banal too, by extension.

                                        But it has been said, with reason, that being a scientist isn't about bringing forth skepticism. Each time a scientist breaks the barrier of what it's possible, or dreams of bringing an utopic world, it shouldn't be Banal, I think.

                                        The books aren't clear on the issue, and if a poorly written crossover with Mage it's all the proof on the contrary, I think those aspects of science that bring forth new dreams shouldn't be Banal. Even if science can bring Banality too, when it plays it's "skeptic" role.

                                        One thing that always bothered me about Glamour/Banality it's the fixed idea that these are opposite and can't coexist (exept in Changelings, that can have both a high ratting of Glamour and a high rating on Banality). I think that these two can coexist. A person can be very tied to the real world, and pour a lot of creativity in being so. If Glamour and Banality could coexist, then there would be no problem in a scientist being skeptic and creative at the same time.

                                            One thing that I have to add, it's that despite what I have said about Technocracy in general, I think that giving Banality 8 as a minimum to Technocrats it's a gross generalization.

                                            While I think that, as an institution, Technocracy represents security and prosperity more than science and discovery. That's not to say that discovery and science aren't important within the Technocracy. Of course they are. Science may be secondary to protecting humanity, but it's there as a fundamental component of said protection.

                                            And I think that, as an institution, it's ok to make Technocracy Banal. Not only for the "I don't believe in fairies" shtick, but also because the Will of the Technocrats works towards a world where the faeries don't exist. I think that giving them a high Banality rating it's a good "in game" way to express how the Will of the technocrats affects Reality around them as agents of that everyday reality they work so hard to maintain (to the point of denying the masses impressive advances and discoveries, for a justified fear that they might be too much for them and cause a backlash).

                                            Even then, saying that all technocrats are Banal it's missing the point of technocrats as individuals. Individuals who may believe in utopia, and want to change things. Or that may want to study that reality deviant more than eliminating it's danger. Or that may spend more time discovering new cures than in deciding if these are too wierd for the Consensus. Said individuals are the ones that strive to push forward the Time table

                                            But I think that can be changed in a C20. That Banality table it's not core to the system.
