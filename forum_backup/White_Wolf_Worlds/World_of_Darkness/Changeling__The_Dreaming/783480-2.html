    Originally posted by Mental-Mouse View Post
    Baseball Gnomes totally sold me on Changeling...but is there anything like that in an actual changeling book? Does it have a City book or am I gonna have to build my own?
    There isn't an official build for them (although we did get steampunk goblins), but the first type of Baseball Gnome would be Boggans or Nockers (or possibly some of both), who belong to the Olympian clique. Cliques are the "z-splat" of the game, and revolve around preferred ways to pursue Glamour. And the Olympians pursue the Glamour born of sports dreams and the pursuit of physical perfection.

    If you really want to drive the idea home, go with a freehold (the changeling equivalent to a Node or Caern) with a strong baseball tie-in. Since freeholds are fueled by the dreams in that area, a stadium turned into a freehold (or the bar where the players used to celebrate, or . . . ) will become a veritable temple to the sport (at least within the Dreaming). The Hall of Fame pictures talk about past games, or give tips, people who break the rules of good sportsmanship get cursed . . .

    Kith plus clique plus freehold - the three combine to give you the exact nature of the dream your character represents. Boggans (gnomes/brownies), who wrap themselves in Glamour created by humans dreaming of a win for their team, and who control a baseball-themed freehold. Presto - instant Baseball Gnome

    Alternately, you could use redcaps or trolls . . . but I'd expect to see them in something more . . . full contact. Hockey or soccer or (American) football. Sports where their strength and aggressiveness gives them an advantage, and where they can indulge in a good post-game riot




    Edit: For gnomes who look like baseballs, they could be a form of Inanimae - "cousins" to changelings, who bonded with inanimate objects instead of humans. Dryads, mannequin people, etc.

    Or they could be some of the local chimeras (the spirits of the Dreaming). Barring any special powers, PCs could buy them at character creation . . . Say, six baseball gnomes per background point,

        The only city book I can think of is Immortal Eyes: The Toybox, which was one of the very first supplements and covers San Francisco. Other setting books are a little broader, with Immortal Eyes: Shadows on the Hill covering Hawaii, Immortal Eyes: Court of All Kings looking at Ireland, Kingdom of Willows being about the American South, and Isle of the Mighty being Great Britain. I've heard that last one isn't very good, but it's one of the few books I don't own so I can't say myself.

        Also, the entire Immortal Eyes trilogy, both the supplements and tie-in novels, is available as a $10 PDF addon in the Kickstarter.
        It's probably worth mentioning Freeholds and Hidden Glens, too. Its a set of seven freeholds scattered across the United States. It doesn't go into much depth on the surrounding city, but it does touch on the nature of the local Dreaming.

        But here's a quick glance at some of the locations PCs can visit:

        Graymere Glen: From Beowulf to the Friday the 13 movies, humanity has long told stories of the "dark lake in the woods". They're all talking about the same place. They're talking about Dudleytown.

        No one really remembers what fell dream gave birth to the dark lake in the woods, but clearly it was a terrible one. Even the most banal Autumn Person can feel the fear etched in the local Dreaming, and for centuries, none would dare the woods. In search of tin, though, the men of Dudleytown dug up the surrounding hills, ignoring the warnings. And every night for a century, their children woke screaming, as the lake mined their nightmares for Glamour.

        Dudleytown has been a ghost town since the 1920s, but the freehold has lost not of its dark power. A place where the willows walk, and . . . things born of the children's nightmares still watch from the shadows. At night, the lake glows, and on the high holy days of the changeling calendar, it even freezes over (even in mid-summer), that the local changelings might dance upon the surface. Only a fool would dare touch the waters on any other day . . . but there have been many such fools over the years. Dragged beneath the surface by unseen hands, never to be seen again - locals whisper that the lake itself has taken them, to devour their souls.

        Florist Primeval: One upon a time, a boy loved a girl who dreamt of flowers. And to win her heart, he promised to build her the garden of her dreams, hoping that would make her smile . . .

        Located in Savannna, Georgia, "Florist Primeval" is acres and acres of botanical gardens, greenhouses, and hedge mazes. The mundane world knows it as the headquarters of a premier florist and landscaping company. But as a freehold, the gardens of Florist Primeval are a favorite of local lovers, who come to flirt, picnic, and court among the blooms.

        As they wander the grounds, many sidhe experience flashbacks to Arcadia - which comes as no surprise, to those who know the story of the freehold. When both were college students, a human gardener fell for Lady Una Morrowind. And as his wedding gift to her, he built Florist Primeval from her memories of the gardens of the fabled fae realm.

        Now, in memory of her own romance, she watches over all the young lovers who visit Florist Primeval, turning none away. Seelie and Unseelie, human and fae . . . all are welcome to laugh and play among the gardens.

        Except for the unfinished hedge maze. That is hers, and hers alone. At night, she wanders through it, weeping softly.

        Remembering how, once, a human boy had loved her. And just to see her smile, he built her a garden, grander than any in Arcadia. But only when it was too late, did she realize that she had loved him, too.

        Goblin Town: Located beneath the streets of a New York City, Goblin Town is no mere building. The underground freehold is actually a massive machine, a steampunk wonder sure to delight any Son of Ether. Every couple weeks, the entire place shifts into a different shape: halls and rooms and furniture sliding effortless , until it returns to its original shape on New Year's.

        Since its founding in the Nineteenth Century, the "goblins" (a group of Unseelie nockers) have worked relentlessly to expand the freehold, hoping that it will someday allow them to "mine" Glamour directly from the Dreaming itself. In the modern day, Goblin Town now extends under most of NYC, intertwined with the mundane subway and sewer systems.

        Besides expanding the great machine, the goblins like to dabble in intrigue. They know who really killed Lord Dafyll during the Accordance War, for example, and have their own plans for the future . . .
