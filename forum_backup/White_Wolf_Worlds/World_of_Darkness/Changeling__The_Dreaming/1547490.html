    Am I missing something about the Sluagh?

    01-03-2025, 01:07 PM
    I'm trying to create a Sluagh NPC and it seems like the kith are very difficult to fit into a role - other than creepy store owner, sewer-dwelling loner, or slinky goth.

    Moreover it feels like their birthrights just don't do anything useful - after all what's the point of being able to see/speak to spirits but have no other ability to affect them? There aren't even any arts that would work on ghosts, unless I'm mistaken?

    So what's the deal with Sluagh? Are they just one of the authors collection of spare parts to fill a space or do they have some meaning within the whole?

    I'm at a loss, please help.

          The Sluagh do suffer a bit from the 1e of the WoD tending to do cool concepts first, and function character types for group play second. Though IME people tend to be more frustrated by how much the books spend on them being antisocial than their Birthrights (as both are very useful to the kinds of concepts people associate with Sluagh broadly).

          When it comes to ghosts/wraiths: first who even needs Arts when the vast majority of changelings can't sense the dead at all? Finding a ghost with enough intelligence and rationality to make deals with means the Sluagh can offer their services to protect Fetters in exchange for favors such as spying in places the Sluagh might not want to do to preparations for fae intrusion.

          There's also nothing stopping Arts broadly from working on the dead. Wraiths, like all Prodigals, fall under the Actor Realm (which means befriending them and getting to know the the ones haunting your local area makes working powers on them even easier). Though many physical Arts don't work on ghosts due to the whole inability to touch them, Arts like Contract and Naming are completely functional on the dead.

              Something that helps me when I think about the kith (or any splat in general tbh) is to try to find some archetypal or thematic core to them that I can chew on and play with (along with some signature concepts and myriad tid-bits). Now YMMV depending on the tone and play style you go for but here’s some ideas I try to play around with.

              IMO both the Slaugh & Redcaps are among the trickiest core kiths to nail down because at first blush you have to almost ask yourself: ‘why aren’t these guys thallain?’. After all, they’re described as often drawing upon their glamour from fear and horror, which certainly resembles the behavior of the Children of Nightmare. Now not everyone is satisfied with the explanations about how as kithain they rely upon the ‘safer’ forms of terror and fright, at least compared to the thallain, but YMMV.

              So the Slaugh are the spookiest of the core kiths. They’re gothic spirits who like to hang out in the sets from a Tim Burton movie (the sewers from Batman, the scientist’s manor from Edward Scissorhands, etc.) as well as places like Arkham Asylum, the House of Usher, Lou Cipher’s pawn shop, etc. They live on classic gothic trappings: abandoned (probably haunted) houses, falling leaves, mildew and cobwebs, antiques. They’re often described as wet and moist, almost resembling humanoid eels (I find this description of wet, cool, and moist interesting as it opposed a possible description of dry and dusty; it implies that the Slaugh are still filled with life and vital energy, albeit the life of crawling things and teeming ooze).

              Their dedication to being the gothic kith is also demonstrated in their tendency towards being antiquarians. The gothic is fascinated with the old and abandoned, often luxuriating in the idea that the past is never really past and that the ghosts we think we’ve buried still haunt us, whispering secrets just out of the range of our hearing. The original gothics, as Romantics, often chafed under contemporary ideas of enlightenment rationality and advances of science which had banished the enchantment and magic of the old world as ‘dusty superstition’. It’s no coincidence that so much gothic fiction showcases the undead old worlds of mysterious sorcery returning to plague the moderns who cannot comprehend them. This same infatuation with old things and harkening to buried traditions is reflected in the goth subculture movement and it’s ironic fascinating with Victorian aesthetics, a fascination explicitly shared by the Slaugh.

              These things described above as well as a few other tid bits are what I try to use as my basis of inspiration for the Slaugh. Some of those miscellaneous tidbits including: the original Slaugh kith book describing how albeit Slaugh often being boogie men used to frighten children into obedience, proper Slaugh consider actual harm against children to be anathema (unlike Thallains) and only ever participate in sinister theatrics to frighten children back into the correct behavior (one could make an interesting story about how the Slaugh themselves do not necessarily control what ‘correct behavior’ is and that perhaps some Slaugh may feel conflicted about the social norms they are obliged to enforce upon the unruly children they are supposed to scare into good behavior), as is their prescribed role in the stories told by parents and elders; also Slaugh’s ‘Squirm’ birthright makes them capable of getting into just about anything-anywhere, which makes an interesting aspect of the Slaugh their capacity to show up where you’d least expect and be virtually impossible to keep out (as befits a kith obliged at times to slide under window sills to hide under beds or crawl through holes in floorboards in order to lurk in closets); the last little tidbit I like is that I always try to have at least one Slaugh in my setting whose a Cryptkeeper type (can’t let the Nosferatu have all the fun).

              So taking this all together, where do we go from here? Well here are some ideas: Slaugh’s fascination with old things leads them to naturally learn the history of places and things from the ghosts that linger there (do the Slaugh attempt to help the restless dead solve their unfinished business or do they refuse for fear of losing their spectral friends and haunted playhouse? how do the Slaugh deal with ghost’s who have values and desires that are incompatible with the morality of the present day living? What other forms of antiquing Slaugh can we have other than the classical Neo-Victorian? Are there Antebellum Slaugh? Turn of the Century Slaugh that sleep under the hoods of old Ford T-1s? Neo-Roman Slaugh who haunt ruins in tattered togas and recite funeral dirges in Latin?), they show how festering with life abandoned places can be (do Slaugh try to lure humans into abandoned places? How does a Slaugh feel about someone attempting to renovate an old ruined house? Do they permit human life to return to the place or do they seek to defend the forms of life which human habitation which necessarily destroy - the birthright of mold, spiders, & rats? What if the Yuppie owners want to convert a distinguished regency home into chic modernist apartments?), they play the role of boogies for human children (see above for potential conflicts in this role), they naturally become reservoirs of lost knowledge both mundane and arcane (do they share it with fellow changelings? can a sidhe squire trying to woo her lord-love really be trusted with the ingredients of the invisibility potion needed to sneak past the chaperones?), they are capable of getting into just about anywhere and doing it quietly (under what circumstances would they agree to work as spies, thieves, or assassins? and, for that matter, under whose employ?), and so on.

              This has been somewhat rambling but I hope that there are interesting or useful ideas somewhere in there!

                  One of my dislikes with Changeling: the Dreaming is that it is not always easy to connect a Kith with the kind of fairies they are supposed to emulate. Some are easy. Boggans relate to any kind of household or domestic fairies of which there are many examples. Nockers are obviously fairies who live underground, mine, and create magical items. Other kiths are either too specific or too vague. Sluagh are among the later.

                  Yes, sluagh are obviously inspired by the Scottish fairy of the same name, but they are the host of the unforgiven dead which is not the same thing as the kith. Instead, the kith just seems to be a generic "spooky fairy" type. However, there don't seem to be many such fairies, and those that are scary often fit one of the other kiths better. Despite my attempts to read more fairy tales and folklore, I can't find any examples that make me go, "Ah, that should be a sluagh!" Instead, at best they just fill the role of general childhood boogeymen that are scary, but not very harmful.

                  Folklore does have many examples of fairy type creatures whose purpose seems to be to keep children away from dangerous places until they become old enough to safely handle those places/situations. Or to stop being nuisances that bother their parents or other adults. But again, many actual specific fairies of this kind seem to fit other kiths better. So we're faced with the most generic type of nursery bogie. Still if we go down that route, we have another opportunity to use that theme to make them a kind of protectors of children whether they be kind neighbors, store owners of products that children like (maybe candy or toys), babysitters, governesses, school teachers and the like. Whether the sluagh kith is well designed for that is another issue.

                  Originally posted by Dogstar View Post
                  Moreover it feels like their birthrights just don't do anything useful - after all what's the point of being able to see/speak to spirits but have no other ability to affect them? There aren't even any arts that would work on ghosts, unless I'm mistaken?
                  While they cannot control or threaten ghosts since they don't have powers, they do have something else they can do which does not require special powers to affect the dead. They can befriend ghosts and do favors for them. In a sense, they are just very good mediums that can do that "job" much better than most human mediums. So they can protect fetters, assist them in hunting Pathos, and do other favors for them in the Skinlands that they cannot do themselves. In exchange, the ghosts can do them equivalent favors like spying on people, using the occasional Arcanoi to accomplish something for them, and other favors.

                  Of course, that means the ST needs to work a lot on developing who these ghosts are, what they want, and what they can do. Some STs are always up for that challenge, while for others it is a difficulty so they neglect it, and PCs of that kith can never make good use of their birthright. But if the ST is up for it, then the sluagh just need to be friendly and kind to ghosts and want to help them and earn their gratitude.

                                                  The Spirit World (pp.419–420) is a set of systems allowing Nunnehi and Menehune to interact with spirits and the spirit world. It consists of three parts: World Sight, Spirit Speech, and Spirit Walk. A Sluagh's Sharpened Senses already provides ghost-only versions of the first two; and Spirit Walk requires the traveler to have the Wayfare Art. So why not adapt that for the Sluagh? For example, say that a Sluagh with at least a dot in Legerdemain can couple it with the "communicate with ghosts" aspect of Sharpened Senses to physically interact with the Shadowlands kind of like how they interact with chimerical reality: for this Sluagh, nearby ghostly phenomena are effectively chimerical. This would let her use any Cantrips on them in the same way they can be used on chimerical beings.

                                                          I'm inclined to agree with the others. If you have the ability to see and communicate with an entire parallel set of NPCs, with their own goals, society, and supernatural powers (with variable ability to affect your own reality), and you can't think of a way to leverage that, I don't know what to tell you.

                                                          Sluagh are the social butterflies of the Changeling world. Just, you know, with ghosts. They can pull strings, gather info, or call in backup that no one else can. Such a PC is effective when they sit at the heart of a web of contacts, allies, debts, and obligations.

                                                          I can also imagine Wraiths prefer dealing with Sluagh more than, say, the Giovanni. Not just because their aura isn't rancid, but precisely BECAUSE they can't necessarily control a Wraith directly. (Though I agree with Heavy Arms's point that their Arts should be able to target them, where appropriate). Clan Giovanni are the slavers of the Deadlands, colonial powers that shackle the dead and force them to work against their will. Whereas a Sluagh, like many spirit mediums, is a contact that can be reasoned with.
