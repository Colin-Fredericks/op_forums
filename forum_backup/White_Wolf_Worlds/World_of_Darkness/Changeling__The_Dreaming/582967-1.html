        Okay, in a lot ways, Changeling is about standing your ground and refusing to let the world roll over you. Its about being unique and looking at the world in way that few others can. the world around you is saying "this is the way the world is." and you're all like: "But its so much better this way." its about being inspired so much that you make the world a little more wonderful all the while everyone around you refuses to believe its even possible. it's about finding beauty in the mundane and stability in the fantastic. it's about grabbing on your passion with both hands as the cold and dreary world around you freeze your fingers black and turn your tears to ice. it's about being so scared you feel alive and so entranced by beauty your heart breaks. Changeling is about remembering who you were and the excitement of who you can become if only you can break the chains of "impossible".

        Changeling is about LIFE when surrounded by death and the soul crushing despair of merely surviving. its about looking at the world and saying: "You haven't broken me yet." the rest is just window dressing.

            One nice thing about Changeling is that you can play around with ideas that just don't fit elsewhere in the World of Darkness (and which you wouldn't want spilling over into other parts of the setting). Changelings build with dreams, their equivalent to nodes/caerns/etc. are places where human dreams can manifest in tangible form (for them, at least). So the sky's really the limit.

            Any sort of "pocket dimension" or "private world" works wonderfully with Changeling. Willy Wonka's chocolate factory, Silent Hill, Narnia or Neverland . . .any sort of story where a place of magic and wonder exists alongside "normalcy", which ordinary people can't reach. Changelings, like small kids, know that a hop, skip, and a jump can sometimes take you "under hill" or through the looking glass.

            Want to visit Camp Crystal Lake (Friday the 13th) or Grendel's mere (Beowulf)? You're talking about Graymere Glen, a freehold in Connecticut. Where the willows walk, and only a fool swims in the lake.

            Feel like going steampunk for a bit? Consider visiting Goblin Town, a giant clockwork town/machine built under New York City.

            Want to sing "Let It Go" in Elsa's ice palace (from Frozen)? Or maybe play a friendly game of croquet in Alice's Wonderland? How about skipping through the Emerald City (which is perfect for a Seattle based game)? A changeling can visit any of those places - and drag their friends from the other game lines along for the ride (the Jabberwocky vs. an elder Tzimisce should be loads of fun to watch).

            Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
            In a sense, Changeling is about living in the World of Darkness when you're NOTHING like it.
            Building on that idea - you could argue that Changeling is about LARPing in the World of Darkness . . . except that its all real - and your in-game actions have real consequences. The "prop" gun really does fire. The "imaginary" dragon can set a building on fire. You really are a fairy princess, or knight on a white horse . ..

            . . . at least until Banality takes you, and you forget that any of it ever happened.

                Alright, I'll admit, I am a huge fan of Changeling: The Lost and was initially dismissive of Dreaming, but in all honesty, I have never read or played it and know next to nothing about it. Maybe I should give it a fair chance. I'm most familiar with Vampire and a little bit of Werewolf and Mage, (Don't stir this pot again, you've been spoken to about it repeatedly and will be banned if you do so again. Darksider) So, maybe it may be worth looking into, though I know very little of it.

                So, to all the Changeling: The Dreaming fans, tell me the merits of this game and why I would like it.


                Right well I watched a video of somebody read through the Dreaming's core book and here is my abridged version of its premise and backstory.



                Thanks to the mages being greedy pricks, they practically killed humanity's ability to believe in the supernatural. This caused magic and imagination to dwindle in human society. The Fae are creatures born of imagination and dreams, so they slowly began to die off. In an effort to survive, the nobility fled back to the world of the Fae, abandoning the peasantry to the human world as they shut and locked the doors behind him.

                The peasants found a way to survive though, by merging themselves with human hosts, thus becoming the first Changelings. They struggled to rekindle humanity's imagination and wonder alive because that is what sustains them. A Changeling who loses their belief in magic and dreams either becomes a full human or they simply die.


                In the modern era, the mages orchestrated the moon landing in an effort to prove to humanity that there were no supernatural horrors living there and thus give the mages a chance to construct a base of operations on the fucking moon, which backfired tremendously for them. For a brief moment all over the world, humanity believed they were capable of anything, and this belief was strong enough to blow the doors open to the Fae realm and the long isolated nobles came flooding back into the world. But unlike the peasantry who had survived for centuries now without a monarchy system, the nobles still believed they should rule by default, and so they began demanding the peasants hand over all their land and valuables and get on their knees once more.


                Naturally the peasants fought back, and with the nobles being centuries out of touch with the world, the peasants had the upper hand. Cities were practically overrun with iron constructs. If they wanted to survive, the nobles had to adapt, and so they became Changelings as well. The nobility are called the Sidhe (pronounced She) and while they primarily rule Changeling society now, they have begrudgingly allowed the peasants to join their noble households, provided that they prove themselves worthy of it.


                The story of Dreaming is, as mentioned in an earlier post, an extended puberty story. Your character starts off with all these cool powers derived from dreams and emotions, but as you grow older and the human world has bigger expectations for you, you slowly corrode. It is rare for a Changeling to survive past adulthood without suffering complete banality, which means most Changelings are either children or teenagers.
