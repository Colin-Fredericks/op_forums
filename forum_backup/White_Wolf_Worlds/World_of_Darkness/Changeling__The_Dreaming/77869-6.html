        Possessed: do me a favor and get off your high horse — don’t assume that I’m ignorant of what the book says or that I think that pookas need to be completely reinvented from the ground up; neither is true. In fact, we’re not even in disagreement, for the most part. Bear with me for a moment, and I’ll explain:

        Here’s the complete text of the Pooka Frailty, from the 2nd Edition:
        • Lies — People may trust a pooka, but no one in their right mind would believe one. They’re infamous for their elaborate lies. No matter how important a issue may be, a pooka must always mix in a lie. It’s her nature. A pooka must make a Willpower roll (difficulty 8) to tell the whole truth.

        Different pooka have different styles of lying. Some Seelie pooka always say the opposite of what they mean, while many Unseelie purposefully tell the truth now and then to throw listeners off-guard. Unseelie pooka sometimes couch their lies very carefully — if an Unseelie makes his Willpower roll at the beginning of a scene, he can mix lies and truth freely. This can make him even more dangerous....
        I agree that “lie” can be interpreted more broadly than the above text suggests, and I’ll take you at your word that Kithbook Pooka addresses this issue. Indeed, it can be potentially be interpreted so broadly that the label “lie” is no longer technically accurate, as a “lie” is technically an effort to deceive: a Seelie pooka who engages in opposite-speak isn’t trying to deceive; quite the opposite, in fact. An owl-pooka who “lies” by speaking in riddles or parables intended to force the listener to think also isn’t trying to deceive. These fit the strictly mechanical requirement of having to make a Willpower roll to tell the full truth; but they’re not attempts to conceal the truth, and so technically don’t qualify as what most people think of as “lies” — which is why I’d like a different name for this Frailty.

        As for the text itself: I don’t want every reader of C20 to have to go to the message boards to get advice from veteran Changeling fans or to have to buy Kithbook Pooka in order to realize just how broad this Frailty can be. I want C20’s text for this Frailty to more clearly spell out how flexibly it can be interpreted. As I said at the top of my first post on this issue, I want C20 to address these things first by clarifying existing rules, and only resorting to rules rewrites if clarification is inadequate. And if a rewrite of the mechanics is needed, try a rewrite that expands on the original functionality rather than replacing it: rewriting a Frailty to do something entirely different from what it did in 2e should be a last resort for only the most broken of rules.

        In summary: The issue I’m talking about concerning pooka Lies is addressed in the 2e gameline — but it’s addressed in a supplement; in C20, I want it to be addressed in the core rulebook, so that nobody walks away from reading its description of the Pooka with the impression that there’s only one way that they’re intended to be played. Broad archetype, not narrow stereotype.

        On the Fuath: which supplement were they published in? I’ll grant that they may address my concern; but one issue that C20 is going to have to deal with that V20, W20, and M20 didn’t is the sheer number of Kiths out there — especially if you go beyond the Kithain and try to include Menehune, Nunene (sp?), Thallain, Inanimae, Adhene, etc.: unless you go with super-condensed write-ups for each, the Kith write-ups would fill up a book all by themselves: I just don’t see including every Kith in the corebook as a practical option. I’d want to restrict the core book to Kithain and Thallain (and even the Thallain I’d present in highly condensed form, as boxes attached to their Kithain counterparts), with other kinds of Fae being addressed later in a supplement dedicated to them, much like Changing Breeds did for W20.

        So are the Fuath Kithain who would end up in the core book, or are they among the many Gallain who would end up in a supplement?

                    Technically, post #93 does change the actual game mechanics of the Frailty: as written in 2e, a pooka only needs to make the Willpower roll in order to tell “the whole truth”: that is, it would require a Willpower roll to be factually accurate but in a deceptive way (e.g., saying something that’s 100% true but in a sarcastic tone of voice that carries the subtext that what you just said is obviously false). The above rephrasing not only conforms better to what I understand the intended spirit of the rule to be; but it also suggests possibilities where deception doesn’t even enter the picture.
