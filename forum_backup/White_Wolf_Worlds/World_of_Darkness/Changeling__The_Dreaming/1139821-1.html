                                            Looking over their rules, there's nothing there saying a selkie HAS to be near the sea. Sure, they won't be able to use their Seal Form, but they still have Ocean's Grace. And looking at the RAW text, it applies to their human form and works in all types of water, not just seawater. Their old Frailty, "Longing of the Ocean Shore" (aka the one that would give a selkie Banality points every day they were away from the sea) wasn't carried over into C20. And thus far, no one seems to miss its' absence.

                                            Yeah, I'd be extremely confused if a player wanted to play a selkie when a chronicle is set in the middle of the Gobi Desert...but technically there are no mechanics that stops that player from making that selkie in such a location.

                                            ------------------------------------------

                                            Anyways, my personal hotfix would be to just combine Seal Form and Ocean's Grace into one Birthright. Something like:

                                            Ocean’s Grace:
                                            Selkies can take the form of a seal or sea lion at will. They must be wrapped in their seal coat and touching (though not necessarily immersed) in sea water for this to occur. Additionally, Selkies reduce the difficulty for all rolls involving Dexterity by two while in the water. This applies either in human or seal form. Because of that, they can never botch a Swimming roll. Finally, selkies can hold their breath for hours, whether in water or out of it.
                                            And then just use Seal's Beauty wholesale.

                                                Originally posted by tasti man LH View Post

                                                And yet mechanically, nothing is stopping a selkie from leaving the ocean or the coastline.
                                                No, of course there's not. Why would there be?

                                                I don't think it's a controversial notion to say that the choices players make during chargen should influence the setting of the game, or that, depending on how you're doing it, the setting that the Storyteller has put forth should guide chargen choices. That's one of the reasons the example of character creation in C20 is written the way it is - it's not one player creating a character in a vacuum or even with just the ST, it's a group creating characters and we're just zeroing in on one.

                                                Kithain get two Birthrights and one Frailty. One thing we tried to do was smooth those out, because OWoD mechanics liked to try and stack multiple things under one header and I always found it confusing. One example: Selkies, so much of their power and identity wrapped up in being able to change into a seal, but no mechanical support for that. Why?

                                                Understand, I'm not saying your fix is wrong. I'm just explaining the thinking from my perspective as the designer.

                                                        My reading of the kiths as presented in C20 as a whole is that the the core Kithain are supposed to be the most broad and accessible as player options: the options that are the most versatile and adaptable to any kind of story you want to tell. Sure, officially in-story the Kithan are supposed to be indicative of Euro-American mythology and culture, but I personally find the archetypes they represent to be flexible enough that you could accommodate them in any place in the world, especially if your players and ST don't want want to do a full conversion of making new kiths from the ground up with their own mechanical sub-systems.

                                                        For instance, I find Japan's historic roots in feudalism and imperialism to be a pretty close mapping to the Euro-feudalism, that making a palette swap of the Kithain to be a fairly simple affair. Trolls as Oni, anyone?

                                                        When you start getting to the more offshoot and weirder options like the Bloodlines in Vampire, the Fera in Werewolf, the Crafts/Disparates in Mage, and in the case of Changeling, the Gallain, THAT'S when you start needing to consider doing more niche and specific stories for chronicles, because the Gallain in of themselves are more niche with more specific concepts behind him. I have full expectations of a Hsien game being completely different from a game that's primarily of the Kithain.

                                                        That shouldn't be something that's in the core Kithain, including the selkies.

                                                        Selkies should be as broad and adaptable as the other Kithain to any of the type of chronicles in a Changeling. To do otherwise makes them stick out...and not in a good way.
