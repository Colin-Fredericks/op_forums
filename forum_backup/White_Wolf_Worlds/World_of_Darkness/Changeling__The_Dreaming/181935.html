    My take on CtD, with some tweaks.

    06-27-2014, 10:32 AM
    I bought a paperback copy of CtD 1st ed corebook and browsed through it about three times when I had the opportunity. While I haven't been able to read it in depth fully (was able in certain parts). These are my thoughts.

    Likes.
    The artwork is pretty creative. And majority being in color is great as well as the few black n white pics.
    The self contain lore is awesome. Granted each has a brief overview, Changeling has theirs a bit more though out and the explanations fit per their universe.
    The Redcaps, Slaughs, and Trolls are some of my favorite Kiths. The others are pretty good, but they add that hint of darkness.
    The antagonists remind me of the Spectres from Wraith. The dark reflections of themselves that are not the Unseelie.
    How magic is set up is awesome. I like the combination possibilities and being able to pull it off on the fly. And the Bunk conditions are great prereqs.

    Changes I would make.
    The age range of the Seemings. Childer makes sense, but with Wilder and Grumps seems too young to me. Speaking of Seemings, not liking that its how "old" you are rather than what you are.
    While I like the card setup. I can see somebody stacking the deck so they can pull off 4 and/or 5 dot abilities with only 1 or 2 dot Bunks.
    The Eshu doesn't look very Changeling. While with the other Kiths does look the part, outside of their abilities the Eshu just looks more like regular mortals.
    The Unseelie should get a name change. While I can see the nobility using the term Unseelie as something to say in front of the people without trying to scare them. But when your down a dark alley and find something and realize that it's a (insert intimidating name here), you should change your underwear soon.
    I'm gonna skip my thing with Modern Fantasy. I'm pretty sure all the complaints have been heard so mines is probably going to be nothing new.
    Incorporating more technology. Not just having tech savy Changelings, but more high tech treasures and weapons. While some tech have been addressed but it seems more glossed over than fully fleshed out.

    Onyx Path Kickstarters I back.
    W20, MtC, CB20, DtD, MtAs20, BotW20, Wraith 20th.

          Oh wow. My friend, tell me, is Hansen still on the radio in your time machine? Like Darling says, pick up 2nd edition. it doesn't clean up EVERYTHING (Liam house boon, I'm looking at you!). Actually, Changeling's stuff is such a scattered mess, you might really want to work on filing the library before you go looking to make edits.I've had every changeling book for almost a decade now, and I'm still not sure what book contains what information I need - sometimes said information is 1st edition, sometimes it's 2nd edition, sometime it's that awkward teenage phase Changeling went through towards the end...

          To address your specific points though...

          The artwork is pretty creative. And majority being in color is great as well as the few black n white pics
          Funny, I found most of the art in 1st edition stuff to be kinda goddamned horrid.

          However, while i found the style crude i did sort of like that all the splat section art are characters from the immortal Eyes novel trilogy (though that Redcap doesn't seem very Edmund-ish to me...)

          The self contain lore is awesome. Granted each has a brief overview, Changeling has theirs a bit more though out and the explanations fit per their universe.
          best thing about changeling lore, is that you can literally make it up as you go along. or you cna use what's there. or both. Great thing about nobody in the game actually having a clue about What Really Happened™, instead piecing history together out of some common stuff kinda-remembered over various incarnations through the mists.

          The Redcaps, Slaughs, and Trolls are some of my favorite Kiths. The others are pretty good, but they add that hint of darkness.
          Oh, you can get darkness out of any of them. best thing is, the players never, ever, EVER suspect the seelie boggan to be involved in anything less than chaste and good...

          How magic is set up is awesome. I like the combination possibilities and being able to pull it off on the fly. And the Bunk conditions are great prereqs.
          2nd edition does away with pre-set bunks, instead allowing you to freeform them on the fly.

          The age range of the Seemings. Childer makes sense, but with Wilder and Grumps seems too young to me. Speaking of Seemings, not liking that its how "old" you are rather than what you are.
          It IS awkward... until you realize that really, the age ranges are supposed to be considered for when the character awakens to their fae nature. This is touched on in The enchanted - basically if your character starts out as a Childling, they don't immediately get shunted into Wilder when they turn thirteen; it's more dependent on their banality (of course, all those years of public school probably makes it match up pretty close) so you CAN easily have a 14 year old Childling, or a 25 year old Wilder, under certain conditions (especially when you measure in time spent in freeholds...) BUT a character who undergoes their chrysalis at age 15 or 26 is going to be a wilder or a grump, respectively; they've had ll the banality of a human tjeor age, but little if any of the glamour a changeling would have had.

          While I like the card setup. I can see somebody stacking the deck so they can pull off 4 and/or 5 dot abilities with only 1 or 2 dot Bunks.
          You would have liked it less back in the day this system was actually relevant - an eight-card booster pack cost two dollars and fifty cents, with over a hundred cards, all completely randomized... at $2.50 each. Dunno if you ever played magic, but back in 1994, $2.50 would have gotten you a 15-card booster pack with one guaranteed rare, AND you could use the cards themselves for a fun game. Changeling would break your bank for these dumb things and you couldn't do a damn thing with them besides this... though they seem to be premum collectors items these days, so hey, maybe they'd be worth the investment at the time.

          2nd edition completely discards the card system. Hell, I think most of the 1st edition material ignores the hell out of it as well. More than anything, Changeling's initial reliance on peripherals - expensive peripherals that were clearly part of a fad-grab - is likely what doomed the game to one of the smallest of fan bases in the WoD systems.

          And then they came out with Arcadia: The Wyld Hunt... Sigh.

          The Eshu doesn't look very Changeling. While with the other Kiths does look the part, outside of their abilities the Eshu just looks more like regular mortals.
          Their differences are hardly so pronounced as being nine feet tll, horned, and blue yeah. But an Eshu calling on the Wyrd would still stand out in a crowd, what with their exotic attire, uncannily slender and long forms, and the whole "hi, we have a galaxy in our eyeballs" thing...

          The Unseelie should get a name change. While I can see the nobility using the term Unseelie as something to say in front of the people without trying to scare them. But when your down a dark alley and find something and realize that it's a (insert intimidating name here), you should change your underwear soon.
          Seelie and Unseelie are not terms invented by Changeling. They're actually both Middle English terms, from the Scots-gaelic root word seilie, meaning "luck / fortune / blessing."

          I'm gonna skip my thing with Modern Fantasy. I'm pretty sure all the complaints have been heard so mines is probably going to be nothing new.
          Incorporating more technology. Not just having tech savy Changelings, but more high tech treasures and weapons. While some tech have been addressed but it seems more glossed over than fully fleshed out.
          Well, Treasures ultimately depend on the Dreaming.. .and the Dreaming, somewhat ironically, lags behind the modern world. it takes time for technological constructs and scientific conceits to become so widespread among humanity that they not only show up in the universe of dreams, but also work as intended. Chemical reactions don't work in the dreaming (Aside from fire and maybe some other simple things) because chemistry doesn't really work in dreams. it's mutable, just like everything else physics-derived.

          Now that said, the Nockers are able to overcome this with their Forge Chimera ability and some of the skills and Arts out of their kithbook... but they're pretty much alone in this (and of course whatever they make is flawed - Scientific Progress Goes "Boink" - and it's limited (gunpowder yes, NASA supercomputers, no... Hamster-powered babbage engines, maybe). if you want cutting-edge technology paired with sci fi / fantasy technobabble, you want Mage, three doors to the left, just follow the trail of lawn furniture.

Seeming is dependant both on banality AND age, but seems to rely more on banality; a childling who catches a third permanant Banality is more likely to slide into early Wilderhood, than a Banality 1 Childling is to become a wilder right smack on their 13th birthday.                       But the "hard" ages are assuming your character just had their chrysalis not too long ago, coming out of hte mortal world into that of the changelings very recently. As a mortal, you weren't able to avoid banality through use of glamour or questing or oaths, so you've got that baggage on you.

                      Think Varys, from Game of Thrones.

                      Lots of freeholds are run by boggans. THose that aren't, usually have Boggans as stewards. And cooks. or whatever. What noble's entourage lacks a boggan to act as seneschal, or to take care of the wardrobe? Boggans are everywhere. They see and hear everything. They're ingratiated up and down the social strata of the kithain, from the powdered and primped Boggan herald at king David's court to the one-armed fry cook down at the local unseelie mew. Everyone's their friends, because boggans are so helpful, and so harmless! What proud troll wouldn't stand as champion for some hapless boggan being accused of something untoward? Even if he doesn't LIKE the boggan, odds are he owes the Boggan for something, down the line. Boggans gossip constantly, among themselves; what one boggan knows, thirty others will find out before the week is out... and they keep it to themselves. They ca see who the movers and shakers are in a given scenario, at just a glance.

                      Boggans are great for antagonists centered in spiderwebs of intrigue, obligation, and rumor. No, they're not great to use as up-front, sword-in-your-face antagonists... But the look on your players' faces when they realize they've been getting played all along by the tubby little tailor who wanted revenge against the local Eiluned, and more that they can't touch him? That's gold.
