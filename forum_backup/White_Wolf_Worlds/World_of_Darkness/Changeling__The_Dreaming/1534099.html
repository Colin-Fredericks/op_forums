    Weapons and Armor Feel Completely Unbalanced

    04-12-2024, 01:26 PM
    I'll be running a Changeling: The Dreaming game soon, and going through the equipment section feels like it was written without any balance in mind. Lets start with Shields.

    "Shields: Shields are large items the wielder interposes between attacks and his body. Shields increase the difficulty of attacking the wielder in close combat by +1 or +2, depending upon the size of the shield — the attacker needs to work to avoid hitting the shield rather than his target. The wielder can also use a shield to parry both close combat and ranged attacks. Rolls to use a shield to parry ranged attacks with arrows or thrown weapons are made versus difficulty 6 (rather than the normal difficulty 8 for parrying ranged attacks). In addition, if the wielder makes a successful parry roll (also versus difficulty 6), she can use the shield’s armor (see Shields, p. 285) against attacks by firearms. Using a shield to parry or provide armor against an attack requires a successful parry maneuver, but when used to provide armor against a firearms attack, the parry roll isn’t resisted — the character just attempts to place the shield between her and the bullets."

    So Shields give the opponent a passive difficulty increase of +2 for a regular shield. So far so good. However, you can also use the Parry Maneuver with it against both Close Combat and Ranged Attacks. Using Parry against Firearms seems to have it's own special rule, where you don't contest it, but instead roll Melee + Parry at Difficulty 6 and all you need is 1 Success to get to add your armor to your Soak. Easy to understand and seems fair. You also have an easier time Parrying Bows, Crossbows and Thrown attacks with a shield. Again understandable.

    It's when you get to the Parry Maneuver versus Close Combat that it starts to feel a bit wacky. I'm not quite sure how people are supposed to actually fight each other when they each have shields. Not only are you at a +2 Difficulty to hit them, which is already a huge disadvantage in combat, but it seems that Shields can use the Parry Maneuver as normal, with no downsides, while fighting in Close Combat. A Wooden Shield in particular only gives you a -1 Dexterity Penalty, which is negligible compared to the passive +2 Difficulty you're imposing. Getting both the Passive and Active defense with a very minimal penalty feels wrong, and two people both fighting each other with shields feels like it would last forever, especially if you combine it with armor. And fighting with a big two handed weapon, doesn't seem like it would make up for the increased Difficulty. Even a Great Sword (Which I go into detail below), by far the best two handed weapon, turns into a Difficulty 7 to Hit, that can still be parried at Difficulty 6. And while it hits hard at Str +6/L Damage, if the opponent has Fae Mail, they're still getting +3 to their Soak. All of that is before the fact that increasing someone's Difficulty also increases their chance to Botch, swinging it in favor of always using a Shield even further.

    As for weapons: A Short Sword is Difficulty 5 to use, with Str +2/L damage. A Sword is Difficulty 6 to use, with Str +2/L damage. A Broadsword is Difficulty 6 to use with Str +3/L damage. Why does a Sword even exist? A Short Sword has the same damage and is easier to use, and a Broad Sword is the same difficulty to use, but does more damage. A Great Axe and Great Sword are both Str +6/L that can't be concealed and require two hands to use. But the Great Axe is Difficulty 7 and the Great Sword is Difficulty 5? And poor Huge Spiked Club is ALSO Difficulty 7, non-concealable and requires two hands...but is only Str +4/L. Other than if you REALLY wanted to roleplay having those specific weapons, the Great Sword outperforms both of the other weapons my miles due to the -2 Difficulty (And +2 damage over the Huge Spiked Club).

    The balance of equipment just seems all over the place, often copy/pasted from other World of Darkness gamelines, without much thought on how these will interact in a game that is much more focused around the fact that people will likely be using more weapons and armor, due to the more medieval vibe the game gives off.

                                  First off: yes the weapon and armor stat blocks are wonky. You can adjust them if you'd like, but it really depends what you want if just moving some numbers around is going to change anything compared to doing a more comprehensive combat overhaul.

                                  Second: Mean average results are not destiny. If the defender as a mean parry result of 2.5 and the attack has a mean attack result of 2, that's roughly 1/4 of the time the attacker will hit. Given that CtD (and the rest of the WoD) lack resurrection, characters have small Health pools, and soak isn't that impressive for most things in the games, that one hit from a heavy weapon could be fight ending (stunning if not outright incapacitating).

                                  75% of the time you win but will likely need weeks of recovery or healing magic before you can risk combat again, and 25% of the time your PC is dead and you have to make a new one? How many players really want to risk that?

                                  There's a massive gap between completely imbalanced weapons rules (and not even ones that make sense from some "realism" perspective), and rules that are so broken there is a One True Build that will let you win every fight without risk or worry and make combat meaningless.
