              The general idea is when the world was young and new, wonder and mystery was everywhere. As mankind grew larger in number, settled in more places, and became more in control of its own future (with new technology, improved social institutions, economic growth and prosperity), the land of the Fae become less and less accessible and the Fae race started to die.

              Think of it simply that humanity and the fae are competitors. As humanity thrives and expands, it is pushing out the area the fae can claim their own in the same way the coming of cro-magnon man displaced the neanderthals.

                                              Originally posted by Mercurial View Post
                                              What has changed about humanity from over a thousand years ago that would cause the Dreaming to go from healthy to dying? Is it the lack of superstition? Actively disbelieving in spirit worlds? Less art? More knowledge, thus fewer places to insert a God of the gaps (or Fae of the gaps)? Considering how much the past sucked, and how hopeless the situation was back then, I am not sure what it is that is killing the Dreaming.
                                              The Dreaming isn't dying per se. Rather, humanity's embrace of the certainty of Banality (over the wildness of Glamour) is homogenizing it, turning it into a sterile replica of mundane reality.

                                              As for why . . .even the fae themselves don't fully understand everything that happened. However, the Shattering (which forced the fae to flee to Arcadia, or go changeling) occurred roughly around the same time as the other splats embraced "plausible deniability". The vampires set up the Masquerade, the Technocracy begins the Pogrom, Charon imposes the Dictuum Mortum . . . all of which involve convincing (or outright brainwashing) mundane humans that there are no supernatural beings in the world

                                              And that's likely something that humanity was all too ready to believe. The World of Darkness is terrible enough with just the "real" supernaturals - why add more horrors to it? As a force, Glamour is amoral - all it cares about is the potency of the dream/inspiration, regardless of whether that results in something wondrous or nightmarish. So, for a mundane human, buying into the new pro-Banality worldview makes sense. Being eaten by a rogue (Garou) werewolf is bad enough - why dream up a giant firebreathing (chimerical) one with three heads?

                                              Sure, that's filling the Dreaming with the metaphysical equivalent of gray sludge . . . but who knows? The greater the threat, the greater the victory. Maybe the current state of affairs is simply building up to the climax of the greatest epic in millennia, when humanity takes back the world from their supernatural overlords. Not much consolation for the fae and chimera who get plowed under in the meantime . . . but that's the way the Dreaming (and a good story) works.



                                              Edit: On a side note, the spread of Banality (and the withering of the "pro-fae" sections of the Dreaming) is probably helping a lot of mental patients. Again, Glamour only considers the intensity of a dream . . . .and the delusions & night terrors of the mad are often quite potent. Plus, many mentally ill individuals often have "low" Banality scores - leaving them vulnerable to monsters literally born from their worst nightmares. Monsters that modern pharmaceuticals (or therapy) can't protect them against

                                                          The Dreaming is a series of Oneiric Realms constructed by the (Jaggling) Umbrood known as the Oneiroi from the dreams of mortals. In essence these Umbrood are much like the "Great Ones" from H.P. Lovecraft's Dream Cycle. Omnipotent in their home realm, but not very powerful outside of it. Dream Realms are pretty unstable for the most part, but if they are linked with bigger mythic concepts, they can hold together and even grow quite strong. But this course separates them more and more from the Superego, clinging to the Ego and Id of mankind.

                                                          Another "dying" aspect of the Dreaming is that for centuries a number of powerful entities (Fomorians, K'llasshaa, etc) have been held back from the Mortal World (and mans Superego) by the magics of the Dreaming. Now as Banality has been steadily eating away at the Dreaming, those magical bonds are weakening. As these things get increasing access to mans innermost self, the world and its dreams get darker...
