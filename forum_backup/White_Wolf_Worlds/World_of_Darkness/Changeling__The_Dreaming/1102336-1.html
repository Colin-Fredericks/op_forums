                                The C20 Hsien are more playable than before, but still vague in some surprisingly key places. If you squint, it can all be interpreted in a pro-integration light.

                                For example: The text says that the Hsien are unrelated to dreams or the Dreaming, but it's clear that they rely so heavily on human hopes and fears -- which they call prayers-- that to an outside observer, Yugen (which is described as "ineffable and brilliant") is basically Glamour in all but name.

                                The text also says nothing about chimerical reality-- doesn't say Hsien don't see it, or don't believe in it, nothing. I don't think the author even uses the word "chimerical" once. For word count reasons or otherwise, they simply don't describe how the Hsien see the world.

                                There's ALSO no information given on how the Wani forms of the Hsien actually work: how they are activated or deactivated, what happens when a mortal sees the Wani form, and so on. It's a big blank.

                                So, given all that information, the Hsien could be interpreted as fae just like the Kithain, just under a *vastly* different Changeling Way. Their version transfers fae souls to stillborn and dying humans, and their mien is either Wyrd-active or completely hidden; there's no passive form. The Hsien, by canon, are completely barred from any spirit world (including the Dreaming), but they could arguably see the Autumn World in its chimerical glory just fine-- They just don't connect it to "dreams," and why would they? They're starting from the assumption that everything is powered by "prayers."

                                The only outright reinterpretation necessary would be the Hsiens' relationship to Banality. Personally, just like the Wani form, I would say that Hsien suffer from an "all-or-nothing" form of Banality. Rather than tracking Banality points and dealing with concepts like Undoing, the Hsien simply die quickly. Their mortal lives are brilliant and, with few exceptions, tragically brief.

                                This isn't to say that the revised Hsien are all gravy. I think their powers and kiths are too limited in scope, so I would personally make sweeping changes there. If anyone's interested, I can go into more detail on that... But as far as running on just the text alone, I don't think it's hard at all to have Kithain and Hsien together.

                                                            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post

                                                            Yeah, please do go into more detail.
                                                            Sure thing. Just like everything I mentioned in my last post, I'd aim for simplicity and compatibility first.

                                                            The current powers of the Hsien present themselves as flexible and potent, but in practice, the system does a poor job of executing effects that starting players might actually want. What elemental alchemy does a Tanuki use to disguise themselves as a local politician, or to shapechange into a young man's pillow for dubious overnight purposes? What does a Sui-jen noble use to convince a nightclub bouncer to tell her the truth about a mysterious event? I know it can all eventually be figured out using the Wu Tan, but not efficiently or all that well.

                                                            It turns out that, for the right mix of potency, flexibility and concrete effects that players can hold onto, the best system is actually C20's redesigned Arts and Realms. Thus, I would have the Hsien use basically the same system as the Kithain.

                                                            That said, the Arts have some pesky western biases. Some powers of the Art of Autumn, especially the ones that involve fear and ghosts, would make more sense in Japan as part of the "Art of Summer." In places like Korea and China that still honor harvest festivals, the "Art of Autumn" would actually have some effects associated with the western Art of Spring. The Art of Metamorphosis is mostly fine, but the ability to transform into inanimate objects is arguably just as important in East Asian mythology-- And those are just a few examples.

                                                            To solve this, I would suggest that the Hsien have access to alternative cantrip levels. Of course, to them they aren't "alternate"-- To the Hsien, the Kithain are strange cousins who can shapeshift just fine, but somehow or other they're not able to turn into umbrellas or cabinets, which everyone should be able to do. A Hsien will use Wayfare to walk on water and up walls like a wushu movie, and when their Kithain friends need to Unleash to do the same, they'll just shrug and chalk it up to the way they were taught.

                                                            As for the kiths (no idea why they're called kwannon-jin-- a reference to the Bodhisattva of Mercy? Maybe kannon-jin would be better then):

                                                            I find the fundamental split between elements and animals to be interesting and evocative, and it should be kept. To encompass the possibilities of myth and legend, though, they just need to be opened up a lot more instead of hewing to an artificial five-element scheme. Players complain that they can't be tengu or kappa or oni, and this would solve the issue nicely: Perhaps the kappa is a unique form of water noble, or a misunderstood form of commoner based on river turtles. Perhaps an oni is an elemental noble of fire and earth-- or it could just as easily be a strange mixture of tiger and bear and wolf, a type of commoner that embodies the wild woods.

                                                            Basically, just as no human myth fully embodies the Houses of the Sidhe, or the way Sluagh or Nockers work, human myths should also be a slightly warped take on the elemental nobles and animal commoners, who are as varied as all the things on earth. That's how I would run the Hsien to my satisfaction.
