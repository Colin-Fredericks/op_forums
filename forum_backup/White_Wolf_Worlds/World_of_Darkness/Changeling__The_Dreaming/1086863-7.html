                            So I've been doing a few read-throughs of the book, to the point that I posted an actual review somewhere else (not going to inflict it on people twice). I'm ultimately finding C20 to be something of a pretty mixed bag for me.

                            There's a bunch of stuff I really, really like (crafting, the new take on seemings, ways to deal with banality, the great art, curses and frailties being cleaned up and playable, a lot of other stuff). There's things I dislike but can live with (still no siochain guidelines or exploration of what it does, Dice being the head of house Danaan and or existing at all, Dragon's Ire being objectively terrible and useless at most levels, magic being harder to use from how it reads, various other things).

                            But there are a few things I just can't bring myself past. The metaplot is one of the big ones. I'm not a fan of High King David as broken compromised nonentity, and I'm super not a fan of the whole 99.999% of the nobility in Concordia have become awful hoarder d-bags letting the fae outside their holds die (barring Liam and Scathach I guess). I liked that a lot of the conflicts in changeling didn't have an objectively "right" side (and there was vs the Fomorians/Thallain to balance that out as far as being more clear by contrast), but that seems to have been entirely abandoned as far as the commoner/nobility thing, to the point that the latter read as the default setting villains barring the Fomorians/Thallain.

                            The other thing is Unleashing. I don't know, it just feels too good? Like to the point that any significant combat or other plot resolution scene may as well boil down to who manages to get an Unleashing off first. There aren't a lot of guidelines or sense of limitations to it, with examples of buildings being thrown around, time travel, and what seem like insta kill effects. I would have really appreciated a firmer sense of how to adjudicate that stuff other than "narratively wing it". It just otherwise feels like one of those things that can be a path to ST/player acrimony on what any given Unleashing manages to do (yes, less likely say with in person table top, where you know people and are interacting directly, but for instance in online gaming where people are often entirely strangers to each other, whole different bag).

                            I suppose it helps that in going through the book, you can honestly feel the love of the game motivating the writing and the ideas, which is a major plus. It makes me want to try and work past this stuff, but, I don't know. Like I say, mixed bag.
