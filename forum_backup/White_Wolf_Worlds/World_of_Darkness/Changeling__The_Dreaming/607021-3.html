        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
        And if science isn't a banal thing by itself, no Technocrat should have high Banality.
        And if it were banal, Glass Walkers would be ahead of standard werewolves. They're not.

        And further, what the technocracy does is not "science." If science could accomplish the stuff the technocracy does... there wouldn't be any reason for the technocracy to exist. Theirs is a case of abusing the (reversed) adage that "magic being indistinguishable from sufficiently advanced science." They're like Star trek. They do fantastic, scientifically impossible stuff, but they involve a console and talk about photons passing through the auxillary data continuum to create a warp harmonic barrier. The technocracy's involvement with science is that they use (variations of) the modern consensus as paradigm. You think the standard off-the-rack NWO censor knows the inner workings of his energy pistol? No, he's James Bond, and Q just gave him something and said "it shoots stuff."

        The banality of the technocracy comes from their agenda and worldview. The technocracy wants to make the world "safe" for mankind. This safe world has no magic. No spirits (and no deep spirituality.) it has no cultural differences, it has nothing with would ever impede or harass humanity, or elevate some humans over others (the technocracy accepts its own "elevation" as needed to bring about human equality in the long run see...) And of course, the technocracy are the guiding, controlling hands of this future, shaping - ruling - the world they want to see. There is no space in this Brave New World for things like the fae - and certainly no place for the wonders and horrors of the Dreaming!

        After all, remember that only one Convention has any real knowledge of Dimensional Science... and Void Engineer agents who begin to see subdimensions as anything other than hostile, alien threats is re-assigned after receiving therapy!

        That is what makes the technocracy banal. not the tools they use or the jargon they spout. Back when they were the Craftmasons and such, with faith as the leading edge of hteir sword, they were still as banal - And as House Liam shows us, not becuase faith is banal, but because it's that desire for control, repression, and active efforts to cleave the dreaming away from the world.

        And yes, there is plenty of dissent among the technocracy (especially if one follows the Reckoning plot) reform movements to make the technocracy less stifling, more open to potential . But that's Mage. To Changeling, the technocracy are still "The Hidden Ones," people pulling strings with magic to further banality by squeezing the Dreaming out.

                                Okay, some stuff in specific...

                                Re-write the Accordance War Sidhe: I was gonna be happy with just asking for the accordance War to be less "black hats vs. white hats," owing to the plain fact that civil wars are messy affairs that don't really work like that. But as another thread on the forums is pointing out.. .the sidhe are really written as total crapsacks all over the place. it's like the 2nd edition trend of "the man" splats just got totally flanderized. The sidhe aren't just arrogant - they're completely and totally undeservedly arrogant. sure the other "The Man" splats in the earlier games are snots. But.. .the Ventrue get stuff done. The Silver Fangs REALLY ARE heroes. The Order of Hermes are the strongest and most diverse magical tradition. The sidhe? The Sidhe are just pretty. And all three of those "The man" splats got a decent revision that made them multidimensional, engaging characters. The sidhe, and the affairs around them, really need a good overhaul. Either that or the game really needs to be refitted into "Commoner: the Resistance" and just leave the Sidhe as "Well, you can play them, but..." splats.

                                Balance the Houses: I know, I know, "game balance" is the perpetual bugbear of these discussions, but this time, it's completely reasonable. Compare House Eiluned; their boon(s) are that they get a free success on every cantrip ever, and an unstated boon that they can get around the Gwydion lie-sense. Their flaw is that they are compelled to engage in intrigue - which means that their "flaw" is that they get involved in the side-plots the storyteller set up for them anyway. House Liam? -1 difficulty to enchant mortals (which in 2nd edition has no difficulty is their boon, while their flaw is basically that nobody ever trusts them about anything ever, period, forever. House Gwydion's "unstoppable kill monster" flaw is neat, sure, but does it really line up against Fiona's "don't afraid of nothin'" flaw? And is there any way to actually make Leanhaun playable?

                                Expand the non-core Houses: There's tons of stuff for the five "Seelie Houses." A fair bit for Ailil and Scathach as well. But what about the rest? I definitely want to see more from House Aesin (speaking of fixing boons and flaws...) and beaumayn. Speaking of Leanhaun, how's their Master Plan coming? A full "Houses" chapter would be really nice, really, but that might have to be its own book, like "Lore of the Clans" or whatever...

                                The Arts and Realms are Slapdash: A five-success roll with Ensnare deals as much damage as getting run over by a riot tank (15) - But five successes with Primal 4 deals five damage. Talecraft is legendary in how not-good it is. The Arts introduced in Denizens follow some entirely different system, but are still regarded as available arts for changelings. Speaking of which, do inanimae and Hsien need totally different systems? Needing Actor 5 to affect anyone who is any sort of opposition to you is silly. Do you split successes when using hte Scene realm or not? Is every Realm usable with every Art (they should be). Nature 5 vs. Primal 2, wut? On and on down the list.

                                Not Just the Sidhe: Okay. I get that the kith are archetypal beings. And of course, "you can make any character you want." But... there needs to be internal support for diversity. A troll sorcerer or a boggan buttkicker SHOULD be options. The splats don't need mechanical helpers for this stuff, but loosening the lacings a bit, exploring how the various kith operate in a variety of situations and endeavors is definitely needed. What are redcap matchmakers like? ("NOW KISS!") How do Pooka nobles do their thing? ("NOW KISS!").

                                The Eshu are dicks. Fix them: I recently read through the Eshu book. And, I have to say as badly as the sidhe are portrayed, the Eshu are just completely insufferable on all levels. Yes, one expects a splatbook to promote that splat with "we're the best, everyone else isn't" but DAMN, the Eshu are complete and total mary sues. Their only flaw is that they are so flawless that other people get jealous. Their major failings? A few times when commoners (I almost typed "other commoners" but no, the Eshu see themsleves as nobles...) didn't heed the Eshu. Yup, the failings of the eshu are completely the fault of their "Lessers." And of course the Sidhe are portrayed as the crappiest crapsacks ever. Seriously, the Eshu come off as having major, major compensation issues (and looking at their kith-specific art, I almost can't blame them.) But if you don't use their Kithbook... there's really not much to them, they're the "dudes who wander around" splat, which is rarely compelling. Their frailty is interesting, but their Birthrights are both garbage... A power of being where the ST was going ot send you anyway, and a second one where you get a little more experience? Not engaging stuff.

                                Chimera PC rules: Please please please please.

                                Outlines for Historical settings: The day before the Resurgence, what was the Turtle Empire like? How did the fae handle the High Renaissance? Will there be Dark Ages support?

                                Speaking of Dark Ages...: Okay. I've waggled my finger earlier in the thread about "if you want to play a different changeling game, play that different changeling game" ...but... Dark Ages: Fae does have lots of stuff that could be pulled in in some fashion, even if only for fluff or inspiration for Arts or something along those lines. Fine, the Kith don't come in infinite varieties, that's fine, but does the Seasonal court system still have some unrecognized pull in the modern day? Are there actual True Fae extending their weird hands out and tugging things in the world? Are there still Gold Oaths binding fae and humanity that have been forgotten in the mists?
