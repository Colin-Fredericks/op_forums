                  I'm sure there are people that don't.

                  But I'm not really sure "extended puberty metaphor" is a good sell for CtD. For most people, puberty is about the difficulties of growing up with the rewards of life getting better on the other side via importantly cultural milestones, and increased social independence/power. CtD's themes that focus on the idea of "growing up is bad" run counter to a puberty metaphor, because instead of an emotionally trying time that you hope leads to greater possibilities, adventures, and self-expression once you make it through, the game says growing up does the exact opposite as it starts to limit you.

                  I'd kind of push the metaphor into more of the transition from college to full social adulthood rather than puberty.

                                  The very first thing to say is: forget Changeling: The Lost.

                                  It doesn't have anything to do with The Lost being worse. It does have to do with both being completely different games.

                                  Dreaming is about Living Dreams trying to saty around in the nihilistic setting of the Classic WoD. Your character is a fae that had long ago to go through The Changeling Way, a powerful spell that made you reincarnate on a human body to protect you from Banality, the anti-dream stuff that fills the world each passing day. You're almost immortal if you avoid cold iron, bringing old memories to each new life, but still have to deal with your human body.

                                  As a common theme, is a game about balancing wonder and pragmatism, with the risk of madness if any side takes too much from you. Is about learning to grow when you're a child (because the game is MADE to support child characters), and remembering to stay a little childish when you grow up, so you never let the banality of life take the wonder that gives it sense.

                                  From there, a lot of interesting and very diverse stories can come.

                                                          The best way I can try to describe/sell it is that it starts out a bit like the main character in Hook, trying to rediscover the things that made him both happy and that long lost and forgotten aspect of himself that many others but him/herself can still see glimpses of. After that, or at least the early road of that, it begins to shift into a (more fantastical than technological version of the Matrix combined with some other aspects of maybe Hellboy. Both kind of walk in two separate, but partially linked worlds where a lot of others are either out to get them or to destroy/corrupt what aspects they need to exist. Another sort of way to look at it is vaguely similar to nWoD's Geist the Sin-Eater. But only vaguely.

                                                          A lot of the issues with CtD is that it is so hard to get into and to take seriously as a game. I don't mean this to be insulting. But it really kind of goes a long way away from basically every other WoD game there is, and that is basically what killed it. It's very difficult to be compatible with the rest of the WoD. It really dives into the pre-teen and teen angst, but also is supposed to be a mature (and I mean mentality here, not pornography) game, too, which are two very different themes to push.

                                                          It doesn't help that they are also, generally speaking, not a very scary "monster", but also pretty dang close to "superheroes", struggling against well, natural forces that in a lot of ways most people either embrace or accept to a certain extend as a good thing, and is only really something bad to them. Again, it's kind of difficult to take them that seriously in that respect.

                                                          Similar to VtM and WtA, there is a certain level of political tension as well between the courts, but in all honesty, even amongst a lot of CtD fans, the material is not terribly well explained.If this would have been much the main focus and delved into a lot more deeply, I think the game might have done a lot better. Basically, you have two big groups with the same goal, but different methods and beliefs about how to achieve them, which are not mutually exclusive. Unfortunately, it kind of seems that the setting as a whole favored the lighter, nobler group over the darker, scarier one, and it really undercut both the whole deal with the political factions and the Unseelie as a whole.

                                                          A lot of the issue with the game is that, well, ok, Vampires have their nightly struggle against the Beast and the eternal struggle for power and position. Werewolves, a bit closer to Changlings, also fight a huge ongoing war against primal powers. But for t Garou, it's an epic battle that they have basically already lost. Now, they just want to defend as much as they can for as long as they can and go down fighting. Not so much for the Kith. They "fight" a sort of psychological war against the, um, lack of creativity. The more they "loose", the more people go see a movie instead of write a book. The more they play a video game instead of painting a picture. The more they develop voice amplifying and altering software instead of singing a song. Well, that's pretty true, but it's also a bit of an oversimplification. It also means a lot of the creatures, beings, places, spirits, etc. . . that have been created "from the Dreaming" cease to exist more and more. That's why, for example, real dragons basically don't exist any more. Well, they do in the Dreaming, the sort of secret Faery world. Sort of like the Matrix, but Neo the Faerie sort of exist in the Dreaming (Matrix) and the real world at the same time. If you are familiar with it, it's similar to the Maxx in a way. The more that Banality (not-creativity) grow and grow, that is the sort of thing that Changlings all "fight" against, (but it's not an actual thing like the Wyrm, Technocracy, or Antediluvians, that is, you can't actually go out and fight it), the more one of their worlds shrinks and dies, and the less of the energy they sort of feed upon there is to go around.

                                                          Changlings also have access to certain magical abilities, and the best way I can kind of describe it is kind of like the Norse myth of Baldur and Loki. Where they went around and made a deal with everything in existence to not hurt Baldur, except they forgot to talk to Mistletoe, and someone throws a Mistletoe dart and it kills Baldur. That's kind of how their magic works. They made a pact with something/someone to be able to do certain things. Kind of brief, but that's the basic idea.
