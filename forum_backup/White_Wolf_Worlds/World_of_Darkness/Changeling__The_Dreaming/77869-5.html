    Could some of the difficulty with breadth of Kith choices stem from the source myths used? For most gamelines, the core concept is basically one core thing: a human-animal shapeshifter, a human-turned vampire, a spellcasting human, an enlightened human scientist, a human ghost, etc.

    But then in Changeling, it seems to me that a much wider variety of mythical beings are brought in as possibilities. Your origin isn't human, your themes aren't tied down to one culture or just to animal aesthetics or just science or just death, etc. There seems to be as much variety of potential myth-origin in one single Kith as in an entire fatsplat of most other gamelines. As a result, the variety of options that you want to have available in a game of Changeling may feel much wider.

    I could be misinterpreting, of course. Not a Changeling player.


    (That said, if I were making from scratch a game about all the variety of mythic and folkloric creatures that could loosely be interpreted as faerie or gods, I'd make their magic as flexible as Mage spheres.)

The faerie in the things that inspired changeling are of a fairly consistent nature in the tales that tell of them, their variability comes more often in attitude or through magics other than simple innate natures.

                    I outlined my overall philosophy about how CtD20 should be approached in post #32. Applying that to Kiths:

                    1. Start by rewriting the fluff to present them as archtypes rather than stereotypes.
                    2. Keep the “two Birthrights and one Frailty” thing, but consider revising some of the existing Birthrights and Frailties to be applicable to a wider range of concepts.
                    3. Consider giving every Kith in the core book access to a unique Art of its own. This isn’t so much a change from CtD as an expansion on it. There’s also a reason why it’s #3 and not #1.

                    Only if the above steps prove inadequate should you get into such things as providing variant Birthrights and/or Frailties for the Kiths; and even then, such options should be presented as options: “Pookas have these two Birthrights; if you want, here’s a pair of alternate Birthrights that can replace either or both of them”, as opposed to “every Pooka has two of the four following Birthrights”. Mechanically, these two options are virtually identical; but in terms of presentation, the former preserves more of the existing rules structure while the latter uproots it. The former also allows the “variations on the theme” to be moved to an Appendix or the Companion, should the writers choose to do so; the latter forces you to present all four up front.

                    I have similar thoughts about the Arts: start by seeing how much you can do with what you have; and if you need to broaden them, start by providing optional Cantrip alternatives for existing Arts (much like Wraith did in its Guildbooks for the Arcanoi), then consider adding some new Arts (such as the aforementioned “one Art per Kith” idea). I’ve been toying with an “Unleashing the Realms” flexible magic system for CtD, inspired by DAFae’s “Unleashing the Dominions”; but such a thing doesn’t belong in the CtD20 core book. An optional system in a Companion book? Sure! But not presented as part of the core book’s magic.

                    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                            I don’t know how much of an impact switching to Revised rules will have on the design; for the most part, it boils down to “botches are rare now” — which was only an issue in Mage because its existing magic rules relied so heavily on botches, and wasn’t much of an issue for [b]Vampire[/i] or Werewolf at all. Most of the controvertial changes in the Revised games were metaplot-related; and the 20th Anniversary Editions have thus far been universally metaplot-agnostic. So I wouldn’t expect C20 to start laying down a “High King David is back!” thing, or “the gates of Arcadia have reopened!” thing — at least, not as default changes to the status quo. Certainly, they’re not going to declare that an entire Kith or House has been wiped out or withdrawn from Kithain society, or that Calling on the Wyrd inflicts damage to the changeling doing it (though I do think that adding some consequences to Calling On the Wyrd would not be inappropriate — just not as a “oh, look; the world has changed!” thing).

                                                        Your response to my point about the Pooka misses the heart of my point. As written, the Pooka are the only “animal fae”, and they’re universally compulsive liars.
