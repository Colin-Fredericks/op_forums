    Originally posted by zenten View Post

    Every story involving a Dauntain is just "how do we stop this objectively evil monster that everyone agrees is a monster". No moral dilemmas, no trying to figure out who is a Dauntain, just a short battle that goes one of two ways.
    Not really. Space was light on that section, and I didn't have nearly the word count I wanted but hopefully there will be more details to expand in possible future supplements. Dauntain are not objectively evil, they're corrupted, broken things being punished for something they did or had done to them long, long ago. In a way, they're mad, not evil.

    Is a Typhoid, with no control over his poisonous dross, evil because he spreads plague? What about the Nihilist who becomes obsessed and shaky the longer he goes without destroying an object or person infused with Glamour? What about the ones who can't sleep soundly and can't harmlessly harvest Glamour? Who can't safely enter the Dreaming? Who loses autonomy if anyone finds their phylactery?

    They're monsters, sure, and changelings recoil from them as anathema, but they're victims too. Not one of them has done anything in their present lives to deserve the suffering. By and large, they're still suffering for the sins of someone else, or the sins they committed so long ago they can no longer remember what they did, or if the judgement was fair.

    I think there's plenty of stories in that.

                                                There are two key points here:

                                                1. The Dreaming doesn't care if you kill Dauntain. In fact, it encourages it.

                                                2. A Dauntain, like any changeling, is still half human. While it might be okay to wipe out the fae half of its soul without incurring Banality, the human side of you will still have to deal with the fact you've destroyed a human life too.

                                                So I don't agree with the argument that you can wantonly kill Dauntain without consequences, and I don't think Maggie was saying that either. She's just pointing out that there aren't mystical consequences. There will still be mortal consequences, not limited to guilt, shame, criminal investigations, distraught family and friends, media coverage, etc, etc.

                                                Arguably, the Dauntain can be used to highlight the disconnect between human and fae society, to create mundane consequences that pull fae characters back into the Autumn World and its own rules, and to create more horror as once-giddy fae find themselves racked with guilt when they realise how their seemingly noble actions have consequences for people in the Autumn World.

                                                tl;dr: Just because one side of your soul says it's okay to kill Dauntain, it doesn't mean the other side of your soul will let you off that easily.
