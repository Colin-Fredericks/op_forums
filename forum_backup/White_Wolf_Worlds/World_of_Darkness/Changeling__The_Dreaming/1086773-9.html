    Faerie Eternity, p. 186: "As long as your fae seeming is active, you will age at one-tenth that of a normal human or changeling and remain a childling". Since in C20 Seemings are supposed to be a reversible/switchable preferred mental/spiritual condition for changelings rather than a strict reflection of their physical age, for all characters with this Merit to be forced in the same Seeming just seems wrong. After all, in this edition you may easily have 50-year-old Childlings and 12-year-old Grumps. On the other hand, the price tag for the Merit feels just right.

    Preventing Aging, p. 47. The paragraph would be much better if rewritten to explictly state the only tried-and-true method for changelings to extend their lifespan is spend time in a freehold or the Dreaming. After all, freeholds do work because they are close to the Dreaming and provide abundant, steady sources of Glamour, and the Dreaming itself works much better at both of these things than any balefire. So the Dreaming should be explictly mentioned to be just as effective as freeholds to extend the lifespan of changelings.

                                                    Originally posted by beccatoria View Post


                                                    I think the problem with this is that it assumes that lowered inhibitions can make people make decisions that are risky or that their sober selves would not be comfortable with. Drunken consent may not be genuine consent - someone may still rightly feel violated if they slept with someone only because that person got them drunk (even if they secretly wanted to).

                                                    On the other hand, honestly, that's just what the birthright is. Fae are creepy sometimes. Cantrips can also be used in really violating, gross ways too. Since the birthright has applications that aren't gross, I don't have a problem with the fact it can be?
                                                    I think the problem people may have with this birthright is they mistakenly equate it with being roofied or made drunk against their will, which IMO goes against the intent of the authors. I prefer to equate it with being presented with the opportunity to indulge in an incredibly appealing sensual stimulus. Think of an incredibly sexy and charming person of the gender you find attractive walking up to you, with the food you most fancy in the world, and the music you prefer playing. In normal conditions, the temptation to indulge would be overwhelming, yet if the person truly wants to, they can resist and turn down the offer.

                                                    On your second point, yes, the Dreaming absolutely does not play favorites for humans or their ethical expectations, nor it can reasonably expected to. It does not have a problem with the Fomorian Dream getting dominant again, and it is so vast and ancient it spans the whole psychic imprint of history and the world, so it would be silly to expect it would pay special regard to 21st century ideas about consent. Given how many of its features reflect the archetypes of power, violence, domination, and seduction, and how much these archetypes are fundamental to human/cosmic collective consciousness, It is only to be expected that several of the manifestations it spawns may have creepy and violating implications. We may choose not to give explicit representation to these aspects at our tables, but it is unreasonable to expect they wouldn't exist somewhere in the setting.
