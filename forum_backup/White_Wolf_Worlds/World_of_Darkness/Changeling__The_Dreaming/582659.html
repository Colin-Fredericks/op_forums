    Idle thoughts about Changeling

    07-07-2015, 03:59 PM
    I don't really have much of a point to this post, but I've just got some thoughts I'd like to share.

    I've been playing World of Darkness games since the late 90's, but until now I've never really gotten into CtD. It's been on my mind a bit lately, mostly due to mentions on the excellent Midnight Express podcast and the very good web comic Chrysalis.
    I think my previous lack of interest was due to a combination of factors, the fact that, as some posters on this forum have mentioned, some aspects of the setting are not as well explained as they could be, and also I think i needed to be a bit older just to "get" some of the themes. Nostalga about childhood is something that becomes a more meaningful theme as you get older, I think.
    To help us understand a particular game my group sometimnes find it helpful to find what we call a "hook"; a similar film, or book, or character to use as a referance point. The ones that most quickly come to mind are things like Peter Pan (The original, I understand, was quite dark) Chronicles of Narnia, and The Hobbit. I can imagine Bilbo as a new changeling roped into an adventure by Motley of player characters.
    Other maybe less obvious ideas. Horror movies. Some people claim that CtD does'nt fit in the CWoD, but I can imagine a group of young changelings at a summer camp listening to stories about a bogeyman called Jason, and then a Chimera Jason is Dreamed into existance and comes after them. The Steven King novel IT works quite well also. I can totally see IT as an evil Redcap dressed as a scary clown terrorizing the children of a town, and a motley of childlings taking it upon themselves to stop him.

    This is pretty much all inane rambling, I know, but sometimes it helps my train of thought to type it out like this.

      Pretty much anything by Neil Gaiman is a good inspiration for Changeling: The Dreaming, especially Sandman, Neverwhere, American Gods, Stardust, and Ocean at the End of the Lane.

      Jim Henson's stuff is also good, especially The Labyrinth and Dark Crystal. For other movies and TV shows, I'm partial to Big Fish and Lost Girl. I hear Once Upon a Time suggested a lot, but I've never seen it.

      For musical inspiration, my go to's are Carnival by the Cruxshadows and Cruxshadow's version of White Rabbit. Voltaire's Riding a Black Unicorn and Offspring's Meaning of Life are also good. The music video for Imagine Dragons' Radioactive is great Changeling fodder. A pretty strong case can be made for My Chemical Romance's Welcome to the Black Parade.

      Revolutionary Girl Utena and Princess Tutu are both great for some anime-based inspiration. [Edit to add: Steamboy is all about the budding romance between a sidhe and a nocker.]

      If you're into artbooks, Tony DiTerlizzi recently released Realms. There's some great visual inspiration for Changeling in there, and not just from his Changeling: The Dreaming pieces.

              Off hand, one anime series that recently came out had some serious Changeling the Dreaming vibes.

              Chuunibyou demo Koi ga Shita is a series in which the characters are highschool students who believe they have magical powers, and often times the world s filtered through their perceptions. So while two characters might be fighting with an umbrella and a cooking pot, in the "imaginary world" they're battling with huge magical spells and powerful magical weapons. So it sort of mimics the idea of the "real world" vs the Dreaming. Also the two major themes of the show is the difficulties of growing up, as well as the importance of balancing reality with fantasy (and vice versa).

              So while the show is fairly comedic, I think it touches on some of the ideas of Changeling very well and it might be worth checking out.

                                          Originally posted by Ersatz View Post

                                          That sounds REALLY like Changeling and worth looking up.
                                          Here's an example of one of the fights. It's a good visual idea of how things could look in Changeling, though definitely played up more for comedic effect.


                                          Another franchise I would would recommend is Umineko: When they Cry. It's a visual novel series (if you're not familiar with those, think of it like a video game, without any gameplay; just text, graphics, music and voice overs). There was an anime, however it was a terrible adaptation and simply ends halfway through the storyline.

                                          To summarize the basic plot, the main character gets kidnapped by a witch and is forced to engage in a game with her, where she murders people using magic, and then he tries to use logic to come up with ways that she could have killed people using more mundane means. You could think of it as a fantasy vs anti-fantasy (or mystery vs anti-mystery) story. Further it gets very meta, with some scenes taking place in the magical world of the witches, some scenes taking place on the witches "game board" (where she manipulates people as she desires), and yet other scenes taking place in the real world.

                                          The multiple layers of reality and the way people interact with them is incredibly Changeling, even down to the way magic works in the setting (magic will only function for and against those who believe in it, so if you want to affect someone with magic you need to convince them that magic exists). Banality and disbelief can utterly annihilate the creatures of the witches world, and there is one great scene in which a witch, when her familiars are unable to help her, slowly destroys each of them one by one, simply by telling them that they don't actually exist - a process that causes her to completely lose her powers. It was one of the best examples I've come across in media of the way that Changelings can lose their fey soul.

                                          Honestly if you substitute the word "witch" for Changeling or Fey, at times it could be very easy to forget that you're not reading something right out of Changeling the Dreaming.

                                          Unfortunately there's no official English translation (though the unofficial translation group, which called itself Witch Hunt not only got permission from the author to translate the series, he even added in a group named after them in one of the later novels). But, this means it can be a bit of a pain to get ahold of and it's also quite long (there are 8 chapters, and it took me around 8-10 hours to finish each one). Finally the first chapter can be a bit slow, since it's primarily setup and character introductions, but that can turn some people off.

                                          Still, I don't think I've ever come across something that made me go, "Wow. That is Changeling the Dreaming," more than Umineko did.

                                              1) Don't worry terribly about "making it dark." All that ever does it make things come off pretentious. A solid story has plenty of conflict, and in RPG's, most conflict is of a pretty dark nature, as is its resolution (rare is the RPG where the conflict is rival school pride, and settled through competitive dance...) You don't have to worry about how to make changeling "fit" with say, Vampire or Wraith. It doesn't. That's fine, it's its own game, with its own themes and agendas. it shares a common world with those other ones, but has a different perspective of it

                                              2) Even so, changeling is a pretty good medium between the three "flagship" games - it has all the politics of Vampire, plenty of sword-swinging for the Werewolf fangs, and a good dose of pretentious metaphysical gibberish for Mage fans! But unlike those games, you can actually do this stuff without your characters tiptoing on eggshells all the time. In fact you have to tiptoe on eggshells when being perfectly normal.

                                              3) for inspiration, don't stick to the old fantasy standbys. yes yes, Labyrinth, dark Crystal, Last Unicorn, Pan's Labyrinth... same old same old. Fantasy films are good for a vibe, but can actually fall kind of flat for full-on inspiration ("Secret of Kells" gets me into one hell of a Changeling-playing mood because of the style and mood... but is basically a very bare-bones "hero's Journey")

                                              Erin Brockovich: A Piskie and a Boggan bust their butts under threat of grinding banality to not just protect their dreamers, but also deal a bow to the creep of banality in their community.

                                              The matrix series: The mundane, mortal mind struggles to retain a sense of normalcy and identity against a seemingly overwhelming threat of bedlam. if you haven't guessed, Agent smith is the hero here. a doomed hero. How iconic!

                                              netflix's Marco Polo series: Origin of every "stranger in a strange land" trope found in western culture, basically - which fits very much with Changelings. Good look at court intrigues and honor for the sake of honor, rather than for the sake of righteousness. Also, a Fiona satyr provided set and costume direction. There's no other explanation for that much flesh.

                                              Harry Potter: What? Isn't this the trite fantasy stuff i was just brushing off? yes, well... except for the part that while this amazing and fantastical six-year conflict is unfolding, the mortal world is almost completely unaware of it... almost. Enough magic pops up to create a sort of feeling of dread and "shouldn't be happening" - like the Death Eater shootout at a delicatessen.

                                              While granting fantasy concessions, Game of Thrones is a good look at even more court intrigue and house rivalry. But then, so is the entire Dune universe. For game material, go with the Brian Herbert books - trying to unpack Frank Herbert's stuff for Changeling is probably completely exhausting)

                                              speaking of Brian Herbert... Pick up old cheap pulp fictions and sci-fi novels. Changeling can be a very tonal game... but sometimes lifting a straight up black-and-white two-fisted tale of heroism and villainy is just what the game needs. A villain who actually curses with "Curses!" certainly has is place in changeling, and these things are FULL of 'em (and are full of... other things as well.) Gold- and silver-age comic books are just as good. Those old Superman stories might be laughable by out modern standards for Rob Leifeld-drawn "Gritty McGrimdark" antihero superheroes... but in Changeling, maybe your ginger buddy actually DOES get turned into a dinosaur, and you have to fix him before fifteen pages are up!
