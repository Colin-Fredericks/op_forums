    RE the Sluagh Birthrights, it's important to note that since you can see ghosts, you can target them by using the Fae Realm. Fae 4 lets you target all kinds of spirits, and since you know where they are, Sovereign + Fae 4 + being a Sluagh makes you a peerless Necromancer. With Wayfare or Dragon's Ire you could grant a ghost success in some scheme or battle, with Naming you could alter a ghost's nature, with Contract you could enter into binding deals with ghosts.

    Hell, worth remembering that there are Trods which lead to the shadowlands, and that Wayfare can get you into the realms of the restless dead. All this to say that the Sluagh Birthright is actually pretty amazing if you want to use Cantrips on ghosts, and there are plenty of reasons you'd want to do so.

                    Oh, worth noting that since Fae 4 covers the Wraiths of WTO and the Spirits of WTA, and so on, any random Changeling with a Fae of 2 can spend 2 Glamour to 'cheat' out a Fae 4 Cantrip.This makes Changelings very, very dangerous if they know what they're doing.

                    For the Spirits of WTA, Form is Reality. A simple Metamorphosis 1 Cantrip can turn a salmon into a cod, so think of what it might do to a Salmon Totem. Changing a spirit's appearance changes what it is, their function follows their form. Similarly, Fae magic can easily blur the lines between a Wraith and their Shadow, or totally redefine a Fetter and its relationship with a ghost.

                    To cut a long story short, Fae 4 can make you a very upsetting individual who challenges the core assertions of a whole 'faction' in the WoD. The humble "Eidolon" of a Technocrat might be hunted down in their dreams and turned into a frankly mystical Boogeyman, a creature which is hard to interpret as anything other than a mystical Avatar. Of course very few Changelings would entirely understand what they were doing, but if you're invading the dreams of an enemy you're going to start messing around with things that seem important to them, see what harms them most keenly.

                        Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post
                        For the Spirits of WTA, Form is Reality. A simple Metamorphosis 1 Cantrip can turn a salmon into a cod, so think of what it might do to a Salmon Totem. Changing a spirit's appearance changes what it is, their function follows their form.
                        Is this actually true? There are spirits who have the Charm Shapeshift. It clearly states that it only changes the spirit's form, not its abilities of powers. So obviously there are spirits whose form is not reality. Are these spirits simply an exception? Or does it reveal the limitation of such "spells" when talking about spirits in general?

                        Do such powers actually work across the Gauntlet? Or would it only apply if the spirit is materialized in the physical world, or if the changeling actually enters the Umbra or the spirit enters the Dreaming? Personally I would not allow such powers to work across the Gauntlet, but would be open when both parties are in the same "dimension". Likewise for wraiths and the Shroud.

                        Also, the Metamorphosis Art clearly says it can change a living being into another living being. Does that mean it cannot affect undead like vampires or wraiths even with the Actor Realm? Is the mention of the "living" incidental and should be taken as really meaning anything affected by the Actor realm? Or it an inherent limitation of the Art? Does it affect corpses? Or would Primal be the appropriate Art there? As ST, I might rule it can affect vampires because they are part of the "living" world and some semblance of "life", but not wraiths because the Art itself does not affect them (this being a limitation of the Art, not an issue of the Realm used). Also, some of the individual arts of Metamorphosis seem to state it only affects the changeling casting the cantrip as opposed to also other living beings. Again, this may just be poor wording on behalf of the writer, or actually be an intended restriction.

                        Because such powers are written in ways to communicate what it can do in typical situations, but not written to address crossover elements that are not typically included in that setting, I am often leery at simply applying RAW and not thinking things through. I might be willing to allow such powers affect Gafflings, but automatically fail for Jagglings and allow Jagglings to be able to "flip" Gafflings of its type back to what they are. Just say there's a new Charm for that and Jagglings always have it. Not to mention said changeling may now have lots of spirit notoriety. Or maybe not worry about it because such transformations are temporary anyway.

                        Does Fae actually cover spirits? Wraiths are specifically mentioned in Actor, while Fae itself just mentions creatures of the Dreaming.

                        Please note I do not have C20, so this may be something clarified or changed in that edition. Or some other sourcebook provided possible crossover rules.

                            C20's Fae Realm specifies that it lets Cantrips target "spirits", yeah, with the implication being that in the Changeling cosmology the Wraiths and Spirits of the WoD are composed of some exotic glamour one step away from that of the Dreaming. There are also Trods leading into the Middle and Low Umbra, and you can use Wayfare to cross into the Umbra, and also turning a regular Trod into a Trod leading to the Umbra is possible with some Cantrips.

                            And yeah you're absolutely right about the inconsistency in Form = Reality, there's a line in the W20 Umbra book about how important a spirit's form is to it, that changing its shape changes its reality. Arguably a shapechanging Charm is just a simple 'disguise' instead of an actual transformation. Metamorphosis meanwhile, which in C20 can do stuff like turn a bowler hat into a stetson, for example, not just change living things, might be transforming, or it might be applying a 'glamour'.

                            There is a viable crossover implication that a random Pooka could make an enemy for life by turning a Falcon Spirit into an Eagle Spirit (the difference is likely very significant to the spirit and its brood!), but it's also reasonable to expect that powerful spirits would be more resistant to such change and have defenses against it!

                                Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                                Yes, sluagh are obviously inspired by the Scottish fairy of the same name, but they are the host of the unforgiven dead which is not the same thing as the kith. Instead, the kith just seems to be a generic "spooky fairy" type. However, there don't seem to be many such fairies, and those that are scary often fit one of the other kiths better. Despite my attempts to read more fairy tales and folklore, I can't find any examples that make me go, "Ah, that should be a sluagh!" Instead, at best they just fill the role of general childhood boogeymen that are scary, but not very harmful.
                                The Sluagh remind me of (in concept) the witte wieven (white wives) of Dutch folklore, the kind of fae who straddle the line between fae and ghosts and witches, wraith-like figures who hide gold in their hills and marshes and forests and spirit people away to dance with them in the night.
