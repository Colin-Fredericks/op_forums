    I can see NPCs being easier to integrate, but mixing various PCs from various splats can work well with a bit of ST and player coordination. The first thing to do would be to determine which is the 'main game' or splat. If it's Changeling, then your Werewolf tribes probably should be Fianna, maybe the Silver Fangs, possibly the Children of Gaia, or one other tribes that gets mentioned a fair amount in Changeling source material that currently I don't recall. And their choice of powers should include abilities that enable them to interact easily with Changelings and their world. A similar thing would be needed for a Mage character (any of them likely would need Spirit at a minimum of 1, with a preference for 2, and possibly Changeling and/or Dreaming Lore, etc.). Prime likely would be a good idea as well. For Vampires, perhaps Malkavians and/or having Auspex would be necessary (though perhaps Vampires do not mix well at all, absent a specific Changeling PC group composition that seeks out interaction with Vampires).

    If the main game is not Changeling, then a Sidhe character interested primarily in Fae court politics might have to shift their focus to politics between Changelings and other supernatural groups in the area (think the setup they have for Santa Cruz in Changeling's source material). They will have to realize that aside from a few solo scenes (or perhaps solo sessions, if the ST and that player have time), internal politics won't be a focus for the chronicle.

    I do think more coordination ahead of time is required for Changeling as a main game. We had an oWoD crossover featuring mostly Mages with a Vampire that worked just fine (though the Vampire didn't get many chances to interact with Vampire society), a nWoD game with oWoD Mage setting but nWoD Vampire and nWoD Werewolf PCs (and an otherwise nWoD setting), and that worked fine.

    The worst crossover (beyond a very short-term chronicle) I can think of likely would be Prometheans from nWoD, actually. I don't know if they released a second edition, but based on the first edition, Prometheans being around anyone (including supernaturals) for any significant amount of time eventually spells disaster.

                I've run a mixed game for years. Most of the players are either changelings it Fera, and I've had little to no problems. Even power scale wise. I will admit to throwing the changelings a few more XP towards Arts early on, but that made them quite...terrifying in battle. A Werewolf might by a walking blender, but a changeling with a few well placed cantrips will completely hamstring the opposing bad guys.

                The one house rule that I did have to decide on was changelings being immune to delirium and werewolves mostly being free from negative effects of long-term enchantment.

                What results from their integration, I find, is a very dynamic game of beige of hope caring for the fragile psyche of creatures in a constant state of despair. We've had some beautiful as well as chilling scenes as as result.

                There was also the incident of the 'Ensnair - with - silver - piano - wire'...but we shall not dwell on that...never let a pooka plan ahead.

                                    Had some thoughts on other templates being redone as apart of changeling. I mentioned enchanters before, now for werewolves

                                    Sir Marrok was a Fae Blooded knight in service to a seelie lord long before the sundering. He served his lord well and was given a blessing tied to his knightly oaths. He gained a chimeric form through magics long lost to the Fae. When Sir Marrok's wife became ill he went to her to stay with her as she lay dying however when he did so assassin's crept upon his unguarded lord and slew him. His oaths broken Sir Marrok's blessing became a curse, his lupine form becoming twisted and driving him mad. Worse still his oaths bound not just him but all of his bloodline. His name became lost in time and woven into other legends. His descendants, now bound by the light of the moon become craven beasts under the full moon light hunting and killing those who smell of fear, their crimes hidden by the mists. But all is not lost for the oaths remain in effect and any of Marrok's descendants may renew those oaths to another lord of the sidhe and have their curse become a blessing once more.

                                    Mechanically these werewolves are Fae Blooded who bear the blood of Marrok. They may spend one willpower to create a chimeric form of a large wolflike humanoid, that has the stat adjustments of a garou crinos form making them quite powerful. Under the light of the full moon they automatically enter this form while calling on the wyrd, allowing it to effect the mortal world. While in this form they are savage hunters preying on the fearful, unable to maintain rational thought. By making an oath of fealty to a sidhe lord they may negate this effect, giving them full control of their faculties while in their lupine form, they no longer shift automatically and may enter the form in a wyrd state at will once per month, refreshing during the full moon.

                                    (it should be noted i do not have C20 yet and thus don't have rules for the fae blooded, i may amend this once the book is out on DTRPG)

                                        I've never done crossovers because I think they would ruin the game atmosphere. I mean, Changelings are creatures that walk a tightrope between believing and not believing what they are. It's like the "Pan's Labyrinth": Ophelia doesn't have a full proof that she is a princess, but if you believe, you can see evidences (fragile evidences).
                                        In CtD it gets worse: if you believe too much you can go to Bedlam, but if you start doubting too much, you risk go to ruin.
                                        When you do crossovers, your character deals with people that are alive for the last centuries or can shapeshift into a killer bestial machine. I cannot imagine how this changeling could doubt that she is a fairy trapped in a human body, so I think this could easily change the game's atmosphere.
                                        Well, that's totally ok if its YOUR wish, but keep in mind that there are a lot of rules and drama that lacks sense if you do that. In the end, it's an issue that requires more thinking that just dealing with rules.
