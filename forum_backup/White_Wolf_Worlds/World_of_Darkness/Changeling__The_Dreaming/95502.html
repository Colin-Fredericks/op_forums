    Running Changeling the Dreaming for the first time.

    03-21-2014, 07:03 AM
    The recent influx of cWoD anniversary editions is causing me to look at cWoD seriously for the first time, and I find myself really drawn to it in a way nWoD never really drew me in. My gaming group seems actually very interested in giving Changeling the Dreaming Second Edition a shot. Im a somewhat experienced DM but if I run this, it will be the first White Wolf / Onyx Path game ive run myself (though I have played in Scion campaign and some nWoD one shots before.)

    From what ive read lurking here and in RPG.net Changeling has some rough edges mechanically. I am looking for tips, generally recommend houe rules and anything else thight might help file some of those rough edges off.

    Thanks!

      I don't have time for anything too in depth right now, but I'll give some highlights now and expand on it this evening.

      The first thing to watch out for is glamour. Strongly recommend to your players take a little bit of the Dreamers background. Without it, there's a good chance they'll accidentally burn through their entire glamour pool in one session and have no way to replenish it for the next several sessions if not the entire rest of the chronicle.

      Banality. The book gives several contradictory definitions of banality in different places. You'll want to decide what constitutes banality up front and clearly communicate what you'll be considering to be banality up front to your players so you'll all be on the same page.

      The Nature realm is awful. As written, there's no way to use low level Primal without maxing out Nature. I've always house ruled that you can summon elements with Primal, but you can't control them after they come into existence without Nature 5.

      Sidhe vs. all of the other kiths published in any book ever. The 2E core book isn't too bad about this, but later sourcebooks really started playing up the noble/commoner divide to crazy levels. Kithbook: Eshu is kind of terrifying about with the sidhe hate. Passions on both sides of the divide tend to run high in fandom. I've had several games nearly fall apart just because one player wanted to be a sidhe for the social bonuses. The rest of players all gang up on the sidhe player because "it's one of those damn sidhe!" Alternately, I've seen groups blow up because the sidhe player decides to use all the mind control powers on the other players because "they're just a bunch of commoners." If you don't want your game to turn into nothing but PvP, make sure you communicate that clearly ahead of time. I've gone so far as to house rule that the sidhe are the social kith instead of the noble kith and nobility only comes from house membership. It was the only way I could prevent the player driven sidhe/commoner conflict from completely engulfing and destroying the game in groups that aren't all sidhe or no sidhe.

      Charlie Cantrell
      Onyx Path Freelancer
      Changeling: The Dreaming 20th Anniversary Edition, Conquering Heroes, Book of Freeholds, Guide to the Night, C20 Players Guide

              Personally I would suggest adding Nobles the Shining Host to that combination at least if you intend to allow Sidhe PC's or want to add a certain air of disconnection to your Sidhe NPC's. Even though the source material occasionally get's a bit over the top on the Commoner/Noble conflict I've been lucky(?) enough to avoid problems that PookaKnight has had with the divide. Even in my CtD LARP back in the day things stayed civil even though there was only 3 Sidhe actually present in the town versus nearly a score of commoners. And I think that Nobles the Shining Host (NtSH) does a pretty good job explaining why the Sidhe aren't generally ganged upon without a very good reason.

              Without NtSH my fear is that you are getting a very one sided look at the Noble/Commoner divide.

              But the Glamour issue brought up by PookaKnight is unfortunately very real since almost everything from many Birthrights to Catrips and more costs Glamour and it is somewhat slow to be regained. Another background, worth pooling up amongst the PC's, that can help with it is of course Freehold, or Treasure if you have a Nocker or two in your group to get the Glamour Battery (Kthbook: Nockers). Even these means are kinda stop gab methods but actually resolving the issue would require notable changes in the Glamour system as a whole.

              I also agree on the matter of Banality, it should by it's very nature have a bit stricter more clean rules and most of all it should be somewhat predictable. So choosing your poison and sticking to it is a good method here as you noted.

              The Realms do have some wonkiness to them, the Nature really being the worst culprit but unfortunately not the only one. Yet these are things you can live with and adjust where needed as you get more used to the system.

                  Either make Glamour easier to optain, or decrease the cost. I usually do a Mage sort of thing: roll permanent Glamour (Arete) when casting a cantrip, only spending temporary as a modifier or if the effect is Wyrd (Vulgar).

                  As for updated history and good house/Revised rules, I look to this site: http://wodcityofangels.com/page.php?109.4
                  You can fiddle with the details, but I usually always start with David Ardy's death being subverted. It makes a lot of stuff less of a headache.

                  Uhhh... piskies. Don't let them be an excuse for endless pickpocketing. But I think they're fun, especially as wandering defenders of children and having roving Freeholds.

                  Don't let the two-page splat hold you back, for any of the kiths. Changeling is first and foremost about imaginative narrative and the struggle to keep in in the world.
                  http://wodcityofangels.com
