    The Rage fansets started around 2002-2003 (If I remember correctly for Yahoo Groups' Rage_Info) after the whole thing with Azaral Productions fell through. You can read the whole thing here.
    I also remember that the White Wolf website even posted news about the Rage fansets near the end or middle of the New England Block.

    Vampire: The Eternal Struggle was still being made around that time, so it took them much longer to get to the point of making fan-set/printable cards.
    The Arcadia CCG even has some support (No where near as big as Rage or V:TES, but it's something. And I collect the cards as well).

    In addition to the Cantrip cards, there were "Make-your-own" Bunks cards. These make-your-own cards were part of the Changeling Player's Kit (Which you can find on either Amazon or Ebay).

    Ever since the announcement of the Drivethrucards.com website, I'm awaiting when they put out cards along with the novels and Player's Guides. Also, I remember that I kept coming across online references to a Arcadia Player's Guide book in the past, but the book doesn't exist. Was this just vaporware?

    I would love it if Stephanie Pui-Mun Law would do artwork for Changeling 20th. I remember buying a print (Catknots) from her from the Artist Alley at Anime Expo one year.

                                        If he's referring to how changelings lived as humans until their Crysalis, and even now have both a mortal Seeming and a faerie Seeming — if that's what he means by "possessing mortals' bodies" — then that's exactly the sort of alteration of the game that should not be considered for C20. If you want something without the Chimerical/Banal duality, you're no longer talking about Changeling: the Dreaming, and would be better off adapting Dark Ages: Fae to the present.

                                        Originally posted by Darling_Rose View Post
                                        I'm all for making changeling a bit more 'grown up' though in terms of the imagery and impression it gives people. Less Wizard of Oz, more Return to Oz.
                                        Or more like "the Wonderful Wizard of Oz", the book on which Judy Garland's movie was based. It was considerably darker in tone than the movie in many ways; though it at least avoided the business about Glinda being a master manipulator who arranged to send a teenaged girl on a suicide mission. "Return to Oz" managed to capture the feel of the next two books of the series ("Marvelous Land of Oz" and "Ozma of Oz") quite well; and they were comparable in tone to the original book. It wasn't until the fourth book of the series ("Dorothy and the Wizard of Oz") that the series began to derail into "light and fluffy" territory, with the return of the Wizard and the whitewashing of his character from "usurper of the throne" to "good-natured crank".

                                        But I digress. Yes, the illustrations of a cartoonish anthropomorphic bear need to go. I wouldn't go with washed-out colors; that's suitable to the Banal side of things only; the Chimerical side needs to be mad with color — emphasis on "mad". Have you seen the Witches' Labyriths in Puella Magi Madoka Magica? That's the sort of insanity that needs to come through when portraying the Dreaming. Changelings, meanwhile, exist in the balance between the insanity that the Chimerical World can be and the drudgery that the Banal World can be. You know the illustrations on pages 32 and 33 of CtD2e, with a faeire looking down on mirror images of the World of Darkness and the Enchanted World? The World of Darkness side is OK, though it would be improved by being a bit more drab (doing it in monochrome would have helped considerably); but the Enchanted World side is a bit too cheerful in its presentation. If I were to change just one thing in it, I'd have the elf's eye open, with the sort of glint of insanity in it that you'd expect of Heath Ledger's Joker.

                                        The last line of the "Enchanted World" text understates what needs to be stated: "Like, yet unlike, the rest of the World of Darkness, it is a little brighter, a little more colorful, but sometimes no less frightening." Strike "a little" and "sometimes" from that: the Enchanted World is much brighter than the Banal World, and it's much more colorful; and in spite of — no, because of that, it is every bit as frightening.

                                        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                            I'm glad for cantrip cards discussion. I understand printic, economical restrictions.
                                                            I just wanted to present statement that a quite large deck of cards, especially with random and abstract conceptions would create infinite bunks. Because lets see - a Mage has to focus on specific foci and style. Changeling... also has his own style - I mean good players invent something remarkable about their players, but they are not ristricted so by MECHANICS as are Mage players. I know, handbooks invented "typical" exemplars of bunks for every kith and every standard Art - that was helpful about creating.
                                                            Well, this would lead to abuse of one player using some easy bunk. There are some rules (leaving decision to the Storyteller) that more impressive and better fitting to the kith, Art, situation bunks give better buffs for rolling a dice - but still somehow inconstistent

                                                            Originally posted by Toreador View Post
                                                            If they do make a C20, I would really hope they would make it less childlike and more darkly serious- less colourful prettiness and more washed out and desperate (or maybe things may have changed in the last ten years?).

                                                            I'd want it also to be more similar to actual faerie lore than what it was originally. I.e. no female satyrs, less 'goodly' fae and more alien fae. Ramp up the 'fair folk' trope. Ditch the whole 'possessing mortals' bodies' crap too.
                                                            The problem is that we cannot change the Changeling, but can only make use of not-pointed-out parts of both Chageling and World of Darkness.

                                                            Well, about giving the World of Darkness darker theme is partially job of The Antagonists chapter (even if it is "sadly" called an Appendix"). That's what went on my mind when I was reading The Dreaming again.

                                                            Okay, every game-line can have enemies from other games. But each game also has it's own ones (and people avoid cross-overs from obvious reasons). Vampires have the horror of sabbath, tzimisce, ghouls and tremere expeiments as most horrific dangers. Werewolf have both fomori, banes and Pentex subsidiaries. Mage has all who aren't Tradition mages + Umbrood + weird shit anomalies + paradox phenomena (while mage is a magnet for the last 3).. Wraiths is terrific in it's own trivial soulforging.. but even then we have typical variety spectres, then barghest, drones, and even lovecraftian tempest dwellers.

                                                            And then.. Changeling. There were mentioned Fomori as being really bad Unseelie, banal and unnerving of glamour, but still werewolves took care of them mostly. If you remove all other-game-antagonists there are chimera. And there are pretty dark, gloomy chimera. Looks like playing a changeling, especially walking the suburbs at night, would end in meeting large spiders from the trashcans, Black Dogs, tentacles from sewages etc. That feels me with lovely terror. Then it would be a much darker, peculiar and sinister game if they made Changeling: The Bloodlines :s

                                                            And another thing I read from core book. Theme: Nightmares in narration. Looks like changelings would fight the nightmares, but they are so scared by most of them that they instinctively want to run into embrace of Banality just to kill these nightmares. So.. there is a world of nightmares and dreams alike, but you can't kill nightmares cause it will cost you banality? Tragic conflict.

                                                            But still I can't imagine the chimerical world. When I did a reading in Mage handbook about Penumbra, it was really intuitive that mage sees true selves of people and things. That lovely book glows with warm, touchable emotions. The same as forest hut. In Changeling the chimerical reality is just like the same. But still it isn't something easy to narrate. Is EVERY thing seen as some kind of hyper-reality. From sewers thru lanterns to the very starry night, is everything chimerical? What is %-tage of meeting wandering, creeping chimeras, good or bad. It is somehow another world not seen by any other supernatural (just like the Wraith game) so both inanimate and animate chimeras would make cities crowded with fairy stuff? Or not? Well I don't think the book explains it best to me. The setting isn't unequivocally clear for me.
