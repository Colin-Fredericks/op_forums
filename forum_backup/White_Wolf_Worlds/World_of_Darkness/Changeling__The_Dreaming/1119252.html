          The over all theme of the game is a coming of age ceremony. The Fae are creatures of imagination and dreams, which are quite strong during a person's younger years. But as they get older and more mature, they are expected to leave such childish fantasies behind, or else they risk serious complications later on in life.

          This is why it is rare for Changelings to survive past their 30's, as most have succumbed to Banality at that point. The ones who still cling to the dream are usually considered insane by 'normal' society. You ever seen someone in their late 40's using a teddy bear to scare off the Bogeyman?

                          What do Changlings DO?

                          Long Story Short: they try to rekindle humanity's imagination.

                          ---------------------------------------

                          When the Technocracy shut off humanity's collective belief in the supernatural, they started a process of slow degradation as science and reasoning came to replace faith and wonderment. The Age of Reason is killing the Fae, which is why their nobles abandoned the human world to retreat to the Dream world. Those who were left behind were forced to bond with human hosts in order to survive.

                          The moon landing rekindled humanity's collective belief that anything was possible (temporarily), thus allowing the Fae who had abandoned the world to return to it in full force. But now they're dying out again because humans don't believe in magic anymore.


                          While every other splat in the series is trying to make sure humans think they're just a myth, Changelings actively want humans to believe in them again, because that's how they survive.

                              Changeling games can be as political as Game of Thrones. Seriously. I got into the novels many years ago because I was preparing a heavily political game and wanted some inspiration. I asked online about political fantasy novels and was directed towards Martin's series (which only had 3 books at that time). Court politics can be just as nasty if you want it to be that way.

                              Games can be as fun and silly as kids playing pretend in their backyard. Except the dragons and goblins are real.

                              Games can be as epic as an Arthurian legend, seeking out a powerful legendary artifact in the depths of the Dreaming in order to stop a horrible event from happening.

                              They can be about very personal issues of redemption from the banal stresses of life, and trying to claw your way back from the depths of despair and boredom.

                              They can be about a up and coming indie rock band that tours the country trying to bring a unique creativity to their audiences as a way to stave off the cold winds of the coming endless Winter.

                              They can be about fostering nightmares in a city, working in the shadows to bring people into a belief in the fae in order to once again take over as the dark lords the fae were in a forgotten time.

                              I've played and ran every one of the original WoD games. None of them have the flexibility and variety in themes that you'll find in Changeling: The Dreaming. Each of the other games has some inherent limitations that keep you to a certain set of styles of play. Yes, there is still a great bit of variety in what you can do in Vampire, Werewolf, Wraith, and Mage. But in my opinion none of them compare to Changeling. It can be epic or personal, hopeful or bleak, silly or nightmarish, political or adventurous, and anything else you can think of.

                              What is playing Changeling like? What can you do? That's a harder question to answer than in any other WoD game.
