    Balefire Implications & different balefires

    05-18-2020, 08:47 PM
    Recently I've been thinking about balefires. They are the soul of a freehold, without one, it's just a safe house, if so. But sometimes we tend to thinkl about them only in dots.

    I don't remember where I did read it. But there is a great balefire in tara-nar, and in a certain day of the year messengers walk the realm spreding it's fire between all freehold in concordia, to renew the fire of the lesser balefires(basically, all other balefires). This is some pretty interesting lore that got me interested in a few questions.


    What is the cost of creating/mantaining a balefire?
    In some books it's stated that creating a balefire it's something as "simple" as many changelings gathering to give a bit of glamour each. But this is the cost only to "kickstart" a balefire? And it still needs to be renewed by the great balefire? If so, so what maintains the great balefire? What kind of price is needs to be paid? Is it the dross collected as taxes by the nobles? Or the Great balefire is just a unique balefire with almost infinite connection to the dream?

    Before Tara-nar was built, which great balefire charged all others? Was there even a Great balefire? Can a balefire be upgraded to a great balefire? Or lose it's power?
    So, if balefires need to be renewed, every realm or independent territory needs it's own great balefire, so it's probably possible to create or upgrade a balefire, but how?

    If all balefires needs to be renewed with the fire of the great balefire, this means the sidhes have much more power and influence on the commoners than previsouly considered?
    If someone rebels against the king, or displeases him, he can just refuse to renew the balefire of such person. This might have some implications. Any rebellion needs to win with a deadline of 1 year? Every rebellion needs to be at least be strong enough to create their own great balefire before even considering fighting? I imagine this could be a entire chronicle, a party made with the single objetive of upgrading the biggest balefire in the rebellion to a great balefire.

    This also means that a tyrant king can control the glamour which commoners get, a athoritarian sidhe could become a nightmare real fast.


    This is more of the homebrew part, but have you ever created a special balefire, or something special related to balefires?

    In my games a few things revolved around balefires. My first chronicle was about purifying a balefire. There was a nervosae chimera living inside of it (the chimera was made of fire and could not be hurt by any means) it made the balefire produce dark glamour, which also gave nightmare dice with every point of glamour. The balefire also made a lot of black smoke, that turned into semi-solid smoke spiders that protected the freehold against anyone who dared to enter. The chronicle was all about how the motley thought the spiders were the main enemy, only o realize there was something more.

    In the second chronicle they visited a lost freehold where the crystal circle used to make their studies. There they found the cold balefire, it was not a fire, but a pure light floating in the great hall. The cold balefire generated glamour, but it was said that it never weakened in thousands of years, and no know power could make it disappear or weaken it.

    And finally, We created a special treasure, it was a ring that the owner of the freehold had acess to. It allowed him to draw glamour from the balefire wherever he was, and to teleport freely while inside the freehold.

      The intricacies of balefires and freeholds are one of the fascinating parts of the Changeling setting and one of many things that makes me wish it had gotten more material, although we did get a book for Freeholds in C20.

      To take a swing at answering your questions though...

      •Cost of Creation & Maintainance?
      -Technically all one needs to create a balefires is an ember from an existing one and the willingness to pore glamour over it like oil on a waking fire to warm an area against the Autumn World. I've always seen what comes after though as depending to an extent on ST interpretation and desire but ultimately being to keep the balefires hot and stoked through passion and glamour so that it isn't driven out by the chill winds of Autumn or the glacial rains of coming Winter. This means keeping those gained by Banality far from the balefires and keeping the freehold warm through drawing glamour to it, all the while remaining wary of Unseelie Glamour Raiders and outsiders from rival households to Willworkers who may seek to steal the embers and sparks from your balefire. In that sense, I see the ritual of embers being spread out through the balefires of Concordia as both to bring heat into every balefire to help it remain while also further cementing the connection of every household to that of their superiors and ultimately High King David.

      For instance due to the sheer scarcity of personal, I imagine that the Great Balefire at Tara-Dar doesn't send embers to *every* balefires in Concordia itself; there's just too many and too few dedicated messengers. I find it much more likely that every King or Queen within Concordia receives an allotment of embers for their own balefires and they in turn provide embers to their Duchies who provide embers to their Counties who provide embers to their Baronied who provide embers to their households; as each sees fit. This adds in some interesting ideas of both Concordia having a sort of Trickledown Glamour-nomics (with potential for all the same pitfalls) and puts emphasis on the themes of feudal obligation, particularly as what happens when selfish lords horde embers for themselves or when rebellious households find that the local lords are refusing to send embers to their balefires, despite the threat of any one balefire snuffing out drawing Winter one step closer for all changelings.

      •Before Tara-Nar
      -Presumably before the Resurgence in 1969, there likely weren't any great balefires in the Autumn World. I could perhaps see a few forming temporarily in places of great glamour such as the Globe Theater or Olympic Stadiums or Stonehenge or Woodstock or what not, but presumably before the Resurgence the world was simply too cold to allow for them.

      Thus when the nobility returned through the Resurgence, riding waves of glamour as well as raising commoner freeholds for glamour, they were the ones most enabled to gather the sheer quantities of glamour and embers necessary together to create Great Balefires such as the one at Tara-Nar.

      My interpretation is that a well tended balefire does not necessarily need an ember from a Great Balefire, but they certainly help and with the Autumn World growing more chill by the day, there is no Balefire in the world so hot as to not like to have an ember from a Great Balefire or even another low Balefire for that matter.

      •Sidhe Power
      -I definitely think this is very much meant to be part of the setting, is far by hoarding balefire and particularly the Great Balefire, the sidhe are able to enforce their feudal worldview by making many commoners dependent on the nobility for the ever-necessary.

      When loyalty is rewarded with balefires and access to freeholds and treachery or rebellion is rewarded with expulsion from freeholds or having ones balefire stripped from them; it creates a strong incentive to cleave to the feudal structure.

      Yet in the modern world where the frail Arcadian Sidhe especially but all nobility horde glamour to live in fantasy worlds and stave off the Autumn, we see how the rich get richer and the poor get poorer. Commoners once again cleave together desperately as they had in the days of the Interregnum and every time that their pleas for aid are denied by an aloof or greedy liege lord, they grow one day closer to abandoning their reconstructed feudal society in favor of their own independent communes and freeholdings as they had during the Interregnum.

      •Special Balefires
      -I definitely think that providing character and flavor to different glamour and balefires is helpful in storytelling and worldbuilding. Just as glamour created through giving clockwork toys to small children will feel different from glamour created by teenagers discovering each other at a beach party, balefires will feel different based on the sorts of glamour that created them, the glamour that surrounds them, and the one who created and/or maintains the balefire.

      Changeling works with the Fisher King concept that the land reflects its masters and I think that freeholds and balefires are great ways to show that. The balefire of a Gwydion Baron shines with a brilliant green light while his domain is filled with clean air and cloudless chimerical skies where light pierces every shadow. The balefire of an Eiluned Countess is set within a pit of mirrors and shines with a warm orange and purple light that casts deep and comfortable shadows that fill her chimerical domain. The balefire of a Boggan maintained commune roars cheerily in its hearth and fills the old home with the scent of a cookfire and a cozy warmth. The balefire of a Redcap corby's lair crackles with an icy chill and bleeds hot blood like soot as chills and dark stains fill the walls of the abandoned factory that houses it.

      And that's all just based upon the glamour and balefires of Dreaming changelings. One can only imagine what the sinister domains of Nightmare chimera and thallain might feel like...
