                                                        Depends on the Art and the request. If is a "freeze my enemies" with Winter, the legs (or even his entire body) of the Changeling might freeze too, leaving him locked near his targets. If it is a classic "slay my enemies" with Dragon Ire it would hurt allies or destroy nearby stuff, but with Primal the fireball would burn the target and make anything flammable near by spontaneously explode in flames. If it is a "show me the most beautiful on the land to me to marry" using Contract it could show many people, or leave the target aware of him and his interests, or even redress the changeling in wedding clothes. It is very situational, and can vary a lot by how many success it goes beyond the art level. The most important thing I think is try to keep the tone of the desire and the situation (dont make it always funny nor always dangerous), and to not always make the desire effect useless or worst to the Changeling (like freezing everybody, not just the changeling, or making the "slay my enemies" kill the Changeling). Think what the Dream would do if he herd that desire screamed and gone overdrive.

                                                        ------

                                                        If I'm not mistaken, there is no relation between the number of success in the Unleashing and the level of the effect. One success is all that is needed to create whatever Unleashing is done. More success is, actually, worst for the changeling, cause the more success he gets, easier it is to go wrong for him.

                                                        I like the idea of Unleashing, but it really is the most dangerous power in the World of Darkness. Mages can't do this level of stuff without spending a lot of Quintessence and exploding in paradox afterwards, Changelings just use two glamour points and get a Nightmare point and the world curve himself to his desires.

                                                        As it seems over-powerful, I suggest the following rules:

                                                        *- The effect can't affect something that the target can't perceive, but can show stuff beyond their regular perception.
                                                        *- The bigger the amount of (people, space, size, etc) that the effect is going to affect, the bigger the amount of success needed. If the amount is insufficient, the effect still happens, but only to the amount that it achieved. (If trying to affect a whole army, is needed 5 success, but if the Changeling only get 3, he will affect only about a parcel of them).
                                                        *- The Dreaming will not allow you to jump challenges that the own Dreaming put on your way. You can't summon the object of a Quest, or to have the answer of a Riddle to enter a Trod appear to you by means of Unleashing.
                                                        *- It cannot be used to do any effect that would wilfully call for a banality trigger to the Chalenging Unleashing (like destroying a treasure or killing a Fae Mortal self). The own banality building up inside of the Changeling closes the gate of the Unleash close. But of course that the Unleashed effect can get closer (like leaving the Mortal self of a Changeling badly harmed, but still alive).
                                                        *- The Unleashed effects after unleashed are beyond the control of the Fae. If he invoked a "Ice to froze his enemies forever" but later decide to unfreeze them, he will need to find a way to unfroze them by himself (maybe another Unleash, but it would not work if it is the target of a Quest).

                                                        And, to help to counter it on Fae fights, I suggest the Counter Unleash and the Banality Unleash. The first is a defensive action to use an Unleash against an Unleash. The target can use the same art to null the point of the Unleash, or be creative on the counter attack. Naturally this would be a resisted test of the Unleashings. It costs the same as a Unleash, plus a Willpower point.
                                                        The second would be a way to negate a Unleash by summoning the banality on you. You gain a point of banality and have a banality trigger (so you could gain 2 points of banality) and roll your banality resisting the Unleashing, difficulty 8. You can increase the number of dices, adding your banality dots to the roll by each extra banality point your character accepts. Nothing happens in case of failure, but in case of botch the Changelings get an extra banality point and can't use arts for a scene. You can't Unleash and Banality Unleash on the same scene.

                                                        What you guys think? Do you think this helps?
