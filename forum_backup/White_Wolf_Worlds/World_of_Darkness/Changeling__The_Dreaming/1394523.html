          While many changelings have some sort of internal system of status or ranking, as far as outsiders becoming noble the most common seem to be those such as the Boggans, Eshu, Satyrs, and Trolls.

          Boggans, particularly for the western kithain, maintained many of the freehold communes that filled the world as commoner changelings cleaved together for survival and this history of leadership combined with their inherent social dedication as well as their natural inclination towards polite society. They make great servants and lords alike.

          The Eshu are proud and many do consider themselves to be ennobled, even though amongst many of the western kiths they are seen simply as nomadic outsiders. The Eshu do however claim divine ancestry and nobility, which is acknowledged in others at times.

          The Satyrs are also mentioned as being particularly capable to become noble commoners, although most often amongst Graybeards. The elder satyrs tend to be scholarly and wise as well as being acquainted with leadership after years of guiding tragos.

          The trolls are probably the main rivals of Boggans and Sidhe for the most common kith to be ennobled. As they uphold ideas of duty, honor, and pride; they are seen as trustworthy and strong leaders and many changelings would trust a troll with their lives.

          There are also Autumn Sidhe to mention in the C20 timeline although they are most commonly representatives of Houses Liam and Scathach, although there are also mentioned to be scattered remnants of others.

                                                          I wanna start off by saying that I definitely agree with other posters here that the concept of an alternate setting for CtD where the Sidhe never left earth is an interesting one that is rife with opportunities.

                                                          (I also think that a recent Sidhe Resurgence setting is an interesting idea but that's probably another post)

                                                          It would definitely create a more natural progression of changeling history as the commoners and sidhe alike had to endure the progression of human history and the encroachment of the World's Autumn and the coming Winter. It also compliments for me a concept that always stuck with me when I heard it, that apparently certain folklore establishes that the fair folk often resemble just-so-slightly-out-of-date human culture.

                                                          So like mentioned above having Sidhe in the court of the sun king, Victorian England, or the Roaring 20s would be incredibly fun and interesting for concepts. I also agree how it might better serve to illustrate the romantic/gothic idea that the golden age has past us by that perhaps the Resurgence of canon undercuts somewhat.

                                                          I agree that this would definitely be a fun idea for a setting shard or an Alt-WoD concept, although on the other hand - to be Balor's Advocate - I do think that the current canon approach has some distinct advantages.

                                                          For one thing, I think it helps to justify in-setting the medieval aesthetics of changeling society. If western changelings had been living alongside humanity for the past 600 years, they would probably have bypassed fuedalism (with the history of commoners in the Interregnum saying as much) and the sidhe would have adapted to modern dreams of leadership - mayors, politicians, executives, etc.

                                                          However by having the distinctly medieval Arcadian Sidhe return in the near-past of the CtD setting we can have the whimsical hybrid of modernity and medieval-aesthetic high fantasy that we do. Aside from motifs though and visual presentations of mood, I think it brings a strong theme into play.

                                                          The Sidhe-Nobility of CtD play their archetypical role in the setting allegory as 'The Out-of-Touch Elite' to the T. They maintain supreme authority, they centralize resources and power, and they very much become emblematic of the sort of 1% attitudes/activities that (imo) they are meant to critique. While I acknowledge this theme could have also been presented with modern sidhe, I think that having them literally be throw-backs to a feudal age definitely illustrates a point while also serving the western fantasy motifs and visual theme.

                                                          (Also this isn't to say that I think the Arcadian Sidhe can only be used as caricatures or antagonists, I think they're very interesting and some of my fave CtD characters have been Sidhe. They can *also* represent the noble ideals of a lost time, the forgotten romance of chivalry, the duty of our leaders to stand amongst us and battle on behalf of our safety and security; etc. I simply meant to illustrate that as part of their role as a setting element in a Gothic-Punk setting, they are definitely allegorical for elements of real world elitism and privelage)

                                                          All of that said though, I do enjoy both approaches to the concepts of CtD and I distinctly like both settings, I simply wanted to write this post to illustrate what I view as some of the definite merits of CtD's approach.
