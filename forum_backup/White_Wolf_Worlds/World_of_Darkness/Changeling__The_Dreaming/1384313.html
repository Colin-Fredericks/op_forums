    Garou Packs of Stag among the Fae

    04-27-2020, 08:59 PM
    I am doing research to help me figure out some ways to include a Garou pack with the Stag totem in the world of the Fae. This is for a Werewolf game, but I wanted to get insight from players of Changeling.This is specifically about Garou packs with the Stag totem, which is something I have not found specifically mentioned in Changeling books. The only Garou references I find are vague generalities concerning tribes. But packs of Stag have a concrete ban involving aiding the Fae so it is something that should be explored in more detail.

    So after looking at existing material     talking about Garou/Fae interaction, particularly in regards to the Fianna (for obvious reasons, although the tribe does not have a Ban unlike packs), and also research into the role of actual Irish warbands (real world historical and mythological fianna) as an inspiration, I've come up with some ideas and wanted to run it by you. My goals are to get an idea of what elements of Changeling should be included if I want to run a good integration between the game lines; find a balance between making the ban real as restriction on the PCs, but keeping player autonomy; allowing the ST (me) to use this as an RP tool, but not trapping me to run a Fae dominant game.

    Looking for feedback here on how well you think this could be incorporated from a Changeling specific. In other words, rather than how to fit Changelings into Werewolf, I am wondering how a Changeling game would handle a Garou pack of Stag as a normal part of their setting. While not all domains of Changeling would have a local pack of Stag to call upon, it should be common enough that a sizable minority would have access to one. I can then work back as needed to determine how it appears from a Garou perspective for a Werewolf game.

    If there are parts people disagree with, or believe new parts should be added for consideration, let's hear them and the reasons why.

      Here's some general principles on the nature of the connection between these packs on the Fae. I've used them to determine some general principles, so changes here will affect things downstream.

      On the Nature of Stag (and his Children) and the Fae
      1. The Stag Incarna spirit has a special role among the Fae. Perhaps because of some pact between Stag and the Tuatha de Danaan. Perhaps some other reason.
      2. While some interaction with the Fae will be done in the real world, at some points these packs should also be entering freeholds and the Near Dreaming.
      3. The Ban of Stag represents something very real between those Garou and the Fae. There is an implied Oath between that pack and the Fae. That “Oath” should drive the relationship between these two groups. It'd be nice to have an articulated one, but simply having guidelines to give to PCs should be enough.

          Now I had to ask myself what are the general rules that govern relations between actual Garou and the local fae based on the totem ban. So this would set expectations for both kinds of PCs (Garou and Changelings). I wanted expectations to be clear, but flexible. And open to many kinds of specific details between chronicles.

          On the role of Packs of Stag among the Fae
          1. Packs of Stag are semi-independent band of hunter-warriors pledged to protect the Dreaming. Children of Stag, in exchange for their Totem's patronage, are expected to keep order and protect the Fae on Stag's behalf.
          2. While packs of Stag have the duty to aid the fey, there are limits to what the fey can ask of them. No fae can command the pack (although as relationships develop, individual packs and members of such packs might decide to swear additional oaths on their own in exchange for other favors just like changeling can decide to swear oaths to each other). But “local” fae can request help. But how to do so is left to the pack's discretion most of the time.
          3. The primary duty of such packs is to patrol the “frontier” or other dangerous territories to defend the fae from external threats (most likely malicious chimera or other invading forces), not internal policing.
          4. Packs of stag are not obligated to involve themselves in internal fae politics, so rival fae domains at war and such don't interest them. They are more like the Night Watch in Game of Thrones – the shield that guards the realms of fae.
          5. So there is an expectation that packs of Stag are proactive in this kind of patrol. Which means they need to be able to enter the Near Dreaming, or perhaps identify freeholds in the mundane world. This kind of knowledge can be imparted to them by their pack totem, who as a jaggling avatar should be able to follow airts, identify Trods (Garou definition, which I believe maps to the Changeling concept of the Green Paths of Balor), and have basic knowledge to serve as an interpreter and guide to the Fae to its pack.
          6. In addition to that, packs of Stag serve as a form of mobile military reserves for the local fae. They can be called out by the local fae authority (either a fae lord, or another authority if it is a commoner held area) if there is a very big threat.
          7. Therefore, the fae need a means to “summon” local packs of Stag that could help them. This could be done by various means – certain fae Treasures, certain cantrips, or personal visits. Before such summons are done, it is probably best that the Fae and Garou meet and interact so there are no surprises on either side.
          8. Packs can also be asked to avenge wrongs done to the Fae after the fact, provided they are things the pack would have defended the Fae if they were at the right place and time to prevent.

              Lastly, I needed some details on actual interaction between them, especially in terms of how these characters are introduced to one another. I have some general notions, but it is here where I'd need more specific details from forum members on how this can work.

              How the Fae and Packs of Stag Actually Meet and Interact with One Another
              1. So at some point early on the pack's career, the Fae need to make contact so the Garou pack can actually fulfill its voew/ban. While some proactivity by the Garou is appreciated, since the Fae are experts on the Dreaming, they are more likely the ones to reach out first. Therefore, they have to know when a new pack of Stag is formed, or when it arrives. Again, this is probably done by appropriate Cantrips (like Soothsay) or Treasures. If you can think of other means, let me know.
              2. The Fae also need to be able to contact and request help on occasion, though contacting the Pack is not like a 911 call. Instead, it is more like a plea or petition. While a pack of Stag is obligated to assist per their ban, they may also demand “payment” of some kind, or everyone understands a future favor is owed, or at least that the fae requesting help is under social obligation to the Garou pack. Every request of help further binds the Garou pack and those Fae, creating stronger bonds. But I need to figure out how a random individual Fae can do that. How can a Fae track down the pack when needed? What if it is a Fae who has never before encountered the pack? How do Fae in general learn about the pack?
              3. Since these packs are semi-independent, almost mercenary, it is alright for packs to move around to different territories. So if they particularly don't like the fae in a certain area, or have more affinity elsewhere, it is perfectly acceptable for packs to move around so they are far enough away to not help certain fae, but can help other fae. Packs of Stag have a responsibility, but they have a lot of discretion on how to perform it.
              4. Nevertheless, as the pack increases their prowess and fame, they may see themselves getting requests from fae further away because they know of that pack's reputation and have a serious problem.
              5. The rule of chiminage still applies. While packs of Stag aren't “paid” for the protection they provide, all relationships need to be maintained by the exchange of gifts and services. While some of this is provided by the benefits of the Stag totem, the Fae should be providing the pack something to show their appreciation.
              I think the “proper feel” of a Garou pack of Stag among the Fae should be the Garou are like a marauding band of “mercenaries”. Hired by Stag that is. They are part of Fae society because of Stag's Oath, but not a reputable part of it. They are uncouth and dangerous. They are there to help maintain order, but could potentially pillage and raid the very people they are supposed to protect. But they might become true and valued friends of the local Fae.

              If you disagree and think the relationship would be different, I am interested in hearing what you think it should be. As well as any other comments on how this relationship and oath should be depicted.

                              Stag as a pack totem is found in every Werewolf corebook going back to first edition. It's a basic part of the game. One of that totem's bans is a requirement to help fae in need. But that's about all there is. There isn't much more description, or other details on how such packs should be run (but that is the case for all pack totems, there's only a few paragraphs description). If you don't have the Werewolf corebook, you can probably find the totem write up somewhere on the internet. There's lot of ongoing or former online games that reference them.

                              The Fianna tribebook, which deals with a tribe of Garou with Stag as the tribal totem, has more detail on culture and some Fae interactions. That can be used as partial inspiration, but packs and tribes of the same totem aren't identical. The Fianna tribe versus a a pack of Stag comprising Uktena, Wendigo, and Red Talons, or a pack of Stag with a Get of Fenris, Shadow Lord, and Silent Strider will be very different. Tribal culture is much more defined, while a pack culture is very dependent on how its members choose to define themselves and honor their totem.

                              Stag as a Garou pack totem is certainly is not limited to the Dark Ages setting. I don't know where you got that.

                                  Originally posted by Eldagusto View Post
                                  Personally I was peeved that was never really fleshed out. I mean we get some stuff about Fianna and Fiona being allies, but say Dark Age Werewolf/Fae it didn't make any sort of sense how the Fianna had a fey history when the Dark Age Fae book was all LOL we tricked some werewolves into helping one of our gang fights what rubes!
                                  Many sourcebooks had topics that were often only generically addressed, or key points entirely ignored. The ban for packs of Stag to aid the fae existed since the first Werewolf corebook. It was one of those obvious crossover hooks planted in the early days like Malkavians and the fae, or Giovanni and Silent Striders and Wraith. But the game never properly developed guidelines on how that was supposed to happen.

                                  Part of the reason is that as time went by, the World of Darkness splintered into feuding Developers, and all the gamelines developed some very antagonistic stances to each other and downplayed any kind of connections.

                                  I don't do crossover in the sense I don't allow PCs of different types (vampires, werewolves, mages, etc.) together, but I do set my chronicles in an integrated setting where all those elements might be encountered. And the selfsame Fae that Garou may be assisting could be involved in things that don't square perfectly with a Garou's other obligations. So I think now is a good time to discuss that.

                                  I like the idea that there is an implied non-aggression pact between packs of Stag and the Fae in general. However, not all Fae are going to be doing things the Garou like. So I don't think being a part of Stag actually means there is complete non-aggression between them and the Fae.
                                  • Some Fae may like vampires, or have an enchanted Malkavian at court.
                                  • Other Fae might be hanging out at a Glen that some other group of werewolves want as a caern and might attack it (a situation similar to one established in Rage Across New York describing the Catskill Mountains – home to the story of Rip Van Winkle and the little men playing ninepins.)
                                  • Members of the Shadow Court and House Balor might have plots with the Black Spiral Dancers or other Wyrm minions.
                                  • There may be less horrible, but still troublesome plots of the Fae that negatively effect local Garou or the Garou as a whole. They might be manipulating another Garou. Or maybe they're protective of a mortal that other Garou (or even the pack) want eliminated. How are those conflicts handled by Garou who have the Stag totem?
                                  So there should be plenty of room for conflict between packs of Stag and the Fae, just like one Fae can opposed one another. In such cases, what are the expectations? Should there be such an implied non-aggression pact? Should there be loopholes or exceptions? Is there a different standard?

                                  On one hand, there is nothing wrong with commitments that come into conflict. In legend, the hero Cuchulain had a geas to never refused hospitality, but also had another geas to never eat dog meat. At some point, he was forced to violate one of those when he was served dogmeat by the host of the house he was visiting. And sometimes it can be fun to see how the PCs work around conflicting bans. However, players can feel angry if they think they are being screwed over or put in a no win scenario. At the very least, these kinds of conflicts need to be addressed up front so the PCs know what they are getting into, and can make preparations to try to avoid such no-win scenarios.

                                  (Because of that, I completely throw out the idea that the entire Get of Fenris have a blood oath against all Sidhe. That was something introduced in Changeling, and I think should be an example of "failed" crossover. It was ill considered. It would retroactively ruin the chronicles where Get of Fenris PCs had been in a pack under Stag. So I replace that with the idea that there is one specific House of Sidhe that the Get have sworn revenge on. In my chronicles, this is the "House of Vanin" who are enemies because they made alliance with the All High (the Gangrel vampire who is basically Odin). That house replaces the similar House Aesin. I changed the name because if you are going to have a Changeling noble house based on the Norse, it should really be named after the Vanir, not the Aesir.)

                                  I like the idea that dealing with the Fae can be treacherous, and that their interests may not square with the Garou. There is an element of fun with that. But I don't want to set up the PCs to fail, be betrayed, or – worse - get upset in real life. What's a good balance that is fun to play out?

It's just a matter of how you portray the mixing so that it appears interesting, cool, and mysterious.

                                                  The way I handle Shapechangers and the fae is pretty much how you have detailed. Fianna will often have a pact of mutual aid with the local fae. Fae courts will often have Fianna representatives at court, though such persons are usually Kinfolk who have receives the Ritual of Parted Mists (permanent enchantment). Having fianna packs watching the borderlands of fae territory is a great way to portray this relationship. Fae assistance for the garous should not be underscored- fae can be very potent combatants, and their magical powers are potentially veryscary. Fae nobles should be Inhumanly beautiful and having seemingly limitless magic.

                                                  Other shapeshifters are also frequent visitors, especially Celican were-cats, Corax were-ravens and Nuwisha were-coyotes. Less often seen, but still present are Kitsune werefox’s, Pumonca were-puma, Qualmi were-lynx’s and Black Spiral Dancers (Shadow court only).


                                                  TOTEMS AND THE FAE
                                                  Along with Stag, there are many Incarna spirits with ties to the fae. From a Changeling perspective, I treat such spirits as the true fae dream lords known as the Oneiroi. If they can be personal totems, I am open to fae serving some of these spirits rather than a fae house (buying dots in Totem instead of Rank), but can never accept ranks in a Noble House as a result.
                                                  Some totems that I use, with solid fae ties are:
                                                  Bacchus (Dionysus)- This ancient Incarna of intoxication, sex and fertility is very old and powerful. Considered an ally by Black Furies and Satyrs (who claim he is an Unseelie fae, and the father of their kith), this entity is also respected by Fianna, Glass Walkers and some Malkavian Vampires.
                                                  Balor (Rorq, Incarnae of the Asteroid Belt)- Though defeated by Lugh of the Long Arm in ancient times, this Nightmare Incarnae is still around. The Sidhe of House Balor, Thallain, Unseelie Kithain, and Red Talons all have deep ties with this entity. Balor cannot be taken as a personal totem. Registers as a Incarna servant of the Wyrm of Balance or a really angry Wyld Incarna.
                                                  Coyote (Nuwisha)- Coyote is a totem spirit of Cunning, and also the patron of the Nuwisha shapechangers. Among the fae, the Canotili, Eshu, Nanehi, Pooka, and Satyrs kiths are the most likely to have ties with Coyote. Coyote cannot be taken as a totem by fae, though he generally likes fae.
                                                  Pan (Kokopelli)- Pan is the Dancer and the Lover. Black Furies, Kachina, Nuwisha, Pumonca, Uktena, Satyrs and Surem all have deep ties with this totem. Pan makes for an excellent personal totem for Kithain.
                                                  Loki – This fiery and argumentative Incarna is considered a troublemaker by shapechangers and fae alike, but he is a powerful, if quixotic, ally. Get of Fenris, Nuwisha, Redcaps, Sidhe of House Aesin, and Trolls have ties with Loki, though few serve this spirit directly.
                                                  Oghma – One of the Tuatha De Dannan, this Incarna is called the Bard and the Judge. Celican, Fianna, Nuwisha, and all Kithain have deep ties with this spirit. However, this spirit can only be bonded with through roleplaying.
                                                  Raven – This Incarna is well known among the fae. Though greatly respected, this totem does not take fae followers. Raven is most widely acknowledged by Nunnehi, Crow or Raven Pooka, Redcaps, Wendigo, Uktena and Fianna.
