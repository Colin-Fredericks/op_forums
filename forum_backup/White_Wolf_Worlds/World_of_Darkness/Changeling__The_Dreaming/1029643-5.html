    Good question. It's certainly not something that's normally done, but it certainly seems possible to do. The resulting chimera won't be the actual person, just an imperfect copy. Even the Art of Spring isn't capable of true resurrection.

    But yeah, I could see several plausible consequences. Because of his inability to let go and accept the changes that are part and parcel with life and instead preferring stasis, he might be inviting a Banality trigger. The way others react to him would vary from individual to individual. His own reaction might change as he realizes the chimera isn't actually his dead wife. Over time, he might grow to resent his creation, or he might learn to accept it as a new, unique chimerical individual. In any case, I could see lots of good fodder for a chronicle there.


    Yeah. It isn't... exactly Pygmalion here, and chimera creation in the sense that you mean isn't completely understood — some chimera just come to life on their own, while others that might have been created at the same time remain mindless. Some chimera are very good at faking the appearance of self-awareness, but they're just cleverly programmed (in the sense of forged chimera especially). Some attain sentience just as their creator intended, and others were never meant to be sentient, but wake up one day with a sense of self-identity.

                            Originally posted by Firkraag View Post
                            Nockers LOVE firearms!

                            I don't think, much more else can be said.

                            Also, considering, that chimera and fae tend to be ideals (in Plato's sense) the biggest danger might be that such chimera will be too much like a person it replaces. A perfect replice. Just enough good traits and just enough bed ones. Lotus Eater Machine of sorts.
                            Chimera spring from dreams, either the ambient dreams of an area that reflects that area in some way, or direct dreams. They can be created by changelings. They can be created by other chimera. They're not really Platonic ideals, since they can be abstracts, absurdities, or parallels. They're not Real Forms. A chimera birthed from the grief a person losing their partner will likely be touched by the emotions and thoughts that gave it substance, and might not be harmless. It could feed on the person's darker emotions, drive them deeper into it. It might not even be birthed from the person's desire to see their partner again, but an extant chimera using Redes like Shapeshift and Befuddle to mimic the dead partner.

                            Also, how realistic can be playing a chimera in C20?

                            Can't recall, whether stretch goal for Chimera expansion was taken or not...
                            I don't recall it as a stretch goal, but chimera creation and various kinds of chimera are detailed in the C20 core book. I probably wouldn't allow a player to create one, since they're not really meant to be player character options, IMO, but Storyteller tools. I suppose you could use the rules to play one, but I'm not sure how broad the game would be in scope. Chimera are pretty limited to just the Dreaming, and often to their particular area of the Dreaming, without certain Redes that tend to be rare (and expensive in point-buy cost).
