        Originally posted by Irioth View Post

        I think the problem people may have with this birthright is they mistakenly equate it with being roofied or made drunk against their will, which IMO goes against the intent of the authors. I prefer to equate it with being presented with the opportunity to indulge in an incredibly appealing sensual stimulus. Think of an incredibly sexy and charming person of the gender you find attractive walking up to you, with the food you most fancy in the world, and the music you prefer playing. In normal conditions, the temptation to indulge would be overwhelming, yet if the person truly wants to, they can resist and turn down the offer.

        On your second point, yes, the Dreaming absolutely does not play favorites for humans or their ethical expectations, nor it can reasonably expected to. It does not have a problem with the Fomorian Dream getting dominant again, and it is so vast and ancient it spans the whole psychic imprint of history and the world, so it would be silly to expect it would pay special regard to 21st century ideas about consent. Given how many of its features reflect the archetypes of power, violence, domination, and seduction, and how much these archetypes are fundamental to human/cosmic collective consciousness, It is only to be expected that several of the manifestations it spawns may have creepy and violating implications. We may choose not to give explicit representation to these aspects at our tables, but it is unreasonable to expect they wouldn't exist somewhere in the setting.
        I don't want to derail the thread too much, so I'll make this my final response, but basically, I don't think authorial intent matters much if it's easy to read it in another way. Your examples might still be cruel in various contexts. Tempting an alcoholic to drink, for instance. The Satyr might not be aware, but it could still be damaging. Or perhaps someone consents to sex they would not have otherwise consented to - perhaps a libidinous desire exists but sleeping with your spouse's boss is still a terrible idea. There really is no getting around the fact that the birthright can cause you to do things you may very much WANT to do but will still have dire and dramatic consequences after the fact.

        However, as I've said, I don't mind because it's not inherently exploitative. If the ONLY way for Satyr players to use the Gift of Pan was creepy then there would be a problem. But I don't believe that's the case.

        With regards to 21st century ideas about consent, well, it's a 21st century game, played by 21st century humans. I think the actual problem is that what constitutes consent in the 21st century is still disputed, particularly by certain less savoury "alt-right" aligned parts of gamer subculture.

        My position is that the Gift of Pan has the inherent capacity to be gross, but that this isn't a problem as long as everyone is on the approximately the same page about where the line of "gross" lies and what topics they're comfortable exploring in the game.

        Others - as I understand it - feel that they are already at times besieged by an unsafe environment and find the Gift of Pan's potential grossness a red flag that will make awkward situations more likely in a game environment.

        Then we get into the matter of gaming in spaces that feel safe to us, and it's easier for me to say that I wouldn't game with jerks like that because I have a realworld gaming group. Others have to play with strangers online and so are more leery of ethical grey areas like this. I understand that, and I also understand that the whole "but it's just what you secretly wanted!" aspect of the birthright could make it more difficult to call out as creepy (whereas using dictum to demand someone do something is inarguably a violation of free will so one could assume the game would treat it as such). But ultimately I still feel like there are many, many other cantrips and abilities in this game (indeed in most games) that can be used in gross ways, that the debate over the Gift of Pan is in part a flashpoint for wider concerns about gaming culture and how to negotiate what's off limits and how to handle uncomfortable material.

        I still mostly come down on the side of C20 not being responsible for that, and the answer not being to make the orgy fae less good at orgies, but instead just realistically dealing with when it's okay to start an orgy and/or dealing with the fallout when it wasn't if a gaming group is comfortable exploring that.

        I'm not unsympathetic to the alternate opinion, though. As I said, I'm conscious that I am speaking from the perspective of someone who has a gaming group she feels fairly comfortable with.

        Anyway - sorry for the derail. It's an interesting question and I didn't want to let it lie. Maybe we should start another thread if we want to continue the discussion?

                        About Neustria (p. 76).

                        I always figured that the Duchy of '"Bayeaux" was named after the medieval town in Normandy which proper spelling is "Bayeux" (without the second "a").

                        Also, the name of the duke is Conte Hillaire du Lille.

                        First, it should be spelled "Comte" (Count) and not "Conte" (which means a "tale", like a fairy tale).

                        And more important: Bayeux is in western France and this area has a very different history and dream I would say than northern France where Lille is located.

                        About Burgundy: I concur with the forum member who said that Lord Guy de Cheval sounds a little bit ridiculous, which is not a good idea a for sidhe.

                        Lady Genève is not French. I also concur with the forum member who proposed instead Guenièvre.

                        Aquitaine: since it is an independent desmene, then it should not be a duchy, because a duke is a war lord in the service of a king. Rather, Aquitaine should be a principality, and Rathesmere the Prince of Aquitaine (which would have a nice ring to it, as it recalls the Hundred Years War and would explain why Aquitaine is independent from Neustria).

                        Finally, a last question: you seem to have had the rights to use the kiths from the Monde des Ténèbres: France book, so why not use the duchies and counties and kingdoms from it too?
