                        Originally posted by PookaKnight View Post
                        While certain sidhe could definitely be horrific, I'd hardly call them the central horror of Changeling. The true darkness of Changeling is that no matter what you do, no matter what you accomplish, you are doomed forget it and you're true nature. No one will ever know what kind of life you lived.

                        It's actually something that hits me a lot harder these days than when I was playing the game back in its heyday. The past several years I've had to watch as Alzheimer's has slowly taken my grandmother. Now days, her body is there, but she just isn't. Believe me, that's some gut wrenching, horrific stuff, and it's the fate of every single changeling. The lucky ones are the who go out in a blaze of glory fighting some monster or an evil sidhe ruler.



                        I agree, a lot of people liked to hate on classic Changeling because the artwork was more whimsical and happy than the rest of the series, but that is exactly what made it so heartbreaking to play a Changeling in my opinion. You start off with this sense of childish whimsy and imagination, and then you're slowly crushed by the passing of the ages and the demands of society as you come to maturity.It's the story of a child losing their innocence and becoming bitter, unimaginable people because the world doesn't work the way they thought it did. Even if you manage to retain your Changeling nature into adulthood or beyond, people aren't going to think you're cute or playful when you start swinging a pen at an invisible dragon, they are probably going to throw you into an asylum while you desperately try to provoke their imagination with grandiose tales of magic and wonder.


                        And then, of course, there are the consequences for having lived as a supernatural in the first place. It doesn't matter if you forget what you are or if you become human again, you have been living among the supernatural societies for years and it is very likely you've made some enemies. Vampires, werewolves, mages and yes even the Sidhe, they're known to hold a grudge and now you have no special powers to defend yourself. Hell you might not even remember they exist anymore! Without your powers and memories, you are defenseless and they can strike at any time.


                        Out of all the classic splats, Changeling is the one I would be least eager to play. Not because of the artwork or the peter pan style characters, but because the overarching theme of the game is depressing, which makes it a perfect addition to the World of Darkness!

                                                            Originally posted by Astromancer View Post
                                                            . . .                                                             Reading through your posts, I think you're getting hung up on the nature of the changelings, and the Dreaming in general. The fae aren't, and never were human. Broadly speaking, they're living out, over and over again, the stories that humanity has dreamed for them.

                                                            As a result, the fae - any of the fae (not just sidhe) - are capable of both great evil and great good. A sidhe born of the myth of the Good King will be the Good King: wise as Solomon, brave as Arthur, willing to die for the least of his subjects. Particularly for a non-Scathach sidhe, there's no humanity (or human soul) to water that down, or be corrupted. Every action of such a sidhe will be in accordance with every hope and dream humanity has every had for a leader.

                                                            Real world mythology is full of legends about heroic nobles and aristocrats. Horatio at the bridge, where a lone aristocrat fought to the death against an army, all to protect Rome. Roland, the warrior bishop, during the reign of Charlemagne . . . Heck, the stories about the Founding Fathers (of the American Revolution) may well have spawned a sidhe or two . . . "I cannot tell a lie" . . .

                                                            Of course, not every story has a happy ending. And any good story needs villains to oppose, monsters to slay, dark fortresses to storm . . . So when a fae (or freehold) goes bad, they go really evil. The boggan servant, who betrays and murders the "Good King" sidhe who saved him as a child, all for a few bucks. The troll who decides to turn a preschool into a snack bar (remember, Unseelie trolls are called "ogres" for a reason . . . ) The nocker, who decides that Jigsaw (from Saw) had the right idea, and starts trying to "fix' humans with his diabolic machines. . .

                                                            Does that let evil sidhe off the hook? No . . . and doing so would be missing the point. But as long as humans believe that good will triumph in the end, the damage they can do is limited - which limits their ability to be horrific, keeping it (reasonably) localized. Which prevents them from being the greatest horror (particularly since in many places, the Commoners pushed back hard).

                                                            If, as the play says, "I dreams begin responsibility." then the Sidhe are a total abdication of responsibility on all levels. Much like the 14th century English lords that were the models of the Sherriff of Notingham, the Sidhe can't see why their desires aren't seen as normal, good and fine. Just as the nobles saw the rebellious peasants as inspired by the devil, so the Sidhe can only see the commoners as warped or sick. Just like the 19th century slave-holders who saw the desire of their slaves to run away as a mental illness, or present day Europeans like yourself who see all Americans who don't want to ritually humiliated 24/7 as right-wing goons, you avoid seeing that the critique has a point.

                                                            The great horror of the Sidhe is the basis of tragedy, the moral blindness of good people. The Seelie Sidhe do have high ideals, but because they can not see that they are hundreds of years out of step with the times and that aristocracy is no longer valuable, this makes them promotes great evils because of a basic blindness. Just as the standard European view of Americans as worthless Nazi trash poisons the possibility of trans-Atlantic communication.
                                                            Again, I think you're missing the point of the sidhe. Yes, there are reckless and irresponsible sidhe. Quite a few are outright evil. But they're there for the "good guy" sidhe, or heroic commoner changelings, to take down or redeem. A story where everyone's good, happy, and wealthy is usually quite boring, after all.

                                                            And sure, there are sidhe who don't get the whole "democracy" and "human rights" thing - the intro to Nobles: the Shining Host was written by one, after all. But by definition, a good guy sidhe has to care about the commoners, his story "DNA" requires that he be a good leader to them. If that means giving them a constitution, or a parliament, or rule-of-law courts . . . that's what he'll do.

                                                            Now, you could get some good stories out of the "moral blindness of good people" - a staple of stories since . . . Oedipus Rex or so. But the sidhe don't just play aristocrats in the Dreaming - what they are requires that they live their lives (even the mundane parts) as befits their ancestral story. The brave firefighter who races into a five alarm fire to save an old lady or little kid is very much a staple of the sidhe, as is the charismatic nihilist who leads a band of anarchists in city-wide riots.



                                                            Edit: On a side note, given how popular (and inspired) "Lost Cause" literature was, I wouldn't be surprised to find a Gone with the Wind-themed freehold or two. Heck, I'd be surprised if there wasn't a chimerical Tara or two out there. Changelings build with dreams, after all.

                                                            But by the same token, most of the famed sites from the Civil Rights movement (at least those not claimed by other supernaturals) have probably been turned into freeholds, too. The dream of freedom is one of the most potent, after all. And it wouldn't be the first - the site of the Liberty Tree is now a well-know freehold in Boston (The Fool's Gambit)
