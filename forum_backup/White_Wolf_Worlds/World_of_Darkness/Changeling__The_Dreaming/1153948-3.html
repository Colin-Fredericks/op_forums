    No you don't get it, do you? It's very easy to grab one and beat the chimerical snot out of them until they are undone and their fairy souls go dormant. Then you leave the human behind and deal with the Dauthain's return when it comes back. Just keep an eye on them with Kenning and Soothsay. The mists take care of the rest. No big deal. Everyone has to go alone some time and they won't remember since their Banality is high as heck. Autumn world impact: Some guy sleeps in really long on a weirdly regular basis. No moral qualms. Killing a Chimera has more impact. Changelings die differently from humans. You have to keep that in mind! And no, that's not gaming the system. The Fae know how the Dreaming works.

    If a Dauthan can be reintegrated on the other hand (like they could be in CtD, heck, "The Autumn People" had rules to make your character a Dauthain for a while so you could play a redemption arc) you actually have a conundrum. You want to basically take a person who oozes banality and who might hate the Dreaming and shove as much Glamour back into them as possible. Maybe they fight back. Maybe they go along and screw up your personal quest. Maybe they go along and betray you. Maybe something else happens. But at least you have OPTIONS! Heck, having some incredibly strict politician around, who runs on a platform of getting fiction books out of public libraries and having them take part in a Satyr song contest would make for an amazing session!

    (PS: The Path of Iron is the dumbest thing I have ever seen! Changelings CAN SEE WHAT HAPPENS WHEN THEY DIE! Chimerical death: Mien dissipates and human remains, asleep but alive. Real Death: Human falls over dead, Fae mien remains visible via Kenning and becomes more and more ideal until the darkness itself pulls in back into the body it will reincarnate in. Death by cold iron: The fae mien is visibly torn asunder as it suffers the death of consciousness and the perpetrators get permanent banality every time, while being incapable of filling up their Glamour and remaining in constant pain as long as they keep their weapons. Nobody is that stupid! No, not even Ogres!)
    (PPS: To express how I feel right now https://youtu.be/uR0WoO87Qyw?t=4m56s )


    You also have to keep in mind that when a faerie soul permanently dies, without killing the physical body, the former host loses all memory of their fae identity. So that Dauntain you just beat the snot out of? Yeah, he's going to wake up to find he's missing a significant gap in time and is probably many miles away from where he was before the Dauntain bonded to him.

    And Chimerical Death doesn't kill the fae soul, it just ejects them from the dreaming and they suffer a bit of amnesia. To truly get rid of them, you have to kill the soul, which involves cold iron, or extremely high levels of Banality.

    As for the Iron Road, I merely brought it up as a suggestion. And it's one that one of the noble houses continues to practice in secret, even though it lead to them being imprisoned for centuries the first time around. Also, forgot to mention that the book describes their murders as being ritualistic, which means they don't just walk up and stab you to death.

    As for not being able to redeem a Dauntain, who's to say you can't? I've seen a bunch of players around here who tell me that the rule books don't have to be followed strictly to the letter, you're free to home brew some stuff if you want. First edition of V:tR, flat out says that some players choose to just ignore the Predator's Taint mechanic entirely, due to its complications.


    The 20th anniversary books are focused almost entirely around the basic mechanics of the game. It gives you all the rules you need for running a game, but doesn't go into great detail about the fluff material. If you want there to be a way to heal the Dauntain in your game, I don't see any reason why it can't exist. Remember, Changeling is a game about daring to dream, and if conventional wisdom says that something is impossible, then any true Changeling should respond with "challenge accepted!"

                zenten Davesknd

                I can't remember the specifics of how it worked, but something in the C20 rules of death mentions how perma-killing the soul of somebody who is in the Dreaming causes the physical body to slip into a deep coma from which they never awaken. And when somebody dies in the dreaming, their physical body (if they have one) is deposited back into the physical world.

                In order for the host body to return to their normal life (if that's even possible) is for the fae inside of them to be Undone, which requires very high levels of Banality. This would be a very time consuming and difficult process for any Changeling to accomplish, since they're literally trying to force Banality onto somebody and doing so at such a level that the Dauntain's spirit completely fades from existence.


                As for that quote about the possibility of rehabilitating them, notice how it says "as if they're criminals of society." Because that's very important thing to notice.

                As we said before, the Dauntain are traumatized individuals, their chrysalis and awakening into the Dreaming was a painful and horrifying experience. They didn't just wake up one day and decide to go on a killing spree of everything related to the faerie folk. They are scared, confused and suffering. It's going to take a hell of a lot more than locking them up in prison and forcing them to perform civic labor to get them to straighten out their behavior. They might very well require therapy, but the last time the Sidhe did any research into the subject, people were getting lobotomized with metal screws!


                As for your suggestion of just chimerically killing them every time the Dauntain's spirit resurfaces, think about what kind of ramifications that would have upon the host body? If they just randomly start losing gaps in time, or waking up in strange places with no memory of how they got there? Even if we assume the Changelings are waiting in the shadows with a laser pointer aimed at their skulls 24/7, the host is eventually going to suffer from some kind of psychotic break down, which could very well make their Dauntain spirit even worse than it already is.


                The world has changed, the Dreaming has changed, and even the immortal fae have been forced to change. So is it so hard to imagine that their way of dealing with enemies might change, too?

                Just because nobody's ever done it before, doesn't mean it can't be done. Which, again is what the spirit of the Dreaming is all about, daring to do the impossible. Finding an effective treatment option for the Dauntain could even be made into a quest for a Changeling who's acquired too much permanent Banality and is at risk of becoming Undone.
