    Originally posted by PookaKnight View Post

    Unlike V20, W20, and M20, C20 is going to be a true 3rd edition along with being an anniversary edition. Pretty much all of the mechanics, including the seemings, are getting tweaked at least a little bit. The goal, though, is to get to the heart of Changeling: The Dreaming and really make everything build to the core themes of the game. Everyone involved is a huge fan of Changeling: The Dreaming. Lost and DA: Fae are great games, but they're not Changeling: The Dreaming. We all want to make this the Dreamingest edition of Dreaming that's ever been Dreamed!

    One thing I will say about seemings - in my first play test session I was lucky enough to have all of my original players from back when we all first discovered Changeling: The Dreaming together in one place again. We all remade our very first Changeling characters using the C20 play test documents. Back in the day, all of these characters were wilders, but this was the first time I've ever had a childling, wilder, and grump all as PCs in the same group. It was glorious!

    Yeah, we never got a proper revised edition, which was always a real shame. That and the Book of Glamour. I adore the magic system, it has some of the openness and creativity of Mage but without stepping on that game's toes and while maintaining a great, off-kilter feel. But it would really have benefited from a clean-up pass and tidy-up. As you say, DA: Fae and Lost were great games, and for many people, I'm sure, better than Changeling, but... Yeah. I've loved it since I was barely more than a Childling, and I'm now a long way into my Grump years and I've yet to find any game I love quite as much.

    Also thanks for the teasers about the playtesting. We're currently running an epic, All-Bard D&D Planescape game, but our group has agreed, once that's over, we really need to revisit Changeling either with the new 20th Anniversary edition, or in preparation for it. I get that sometimes it can be challenging, but I've always found mixing the seemings to be great fun too. It adds a really interesting additional element with regards to mortal society.

                                                        Originally posted by Lian View Post
                                                        the ability to play a banality 3 30 yearold should be possible it should just come at a price. Just like if I want to to tell the story of the death of childhood in a pure childhood setting that should also be an option.
                                                        So what should the price be? As I've said, I'm not against the idea of there being specific merits or flaws that shift these boundaries, or for specific STs to houserule things in ways that work for their stories or groups. And there is a core mechanic for reducing permanent banality - it's noted to be possible through the completion of epic quests and triumphs. I also always assumed the starting banality of wilders and grumps referred to an average - how much most Changelings would have accumulated by then. A 30 year old noble who has refused to leave his Freehold in years might well still have a banality of 3 (and probably be at higher risk of Bedlam than being Undone). Likewise a truly heroic grump, who has completed many heroic quests may have succeeded in reducing her banality and keeping it low.

                                                        If you are creating NPCs, there is no need to adhere to the starting stats, and if you want to play a player character which doesn't stick to them either, then that should be a conversation between you and your Storyteller. If you want to tell a story of children trapped in awful conditions and agree that everyone will play childling characters, but begin with grump tempers to reflect the specific atmosphere and environment of the campaign, power to you. Themed campaigns can be really affecting and engaging.

                                                        The setting and the mechanics that describe the setting are designed to be tools that establish the world in its most common configurations. This means that where dramatically appropriate they can be inverted, or subverted to great effect. But that is also possible because they establish the thematic baseline in the first place.

                                                        In this case, that the world wears on the faerie soul, and the longer you are in it, the greater the struggle.

                                                        So we come back to that 30 year old character with a banality of 3. What should the price be? Decoupling age from seeming means that there will be no price, it's player choice at no price. It ceases to be unusual. It ceases to be triumphant.
