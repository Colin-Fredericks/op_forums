    My Probelm with Changeling

    02-25-2014, 06:28 PM
    I know that there's a C20 thread right now that's gone off tangent a bit towards this, but I felt that I'd rather start a new thread instead of risking derailing that one further.

    With M20 around the corner and Wr20 in the works, to some extent talk of C20 is a bit early. However, as has been noted elsewhere, CtD has always teetered a bit on the edge of whether or not it is popular enough to really sell and if we're going to see C20 drumming up support for it is important.

    My problem though, is that I really don't like CtD as is. I think it has a lot of potential as a game, but to fix it enough that I'd really want to buy it I would need one core issue addressed that goes beyond cleaning up rules, fixing some of the fluff, or promises to avoid artwork that completely fails to sell the game's potential.

    I think my position is best summed up simply as: Changeling: the Dreaming lacks nuance to the point where it actively harms the theme of the game.

    Having a game about the loss of creativity, imagination, and so on in the world is a good start. But the game shoots itself in the foot by being extremely heavy-handed and binary about the concepts of what "wonder" is, and what the central struggle of the game is supposed to be.

    The game presents lots of interesting places to take things. For example it presents the concept of Court as being mutable with shifts between Seelie and Unseelie some thing that occurs for many changelings. But it never discusses it in any real detail (and then starts throwing in groups that are always one or the other anyway). There's no incentive in the games to have your character explore both sides of themselves. There's no conflict between these two aspects of their personality.

    Banality is a neat concept, but the game avoids a nuanced approach to what counts as Banal for borderline insulting generalizations (aging = banal, science/tech = banal, social norms = banal or art = not banal, liberal arts colleges = not banal, deviant behavior = not banal). Sure the game tries to walk this back a bit (if a scientific achievement captures the public imagination in can cause an explosion of glamour... but what about the simple events like a group of scientists discovering a brand new species?), but never really takes the steps to codify this as something that matters to play.

    Even the artwork, which most people seem to agree does a poor service to the game, is really problematic because of it being very one-sided. Typing this reminds me of an episode of the SyFy show Face Off where the contestants had to design "Burton-esque" (which I believe is the title of the episode) costumes. They had to capture something both whimsical and fantastic, with something dark and twisted. One of the top designs was a Cellist. It's a beautiful piece. But what twists it into also being dark is that her cello is also her lover... and if she doesn't play often enough he dies. It is touchingly sweet and disturbingly tragic at the same time. Something CtD's artwork and fluff often seems to fail to find the right balance for. It isn't that a balloon carrying talking bear can't be part of the artwork, but it needs a twist. Even something as simple as the balloons having faces; they show fear if the bear offers them to another, or start to silently scream and cry if they're released. We don't even need to know why. It just adds the edge of something not being 100% right with the situation and lets us see a hidden darkness behind the bubbly art.

    My biggest concern with getting interested in C20 is, well, that none of this is going to change.

              I think there's room for Dreaming and Lost to coexist. They focus on very different things in the same genre. I enjoy Lost immensely because it is a very solid game. But I don't think Dreaming needs to be Lost. It should absolutely have everything in it that makes its fans love it so much. But there's so much room to make Dreaming the game it always could have been that would sell to all the people that could never get past some of the frustrating elements of it.

                  I will agree that there is room for both Dreaming and Lost. Let's also not forget Dark Ages Fae, which I personally believe to be BY FAR the superior White Wolf Fae game line. All three are very different games though and should each be judged on their own merits and weaknesses.

                  The difference between C20 and V20 or W20 is that Vampire and Werewolf were successful lines up until the end. Changeling, whatever anyone may think of it, collapsed. It just wasn't popular enough. That means that when making a new edition it's not good enough to just rehash the old stuff the fans loved and repackage it with some setting and rules updates. It won't sell. It didn't sell then and it won't now. If they want C20 to be successful, as I do, I think they really need to take a harder look at why it failed in the first place and address some core issues with the game itself. Vampire and Werewolf and (hopefully) Mage are anniversary editions appealing to all the many people who loved them in the old days. Changeling needs to be more if it is going to stand up on its own.

                                  I think on the topic of describing Banality, it would be good to take the route that the Mage games have regarding vulgar vs. not vulgar magic. Give examples of superficially similar situations where one is Banal and the other is not. For example:

                                  Flipping burgers at O'Tolley's is dreary mind-numbing work and is Banal. Being a chef in a craft burger joint lets you be creative and artistic and is not Banal.

                                  What you're doing in both situations is effectively the same: making burgers. The technology and the materials aren't the key difference. The difference is that one is cookie-cutter and designed for uniformity, while the other is designed to be unique and experimental.

                                  I feel that using a few examples like that help get the point of Banality across and avoid some of the sillier over-generalizations of the original books.

                                              I loved the premise of Changeling the Dreaming. Each of the original games was a vehicle to explore critical philosophical dilemmas of humanity, and the question of Changeling was: How does one balance the needs of the child within against the demands of the world without? To that end, I never concerned myself with the artwork, or the pseudo-descriptive terms for banality and glamour. What was most distressing was the inability to see the congruence of meaning above the limitations of preconception. Inspired science is as glamorous as inspired jazz. Equally, uninspired science is as banal as Leonard Nemoy singing "Sitting on the dock of the bay". (If you have ever heard the LP, you know what I mean, and if you haven't - protect your soul and don't.)

                                              Where Changeling the Dreaming was impossible for me was the actual mechanics of the powers. The total 'if-iness' of whether or not the cards would combine to permit a bunk within the purview of the PC left me unwilling to play the game. However, I still love the setting, the factions, and kithain. In my private outline, I have place for a population of 300 Nunehi and traditional Kithain. However, because of the radically different mechanics I have no intention of making them a functional part of any action.

                                                          V20 was written as a "love letter to and in honor of the fans of V:tM" (paraphrased.) Other than update the book so that the material wasn't stuck in 1999 (updating technology and getting rid of costs of living, etc.), making some rules tweaks, and including mechanics/splats for everything they could find, V20 didn't really introduce new concepts to the game. That was done purposefully, and it shows. Even the supplements (so far) haven't really upset the understanding/concept of the game.

                                                          W20 was very similar, taking the expected and understood stuff that fans knew, updating it, and including as much of the world as they could into one book. It too hasn't really introduced anything that reveals "new truths", or alters concepts.

                                                          M20 is going to be our first time I think we see what happens when a developer has gone back and basically re-written the core book from the ground up, but still takes everything that made the fat splat what it was and sort of re-presents it all at once. I get the feeling that Phil didn't lift a single word from previous editions, but rather took what was the "truth" of them to build 20th Anniversary Edition for all fans of Mage: the Ascension.

                                                          Wraith:20, IMO, may be one of the first opportunities for one of the X20 books to really have to look at itself and ask "do I keep the mechanics/game as it was, or do I alter it to appeal to a larger audience?" I suspect we will see a similar treatment to Wraith:20 as we'll have seen from the previous 3, but maybe we'll get some "Storyteller options", or new light cast in some ways that make it more... digestible to a greater audience. Then again, the X20 lines are about honoring the fans of the original games, not necessarily drawing new players in. It was also the only line to have an official end in the story, so we'll see how that's handled too...

                                                          C:20 will be influenced by the previous 4, and by the developer chosen for the project--if the developer just wants to re-hash the old book, then there won't be much change, but if they really take to it and use this to create something that it never had a chance to become, then we may see something that has learned from the last 10+ years of games and the emergence of the mainstream Modern Fantasy that it was the beginning of as well. The parts of T:tL that were also in C:tD but under-represented may be highlighted IF they would enhance the game, and it'll be interesting to see if Cantrip cards make a return as well (even as an optional rule, or stretch-goal on DriveThruCards.)

                                                          Will highlighting old areas of the C:tD game that weren't well illuminated make the game more fun for old players and introduce and be fun for new players? I guess we'll just have to see what the direction Rich and the new author/developer decide to do.
