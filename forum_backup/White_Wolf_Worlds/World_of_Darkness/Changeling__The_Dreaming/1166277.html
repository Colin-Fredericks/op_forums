    Some C20 Questions

    12-09-2017, 10:33 AM
    I'm planning on running a Changeling The Dreaming 20th Anniversary Edition campaign in the near future. I was a fan of the CWoD for years as a teenager, but haven't really picked it up much for over a decade.

    I was hoping that with the X20 editions a lot of the flaws from poor game design and unclear rules/systems would be fixed seeing as NWoD/CoD is fairly tight mechanically. Unfortunately that doesn't seem to be the case and now I have a ton of systems that need to be clarified and seemingly incomplete mechanics that I need explained.

    Some of the issues are C20 specific, while others may be more CWoD 20 general, but I'm just going to list them all and see what you guys can do to help out.

    Delaying/With holding actions - I think I understand this one. As I interpret it, when declaring actions in reverse initiative, if I choose to withhold my action, I am basically sacrificing my action that round in order to go first next round. Is this correct?

    Shields vs. firearms - I also think I get this one. So if I try to use my shield vs a gunshot, it says that I have to make a successful parry, but I don't subtract successes from the attacker's roll. So I assume this means that if someone shooting me got 4 successes on their attack roll, I would need to match those 4 successes on my parry roll to be able to use my shield soak. Correct?

    Mounted combat - C20 states that your Dex+Ride is the maximum pool you can use for combat actions when mounted, and that when my mount may lose control I have to make a reflexive ride roll. The problem with this is twofold. First Ride isn't a skill in C20 so I can't roll it, and second it doesn't say what attribute to roll for the roll to maintain control. I assume you can sub in Animal Ken for Ride, but what attribute would I roll to maintain control? It seems implied that this would also be Dex, but that makes no sense at all, as Strength or Charisma would likely be better options with what little I know about riding animals.

    Medicine ability - It doesn't actually do anything. There are no mechanics for how it can be used to treat injuries or diseases, so why even have it as an ability?

    Rhapsody - How much Glamour does it actually generate? Is it just the amount initially invested in the process, or is it one per success on the artist's creation roll? Either way it really doesn't seem worth doing, even before considering the non-mechanical in-game consequences of doing it. To even consider Rhapsody one would have to be both Evil (TM) and really dumb. So how can Rhapsody be made to be something actually tempting to try?

    Effects of Banality - p. 270 of C20 states several mechanical effects of Banality, but it is pretty unclear as to under what circumstances the GM should apply those effects to a character.

    Invoking the Wyrd - Does this take an action or can it be done reflexively?

      Delaying your action means precisely that: you opt to take it at any point after your normal initiative for tactical reasons. It's not totally clear because it first refers to holding, and then to yielding, your action. There's also ambiguity in the way it goes on to describe this. It seems to imply you can both act at the end of the turn *and* go first on the next round of combat if you've got a low initiative score. But I don't think that's what it actually means, because otherwise characters who act last naturally would always yield to go first the next round.

      I think holding/yielding is actually aimed at high initiative characters rather than low initiative characters, and is designed to prevent slower characters defending against your attacks. When it says, 'Doing this allows characters with lower initiative scores to act first', what I think it means is: 'Doing this allows a faster character to hold off revealing his action until slower characters have first acted, in order to prevent slower characters defending themselves.'

      The section on shields is definitely unclear. It seems to suggest multiple things: first, that shields can act like armour; then that they can also be a resisted action; then that they're a simple action against firearms. Which is it?

      It seems like a mix of the first and last options. So first you have to make a successful parry roll (only one success required), and if you succeed, then you get to add the shield's full rating as extra armour to your soak roll. Whether extra successes on this parry roll should add to the soak dice too is up to the Storyteller--though I'd probably argue they shouldn't, because you either hold the shield in the way or you don't. It would seem you can only defend against one shooter this way, too, unless you split your dice pool for multiple actions. What you describe is a resisted roll, which this explicitly isn't (but it is how parries normally work in melee).

      The *20 lines have blank lines for custom/rare/niche Abilities at the end of each section on the character sheet (Hobby Talent, Professional Skill, Expert Knowledge). This is where Ride goes--specifically, it would probably fall under 'Professional Skill'. However, you could also rule that it's a Secondary Ability. Rules vary for Secondary Abilities, but they're usually half price OR offer a reduced difficulty (-1, I think) because they're a bit more focused/niche. I always go for the half price thing, since it encourages characters to specialise. As a third option, you could make it a specialty of Animal Ken or Athletics.

      I would argue that for regaining control of a horse, it's Dexterity + Ride, since that's the dicepool it mentions. If it were a different pairing, it would mention it instead of just saying 'a reflexive ride roll'. However, cWoD usually leaves lots of leeway for Attribute + Ability pairings, so you could probably sub in Strength or Wits here if your ST agreed (my rule is that if a player can convince me one pairing should be used over another, I allow it).

      If you're subbing in Animal Ken, then I'd make it Manipulation + Animal Ken or Charisma + Animal Ken, depending on how the rider interacts with her horse (firmness and cajoling versus charm and placating).

      Medicine - This happens a lot. If in doubt, Golden Rule it. Other books do have systems for Medicine, I think, but I could be wrong. I'm pretty sure I read something about first aid and healing somewhere, but I may have imagined it.

      Personally, I'd use Wits + Medicine for triage (stops a character bleeding to death if they've lost their last health level) or for temporarily reducing dicepool penalties from injury for a period of time (maybe one success equals either one die restored or one extra turn for the reduced penalty). You could diagnose with Intelligence + Medicine. Heal damage quicker (maybe reduce the healing time of one health level by one step for each success rolled, so that if you roll three successes, the last three health levels each heal one step faster). Understand the effects of a drug. Figure out whether a character has been poisoned. Plenty of uses. It's just sadly left up to the ST.

      The Rhapsody write-up specifically says it's two points of Glamour for each success rolled, but this is shared between all changelings present when the artwork is destroyed. Let's say four changelings put two points in each, so the ST rolls Manipulation + Expression + 8 dice (difficulty 7). That nets, say, 12 successes. Each changeling would only get three points of Glamour. However, if one of the changelings decided to sneak into the artist's studio and destroy the artwork without his friends present, he could take the whole lot (up to his maximum Glamour pool) and the rest would be wasted.

      Yes, Rhapsody really isn't worth it, and it never has been. Mind you, most Musings require lots of time and attention for little reward too, so it's not just Rhapsody that suffers. Only Revelry is really worth it, but that can only be done once per story.

      My personal hack has been to allow characters to roll Glamour + Dreamers once per session, so long as they're maintaining ongoing connections with the humans they've been Musing, to regain one point per success (difficulty 6 if they're regularly working on the Musings, 7 if part-time, 8 if they've been flaky). This allows a steady flow of Glamour from people you're Musing, which makes more sense to me anyway, since art is as much about process and inspiration as it is about the finished product.

      Read p.269: the instances relate to a character's Banality rating. So a human with Banality 10 will completely forget her experiences with the fae as soon as they're over. A human with Banality 1 will have Complete Recall. Banality 5 humans will have a Hazy Memory, etc. The effects kick in as soon as the fae exit the scene or they are no longer in the Wyrd-state. Ignore the durations on the table, though, as those refer only to the length of a changeling's coma after chimerical death.

      Invoking the Wyrd takes an action; it's not reflexive. However, you can Unleash instead and automatically invoke the Wyrd alongside your use of an Art (more cost-effective if you can't risk losing a turn in combat).

                                                          That last quote of yours means that if a high initiative character yields his action to wait and see what the lower initiative players do he also allows them, characters with lower initiative, to act first, that is before the high initiative player who yielded.

                                                          So holding back your action means you get to act at a lower initiative than you rolled, like if you just want to see what the rest do before acting, but since you wait untill you see others act you naturally act after them. The main use usually is to delay your action so that you can interrupt a lower initiative character or to react to their action instead of acting first.

                                                          Hope that clears the matter a bit even if I find it rather difficult to put in simple terms.

                                                          Edit: I thought it prudent to underline the fact that yielding your turn or holding your action never affects your next turn. So no it doesnt allw you to act first the next round. Such effects in WoD are reserved for supernatural abilities and powers, no normal action can give you an auto-win on initiative.
