    CTD Glamour Economy

    05-17-2014, 05:44 AM
    Okay, it's been noted in different places that some folks feel the Changeling Glamour Economy is too tight, unbalanced etc.

    Now I've never had a problem with it mostly through the interpretation and use of Dross in both the games I've run but also that I've played in (typically over the net) with a whole host of ST's. Maybe because that's I've always asked the question about my character's environment to notice potential sources of quick glamour and so forth.

    I'd quite like it hear peoples views on the matter, but more than that, how would YOU fix them at your table?

    I'll start off though with how I've always done it.

    I use the Ravaging and Musing thresholds and systems as are, I like them and it seems that most of the CTD die hard crowd on here likes them. I like the idea that a Changeling's personal Glamour pool is a rare and powerful resource and that to rip creativity from a mind is a "bad" thing. I like that nurturing Creativity creates a returnable resource. I also like the variable and yet potent way it can restore Glamour (don't forget it's Successes in Glamour points returned).

    Dross, this is what i believe is the key to the Changeling Glamour system and economy and one that is often over looked due to it's lack of "hard game mechanics" in obtaining, it's not there in any backgrounds, it's very very variable on what dross is and so forth. Yet there in lies it's beauty and power. It's precious but common enough to use as a form of currency amongst Changelings (just look at the Market over the Wall in Stardust or any number of other faerie markets, they don't use money but things of 'worth'). It's applications are also varied.

    Fonts: Restore Glamour points to Changelings, they are rare and often Freeholds are built around them but they could be found anywhere. It's a nice extra little boost next to the balefire of a Freehold if it is found within one and a bonus if stumbled across outside of one.

    Chimera: Eating the flesh of a Chimerical Creature (Dragon hearts, leg of White Hart, whatever else tickles the fancy) can restore Glamour points, although doing this TOO often can cause problems (like bedlam, or poisoning from dangerous beasts etc), but kind of like the Neverfood of Neverland in Hook it'll restore your Glamour (Feasts at the Noble Court or Common Hold you say?) depending on how powerful the beast is. You can also draw Glamour from broken chimerical items but this for me feels a little less 'in theme', but as a rule it's there.

    Treasures and Mementos: They're essentially the same thing when it comes down to it and it's where most of the Dross comes from in it's currency format and randomness. Some of the examples listed are wondrous works that are probably actual Treasures (per the Background) and destroying these for Glamour is Bad (gaining Banality). But other things listed are far more reasonable, this is where the children's storybook, the old pocket watch, the photo of friends, the graffiti on the wall comes in. There are 1 pt pieces of dross lurking throughout the world of darkness and if a Changeling has the wit (and the Keening) to look for them he'll find tiny sources of power all around.

    Good example of the "treasures" being my Sidhe Knight confronted by a Corby in an old underpass, that had seen centuries of rebellious teens come down here and draw or paint the walls, scratch slogans in the cement etc, all the creative outpouring of emotion. I need a boost of glamour Quick to use Sovereign on the Caps so hand slaps onto a glimmering vein of Glamour running through the Graffiti, the images fade away and some cement dusts as I draw it into my character and use it to weave my cantrip.

    So, what's everyone else's thoughts ad how to and so forth?

                  Glamour is used anytime you want something chimerical in nature to part the Mists. It's used to fuel certain Arts and empower oaths. You can spend it to enchant mortals and create chimera. It can be spent to help overcome Banality and as a health track against certain Banality fueled attacks.

                  In short, it can easily be spent constantly in any given game session where something interesting is happening.

                  How does a character regain spent Glamour? The book presents the most common method as the four paths to epiphany - Reverie, Rapture, Ravaging, and Rhapsody.
                  • If a person is extraordinarily lucky, they'll be able to engage in Reverie that only takes a few days or weeks. More often, they'll need to spend between a month and a year on the Reverie attempt. This massive investment in time will only produce about 2-3 points of Glamour on average.
                  • Rapture does not have time frames listed, but is described as a long and laborious process. It too only nets the changeling 2-3 Glamour points on average. It also has the very real danger of causing the character to gain Banality instead of Glamour.
                  • Ravaging is fairly instantaneous and provides the same 2-3 points of Glamour on average, but it leaves the mortal burned out and unable to produce Glamour again for some time. It also runs the risk of the mortal gaining Banality or being cut off from the Dreaming entirely.
                  • Rhapsody is ambiguous on the time frame. It requires at least one point of Glamour be spent by the changeling to initiate it. It produces the same 2-3 points of Glamour on average for Seelie characters, but it produces 4-6 points on average for an unseelie. However, it leaves the mortal unable to ever produce glamour again, usually dead.
                  The only one of those methods that can hope to replenish Glamour as fast as it gets spent is Ravaging, but constant ravaging is likely to be pretty detrimental to a changeling's glamour sources.

                  There are other sources of glamour given:
                  • Dross: Destroy the Mona Lisa, an original manuscript of one of Shakespeare's plays, or the statue of David for one point of Glamour each. This isn't exactly a good long term strategy... or any kind of strategy really.
                  • Freeholds: Gain one point of Glamour for every full, restful night of sleeping in the freehold or glen. This is probably the most stable source of Glamour, but it's probably not fast enough for an active changeling who goes out and does stuff - any PC. Also, freeholds are rare and hotly contested commodities. A changeling may end up spending more glamour than they ever gain from it simply defending the freehold from rivals or threats.
                  • Dreamers Background: The core book says this is a source of Glamour and gives exactly zero rules for how the Dreamers background provides Glamour.
                  And that's it. Those are all the ways a changeling can gain Glamour. There's no way that can keep up with as fast as the game requires Glamour to be spent. In some ways, that's a good thing. One of the big tensions in Changeling: The Dreaming should be the decision to magic away a problem or figure out a more mundane solution. The huge problem is a character can easily spend all of their glamour in the first session and be completely unable to regain any for the rest of the chronicle with the rules as written.

                  I would address this in two ways. First, I'd make it so Glamour is spent less often. Off the top of my head, I'd eliminate the Wyrd/Chimerical keywords from the Arts. A changeling would only have to spend a Glamour point to cast a cantrip if they want the cantrip to pierce the Mists.

                  Second, I'd make Glamour a little easier to gain. I might decrease the time frame involved in Reverie. I might keep the time frame the same but increase the reward so the time investment feels worthwhile. I'm not sure which approach would be best. I would get rid of the Seelie restriction on Rhapsody. It should be absolutely horrible but possibly tempting regardless of court. I would come up with a way to gain incidental Glamour from crowds in highly inspiring or emotional events - something like a live concert, an author reading, a puppet slam, a haunted corn maze or haunted house, etc. It would only be worth 1-2 points of Glamour, but it would be something doable in a single game session.

                  I would give the Dreamers Background the rule that those are people your character has already connected with and can make a Reverie attempt with them at a reduced difficulty.

                  Edit: Also, I would make the reward for destroying something like the Mona Lisa a bit more comparable to the atrocity of actually destroying it.

                                          There's a difference, at least to my reading.

                                          Dross is, in practical terms, "crystallized glamour." it's spontaneous and incidental. A strangely swirled geode, a five-leafed clover, a spot of mold in the shape of Elvis on the freehold's refrigerator, a tuft of yeti fur, something like that. Using Dross simply frees the glamour from its physical form (though as per the book, it can have unusual effects on the cantrip, similar to how resonance works in Mage I guess).

                                          Treasures however, are created items that have either collected glamour or been intentionally imbued with glamour - the term covers the magical items yiou buy with backgrounds, as well as stuff like well, the Mona Lisa. Destroying a treasure does release its glamour - but the act also creates banality, since you're not just releasing glamour (like with dross) but also destroying something that can generate and accumulate glamour forever.
