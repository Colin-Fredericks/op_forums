    Originally posted by mattboggan View Post

    PookaKnight,

    The "Reign of Terror" is an expression which was first coined by very conservative historians who wanted to discredit Robespierre by saying that he had "reigned" over France, which is very, very wrong. Robespierre, as many historians have now shown convincingly, was a rather centrist-like figure who eliminated opponents from the left (Hebertists) and right (Dantonists) because he felt that they both threatened the stability of the newly-established French Republic.

    Also, Robespierre never reigned over France. He was just one of the twelve members of the Comity of Public Safety who constantly fought among themselves, and who were accountable to the National Convention and its 700+ elected members (elected with universal suffrage for the first time in history!). France was surely a dictatorship at this moment (suppression of liberties) in order to fight almost all of Europe in order to survive, but it was a legislative dictatorship, not an executive one.

    The war in the Vendée was most certainly a moment of Dark Glamour.

    Finally, the immediate outcome of the French Revolution was bleak: Napoléon Bonaparte took power with a coup on 9 November 1799 and then set up an executive dictatorship to prepare for his conquest of almost the whole of Europe. This conquest and the Napoleonic Wars were certainly also a moment of Dark Glamour, though it is very ambiguous: many peoples resisted against Napoléon in the name of the very ideals of the French Revolution that he claimed to bring to them. And for some (the Irish for instance), Napoléon was a liberator.
    I agree that the revolution itself was a immense moment of hope, quickly dashed by the successions of dictatorships that followed. For a moment people thought they were going to be free, that they could attempt to make the ideals of the Lumières a reality. The tyranny of the nobility and the Church ended, slavery was abolished, women involved themselves in the struggle just as hard and prominently as men. And then those hopes were betrayed, dissenters were brutally eliminated, women lost whatever influence they'd gained during the revolution, many prominent female revolutionaries being executed, Napoléon became emperor and reinstated slavery. The Reign of Terror, which was just a prelude to more horror, was perhaps not quite what it's commonly thought to be but it still deserved its name.


    When will you rage?

                                            Originally posted by Nitenshi View Post
                                            I hardly see how French Revolution would not bring Dark Glamour into the World. I mean, the event was caused by a massive economic crisis (due to the wars leaded by Louis XIV) and resulted in a Reign of Terror, wars, and an Empire some years later (with more wars).
                                            In reading Wikipedia, I even see something from François Furet stating that a moderate crushing would have been enough.

                                            Of course, I can see some hope in it but it looks like romanticized vision of history to me.
                                            François Furet was a liberal historian who hated the French Revolution and equated it with totalitarianism. He wanted to prove that the Revolution had not meaning in contemporary France, as he, along other conservative historians, thought that the apex of political felicity was the liberal democracy and capitalism.

                                            No, the French Revolution was not caused by an economic crisis due to the wars of Louis XIV (which were about 120 years before!).

                                            There was economic crisis in 1788, but not worse than any one before. Revolutions are not only the products of economic crisis, though economic crisis help spark them, sure.

                                            Also, the Revolution would be dark because it sparked wars. What about the hundreds of wards sparked by monarchies before? That's not dark? Because they are waged for crowned heads, they're bright? And consider that the Revolution sparked war... because all other countries (ruled by monarchs) could not stand to have a country getting rid of the absolute power of its king; it threatened their own rule. (Granted, the leading faction at the moment in April 1792, the Girondins, led by Brissot welcomed the opportunity and declared war in order to cement the French people around their revolution... because Brissot wanted to emulate the example set forth by... the American war of Independence.)

                                            As I already wrote above: sure the Revolution saw some moments of Dark Glamour, but it was also (and mainly!) a moment of hope of levels never reached before. This is why it should be labelled as only a moment of Dark Glamour.
