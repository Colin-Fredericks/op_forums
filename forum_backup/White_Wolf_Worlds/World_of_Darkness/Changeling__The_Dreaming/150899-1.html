        Now that I'm back in town and at a real computer again, it's time for more! First though, I want to thank everyone for their feedback on my alternate Banality rules, lots of good stuff there! I'll see what I can do to address or incorporate some of it.

        My next post is how I imagine Banality might have been described in an imaginary Changeling revised edition's setting chapter. It's written in first person as was often the case in the revised era. As the other revised editions did, it brings some of the metaplot front and center. It gives banality a, hopefully, clear definition, and specifically says that science and religion aren't banal in and of themselves. It also makes Banality less abstract, and gives PCs something tangible to struggle against. I like that it also really drives home that events in the Dreaming directly affect Earth and events on Earth directly affect the Dreaming. The two may be separate physically, but you still can't get away from your problems in one by fleeing to the other.

        As is the nature of the beast, I expect this one will be somewhat controversial. Without further ado:
        What is Banality? Some say Banality is the result of religion or science in our world. Those people claim that if we could just get rid of all of the churches and accountants, the gates to Arcadia would open, Earth and the Dreaming would reunite, and we would reclaim our lost glory. Those people are idiots.

        You can’t tell me that all the music, art, and poetry inspired by religion are utterly devoid of glamour. If you don’t believe me, look at the Pietà, the Blue Mosque, or anything from Tintoretto. If you need proof that science is inspirational, just try listening to the likes of Carl Sagan or Neil deGrasse Tyson without immediately wanting to explore the universe. For fuck’s sake, the Resurgence would never have happened without the moon landing!

        No, Banality is far older than science or even religion.

        The original inhabitants of the Dreaming, the Fomorians, arose in a time before humanity. In that world there was no intelligence, no love, no hate, and no wonder or passion of any kind. Existence simply was. Creatures operated off of nothing more than primordial instinct and base survival urges. They lived short, brutal lives. They were born, ate, reproduced, and became food themselves. There wasn’t anything to aspire to - nothing to gain, nothing to lose, nothing even to fear. Eternal apathetic subsistence — that is the Fomorian dream.

        That is Banality.

        Impossibly, humans and the Tuatha de Danaan emerged from the morass. Humans longed for more than mere existence. They were full of fear and wonder at the world around them. They loved and hated with equal passion. They built vast creations and waged terrible wars. The Tuatha formed us from humanity’s boundless imagination.

        Like matter and antimatter, the Tuathan and Fomorian dreams could not coexist. A conflict over the fate of humanity raged known as the Tessarakonta, or, the War of Trees. In the end, we were victorious. The Fomorians were imprisoned, safely sequestered far away from Earth and the Dreaming. Humans were free to drive us just as we inspired them to new heights. However, our victory ultimately set the stage for our downfall.

        Triumphant, the Tuatha departed for realms unknown leaving us, the Kithain, as stewards over humanity and the Dreaming. In their absence, we forgot how precarious our being once was. Humanity’s limitless creativity gave us the power of gods. We grew ever more enthralled in our own petty conflicts. We came to see humans as mere playthings, pawns to be used and discarded. Imperceptibly at first, human kind’s imagination began to wane. Is it any wonder that with their fates beyond their control they turned to apathy? It all came to a head when, during a long forgotten dispute, we created a weapon so terrible it actually fractured the Fomorians’ eternal prison.

        Banality came rushing back into the world. Humanity embraced it and used it as a weapon against us. They shattered the bonds between Earth and the Dreaming, cutting us off from our homeland. Since that time, Banality has only grown and multiplied. Today it saturates the world.

        You see it in the minimum wage worker working three jobs, living off food stamps and still not able to make ends meet. You see it in the corporate drone sitting all day in his cubicle only to come home and sit all night in front of his TV. You see it in the high flying corporate executive working for less than a year, fired for his incompetence, and awarded a golden parachute in the tens of millions. No one has time or incentive to dream anymore. The best anyone can hope for is to survive from one day to the next.

        Now we stand at the brink of the Endless Winter, a time when Banality grinds everything back down to mere existence and the Fomorian dream holds complete sway over Earth once more.

        Charlie Cantrell

                        It's something that gives Banality in the actual Changeling rules. It's also something I usually ignore, because, like you say, there are plenty of valid character types that wouldn't have a problem with chimerically killing. It might even seem Glamourous due to the nature of the story.

                        I wanted the Acts of Banality chart to hew as close to the actual rules as possible while making Banality gains feel more like a consequence and less of an arbitrary punishment from the ST. I left it in for three reasons. First, the act of chimerically killing another changeling does actually diminish Glamour in the world, even if only temporarily most of the time. Second, it's low on the Banality chart. A starting Grump would never even have to test against it, which makes sense. Grumps are the veterans of life - they've adventured, gone to war, made the difficult decisions, taken their lumps, and have the scars to show for it. They know the actual price of the glories a Wilder might be chomping at the bit to embark upon. Finally, the character is allowed a roll to resist the Banality gain, unlike the actual rules.

                        For example, a Childling would have to check for a Banality gain after chimerically killing someone. However, odds are very good they won't gain Banality from any particular act. Childlings mostly have to worry about Banality from the frequency of times they'll have to check for it. Eventually, they're going to get some bad rolls.

                        So yeah, if I were to use the Acts of Banality chart in a game, I'd probably leave that it in. Over all, I think it's more thematic than it isn't thematic. Characters that do a bit of chimerical killing are still pretty viable since they can resist the Banality gains and even become immune to them at a certain point, which isn't the case in the actual rules.

                        As an aside, Nicky and Jackie, the former Changeling developers during Changeling's Arthaus era, were asked a similar question about Banality and chimerically killing others after War in Concordia came out. Their answer has always stuck with me. To paraphrase them, if a changeling is planning on going to war or a chimerical killing spree, they better plan on having a reliable source of Glamour to offset their Banality.

                            Recent political issues have got me thinking about Changeling.

                            Classic World of Darkness has always worn its politics on its sleeve. That's not a complaint - its part of what makes the setting so vibrant and engaging. The interesting thing is, 20 years later, those issues are still affecting us. For example, things in Werewolf that were grossly over exaggerated satire became, sadly, almost prophetic. Now, between the great recession and recent SCOTUS rulings, the problems of Changeling are more immediate than ever before.

                            Earlier in this thread, I posted a write up of Banalty. The point was to show how Banality is relevant and immediate, more than just simply nostalgia for childhood. I tied it to the Fomorians and made it an active force in the world. To do so, I used the issues we're all facing from the great recession.
                            • No one can dare to dream when they're working multiple jobs and still struggling to put food on the table.
                            • The only solace when you can't follow your life long passion because no one is hiring is mindless escape so you can't think about it.
                            • Corporate executives have no incentive to dream big or find creative solutions when they get rewarded in the tens of millions for running a company into the ground instead. It also directly leads to the other two points.
                            • Taken together, they all result in a world of passionless subsistence and dull mediocrity.
                            If I were to do it over again, I'd include the recent SCOTUS rulings. Citizens United is a clear victory for Banality to get it's status quo entrenched. The Hobby Lobby case institutionalizes corporate religion trumping an individual's beliefs, forcing them to conform.

                            I dearly hope there is a 20th Anniversary Edition. Now more than ever before, Changeling: The Dreaming has much to say.

                                With all due respect — which is considerable — I disagree. Banality shouldn’t be primarily about class warfare, with the tendency not to follow your dreams being attributable to too much or too little money. Closer to the heart of Banality, IMHO, is red tape, or excessive bureaucracy (both corporate and government) that insulates decision-makers from the people their decisions affect (making it easier to be heartless) and shackles everyone involved with regulations that are often well-intentioned but ultimately strangle the human spirit. The root cause isn’t greed; it’s a tendency to meddle in other peoples’ lives. The soul-crushing effects of poverty can be in there, too; but not as the primary source of Banality.

                                I’d also like to see less in the way of politics in cWoD games. As a conservative, I’ve found that there’s a lot that I like in said games; but when they get preachy and start identifying conservative causes with the source of a given game’s primary antagonists (e.g., the Weaver and/or the Wyrm in Werewolf, or Banality in Changeling), it hinders my ability to enjoy the game by shattering my willing suspension of disbelief: such portrayals of conservatives clash severely with my personal experience. And I get enough self-righteous indignation from liberals in real life; I don’t need it in my hobby as well. To the extent that politics do show up, I prefer them to remain squarely in the realm of IC opinions rather than being presented as OOC statements of “fact”.

                                Of course, I also tend to view the current political climate as needlessly antagonistic, with a tendency to think that if group A disgrees with group B’s methods, they must also disagree with their goals as well, leading to group B demonizing group A.

                                        I hate to double post, but I want to get this one out here before I head out of town again for a little while - a modified version of the Nature realm. This is one I've been thinking about for years. I tried to make it scale appropriately while still being useful at only one dot. I also tried to keep it as close to the original as I could while filling in the types of natural occurrences that went unaddressed. Here it is:

                                        Nature
                                        This realm encompasses the whole of the natural world, including the elements, flora and fauna.
                                        Level Title Description
                                        1 Base Element Any nonliving, naturally occurring material consisting of the elements of the periodic table or the classical elements of earth, air, fire, and water. If the target has been worked in any way, it falls under Prop.
                                        2 Verdant Forest Any living vegetation.
                                        3 Feral Animal Any living, non-sapient creature.
                                        4 Natural Phenomena Any natural occurrence such as weather, volcanic activity, earthquakes, etc.
                                        5 Laws of the Firmament The fundamental essences of nature such as gravity, light, quantum mechanics, etc.
