        “A parley was a pretty tough sell no matter who you talked to. I don’t think it helped that the Vietnam talks were all over the news continuing to get nowhere. Always one step forward then two steps back with no end in sight. Meanwhile people just kept dying. Only the real hot heads wanted war with the Sidhe, but nobody seemed to have much faith in diplomacy either. Even when people would listen you had to write down all their demands and compare it to the conflicting list of demands you already had. Where to talk, who was talking, what would be talked about. The first word was looking more and more like it would be the last one as well.”

        -Zelda Skeller, Sluagh


        “First of all, the Dega Street hostages had to be released. No question, no quarter. There was no way we’d play nice and talk it out while they rotted in a dungeon. How could we even be expected to negotiate when the Sidhe held knives to our friends’ throats? They had no authority to do what they were doing. No matter how much ‘nobility’ they thought they had, and no matter what the Escheat said, that did not give them arrest powers. This was kidnapping plain and simple. Even if I wanted to compromise the rest of the RCW would riot, probably literally. The release of all illegal prisoners had to be the foremost condition of our participation.”

        -Elise Kennedy, Clurichaun


        “Under no circumstances. Never. The Dega Street criminals could not be allowed to return to the field, which they surely would if allowed to escape. They had already burned a Freehold, assaulted officers of the court, and incited violence that led to the death of a Kithain. How much more did they have to destroy before people realized they were dangerous? Maybe once the rest of the Common Front’s allies had laid down arms and stopped their reckless attitude we could return them safely. Maybe. Until then I refused to even consider it.”

        -Gabriel Garnier ap Gwydion, Sidhe


        “They were hard to find, but there were peaceniks in the Sidhe ranks too. I didn’t like the way they stared at my limp and my leg brace, but if it got them to hear me out I wasn’t going to complain. What we had to get past were people yelling ‘appeasement!’ Mortal memories are bitter, and they exerted a powerful pull even on our fae miens. We needed to find a way to make this seem like a win for everybody, to put a bug in the ear of powerful people and let them think it was their idea. What I wasn’t expecting was how helpful Lady Aeris turned out to be. She knew how to talk to the aristos, the play the conversation games that put her in rooms with the right people. Maybe it was always doomed, but I’m still convinced the parley could never have happened without her.”

        -Nathan Brown ap Dougal, Sidhe


        “People give me too much credit. I just convinced some of our more conservative cohort that this might be the best way to get everyone in a room together. All of our leaders, and all of theirs. Just put the whole thing in the ground once and for all. I had to do my part for peace after all. I owed Alexander that much. Everything that I did, I did for him.”

        -Aeris Morgan ap Fiona, Sidhe

            “I can’t remember who first put out the idea for Beltane, but it seemed like the perfect choice. It had a traditional aspect, an ancient Kithain festival and the changing of the seasonal courts, as well as a forward aspect, the coming of spring time and change. Something to bring both sides together. Once we had our first commitment others started to follow, noble and commoner, like rocks rolling downhill. It seemed for a minute like sanity had finally won. We could work this out. All we had to do was hash the details.”

            -Hobart “Hobnails” Zachary, Boggan


            “When it became clear that the Beltane talks were going to happen one way or the other some people got more receptive. No matter how you felt about them the one thing you didn’t want to feel was left out. Now the problem was who to send. After a lot more yelling we came back to a familiar solution. Those members of the RCW that wanted to go would go. Those that wanted no part in it, stayed. All except for me, once again. It wasn’t that I wanted war, but there was still no way I was going to look at Aeris Morgan across a table and smile. Besides, Johnny was already going as our chairman, and being vice chairwoman I needed to keep the home fires burning. As it turns out the trouble was keeping them from burning out of control.”

            -Elise Kennedy, Clurichaun


            “There was still the problem of the guys from Dega Street. The saber rattlers wouldn’t stop wouldn’t stop until they walked away scot free, but Gabe had them locked down and wasn’t giving up the key. Not that I blamed him, but big people were starting to want this Beltane thing and somebody had to give. So I brought it to one of the nobles pushing for the meet up and we worked out a plan. This Nate guy would take the place of one of the prisoners, swearing oaths that he wouldn’t walk free until they did. Then I would take custody of the guy he replaced and together we’d go to the truce talks as a sign of good faith. It wasn’t a perfect setup, but it was the best anybody was going to get. It would have to be enough.”

            Vick Jensen, Troll


            “It wasn’t a tough decision. Ever since I got back to the States, even getting caught up in the changeling world, I couldn’t stop thinking about my buddies back in ‘Nam. Especially the ones that were MIA or POWs, the ones that were neither dead nor alive, just holes in the world they left behind. I couldn’t help them, but I could help some people like them. The image of a Sidhe in chains might just shock some people into seeing sense. Even still, seeing the Blaec Dun is enough to make anybody question their conviction. No matter where you stand its tower blocks out the sun, you can’t stand near the moat without feeling like it’s going to swallow you, and that’s just the outside. I took the shackles off the man they released and put them on myself. I took a deep breath and swore the oaths, hoping that my voice didn’t crack. Then I took one last look at the light and walked inside.”

            -Nathan Brown ap Dougal, Sidhe

                        05-10-2016, 09:48 PM
                        “They decided to have the Beltane meet at an abandoned summer camp turned failed hippy commune. Somehow I got the job of running security for all the Common Front delegations, all of them. Most of them didn’t know who I was or even know they were supposed to have security. All of them had a problem with authority and didn’t like taking orders from anybody. Some of them especially didn’t like taking orders from a dark skinned brother. They believed in equality with the Sidhe but not equality with me. Revolution makes odd friends though so I had to make it work, whether that meant knocking them down one or gritting my teeth and putting on a show of deference. Eventually enough of them came around that we went from being a mob to just being a giant mess.”

                        -Ibrahim Jones, Eshu


                        “I was a squire to Lord Trevathyn at the time. This was to be my final test before joining his House as a full knight. Needless to say I was in way over my head. Half the nobles didn’t even want to be there and all of them were holding grudges against the rest for Arcadian reasons I couldn’t begin to fathom. The further spread out they were the bigger the perimeter had to be, and the bigger the perimeter the more people I needed, people I didn’t have. It wasn’t as if the counts and barons would trust the commoners to guard them, and their own knights weren’t going to take orders from a squire. So everyone got corralled in an old cow pasture and immediately started pushing against the seams. Hangers on were demanding their own tents, entourages started biting thumbs at each other, and mortal enemies always seemed to end up camped right next to each other.”

                        -Kyle Berkowitz ap Fiona, Sidhe


                        “I’m amazed we even made it to Beltane in one piece. The road trip from the Blaec Dun to the camp wasn’t exactly short and my passenger wasn’t making things any easier. Wouldn’t even give me his name, just kept saying ‘42601’ like he thought I didn’t know what he was talking about. Getting him there was the pin keeping this whole damn thing together, but after three times running him down like a linebacker even I was ready to cut him loose. Chimerical leg cuffs put a stop to that, but then the little bastard nabbed the key from me and you know what he did? Did he let himself out and make another break for it? No, he swallowed it. That was the whole point of the escape attempts, to get me to lock him up again and then make it so I couldn’t take them off. The little bastard knew that showing up with him in chains would light the place on fire, and I’d helped him do it. When we finally got there Berko and Abe chewed me two new ones between them but they promised they’d deal with it. Each of us swore oaths with them to do no harm to anyone present and that was… well that was the last time I ever saw 42601.”

                        -Vick Jensen, Troll

                            05-11-2016, 09:55 PM
                            “Despite their name, the Common Fronts really didn’t have a whole lot in common. Yeah they all wanted to flip the bird at the nobility but that’s about where the commonalities ended. There were Aquarians who talked about living openly with humanity and partied like they were still in Haight-Ashbury. Then there were Johnny Rebs who talked about states’ rights and nullification while wishing the last ten years had never happened. They and all the shades between then got along as well as you’d expect. Communists, anarchists, people dressed like Indians, guys wearing tri-corner hats, teetotalers and hash-heads. The oaths kept them from laying each other out but it didn’t stop them from trying to out-shout each other. We hadn’t even met with the Sidhe yet and I wasn’t sure any of us would be left by morning.”

                            -Ibrahim Jones, Eshu


                            “It was impossible to keep the assembled nobles civil with each other. Even with an army of would-be insurgents parked less than a mile away they were more focused on finding loopholes in the oaths that kept them from dueling one another. I just couldn’t understand it. Not the rivalries necessarily, but the chaos of it all. I’d seen notables law down a Dictum for far less egregious disorder than what was happening now. It only occurred to me later that no one would invoke their Sovereign because no one wanted to be seen taking responsibility. It was around the time Lord and Lady Xaviar challenged the Landrake brothers to their fifth duel in two days that I reached my wits end. I can remember those four because they caused me so many headaches and because none of them were left by the end of the war.”

                            -Kyle Berkowitz ap Fiona, Sidhe


                            "The sun came up, and I had no idea what to expect. People started walking toward the main hall from both sides. It wasn't supposed to be time yet but I guess people were done waiting. The silence was more awkward than tense. Now that they were there nobody seemed to know what to do. As many names as they'd called each other no one could think of anything to say. The renegades suddenly got polite, the nobles suddenly got deferential, it was the damnedest thing. People bummed cigarettes and made small talk. There were musical instruments on both sides and pretty soon they found a song everyone knew. By the time politics came up nobody seemed to want to pick a fight. There were apologies, questions, agreements, discussion of common ground. Some wine bottles appeared, joints got passed back and forth, it was totally unanticipated. People started peeling off in mixed groups as the day went on, it was like the Summer of Love never ended. The sun set though, and darkness came down to snuff it all out."

                            -Ibrahim Jones, Eshu



                            "About the oaths. I knew that was going to come up. Despite having been placed in charge of security and acting under the auspice and authority of Lord Trevathyn I was still just a squire at that time. As a squire I was told that a number of the knights and barons could not submit to an oath to me personally. There were other nobles who, having sworn the oath of nonviolence to me, were then charged with the task of having the rest swear it. Lady Aeris in particular, having brought a number of the notables under her own invitation, was instrumental in seeing this done. I... don't know whether to tell you that it was an accident or a conspiracy, because I don't know either. It was a failure, despite the honors I got later. In my own mind I failed, and those deaths are on my head."

                            -Kyle Berkowitz ap Fiona, Sidhe

                                    05-12-2016, 10:00 PM
                                    “The lights went out everywhere. Someone cut the power and a wind blew that put out all the fires. Then the screams started. It was complete chaos. I flew to the nearest ones I heard and saw a group of knights moving through the dark. They had knives in their hands and bodies at their feet. I felt the Banality crawl through my veins as I reached for my sword, breaking my own oath, but I didn't care. The rest of the night stank of blood. Real blood. This wasn't a game anymore. I wanted to cut down every treacherous murdering motherfucker there but I needed to get people out of there. Some I did. Some I didn't.”

                                    -Ibrahim Jones, Eshu


                                    “I was trying to get over to where the screams were coming from when the first guy came at me. I could duck him and block him but any time I raised my fists my heart got tight in my chest. Trolls don't break oaths easy. Can't. Then the next guy showed up, and the next. It was anarchy. Nobody knew what was going on. I had to stop, drop, and roll to throw them all off of me without hurting them. Not that I didn't want to. Long legs are probably what saved me, hurdling over everything in my way trying to put as much distance between me and them as possible. The way a lot of people tell it we were the ones who hit first. I don't really know what to say to that. I've never heard any names. All I know is that they wasted no time coming after me. Didn't matter that I was a commoner. The battle lines were clear now, drawn in sand and blood.”

                                    -Vick Jensen, Troll


                                    “It happened in slow motion and yet by the time I got down there it was all over. I just stood there for a long while, taking it all in. It was a crime scene, and I was an accessory. It was too much to process. Nothing had prepared me for it all to get so... so real. It wasn't supposed to be like this. Part of me is still there, taking it all in. I don't think it'll ever leave. Not until I finally understand it, until I can make sense of it. Everyone who left that place came away a little colder. Innocence was dead, and we killed it. We killed them. Even if you didn't hold a knife you knew that someone else there did. That guilt, that paranoia, it changed everything it touched. It made the way things are now.”

                                    -Kyle Berkowitz ap Fiona, Sidhe


                                    “Words... don't describe the feeling when we heard. I can only say it was like... a weight settling into your gut. So angry you can't even scream, so sad you can't even cry. None of that changed the fact that I had to lead these people. I was suddenly chairwoman in chief of the RCW, and there wasn't anyone who didn't want revenge. No one left anyway. God I wanted it too, in the worst way. Revenge on who though? We had no idea what Sidhe had known about the massacre, or even who had committed it. I couldn't escape the thought that... somehow I had caused this. That when I went and met out mob justice that I had set in motion events that ended in this massacre. I couldn't do that again, not if I wanted to look myself in the mirror. I could barely do that as it was.”

                                    -Elise Kennedy, Clurichaun


                                    “I was in the freehold when the rumor of what had happened started going around. We didn't want to believe it. Noble or commoner the people who were there that night were there because they wanted peace, because they had believed the best in one another. This... this was too much though. The severity of it, the heinousness of the betrayal, it destroyed friendships even among people who weren't party to it. Arguments broke out. Accusations of murder and lying back and forth. The Baron tried to lay down a Dictum to restore order, and that's when all hell broke loose. Escheat be damned, there was a riot in the heart of the freehold. Even I just... I just lost it. I reached into my pockets and came back with nails clenched in my work gloves. Iron nails. I started laying into anybody who got in front of me with... anybody with point ears. I couldn't stop. I didn't want to stop. I punched and raked and pounded with my fists until the nails were bloody. I destroyed seemings, I disfigured people, I crippled them. I've never done something so awful as what I did that night. That's when people started calling me 'Hobnails' again.”

                                    -Hobart “Hobnails” Zachary, Boggan


                                    “Break your windows, break your vows, pound your swords out of your plows. Honor won but injustice lived, vengeance the gift that begs to be gived. Heads I'll count up all our sins, tails you lose and no one wins.”

                                    -Sanyaasi, Pooka
