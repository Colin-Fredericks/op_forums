            "Injustice" it's not a tool, injustice it's a moral statement, perhaps a moral sentiment.

                There's another political issue that both commoners and nobles are guilty of, and that's one of colonialism. They went over to the Americas and took the land from the nunnehi in order to set up their own political system (and take all the freeholds for themselves). So, really, the sidhe returning to rule the commoners is no worse than what all of them did to the native fae. I think that potentially counts as at least an equal horror in CTD, but not one most PCs have to deal with, because the perspective is that of the conquerors'. Again: morality, subjective, blah blah blah.

                The problem, I think, isn't really morality but nuance. The sidhe are often stereotypes. The problem with stereotypes isn't that they're untrue, but that they tell only one truth. Hence, they're partial and flawed. Sidhe being heroes or being villains isn't the problem--it's being nothing but heroes or villains, and not occupying any spaces in between. This means one-dimension characterisation and fewer options for players. Well written splats suggest seeds for more rather than fewer characters.

                This is not just a problem with the sidhe, however. To me, the kiths as written appear more restrictive than the splats in other gamelines, but that's just because CTD never went through the Revised-era process of filling in the gaps and presenting a wider range of possibilities for each. This is something I'm sure C20 will rectify.

                    Originally posted by Sapphite View Post

                    Monarchies are not inherently unjust. Nor are democracies inherently just.
                    True as far as you go. But monarchies tend toward injustice because they favor hierarchy at the expense of other possibilities. Democracies aren't automatically good, but they offer more possibilities for good outcomes and more opportunities to challenge entrenched evils. Swimming in deep and turbulent waters is easier in a life vest than with a millstone around your neck. Monarchies are normally millstones, not always of course, but they tend towards Saudi Arabia more than Denmark. Democracies can fail, most do fail, but a hope of justice is far better than a certainty of chains.
