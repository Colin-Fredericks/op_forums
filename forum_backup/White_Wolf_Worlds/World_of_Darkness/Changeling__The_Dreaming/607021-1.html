    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    Rules should open avenues for distinct stories, not close them. So maybe supernatural critters could be immune or, at least, resistant to the Mists. It is very, very hard to work Changelings in a mixed chronicle because of the Mists. And to reduce their Banality in general would also be good for such (even if Technocrats still have high Banality, with I am one of those people who argue that they aren't).
    Mages range from 2-8, with Marauders at 0-2 and Technocrats at 8-10. I do think those ranges work, not least because people with low Banality ratings are pretty much insane and very easy for changelings to affect with magic. It's the Mists chart that's more of a problem.

    I've got round this by allowing mages to resist the effects of the Mists with Willpower, so that they can effectively skip a month-long coma and instead just 'sleep it off' when they go to bed for the evening.

    One thing I did leave off the list above is Legacies. I think the Legacy system is pretty one-dimensional and cartoonish - at least, for the Unseelie Legacies. It's hard to create a nuanced Unseelie character who isn't just 'eeeeeeeviiiiiiiiiil' using the Legacies from the RAW. The Nature and Demeanor system actually works better at the moment.

                        Originally posted by adambeyoncelowe View Post

                        I don't think it's the number of kiths that's been a problem for me. More how the basic premise of a splat (as a type of character class) is limiting for beings of the dreaming. Kiths with greater customisation as an optional rule would be perfect for me. Just something as simple as using Freebies to round out the fae mien and to buy Redes to create a more unique changeling. Merits and Flaws don't quite allow for the same flexibility. It would also shift the balance from cantrips to more individual talents during gameplay (which is good, because the cantrips are sometimes clunky).
                        Well, there's actually a reason for this, mentioned time and time again in the changeling books. it usedto be that there were no "kiths," just an infinite array of fae, godlings, Good Folk, and the like. However, the Shattering changed that. Arcadia closed up, and the flood of glamour it provided the Autumn World slowed to a trickle, while banality surged in the Autumn World. The fae who remained took on the Changeling Way - basically embracing Banality to some degree. This had the effect of "regulating" their Kiths. Similar sorts of fae basically grew together, as legends consolidated; the house-elf of a manor became the same as a field-harvesting spirit on the peasant's land, and they both became "boggans." It wasn't a fast change; Kithbook: Sluagh mentioned the "buttery Spirits" who were an independent kith, but who apparently merged with the Sluagh as their myths became merged by mortals. Kithbooks Redcap, Satyr, and Troll all also mention kiths that became extinct in this time, but who they identify as being "related." When the Sidhe came back to earth, it caused a new wellspring of glamour... but not so much that it actually forced banality back. To survive, they had to adopt mortal flesh as well, restraining themselves with banality just as the commoners did.

                        Banality is the reason that the Kith exist as defined "species" of fae, rather than every changeling being a unique, wild assortment of potential like we see in Dark Ages; Fae.

                        The system you're looking at is, in fact, easily replicated in merits and flaws. MET Changeling actually lets you take other Kith's Birthrights and frailties with a merit or flaw. And creating a merit that gives your character a Rede (at variable cost) seems easy enough to me.

                                                Originally posted by herkles View Post
                                                1.) GETTING RID OF SCIENCE = EVIL AND BANAL!!!!!
                                                It never was. Never. There's an entire Kith focused on science - hell, there's even a Nunnehi Family that embraces technological advancement, in their way. What's banal is how these things are used. Television is a good example. Television is generally banal not because of some inhernet evil of cathode ray tubes (or plasma screens, I guess) but because it lends to people sitting on their butts staring at it all day, letting it think for them, pioping someone else's thoughts into their heads for an average of seven hours a day. But television is also the cause of the Resurgence, the biggest explosion of glamour since the dark ages, due to people captivated by the Moon Landing - itself a product of hard science.

                                                But at the same time, subsequent Apollo missions became "boring" - we knew we could do it, it had been done, and became banal. The moon went from a massive achievement, a great leap outward for mankind, and became a lifeless ball of dust where some nerds in doofy diaper-suits played golf.

                                                The use of science to "reduce" things is banal, but the use of science to discover things is not. Over-reliance on and total absorption in technoilogy is banal, but technologfy itself is not - technology is a method of solving problems and improving lives, and clearly needs inspiration to get going.

                                                It's a balancing act. A changeling who owns a smartphone is in no danger from banality. A changeling who spends all day playing candy crush on the thing and only communicating in grunts when spoken to, however, is in danger from banality.

                                                It's sort of like how visiting Disneyland is a fun vacation, but working at Disneyland is a soul-crushing nightmare.Disneyland is not inherently one or the other, it's the human experience and interaction with Disneyland that makes the difference.

                                                2.) Chemerical reality being less stupid
                                                Chimerical reality is literally what you make of it. If it's "stupid" at your table, maybe talk to your ST about it?

                                                3.) changeling20 having a clear goal of what it wants to be.
                                                THIS, I totally agree with. The latter portion of Changeling's print life ended up looking a lot like "werewolf with pointy ears." Massive war!
