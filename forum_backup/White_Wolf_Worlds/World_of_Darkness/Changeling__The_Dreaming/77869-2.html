    Changeling: The Dreaming, 2nd edition pages 208-209.

    Bedlam
    Originally posted by First Threshold
    • Color Change: Everything changes color, either randomly or in patterns
    • Whispers: The changeling "hears" telepathic or audible whispers that impart secrets or prophecies, or that spew unintelligible gibberish
    • Dread: A feeling of complete and utter dread engulfs the changeling; shadows distort into monstrous shapes
    • Lights: Bright lights flash in and out of existance, surrounding people with nimbi or other illuminations
    Originally posted by Second Threshold
    • Don Quixote Syndrome: The character believes everything to be from an ancient time or fantasy realm
    • Delusions of Grandeur: The character sees everyone as an underling, fit only to serve him. No matter what utter nonsense he spouts, he expects others to laud his ideas and cater to his insane visions.
    • Social Darwninism: The character sees everyone as either predator or prey - and herself as a predator of great strength and skill. This is an insidious form of madness, as it can remain undetected for some time. Soon, however, the character will strike - and death will follow in her wake.
    • The Walls Have Ears: The character believes that everything has a personality and is alive. Manifestations of this madness range from a changeling holding quite conversations with fence posts to uttering apologies every time he takes a step on the street.
    Originally posted by Third Threshold
    • Berserker: The character attacks all around her with whatever weapons are nearby.
    • Autism: The character withdraws into himself, not recognizing the outside world at all.
    • Feral Cunning: The character reverts to an animalistic state; not a frenzied attacker like Berserker, but a cunning, predatory animal that doesn't communicate and seeks only to escape or kill.
    • Perversity: The character descends into the depths of her psyche and performs inhuman acts barely conceivable by even the most depraved soul.
    Originally posted by Note
    If left untreated, Bedlam completely overtakes a character. She loses all free will and passes into the world of dreams. One night, while the changeling dreams, she simply ceases to be, disappearing entirely from the face of the Earth. At this point (or perhaps sooner), the player may no longer run the character and must create a new one if she wishes to continue play in the chronicle.
    I see lots of warning signs, drawbacks, and debilitation here.

    Edit: On second read through of your post, I see what you're getting at. I'd be concerned that would take away from Changeling focus on Earth. Right now, the Dreaming is *hard* to get to. Trods are rare and to magic yourself into the Dreaming requires a level 3 Art and a level 5 Realm at minimum. Bedlam isn't as immediate of a threat, but it is there waiting if a changeling purposefully goes too far that direction.

        While I agree that there’s much about Changeling that needs to be fixed, the hypothetical C20 should approach such changes within the existing structure as much as possible. Don’t go introducing whole new stats (e.g., Inspiration) or replacing the Kiths with a build-your-own-form system (as tempting as that is). As much as possible, start by identifying how many of the perceived problems are matters of misunderstandings, and address as many of those as possible through clarifications of existing rules. The sorts of changes I’d favor would be things like rewriting the Seelie and Unseelie Legacies so that they feel less like Virtues and Vices and more like two competing visions of virtue: while the fluff says that the Seelie and Unseelie Courts are both potentially good guys, the Legacy write-ups make it hard to picture Unseelie protagonists. That’s the sort of thing I’d like to see: leaving as much of the overall structure intact as possible, and overhauling the details within that structure as necessary.

                            • You drink alcohol or use drugs or have sex to excess.
                            • You spend more than half of your waking time creating art of some kind.
                            • You have no mortal family.
                            • You have no mortal possessions.
                            • You are in a state of unrequited love.

                                                            I'll start by copping to the fact that I am NOT a fan of Changeling: the Dreaming, but in light of that, I'd support the heck out of a C20 offering. Here's why (kinda for both):

                                                            Changeling: the Dreaming does have darkness in it, but the CORE rules don't make it very obvious. There are scads of supplemental material that brings up a much wider level of possible diversity among these creatures, their domains, their histories. If Changeling: the Dreaming had received a Revised Edition, I would'a expected more of that to have shown up in such a book. A 500+ page C20 could encompass a lot of that, offering looks at the Unseelie, the Shadow Court, the Thallain, and more. After seeing how much more of a nuanced view we got from the 20th anniversary edition of Werewolf: the Apocalypse versus what we got from Vampire: the Masquerade's 20th, I'd hope for a full view of this game.

                                                            ...However, I think that a LOT of how a C20 will look shall depend on how the 20th Anniversary of Wraith: the Oblivion will look. Last I heard, the decision whether the game would get something closer to a 'Revised-Plus' treatment around 200 pages or a full, 500+ page version was still a point of conversation at Onyx Path. My information may be off on this, so don't send for a solicitor if I'm wrong, but there's still a bit of time until it's going to be an issue. Regardless, I'd look forward to seeing such a thing.
