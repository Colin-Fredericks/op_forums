                    Originally posted by mattboggan View Post
                    I perused this thread and often see people complaining about how childish Changeling supposedly is.

                    One of the biggest misconceptions about the games in the cWoD lies in this assumption. It comes from the confusion between tone/ aesthetics and maturity. Yes, Vampire is "dark," aesthetically speaking. But in now way can you relate to a vampire from V:tM. These are alien, blood-drinking creatures driven by their Beast. And this is why I would argue that V:tM is the most fun because the less mature of the games. You play one of these inhuman creatures, for Dana's sake! In Changeling, the issues and themes are deeply relevant to our everyday life. It's about what it means to grow old, to face everyday dreariness and to try to keep your sense of wonder from falling under cynicism and despair. In the 1st edition book, the prologue was called "Never, never grow up" and the author wrote about how changelings were embodiments of people who played RPGs. Gamers try to find fun and recreation in games of imagination; changelings must do it, because this is what they are. Now, you have to try to play one of these guys.

                    Actually, if you think really about this, you may find out that Changeling is the most mature game of all.

                    So it doesn't need to be "dark" aesthetically. It is dark enough. And since it's set in the gothic-punk environment of the WoD it shares the dark aesthetics of the other games. Just describe a mental hospital to Changeling characters with that (and Terry Gilliam's films like Brazil, Fisher King and Twelve Monkeys) in mind... To the contrary, the focus is -- rightly so -- on the fantasy elements of the game. However, what I find difficult to do, as a storyteller, and I think what most people find difficult to do is how to set these fantasy elements in an "urban" setting in order to produce urban fantasy. This is what the storytelling section of the book should focus on.
                    I totally agree with all of this and more. I often wonder just what is is about Changeling that gives people the impression that Changeling is all silly rainbows when actually very little in the books is rose-colored glasses. I think maybe it's an unconscious association given that the system previous to this was Wraith: the Oblivion core book AKA "The Big Gray Book of Depression", and along comes Changeling in full bright (sometimes eye stabby) color and apparently it's about Faeries, so without even reading it, people get the contrast impression that it must be the opposite extreme of Wraith and therefore happy shiny even though it's still set in the World of Darkness.

                    *Shrugs* Just my ponderings.

                                        Originally posted by Darling_Rose View Post
                                        Just how many people were writing for Changeling on projects that never got finished?
                                        That depends. There were two unreleased books officially on the release schedule at the time Changeling got put on hiatus - Keys to the Kingdom and Book of Glamour.

                                        KB: Boggans was an unannounced book that everyone was certain was being planned. Given how far in advance books had to be started in order to meet their release date on the old supplement treadmill, there were probably a few other books in the early stages that were never announced publicly. Mattboggan's World of Dreams would fall under that category. The only people who would know the complete list of those would be Nicky Rae, Jackie Cassada, and who ever was over Arthaus at the time.

                                        There are a whole lot of fan books that got started shortly after the game got put on hiatus. Most of those never went anywhere. A few got significant progress, but were never completed like Alqamar's Formorian book. The only one I know of that got finished was KB: Boggans.

                                        Your friend's Seattle book is almost certainly a fan book. Location source books were (and still are) terrible sellers. Changeling did marginal sales back in the day, which is why it got moved to Arthaus. As I understood it under Arthaus, books didn't need to sell as many copies to be a success, but if a book sold particularly poorly, it was pretty much the death of the line. I have no insider information, so I don't know if that's what happened to Changeling, Wild West, Sorcerer's Crusade or not. I would be willing to bet, though, that they never would have wasted a book slot on something that was guaranteed to sell dismally.
