    Improved Cantrips: Expanding the Possibilities of the cantrips' realm-based

    02-20-2024, 01:58 PM
    So hi, good morning from Brazil!

    I made some different things for changeling, i storytelling some years about the plot and I'm here to share some thoughts on the arts and realms in our role-playing game, and to propose a new, improved cantrip mechanic. I would love to hear what you have to say about this.

    Throughout our campaign, I have noticed some limitations and challenges regarding the arts and realms system. While they are fundamental to the gameplay experience, I feel that some of the abilities on offer may be a bit restrictive or not very versatile for a dream-driven narrative system. Additionally, progression within these systems can seem somewhat linear and predictable.

    To address these concerns, I'm proposing the introduction of improved cantrips. These cantrips would be advanced versions of the basic arts and realms, offering characters an alternative way as a new options and significant enhancements to their abilities. They could be unlocked through training, achievements, exploration, or even specific narrative events, adding an additional layer of progression and customization to characters. Much like the hedge magic system of sorcerers, improved cantrips always use Willpower with the dice pool at difficulty 8, but the realm influences this as a modifier below this same difficulty.

    So as I finish my book, you wi'll see:

    ORIGINAL CANTRIP - it's the C20 there.
    VARIANT CANTRIP - new cantrip as an alternative path to the linear progress and
    IMPROVED CANTRIP - based on static Dark Ages Fae cantrips, but here you use willpower permanent to execute it but you can't use Bunks and other additional Realms. As a numina, it does something and that's it.

    The Improved cantrip has a:

    1. Activation Cost: The player does not spend resources to use an improved cantrip, but is limited by his permanent Willpower level. This means they can use a limited amount of enhanced cantrips based on their Willpower.

    2. Additional Effects: Improved tricks have additional effects, always being considered fateful and affecting both the fairy world and the human world. However, in areas with high banality or in the Dreaming Realm, they may be less effective.

    3. Improved Cantrip Types:
    Until now, there is 3 improved cantrip types:
    1. Those that allow Changes to include more realms and alter with additional benefits to the original cantrip.
    2. Those changes to the faemien along with the desired art, with specific mechanics that can be acquired as "Rights Owned".
    3. Enhancement of existing mundane skills, with no activation cost, but requiring XP for development.
    4. Tricks that function like spells, using the Changeling's Permanent Willpower to manifest static effects.
    4. Limitations:
    • It is not possible to add more Realms than those indicated in the cantrip description.
    • The player can cast these cantrips as many times as they want, but they run the risk of becoming Undone if they use too many tricks.
    • Being Undone means forgetting the true fey essence, and may be confused with other races and supernatural classes. The Mists alter the true essence of fairies if they are migrating out of the Dreaming, not only in body, but also in soul.
    for a clearer example see how it looked in the Spring art:

    Spring — The Earth, grow, heal, bloom.
    ORIGINAL EFFECT (it's as it is in the C20 book, nothing changes here)
    1. Awaken anything inert.
    2. Grow vegetation over the target and spread everywhere from it
    3. Heal one level of bashing or lethal damage each turn/success.
    4. Create a circle that offers protection and concealment whether mundane or magical.
    5. True resurrection. The target is kept alive by Success/Scene.
    VARIANT EFFECT (if you level up your Art, you are not obliged to follow the original, you can get a variant effect)
    1. LV2 - Use nature and simulate the effects of primal 2-3 for the EARTH element only. (or WOOD if the player wish, but he chooses one only)
    2. LV4 - Use nature and simulate the effects of primal 4-5 only for the EARTH element, including its variants.
    ENHANCED EFFECT (these spells only work this way, they are "ipsis litteris", that means you must to follow exactly all the rules described and their limitations but, they are more potent in this sense)
    • LV1 - Night Habits - Use the Realm of Time and reduce the time needed to gain effects of night rest by hours per point.
    • LV2 - Carnivorous - creates a hedge of flexible trunks equipped with short, sharp thorns. Anyone trying to cross finds branches very tight and intertwined. The fence has a Strength score equal to the caster's Nature Realm, and they attempt to grab their opponents. Once the victim is entangled, the vines contract, viciously piercing the victim's flesh with the thorns (inflicting Nature + 1 lethal damage). If the target takes any damage, the true horror becomes apparent: the spikes suck blood at the rate of one level of damage per turn, until the victim is dead or your player generates two successes on a Strength + Athletics roll to break free. Three logs appear per success, within the trick area ( 6 ft by 2 ft).
    • LV2 - Wooden Arsenal - The character with this cantrip is never unarmed, as long as he can touch anything made of wood. Branches of a tree turn into arrows in his hand, while a table leg turns into a club. The player rolls Prop. For the remainder of the scene, any source of wood he can touch turns into a melee weapon of his choice. Any weapon thus formed inflicts the usual damage for a weapon of this type, but all damage is considered lethal.
    • LV3 - Immortal Tears: If a target is healed, use Ator to heal yourself up to half efficiency, future attempts increase the difficulty.
    • LV4 - Red Sap: Per session, if you take damage, roll Stamina + Permanent Glamor. Every 2 successes gain 1 glamour, but never more than the total vitality lost. Even in human form the odor of his "blood" is a radar for creatures with supernatural sensitivity. By appealing to fate, your life-sustaining fluid possesses magical properties that are coveted as magical ingredients described in ancient forms of some tomes of hunters, sorcerers, and some lycanthropes, although you no longer produce blood while in fey form.
    • LV4 - Help of the Forest: This powerful trick summons trees from the earth to help. The player rolls Nature (difficulty 7). If successful, nearby trees (up to 20) lend the caster their assistance. The fée must be specific as to the request; but if it is reasonable, the trees are obliged to help. They can, for example, help the fairy travel from one branch to another with ease, escaping her enemies. If a fée hides among the branches, he gains two additional dice for any soak roll against bashing or lethal damage. Trees may be willing to attack the fairies' enemies. If this is the case, use the faith's Nature+Successes for an attack roll and determine the damage according to the size of the tree, from four to 12 dice. A large tall oak, for example, would do more damage than a young willow (although the willow can ensnare an enemy in its branches).
    Some improved cantrips can permanently alter the appearance of the fairy, the fae-mien, but functioning as a passive ability or innate right, or as I here naming an acquired right.
    However, each art now has a tree of smaller skills that have variant and improved tricks.

    So a player who has 5 in a Realm could perform an Realm-based improved cantrip at difficulty 3, but Bunks cannot be used to further reduce the difficulty, as they are less effective in the dream realm. A cantrip that was made to work this way cannot be modified, as can the equally dramatic consequences of overusing these cantrips. This not only reflects a simpler mechanic of dealing with a dice pool for these cantrips, but also a clear description of the range of a cantrip.​

    Some fellowships and fairy houses has some cantrips in that way, and there a mechanics as background Title and Remembrance to learn and use that improved cantrips. that was already made but I'm here to hear what you guys think about this idea(s).

    I'm really interested on your POV's.. Have you also felt that the arts and realms could be more dynamic and engaging? What kind of improved tricks would you like to see in our game? Your opinions and suggestions are critical to shaping the future of our campaign and ensuring everyone has a richer, more satisfying gaming experience.

    I intend to publish the final proposal, in the not so distant future, in the Vault and providing an opportunity for the community to discuss what they think about the proposal. It's valuable to have different perspectives as we continue to refine and develop our gaming systems. Between the sessions made here (going next to 20th session) the improved cantrip make his place, the characters stay more engaged with the cantrips and have more opportunities at his side, some arts who only work in a specific context or realm.. Please share your thoughts in the comments below.

    I look forward to hearing what you have to say and discussing ways to improve our skill system together.

    Thank you for your continued attention and gentle contribution.

      It's an interesting idea, and it does reflect a concern from CTD1e onwards. My main counterargument would be: why do we need variant and improved Arts if we already have Unleashing?

      In most cases, Unleashing does what this system does. It lets you bend the rules at a cost. It lets your creativity run riot. It's something with limited use; you can't spam Unleashings easily. Moreover, compared to this system, it's really quite simple.

      It may just be how they're written and laid out, but these current rules also seem overcomplicated to me. Where possible, you want a new system to use or mirror existing systems as much as possible. In this case, using the existing system for Arts is much better. You can always then add a limitation or exception if needed, but try to keep it simple (no more than one or two exceptions to the core rules).

      So, perhaps they work like normal cantrips, but you can't use Bunks with them or they always have to target the same Realm, because that's how you've learned them. That's it. They still use the same rules as other cantrips besides that.
      ​​​​
      I would suggest reading the rules for Devotions in Vampire: The Requiem 2e and using those as a model for how to present variant Arts instead.

      You don't need separate systems for both variant and improved Arts; just combine them into a single system. Improved Arts would just be variant Arts with a prerequisite of the Art they "improve".
      ​
      In Requiem, Devotions can be alternate powers for one level of a Discipline, or they can be the equivalent of combo Disciplines. Unlike in V5, however, Devotions don't replace the core Discipline powers; they're just unique abilities you develop alongside the core ability set.

      IMO, this is better than the V5 system of Disciplines, where you only get one power per dot, so have to skip some powers to take others. It's better for players, because they don't need as much system mastery, nor do they suffer from decision paralysis. They know that the core powers will cover the basics and there won't be any trap builds. Anything else they purchase is just a bonus on top of that, as it should be.

      It's also easier for the ST because you know two Gangrel with Protean 2 will have the same core abilities, but you can give them different Devotions to distinguish between them.

      ​​Keep variant Arts relatively cheap if you want people to have lots of them, thus increasing diversity between fae without having to strip them of the "core" powers in their Arts. Make them more expensive if you want them to be rarer.
      ​​​​​
      Alternatively, you can just rewrite the Arts to be more like Spheres. Maybe take a look at Ars Magica, since that informed the design of both Mage Spheres and Changeling Arts + Realms (they use a noun + verb system, so fire + create = create fire but fire + destroy = quench fire).

      Now, permanent or semi-permanent changes to the fae mien is where your proposal really shines. This is something unique and not something CTD really does yet. I would probably make these very costly compared to other variant Arts and tie them to a minimum Glamour rating. If your Glamour rating drops, you lose them.

      They might even be better handled as unique Merits that can only be gained through certain circumstances, with specific prerequisites, and they should probably have a long-term consequence -- e.g., you have to sacrifice a dot of Glamour permanently to manifest a very powerful enduring change, or you permanently mark a point of Willpower as imbalanced.

They're more like the relationship between the modern and Dark Ages versions of Mage - creatures that so intimately connected to the zeitgeist of their Age and so very different eras result in very different outcomes. I don't think Dark Ages Fae would work any better in modern day than Changelings would work in the Dark Ages.

                      The lore continuity, I think, is instructive. Dark Ages Changelings are human/fae hybrids. Modern Changelings are Dark Ages Firstborn who have been reborn into human bodies, as a last desperate ploy to survive the end of the Mythic Age. Where modern fae fear banality overwhelming their Fae side with their human one, the (literally) godlike Fae of the Mythic Age feared Echoes - sharp-edged beliefs.

                      All that is to say, I think it would be more interesting to walk that forward - take the Dark Ages system of mythic god-powers and think about how they would change if the Firstborn were forced to continuously reincarnate in human bodies that shield them from echoes but make them vulnerable to Banality.

                                  You're not the only one, but unfortunately I don't think it will happen. By C20 (an excellent work) it's kind of confirmed that medieval fae just followed similar rules to modern ones.

                                  There's even a class of fey that do not rely on human bodies at all, and is easy to extrapolate to the others so we have a sense of what pre-changeling Kithain would look like.

                                  Other than that, for this thread I think adambeyoncelowe said it all. Unleashing does most of the work in C20, and other than that, Requiem's Devotions do exactly the same work Elderitch Unseelie is doing, but with simpler rules.

                                  Basically, a Devotion is a separate power with relatively low cost, but with specific dots in Disciplines as prerequisites. It may be an alternative power to that level, a combination of Disciplines, or an enhancement to its prerequisites, so it literally covers the same bases as this system here.

                                  If making those extra powers more constrained is so important to the system, those "Iconic Cantrips" could be locked to the Realms used as prerequisites, but be cheaper.

                                  Let's say you want the power to give your retainers a move bonus from any distance. It could have as prerequisites Naming 2, Hopscotch 2, Actor 2. Actor doesn't count for the XP cost, so you end up with a power that costs 2 XP to learn, but it only affects Actors and can't be modified with any other Realm. It gives a bonus based on Hopscotch 2, but to movement in general, to a named target.

                                          The improved effects are specific versions of cantrips of a given Art, adjusted to perform more focused and powerful functions.                                           • Change of Original Cantrips: Allow you to include more realms or specific benefits in an existing trick.
