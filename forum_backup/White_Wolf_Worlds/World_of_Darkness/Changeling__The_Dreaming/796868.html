    Newly Returned sidhe/ fae from Arcadia -- how does this work?

    12-30-2015, 12:42 PM
    Or: Why Answering this Question Means Defining the Changeling Way...

    Hi everybody,

    I would like to discuss with you one of the very important aspects of our beloved game, but which is poorly understood and about which many confusions still remain, i. e. the Changeling Way, and especially how the sidhe’s peculiar use of it during the Resurgences (plural intended) are presented in what appears to me as contradictory ways.

    So. Bear with me: it’s going to be quite long. But I promise it might lead to interesting considerations.

    First: what do we know?
    “Changelings first discovered that they could escape Banality by assuming the guise of mortal infants and youths, taking the place of the mortal young in the physical world (replaced children were transported over to Arcadia). By entering the mortal world in this way, changelings grew up as mortals without triggering Banality. This was (and still is) the most common way that the sidhe came to Earth.
    “Commoners, on the other hand, who by and large adapted to existence in the mortal world following the Shattering, are reborn into mortal families (usually those that carry faerie blood) when their former bodies pass away. Their spirits join with the souls of unborn children and the two become fused. Common changelings therefore grow up as humans, all their faerie memories utterly forgotten.”
    -- Changeling: the Dreaming, 1st ed., pg. 57b.

    “We arrived individually, or in small groups of two or three. There was no pattern to it that I could discern.”
    -- Nobles: The Shining Host, pg. 23a (narrated by Baron Edgewick).

    “We were new to the world of man and avoided contact with modern technology at all costs. In fact the very act of using a motorcycle at that time would have involved an unacceptable charge of Banality. Our skins have toughened somewhat since that time and even now…”
    -- N:tSH, pg. 24a (narrated by Baron Edgewick).

    “During the early years of the Resurgence, sidhe were forced to “possess” adult humans in order to protect themselves. (The human is taken back to Arcadia, hence the name changeling.) Many liken the experience to that of being born. Thrust without preparation into the cold, Banality-saturated realities of the modern world is a painful and disorienting experience, one not softened by years of life as a mortal (like most commoners). The experience drives some sidhe slightly mad, though this madness is almost always temporary.
    “As the sidhe psyche emerges, it often experiences flashes from the human’s receding memory. These “psychic jolts” can be both painful and disorienting. In the end, however, the human memory becomes faint and distant. Just enough remains to allow sidhe to begin exploring her new world. Often the sidhe will meet the friends and family of the person she changes places with. This can be an excruciating experience for both parties. The loss of this second set of mortal memories just after losing their Arcadian identity is a devastating blow to the sidhe. It is a credit to them, as a people, that they are able to bear this burden.
    “[…] It is obvious, however, that the sidhe’s psyche exerts some influence over the body that it assumes. (Human imperfections often fade away as the possession takes place.) […] During the early years of the Resurgence many sidhe found wounds on their human bodies when they came through. It is believed that these injuries were caused by wounds inflicted on the sidhe as they battled their way from Arcadia.
    “Now that the sidhe are somewhat better established in the Waking Lands, they usually start their lives as children. Adult sidhe protect their newfound childlings (many of whom were probably adults in Arcadia) with a passion. As a rule, the transition is much easier for childlings, who retain most of the memories of their mortal seemings. In general, the younger a sidhe changeling is, the more she remembers of her human life. At earlier ages, the area where the Kithain persona begins and the human persona ends is nearly indistinguishable [I would say this hold true for commoner changelings as well.]. Some believe that very young sidhe are not true changelings at all, but symbiotes [again, much like commoners, except for one crucial detail]. Sidhe childlings regain their pre-Interregnum memories very slowly, often not until adulthood.”
    -- N:tSH pg. 87

    “In order to survive in a world saturated with Banality and severed from the Dreaming, the fae who were stranded in the mortal realm by the Shattering [i. e. the commoners and Scathach sidhe] devised a means of protecting their fragile spirits. This process, known as the Changeling Way, consisted of creating a shell of mortality to serve as a house for the faerie spirit. Just as humans used clothing to shield themselves from the elements, changelings clothed themselves in mortal flesh as a buffer against Banality.
    “The first generation of Earthbound fae merely disguised themselves as humans, covering their raw faerie natures with layers of Glamour tinged with minute doses of Banality. In this way, they could pass themselves off as humans – albeit eccentric examples of the species. This method worked only so long as the camouflaged faeries limited their contact with mortals, thus minimizing their exposure to human disbelief. Adapting fully to the new environment necessitated finding a more permanent and durable solution.
    “Through trial and error, the stranded faeries discovered how to successfully implant their spirits into the bodies of young children or infants, fusing themselves with their host’s mortality without displacing their souls. By beginning their lives in human flesh, changelings could avoid being destroyed by Banality. In order to accomplish this, however, the Kithain had to sacrifice their immediate knowledge of their true nature, forcing it into dormancy until it could safely emerge during the Chrysalis.”
    -- Changeling: the Dreaming, 2nd ed., pg. 57a.

    “The doors to Arcadia slammed shut once more. The sidhe had to act quickly to prevent Banality from destroying them outright. They fell back on the tried-and-true method of switching bodies with mortals, sending a host of unwary mortals back through the gateways that had briefly swung open. Since the Resurgence, most sidhe who have entered the world as outcasts and exiles still use this method of protecting themselves from Banality, seizing upon convenient mortal bodies to house their delicate spirits. Although the true fate of such disposed human souls remains unknown, most sidhe believe that these mortals enjoy an awakening in the Dreaming. In other cases, newly arrived sidhe avail themselves of the presence of very young children or unborn infants and insinuate their essence into the psyches of these impressionable beings, coexisting in symbiosis rather than taking outright possession of mortal souls.”
    -- C:tD 2nd ed., pg. 57b-58a.

    “The sidhe of both Courts formed an elite oath-bound circle of keremet, nine for each Court, known as the Soul Bearers, to ferry human souls to their destination in Arcadia when the sidhe possess their bodies in the Autumn World. No sidhe who has returned to Earth remember this.”
    -- Denizens of the Dreaming, pg. 58a.

    Now, from this information (and some others too, here and there), I deduce the following:

    1) There a three different Changeling Ways. The first one was used just before and after the Shattering. It consists in hiding from Banality (and especially from humans who carry Banality with them) by passing off as humans. It does not work very well, especially for any prolonged period of contact with humans, but it’s useful. Since Denizens of the Dreaming, we know that there was an Art, invented by the fae, the Autumn Way, to improve this method.

    The second one is the sidhe Changeling Way. It consists in displacing a human soul and taking her place. Now, there are two variations here: either the sidhe displaces the human soul and takes possession of the body, pure and simple, and, because of that, the sidhe doesn’t experience a Chrysalis but instead this kind of painful and disorienting “birth” and her fae memory replaces most of the human memory. However, enough of the latter remains in order for the fae to be able to navigate through the modern world (in other words, she retains, for instance, her host’s Drive Ability). Or the sidhe shares the body with the mortal soul. Because of that, this mortal side makes the fae spirit slumber, and she lies dormant and experiences a Chrysalis (for instance like the character of Morgan from the Immortal Eyes trilogy, her Chrysalis is narrated in the Changeling anthology). It is even implied that there is no removal of the human soul, and that the sidhe spirit coexist in "symbiosis" with the human soul. But they are not fused, unlike the "commoner" Changeling Way. This is the way that sidhe PCs underwent. Their sidhe spirits must have come during the Resurgence as the trods then closed again, but they might also have lain dormant wherever they were before choosing a mortal host and coming into this world.

    The explanation of why the sidhe came in small groups during the Resurgence is, of course, the keremet. They are only nine of them to act as Soul Bearers for each Court, and while they travel fast along the trods, they could not ferry all the souls from all the humans who had been taken as hosts by the sidhe who came back during the Resurgence.

    The third one is the commoner Changeling Way. It’s quite well described, and we understand how it works (more or less) though we do not know how it is done. The fae spirit and the mortal soul fuse, becoming for all intents and purposes, one.

    Now, my questions/ remarks:

    It appears from the books that the sidhe who came directly as adults during the Resurgence were very powerful. (For an instance of a newly returned sidhe, see the stats of Yrtalien who came directly into an adult body as it is narrated in the first novel of the Immortal Eyes trilogy and his stats are in Shadows on the Hill.) In fact, they retained some of their faerie memories (though none of Arcadia except for a few glimpses) as well as most of their faerie powers, though they were constrained by their new mortal bodies. In game terms, this would mean that they would have all their Arts and Realms, but their Glamour score would be limited to 10 (and those who had higher scores, as we know True Fae can have -- see Meilseior from the Immortal Eyes trilogy -- would lose them up to a point). Unfortunately for them, on the other hand, this would also mean that newly-come sidhe would also suffer from a greater sensibility to Banality. It is due to the fact that their fae spirit is not used to the coldness of the modern world. As such, technology is not banal per se, but it implies an understanding of the dreams of the modern world that these sidhe just lack. In game terms, these sidhe would suffer the sidhe’s Frailty, but amplified (maybe it’s three or even four Banality points for one). How would their “skin toughen” remain unknown? By what process? Over what amount of time? These questions are yet unanswered.

    To answer this, we can remark, by comparing these three different Changeling Ways, that resistance to Banality is proportionate to how soon the faerie spirit enters the mortal existence as well as to what extent the changeling is a mortal. In the case of the commoner/ Scathach way, it’s even before their birth, and part of their soul is mortal (in fact, they have a soul only so far as it comes from their human side). It would then mean that resistance to Banality comes with one’s being more familiar with the modern world of humans. Much like the Adhene see that their relationship with the Dreaming change once they acquire two permanent points of Banality, I would argue that it is the same for sidhe changelings who came as adults. Only once they have two permanent points of Banality does their Frailty cause them to “only” gain the double amount of Banality points (instead of three or four). But, how much Banality would have they as the start as they posses their adult bodies? In Denizens of the Dreaming, the Merit Human Shell (pg. 70a) implies that a human body means that a changeling has two points of Banality. So my rule would not be logical, unless you accept True Thomas’s statement that sidhe bloodhounds were roaming the streets of San Francisco just on motorcycles after the Resurgence to take possession of freeholds (The Toybox, pg. 32). But how would reconcile the two conflicting statements from True Thomas and Baron Edgewick?

    Another consequence of this distinction between adult-“born” sidhe and infant-“born” sidhe: the sidhe who came with the Second Resurgence came as adults. So they obey to the same rules (more powerful but more susceptible to Banality). So, how do you make them PCs? Because we only have rules for those sidhe who grew up as humans, and experienced Chrysalis. Or, you have to only allow characters who came during the Second Resurgence but who took the bodies of infants/ small children, making them unsuitable for play until they undergo their Chrysalis… which means years later. What do you think?

    2) We better understand what Rapture is about:

    From C:tD 1st ed.: “Changelings possess both mortal and faerie natures. By reaching into their mortal souls, they can make contact with their inner Dreaming. This is very lengthy and difficult process, requiring Zenlike balance between their mortal and faerie selves, but has potential for enormous gain.” (pg. 189a)

    From C:tD 2nd ed.: “Changeling possess both faerie and mortal natures. By allowing their mortal sides to be inspired – achieve Rapture – they can get in touch with the Dreaming as if they themselves were mortal Dreamers. This is a very lengthy and difficult process as it takes a lot of soul-searching and understanding of one's faerie and mortal natures, but it also has the potential for enormous gain.” (pg. 214a).
    Notice the difference? In the 2nd ed., the word “soul” has been dropped in favor of “sides.” I think it was meant to allow sidhe characters to be able to achieve rapture, even if it is explicitly stated that most of them displaced and still do the mortal souls from the bodies they inhabit and, as such, don't have mortal souls. Hence, I would give sidhe characters a higher difficulty to achieve Rapture (the game system of which is unclear and badly phrased in my opinion, but this is for another discussion), and would even differentiate between those who displaced their mortal bodies' souls and those who live in symbiosis with it.

    3) Now, the big secret.
    Let’s re-read one of the passages from the quotes above as it holds a clue to how the CWR is done: “In other cases, newly arrived sidhe avail themselves of the presence of very young children or unborn infants and insinuate their essence into the psyches of these impressionable beings, coexisting in symbiosis rather than taking outright possession of mortal souls."

    So, some sidhe managed to “insinuate” their fae spirit “into the psyches of these impressionable beings.” The adjective impressionable is important here. I think (but I’m not sure) that this is what prompted me to deduce that the Changeling Way ritual was an oath that the fae spirit proposed to the human soul. And this is an oath that can’t be forced. But, like all oaths, the fae can trick the mortal into taking it. Hence the importance of choosing “impressionable beings.” And who could resist the lure of promised immortality? Especially if one has just died.

    Because I think that this is the boon, if you will, that the fae promises to the human soul: “you won’t be threatened by Oblivion, you will reincarnate with me.” However, and that’s what is the most interesting, this is all but a trick, a clever lie, as the fae are prone to do. Because, as those familiar with Wraith will know, it is mortal souls who reincarnate! When they fall into Oblivion, mortal souls are thrust back on Earth, to live a new mortal life.

    This would explain why sidhe who possess their mortal bodies don’t reincarnate. Faerie immortality was tied up with the Teign and with the Bright Road. In Shadow Court, we learn that after the Shattering, the Bright Road couldn’t be walked any more and the Teign was all but forgotten and those who walked the Bright Road “never returned” (Shadow Court, pg. 19a). So, sidhe who die go on to walk the Bright Road, but for the moment, they have not returned.
    And why do you think commoner fae decided to fuse their fae spirit with human souls? It was not, at first, for the love of humanity (since their mortal side would mean that they would be more vulnerable to the Mists and more Banal) but it was because it was the only way to survive Banality and, more than that, the only way to re-incarnate!

    And maybe, just maybe, the sluagh theory of kith degeneration can make much more sense when you know that it is the human soul that allows fae to reincarnate…


    What do you think?

                      I re-post here something that I had written long ago on the SnE forum: All this discussion makes me think about how I have re-vamped completely the Ways in my Winter game. At the beginning of character creation, I ask the player what kind of faerie/ non-faerie he wants to play:

                      - Kithain
                      - Gallain (Inanimae, Nunnehi, Menehune)
                      - Adhene
                      - Thallain
                      - Kinain
                      - Human
                      - Chimera

                      Then, I ask him to think carefully how his character will survive on the Waking Lands. Of course, depending upon his earlier choice, the answer can be automatic: kinain and human are... humans. But in the case of Kithain, Adhene or even Inanimae, they have several options:

                      - The Changeling Way with two variants: symbiosis or exchange;
                      - The Secret Way (the Inanimae, i.e. crafting a Husk from an Anchor to venture forth in the mortal realm);
                      - The Autumn Way : living in dreams, possessing humans or crafting a human body out of Glamour (as most Adhene do).

                      And, in my game, no one can play a sidhe (or a commoner for that matter) directly coming from Arcadia. Why? Because when I first read the Immortal Eyes: the Toybox (both the supplement and the novel) as well as N:tSH, I realized that Arcadian sidhe who took possession of human bodies kept their abilities, Arts and Realms (think of Dafyll, Edgewick, Dray, Yrtalien and the rest). This is why they won the Accordance War: they were mighty!

                      Sidhe like Morwen, David and other belong to the 2nd generation, born in human children and having their Chrysalis later. For David, it was not very late, since we know he found Caliburn in 1974 (Jan. 1st, according to FL: TWofC pg. 37b). We know that David was born "in the early 60s" (C:tD 1st ed. pg. 54b and 2nd ed. pg. 59b). So, in 1969, during the Resurgence, his human host must have been between 5 and 9 years-old. In 1974, he must have been (depending upon the time he spent in freeholds with True Thomas) 10 to 13 years old, having recently been through his Chrysalis and Saining.

                      So it may mean that David (as well as Morwen and other sidhe) did not possess their bodies but merged with their hosts' soul and then later entered Chrysalis. It is said that David was sained. Sidhe who posses their bodies didn't need to be sained: they were under their fae mien from the moment they came to their senses from the other side of the Mists (as Edgewick recalls in N:tSH).

                      I assume that all sidhe PCs are 2nd generation sidhe, which mean that they are symbiote changelings.

                      Ergo, it means that Banality's Curse is linked with the sidhe kith itself, not with the CWR. Or it is linked with the fact that sidhe have spent 600 years secluded in Arcadia and as such are more vulnerable to Banality. But it is not due to their method of entering the world. This is confirmed by N:tSH when Edgewick says that when they just came from Arcadia, using a motorcycle would have caused too much Banality.
