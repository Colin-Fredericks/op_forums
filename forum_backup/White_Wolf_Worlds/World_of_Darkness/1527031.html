    50 Caliber Sniper Rifles, and other Mundane Alpha Strikes

    12-17-2023, 11:53 PM
    This is an idea that has probably been in my brain since I first read Beckett's Jyhad Diary. I am not going to dig up all the details right now, but one of the chapters begins with Beckett being saved from attacking Garou (if memory serves, there may have been details implying BSD, but it's been a while since I've read the chapter) by a Banu Haqim sniper hitting them from around a mile away with a .50 caliber rifle. (I think the chapter even includes mention of a club among the Assamites that specializes in sniping). I'm not super familiar with the rules from WtA, but I am aware that Garou have at least some capacity to come back from injuries that should kill them by rolling their Rage. So a part of me has always wondered how much that scene might hold up if it wasn't being written from a Vampire-centric narrative.

    A recent thread discussing crossover between Vampire and Werewolf included the usual discussion of ambush-from-the-Umbra as a method of attack, as a means of assassinating a Vampire Prince for example, and it was mentioned that a bullet Talen with the capacity to do Agg damage would probably be just as practical. And my mind went to the .50 caliber sniper rifle immediately, and the thread followed suit soon after.

    A .50 caliber rifle is not something you just find lying around. And the training required to use one effectively would probably be fairly significant if you aren't just looking to fire at something you can clearly see with the naked eye. But I get the feeling that, in many cases, training someone as a professional sniper might be the "low budget option" compared to training a member of a splat to be capable of a nasty "alpha strike" style attack through more supernatural means, especially if you want the attacker to be at minimal risk. It's not the sort of thing that's going to come up much for a player (if the Storyteller isn't a monster) because "you didn't have the right ward to keep a sniper from putting a bullet in your brain from 2 miles away, so game over" isn't a very fulfilling conclusion to a narrative arc if you didn't all but literally ask for it. But it does feel like the sort of thing where you would want to consider the implications for the wider setting.

    To what extent are various splats more or less vulnerable to something like an unexpected sniper attack? I say 'unexpected' because I realize that just about any splat can probably set things up to give themselves at least a decent chance even if you can't just avoid exposure.

    (I just realized that I've perhaps implicitly raised a somewhat separate question, whether or not any given N/PC should be expected to be routinely walking around with their defences at "maximum," or how much of a difference you should expect between someone who is expecting that kind of attack versus someone who doesn't have any reason to expect to be targeted by a professional assassin. I get that PCs are allowed a degree of paranoia, but I don't imagine that NPCs should be routinely played as expecting death around every corner without having some particular reason in their history.)

    Similarly, much like real life politicians and celebrities, N/PCs that manage to achieve higher ranks of status and such among their peers probably *should* expect this sort of thing, and you would assume that they would either rearrange their lives around that possibility to some extent or adopt some kind of countermeasures. I'm as interested in mundane solutions as I am supernatural answers. One of the joys of this forum is frequently getting to enjoy the expertise of people who have more experience with these sorts of things than I do!

    If I was going to try and sum up the various ideas put forward here, it would be this question: to what extent do .50 caliber sniper rifles (and other "mundane alpha strikes") "level the playing field" between various splats in the situations where they are in conflict with each other?

    Arguments about why one given group of supernaturals doesn't wipe another group off of the map come up somewhat regularly, and ultimately some amount of narrative discretion tends to be involved in determining the right "balance" between splats competing in a crossover setting. I'm just generally curious about the influence that relatively mundane means of eliminating a target with minimal risk to yourself would have on the wider world when conversations about supernatural methods of "alpha strike" (e.g. attacking from the Umbra, Obfuscate, Mage Rituals, etc.) tend to get more attention. To what extent do you think that the existence of things like snipers realistically serves as a check on the agendas of various supernatural groups?

      Originally posted by Kharnov View Post
      (I think the chapter even includes mention of a club among the Assamites that specializes in sniping).
      The 1000 Meter Club, which is a largely unofficial social group with in the Clan. The very important part of earning membership is getting a 1000+ m kill solo without Disciplines (some of them even Embrace mortals for pulling it off because that kind of skill is rare). One of the reasons to bring this up is it highlights a very overlooked part of long range shooting: it's a team activity. At the most basic level combat sniping (even at closer ranges than that) is done by duos, a shooter and a spotter, who have trained extensively with each other. This teamwork is considered essential for practical engagements at extreme ranges. In a military context, most sniper teams are also in regular communication with an intelligence officer of some sort to establish that they have the right target, if the rules of engagement allow for the shot to be taken, etc. And that officer is probably working with a whole team of people pouring over pictures, maps, and so on. All of that sort of effort has only lead to a few dozen 1000+ m kills (though some of those records go back a very long way, and while the .50 cal is a regular on the list, there are others like the .338 which is actually the standard for most modern combat snipers in US/NATO that are targeting humans in the open and not nearly that far away).

      There only fourteen people in history known to have killed someone at a mile or farther with a rifle (and again, they all had at least a spotter with them if not more help). This also skips ahead to one of the best defenses against this: it almost never happens. In centuries of using explosions to hurl metal at people, this is basically a fluke occurrence where dozens of factors have to line up perfectly for the shooter to even consider pulling the trigger at those sorts of ranges. Supernatural powers can also only do so much about many of the reasons why this is so rare:

      1) These guns are huge. The Accuracy International rifle used in NATO armed forces chambered for the .338 weighs 15 lbs unloaded and without optics, and is 40 inches long. The Barret .50 (the most famous .50 sniper rifle as much as one can call it a sniper rifle) is 33 lbs unloaded and no optics and 57 inches long. These are not guns you walk around with and then bring up to your shoulder and shoot when you see a target. These are not guns you tuck under a trench coat. The whole "assemble on site out of a briefcase" thing in movies doesn't work, because that can completely throw off your aim at long ranges because the barrel is aligned 1mm off from where it was before. Obviously supernaturals have powers that can help with a lot of these issues, but it's still a lot of work just to get these rifles into position.

      2) Once you're talking about extreme range shooting lots of things start coming into play. One of the reasons huge bullets are used (even the .338 is a beast), is for the inertia the bullet has, not because of the giant holes giant bullets make. Over these sorts of distance the bullet can travel through multiple microclimates. wind might be blowing 5 mph to the east for a bit, and then 10 mph to the west for another, and the bullet with sway in its path over that long of a shot. The bullet passing over different surfaces means going through air of different temperatures which causes vertical deflections. Shit like the Coriolis effect can even matter at the longer ranges of rifle shooting.

      3) Most targets are moving. If it takes you 2 minutes to get yourself in place, and 30 seconds to do all the math on your shot... you might have completely missed the chance. Even if you were setup ahead of time, doing all of the calculations (again, these people work in teams for a reason) while a target is moving is nearly impossible (and Celerity or Rage isn't going to help you there).

      I'm not super familiar with the rules from WtA, but I am aware that Garou have at least some capacity to come back from injuries that should kill them by rolling their Rage. So a part of me has always wondered how much that scene might hold up if it wasn't being written from a Vampire-centric narrative.
      It's a difficulty 8 roll, and each success only heals one level of damage. If our vampire sniper is familiar with WtA werewolves (or you're a WtA sniper), and is skilled enough to one-shot at thos ranges? You just double tap (Celerity or Rage are very useful for that part): the first shot triggers the roll to stay active, they Frenzy (so no taking cover from your second shot) but only have a few HLs back, and then you shoot a second time and they don't get to roll again that scene. The harder part is consistently doing that kind of damage against a werewolf since very few splats have ways to seriously increase the damage of firearms (and Werewolves soak Agg with their full Stamina in a combat form, so you need a lot of damage dice... or a bit fewer and silver bullets).

      Of course, for the sake of a vampire story, the Garou could have just failed the Rage roll and died. With Diff 8, it's going to happen to most werewolves eventually if they try to rely on that backup regen too much. So it's not some ludicrous WtA ignoring thing for that roll to not work.

      A .50 caliber rifle is not something you just find lying around.
      Depends where you live? They're completely legal in 46 US states, and only banned in California and Washington DC. Beyond things like background checks and so on, the big difficulty is that they're expensive. A true military grade .50 cal sniper rifle with all the fixings is easily going to cost ~$20K... and the bullets aren't cheap either. If you needed to be budget sensitive, you can get a .50 cal hunting rifle for closer to $5K (though not all of those can handle the higher end sniper .50 cal rounds). For getting one good shot off, the big thing you're lacking is high end optics (but that's one of the easiest things for supernaturals to get around), but you're going to be in trouble when people notice you and your giant gun, since you'll be very constrained in defending yourself (though most military snipers just carry an assault rifle and switch to that if it turns into that sort of situation).

      But I get the feeling that, in many cases, training someone as a professional sniper might be the "low budget option" compared to training a member of a splat to be capable of a nasty "alpha strike" style attack through more supernatural means, especially if you want the attacker to be at minimal risk.
      The big problem goes back to how much supernatural powers can make up for the difficulty of using these sorts of weapons practically. You're already looking at training two people on an incredibly expensive piece of hardware that costs a decent chunk of change for every practice shot ($3 to $5 per bullet should be expected). Training a supernatural that can mean reducing the need for a spotter is a huge saving in time, energy, and financial costs. Supernaturals are also going to be able to escape with their huge expensive rifles far more easily. Relying on mortals/minion splats is a big risk for something so intensive anyway.

      To what extent are various splats more or less vulnerable to something like an unexpected sniper attack? I say 'unexpected' because I realize that just about any splat can probably set things up to give themselves at least a decent chance even if you can't just avoid exposure.
      Vampires have the advantage of being magically less impacted by guns (though the ST might rule otherwise for these sorts of firearms), and there aren't that many ways to make bullets do agg (though with something like WtA Talens that's a bit ST dependent, but the examples in the books don't really support just a flat out upgrade like that; mages and changelings are more troublesome as snipers... but they need sniping for these things even less anyway). Vampires are also naturally night time city dwellers. SWAT snipers don't bother training in shooting beyond 300m because cities and suburbs never provide that kind of clear line of sight. Most militarized police around the world aren't allowed to shoot farther than 100m without explicit permission because populated areas have too many complications for the risk. Most powerful vampires don't have to worry about being sniped from a mile away because cities are natural cover, and the closer the sniper has to be, the more those big honking .50 cals aren't practical even with some supernatural boosts.

      Werewolves are difficult because beyond their innate powers (which do have some decent defenses against this) their bought powers are all over the place. One werewolf might have zero supernatural defenses against guns, while another might have a bunch of things to make sure they don't get shot (there's a sample Talen in one of the books that's an example of Talens made for kinfolk to be able to use without Gnosis, that reflexively acts as cover if someone tries to shoot them, so that sort of trick is certainly available to the shifters). The Garou also gather in more rural areas, where long range shooting is more viable, but the Garou have access to lots of supernatural senses that means being a mile or two away is zero guarantee one doesn't notice you. So it's a big mixed bag. IME though, because the Garou are sensitive to things like humans shooting at wolves, they tend to think about guns in their territories more than other splats. But that doesn't mean they're prepared for full splat supernatural offensive snipers (defensive ones are a different matter since Pentex as those).

      Mages come down to the normal issue of mages: if they're prepared, you might as well not bother. If you actually surprise them, they can crumple fast. Their versatility does mean it's not hard for them to have one or two spells going to defend themselves against attacks. Mages can also be very tricky because most other supernaturals aren't great at spotting true magic. A mage in a business suit might have an armor rating of 8 without it being an active effect that would ping most other splats reflexive senses, or even get picked up as specifically super-armored clothing by active senses. As well, with the Technocracy around, guns are very much on the minds of mages as a threat to be wary of. Mages make for ridiculously good snipers if they're willing to use guns, so mages that don't want to get sniped by a Technocrat (including other Technocrats worried about internal strife) are going to want something in case they're in a sniper's crosshairs.

      Changelings would be great snipers if most of them didn't suck with making magical modern weapons. They also have a lot of good ways to defend themselves against snipers... but very little reason to bother unless prompted. While they like to be around people, offering many of the advantages vampires have for it being difficult to position a high powered rifle in the first place, it's just less on their radar because it's not really a threat they regularly face. They're not built like the tougher splats, so hiring a bunch of thugs with pistols is generally more effective so you can overwhelm their defenses with numbers instead of the one shot one kill with massive damage that they might be able to deflect on the fly.

      Wraiths... not really part of the equation rally.

      The Wan Kuei are pretty much in the same position as western vampires.

      The Imbued are generally just screwed against this kind of attack. Though if one of them has Bluster it can completely stymie a sniper for long enough for the group to get out of harm's way. Though, like changelings, they're not sturdy enough to really worry about all this time and effort for a massive sniper rifle. A small gang of normal humans with smaller guns are far more dangerous (and Bluster can't stop lots of attacks at once) because you don't need to worry about all of the effort of getting an elite sniper.

      Demons are a weird case because they have insane sensory powers for supernatural shenanigans, but they are (or at least can be) tough enough to justify wanting high end firepower. They're probably the one time where a highly trained mortal assassin with big gun is actually the way to go. But like the Garou their powers are all over the place and it can be really hard speculate on because some are going to be screwed in these situations, and some can basically flip your shooter off and walk away.

      Living Orpheus agents are pretty screwed... but you just turned them into a ghost that has a strong reason to want you dead and isn't bound by the laws of the Underworld like a normal wraith. If you can't make anti-ghost bullets... this will probably backfire.

      If I was going to try and sum up the various ideas put forward here, it would be this question: to what extent do .50 caliber sniper rifles (and other "mundane alpha strikes") "level the playing field" between various splats in the situations where they are in conflict with each other?
      I actually think they make the playing field even worse. The splats that have the best sniper tools are also the splats that have the best sniper countermeasures. A well built gun-bunny Garou can wreck vampires like their klaive wielding brethren even if it might be a bit more convoluted and/or limited to get there...and for once the Umbra is a great benefit because getting into the Umbra after shooting a major enemy when you're very far away is actually practical much earlier than "hop out of the Umbra, claw claw claw, hop back into the Umbra" will be. Glass Walkers (because if you're going guns and Garou, you want a Glass Walker or a Silver Fang with Glass Walker friends but Silver Fang gun users aren't terribly popular in their Tribe despite them getting firearm boosting stuff in their Gift list) have a lot of easy access to anti-gun countermeasures as well.

      This even goes internally as well. As I noted Technocrats have great guns, great firearms training, and great access to anti-magical-bullet materials as well. I've also had to remind an Akashic player before that mastering unarmed combat in the modern world does not mean people aren't going to shoot guns... and that went unheeded to very bad results. "I know Do" doesn't make you Neo... but the Euthanatos have no problem training to be Deadshot (sure Suicide Squad sucked, but that ricochet shot at the beginning of the movie is literally a rote in the Revied Euthie book) and going gun-fu on anyone silly enough to forget what era this is.

      So the sniper-alpha strike isn't really a great check on supernaturals because it tends to just exacerbate the strengths of supernatural alpha-strikes once they get mixed together.

                          Incendiary rounds dealing agg to vampires really depends on how cinematic the ST wants to be.

                          "Realistically" the concept is silly. High power incendiary rounds wouldn't be in contact with a human-ish target for long enough to actually cause burns from the chemicals (keeping in mind that bullet wounds do release considerable heat as it is just from the physicals of metal hitting wet flesh). Incendiary rounds were invented for and are still exclusively designed for, "hard" targets such as vehicles and supply caches. Only the oldest incendiary rounds don't rely on hitting something hard enough to stop high powered rounds (the deformation on impact is what then brings the incendiary charge in contact with the compromised armor) because they were shooting at hydrogen dirigibles and... you don't need a lot of fire to cause massive damage to those.

                          Even the very Internet famous dragon's breath shotgun rounds are really not going to do much to a vampire (though Rotshreck risks should be real) because the heat isn't continuous it's a short burst of very high heat with minimal ability to penetrate the human body (esp. bony parts like the chest or skull), and no way to keep the heat on the target long enough to cause it to catch fire. to try to put it in some perspective, if you shot a person in the stomach (no pesky bones) at point blank range to maximize the damage, you're create a half-orange sized section of extremely burnt flesh, while doing basically nothing physically to the rest of the body. Now, that's certainly an extremely serious wound to a human (depending on how much fat they have in their belly, they could have some intestinal damage, as high grade burns are slow to heal and major sources of secondary infections), but you're not going to kill them on the spot, or send them up in a gout of flame. It's far less deadly than a normal 12 gauge buckshot or deer slug, as it might not even be incapacitating in the heat of a fight.

                          So realistically anything short of an actual flamethrower should really be dealing a few levels of fire damage at worst. We have things like flamethrowers precisely because small metal bits flying at high speeds are hard to jam any serious amount of incendiary material in, let alone make effective against a human-ish target.

                          If one wants to be a bit more cinematic about it, of course it's easy enough to say that fire is a bane of vampires and they're more impacted that physics says they should be because it's a metaphysical curse.

                          ------------

                          On silver bullets, besides how expensive silver is, the simple solution is to cast and load your own silver ammunition. Silver isn't actually a bad metal for bullets on its own, but it requires a completely different set of casting tools than convention bullet metals use. Once you have the appropriate casting setup (and the money for all that silver), loading a casing with gunpowder isn't any different. There's a lot of Internet debates on things like how much wear and tear silver bullets would do to the barrel and rifling, but not a lot of people have the money to pop off a few hundred silver bullets to determine what a proper maintenance regimen should be for silver ammunition in a modern barrel (or if there might be better alternative barrel designs).
