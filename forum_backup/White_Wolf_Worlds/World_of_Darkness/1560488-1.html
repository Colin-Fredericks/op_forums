My point is that the Brood serve as the "murder hobo" antagonists for VtR.                                         For me it all sums up to the idea that the Sabbat is many things not just out of design inconsistencies, but as a result of being a big organization.

                                        Anything that's said about the Sabbat is true with a lot of caveats, which means it's also not true if taken to extremes:

                                        They are *not* murder hobos. Or numerous. Or evil. Or inhuman. Or on Paths. Or functional. Or dysfunctional. Or what-have-you. They *can*.

                                        Most depictions add the problem of unreliable authors. The original depiction itself was ostensibly a rumor running on a Camarilla city with no real contact with the sect whatsoever. The more the author of a given text has actual contact with the Sabbat, and reason to not be just messing with the reader, the more nuanced they get.

                                        So the main reasons it all works is:

                                        1. The Sabbat doesn't nearly kill all that many mortals in a daily basis, the existence of *Lost Boys* style murder hobo packs notwithstanding.

                                        2. Most of the actual killings do meet the Paths of those involved.

                                        3. There is room for errors, from members that don't attend to Ritae as frequently as they should to Path Followers that aren't exactly a pristine success example.

                                        4. Everything is context-adapted. Of all the things the Sabbat can be, what it will actually be depends mostly on what's feasible at a given place and time.

                                        If you want to conciliate the idea of frequent blood-soaked orgies at night with a functional Sabbat, all you need is for the local Sabbat to exist in a context where it's feasible, which includes that those orgies fit the PoE actually present.

                                        But if you really want to keep the headcanon of the Sabbat being universally like that, then truth is that your headcanon doesn't work with many PoE.

                                                        The Sabbat gained a lot of detail and lore late in Revised that made it more compelling but also more realistic. Guide to the Sabbat filled in a lot of gaps, but Caine's Chosen and Archons and Templars provided a lot of information about the Black Hand and Sabbat Inquisition, and institutions such as the templars and paladins.

                                                        When you add in the lore from New York by Night and Mexico City by Night, you get a lot of details about their history and the three civil wars which makes them so much more interesting as a group. They also help explain how everything hung together like it did.

                                                        Chaining the Beast did a lot to make paths more plausible. Even as early as Montreal by Night (1996), though, you had things like the Path of Cathari becoming more spiritual and less "LOL you're a sadist torturer". All of this stuff helped make the Sabbat more nuanced.

                                                        Montreal by Night has some very iffy stuff now, in part because it was written at a time when White Wolf and Black Dog were still reacting to the whole Satanic panic thing, but it's also a fantastically evocative book and one of the best By Night books out there (probably my favourite, at least). It singlehandedly showed what the spiritual side of the Sabbat could be. Mexico City by Night really complements it, and i love the diversity of packs it includes. It shows how the packs are the heart of the Sword, and the Vaulderie is the thing that binds them together (that and the might of the Black Hand).

                                                        Caine's Chosen hammers home how the Sabbat really is just an assortment of groups that came together at the end of the Anarch Revolt for various purposes (pp. 15–16, "Roots & Branches", Caine's Chosen: The Black Hand). This actually makes it resemble the large political parties in the US or UK, which are also broad coalitions. Some Conservatives are closer to Labour Party members than to each other, but they huddle together in big blocs for convenience, power, and tribalism. Sometimes politicians side with things they don't really believe in because they oppose the other side even more.

                                                        Basically, I think there are plenty of reasons why the Sabbat survived, and its competing, diverse factions are actually a big part of that. It makes it adaptable, but it also gives Cainites the drive to go on—even when they're not fighting the Camarilla, the fact they want to beat out the other factions keep them determined and driven in a way that prevents them from becoming complacent in the same the Camarilla does. They hang together not just out of the loyalty of the Vinculi, but also through sheer spite and perversity.

                                                            Originally posted by Reasor View Post
                                                            To be fair, the morality system
                                                            The important part is to understand what each of those systems do, that they're not trying to achieve the same goal or state the same thing, and if you think the problem is Wassail not being reachable, then you greatly missed the point.

                                                            First of all, none of those are hard to balance, not really. And that's important because they're a tool for worldbuilding first. For a Vampire society of any kind to even work in the first place, you need a stable default, no matter if it is 3, 5 or 8. There is a point where most chars will fluctuate at, otherwise those who don't wouldn't stand out, and society itself would crumble anyway.

                                                            ---

                                                            Up to V20, VtM Humanity is about the price of power and your willingness to pay it. It's the easier one to keep at any point you want and that's a design feature, not a failure.

                                                            Here the unreliable narrator matters a lot, because the text repeats the spiel about degeneration being an inevitable curse as a deliberate red herring. Actually believing this is a staple of Kindred Society just because it help them to live with their choices. It's defensive hypocrisy.

                                                            For the game, the point is to present the PCs with situations where risking degeneration is convenient, not to find excuses to make them roll. It's imperative that in the end every check for degeneration come from a deliberate decision the character can rationalize however they want, but they truly could have done different.

                                                            And if that never risks Wassail, no prob either. In this context your level of Humanity is above all a character statement. It's you saying where, at least for now, you feel comfortable drawing the line. That stability helps grounding a char Humanity score in their characterization rather than luck.

                                                            ---

                                                            Requiem 2nd ed Humanity is about connection, not morality. This is a nuance that shouldn't be lost, and is the reason you can get extra Bans to buy some Free Out of Prison cards in your Humanity track.

                                                            A low Humanity Kindred has an easier time being an asshole without mechanical consequences, but this is not what's important to measure. A high Humanity vampire can be a Nazi, a low Humanity one can still believe in the sanctity of life, even if it doesn't have an emotional engagement anymore.

                                                            Humanity here is under attack from your reality as nonhuman, and the risk is disconnection, to lose your ability to feel like you're a part of the crowd, regardless if you believe it or not, if it is an identity marker or merely an ambush tactic.

                                                            The crux of it is the Touchstone system as it works in Requiem, because the key dynamic isn't what causes a Degeneration Roll, but how you manage your Touchstones and bonuses they provide. How your character reacts to the attacks on your sense of belonging and which strategies they chose to deal with it.

                                                            Which again involves deciding at which rung they feel more comfortable at staying, just now being a slightly more fluctuating thing, but between the strategies you can employ and the cost of buying a dot back, it isn't really hard to find a stable footing at any level you want.

                                                            ---

                                                            V5 commits to the red herring, which isn't a bad thing, but is an entirely distinct approach. And that's one of the reasons it is divisive, it is a subtle but fundamental difference in what the game loop will be.

                                                            Degeneration is truly expected to be almost inevitable, a real slippery slope where staying moral is an uphill battle.

                                                            This is staple of current horror trends. Previous editions follow the Naturalistic Moral of classic Gothic literature, where the horror is in the choices people are willing to make. In V5 the horror is in the intermittent loss of autonomy, the idea that the Beast doesn't simply makes it harder to stay moral, but actively forces you to be immoral no matter how hard you want to avoid it.

                                                            It is the crushing system that really gives you no choice in the matter, or any matter, and you ultimately can't really step outside of it or beat it for good, only try to do the best you can between crisis, between failures, between cleaning after your last catastrophic depravity and the next, while depravity is the cost of existing in this world.

                                                            ---

                                                            Three takes on horror, tragedy and how the Curse opposes identity, each exploring distinct themes and logic. I personally find V5's take cheap, but that's on me.

                                                            I only have one actual criticism about the rules: I actually find Oblivion's stains a deeply stupid idea that misses the point royally to capitalize on cheap signaling.

                                                            All Disciplines come from a place darker and hungrier than death itself, it's the Curse of the Beast. The thing that moves you to prey on the living, threaten the innocent and abuse those around you physically, emotionally, spiritually.

                                                            More importantly, they're all means to abuse and victimization in some form, usually quite direct. Oblivion is, if anything, in the lower end of the spectrum.

                                                            What it does is to cash in cool factor by using the edgy description of the Abyss in lieu of actually arguing anything. And the effect is the opposite of what it should, because any user of Oblivion that isn't a moron would notice A.S.A.P. that their best bet is to be conservative with its use and otherwise be a decent person, as they mechanically need their soul in a good shape to keep fueling/risking it in Oblivion.

                                                            Oblivion is precisely for two clans known for being reliant on spamming their signature powers and being openly more imoral than other kindred, but works as a reverse incentive.
