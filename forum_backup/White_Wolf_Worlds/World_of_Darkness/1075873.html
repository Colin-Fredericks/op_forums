    ​Critical Thoughts on the Potential in a 5th Edition of the Game.

    04-10-2017, 10:26 AM
    In another thread I was compelled into a confrontation over the nature of the ST System and difficulties in the current market. My honorable opposition is not an intuitor primary, and the difference in our posits has more to do with the division between the analysis of sensual perception and the analysis of conceptual perception. These are both valid approaches, each with their own inherent pros and cons.

    A problem for all Fantasy Entertainment, is the combination of treacherous bubble and economic downturn. Real wealth incomes in the US, and the majority of the Western World, have been in decline since the economic policy shifts of the 1980's. While the numbers have increased, the value of those numbers has decreased. Because reality has been more difficult, the celebration of reality has been less - that is people prefer to buy fantasy when the reality sucks. Hence, the mass support for superhero films, an entire network devoted to fantasy (CW), the history channel focussing on myth rather than modern events, etc. It was the same during the 80's when artists who sold fantasy illustration or subjects of a by-gone golden age made money hand over fists while those who sold portraits, still life, and landscapes struggled. So, while the current economic climate of the past three decades supports the sales of fantasy entertainment, to include RPGs and video RPGs, that market share has been the facade of austere economic pressures where the bulk of society is receiving less and less in real world assets. From the distant vantage, the move from mass published HB rules to digital publishing is just another leaf floating along the dwindling stream of resources supporting the general public. Those with a more myopic vantage point will not see this because they stand amidst the trees.

    Following this stream of events, the directing of budgetary support de-emphasized (conceptually abandoned) the Classic World of Darkness. This was an event that did not sit well with those of us who were not done with what that avenue had to offer. Further, there were many of us who simply felt the way it ended was petite for an apocalypse - like a small lump of bread pudding to cap off a 12 course meal of beautifully crafted gourmet presentations. A truly apocalyptic ending would have restored the Bringer of Harmony or devoured the Wild leaving the Weaver nothing to weave but the broken remains of what could have been. The new materials moved toward a more universal concept of play, and abandoned an intentional meta for an unintended one implied by the materials of the game.

    Some consider the evolution of the games in such short a time to be a simple response to market pressures. These would be short term market concerns. When the Japanese began defeating the American Auto industry it was because they were making decisions based on generations of impact rather than merely responding to short term market influences. A more Jungian approach, one that responds to the universality of the soul, is already inherent in the Story Teller System. However, it seems to be treated as an occasional daliance between flirtatious friends rather than capitalized upon for the expansive possibilities it entails.

    One point of disagreement is whether or not the Story Teller System is or is meant to be more universal in its approach. Another is whether it encourages the suspension of disbelief. That the rules promote the deliberate deferrence to the suspension of disbelief over game mechanics is and of itself is a mechanic in the rules encouraging the suspension of disbelief. This is, as I view it, also a problem for marketing against the larger more familiar and less imagination supporting game systems.

    Where other systems, such as Shadowrun, AD&D, etc. have dominated the market it is in large part because they have a massive amount of edutainment support in the video gaming industry. Seven year olds will learn the basic ability scores, hp system, leveling up, etc. of less imaginative gaming systems from the various games they play. In many ways, AD&D is just a video game on paper where mass accumulation of points, like mass accumulation of wealth, is the only goal and the expansion of the mind, the feeding of the soul, is utterly neglected. "Give me four years to teach the children and the seed I have sown will never be uprooted," said Lenin. Well, the opposing team gets many more than that and it took 3/4s of a century to undo what Lenin did. So, while it remains true that familiarity may breed contempt, it is more true that familiarity embraces convenience. The greatest boon to the ST system would be a base game which can be easily promoted at the juvenile level both in Pen and Paper and in Video.

    From an intuitive point of view all of the games published by White Wolf and Onyx Path are merely variations on a theme. All of them can be re-interpreted into a singular gaming action. Whether it is from WoD, CoD, Exalted, Scion, or Trinity - the conceptual material is adaptable across the games because it is born out of a consistent theme of struggle and similar mechanics. They are not however sensually identical, which makes them difficult to plug and play with one another without hours of conversion for each. So, while the mechanics of these systems are universally applicable, they are not conveniently interactive. The Translation Guides deliver a base support for abandoning one game series for the other. They demonstrate how to transition characters. They fail to transition things like Axis Mundi, to make the metas of each system playable in the other.

    This latter was a problem for me in playing the initial games. The constantly changing mechanics within the same system made adapting them into the same world difficult. The worst two for this were Wraith and Changeling. The structure of wraith made it virtually impossible to interact with the Skinlands where all the other games took place. The system of powers for Changelings simply came across as an unreliable game of table craps. One of the reasons I collected the books, but never played or even tried to play a Changeling. In every aspect of mechanics the CoD was an improvement for both play and for interaction between the various games.

    The only thing that the system has ever really lacked for universality of play is a system of rules for tech level. Tables of Equipment and pricing for different eras: Stone Age, Bronze Age, Iron Age, Renaissance, Enlightenment, Industrial Age, Computer Age, Interplanetary Age, Interstellar Age, Intergalactic Age, and potentially a Transdimensional Age. Some of this is adaptable from the different publications by Onyx Path. Some of it has not been deliberately explored.

    Another aspect of the early games was the brilliance of its inculcation of the classical mind into the ambiance of the game at the very warp and weft of it. Intentionally or unintentionally the game congealed the roiling thoughts of humanities best thinkers into an expression of its variant philosophical approaches, its most important ethical dilemmas, and the epic journey of the flawed hero. Each of the five base games explored a specific moral dilemma for humanity. In vampire the question was begged: If we are evil by the nature of what we are, how do we strive to be good and remain sane? In werewolf: How do we deal with the frustration of being the only one's who can see what is really going on in a world destroying itself? In mage: How do we find a way for utterly disparate paradigms to exist peacefully side by side off of the same limited resources? In Wraith: If we are the victims of an extreme trauma how do we over come the shadows on our soul it leaves behind and transcend to the better? And in Changeling: How do we balance the curious playful adventurous side of our soul with the mundane pragmatic risk-averse necessities of social life? Those questions became less conspicuous in CoD than they were in WoD. And this made the journey of the flawed hero less observable. There was no longer a path directing the journey from where we find the hero of our story and the character development they must inevitably achieve or tragically fail to make.

    Assuming there will be a 5th Edition game, it would be my hope that it would address these concerns.
    1) Create a marketable version for a juvenile game which captures a market share of minds early on.
    2) Establish a better presence in the Video gaming industry.
    3) Use a base character sheet for mundanes as the source for a sequentially written table of contents which explains all basic game mechanics. (e.g. use of Attributes, Abilities/Skills, Backgrounds/Conditions, Merits, Flaws, Virtue/Integrity, Will Power, Health, etc.) This would be a down and dirty pocket reference for quick start introduction.
    4) Prepare a base template system that allows the easy incorporation of all previously published game materials. These could be published separately, in installments, covering different aspects which have previously been separate games. Thus becoming a vampire, discovering one is a were-X, dying with some fetter that prevents one from truly moving on, etc. becomes an addition or development of the base character.
    5) Establish technology based equipment guides supporting expansive play possibilities.
    6) Campaign materials which do not change or add to the mechanics, but which provide support for master plots in various milieus: Past, Present, Future, and on the other side of the looking glass. As always, dazzle us with the art, "the real relation, the underlying theme".

                      The problem for the gaming industry is not the change in editions, the majority of gaming companies wait five to ten years before they come out with a new edition (if not longer), it is the change in consumer preferences caused by the isolating effect of the internet. It is much easier to get people to fork over money for gaming products when they have people who they physically know who will be willing to play with them. In my own experience, I am usually the ST and the first buyer of the groups that I am part of and, when I run a game, my players usually buy their own copies of the books after the first game.

                      One problem that a number of people I know have is that the gaming population is aging and the majority of people have the books that they need. In my own case, I have over three hundred physical books from over a dozen different systems (cWoD, Scion, Exalted, GURPS, AFMBE, L5R, Heroes, Pathfinder, Eclipse Phase, BESM, Conan, Feng Shui, Savage Worlds, Mutants and Masterminds, etc), and that is what is just what is in my living room. I am getting to the point where I really start wondering if I need any more physical books, but I buy them out of loyalty to the companies that I like. There are plenty of people though who just want the books from one or two systems and really do not want or need to expand beyond their current collection unless they join a physical game where the ST is running an unfamiliar system (I doubt that many people join internet games where people are using unfamiliar systems).
