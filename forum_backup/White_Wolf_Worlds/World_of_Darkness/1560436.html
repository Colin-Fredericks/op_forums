    Advice on a setting

    06-07-2026, 02:21 PM
    I had a unique setting idea for a CWoD for either a Vampire, Werewolf, Mage, or Changeling 20th ed game and I would like some thoughts and advice on how to make it fit the tone of the Game




    My idea so far is based on things like Escape from New York and Batman: Arkham City . Like in these pieces of media, a big section of an entire city is turned into a mega prison by a new regime, placing thousands of criminals and other prisoners within its confines, including the different kinds of supernaturals, albeit unknowingly, who are now trying to adapt to the hellhole they found themselves in.




    What would the various splats be up to in this kind of setting and who could other elements of the WoD leak into it and can I have some ideas on what could convince them to stay in the city instead of escaping it.

          Well first things first, what is keeping the various splats in the city in the first place? Most of them could relatively easily escape if they wanted to, at least if it is a mortal creation, so we have to assume that the trapped ones are either too young to be able to get out, or they want to stay for some reason.

          Vampires are probably the best contained, but mostly for the same reason they don't like travelling outside of cities already. They're not going to have too hard a time getting past mortal defenses but they'll have the same difficulties travelling to another city as they do from anywhere. Besides, a lot of vampires aren't going to have a problem being in a prison city. Plenty of herd, lots of favor trading.

          Werewolves have perhaps the easiest time getting out because they can step sideways, and travel out of the city in the penumbra. While, yes, cities and population centers tend to have a higher gauntlet rating, turning a city into a prison doesn't really make it more ordered, structured, or weaver like. Some locations may grow to have a very high gauntlet, but not all, and some locations will probably drop, especially at night. If you want to stop that, there needs to be some force that makes Umbral travel much, much harder, such as a spirit choir that is running rampant or a high ranking Weaver spirit that is spiking the gauntlet, or the Technocracy putting an enormous amount of resources into locking down the area.

          The most likely thing that will make werewolves want to stay is protecting any Caerns that happen to be within the bounds of the prison city. Some may also stay to stop the hellish situation from creating Wyrm tainted areas (I'm forgetting the term for wyrm wastelands in the umbra right now).

          Mages likewise can cross the Gauntlet, but they also have teleportation, invisibility, matter manipulation, good fortune, etc. . Really most spheres quickly make it hard to contain a mage unless something is working very hard to stop them, and it will have to work very hard given how versatile mages as a whole are. Mages are likewise highly varied in why they might want to stay, but almost certainly there will be a faction that wants to support the prison city idea (whether or not they invented it) and at least one faction that wants to experiment with the consensus in this new area. These may or may not be the same group.

          Wraiths, though you didn't mention them, are not directly affected. The Hierarchy isn't going to directly be a part of the prison city idea and it's really hard to stop a ghost from leaving if they want to. On the other hand, many will be working overtime to protect their Fetters and lots of them will come to feed off the emotions of the place.


          I'll let someone else handle Changelings.

              The Technocracy's relationship to this No Man's Land will be interesting. They've heavily invested themselves into the infrastructure, media, and economy of the world. They are at a significant advantage where they can work with them, both in power (because their procedures work with them and they can easily move resources into position) and in influence (because the sleepers rely on them and that gives the Technocracy more access and more control).

              In a traditional No Man's Land setting most of that is gone. No flow of goods and services in and out of the city, no easy communication or mass media, no government agencies to manipulate or impersonate, no reliable power or data lines. The exception being at the periphery where the increased need to prevent movement in and out means an increase in surveillance and whatever manpower, military, and maintenance resources are needed to lock down the exclusion zone.

              So what we see is a dramatic loss of advantages for the Technocracy within the city, and at best a small increase in their abilities at the edges. Now the Technocracy can work without the typical advantages of the city and they can provide alternatives for themselves. They do both these things elsewhere in the world, so it's not like they are crippled. They are weakened however.
              Among other things it is a strong indication they aren't behind the creation of the prison city. Outside of a very compelling and unique situation they aren't going to give up their major city advantages and hamstring their ability to manipulate the consensus.

              Of course that assumes a classic Prison City. You could have other variants, like a propaganda driven prison city. The powers that be maintain basic utilities and a network of reinforced screens and loudspeakers that broadcast state approved messages constantly. Heavily armed patrols periodically move through the city maintaining and repairing these and defending the locations for food and medical supply delivery, but otherwise they leave the inmates to fend for themselves. Several of the Conventions could work well with that setup, especially if we are using a more corrupt and authoritarian Technocracy.

                  First, what defines a No Man's Land in this style is absence, not presence. While the focus of action doesn't necessarily deal with that, it's important to understand that from the perspective of the party responsible this is not a functional strategy or goal, it's a failure state

                  Of course, in the big scheme it can have other motivations and be successful at them, but as a means to manage problems it is you giving up in them entirely and just isolating your loss. Especially since you can't really control what's going on, so you can't control how else it will evolve and get out of hand.

                  This all is important because the first thing all splats will do is react to the responsible party and their goals. They'll spend effort into learning what is happening and why, to know if and how it affects them. It's also relevant for any splat faction that is the responsible party, because you have to define why they chose such an extreme measure and all it entails. Any member still present is essentially abandoned to their own devices, in practice even if not in intention.

                  ---

                  Second, define if splats are there by choice or constraints, because that changes everything. They are too resourceful, and that means the usual assumption that the inmates have to work within the premise doesn't hold: either they can leave whenever they want, or the whole setting is a problem to solve rather than a reality to navigate.

                  They can have all sorts of reasons to want to stay: resources, sacred duties, curiosity, if you go down this path it's easy finding out a reason for each to be there.
                  - Vampires have a lot of people together and mortal institutions chose to leave. This is not for every undead's tastes, but for many is an opportunity to build a "Utopian" domain like the legends of Carthage and Constantinople.
                  - Werewolves will protect any sacred site and keep an eye on their enemies, as this is a prime opportunity for the Wyrm to build a base of power that'll later be too powerful to ignore, so the logics of war dictate they need a foothold.
                  - Mages are drawn by curiosity and ideology with almost preternatural strength. They'll have all sorts of individual interests and the Will to pursue them against all odds.
                  - Changelings are living stories and this is a hell of a story to live. Not to say they mostly live in their own perspective over the world, so many will stay out of lack of any reason not to.

                  In all those cases the splats can either keep in contact with the exterior or accept severance as a cost without making a fuss.

                  Constraints mean each affected splat will be actively trying to solve their barriers for good. None of them will quietly accept this level of intervention in their lives, so you can bet they'll have concerted efforts to tear the whole thing down on principle. Even if their situations aren't bad the arrangement is a huge liability few of their factions will suffer.
                  - How's this opposing our power?
                  - Why it is doing so?
                  - What it spells in the long run?
                  - What it knows we don't?
                  - How can we get the upper hand?

                  Each will ask those questions in different ways, seeking different answers, but none will ignore them.

                  ---

                  Third, a few things sure are likely to happen regardless:

                  1. Bye-bye Mask. Under this kind of constant pressure, close existence and loss of the many institutions the supernatural rely on to stay hidden, keeping The Secret will be an increasingly worsening uphill battle.
                  2. Unexpected new factions. In all sides the circumstances that created the usual factions mostly dissolved, while the new reality and constant proximity dismantle the usual mutual ignorance and forces new compromises. Even if external loyalties remain, local expressions are bound to change in extreme ways.
                  3. Logistics will be an overarching theme. With the infrastructure that keeps the world going down, replacing it is both a necessity and an opportunity. Everything that could previously be assumed to just work can't anymore. What happens when the basic supplies a Caern needs are now better sourced by a vampiric smuggling ring? Suddenly even toilet paper can be turned into a life and death plot point as relevant in the long run as stashes of magical artifacts.
