    From the moment Montreal By Night first saw print, the Sabbat’s appeal, to me, was the fantasy of these creatures trying to forge a genuinely posthuman society for themselves - even with all the hypocrisies that come with it, like the clashes between the generations within the sect. At least these people weren’t going to waste their immortality by hating themselves for what they are. For reasons having to do with being a closeted twentysomething (I’m out and fortysomething now) from a strict religious background, the Sabbat’s core fantasy of striving to accept your real self, finding a community where you belong anyway, and then maybe becoming something more spoke to me in a way that Anarchs Versus Cammie Princes couldn’t.

    Without getting into the accuracy of claims about player demographic estimates, I’m definitely on the side of players who found the game world interesting because of the Sabbat.

            Hello Onyxpath Forum!
            It’s Thomas from Flyos Games, the board game company developing Vampire the Masquerade CHAPTERS.

            After reading your comments, I think I’ll add my input as well.
            Not to discuss the Sabbat timeline (Since we have a NDA) but to provide more details about the Chapters project.

            Vampire the Masquerade CHAPTERS is a narrative board game, taking place in Montreal.
            The starting idea of the project was that it is hard, complicated to play VTM as much as we want. We’ve been playing for 18 years and friends still cancel, snow keep falling every winter (especially in Montreal), and adults are busy/exhausted with life.

            We wanted to buy a box where everything was inside, ready to use: a storyteller (we tried, it’s hard to fit), different character archetypes, a well-written and credible story where choices impact the events, and the possibility to play alone or with friends.
            Unfortunately, that game does not exist… yet.

            So, we decided to use our board game experience (Kiwetin and Until Daylight) and our undying passion for Vampire the Masquerade to create the best project we ever worked on. It would be a bridge between board game and RPG, an introduction for some, something easy to play for new players and hard to master for passionate fans.

            Why Montreal?
            Because we live in it and things have changed in 20 years in Montreal.
            The city has never been so full of joy, arts, happy humans, construction and corruption.
            WoD, the world and kindred organization have changed as well, and humans are more determined than ever before to find scapegoats.
            Sabbat never hide their glory, it makes sense that in the context of the second inquisition, the Sabbat was the first to fall.

            We won’t divulge anything about the story, the plot, or things that may happen in the game.
            However, we can say that we are doing our utmost best to create the best game, story and campaign possible.

            What we can do is talk about our commitment, the team of illustrators, writers, translators, scenarists, miniatures sculptor, graphic designers and game designers, we have gathered.
            No compromise on the quality, no shortcut.
            https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/...erade-chapters

            What we can promise you is that we are relentless.
            Matthew Dawkins is a Lore consultant and is taking an active role in the development of the project.
            We are meeting players at the GenCon, listening to their ideas, concerns and taking notes.
            We are inviting VTM players to our HQ to test the prototype, give their insight and feedback

            What we can do is include you:
            We posted a first post in Onyx Path forum 8 minutes after our press release (April 4th 2019, at 00:08am) asking for anyone willing to participate, give their feedback and help us create the best RPG in a box to come to the Facebook group to help us. http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...ters-announced

            What we offer you is a place at the table to give your input, help us understand how you play, what you like, what in your opinion should we add and would make Vampire the Masquerade Chapters the best RPG in a box possible:

            Come help us: https://www.facebook.com/groups/Vamp...eradeCHAPTERS/

                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                        I think the closest example I can think of is the fact that the Sabbat is a bit like the Galactic Empire in the Star Wars Expanded Universe. A lot of writers felt the need to start humanizing the Galactic Empire with characters like Grand Admiral Thrawn, Admiral Pellaeon, and other "heroic" Imperials. They even had the Galactic Empire make a peace treaty with the New Republic and become the co-stars of the Legacy comics that were the Grand Finale of the Old Expanded Universe.

                        This was something I feel was a serious misstep and missed the point of the characterization. I had a similar feeling about the Technocracy Revised books, which continually and repeatedly tried to paint the Technocracy in the best light possible. Something I was uncomfortable with then and now realize was because, "Wait, these guys are genocidal fascists. WHY are we trying to put a smiley face on them?" I had a similar moment in Warhammer 40K fandom when I realized some people were sincere Emperor fanboys.

                        The Sabbat has continually lost a huge amount of its edge as more of its details have been exposed to the public. They also have been reduced from being a terrifying and mysterious force to just stealing from the Anarch's stick (FREEEEEDOM) and stealing from the Camarilla (manipulative alien elders). Seeing them get broken up into new sects I think is a good thing for people who want to have deeper concepts.
                        From what I've read across other of your posts across the forum on the theme of Sabbat and some other elements of the game, I have an impression that, with all due respect, you are tilting at windmills here. I really don't understand the need to dehumanize Galactic Empire, WH40K either side, traitors or loyalists, Sabbat of 2ed/Revised either if you are playing the game. It feels like, as some of us have noticed, the main problem of V5 is that too much is being dragged from the mindsets and opinions of out-of-characters.

                        Technocracy are genocidal fascists. Imperium of Man is comprised of genocidal fascists. Galactic Empire are genocidal fascists. Ehm, in regards to respective settings, who isn't? I am not going to start a holy war of out-of-game beliefs here. People like to explore extreme things - personalities, emotions, ideologies, themes, views, derangements. The very thing which is the biggest attraction of Vampire games, to me, and I believe I am not alone in this, is the deviance, the alternative, the exploration of extremities of human condition and emotion. How you can do it when you judge a darker part of the setting as unconditionally bad? what about the others then, are Camarilla or Anarchs attitudes towards mortals in any way of human perception and understanding, valid? When you're embraced, you are an abomination of natural order, a thing which has to literally and figuratively feed off other people to sustain itself. How come one of that kind is worse than the other, from the human-centered pov? The human of WoD which is not quite darker than the real one, does not distinguish a camarilla monster from sabbat monster, they are equally mostrous to us. Why do we delve into that viewpoint at all, or, even worse, make it a Final Instance of what is good or bad concerning the setting?
                        Let's not play the game at all then, or try out the Hunter and find yourself as another monster this time bereft of any 'supernatural' excuse - becoming Dexter/Punisher of WoD was solely your choice and there is no greater power or fate to blame.

                        Going back to the concepts of mentioned factions across various settings, I believe to be fair is to dig deeper into what it means for someone to be a serious, genuine 'Emperor fanboy'. I would say that the rule of thumb across any mature roleplay is that immersing yourself that deep into in-game factions and characters should not be accepted as a valid MO. If we are talking about the factions across settings though, why would we call Imperium genocidal fascists in terms of said setting? To a human in WH40k, I'd argue, both Chaos and Imperium present too almost equally bad options but the rest are even worse (I'd argue that probably Tau' way is the 'nominally' best, but again, that's from my viewpoint of 2k human raised in the spirit of rationality, tolerance and human-centric attitude). Humanity itself is such a multi-faceted concept that I don't want to write a novel here about it, just raise a rhetorical quesiton of why we are taking one specific approach to it as a centerpoint of the whole setting? Saying Galactic Emire, Sabbat etc. are just evil, with all due respect, as one-dimensional a statement as one can do especially in regards to such a complex setting as WoD. Why wouldn't authors try to humanize them? Why should they necessarily dehumanize them? Why should we stay on the 'brighter' side without looking at - and through the eyes of - the darker one? Do we have so few examples of deumanizing one side and humanizing the other in real world history? The characters on both sides are humans after all. Even mutated Chaos cultists or the most robzombiesque Sabbat are or were humans and still carry that seed in themselves however vehemently they deny it. Are humans unconditionally good? I believe there's an answer in the question itself. Should we try to explore all sides of what it means to be a Human in the games and, what is the point of this discussion, allow authors to explore all sides of it in game books even though the eyes of the Other, including the Monster? definitely. The only question I would raise in that regard is that with how much class and delicacy you do it? (some parts of V5 did it completely abandoning finesse and delicacy for cheap edginess, you know what I'm talking about, and it was one huge fail).
                        It was always a core underlying theme, a meta, if you will, of the whole WoD, not just the Vampire - "only becoming a Monster allows one to understand what it means and what it takes to be a Human".

                        People join various services and structures with questionable reputations not because they are evil, or want to. History knows countless atrocities committed by people believing they do the right thing to benefit other people not just themselves. Order of Reason was created to save humanity, and Technocracy's purpose is still that, at least a declarative one. Imperium protects humankind with whatever means it finds necessary, most of the time failing horribly. Not a fan or adept of SW but I believe Galactic Empire is not about 24/7 human sacrifices - for some reason some people believe in advantages and dismiss the disagvantages, of authoritarian regimes, they did in the past, they do now, why it shouldn't happen in future? Me, a descendant of communists, always questioned my predecessors' beliefs but always wondered what took it for them to keep going? To persevere in the face of brutal, horrible Soviet reality? They did not do bad things themselves but they surely realized of what kind of regime they were part of. How they coped with it? What made them strive and hope for the best and raise their kids and grandkids to be intelligent, delicate humans in the face of the worst of their reality? What it takes to carry noblebright in your heart when it's grimdark surrounding you? How that noblebright gets warped after decades of surviving said reality? I found my answers to these questions and to be honest, darker aspects of WoD, among many other things, only helped me in that quest.

                        Now, going more specific, not sure why the Sabbat are mischaracterized since the deviation from 1ed depiction of boogeymen. Just recently, Justin Achilli posted in his twitter a picture displaying the main three factions in the game as a three legged chair - Anarchs as Peasants, Camarilla as Aristocracy, Sabbat as Clergy. I think of it as the most valid simplification on the sects' identities. Getting Sabbat out of the Clergy spot into something between all three was a bad choice imo which happened in Revised. Across all sects, Sabbat was at the forefront of going 'other way', of abstaining from playing mortals and with mortals instead trying to evolve, transcend, and explore the 'new' world deeper instead of focusing on the material. The setting itself ecouraged it - being a supernatural thing that can kill any bully in the street or manipulate any banker into giving a credit card number, but being killed by a mere touch of sunlight kind of forces you into a certain predisposition. Shifts your perspective, to put it mildly. The Sabbat was a way to ponder on the nature of vampirism much more thoroughly and deeply than other sects, in my opinion, and it was dark enough to play it properly - primarily through trauma. The PTSD theme, to me, is vital to understanding what Sabbat is and how it should be played and characterized. What happens when a whole sect is an embodiment of the circle of violence and abuse? How do individuals cope with it? At what point one breaks, and if breaks at all? How much of personal will and drive is needed to try and turn things around you into another direction when you and your closest ones are small parts of one military/religious machine? Moving most of the Sabbat from exploring Paths in 2ed to being 70% just around Humanity 4 at permanent was definitely a poor choice, imo. That's what made them 'darker Camarilla' and 'more violent Anarchs' and tore out the huge chunk of their appeal. Combine it with Lasombra' Godfather caricature and shovelheads' Rob Zombie movies and you see why it's a bad choice when you aimed at playing a spiritual pillar of vampiric world.

                        On a sidenote, I genuinely despised some of Russian and other EE players mocking V5 for retconning the worst things which came out since it's start and the whole "HAHA they dont have balls HAHA" imageboard-y bullshit. Here, I am trying to find that golden middle between "going too far" and "not going at all". With the latter, which I believe you propse, we can simply throw out one of the three pillars of VtM out of the window turning the remaining Sabbat crusaders into Diablo 3 minibosses.

                        Going with Gehenna war being simply a feast for Ancients is not good either - quoting Achilli again, of course there are huge chances you and your coterie/pack are manipulated by terrifying methuselahs somewhere out there; there are still some chances you can be lucky by breaking or avoiding their strings and taking the fight to your puppetmaster(s). Without that romantic tiny chance, the setting, the metaplot turns into something predictable, cynical and dull (just as planned all along). What if Gehenna crusade succeeds and the Sabbat are not possessed but make a triumphant return? What if they return not as infernalists but as scholars and prophets (still monsters by human POV, mind you), toughened but enlightened through trauma by the worst of both human and vampiric worlds and striving to elevate Cainites and oppose Ancients in some new unexpected ways? Just a thought, but I'd be very disappointed if they go full braindead Jyhad.

                        Maybe that gap between 1ed mysterious threat and revised' freak circus is what made you consider Sabbat due a redesign in v5. Probably we agree on that. It's just me that found the most interesting themes in the sect in the 2ed and in the Montreal by Night specifically. For me, this book was the turning point and my interest of VtM and WoD overall went from casually scrolling through rulebooks to zealously (hah) devising believable backgrounds and stories, drawing NPC portraits and reading through tomes in order to create my own chronicles full of secrets to uncover. It was also my first Sabbat book and everything Revised I read after felt like a huge step backwards, although I realized that the authors decided, for reasons unknown, to make Mexico/NY a mainstream Sabbat, and Montreal - a rare deviation, an anomaly in the lost boys' club. The purge of Path of Harmony followers is an epitome of that stupid turn. Sad, and if you ask me, unnecessary, but maybe that clears up things how I and some other players viewed the sect and what it should have been.

                        Another thread on this forum, BJD Metaplot diary, had your post on the Montreal chapter and one part of it actually made me compose this wall of text. You mentioned that Lucita is looking for friends and camaraderie in the Sabbat, and good luck to her with that. Ironically, the Montreal book explores this part of Sabbat identity better than any other. The packs are disciplined, organized and driven. Just a couple of examples:

                        Two Lasombra love each other to the point of one violating his beliefs and risking his own status to protect and vouch for the other despite her weakness and obsession with her mortal relative.
                        Gargoyle Loyalist is enthralled by a bishop and can't control herself because of the feelings; out of sheer camaraderie he, follower of path of power, violates it's tenets in order to go out of his way and help the 'weak' to join another pack and find company; things are worsened because the bishop is Ultra and the pack he sends Gargoyle to is of Loyalists, so he's strengthening his political rival but ultimately believes it's all for the good of the sect, falling behind on his own Path. His packmates, Black Hand members, know of this sacrifice and respect him all the more because of it.
                        Another cainite sent to kill said bishop joins his pack instead after a night of the duel with that bishop during which he made the assasin question his beliefs through conversation mixed with combat; a secret is that the bishop has no vinculum bonds over the assasin - after that duel he follows the bishop out of genuine respect.
                        A former archbishop repents and abandones his infernalist ways trying to save his childe and the city while realising it's almost impossible in the face of mistrust, outright hostility and the other supernatural forces at play. Still he perseveres while constantly tempted to turn back to his hellish powers and reclaim everything he lost with brute force.
                        A pack takes in an accidentally embraced cainite who proved his worth in combat, even if it goes against Sabbat tradition and he's not True Sabbat - they value his tenacity and personal strength and he is ready to die for his new family.

                        From human POV, all of them are unapologetic murderous monsters with their hands in blood up to elbows who see themselves far above humans while refusing (to varying degree) to recognize how close their passions and emotions are to human ones. Some do, most don't, but the smartest of them realize they are mere pawns in the game with their souls at stake and that it's their Paths and spiritual evolution which can only save them; there is no going back to Humanity - only those around you are the only solace and light in the pitch blackness which is Montreal's supernatural world, and even they hide their own secrets some of which are darker than others. Again, the rest of them wallows in spiritual rot at the heart of the city, oblivious to corruption surrounding them. Political chess, scholarly rivalry, personal vendettas form the web which entangles them while the city is quickly going to hell. The sheer challenge of untying that web and saving what is worth - and defining what even is worth - saving is what appeals to me in the Sabbat since said book. Not sure why humanizing vampires of Sabbat, basically transitioning Humanity dilemmas and relations with mortals to vampire-only community in such a way is worse than portraying them as mindless boogeymen, or worse than humanizing Camarilla elders or Anarch gangsters. Quoting yourself, Humanity in WoD is just another, 6th kind of monstrous species and I totally agree with it.
                        The city has it's own Litany of Blood, a ritual you mentioned Beckett at in BJD; you see a 'typical Sabbat thing'. I see a holy - for participants - ritual honoring the huge effort, loss and dedication of those who put their unlives for their common cause; a tradition of honoring your dead has a trace throughout human history and appeals to the better parts of human nature. What other Vampire sect or society unironically and diligently preserves the memory of it's fallen members for centuries? Or, while we are at it, what other vampire society has so many members willingly putting themselves on the line for some higher purpose? Sabbat was designed to explore human themes uncovered by Anarch-Prince dichotomy under angles untried before, and it's kind of sad it's all got obscured for you, among others, by shallow savagery of Revised shovelheads.
                        Adding a theme of Bigger Scarier Than Thou in the face of self-obsessed Nietzschean vampires makes the whole story believable; Metathiax and his pawns were that in MbN, Methuselahs, Ancients and occasional possessed kindred or even other cainites could fit that role in V5. Not to mention the Second Inquisition.

                        The book has enough examples of things Lucita is looking for which are almot non-existant in other parts of vampiric society, for many reasons not only Vaulderie, but of course, without reading it, the only book which pays Sabbat due in revised is probably Caine's Chosen. Perhaps she should be trying to become a Seraph instead of regent Black Hand also breaks the commonly expressed narrative of spiritual, sophisticated Sabbat automatically becoming Camarilla-like - Camarialla never had it's own CIA/GRU and vampiric warfare should not always resemble '30 days of night' nonsense - new Sabbat should be probably fighting more like Bourne or Alejandro from Sicario.

                        Now, with an attempt to wrap it all up, golden rule and agree to disagree still stand, however I felt the need to oppose things you mentioned as taken for granted or seen as part of mainstream direction the authors wanted to go all along - I never found VtM and WoD overall to be a didactic setting telling us what's certainly bad and what's certainly good and play to 'correct the world' but offering an opportunity truly unique to roleplaying industry to see, discuss, play and decide for ourselves, raising the toughest questions and looking for answers in ourselves and in others. Sabbat, and especially MbN, was one of the things really good for that purpose.

                        Thanks for your time if reading this, and again, no offense intended.
