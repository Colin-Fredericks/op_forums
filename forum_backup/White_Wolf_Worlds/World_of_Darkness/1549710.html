    What the Spheres Say They Can Do

    04-01-2025, 12:30 PM
    Time for a different pass through the Spheres. This time I’m going to look at the Sphere write ups by rank and break down what abilities they say they give a mage. I’ll analyze obvious interpretations, potential exploits, and unintuitive uses seen elsewhere in the book.

    In previous analyses I’ve been quick to call out contradictions and say a passage is wrong. However, the real problem is I missed what the text was actually saying, so let’s take a different approach here. When an apparent false statement arises, I want to search for a possible interpretation that would make the statement make sense.

    If M20 really does contradict itself, though, here is how I will weigh its statements:

    1) A statement from a Sphere writeup about that Sphere takes precedence over a statement from anywhere else in the book, including one from a different Sphere writeup.

    2) A statement from the base description of the sphere takes precedence over any Rank description.

    3) A statement from a Sphere Rank about that Rank takes precedence over a statement from a different Rank. That is, if Matter 2 and Matter 3 disagree about the capabilities of Matter 2, Matter 2 has priority.

    4) Statements from M20 take precedence over statements from other books and previous editions. So other texts can fill in missing information but they can’t contradict a direct statement from M20.

    I can’t be as exhaustive as I’d like, so I welcome any suggestions you have. Especially on different interpretations of powers and alternate uses.

      Correspondence Base


      Purview: Correspondence governs both Space and Connections. Often one is described as an effect of the other, but either way it covers both.


      Special Rules and Limitations

      1 - There are specific rules about using connections to bypass normal distance restrictions.


      2 - Correspondence can extend range, allowing mages to interact with distant things as if they were present. If this requires more than just physical contact, it may require additional spheres.


      The most straightforward interpretation of this is: if they could do something without magic if they were present, they can do it with the appropriate rank in Correspondence without additional spheres. If they would need spheres in person, they still need those spheres from a distance.

      Note: This specifically gives Correspondence 2 the ability to grasp and manipulate items from a distance.


      3 - When using Correspondence with other spheres, a mage’s rank in Corr caps the rank in other spheres than can be used. This was probably intended for effects where Correspondence is altering the range of another spell, or something similar.

      On a strict reading this applies any time Corr is added to a spell, such as adding a Corr 1 sensory effect to an invisibility spell.

      I should point out that M20 does not include the higher rating of Correspondence when it gives Sphere requirements, though other texts do.


      4 - Correspondence deals with whole patterns only. Moving a full pattern does not require additional spheres. Moving a partial pattern requires the appropriate spheres to split the pattern.

      I can think of a few cases where 2 and 4 seem to contradict, but I think this is easily resolved by viewing 2 as a rule about extending range and 4 as a rule about applying Correspondence directly to a target. So teleporting half a piece of paper requires Corr alone if the mage is manually reaching out, tearing it in half, and pulling one half back to himself, but it takes Corr and Matter to teleport half the sheet directly. Teleporting an elephant directly would take Corr alone, while opening a portal and pushing the elephant through would take Corr and a spell to do the pushing. (Though that pushing effect might itself be a Correspondence effect)

          A note on M20s formatting. Each rank writeup starts with a list of abilities and/or rote names from previous editions and then goes into deeper descriptions of the rank. I’ll try to give weight to both the list and the description, although this isn’t always possible. Often the powers in the list are cute but uninformative. I’m not going to complain too much about that; as will become clear, I’m a fan of coming up with creative names for effects.

          Correspondence 1

          • Immediate Spatial Perceptions / Landscape of the Mind

          Basic spatial understanding allows a mage to sense things in her immediate vicinity even if she can’t perceive them with her normal senses. Using that perception, she can estimate the distances between objects; intuitively find a direction (North, South, East, West); notice hidden objects or characters; and spot spatial instabilities – warps, anomalies, wormholes, etc. – especially the ones caused by other Correspondence Effects.
          This is one of M20’s poorly curated lists. The paragraph gives one power- sensing things in the immediate vicinity- and everything else is listed as an application of that one power. Except that finding a direction isn’t an application of that power as it doesn’t rely on sensing objects in range. Let’s ignore the idea that they are related powers for now.
          Anyways, Corr 1 lets a mage:


          Sense Things in Vicinity (Imm. Spatial Perceptions)

          Sense Hidden Objects (Imm. Spatial Perceptions)


          I’m pairing these up because they seem to be descriptions of the same thing. At a direct reading, this allows mages to have a sense of the area around them, without using their regular senses, and regardless of camouflage or concealment. This is potentially very powerful, but depends on what level of detail the ST allows. This is not clear. It also isn’t clear how to adjudicate contests between this and attempts at concealment.

          Based on use in other areas of the book, this should at the very least eliminate penalties for operating without sense.

          Unconventional use:

          Detect Illusion

          This power reasonably can tell the difference between things that take up space and things that do not (even if they visually appear to). Using this, a mage can see through various illusions, unless those illusions are specifically tailored to fool Correspondence senses.


          Know Directions (Imm. Spatial Perceptions)

          As I said earlier, this power can’t reasonably be an application of sensing objects in the local area. It has to be sensing direction/orientation directly. That gives this ability much more potential than it first appears. Cardinal directions are just one of many reference frames. This ability could potentially be used to know the direction “home” or to know the current longitude and latitude coordinates.

          This is a reasonable extension because 1) There’s no better sphere for a sensory effect to tell a mage where he is, and 2) knowing the current location was a rote in previous editions.

          You Are Here

          A mage can know his location in a variety of reference systems. Esoteric reference frames and systems that provide more information may require more successes.


          Know Distance Between Objects (Imm. Spatial Perceptions)

          Ambiguity Resolution - Range

          This is the one power where it matters a lot whether this is an application of sensing objects or not. If it is, it is limited to objects within sensory distance, both because that’s the listed range of that power and it’s the listed range of magic in general. On the other hand, it could be sensing something more abstract, like knowing directions does. That’s why it is so damaging when M20 uses these badly compiled lists, because we can’t use positioning in the sentence to determine what is supposed to happen.

          That said, since we are dealing with objects and since extending range via things like photos is distinctly a Corr 2 power, I would say the objects in question do need to be within sensory range. Given that, this power is quite situational. It becomes much more useful, though, once Corr 2 rolls around and it can be used from a distance. Consider how much use a detective, spy, sniper, or thief could get out of knowing exactly where someone or something is in relation to themselves or something else.

          Unconventional use:

          Follow Your Nose

          When I think of sensory distance I usually think of line of sight, but it should also include other senses. This power could determine the distance to an unexpected sound or smell.


          Detect Spatial Anomalies (Imm. Spatial Perceptions)

          This is explicitly said to be most useful in detecting the effects of other uses of Correspondence. This is not surprising, since there aren’t many naturally occurring spatial anomalies in day to day life. The Old Ways that Verbena can detect with this Sphere rank might be one, or they might be ancient workings of magic.

          Detect Strangeness

          Or are there? An oft overlooked aspect of Mage is that the world itself doesn’t always behave the way we expect. Have you ever had the experience of driving to work and looking up to see you are further along than you reasonably should be. In real life that’s probably just a perceptual trick, but in Mage that could be real. If so, this power would detect what was going on.


          From Elsewhere in M20:


          Know Perfect Spot (???)

          From Thunder Punch it appears a mage can determine the ideal spot to strike. ​

          ​


          Missing Powers: Detect Connection (???)

          Considering how important connections between things are to the use of Correspondence, the Sphere clearly has the ability to determine how strongly two things are connected. This was explicit in previous editions.


          Know the Right Way (???)

          I don’t see this one elsewhere, but if a player wanted a spell to determine which of two (or more) paths led to a destination or was the shortest path to a destination, I’d say that was Corr 1. This would only be in terms of directions, though. Knowing the safest or most reliable path would involve Entropy.

                                      Correspondence 2

                                      •• Sense, Touch, Thicken & Reach Through Space/ Correspondence Sensing
                                      That mage can now extend her senses across intervening space, sensing things in other locations. Such extensions, however, leave minor ripples in space… the kind noticed by Rank 1 Correspondence. Fortunately, she can also thicken space to cover her tracks; each success used in doing so deducts one success from the roll of a character who’s trying to spy on her or notice her sensory Effects.

                                      By adding Life or Matter to Rank 2 Correspondence, the mage can grasp small items or organisms (housecat-sized or smaller) and then pull them through tiny holes in space. This lets her snatch business cards, guns, rabbits, and such from another location, apparently pulling them out of nowhere.
                                      The strangest thing about Correspondence 2 is that the most important ability it has is largely defined somewhere else (the Base Correspondence section). It’s not that it isn’t mentioned at all here, it is just piecemeal and narrowly stated.
                                      I’ll go through what is in Corr 2 before getting to the rest.

                                      Corr 2 can:

                                      Extend Senses (Sense Through Space)

                                      Exactly what it says on the tin. Also known as scrying. Note that this does not limit which senses, so as written it applies to Sphere given senses as well as natural ones. This is consistent with what we see of Corr 2’s powers elsewhere.

                                      In the quest to come up with clever uses for a power I will sometimes deviate towards the esoteric and the sketchy. This ability gets one of each:

                                      Extend Unconventional Senses

                                      There are senses beyond the 5 we usually think of, and I don’t mean ESP. Balance and Proprioception are senses, for instance. I don’t know what precisely extending them beyond their normal range would accomplish, but it is certainly possible with this power.


                                      Other Reference Frames

                                      This is the controversial one, so I put it forward as a potential application, not a certain one. There’s nothing stating that scrying should be limited to the same viewpoint size as the mage. It is possible this power can be used to view the microscopic as well.

                                      You might disagree with me on that. That’s fair. There’s also nothing saying that this can be used for different viewpoint sizes. I would say, though, that this power should be available at some point in Correspondence so I’d welcome suggestions of where else could fit.


                                      Block Scrying (Thicken Space)

                                      A mage can use Thicken space to keep other people from using Correspondence to spy on him. He can also use this to cover his tracks when spying on others. These seem like the same thing but the latter implies a mage can do a bit more.

                                      Cover Up

                                      A mage should be able to cover up detection of any spatial anomaly, not just their own actions. Stumble across a police box with an inside bigger than the outside, Corr 2 can probably keep Corr 1 senses from detecting that. This is a highly situational power based on the nature of the current game.


                                      Conjunctional Effects?:

                                      Pull Things Through Tiny Holes (Reach Through Space)

                                      Teleport small items by pulling it through small holes. Allegedly takes pattern spheres, despite what Base Correspondence says.

                                      Conflict Resolution - A bad description of an effect we already have?

                                      I’ve been searching for a way to consider this a different effect than that described in Base Correspondence, one that would actually use Pattern Spheres, and I have only found one. The mage could be using pattern spheres to disassemble the target, pull it through many tiny holes and reassemble it on the other side. Opening holes in space is not normally a power associated with Corr 2 but I suppose it could be. But even if so, Corr 2 already lets a mage move things around in space directly, so this would be a harder and more Sphere intensive thing of accomplishing something the mage can already do.

                                      All other explanations are just applications of range extension, which doesn’t normally require pattern spheres.


                                      From Elsewhere in M20:

                                      Now, from the writeup in Corr 2, thickening space is the term for inhibiting correspondence senses, but it actually has one more use that is completely neglected here:

                                      Minor Ward (Thicken Space) from Common Magical Effects

                                      HDYDT clearly says that Thicken Space is the basis for the first level of wards, and while I ordinarily ignore that book, M20s common magical effects table also lists Correspondence 2 as the rank Wards start at. HDYDT is not at its best here but Minor Wards basically increase the difficulty acting in certain ways within the ward.

                                      Since Wards are often cast to affect types of things rather than specific things that are already present, there is a good reason to incorporate other spheres to detect. Since Minor Wards were completely left out of this section, though, no guidance is given on how to select those.

                                      My default is that when a spell needs to sense something not present when the spell is cast, it takes one rank higher than the mage directly sensing the thing, so Appropriate Sphere 2. That is my take, though, not a clear ruling from M20.


                                      The biggest oversight, though, is:

                                      Extend Range (Reach Through Space) from Base Correspondence

                                      This is fundamental. It’s used everywhere in M20. It’s such a core part of Correspondence 2 that the Base Correspondence Section specifically calls it out. This is the fundamental basis of casting from a distance.

                                      But it’s not just about casting. This also extends the range of regular actions. If you want to punch someone from a distance, that’s Corr 2. If you want to pick someone’s pocket from across the street, that’s Corr 2. If you want to whisper and be heard by someone across a busy street, that’s Corr 2. If you want your senses to cover an area they ordinarily don't, that's Corr 2. Correspondence 2, it appears, can extend the range of anything.

                                      Indeed, the best explanation of all the manipulation of objects a mage can do at Corr 2 is that he is just extending the range of actions he can normally take at close quarters. He's literally reaching out and moving items as if they were right in front of him.


                                      We Get Signal

                                      Extend Range should also apply to other things, like radio signals or wifi networks. Connecting to wifi as if your computer was in range or listening to radio broadcasts as if your radio was in range should be possible.


                                      I'm Crushing Your Head
                                      ​
                                      Unfortunately, no, Corr 2 can't make the sketch from Kids in the Hall real. The indication is a mage is limited to the actions he can normally perform and he can't size them up or down, hence the limit of housecat size or less. He can reach out and actually squeeze someone's head, though, or do a variety of hostile actions. If he wants to up the effects beyond what he is easily capable of, though, he will need other spheres.

                                                          This is the entry that really cemented the value of this analysis, because Correspondence 3 has one of those lines that looked like a complete contradiction but in an effort to resolve it, I found an interpretation that not only made the line make sense, but was consistent with strange powers other parts of M20 gave this rank.


                                                          Correspondence 3

                                                          ••• Pierce Space/ Open or Close Gates/Co-locality Perceptions
                                                          Now the mage can tear holes in space, large enough for her to step through. These minor gates are small and temporary, but they allow that character to step from one place to another, so long as she’s alone and lightly encumbered. (Teleporting large items, or while carrying heavy loads, demands Correspondence 4.) Scoping out the new location is a good idea, of course. A close, familiar destination requires fewer successes than a distant, unfamiliar one.

                                                          Using the Co-locality Perceptions Effect, the mage can also perceive several places at once. Those locations all appear as ghosts overlapping each other, as if they’d been layered on top of the closest location.

                                                          Also, by combining Correspondence 3 with Forces, Life, or Matter, the mage can move things around from a distance, levitating, manipulating or teleporting them without physical contact.
                                                          Correspondence 3 gves:

                                                          Limited Teleportation (Pierce Space or Open Gates)

                                                          Allows a mage to teleport himself (or others) along with any small items. Larger items explicitly have to wait until Corr 4. These holes or gates also only last long enough for one person or thing to pass through, so gates of significant duration are also not possible at this level. While the first paragraph makes it seem like this is limited to self transportation, the last paragraph makes it clear other things can be teleported.

                                                          Acme Brand Anvils

                                                          Originally posted by Animaniacs
                                                          “And from this bag I can pull out most anything imaginable, like office desks or lava lamps or Burt, who is a cannibal.”

                                                          Animation is famous for characters pulling objects from seemingly nowhere. A mage can do this too. Smaller items would easily fall under Correspondence 2, but large anvils, office desks, and people would use Correspondence 3. If the effect includes physically pulling the object out of nowhere, as animation often implies, the mage will need spheres to augment or replace his strength for heavier targets.


                                                          Co-locality Perceptions (Co-locality Perceptions)

                                                          Perceive multiple places at once. It is not mentioned in this section, but handling multiple points of view at once is difficult without the addition of Mind. At least, I think that's the reason for why existing in multiple places (Corr 4) benefits from Mind 1. Possibly there is another explanation.

                                                          Infinite Zoom

                                                          Similarly to what I said in earlier ranks, it is possible that the scale of perceptions of one location can be different than another. Overlaying local space with the image of something much larger would provide a helpful reference for making models or diagrams. Hollywood sometimes has computer geniuses solve puzzles by mapping an item and overlaying an image of it on some other part of reality, and this is one way that could work.
                                                          Note that even if you reject the idea that one viewpoint can be a different scale than another, much of this effect is still possible through clever use of perspective and parallax.


                                                          Now I need to cover this last paragraph separately.

                                                          Also, by combining Correspondence 3 with Forces, Life, or Matter, the mage can move things around from a distance, levitating, manipulating or teleporting them without physical contact.

                                                          Contradiction Resolution - Physical Contact

                                                          Now in previous threads I pretty much disregarded this as bad editing, because outside of teleportation this seems like a list of spells cast at a distance, which Corr 2 can already do. However, when looking for a reasonable interpretation I realized that “without physical contact” is key, because as far as I can tell Correspondence 2 can’t do that. So, if that is a property of Correspondence 3 and not of the supporting Pattern Spheres, we get:

                                                          Manipulate Position (???)

                                                          Corr 2 was limited to things a mage could do with touch, that is, he could physically manipulate the object with Corr 2 extending his range. With Corr 3 the mage is directly altering the target’s position without touching it. This opens up pure Correspondence effects to include things the mage couldn’t do with his own strength. It also implies that levitation and flight are things that Correspondence can do by manipulating distance/position, which is something that previous editions and the Common Magical Effects chart have suggested.
                                                          As per Base Correspondence rules, Pattern Spheres aren’t needed for Correspondence to manipulate full patterns. Unlike with Corr 2, though, these could be optional but highly recommended locking spheres.


                                                          Other possible meanings could be:

                                                          1 - Corr 2 meant to limit all its range extensions, both physical and spell casting, to house cat size or smaller and going above that requires Corr 3. I can’t recommend this meaning as there’s no indication of a size limit for casting at a distance, most spells don’t have a size, and the spells indicated aren’t obviously above said size limit. This version would arbitrarily curtail many uses of Corr 2, while not actually providing balance as many powerful spells have quite small effects.

                                                          2 - This was supposed to be Corr 2’s cast at a distance section accidentally placed in Corr 3. This means this paragraph is mostly meaningless and we have to discard it and that defeats the point of the thread.


                                                          Missing Powers:

                                                          Collapse Gates (Close Gates)

                                                          Open or Close Gates was listed as a power of Corr 3, but there’s no indication of what Close Gate would mean. Creating lasting gates is a power of Corr 4, but this power should enable a mage to close one already made. This could be used to close (but not destroy) one opened by Corr 4 or block the Limited Teleportation of Corr 3.


                                                          Open or Close Existing Gate (Open and Close Gates)

                                                          Gates aren’t always open. Just because a mage or other powerful being decided to make a portal to some other place doesn’t mean it can always be used. Some are only open at certain times or places, some are always closed unless they are needed. This power would let a mage bypass the normal rules of these gates and open them directly. It would also let a mage close (not end) a permanent gate that is normally open.

                                                          This shouldn’t be limited to gates created by awakened magic. Other ways like Trods or Moon Bridges should be in the realm of possibility, though as they aren’t purely spatial they may reasonably need other spheres too.

                                                          Knock

                                                          Another suspect application. Because of the lack of description, some STs could go so far as letting this work on physical doors or passages. I wouldn’t, but if yours does this is a powerful ability.


                                                          Tether/Forge Connection/Chain (???)

                                                          Somewhere along the line Correspondence should have the ability to forge, strengthen, or weaken connections between things. Revised called it Chain and put it under Corr 3. HDYDT has a related concept, Tether, but is specifically about making something always count as within immediate perceptions.

                                                          I’m placing the ability here because of this line:

                                                          Scoping out the new location is a good idea, of course. A close, familiar destination requires fewer successes than a distant, unfamiliar one.

                                                          That is just barely related to decreasing difficulty by increasing connection. I freely admit that’s an incredibly tenuous connection but it is the closest thing anywhere in Correspondence.

                                                          Wherever you put this power, it is an important power for this sphere and has a profound impact on how mages use Correspondence. I welcome any arguments for another rank for this ability.
