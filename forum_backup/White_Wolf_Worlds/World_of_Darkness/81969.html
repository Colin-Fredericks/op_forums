    On Combat

    03-01-2014, 02:57 AM
    And WoD: Combat. So, we're wrapping up our 18 month Vampire game, and my players want to try their hands at a Hunters game. I'm compiling all my hand-written notes and formatting them into a new Combat book (NOTE: this is neither approved nor endorsed by CCP/WW/OP. This is for my own personal use). I thought I'd get some feedback from you helpful forum people, to make it more, I don't know, universal. Fix and tweak things, you know. I am not including the splat-specific martial arts and the like; this is just a repository for weapons, munitions, that kind of stuff. Plus rules for esoterica (like having to take a turn to pause between firing white phosphorus rounds from your shotgun, lest you melt the barrel). It's in the outline stages currently, but I'll be posting updates when I tinker around with it.

    Thanks in advance for your help.

    cWoD20: Combat!

                  The problem with World of Darkness: Combat is that the maneuvers are often incredibly expensive (2 Willpower points for Neckbreaker -- which isn't even that great of an attack and requires a successful grapple/clinch first? Please), and they're not so good anyway. I've used WoD: Combat in pretty much every game I've run, and I make everybody buy the maneuvers with combat points and so on -- but they just end up doing regular attacks, or not applying their bonuses; but my NPCs do -- and it really doesn't feel decisive. Keep in mind, when there's a penalty, I'd be on top of it and remind people, and if it occurred to me, I'd remind them about their bonuses as well... but knowing a martial art, and doing roundhouse kick or an uppercut, or whatever... it just doesn't really matter in light of the fact that the various games provide super powers which are perfectly effective attacks in and of themselves.

                  I think that if I were to run a game using the cWoD system, but where no one had superpowers (or, at least, very limited superpowers -- like everyone is a ghoul or some kind of not-so-badass sorcerer/hedge wizard) then maybe the combat system would come into play more.

                  What it comes down to is that the martial arts and special moves and such in WoD: Combat should have been more like those particular things in Exalted, rather than White Wolf's frequently forgotten Street Fighter RPG.

                  With regards to weapon stats and the like -- I just don't think there was enough variation between the various weapons. The game would have benefited more from having restrictions on the use of certain weapons. For example, if you have Melee 1 because you avoided and/or perpetrated numerous shankings in prison, you should not be able to pick up a katana and be able to avail yourself of it's full stats. Those stats, if I am not mistaken (this is off the dome) are Str+3 (Damage); Accuracy +1; Initiative +1. That's great and all -- but if your Melee 1 is justified by a passing familiarity with a wielding a sharp piece of glass wrapped in duct tape, or a toothbrush that has had the bristles removed and a razor melted in the head in the bristles place... how can you justify being able to use the katana to its full potential. A game that appreciates this is (I believe), Shadowrun. In that game one's melee/firearms weapon skill (whatever they're called) dictates to what degree a given weapon's stats apply -- e.g., the gun may be 4d6 to hit, but your skill of XX may only allow you to use 2d6; and by that same token, no matter how skillful you are, you can never roll more than 4d6 to hit with that weapon.

                  I apologize for going off on this rant or whatever, but I've always run fairly cinematic games that run a little heavier on combat, and it's bothersome to me that there's was never very much support for that style of gameplay in cWoD (and what little support there was, WoD: Combat, feels incredibly half-assed, and I would guess wasn't play-tested very much).

                  EDIT: Thank you for taking it upon yourself to try to fix and address problems. I hope to have some useful feedback for you regarding same, as overall fixes to the system are badly needed; and from my first, cursory review of the document, it looks like this is going in the correct direction (or, at least in the direction that seems correct to me, in my humble and inexpert opinion).

                                  My biggest recommendation would be to take into consideration the context behind the weapons one might get to use. A lot of games that introduce melee weapons to a modern setting (World of Darkness included) do so in a vacuum. You're given rapiers next to greatswords, for instance, along with stat lines... but no understanding as to why we, as a species, went from big lengths of iron and steel to smaller, thinner, pointier weapons. That would lead into recommendation number two, which would be to base what weapons can and can't do in a game based on what they could and could not do in real life. There are all sorts of fun resources out there that can show us what sort of trauma most popular weapons could inflict. Similarly, there are many resources that can tell you how effective most such weapons were against armor, how they were used to get around such defenses, how damaging parrying other weapons or bashing blades against plate armor could be, etc. The same easily holds true for modern firearms and body armor.

                                              On combat maneuvres: The uppercut is listed as doing lethal damage, with the justification that it is targeted at the head, yet the eye/nose/throat strike does not, though it would seem this is likewise directed at the head.

                                              3.1.4 Accuracy: Keep in mind that lowering difficulty usually has a much larger impact than simply adding a dice to the dicepool, due to the fact it changes the statistics for every dice in the pool. This would mean essentially that weapons/maneuvres with bonuses to accuracy are generally better than those without.

                                              As for the list provided by rderkp, it isn't bad, but there are a few corrections to be made:

                                              Flamberge is a technique/design in blade design. It can be applied to a number of different sword designs, from daggers to zweihanders (the Flambard, as listed). As such, it is a bit of a misnomer to have as types of swords.

                                              The Falchion is nothing like a sabre, and behaves somewhere between an axe and a sword. I would suggest grouping it with the machete.

                                              The long-sword (langes schwert) is technically the name for a fighting style, in which one uses both hands on the sword, i.e using the type of swords commonly referred to as "bastard" or "hand-and-a-half". The use of longsword as a type of sword is purely a RPG phenomenon. Similarly, "heavy cavalry sword" and "infantry sword" are not something I am familiar with. The term most commonly used for a sword intended for use with one hand (other than simply "sword") is arming sword.

                                              Finally, as someone else noted, there is a greater difference between the fencing style of an epee or a foil and the medieval sword, than there is between one handed swords, be they small, curved (use from horseback notwithstanding) or axe-like. This is due to the very different defensive weapons of the times. I'd suggest making a diffrentation there, rather than anywhere else (with the possible exeption of two-handed, since that is quite a different ball game, too).
