                          I really like all of them! Seriously, each of them have really good things going for them in terms of ideals, and the bad things serve to create really interesting complex characters.

                          That being said, I did myself naturally drawn to Verbena since my mother was an actual witch who used to practice herblore and introduced me to crystals and tarot, call it a soft spot if you will.

                          I love the Sons of Ether though, very first character I ever played was an Etherite so I can't help but be interested in how things apply to them when I read.

                          Euthanatos is so much fun, their sense of morality is so understandable yet brilliantly flawed that they become really interesting debate partners, and the subtle danger behind them going rogue keeps you on your toes.

                          Order of Hermes, the most self important of the bunch, I enjoy them for their rigid hierarchy and academic approach to magic, and how on the surface they seem to be most snobby of mages, when if you dig deeper they have one of the most diverse collections of disciplines of all the traditions. Such a wonderful contrast of expectations.

                          Conventions wise, I don't think there's a convention I don't like.
                          NWO are the idealist socialists who drive their agenda via absolute surveillance and control of information. They are big brother and the MiB rolled into one, and while their practices are downright scary, their goal is all too human.

                          Syndicate, these bastards are just brilliant! The conservative capitalists who are more concerned with the status quo than any of the others, and serve as the perfect anchor for the other conventions more ambitious groups. They are ruthless, they are uncaring, and at times they are illegal, and that's what makes them great!

                          Void engineers are probably the most level headed and least hedonistic of all the conventions, and there new militarised version makes them feel more sober than before. They are the most tragic convention and get the least respect and the most suspicion from their colleagues. Also, space marines! What's not to love.

                          I'll admit I haven't read the convention book or the Progenitors or the Iterators just yet, so I can't talk about them much, but I'm already predisposed to liking the Progenitors because I've been studying biomedical science for most of my life, and I see a lot of parallels between the way they organise themselves and the way real left medical science operates here.

                          Keepers of the Wyck: A Chronicle I'm running FINALE: Chapter 39: Green Fairy

                                                          The Solificati! Modern day alchemists and psychonauts with a wee bit more control than the Ecstatics. Outside of the actual faction that joined the OoH and because a House within it, as a Craft/Disparate/Former Tradition, they're OoH-lite, all the ritual, half the snobbery. Their focus on self-perfection through purification and testing also makes them similar to Akashics, but without the self-denial. Sometimes, I get a real Fullmetal vibe from them. They're also down for a good party.

                                                          Otherwise, I love the Dreamspeakers, as they're really the only faction with claims closest to any kind of universal "truth." The spirit world and spirits are a real thing, and we really need to figure out how to deal with them, because a spiritually dead world is a terribly bleak, and possibly actually dead world. I also dig the idea of making deals and bargains with spirits, up to and including allowing themselves to be possessed. I have a concept that might take some house-ruling, but it would be cool to play a shaman that gets ridden by various spirits for various effects. While being ridden, the mage loses access to Sphere magick entirely, but gains bonuses and possibly even Charms depending on the inhabiting spirit. Ferocity and dexterity from wolf spirit, stam, strength, and maybe some additional health/soak from a bear, and possibly burning touch and minor control of flames from a fire spirit. It's possibly game breaking, I know, but if its balanced by the loss of magick while being inhabited as well as some drawbacks (looking more bestial, Willpower penalties, social penalties, altered perceptions, etc), as well as needing to keep the spirit happy through tasks, minor or major, I think it could work. Also, the mage would need that spirit to be present and available, and bottling up a spirit for later use would be awfully rude without making a deal beforehand, and Umbrood are the gossipy type. Word will get around.

                                                          Spirit can be an awfully broken Sphere if allowed to be, but it also comes with all kinds of inconvenient drawbacks, catches, and caveats.
