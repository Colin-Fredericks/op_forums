                                                I doubt any of the Seven were ever friends, or even cooperated. If anything, they're mortal enemies who happen to have become each other's only peers.

                                                Anyway, on the subject of demiurges:

                                                There were less than ten thousand Demiurges to start, which grew into a much larger society once the worlds were opened up to Throne. A lot of the demiurges of Zoss’ time were considered the old guard or elders of the demiurges, who formed its ruling council and the core decision making group of the Concordance (the huge debate hall where they ruled democratically - with Zoss’ oversight of course).

                                                The expansion of the demiurges came with people from outlying worlds or the offspring of those in Throne. They became demiurges through ambition or power, either inherited or seized. These new demiurges were not of the original cadre that broke into Throne, and tended to be slightly weaker in their power and looser in their moral codes. This expansion was so extreme that by the time the second conquest was at it’s zenith and the Universal War was starting, the population of demiurges in Throne was closer to two or three million.

                                                        New Page!

                                                        This (and the last page) does explain a lot of Nadia's worldview, as she's presented it. She always seemed very fatalistic for a reality-warping goddess-queen ruler of a thousand worlds. This helps explain though.

                                                        From a very young age, she became used to the idea that the world held more power over her than she did over it. It was something that couldn't be fought or beaten. Society was something to be bowed to.

                                                        And now, for all her power, despite being a new god and ruling over thousands of worlds, she's not really any more free from society's expectations than she ever was. I only imagine that reinforced her views- no matter how powerful she becomes, there's no way to escape. It's inevitable, so she might as well dissolve into hedonistic splendor.

                                                        I feel like despite this, Nadia is very honest about her role in things. She's, as she said, a coward. I especially remember how she mocked Solomon for thinking that he was still a hero. I feel like she understands exactly how reprehensible she is; just doesn't think it matters anymore.

                                                        But yeah, it's nice to get insight into the antagonist, but that doesn't make them any less terrible.
