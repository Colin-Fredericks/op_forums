                            As I've said previously, my problem with Raksi currently is that while you can interact with her (which is a step up from 2e) you realistically would only do it if you had no other option.                                                         What Raski's portrayal in 3e says to me is that she is a terrifyingly brilliant person who never does anything for one reason if she can do it for five. One of her major goals, at least as her students are concerned, is very rational. "If you are to join our crusade against the Realm, you are going to need to do terrible things to people, with simple murder being the low end. Better that you get over your squeamishness now than to hesitate later and die to a Wyld Hunt." Or, to misquote 70 Maxims, "Two Wrongs is Definitely not going to be Enough."

                                                        The fact that it unsettles her political opponents, makes people worry about what she might do if they oppose her, and allows her to easily take the measure of visitors are also huge benefits. I'm pretty sure she also just straight up enjoys the looks on people's faces.

                                                        And.... Well, my understanding, which may be wrong, is that preindustrial societies tend to have very high infant mortality rates and that some babies might be considered unwanted for various reasons and so might just be left out in the woods. It would feel appropriate to me for Raksi to adopt a "waste not, want not" approach to said unwanted children and, if it makes people think twice about exposing their children, hey that works too.

                                                        As for the seeming contradiction in letting her student have her own way ...well, that's a teachable moment too. First, she was brave enough to stand up to Raksi, which was impressive, so she has some bravery. And second, she also gets to learn that crossing Raksi has consequences, many of which are not immediately obvious.

                                                        And so, after Wenya has spent 12 hours trying to get a crying baby to go to sleep, hasn't slept in days, and hasn't been able to study any occult secrets in weeks, Raksi might come to her and say "Is the life of one mortal worth all this pain? Are you willing to give up on your studies? Or are you going to eat the baby?"

                                                        Or something. Maybe she won't, because she's perfectly fine with just seeing how things turn out (and maybe ending up with a decent Moon Touched lieutenant in a couple of decades).

                                                        Raksi is used to playing the long view and she can probably find a way to turn anything to her advantage eventually.

                                                        Granted, she's not omniscient, and there are significant downsides to having such a fearsome reputation. The situation with her other student might blow up in her face entirely because he's way too scared to actually talk to her, so it's reasonable to wonder just how many "yes men" she has surrounding her and just how much this affects her perception of reality. And ...well, it wouldn't surprise me if she's created some monsters who are even worse than her, people driven to destroy the world. And even if that can be turned to her benefit (pointing them at a target, letting them go, and seeing how many Wyld Hunts they can chew through before they die), I hope she feels some degree of remorse when it happens.


                                                        Of course, none of the above makes her any less of a monster. One could argue that it makes her significantly more monstrous that she's rationally planning and carrying out terrible things than if she were a simple beast, lashing out at all who oppose her. But, like people have been saying, having her be a cross between Hermione Granger, Vito Corleone, David Xanatos, and a mythological Rakshasa is much more interesting than having her simply be the Thugee leader from the second Indiana Jones movie or the monster from a slasher film.
