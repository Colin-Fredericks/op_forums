                    Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
                    Yeah, that's sadly one of the reasons why the Solar Exalted have to be the Corebook splat instead of the second one.

                    Because if the Solars were anyone but the first Exalted out of the gate, they would seem INCREDIBLY cheap. Player resentment for that, and player sentimentality for the Dragon-Blooded as the first and most defining Exalted of the game, would basically doom any effort to persuade people to see Solars as anything but Anathema.
                    Sunder, this is maybe the smartest thing you've ever written and you should be proud of putting your finger directly on something important in a very concise way.

                    One quibble:

                    But if it weren't for those factors, the Dragon-Blooded absolutely should have been the first Exalted of the game, in every Edition.
                    I would say there's another factor; Solars have a lot less baggage for your aspiring hero type or from a player coming in from a system that's a lot less focused on society and history than any other Exalt type.

                    The Dragon-Blooded have basically ruled the world for like 1500 years, have a polity that has a colorable argument for "greatest empire the world has ever known" at their back, and there are enormous societal expectations and the baggage that come with them undergirding them. Sure, you can play an Outcaste who doesn't live in the Realm... which means chunking 9/10ths of your available system resources.

                    Lunars and Sidereals and Abyssals have their own societies and baggage. Sidereals HELLA have that; they focus heavily on finding you and inculcating you hardcore into your role in their ranks. Being a ronin is and always has been... difficult. Lunars are more capable of going it alone but the Silver Pact is still there and other Lunars will have certain expectations regarding how you treat them and how you interact with the Forever War going on between them and the Realm. Abyssals... well. I mean, you literally can't create an Abyssal without a faustian bargain.

                    Solars absolutely don't have that. There's a very clean, hard break between the time when they had a society around them and the time the game is set in. The genocide and their imprisonment was near-complete, and they return in a world as blank slates, without Elders to tell them what is expected of them and to inculcate them into a shared society with shared beliefs. They come from everywhere and can do anything, and the only real baggage they have is "the Evil Empire wants you to die."

                    (It's a little like playing a Jedi.)

                    This is a good way to give players a huge array of options. You can play the classic lone hero, or group of mismatched heroes, who begin the game drinking in a tavern somewhere without having to worry about this giant pack of peers who might judge you and try and fit you into boxes and circumscribe your life. (They might try and murder you, of course.)

                    I feel like this is very important in a starting splat.

                                                        Okay I'm back at the computer time for another write up. I'm planning to do another three sections. One which includes the Immaculate styles and a deeper dive into exalted's power dynamics and combat structure. It's gonna get a little mathy but I promise to keep it at a minimum. Then I'm going to do social charms, comparing DBs to Solars, and then other non-combat stuff like craft/athletics and do the same.

                                                        Ready? Okay here we go.

                                                        Originally posted by MoroseMorgan View Post
                                                        What do you think of the DB's chances with the Immaculate Styles? I'm only a few charms in but they seem stronk™
                                                        First of all, what are the Immaculate Styles doing to help? Well a lot actually. For one we have a good damage adder, Perfect Blazing Blow, doubling 8s up to (Essence) times basically adds (Essence) auto successes to the damage roll. Sweet. We've also got a few charms for soak and some resistant evasion, nice! These styles are powerful. Unfortunately, they sacrifice some stuff for that power. Most importantly, armor. Air dragon can only wear light, Fire Dragon, medium, ect. That's kind of a mark against them, and unfortunately it's more bad news with none of the styles doing much more than the attack/defense charms from the main set to stop attacks.

                                                        Why are Solars So Dangerous?

                                                        Okay so why is this such a big deal? Well to understand that we first need to understand why Solar exalted are SO deadly. Once we have a good sense of why that is, we can see why so much hinges on attacks, particularly decisive ones.

                                                        The first reason that they're so deadly is the intentional structure of the Solar charmset. They have a LOT of charms which are supplemental and/or reflexive, that means they can combo together complementary effects for a massive boost that's greater than the sum of it's parts. Lets take a look at a few, shall we? What follows is a big somewhat intricate Solar combo.


                                                        Take, for example Thunderbolt Attack Prana. An athletics charm actually! Which costs 4m 1wp, supplemental, can be comboed with any other ability, allows the user to jump a range band in any direction and DOUBLES damage after it is rolled. This alone means that a Solar can be deadly at as little as 8 initiative.

                                                        Now lets see what we can combo this with. I like the look of Invincible Fury of the Dawn there. For 5m 1wp that lets me make up to 5 attacks, distributed how I please, move a range band between each attack, and instantly gain initiative equal to the number of attacks I want to make. Also, apart from the excellency, and charm I use on one attack can be used for free on the others. It can also be activated reflexively after using Peony Blossom Technique.

                                                        So since all the stuff is free past the first I might as well load up, Excellent Strike (3m) rerolls 1s until they fail to appear, neutralizing a common DB trick to boost their defense in the process, and Hungry Tiger (3m 2i), which lets our overflow attack successes add to the damage.

                                                        Plus, of course, Rising Sun Slash, which activates on a straight 7,8,9,10 being rolled and gives me one bonus non-charm sux and (essence) non-charm dice on the attack roll.

                                                        OKAY so we're ready to make our attack. With as little as 10 initiative, lets see what we can do. We'll make 5 attacks, instantly giving us 5 initiative. That's 5 attacks with 3 base damage, then we attack. First we use Perfect Strike Discipline to give our first attack a full free excellency, between that and a level 2 stunt we're basically rocking 25 dice to attack, plus rerolling 1s, plus a big non-charm bonus with Rising Sun Slash, which is surprisingly common at those dice levels. Against a typical DB Defense of, say, 10, they've got no hope. Simplified calculations give us around 16 successes. That overflows by 6 so we get to deal 9 damage, which is then doubled thanks to TAP, giving us around 7.2 health levels, which is lethal against somebody without ox-bodies. If we drop our first target, great, if not, hit them again. When they drop we then reset Perfect Strike Discipline, for another free full excellency, and can jump a range band if we have to in order to strike our next target, and so on.

                                                        If we have just one target the onslaught penalty keeps racking up so that's good too.

                                                        And if we have a charm called Foe-Cleaving Focus we actually regenerate a mote for every 10 on an attack that triggers Rising Sun Slash. Which is good because that took like, at least 20-25 motes and 3-4 willpower.


                                                        People are going to point out that wearing artifact full plate would use hardness to defend decently against that, but this was just an example to show how Solar charms work, I don't want to proc a mechanics arms race to infinite xp.


                                                        Phew! That was a lot of stuff! You might have noticed that we used up a lot of motes and a ton of willpower, you may also have noticed that we killed everyone in the process. All of that up there? That can be activated after striking with One Weapon Two Blows, and then Peony Blossom Attack, which SHOULD result in more than 10 initiative. That's just one of many many combos out there.

                                                        This is sort of the nature of The Beast, who's just in it to go full throttle on power. If he can reliably hit you, then he just runs roughshod all over you, just like that. So how reliably can he hit you? If you're a DB wielding an artifact medium weapon your max defense is around 10, his max withering with a light artifact weapon is 26 dice, 21 for decisive, and he's got dice tricks like Excellent Strike and Rising Sun Slash to boost that even further and negate your defenses that use his 1s against him. So yeah he's hitting you.

                                                        Conversely as a DB your highest is 22 withering, 17 decisive, and his parry is 12, his dodge can be even higher. That's why accuracy and defense are so important, because the Solar has an insane ability to generate damage. They likewise have an insane ability to absorb it but I'll be here all day if I go into that.


                                                        This is actually a design feature! Not a bug. Part of the fun of Solars is coming up with those insane combos, or building them as your character grows, and unleashing them. Now let's look at DBs. Well, in contrast so stark we should call it Iron Man a ton of their charms are Simple! They don't combo together at all, they're all just discreet individual tricks and powers that do really cool one-off things. It makes for some fun charm trees, but also kills their ability to mix a ton of stuff together and explode their power like a Solar can. Incidentally, this might answer your question @Apromor.

                                                        Outnumbering Solars

                                                        Originally posted by Apromor View Post
                                                        An issue that I didn't see discussed in this thread is that mote regeneration for DB's is the same as for Solars. If the solars are outnumbered how many times can they use their full excellences before they're down to 5 motes each round? When the essence supplies are low won't 2 dragonblooded make short work of one solar of equal experience? With numbers, won't limited essence respiration typically push a fight in favor of several dragonblooded winning over few solars, despite their better charms and higher die pools?
                                                        Solars simply have the power to end fights early enough that respiration doesn't always matter, at least if there's high essence or a Dawn involved. What if there isn't a Dawn though? And the small circle is like essence 1-2? Then you're absolutely right! A larger group of Dragonblooded will wear down those 1 or 2 Solars. If those terrestrials are unleashing their full power on every attack and you're defending the same, you're going to run dry fast. Particularly because your full excellency is 170% as expensive as the DB one, and doesn't even have any special benefits added.

                                                        That's kind of why the DB charmset doesn't quite work to counter a true combat monster Solar. THAT BEING SAID, it's not hopeless, it's just hard. You need to rely on luck, you might only have a 40% chance of hitting him and he's got a 65% chance of hitting you, but he could easily roll poorly on his join battle, and then you and your Hearth roll well, work together, get a few more lucky rolls to hit him and crash him right off the bat. It can happen, and if it does then you stand a good chance of winning! Which is a huge difference between this edition and the last. If you get lucky, or somehow bend the environment to help you, then you can beat even those odds. It's just that you don't have a similarly devastating combo.

                                                        There's another upside too, and it also stems from Solars being so combo-happy. If they don't have bits of the combo, their power drops off drastically. That's why the Well Rounded Dawn is so much easier than The Beast. He's not taken TAP and all the Rising Sun Slash upgrades, which tanks that whole combo a lot up there.

                                                        Immaculate Martial Arts

                                                        Anyway where was I? Oh yeah Immaculate Martial arts. You know, there's one kind of opponent who's not exalted, but is still really powerful. Spirits. Higher essence gods, elementals and demons can challenge even Solar exalted in my experience, so how are Dragonblooded supposed to beat them? Flippin Immaculate Martial Arts that's how! They're powerful in exactly the kind of ways that Solars are good at defending against, and spirits are not. A circle of Dragonblooded immaculates using these charms on basically any god or demon would be painful to watch. Increasing wound penalties, onslaught penalties, and then crushing them with iconic finishing moves. This is a "vs Solars" thread, but I thought I should just mention that they're still great

                                                        Against a less than optimal Solar, they can actually work. As soon as you start ranking up those penalties you basically add a dipping swallow tax to every attack that you make, that's good. The other effects if you can land them are even better, like Hungry Earth. In the event that you get that off the Solar can't retreat or use any shenanigans to hit and run, which is great. So I guess the conclusion is that if you want to fight Solars, bring Immaculates anyway, but the odds are still not on your side without numbers and environments.

                                                        Aside: Did you know that an Air Dragon master can sit in an open field, using Avenging Wind Strike, chakrams and Lightning Strike Style to fling opponents away and strike them at distance before they even get close enough to swing at her? That's so cool.


                                                        I'll end this post here but start a new one comparing Social charms. Sorry if it was a bit rambly.

They do exist, of course, there are veteran shikari who have stood in against Lunars and Fair Folk and terrible demons and other monstrosities, but most Dragon-Blooded won't have that experience. Going up against guys who just fucking break the rules in the way Solars do is going to be a new, terrifying, and potentially short-lived experience for many of them.

                                                            (I can't speak for other GMs but I always try and bring a pinch of "what sort of CHARACTER decisions would this guy make in a fight?" to my fights. A hard-bitten professional Dragon-Blooded officer in the Legions who has had the best training available and understands how his essence relates to his vocation inside and out and has seen some crazy shit is, in my opinion, far more likely to make 'mechanically optimal' choices than a Dragon-Blooded who has spent the last hundred years being the hardest, baddest, most awesome motherfucker in his postage-stamp sized personal kingdom in the back of beyond, even if they both technically have made the same Charm picks. The former would treat a Solar much differently and perhaps more cautiously than the latter, who might be used to solving problems by simply committing in hard and trusting that when he comes out the other side, he'll still be standing and his opponent will not. Because fuck, that's always worked on everyone else in Creation, right?)
