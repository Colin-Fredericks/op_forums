        Originally posted by Epitome View Post
        I meant that ganging up turns any combatants into super soldiers, not just a Circle of Solars. For instance, a Solar who can defeat Octavian can still lose to 5-6 mortal soldiers so long as they are individual combatants rather than a size 1 battlegroup.
        Well, and I'm not one to try and promote Solars going balls crazy optimized, but Bulwark Stance and Fivefold Bulwark Stance, especially with Hail Shattering Practice, can make that kind of thing 100% possible. Absent those charms, or Circle of Bright Reaving or other ones like them, it is hard, but supposedly 5-6 mortals who aren't grouped into a battlegroup are 5-6 of some of the greatest mortal heroes ever.

        So it's not just 5-6 random guys, they'd be turned into a size 1 battlegroup. It's like Azal Aresh, legendary demonhunter with arrowheads made from the teeth of slain blood apes, Serafina Prinus grandmaster of five animal styles of martial arts, Xian Miu of the Green Blade who weaves a deadly tapestry through the battlefield with his poisoned blade, Vareesh Liang deadly master of kunai who can peg the chink in a suit of fullplate from twenty yards, One River who's strength is so great they can lift and throw a fully grown warhorse, and Pupup the nimble forest hermit who shocked the entire city by being able to loose her third arrow into the bullseye before the first one struck home.

        If your Solar is facing all of them, alone, without Bulwark Stance that's going to be a rough time, but I do feel like it should be. Not every mortal needs to matter individually when compared to the mightiest of exalted, there needs to be room in the game to tear though a room full of mooks, but at a certain point great enough heroes should be able to put up a fight.

        Originally posted by Epitome View Post
        For AoE, I'm thinking of area attacks that grant significant initiative from each target hit. I was sorta traumatized by a certain wolf emanation in Tomb of Dreams, who became ultra-deadly because we had 7 combatants and his Combat Charm makes a Withering against all targets.
        Oh yeah, that's a pretty special exception. I'm actually not quite sure why it's worded the way it is, because I think every other withering AoE in the game at best takes the highest initiative from the attack and gains one additional initiative for each target hit.

        Originally posted by Epitome View Post
        The rule you mention is fairly clear cut to me, I'm thinking more of rules like how the ST has to adjudicate when Withering Attacks no longer award initiative, or decide when Withering 1 enemy and Decisive-ing another is considered cheating.
        I think for the former it's a bit hard but mostly a kind of "Oh crap." strategy for when you accidentally put a character into combat who really shouldn't have been there, and is clearly just going to be giving the PCs initiative way way too easily. Like you accidentally added in a single zombie into the combat or something, who should only ever be a battlegroup or a trivial opponent. Or possibly when an opponent is in their -4s with a ton of effects stacked on them to make them impossible to miss.

        For the later case I'd be pretty lenient, although with the caveat that designing encounters for Solars is really hard and you sometimes need to get creative with how badass the enemies are and their tactics. As long as there's not a mega wide divide between combatants, like there's a tough guy and a few less tough ones and maybe a battlegroup it's okay, just as long as there's not two deadly Dusk caste Abyssals and one single mortal in rags that you can just annihilate for 60 initiative before turning it on the Exalted foes.

        If it's just the two legendary Dusk castes though, and maybe a huge battlegroup of zombies, then crashing one Abyssal to launch a decisive at the other is dangerous enough that it's worth the risk.
        Originally posted by Epitome View Post
        For sure a commander can make a blood ape battle group deadlier than an individual blood-ape, but without a commander 1 Exalt vs 20 blood apes is comparable in difficulty to 1 Exalt vs 1 blood ape given the lone blood ape gets to use their Charms. The battle group actually becomes strictly weaker if using the RAW rules that battle groups cannot damage you with the same attack that crashed you.
        Well, not STRICTLY weaker exactly. The battlegroup doesn't lose stats and gain nothing, although I'd argue that it's at least close. A regular ape has 9 health levels and starts getting dice penalties as they're taken away, also they can be crashed and give their enemy a huge initiative bonus, a battlegroup has 21 health levels and is pretty much 100% combat effective right down to the last one.

        Either way, I've been looking around trying to find the dev quote that says Might is there to represent charms, and can't, what I DID find was a ton of player and Storyteller advice saying to just let them have charms, or that it was obvious they're supposed to have charms. For one, hilariously, a battlegroup of Blood Apes that can't use charms can't materialize, so unless they're already material when the fight starts they're completely useless. I think this might be an orichalcum rule thing if nothing else. If you feel like Might does not properly represent charms for battlegroups of supernatural enemies, which I agree it doesn't, then allow them to use their charms too, which I recommend anyway.

        Which to me makes a whole hell of a lot of sense. A battlegroup that has bonuses from Size, Might, and Drill and using Principle of Motion still isn't remotely as strong as a legit straight 20 heroic Blood Apes in combat, so why not? The only time I'd urge caution on this is maybe, maybe, for a group of 5 Dragonblooded or something. If you let a bunch of Elite Drill, Might 3 DBs use Dragonblooded charms that might get out of hand. Then again the book expressly says Dragonblooded are almost never battlegrouped, so that shouldn't be a huge issue.

                                Yeah, although that level can change depending on the exact setting and combatant numbers. There's a level where foes are so weak that they're not rolled in no matter how many there are. An entire 500 man counting-house of scribes and clerks just isn't going to be rolled into combat between your Dawn and the Essence 5 Ragara Master of Coin. They might even be so thick in the room as to count as difficult terrain, and they're STILL not getting an initiative track.

                                Then past that there's guys like joe realm soldier or guard. Those guys are not being rolled into combat as single people, or even in groups of 2-3. If a fight breaks out and one side has a pair of shlubs in helmets as guards, they're not getting rolled in, they're just scenery. In universe they technically have a chance of contributing, but it's so small and their narrative presence is so light that they're not counted. They only get rolled in if you've got 8-10 of them, at that point there's just too many trained swords, they cross the threshold from being scenery to being at least somewhat dangerous. If they're elite guards against one single foe, you might roll in 2 of them as individuals beside the DB, but they'd have to be strong enough that their presence is a threat, not a boon to the Solar.

                                Also typically battlegroups represent relatively homogeneous groups working together. They're forming a battle line, or surrounding a fugitive together, that kind of thing. Then you have a class of hero that you would not field in a battlegroup. They're just too unique and strong. They're the kind's of mortal hero that may include bringing down an Exalt in their legend someday. If they don't meet that criteria, they're either a battlegroups or background.



                                If you're having some trouble with that from the ST side, don't be afraid to get a little epic with the antagonists in the game either. The Scarlet Empress's 'mortal' bodyguards are giant elite warriors with disturbingly fast and effective sign language, iron will, superhuman strength, and extra powers granted to them by alchemy and sorcery. So you'd think with that awesomeness she'd have like 100, maybe 150? Nope, 5,000 of them.

                                With your PCs, if they're going after a really big important antagonist, there should be at least one fight battled to conclusion before they even see the foe, maybe as many as two or three, and then when they reach them it should be the big bad, maybe one or two really impressive allies, and then a big elite battlegroup of some kind. There aren't many foes in the world of Exalted that you can't justify that for.
