                                        So, I said I'd talk a bit about my game.

                                        It's a starting game of Lunars-only, they've had 5 sessions now so they're at 25xp.

                                        The characters are:
                                        Olaf Keelbreaker, walrus-totem viking with a huge axe made of his own tusk. (FM) All combat charms all the time.
                                        Amira, the spider at the centre of the court, loved by all. (CM) Mostly appearance charms.
                                        Atal, toad-totem genius and socially awkward, arrogant swamp shaman who just wants to make friends; but is awful at it. (NM) Medicine and shamanism charms.
                                        Underfoot, lost in the jungle as a baby and rose to become king of the apes. (Casteless) Some physical charms, intimidation, wits charms for navigation and hearthstones. And flaming fists. Has a bear-god mentor.
                                        Jin, polecat-totem merchant and grifter. (Casteless) Mostly manipulation charms. Has a (huge) horse and cart.

                                        Until last session, there was also a mysterious red panda who just kind of hung around with the PCs. They all thought he was Olaf's familiar. Eventually he started talking, so maybe he was a Lunar. But last session he was kicked to death by enemy spearmen, so no way to tell now.

                                        About a week and a half later, the PCs were joined by Krove the Tiger-Bull, a Full Moon and half-tigerman, half-bullman beastman. He hasn't revealed his totem to them yet.
                                        He's all about the Strength charms; wrestling and feats of strength. I think. I've only seen an early draft of the sheet.

                                        So there's quite a mix now really. Two physical characters, two social characters (one more a courtier, the other more underhanded), a doctor/shaman/lore guy, and an all-rounder.




                                        Before I go into what I've learned about Lunars, a quick summary of the game:

                                        The heroes met because they were all invited to a Moot by King Arcturus, a Changing Moon who rules five clans of humans and beastmen in the far north-east. (If you look on the map, it's the north-east-most peninsula of Mela's Fangs, about 750 miles north of Iscomay.) His realm is suddenly having a lot of problems (Jin thinks someone's trying to make him look bad), so as well as having a Moot because it's nice to have a party to meet other Lunars, he hoped to get some Lunars to help him. They also met a couple of other Lunars there, Tabun (a stag-shaman) and True Voice (unsuccessful bear-messiah of Iscomay), and a lot of courtiers and local terrestrial gods (two river gods, a volcano god, the god of the bay, a mountain god, etc).

                                        Arcturus gave them a longship, and they went to investigate some disappearances in the western-most villages of Arcturus's realm, and it looked like the Witches of Xor, a coven of ancient Raksha hags with an army of fae knights and goblin berzerkers, had kidnapped the villagers as slaves. But from clues and visiting the Witches' citadel, they discovered it was a false flag operation. The real culprits were a band of mercenary humans and mutants, the World Eaters (I didn't name them, Atal's player did with Introduce a Fact), who'd killed Olaf Keelbreaker's family 12 years before.
                                        The Lunars tracked down their base, and fought over 100 ashigaru (some in dirty red, some in white-and-blue, which some of you may know as the colours of the World Eaters) led by two Dragonblood samurai, who called the Lunars demons. Many of the soldiers seemed to come from the far south (not the South, just south for people who live in the far north, like maybe Cherak or Medo or Miklegard) and spoke some foreign tongue. In the fight, Underfoot's arm was severed at the elbow, and the Red Panda was killed.

                                        However, the other 400, led by the 7-foot ice-sorcerer Jadis (she likes giving sweets to kids, and travels in a giant sleigh) had gone somewhere else (possibly to invade the Haslanti League), so the heroes returned the villagers to their village and are now going back to Caer Moroth, Arcturus' castle, to report.



                                        Oh, and as far as the local Lunars are concerned, Solars are extinct. I've never said what year it is in RY (the locals call it the 20th year of King Arcturus), no Solars have been seen or mentioned. None of the PCs have taken Solar mates as allies.
                                        We've been playing Solars for years, and so for now I'm taking the opportunity to completely sideline them and concentrate on Lunars and Dragonblood.

                                            So, having run an all-Lunars game a bit, these are my thoughts so far:

                                            Lunars vs Solars
                                            So I found the Lunars way weaker than Solars; they had real trouble with 2 DBs and a battle group. I was surprised by, say, their lack of excellencies. But this probably isn't really about Lunars vs Solars, it's probably just be because it's an Essence 1 game, and the last game I ran was Essence 5. I guess a starting game of Solars would also frequently find that people have bad pools and no excellencies in, say, Join Battle or Perception+Awareness or whatever.
                                            Saying this, being Lunars, no one's terrible at very much. I mean, there was only one dedicated combat character in the group, but most of them did decently even with few if any combat charms.

                                            Shapeshifting
                                            Gives the players a lot of flexibility. Combat shapeshifting seems best either for people built for a specific build, a way to save motes by getting free excellencies, or to give some combat ability to characters who aren't very good at combat.
                                            Our No Moon for example, found it quite good to turn into a huge snake, which made him a reasonable combatant rather than a rubbish one.

                                            But for the Full Moons, unless you specifically build around something (like a gorilla's intimidation), it generally seems better to be in human (or DBT) form, for the higher accuracy. Though it depends on a lot of things.

                                            I also found that some players struggle with the way animal forms and dice caps interact. Hopefully people will get the hang of it. I guess as people's own dice pools get bigger, it matters less (my Essence 5 Lunar just uses her own dice pools when she's a snake, she just takes advantage of its Camouflage and poison bite), and hopefully they'll get the hang of it.

                                            Travelling
                                            Connected to shapeshifting of course.
                                            Lunars have a lot more options for travelling... but only personal travel. Actually, my PCs have ended up travelling by ship or by horse-drawn cart for most of their journeys, because while they can mostly turn into animals or birds, their companions, familiars, the people they've rescued, etc, can't. This sometimes causes issues where the players with followers want to go slowly, and the ones without want to go quickly as birds or whatever.


                                            Plot
                                            So one big difference I guess with Lunars and Solars is that you can actually give the PCs some kind of support network, mentor, etc, without it feeling a bit forced. And they can be invited to parties with other Lunars, and have sensible reasons to get together without it feeling forced.
                                            I guess that's kind of halfway between Solars (basically no instutional support) and Dragonblood (generally very, very enmeshed in their Dragonblood society).


                                            Charms
                                            Most of the combat charms seen straightforward and there are some fun effects. A bit more dice-tricky-fiddly than I'd like (Ferocious Biting Tooth, I'm looking at you), but not too bad. I like the various charms that let players just replace a bad dice pool with a good one (for them).
                                            I always appreciate Permanent charms as they're easy to keep track of.
                                            (Though that's much less of an issue than in my previous game, because people only have like 17-21 charms, instead of over 40).

                                            The social charms are quite orientated towards certain things though. Trickery, seduction, intimidation and doing stuff with people's culture. In these areas Lunars excel, but I think they're a bit weaker outside that. That's kind of interesting, but whether it works for you depends a lot on whether that's what you want to do with your character.

                                            The mental charms seem to be about 75% witchy shamanism and transformation, which works great, and 25% a random grab-bag of effects. Some of which are pretty fun, though how iconically Lunar they are varies. It works very well for the No Moon. For the Casteless ape-totem guy who favours Wits, it's... odd. He's really good at leading people through the wilderness, but also has a hearthstone socketed in his chest that gives him fiery fists? Just a bit weird.
                                            But, you know, you want to give people some capacity for weirdness and not just fitting into the stereotype of a splat, I guess.


                                            Lastly, I'll say that there's a lot of good workhorse charms at Essence 1, as well as some fun effects, but there's a few charms that I've found to be a bit weak and not very useful; but they're at the bottom of trees which get much better from Essence 2.
