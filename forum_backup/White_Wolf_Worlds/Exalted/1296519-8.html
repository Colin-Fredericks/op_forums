It's very similar to Lilith's looking for her ancient love, so I would argue that at the least there is still the idea that he's supposed to be as important as Lilith, but Lilith wasn't also in the comic and also in the core book; so he's clearly, to me, meant to be more important then Lilith.

                                        Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

                                        All right, so this is begging two questions; why does Rakan need to micromanage the Getimians, and why does the game need to be all about him?

                                        Or at least, any more than a game including a Dynast or Lunar adherent needs to draw in their elders, and how important do those need to be?
                                        I don't think Rakan Thulio has to micromange, but I do know that it was said before that a big part of a Getimian's story will be 'escaping' from Thulio- or it was before-, which is why he's important; in terming it as 'escaping' or similar, there are apparently deliberate similarities invoked to Renegade Abyssals and Renegade Infernals; escaping from a massive power. While Dynasts escaping from their parents to rebel against the Realm may be similar, I feel that the scale is different in what they can accomplish. It may not be actively different, and may have more similarities then differences, but this sort of rebellious break from society seems easier for a Dragonblooded, who, while maybe not liked in all corners of the world, can generally find a place that will at least accept them. A Lunar never has to 'escape' their teacher. A Dragonblooded might have to escape their family, but the implication also seems to be if the Dragonblooded is rebellious or otherwise a troublemaker to a point that they can't be fixed, they're generally going to be put aside rather then pressured into a mold. A Dragonblooded is presented as a hero who can do great things, even if their family is problematic or a struggle.

                                        He is not mentioned in Arms or Fire, but he has been in the first piece of new 3E Media, and then in both the Core and the newest Splat Book.

                                        I think when you sell a character as hard as Rakan Thulio was sold, the game warps around them. By a reversial of the question; why do games featuring Infernals need to almost always end up focusing on the Yozis? Why were the Deathlords so important in 2e? And the answer is because the narrative weight assigned to those individuals far outstrips other antagonists. By talking about something, it becomes important, and as something is talked about more, it becomes more important.


                                        Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                        Gonna break this down over multiple posts as best I can.

                                        Do you not think the Lunar elders give a good impression about how much of a challenge a single old Exalt is meant to be.

                                        For that matter, when are we not told that somebody with a name in the setting is a big deal?
                                        This is an interesting question, and I think one way to talk about it is to point out the Lunar book.

                                        How often do they talk about First Age Elders, how often do they speak of what they do? We have Lilith, Ma-Ha-Suchi, Leviathan, and Raksi. But even the text often says that First Age Lunars are working on plans far beyond the short span of years, that they're embroiled in long schemes and thoughts, they talk of these handful of people who are so busy scheming and thinking they often seem to do little- though they are working on their own plots-, and are more concerned with those plots then helping the pact. They take students, but they are not easy to rouse from their plans to HELP you.

                                        Then they speak of none of the Lunars of the Divine Revolution being alive.

                                        But, Rakan Thulio is alive. And he is one of the first Exalts, with all the power that entails, and he is not passive, he is active. He is not withdrawn from support and working on distant plans, he has 100 (or so?) Exalts specifically working towards his own goals, which are progressing and which he is working.

                                        There may be ancient Lunars, but the way Rakan Thulio is presented is utterly different, not just on the timescale, but also on their focus. While Ma-Ha-Suchi wonders if this has been worth all of this suffering, and other ancients plot out their shadow wars against the Bronze Faction or any other number of things, Rakan Thulio by himself and with his Exalts fights the Bronze Faction in an active thing, something called a War, even. His presence is not the ancient thinker who hibernates and plays at games thousands of years in the making, but someone who is here, and he is here to upend the world, like he has been the entire time. The presentation is different.
