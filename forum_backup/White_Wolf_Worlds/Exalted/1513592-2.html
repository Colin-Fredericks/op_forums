            Originally posted by TryingToBeSlim View Post
            I agree with most of what you said with a few caveats.

            1) Abyssals (at least in previous editions) were those who accepted a bargain from the NB or Death Lords. While I might not say being an Abyssal is something shameful and wrong, bargaining with the NB would and should be in my view.
            Considering that most would-be Abyssals would likely at the moment of taking the offer, understand nothing more from it than they are offered a chance to live again and accomplish great things... and yes, there will be a price, probably a serious one, but one that might seem very nebulous to them? With the alternative being death? Sure, taking that offer is not exactly a heroic deed in itself, but automatically shameful and wrong and immoral? I'm not so sure about it. Solars can turn into much greater monsters, and yet the only potential source of shame would be their actual actions, not what they are. Unless you are an Immaculate Order believer, of course.

            Originally posted by TryingToBeSlim View Post
            2) I agree that "redeeming" into becoming a Solar should NOT be the only moral choice (for either Infernals or Abyssals).

            3) That being said, it should not be that easy to create/find/follow more moral paths.
            That's actually true for all exalted. Great power can easily push people into ditching morality for convenience, and the Creation as it stands at this point in time does not promote moral behaviour either. It's hard to be clean in a world that is not. Especially when there's no clear path for you to follow. That's not however what i meant. Creation is (quite intentionally) presented as place without black-and-white morality. Except for Abyssals, it seems, where being one was presented as objectively immoral, with going for Redemption or offing yourself offered as the only right and moral options anyone can take. That is what i was objecting to.

            Originally posted by TryingToBeSlim View Post
            Because otherwise the NB and Yozi's come across as morons. Both factions had a purpose as to why they created their exalts and want to see that purpose fulfilled. Neither are that concerned what the Exalt/Mortal might think/want.
            NBs are already dead, with only tangential grip on reality. So, expecting them to not have full idea of what they are doing - or lack full control over it - doesn't seem unreasonable to me. Not to mention it's been stated more than once that even the original creators of Exaltations were never in full control over the result - and Neverborn (and Yozi) are not really creators of Abyssal Exaltations. They only modified something that already existed. Also, look at 2e Infernals - it was laughably easy for them to flick a middle finger to Yozis if they only wanted to, with barely any consequences attached to it.

            Originally posted by TryingToBeSlim View Post
            I am not against Abyssal or Infernals having "moral" options. I just don't want it to come across as if those moral options are the DEFAULT. "Moral" Abyssals / Infernals should be the exception (or PC) and no where near the majority or even a large minority. Problem is with their initial numbers? Even a few examples can become too many. (Note: In all honesty this is partly why I plan on increasing the number of exalts in my setting, 10 Moral Abyssals out of 50 is too many. 10 out a hundred or even two hundred? That's better. Similar things for other splats. )
            Again, you completely misunderstood me. I do not want more moral options for Abyssals. I want something exactly opposite - i want the morality to remain a completely personal issue, and not be so massively tied to the splat as a whole. I am okay with seeing an approach "You are an Abyssal, given powers to do Bad Stuff, so you will likely end up doing a lot of Bad Stuff and have an increased chance of ending as a Bad Guy", but would not want to see "You are Abyssal, so you are Bad and should feel bad about it"

                I feel as though Second Edition got to a point where the primary reason to want to become a Solar was because of how Resonance worked. It wasn't that Abyssals had a difficult time doing something other than killing the world because of strictures in their powers or some common inclination in their character, it was that trying to do so would cause them to build up a pressure that would periodically explode through things that the Abyssal probably wouldn't want so cursed.

                Third Edition Abyssals won't have Resonance, and I gather that the Chivalry of Death will have a bit more nuance.

                To say "it should be hard to create a moral path" raises the question of what is immoral in the path that is assumed to be most common to them. Some of those answers should be measured against other Exalted; there are plenty of Dragon-Blooded and Lunar conquerors (and the latter includes many who aren't killing for conquest but are still killing), and Sidereals enforce a system whose decisions on whether or not people die is informed somewhere between obscure esoteric and the self-interest of venal and corrupt gods.

                And some of those answers should be measured against the fact that the dead are people too, and can get something out of having Givers of their own Law to be champions for them.
