    Heavy Arms : Just double-checked Beuracracy. In all honesty I do wish it had it's own system or at least an idea of how and where to use it. What difficuulties etc.


    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

    There's its description under the Abilities heading.

    Occult is an Ability to know what various kinds of spirits can do and what might compel and motivate them beyond their personalities as people. Things like what kind of burning branch produces a smoke that repulses or draws them, that kind of thing.

    It is also an Ability one can use to represent insight into things that are just plain weird about Creation. Things like inexplicable curses coming upon people, or how to recognise the chicken eggs that hatch into basilisks.

    It's not so much an ability for summoning spirits as it is for knowing if a given spirit is naturally inclined to be summoned and what might be assembled to do so. Like, you're not rolling it on its own to do so, you're doing it to know if a given variety of god can sense papyrus inscribed with a given poem being burned on the night of the waning crescent moon and coming before you in good humour.

    There might be some places in the world where such actions can affect the weather, but not across the board, and not with a fully fleshed out system for how it works.
    Thing is how is this any way different from LORE ?

    Because if Occult is only knowledge without actual use, then it really should just be a lore specialty and done. On the other hand if it does have actual use, then there should be a system or examples of how to use it.

    For example take Medicine. If Medicine was only knowledge then it should just be Lore (Medicine) and done. Yet Medicine is the actual ability to practice medicine. To perform medical procedures, set broken bones etc..

    What can a person with the Occult skill do? Can they appease/banish spirits? You mentioned weather-working should not exactly have a system but it's been described as one of the abilities mortal miracle workers possess (see pg 578)

    Everyday Wonders
    Mortal miracle-workers practice alchemy, geomancy, astrology, warding, weatherworking and other crafts, and they’ve also found ways to create drugs and charms that contain some degree of power.​

    Either those Everyday Wonders (tailsmans, miracle-tea, etc) are made by Thaumaturgists and not actually every-day (or there are a LOT more thaumaturgists then the core describes) or they are made/accomplished by mortal occultists in which case the occult skill should reflect this.

                        Here's your answer.

                        I mean technically you can use it as "lore, but for magic stuff" but that's strictly redundant with Lore unless you deliberately lop off all supernatural knowledge from it - normally Lore covers academic knowledge of many fields, while Occult is applied knowledge, but in 3e there is no such thing as applied Occult without unique special powers.​

                        Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                        Occult is an Ability to know what various kinds of spirits can do and what might compel and motivate them beyond their personalities as people. Things like what kind of burning branch produces a smoke that repulses or draws them, that kind of thing.

                        It is also an Ability one can use to represent insight into things that are just plain weird about Creation. Things like inexplicable curses coming upon people, or how to recognise the chicken eggs that hatch into basilisks.

                        It's not so much an ability for summoning spirits as it is for knowing if a given spirit is naturally inclined to be summoned and what might be assembled to do so. Like, you're not rolling it on its own to do so, you're doing it to know if a given variety of god can sense papyrus inscribed with a given poem being burned on the night of the waning crescent moon and coming before you in good humour.

                        There might be some places in the world where such actions can affect the weather, but not across the board, and not with a fully fleshed out system for how it works.
                        Those are all also Lore, though. Unless you want to argue that as soon as something becomes too "supernatural" it vanishes from Lore, which doesn't make any sense - why would a learned sage's knowledge of a region and its inhabitants suddenly stop dead when it comes to spirits? Or, at least, require that they have twice as much effort to learn it?

                        The divide causes problems. Suppose I was a learned sage who wanted to give a lecture on the following things:

                        1. The false (purely cultural or apocryphal in nature) supernatural beliefs, histories, and entities of a particular region. None of the things in this lecture are real; they're just folk-lore. This plainly isn't occult (because it has nothing to do with actual spirits), so it's Lore.

                        2. Supernatural beliefs, histories, and entities of a particular region that have actually valid foundations. Now this is lecture depends on Occult?

                        What if I'm unsure whether a particular bit of ancient lore is reliable or not?

                        Is knowledge of historical weather patterns Lore or Occult? What about knowledge of the capabilities of different types of Exalted and their histories? If I wanted to know something obscure about the powers of various Exigents that have appeared, is that Lore or Occult? What if I want to know something obscure about the powers of various spirits?

                        Creation is a magical setting, so pretty much all of Lore is going to be, to some extent or another, magical in nature.

                                    Ah, mortal understanding of the Constellations is actually referenced in Charting Fate's Course.

                                    Basically, people often have an understanding of what the Constellations mean that aligns with the symbolism by which they inform the powers of Sidereals, but many cultures attribute their own meanings to them, that may or may not be related to the Sidereal ones. People often regard the placement of the stars as auspicious or otherwise, but that's not so much prediction of the future as people letting such things inform the timing of when they'll do something on the assumption that it will be blessed or cursed. The movements of the stars are not said to directly reflect influences in the Loom of Fate, but they can have a certain ephemeral connection to things happening in the world connected to their purviews.

                                    That is to say that being born under the sign of the Lovers doesn't strictly say anything about Heaven's plans for your life, but the meaning of that sign might inform how you live more often than not.

                                    But for the most part, people's relationship with the stars is much like it has tended to be in real life.                                                     For me, Occult in Exalted (and WoD, actually) functions like WoD Politics. Your Politics rating is not a measure how how much you know about politics, it's how well you do politics. How well-connected you are, what offices you hold etc.

                                                    In an Occult context, this feels more like applied knowledge. If you want to know about demons, that's Lore. If you want to actually summon one and do it well, that's Occult. It folds some practical knowledge into the thing, but the ability to perform tasks is the core of the Ability for me. Yes, you may need other things as well, but Occult reflects things like technique be as well, which binary things like "is/is not a sorcerer or thaumaturge" cannot do.
