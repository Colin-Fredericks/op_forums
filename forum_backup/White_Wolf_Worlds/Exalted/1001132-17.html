                            Jianghu Domestic Policies

                            Kasai, as the Lord Paramount of Jianghu, can basically run his new fiefdom however the hell he pleases. Free peasantry or bonded serfs? High or low taxes? Centralized or spread out? Those choices and more are entirely his.
                            So, what I am asking is for you fine folks to decide on the domestic policies governing the new region, on a 1 to 5 scale.
                            • Centralization: Whether Kasai holds onto judicial and legislative powers
                              1. Decentralized (local landed retainers have authorization to hear and decide on court cases, as well as law implementation) [this is what is in effect right now in Jianghu]
                              2. Little decentralized (local landed retainers have authorization to hear and decide on court cases)
                              3. Mix: dependent on situation
                              4. Little centralization (law and court cases must be brought to a specialized representative of Kasai, aka a Magistrate of his choosing)
                              5. Highly centralized (all legislative and judicial rulings come from Kasai himself)
                            • Aristocracy vs Plutocracy: Whether wealth is favored in agriculture or in trade, aka price of raw goods vs finished goods. Has effect on economy
                              1. Highly Aristocratic (Strongly agriculturally favored, attract more Oathsworn) [Current policy in effect in Jianghu]
                              2. Slightly Aristocratic ( Slightly agriculturally favored)
                              3. Mix
                              4. Slightly Plutocratic ( Prices favor finished goods and tradesmen)
                              5. Highly Plutocratic (Trading centers strongly influential, give Liangzhou market-town a charter, allow them to run themselves)
                            • Commoners: generally, the more peasants, the more money Kasai can collect in taxes, and the more peasants, the more food he gets
                              1. All peasants (free landowners, Resources (agriculture) goes down to 0 {effectively, Kasai will have to pay people to work his lands}, Resources (taxes) will go up to 4, as well as more people being eligible to levy
                              2. Mostly peasants ( 7 to 3 split, Resources (agriculture) goes down to 2, Resources (taxes) goes up to 3)
                              3. Even mix (5 on 5 split, Resources (agriculture) goes to 3, Resources (taxes) goes to 3)
                              4. Mostly serfs (7 to 3 split, Resources (agriculture) goes to 4, Resources (taxes) goes to 2) [current policy]
                              5. All serfs (bonded land-workers, Resources (agriculture) goes to 4, Resources (taxes) go to 0, no levyable Men-at-Arms, probably rebellions)
                            • Mercantilism vs Free-Trade:
                              1. Free-Trade favored: encourages external trade. Boost external economy
                              2. Mercantilism favored: protectionist, encourages local trade. Boost local economy
                            • Attracting new settlers
                              1. Offer tax/food-rent breaks to new settlers who move
                              2. Offer lump sums of cash to new settlers who move
                              3. If someone joins your army and serves for 5 years, give them 40 acres (aka ‘standard’ peasant farm size) and an ox (expensive draft animal) when they muster out
                              4. If people move to hinterlands of Jianghu where there are natural resources, give the settlement shares of profits when resource is exploited
                            • Army composition
                              1. “standard” : Peasants buy whatever weapons they want and can afford, and show up for muster (no effect) [current policy]
                              2. Give weapons to new settlers: expensive, but Kasai can guarantee weapon quality
                              3. Assize of Arms edict: dependent on the value of the land one holds, they have to show up with certain weapons

                            • Army training

                              2. Regular Muster: Every Sunday, every landowner has to show up at the local village center for drill
                              3. Summer Maneuvers: every summer, after the harvest and planting is done, every landowner shows up for 6 weeks of long-term drill at Yagura Castle

                                                If Kasai is going to offer weapons to prospective settlers in return for military service, or due to a Militia edict, you folks have to think about what kinds of weapons he is going to buy. I mean, he is basically the 5th or so richest man in Xia at this point, but arms and armor are expensive, and just because he is "rated" at a certain amount of income doesn't mean he is swimming in actual cash.

                                                If he does buy up a stock of weapons, either to give out or just to stockpile in case of emergency (many castles and manors have an armory, both for use and to show off wealth), he would almost certainly order them from the Honorable Guild of Weaponcrafters, aka the place he traded with back in the beginning of the Quest. He knows them, they know him, and would probably give him both reasonable rates and a loan if he needed it.

                                                ** Weapons

                                                -Spears:

                                                Spears are almost-certainly the most common weapon in use across the Hundred Kingdoms, for many reasons. They are cheap (requiring little steel), easy/fast to make (if stock is available, even an apprentice can hammer out a spearhead in an hour or two), easy to use (if you can stand up straight, you can use a spear), and omni-applicable, functioning well against cavalry and infantry, mounted and on foot.

                                                On the battlefield, spear-regiments are primarily used as "defensive" units, presenting spears and shields to the enemy, holding them in place while other troops flank. They have staying power, but not very much offense (that is relative. A spear-regiment can and will 'kill' other units, just not as fast as halberdiers, archers or cavalry.) [think of Tanks in an MMO]

                                                Cost: ~2 dinar for a single spearhead retail, ~150 dinar for a lot of 100 spearheads wholesale

                                                -Halberds

                                                Halberds are probably the most 'effective" weapons of all time; the reach of a spear, the impact of an axe, the cutting capability of a sword. Such effective weapons are surprisingly simple, often times being little more than a spear with an axe or sickle-blade bolted on,and are effective against many different targets; cavalry, infantry, heavy armor, etc . They are popular with guardsmen, town-miltias, peasant Men-at-Arms, and even some Oathsworn.

                                                On the battlefield, halberd-regiments are primarily used as "offensive" units, due to the fact that halberd require two hands to use, preventing the use of a shield and therefore, leaving the halberdier and their comrades comparatively weak to missile fire and the crush of melee. (This is often mitigated by the halberdiers, who tend to be comparatively wealthier than their peers, owning and using heavier armor). While spear-regiments hold the enemy in place, halberd-regiments charge the flank, chewing through enemy units with aplomb. Halberdiers are also often big guys, strong and hearty, who can hold a screamed battle-cry while charging full-tilt across a field while waving a sharp stick. [Think DPS]

                                                Cost: ~5 dinar for a single halberd-head retail, ~400 dinar for a lot of 100 halberd-heads wholesale

                                                -Longbows

                                                After spears, the longbow is probably the second most common weapon on the Hundred Kingdoms battlefield. Nothing, asides from an Oathsworn cavalry charge, is more terrifying than seeing 100+ arrows blot out the sun, and said arrows can decimate an enemy unit, killing men en masse with a sigh like the winter wind.

                                                Most non-Oathsworn longbows are comparatively-simple laminates of wood and bamboo, between 5 and 6 feet in length. Most archers draw to the ear, loosing arrows around 3 feet in length, which are comparatively heavy and when fired in a plunging arc (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plunging_fire, essentially adding gravity to the impact) are capable of punching through light armor and flesh with ease.

                                                The main 'problem' with longbow regiments and longbowmen in general is that there always isn't enough of them. While hunting is pretty common, a common saying said by many Lords is "if you want a good archer, start with his grandfather". Archery, even disregarding accuracy and instead using massed-fire, is hard, and building up the necessary muscle and proper techniques takes lots of time and lots of practice. Generally, unless you recruit people already skilled with the bow, you aren't going to have effective archers...... leading many Lords to enact laws mandating weekly training in archery for peasants of specific wealth-ratings. Most peasants just buy spears instead.

                                                On the battlefield, longbow-regiments are used as skirmishers, plunging mass-fire (more artillery than accuracy), fire over intervening terrain (can loose arrows over a river or swamp, or over friends heads), or as flankers.

                                                Cost: ~70 dinars for a finished longbow from a bowyer (bows are individual pieces, almost works of art, and can't be 'mass-produced'), but most peasants make them themselves, ~1 dinar for a sheaf of 20 broadheaded war-arrows (which are thicker and heavier than hunting arrows).

                                                -Crossbow

                                                Crossbows are a comparatively-rare sight on the battlefield, despite their proven effectiveness. This is mainly due to their long reload times (in the time it takes to load and loose a single crossbow bolt, a skilled longbowman can loose 6 or more arrows, meaning less pressure is put on the enemy), and due to their expense (most peasants can't afford crossbows, as they require a lot of metal and skilled carpentry). Therefore, crossbows are more commonly seen in the hands of town-miltias (due to ease of use. If you can kneel and stand, you can use a crossbow), serf-levies and castle-guardsmen.

                                                The crossbow really shines in siege warfare. Any Lord with even a modicum of intelligence stockpiles crossbows to arm refugees, and even an untrained crossbow-user can aim at a target and pull the trigger. A mass of crossbow-levies can put out a terrifying rain of steel, even if most miss or are ineffective. In addition, a crossbow can be used inside a castle or other building, even one with low ceilings and tight walls, where longbows cannot. At such close ranges, a crossbow with a steel prod will make obsolete even the thickest of Oathsworn armor, meaning a few armsmen covering a doorway can hold off an army, so long as the bolts don't run out.

                                                On the battlefield, when they do get used, crossbow-regiments are primarily used for direct-fire missile attacks, usually within 100 or so meters, as crossbows are hard to use for plunging fire. This means they are often used as close-range skirmishers and as flanking troops.

                                                Cost: ~50 dinar for a steel-prod crossbow, ~ 1 dinar for a box of 20 bolts

                                                -Other weapons

                                                Other weapons, like backswords, daggers, maces, etc, are usually individually picked for use as backup sidearms.

                                                **Armor

                                                - Body armor

                                                Most peasants make due with garments called "arming jackets", thickly-quilted jackets made of multiple layers of canvas and wool-padding, essentially a thicker standalone version of the underpadding worn under Oathsworn mail/lamellar. Long-sleeved, with high collars, overlapping lapels (to give a double thickness over the front of the chest), and extending to the knee, arming jackets are hot (especially in the Eastern summer), heavy, hard to dry and keep clean ..... but they are incredibly protective, capable of bouncing spent arrows and turning aside blade-cuts. Only stabs from close range are really capable of punching through the layers of cloth, but even those tend to be blunted unless they strike perpendicular to the weave.

                                                In most cases, an arming jacket and a helmet might be all a peasant Man-at-Arms has to protect themselves with ........ but they are often "good enough".

                                                Cost: ~5 dinar for an arming jacket of canvas and wool.

                                                For those Men-at-Arms with more disposable income, there is the Brigandine. A Brigandine is basically an arming jacket with metal plates and mail sewn onto the front and back. The metal plates are sewn directly to the cloth backing, and linked with mail rings. The whole ensemble is much more protective than a "regular" arming jacket, for only a slight increase in weight. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karuta_(armour) ) Or, what these folks are wearing (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashiga...sachakun_3.jpg)

                                                Cost: ~25 dinar

                                                -Head armor:

                                                Most Men-at-Arms (and even poorer Oathsworn) use helmets styled along the lines of the familiar conical hats worn every day, albeit militarized. A straw hat can be coated in several layers of lacquer, making it relatively bladeproof while retaining its light weight. Or, for those with more money, a facsimile of the conical hat can be made from lacquered leather or even metal, these of course being more protective.

                                                These styles of helmet are popular because they are relatively cheap (especially compared to a full-helm), while being easy to maintain and comfortable. Many Men-at-Arms drape canvas or other cloth around the inside rim of the helmet to help keep off wind and weather, or paint regiment insignia on the front for ease-of-recognition. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasa_(...Samurai_14.JPG), the top one especially

                                                Cost: ~2 for a lacquered straw helmet, ~7 for a lacquered leather or iron helmet

                                                -Personal armor:

                                                If able to, (aka spearmen or light-cavalry), Men-at-Arms will usually bear shields very similar to their Oathsworn overlords. Made of bamboo, wooden slats and layers of canvas, the shield is probably the easiest and most effective form of protection found on the battlefield, capable of deflecting arrows and blocking blades. In some cases, shields may be painted or decorated with regiment markings, ferocious animals or insults to the enemy.

                                                Cost: ~ 5 - 10 dinar, depending on materials.


                                                *** Unit TO&E

                                                While, when using game mechanics, peasant Men-at-Arms will all be Resources 2, in-universe there often exists a gradation between peasants that dictates which weapons, armor and equipment they will have.

                                                -Bottom: spears, arming jackets, shields, lacquered straw helmets. This is the cheapest equipment, so spear-regiments are most common
                                                -Middle: halberds or longbows, arming jackets (common) or brigandines (rarer), lacquered metal or leather helmets. Halberdiers will often be veterans of prior campaigns, with more productive lands, and longbowmen will hail from the hinterlands and frontiers of a lords domain.
                                                -Top: spears, shields, backswords, longbows, brigandines, lacquered metal helmets, light horses. The "peasant aristocracy", made up of village headmen, accomplished yeomen farmers, or the elder sons of a commoner clan, these warriors are often used as light cavalry (to chase off missile troops, run down routing/wavering units, or as scouts and skirmishers), or as mounted dragoon-style infantry and longbowmen.

                                                Most domains will naturally tend to have a mix of weapons, trending towards spears predominating, due to peasants purchasing whatever they can afford and prefer.
