                    The abyssal fluff line about only making weapons or destructive items is not actually mechanically binding, it just throws a road block in place for people who are not especially creative.

                    Consider the following three scenarios:

                    1: An abyssal could forge a sword (a weapon designed specifically for killing), and then only use it as a kitchen knife for preparing sumptuous meals out of extra large animals and vegetables. In this case a weapon is never used for destructive purposes, in fact it is used to enhance life. From this you can take away the concept that what it is used for after the fact doesn't matter.

                    2: An abyssal could crafter a truly fine quill or other writing implement, not inherently a weapon, but it gives a bonus for written social checks. The abyssal uses it to gain treaties that destabilize the enemies of the abyssals deathlord, thus causing wars and boarder squirmishes that wipe out hundred if not thousands of lives. As such it makes the pen many times more lethal than a sword wielded by an individual warrior.

                    3: An Abyssal creates a necklace of incredible beauty and gives it to the youngest of three royal daughters of a random country. This leads to jealousy between the daughters and eventually family members start killing each other to possess the item. The item has no weapon value on its own, but given the human drive to possess things, such an item can be quite deadly.

                    In the last two scenarios an otherwise innocuous item can be used to great effect, thus turning them into truly potent weapons.

                    For my two cents, the abyssal crafting fluff line should be used as a narrative guide. The ST should ask the player what and how the item will be destructive or glorify death, if they have a decent answer then you go with it. If the Player is just wanting something cool they saw in an anime, movie or other media that really has nothing to do with death then I would suggest that they use it for a different character.

                                            Glad someone asked!

                                            The powers of each Recreated (my antagonist's brand name for them) are contingent on the character and strength of the Ties and Intimacies of the person used to create them. Sadists produce revenants with especially dangerous weapons of made of essence and bone, or which gain physical bonuses at the sight of blood. A couple deeply in love might enjoy powerful bonuses to each others' combat abilities when in proximity. A grieving parent or despised vagrant might have a gaze or poison that induces mind-wracking loneliness and ennui. Someone that believed that all people are truly good inside might be able to induce powerful feelings of trust and compassion in their enemies. Which Intimacy each Recreated is formed from is at the discretion of whoever is attuned to the artifact at the time, and the process itself costs time and essence to initiate and complete. The revenants obey the will of their creator so long as this creator remain attuned to the engine that created them, (not that he knows that little detail yet) and are possessed of about as much cunning and ferocity as a Xenomorph.

                                            This is what has been driving this antagonists' partner, a stealthy, brutal Dusk caste, to venture out into the city under disguise and find the most passionate and committed of its citizenry to murder. The Dusk seeks out those in the throes of great passions or sorrows: sadists, virtuosos, lovers, masters, new parents of slain children (more often than not, slain by the Abyssal's hand), and saints. Then, once the Daybreak's engine is complete, they will create an army of powerful Recreated revenants and strike quickly and brutally at the heart of Chiaroscuro. Based on the profane lore my antagonist has gathered, the combination of the necromantic workings he has been performing at set intervals throughout the Chiaroscuro shadowlands and enough slaughter and fear in a single night will cause the boundaries of the Shadowlands to expand and begin to consume the city.

                                                There's a problem with the premise of the question. The Crafting rules are very much designed so that the Exalted don't need Charms to make things; there's no Solar Charm to make legendary First Age manses or artifacts. The closest you get is Dual Magnus Prana, and that's a capstone Charm.

                                                I would expect to see Charms that give Abyssals dice tricks and additional project slots and crafting points if they're working in the Underworld or in Shadowlands, and it seems likely that the Abyssal Repair tree will focus on breaking things or reforging them so that they can be attuned to the Essence of the Underworld - like rebuilding a temple so that it honours the Deathlords instead of its intended god, or breaking an orichalcum daiklave so that it can be used as a component for a soulsteel daiklave - but I don't have any better ideas. Maybe some Charms to protect grave goods so they're not dissolved by sunlight, like a reverse Chaos-Resistance Preparation? Assuming sunlight melting grave goods actually a thing - I don't really remember.

                                                            First off, I think Abyssals shouldbe able to make anything they want. The only Exalted who I feel may be somewhat limited in that respect might be certain unique Exigents who, depending on their patron might find it difficult to craft certain things or channel Essence to assist with working with certain substances.

                                                            As for Abyssals, I would like their Crafting to heavily incorporate themes like memories, things forgotten, horror and Ideas That Should Not Exist. Anything crafted by an Abyssal should be perfectly serviceable, but also have something wrong, disturbing or creepy about it (either subtly or overtly).

                                                            A portrait by an Abyssal would be just as impressive as one made by a Solar, but perhaps the eyes really do follow you, or sometimes if you stare at it for too long, an odd series of handprints seem to appear on it but vanish when you stop to look.

                                                            These sorts of effects would help to instill certain Intimacies in people and are a natural result of the inherent wrongness of the Abyssal bleeding out into his creations.

                                                            In general I think Abyssals should be great at creating things that instill madness, death, fear and despair, or that carry curses, perhaps getting bonus crafting exp or other breaks when making something like that, buy they definitely shouldn't take penalties (or face limitations) for crafting something useful, helpful or that doesn't focus on those themes.
