          The thorn of it IMO is that holding just one sword and having nothing in your empty hand is so valueless. Dual-wielding and two-handers get away with being a +2 to a specific roll that only a minority of combatants will even care about, but the most obvious equivalent for Shields (+1 Defense) is both much broader than clashes and snaps the combat engine's math in half. "+1 Defense while X" might work but is still a risky proposition.

          So the trick is either finding a similarly niche roll that +2 to would still fit the shield-wielder power fantasy, or making having an empty hand more valuable to one-sword-guy, which means that shields, dual wielding, and two-handers can all be a little stronger as well.

                  Shields try to fit that parameter, but do so in a very unsatisfying way.

                  First, the Shield tag gives you a +1 net Defense (+2 from Full Defense, -1 from Flurry) bonus for 1i and a -3 dice penalty if the other action is rolled. The other action might add more Initiative or Defense costs on top of it.

                  The additional +1 is because you're using a medium weapon with the Shield tag, because that's the only way to get to a net +2. The Shield tag on a light or heavy weapon is worse than just having a medium weapon in one of your hands (even if you're not using it for attacks).

                                                  I don't really think that makes your case.

                                                  Stormcaller isn't, by your own logic, a Reaper Daiklave because it's important for Stormcaller to have the mechanical functions of a slashing sword, but purely for the invocation of a cultural element from the real world. While the weapon form might not be strictly arbitrary, it's divorced from the practical shape in favor of fulfilling a fantasy. Slings are not inherently good at killing large creatures. There's nothing special about slings for slaying giants. But we have an example sling for killing Behemoths in the books to invoke the David vs. Goliath story. If the designers wanted to focus on Hercules as an archer (something that's a bit less popular, but still part of mythology to draw from) you could easily have the same Artifact as a bow instead.

                                                  The arbitrary aspect is which myths and fantasies you pick for your design. If I'm making an Artifact that my War focused character lofts high to inspire troops as it's central premise, choosing between a normal Daiklave (ex: Joan of Arc), a Reaper Daiklave (ex: Napoleon), or even a Grand Daiklaive (ex: William Wallace) is, yes, arbitrary... but they're all equally valid choices to base my Artifact around.

                                                  Mythology and such sources of inspiration are also messy. The Aegis is probably one of the more iconic mythological shields. But the attributes assigned to it don't really care about being a shield (mostly it scares and demoralizes people), and even in mythology it's been a shield, a breastplate, or just a collection of special items integrated into a goat hide that can be waved around as a banner or attached to a shield/armor/over clothing.

                                                  When we get into actual design trends in the books, the farther away from obvious cultural inspirations, the more arbitrary the specific stat blocks get. While an Artifact that's focused on turning blood into sorcerous motes needs to do Lethal damage, why does it need to be a giant axe? Would anything be lost if it was pretty much any other weapon?

                                                  I'm not actually claiming that "a Thunderbolt Shield has no call to go for Evocations that are Evocative of shields in some fashion." I'm claiming that "in some fashion," is so broad that it renders the specific stat block moot because you can find evocative inspirations for so many other options among the list. There's so much inspirational overlap out there that it's exceedingly hard to say there's anything that is unique to any given Artifact stat block:a Thunderbolt Shield, Artifact Breastplate, and a Grand Daiklave, should all have Evocations that evoke those forms... but those can also all be the same exact set of Evocations at the same time.

                                                      Shields in Exalted are just not inherently better at being shields than other, better weapons.

                                                      As a tangent, this reminds me of how in Ars Magica, when you create magic items, things do have associated bonuses based on their Shape.
                                                      For example, a dagger is better at damaging effects than a bandage. You're not punished for making a bandage that wounds, it's just easier to make a dagger do that and the effect would be stronger or at least enchanting it would probably be faster.

                                                      Exalted doesn't have that, beyond the Magical Materials themselves, but that's just the material of the artifact - not its shape.

                                                      So, you could pick up a Thunderbolt Shield orrrrr you could just make a Daiklave or Dire Chain or whatever other Medium Artifact weapon you want and give it Parry-enhancing Evocations and effects - and you could do that without sacrificing other Evocations that enhance things like damage, attacks, create magical attacks and have other utility effects as well.
                                                      True, you could make an artifact shield that does all (or most) of that - but the fact is that any other weapon can do the same exact things while also not gimping your damage output and having other advantages (higher Overwhelming for Daiklave, a host of tags for Dire Chain, Lethal damage and so on).

                                                      Shields may not be bad in a vacuum (if a hypothetical exalt finds herself in an empty cave with nothing but a shield lying around, she couldn't go wrong with picking up the shield), they're just bad when compared with literally every other weapon because every other weapon can do what they do PLUS extra.

                                                      Also, unless I missed some errata, shields don't actually give you any bonus to Parry - beyond the base bonus that all Medium weapons get, that is. The Shield tag just lets you put Full Defense in a flurry while permanently reducing the weapon's damage by 2.

                                                      I'm not sure why the Shield tag doesn't do just a little something extra like give you or another character Cover (even Light Cover). I guess you could do that with a Stunt, but that's like saying "it's fine because at my table it is... My storyteller lets us..."

                                                      I chalk it up to the bitter taste left by the previous edition's inherent problem with devolving into "unstoppable force vs immovable object" death spiral.

                                                          I think it goes farther back. early development and early 1e clearly wanted to regulate shields to the same place as helmets: realistically things of value such that nameless mooks would clearly want them, but beneath the epic grandeur of the Exalted to have to care about beyond being character quirks. Shields and helmets were just ways to code different military forces to real world inspirations. IME the folks that wanted Exalted to emphasize the Sword and Sandals side of the game a bit more than the Wuxia side of the game (which was generally sold as the default style of the game to people getting into it, and it still holds a strong "anime" RPG reputation despite all the non-anime and non-wuxia stuff in it) pushed for better sword & board mechanical representation to balance out all the fancy MA weapons. So they got put into 1e a bit awkwardly, and neither 2e or 3e really tried to find a "place" in the game for shields despite including them because at least enough players wanted mechanically significant ones to make it valuable to include them.

                                                          I think one of the best facts in support of this is that 3e has a "shield" tag to begin with. We don't have "sword" or "axe" or "spear" or "bow," as tags. We have common tags that most swords have, etc. so you can look at a set of tags and guess what kind of weapon it is with decent accuracy. Flame is pretty much the only other tag that's so closely linked to a specific type of weapon (and even then it's actual mechanics could be justified on weapons that don't actually use firedust flame bursts). Instead of having ~3 defense focused Tags and any shields being identifiable by being loaded up with a decent number of them like every other weapon stat block, shields are tautologically linked to a tag. In past threads I've always been proponent of splitting the Shield tag up into multiple tags. One tag is trying to define a gigantic class of combat implements, and it doesn't work in a satisfactory way. Instead of shields emerging from the combat and equipment rules naturally, 3e is still just stuffing them in because people want shields on some level.
