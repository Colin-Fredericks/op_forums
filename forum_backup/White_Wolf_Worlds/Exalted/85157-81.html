                        Predictable isn't always bad. I tend to feel that it's much much worse when stories deliberately try to violate expectations - unless you're doing it for comedic purposes, it doesn't work. Most of the time, when your audience expects something, it's because that's the most logical way for the story to go, and it's the most logical way for the story to go because it makes the best story. Obviously if you have a better idea you can do something amazing by violating that expectation, but just violating expectations for the sake of doing so is a much more serious hack mistake than following them and will result in a weaker story. Plot-twists and violations of your audience's expectations should be reserved for key moments, for situations when you have a strong plan to build on them, and for situations where you can give your audience something they wanted but didn't realize they wanted.

                        Beyond that, the important thing in a story is the overarching narrative, not individual plot-twists. In other words:

                        It doesn't really matter what happens to Felicia here. It's the long-term effect it will have on Allison and Cio that matters.

                        (Also, I mean, if you want to talk predictable, this whole setup is not that hard to predict. Either Allison objects or Felicia dies, probably both. My money is on her dying because I don't think anyone but Allison, Cio, and maybe Oscar are getting out of this run alive. We knew everyone else was a red shirt right from the start, that's not the point of this storyline.)

                                I mean, in a larger sense I'm pretty sure everyone except Allison, Cio, and maybe Oscar is gonna be dead by the end of this run. Cio went on and on about how this place is a deathtrap, and then they brought on a colorful menagerie of expendable characters.

                                It's loathsome because it's supposed to be loathsome. It's a reminder of why Cio doesn't want to be Yabalchoath and why Allison (in insisting that Cio assume that role and take on this mission) forced Cio to do terrible things and become a terrible person again. It also underlines how awful Oscar is, which is perhaps a bit important because he can easily become a bit too much of a lovable rogue.

                                I don't think you can say that it's boring until we've seen the full impact of it, though - how Allison reacts, how Cio reacts, etc. If your objection is that it makes you think less of Cio, well, she was Yabalchoath already - she's done this and worse before. I suspect it's also necessary to show how awful Yabalchoath was and how terrible devils can be for the rest of Cio's plot arc to make sense - we got glimpses of this before, but this puts it a lot more front-and-center than it previously was. You should ignore the fans getting all giddy about it (yes, it can be grating when fans get all self-congratulatory about anticipating a plot element, especially one that is really awful like this). The important thing is to pay attention to Cio and Allison and how this affects their growth going forwards.

                                I don't see it as gratuitous, basically. Allison is high on Incubus right now, and (spoiler) the real danger of that isn't some hidden clause that costs her her soul, the danger is that he's going to push her to become someone like Yabalchoath, not out of any sort of ill-will but because that's genuinely what he thinks is the best possible thing to be. And if it were badly-written, there's a danger that readers could come away saying "wow, Incubus is cool! Yabalchoath is cool! Allison should just stop whining and become like that!" Showing just how awful that would really be is important (and I think that that's the real purpose of this, drawing a parallel between what Incubus said to Allison a few pages ago and what Cio is saying now.)

                                I mean, it's not impossible that Allison will force another option through - say "fuck this" and blow up the door or something (although that would also be pretty predictable, as you know.) But either way the same basic goal is accomplished.
