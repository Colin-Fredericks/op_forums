                            Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
                            And you don't get a Parry benefit from having a sword in one hand and a mace in the other either... so? I don't follow the complaint.

                            Light weapon for the Accuracy bonus. Shield for Parry.
                            If you're carrying a weapon in each hand:

                            Carrying the same weapon in both hands = +2 dice to Clashes. So if thinking you're using a medium weapon in one hand, there's pretty much no reason not to carry two for this bonus.

                            Carrying two different weapons = get the most out of the diversity this allows.

                            The complaint is that the Shield Tag isn't rarely better than any other Tag to include in a mixed weapon setup. A whip on one hand for lots of Tags, and a sword or one handed spear in the other for the medium weapon +1 Parry and better ability to deal with high armor value is way more effective than a short sword and a shield; despite one being a kinda ridiculous combo that there isn't even much in the way of inspiration for, and the other is the infantry standard of the inspiration for the settings current most powerful military.

                            The complaint is that the Shield Tag sucks so nobody wants to use them even if they have a concept that would use a shield, and thus should be better so shield using characters makes sense; and this should work for mortal characters, not just characters that can use Artifacts and Charms.

                            You can kill people with a dagger. You can kill people with a tea cup.
                            Neither of those take a -2 damage penalty to attack people with.

                            I'd rather take a shield to the face than a mace.
                            I'd rather be shot with a 9mm pistol than a 12 gauge shotgun... but neither is a "good" option.

                            The issue is that shields aren't armor. They're not a passive thing strapped on to you. They're active weapons used to control fights and harm opponents as much as whatever you might pair them with. The damage penalty on them doesn't make sense from a simulation perspective, or the abstraction of the system, or the perspective of doing cool shit like being Exalted Captain America.

                                    Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
                                    And I said I'd be okay with giving that same bonus to Shields.
                                    ...But Exalted has lots of suboptimal choices (see Chopping Sword vs Axe).
                                    Then you see to get the core complaint just fine. The Shield Tag isn't good. People want it to not be sub-optimal, instead of just accepting it as sub-optimal and not fixing it.

                                    A Dagger has Damage +7.
                                    A Shield has Damage +7.
                                    A medium shield as Damage + 7. A light shield has Damage + 5. A teacup, as an improvised weapons, has Damage + 7, cost 1i per attack, and can be used with Melee or Brawl freely.

                                    Like a sword. And they give +1 Parry, like a sword.
                                    No, this is just a factor of the medium weapon stat blocks, not swords vs. shields. A short sword (light stat block) or a two-handed sword (heavy stat block) don't give you a +1 Parry.

                                    But they are less deadly than a sword.
                                    Only in the representation of lethal vs. bashing damage. Killing people doesn't care about weapon damage in Exalted 3e. Weapon damage is about Initiative, which shields are tactically better for than swords. You control the fight (aka get more Initiative than your opponent) with your shield, and stab them with the sword for a lethal damage Decisive attack.

                                    And even that is more genre emulation than reality. In reality blunt force trauma is quite deadly.

                                    And you can argue that shields are just as deadly as swords, but there's a reason historically armies which used shields carried an actual weapon do do their killing with.
                                    There's a large difference between small scale engagements (aka PCs fighting small groups of opponents) and armies, and how weapons get used. Shields are actual weapons. Armies throughout history had their soldiers carry an array of weapons because one weapon is a horrible way to fight. The gladius, despite it's iconic status, wasn't the original primary 'killing' weapon of the Roman legions (they started with spears in the third line for this), and it's effectiveness was highly dependent on the scutum to control enemy movements and feed them into zones where multiple soldiers could stab at once instead of trying to fight one-on-one like a more common line of troops. They also heavily relied on thrown weapons to soften and disrupt enemy formations (aka kill people) before clinch fighting (shield to shield) began.

                                    Armies used spears and shields, or swords and shields, etc, because the combination was better than the individual weapons. Shields weren't some afterthought. They were a primary weapon of military combat, and essential to tactics.

                                    Making this all about shields vs. swords is a fairly annoying line of thought. The point of having multiple weapons is tactical flexibility; in reality and in the game. It's not about sword vs shield, but what weapon is in your other hand.

                                    Shields aren't as effective a weapon as swords.
                                    Citation needed, especially as damage in Exalted 3e is not about deadliness.

                                    It's harder to kill someone with a slab of blunt wood than a pointy metal object.
                                    Factually incorrect. Blunt force trauma and penetrating trauma are not directly comparable in terms of how hard it is to kill someone. It's easier to kill someone with a wooden baseball bat than a metal sewing needle, because it's not just about blunt wood vs. pointy metal.

                                    As well, in the abstraction of the system (which this was a response to) all weapons are equally good at incapacitating people. Again, Damage is about controlling the fight (Initiative), not deadliness. A sword and a shield's only difference in killing people, that is in making Decisive attacks, is in the damage type, not their Damage rating. The Shield Tag reduces Damage rating. Nothing stops me from having a spiked shield, or a shield with a sharpened metal rim, and calling it a Lethal weapon, but it would still be -2 Damage.

                                    That's literally what Charms, Martial Arts and Evocations are for.
                                    No, those are for doing cool stuff, not wasting your first few Charms on getting rid of dumb penalties.
