                    So, I came up with this questionable idea. If you compare it to Ivory Orchid Pavilion, it doesn't provide shelter and serves far fewer people (and isn't nearly as ritzy), but provides a serious meal instead of just fruits and nuts. It's a huge step up from Food from the Aerial Table to be sure, but then I always thought that spell was pretty weak. (Sorcery is usually pretty powerful -- a spell that does nothing but produce food should be able to do better than tasteless crap IMO, even at the Terrestrial level.)

                    On the other hand, if you're not supposed to be able to easily keep people fed through sorcery, then maybe I've gone too far here. Feedback is, of course, welcome.

                    (I did briefly consult CoTD: North for the types of foods a Northern tribe might actually serve at a feast, but I admit I didn't spend terribly long researching.)

                    The Chieftain's Table (Celestial Circle Spell)
                    Cost: Ritual, 2wp
                    Keywords: None
                    Duration: Twelve hours or until dismissed

                    This spell was devised by a mighty Northern chieftain some years after being Chosen by Luna. He wished to be able to properly reward his warriors for a successful battle, and honor important guests, even when the hunting had been poor.

                    The sorcerer spends a minute or so speaking an elaborate ritual greeting in Old Realm, inviting those present to dine at his table. As he gestures, up to (sorcerer's Essence) long tables materialize, each with seating for twelve humans and laden with food.

                    The food produced by this spell is of the sort that the original chieftain considered worthy of his honored guests: roasted elk and duck, hearty venison stew, smoked fish, crusty bread, barley porridge, a variety of fruits and root vegetables, and plenty of ale and mead. The food is easily sufficient to feed everyone seated at the tables, even if they are all very hungry people with large appetites.

                    After twelve hours have passed, the tables vanish, along with all dishes and utensils. (Alternatively, the sorcerer may dismiss the spell early with a brief ritual statement of dismissal.) Any food remaining on the tables vanishes with them; food removed from the tables remains real.

                    It is traditionally considered polite when casting this spell to make a small sacrifice to any local deities associated with food (such as field gods, hunting gods, or river gods), lest they be offended at the sorcerer's presumption -- although few deities of that stature are likely to actually make an issue of it, should they feel insulted by a Celestial Exalt.

                    A sorcerer who knows The Chieftain's Table as her control spell (for some reason) may vary the offered menu, creating nearly any food and drink she is basically familiar with -- although they must be mundane things of Creation, not (for example) the roasted meat of some bizarre Wyld prodigy.

                        Originally posted by Jefepato View Post
                        So, I came up with this questionable idea. If you compare it to Ivory Orchid Pavilion, it doesn't provide shelter and serves far fewer people (and isn't nearly as ritzy), but provides a serious meal instead of just fruits and nuts. It's a huge step up from Food from the Aerial Table to be sure, but then I always thought that spell was pretty weak. (Sorcery is usually pretty powerful -- a spell that does nothing but produce food should be able to do better than tasteless crap IMO, even at the Terrestrial level.)
                        I do think that Food From the Aerial Table is actually a decent spell, due to the way it was open-ended and hypothetically capable of feeding as many people as you had motes for, in 10-person increments. That's a spell that could pretty easily end hunger, at least locally. I don't know how 3e would implement that, but I'd expect the open-endedness to stay. Anyway, though, I do feel there's room for other food-creating spells, so this is not inherently a bad idea.

                        That said, I do feel that the effect of the spell you've got here is a bit too boring and mundane, especially for Celestial Circle. Sorcery, in my opinion, should always feel at least a little weird, and generally have an at least somewhat-permanent effect on the world. Compare Ivory Orchid Pavilion, which creates a Major Intimacy in the people in it, and leaves a patch of white orchids afterwards. This one just creates food and a building, which vanishes after a few hours.

                        Honestly, you could probably just vary the fluff of Ivory Orchid Pavilion, just make the food served be stereotypical "chieftan's hall" fare instead of the fruits and nuts. But if you're really looking for a different spell, what about making the feast an "automatic party", inspiring an emotion of "revelry" or something like that in its inhabitants, and possibly actually summoning local gods of food, drink, and partying to join in. Alternatively, you could tie it to hospitality traditions - it has food and space for everyone who comes in the door, no matter how many. But everyone who enters is bound by the laws of hospitality - guests can't attack their hosts and vice versa, and even insulting or other verbal attacks are punished. Make all of that enforced by the equivalent of an Eclipse oath, perhaps.
