            Something I'm using myself, but as it might give some ideas...

                Okay, here are some other options for ya.

                1: Rebellious Phase: Dynast kids don't have a lot of freedom. Their lives are very strictly managed from the time they are tiny...and it isn't until after they finish all of their schooling that they get a modicum of freedom. Take a look at some examples of where stuff like this has happened in the real world...specifically 'Disney Kids.' Child actors for Disney have a very tightly controlled life. They aren't allowed to be crazy, they aren't allowed to get into trouble...they are watched too closely for that. So, very often, when they get too old for Disney and the restraints go away.....you get Miley Cyrus randomly licking things and rubbing her butt on Mexican flags. So, it could be that your 20-something Dragonblooded has been controlled and regulated their entire life, and now that they FINALLY have some freedom, they went: F*#k it, I'm going to go have an Adventure! With people that my family would disapprove of! Because I F%#king Can!

                2: The Skeptic: There are a good number of people who have been raised to a particular belief system and have come to a point in their life where they re-examine what they believe. They ask the question: "Do I actually believe this, or am I just going with what I was taught?" This often happens late teens to twenties (not to be confused with rebelling because you are a teenager...this is a thoughtful examination of your worldview). This is when they take a step back and try to figure out if they actually agree with what they have been taught their entire life, and decide if they are going to stick with it or not. The impetus for this is usually when something happens that challenges their view of the world; for a silly example, suppose you were taught your entire life that the Earth was flat, and today you got on a plane and could see the curvature of the planet. So, your character could be a DB who is either actively re-examining the Immaculate Philosophy, or has gone over it and found it wanting. You don't have to be hundreds of years old to run into something that upsets your view of how the world should work, after all. And there are plenty of people out there who would prefer to think about it for themselves rather than just running to whoever taught them to plead for explanations. Or, this could be someone who is simply extremely logical and wants to see both sides of the story.

                3: The Plant: The Dragonblood may not even know it...but he's a plant. Either through high-powered brainwashing charms (the Dynasty tree has some nasty ones, IIRC), or indoctrination, or some other method...this character believes that they want to join the Anathema for whatever reason. The real reason is because they are a spy and/or a betrayal bomb. At a given command or signal, their 'programming' kicks in and they betray the party....or maybe they have 'strange dreams' where they are actually reporting their actions to a Sidereal observer (Quicksilver Hand of Dreams Style would be good at this). In short...they are with the party because someone more powerful than them manipulated them into it. Of course, because Solar...it's very possible that the brainwashing may be discovered and undone before it is triggered....causing the DB to be outraged at how their own family manipulated them...turning them into an example of Idea 2.

                4: Solar Cheese: Solars can brainwash you. By accident, sometimes. They are just that charismatic. You could have a devout Dragonblooded who is an ardent follower of the Immaculate Philosophy...and then a Solar Social Monster wanders by and blows that intimacy into itty bitty pieces as collateral damage to a speech they are giving about revising tax law or something.

                5: Lunar Cheese: Lunars can brainwash you and make you think it was your idea in the first place. See above.

                And, of course, all of the other things people have said before me.....

                    The most obvious option is "There is something threatening that I can't take care of myself but may be able to take care of with these Anathema that I distrust more then I distrust them"- be that stupid Scarlet Empress + Ebon Dragon insanity or generic army who is against me. Most Dragon-Blooded are loyal to the Realm/Immaculate Philosophy, but save for the dedicated few, they're loyal to simply living more. "I'll work with (that evil) now, and then kill them later" has probably indeed been a philosophy used many a time. Probably, it's even succeeded.

                    There's also the "I see a Solar Anathema, I report back to the Wyld Hunt, then I smile and play nice until the Wyld Hunt arrives to kill them as I can't do it alone but want them tracked"- probably not the best option for a game that's not intended on breaking out in to player-versus-player conflict, but works for a story. Also works for any member of the All Seeing Eye (we need to know what the Anathema and their friends are up to before we kill it!).

                    Jadedness is also a reason: a Heptagram graduate or a member of House Leedal may be a bit more aware of Sidereals then they might 'supposed' to be, and their reaction may be more "Eh, let's see what this one does and if I can use it" then "ZOMG KILL ON SIGHT". Actually, that works for really any jaded person, ever. Many Dragon-Blooded believe in the Immaculate Philosophy, but in terms of 3E, wouldn't have an intimacy towards it: a detached sort of "Let's see if I can turn the Anathema to my own uses and win power/love/fame in the Realm later" works.

                    And, yeah, social-fu. Any Celestial Exalt has their own ways of making one loyal through charms. Some won't even need charms, just some contested rolls.
