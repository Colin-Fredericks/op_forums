        The Great Rivers of the East are more massive than any rivers on Earth. There are rivers the size of real world rivers all over Creation that are simply too small to show up on the map's scale. The River Province though has rivers upon the bulk of the breadth of which you can't see either shore. The major powers of the River Province require full blown navies, and piracy is a substantial problem.

        The gods of the Great Rivers have formed a "Court of Rivers" which no longer acknowledges the authority of the celestial god of rivers and the Bureau of Nature. While each has their own Sanctum, the group has a joint meeting hall beneath the shoreline of Nexus, where they are especially honored, at the meeting of the Grey, Yellow, and Yanaze. While sworn enemies of the celestial Bureau of Nature, the Court of Rivers has allies in Yu Shan in the divine patrons of the Guild who they are allied with. Ayesha Ura is also on better terms with them than her duties as head of the Committee of Wood and the Committee on Essence Users should imply.

        As for Nexus itself - my greatest source of inspiration for my take on it is Braavos in A Song of Ice and Fire (which in turn is heavily influenced by Venice). Built at the fork of Great Rivers, its actually on isles in marshlands and is a city of canals. The Council of Entities does not exist. Nexus is nominally lawless, though everyone knows the merchant princes of the Guild are the unofficial power. The mysterious Emissary enforces but one arcane commandment "None shall be bound in slavery in the city". As such, all slave trading occurs via receipt with slaves penned outside the city limits, or kept on barges at the docks which are sold with them, the slaves never setting foot in Nexus, where the Emissary would enforce their liberation.

        Mercenary companies in employ of various Merchant Princes or small-g guilds keep a relative peace in the city to protect commerce. One example is a group dedicated entirely to hunting and killing rapists in the employ of the city's brothels.

        The fact that the city has no religious orthodoxy enforced of any kind means it's one of the only places in the River Province (not counting Greyfalls) where the Immaculate Order has a monastery/missionary outpost, and Dynasts can do business openly - though obviously they'll have to have somehow gotten by Lookshy blockades to be there, but that's not hard for a small-group. The irony that the same reality that allows for their monastery allows for a Cult of the Illuminated Temple right under the nose of the 7th Legion (who have their own Immaculate Philosophy chapel-shrine, but unlike in the Order monastery it has no missionary agenda, it's just there for visiting Lookshyian forces for the most part) is not lost on the Immaculates.

        The City Father of Gen is a bit of a sniveling lap dog of the Court of Rivers, and in addition to acting as their go-between with the Merchant Princes, has a shrine at the major south-eastern (land-facing) trade gate, where he receives financial tribute from merchants as one of this supposedly government-free city's many unofficial taxes.

                                Page 78 of Scroll of Fallen Races says that Creation's underground is divided into a series of parallel faults beween the rock layers, miles apart. The majority of the Mountain Folk live on the first two faults, and expeditions rarely return from beyond the fifth. A handful have reached the ninth and returned with tales 'too lurid to be believed'.

                                The explanations for why there are so many strange and deadly creatures and phenomena in Creation's underground have always struck me as weak. I posit that the Faults are actually previous Creations with subtly different physical laws and boundary conditions. When the Primordials finished -or part finished- these Creations and found them wanting, they began anew, forming a new land directly on top of the previous one. Some of the bizarre creatures from these proto-Creations still thrive, adapting to incredibly hostile underworlds they were never meant to survive.

                                    She's dead, everyone assumes some cover up, some secrete ploy, but truth is she simply died and those few in the know are doing all they can to salvage their plans involving her

                                    EBON DRAGON
                                    Basically he's Starscream. Clever and traitorous but ultimately a coward that fears Malfeas to no end. Malfeas in turn is atleast aware of Ebon Dragon's desires if not his exact plans however he never stands in the way of his fellow Yozi's nature. Ebon Dragon knows he must tread lightly. For Malfeas can tolerate treachery but not incompetence.

                                    MALFEAS THE DEMON CITY
                                    The city is actually a massive sphere, like an onion. About the size of a planet buried in the desert, with only part of it exposed.

                                    DEATHLORDS
                                    I have greatly modified or altered most of them to the point I started an entire thread detailing my changes on the old forum. Basically the idea is they are unstable and very deeply occupy the uncanny valley. Eye and Seven Despairs should be the standard not the exception. Deathlords are ghosts warped by the Neverborn. They should be mentally disturbed, obsessed with passion plays, and exhibiting compulsions they themselves dont fully understand. Their logic alien to your complete mind, as ghosts are part of a soul not the whole thing. What should scare you most about a deathlord isn't that he can kill you but you can never be certain if he will. A slight that one day The Lion would kill you for without warning, the next he might completely ignore. A meeting with a Deathlord should be constant anxiety and uncertainty. No matter how good your position, no matter how illogical it would be for him to harm you, you should never be able to feel completely safe.

The majority of the Mountain Folk live on the first two faults, and expeditions rarely return from beyond the fifth. A handful have reached the ninth and returned with tales 'too lurid to be believed'.

                                            The explanations for why there are so many strange and deadly creatures and phenomena in Creation's underground have always struck me as weak. I posit that the Faults are actually previous Creations with subtly different physical laws and boundary conditions.                                             This was more or less what I came up with when fiddling with a "Compass of Terrestrial Directions: Below". I gave it up eventually because I never felt like I could get it thematically distinct enough from D&D's Underdark - BUT the basic concept for why there was atmospheres in caverns miles under Creation's surface, underground seas, burning magma, life and carbon fuel deposits, etc etc - is because the process of building Creation involved a lot of "painting over previous canvasses" if you will. The Elemental Dragons piled the elements atop older layers as the Primordials experimented. Some of the Darkbrood even have myths the "Before times" and how the world they dwelt under the sky in came to a catastrophic end.

                                            Also remember that Mount Meru expands downward and outward under the layers of sediment to eventually form the foundation of all Creation - so I'll additionally note that various buried palaces and laboratories of the Primordials and their Deva dotted the outside of the Mountain at various stages and now lay deeply buried (further out the deeper you go, obviously).
