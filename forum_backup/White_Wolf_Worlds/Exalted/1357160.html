    So, I got somewhat ambitious for this, and as a result, it's not actually finished. I figured it was best to post what I already had, and give Noodle something to start with. So far, I've got one member of the Circle fully written up, plus a general introduction to them, and some rules for resplendent destinies that I've cooked up. The other members of the Circle will follow. I'm using wastevens rules for Sidereal Martial Arts, from their Where Fate Has Led project, but I'm homebrewing the other Sidereal charms they have. This isn't because I don't like wastevens' work, but just because, like I said, I got ambitious. :-)

    Anyway, without further ado, here's the first part of the White Elephant gift, the introduction to the Circle:

    The Convention for Disputation
    In every large organization, there are misfits, members who can't quite get along with others. In some groups, these would eventually be let go or otherwise removed. But the Fivescore Fellowship of the Sidereal Exalted can ill-afford any rogue agents, and using more violent methods of removing malcontents risks losing the agent doing the job (since the opponent is, by definition, also a trained and Exalted individual), driving even more Sidereals away from the Bureau of Destiny (no one likes to hear that their colleague has been assassinated, even if you didn't like them much personally), and, of course, means that the Exaltation of the removed Vizier is now unavailable for twenty years or more.

    So, the Sidereals, when confronted with those among their number who just can't seem to play well with their institutional culture, have done the other thing that organizations have done with such people since time immemorial – threw them all together in a club, and hoped they would be too busy irritating each other to bother the membership at large. Over the centuries and millennia, this has mostly worked. Even if the Sidereals so siloed haven't always been very productive, it at least takes them out of every else's hair, and sometimes they accomplish something.

    Recently, however, the tactic has been working much better than usual. Or possibly much worse, the senior Sidereals aren't quite sure yet. For the last 50 years, the designated misfit Circle, informally dubbed the "Convention for Disputation", has actually worked quite well together, both socially and professionally. They may not necessarily like one another, or at least some are unwilling to openly say they do, but they can co-exist with one another without a fight breaking out, which is something that can't always be said about their interactions with other Viziers, and they have managed to accomplish several significant missions when working together that had stumped other members of the Fellowship. By now, the Contentious Convention, as even more irreverent Sidereals call them (it rhymes in Low Realm), have established themselves as more than just an ad hoc collection of Exalts who don't otherwise fit in – they are now friends and teammates, and would be willing to go to great lengths to protect one another. That such an unlikely Circle has formed, only a few decades before the beginning of the Time of Troubles and Age of Sorrows, worries the sort of Sidereal who thinks much of omens and portents. None of the Maidens have commented.

      Part 2: Rules for resplendent destinies!

      Resplendent Destinies
      The Sidereal Exalted can create resplendent destinies, false masks of Fate that convince onlookers that they are different people entirely. A Vizier working under a resplendent destiny is not subject to Arcane Fate, allowing them to suggest courses of action or to take actions themselves that will be remembered after they leave, albeit only as the person the destiny was depicted as, not their true nature. There are two types of resplendent destinies that Sidereals commonly use: temporary and lasting. These two forms have slightly different rules.

      Temporary Resplendent Destinies
      A temporary resplendent destiny is not a fully formed "person" – the persona the Vizier dons is not deep or complex with these short-term deceptions, instead relying on certain archetypes and stereotypes that have been encoded into the various constellations that make up the visible signs of Fate in Creation.
      Donning a temporary resplendent destiny is similar to a Disguise action. Any Sidereal can attempt it, manipulating the local strands of Fate to bend them into appropriate forms. It requires about 5 minutes, and involves some paraphernalia. However, it is rolled with (Intelligence + Occult), and the "tools" needed are not the false facial hair or makeup of a disguise kit, but rather the trappings of the persona the Sidereal wants to take on. If she has dots in the Ability linked to the constellation she is assuming, she adds automatic successes equal to her dots in the Ability to the roll.

      Each constellation has an associated archetype, with an assortment of trappings. For example, the Mast, in the House of Journeys, has an archetype of an infantry soldier, ditch-digger, scribe, and house servant, a solid worker who does their job with little conversation, and has trappings of being drably dressed, unspeaking, strong and honest, and ignorant. To assume a temporary resplendent destiny, a Vizier should have some or all of these trappings available. If none of the trappings are present, the difficulty of the roll to don the destiny is 5, dropping to 3 if one trapping is available, and to only 1 if most or all of the trappings are present. For abstract trappings, such as the Mast's requirement that the character be unspeaking or honest, the Sidereal must accept relevant Intimacies as part of donning the destiny. While she wears it, she must act as though she had such Intimacies with at least Minor intensity. Voluntarily giving up such intimacies or acting against them breaks the destiny immediately. If someone else uses social influence to decrease the intimacies' strength or remove them entirely, the Sidereal must resist as best she can, and if they are removed completely, she must either re-establish them at the beginning of the next scene, or accept the loss of the destiny.

      Once donned, a temporary resplendent destiny protects the Sidereal wearing it's true identity by presenting her as a simple example of the chosen archetype. Most people won't question the Sidereal's nature at all, simply accepting that she is who she appears as. Observers will remember what the Chosen of Destiny does while acting under the resplendent destiny, evading the drawbacks of Arcane Fate. If the Sidereal somehow breaks character or otherwise draws attention to herself in a way that contradicts the destiny, she risks discovery. An observer who witnesses such a lapse may make a (Perception + [Awareness or Integrity]) roll to see through the mask of Fate and perceive the true form beneath. The difficulty of this roll is equal to the number of successes the Sidereal rolled on her original (Intelligence + Occult) roll, minus the base difficulty of the original roll (minimum difficulty 1). If the onlooker overcomes the difficulty, he becomes aware that the Sidereal is not who she appears to be, with additional successes providing more details about which specific elements of the destiny are false. However, once the Sidereal's esoteric disguise has been penetrated, Arcane Fate once again takes hold – the individual who overcame the resplendent destiny, unless protected by magic capable of defeating it, will then forget that the Sidereal was there, losing track of their actions and presence shortly.

      Example: Tidir, Chosen of Journeys, wants to infiltrate a caravan she suspects is doing the work of the Mask of Winters. She decides that the simplest approach is to assume the form of a humble guard, and get hired. This is a resplendent destiny of the constellation of the Mast. Fortunately, the physical trappings are easily acquired – she carries a set of drab clothing for just such an occasion. She wants to be a bit more convincing, however, so she also accepts a pair of Minor Intimacies: "Better to say nothing and be thought a fool, than to open your mouth and remove all doubt" and "Honesty is the best policy". Throughout her deception, she will have to maintain these Intimacies at at least the Minor level, or the destiny will dissolve. The Storyteller declares that these are enough trappings to qualify for difficulty 1. She rolls (Intelligence + Occult), and gets 4 successes. Because Athletics is linked to the constellation of The Mast, and Tidir has 3 dots in Athletics, she receives 3 automatic successes as well, for a total of 7. Later, during her tenure as a humble caravan guard, if someone witnesses her acting out of character, they will have to overcome a difficulty of 6 (7 successes minus the basic difficulty of 1 for the roll) to penetrate her disguise.

      Lasting Resplendent Destinies
      Lasting resplendent destinies create personas with far more "reality" than temporary ones. Rather than manipulating local Fate, they involve weaving the false individual into the strands of the Loom of Fate in Heaven itself. Creation bends to accommodate the new individual into it, changing history and memories to suit. Since it requires manipulation of the Loom, creating lasting destinies is considerably more difficult for Sidereals without authorized access to it, such as ronins who have never been to Heaven, or renegades like Rakan Thulio who have had their authorization revoked.

      Creating a lasting resplendent destiny is an extended action with a terminus of 5, with each roll requiring a day. Each roll is a pool of (Intelligence + Occult), though Sidereals in good standing with the Bureau of Destiny may substitute a roll of (Intelligence + Bureaucracy) if that would be better. Sidereals without Loom authorization require a week per roll, instead of a day. The difficulty of the extended action depend on how integrated the resulting persona will be with Creation. A lasting resplendent destiny's integration is represented by various merits which it possesses, which the Sidereal benefits from as long as she maintains it.

      A resplendent destiny can have various merits attached to it to represent its social connections and people's knowledge of its existence. Such merits should be story merits, and represent connections to broad social groups, rather than individuals or equipment. They typically include Backing, Command, Contacts, Cult, Followers, Influence, and Resources. The base difficulty of the extended roll to create a resplendent destiny is equal to the dot level of the highest value merit associated with it, +1 for each additional merit of the same dot level or one less. Lesser merits don't generally add difficulty, though the Storyteller may decide that many smaller merits do effectively add up enough to increase the difficulty further.
      Any number of successes on the extended roll is technically sufficient to create a lasting resplendent destiny. However, more successes are valuable, as they extend the duration that the false persona will last before the Loom of Fate determines its unreality and deletes it from existence, leaving the Sidereal once again forgotten. A single success buys a resplendent destiny that lasts for a day. Two to five successes allows it to last for a week, while six to ten successes creates someone who will be a part of Creation for a month. Eleven to fifteen successes creates a destiny that will stay in Creation's mind for a season, while sixteen or more successes generates one that will last for a year. Longer-lasting destinies are possible, but only with the aid of charms or sorcerous workings. In addition to allowing a persona to be maintained longer, duration of a lasting resplendent destiny can be expended on certain effects, if the Sidereal has learned them in her studies of the relevant constellations.

      While lasting resplendent destinies are not the simple archetypes of temporary ones, each destiny should still be assigned to a constellation of the Loom, in order to track various effects. Once a destiny has been created, the Sidereal who made it can don it when they choose. This is a dramatic action that requires about a minute, typically involving putting on a few items of clothing or jewellery that match the trappings of the constellation the destiny falls under. Once the destiny is donned, it functions as a perfect disguise – no one who knows the Sidereal will recognize her as herself while under the destiny's effect, only seeing the persona she displays. This deception is impenetrable by mundane means. Attempts to overcome the disguise with charms can possibly succeed, but face a difficulty of 3+the Sidereal's dots in the Ability linked to the constellation the destiny falls under. This applies to any rolls to penetrate the deception, from (Perception + Awareness) rolls to notice inconsistencies in the Vizier's disguise, to (Intelligence + Bureaucracy) rolls to see through inconsistencies in her paperwork.

      Example: Tidir has determined that the caravan is definitely doing the bidding of the Mask of Winters, and needs to be stopped, and the caravan master questioned more closely. She can't do it all alone, but fortunately, she's near Nexus, and knows about a company of mercenaries who recently lost their commanding officer and are waiting for a new one. She decides she'll slip into that role, and begins crafting a resplendent destiny under the sign of The Captain, an ambitious young officer who intends to make a name for herself, commands the loyalty of her troops, and plans to march them hard. Being the officer for such a company requires three dots of Command, and 3-dot Backing to represent her position in the larger mercenary organization. She also decides to add 2 dots in Resources so she doesn't have to live in squalor during the mission. The difficulty of the extended roll will thus be 5 (3 for a 3-dot merit, +2 for two more merits of 2 or more dots). She begins crafting the destiny, rolling (Intelligence + Occult), and after 4 days garners 11 net successes. Determining this is sufficient, she approaches the mercenaries' camp, and announces herself as their new officer. The destiny ensures that the troops remember tales and rumors about this officer, and that her rank insignia, letter from their overall leader, and other proofs of her bona fides are accepted as genuine. She immediately orders the soldiers to pack up camp and be ready to march – they have a caravan to catch!

          Part 3: Tidir, Chosen of Journeys
          Tidir, Chosen of Journeys

          Background
          Tidir doesn't talk much of her upbringing, claiming it isn't relevant – the Southern tribe who raised her in an oasis on the edge of the Burning Sands are centuries dead, shattered by the upheavals of the Shogunate and then entirely wiped out by the Contagion and Balorian Crusade. She was Exalted by the Maiden of Journeys only a short decade before the Usurpation, and had just completed her training in Heaven when the great conspiracy was debated and then enacted.

          Always a pragmatic sort, she chose to follow the Vision of Bronze, agreeing that the Solars and Lunars had grown too powerful and arrogant for Creation's survival, and that they needed to be brought low, discounting the faint possibility of redeeming them offered by the Vision of Gold. In the next few centuries of the Shogunate, she continued to back the party line of the majority of Sidereals, supporting the Dragon-Blooded as they tried to hold Creation under their rule and pushed the surviving Lunars back to the margins and hidden refuges.
          But in the aftermath of the Contagion and Crusade, Tidir has shifted her perspective. The Dragon-Blooded, she feels, are no longer strong enough or numerous enough to hold the foes of Creation at bay, while the Lunars are now almost all of a younger generation, tempered into a mighty weapon that could defend the peoples of the world far more effectively, if they were not occupied battling the Realm. Her pragmatism now sets her at odds with old allies in the Vision of Bronze, believing that it is time to stop sending good Essence after bad and cease backing the Realm, instead allowing the Lunars or other forces an opportunity to rule. The breaking of the Jade Prison has only strengthened her conviction that it is time for a new order to arise, born in the struggle and conflict of the Age of Sorrows.

          Tidir is ruthlessly pragmatic, with little sentiment for any individual in Creation. The only thing that warms her old heart these days is the thought that a new group may soon rise to rule the world and perhaps make her life easier, resolving some of the myriad snarls in the Loom of Fate that have needed resolving since the fall of the Shogunate. She's always been rather grumpy, but this has escalated to full-on crotchetiness in the last few years, as her patience with other Sidereals, corrupt and foolish gods, and "whining" mortals has worn very thin. In particular, Chejop Kejak has earned her special ire and not a few open rants to other Sidereals, as she considers his ongoing support of the Realm to be fundamentally misguided, a matter of committing to choices long after history suggests they should be abandoned. As a result, her political pull among the Fivescore Fellowship has waned, despite her qualifying as an elder by most standards. She chairs no conventions, and mostly has to make do with the assignments given her. This only makes her more irritated.

          Despite her political disagreements with the Sidereal establishment, her position in the Bureau of Destiny as a whole is still secure. She holds official titles in both her "home" Division of Journeys, and in the Division of Battles (her attitude towards struggle and its benefits resonates as well with Mars as it does with Mercury). Additionally, she has overall, if more unofficial, influence with the Bureau as a whole, with a large number of favors owed her and secrets known that can convince various functionaries to listen when she speaks. She rarely uses this influence, but could potentially get some major projects going if she chose to support them.

          She works with the "Convention for Contention" because she grudgingly acknowledges their competence and the fact that she has trouble getting along with other Sidereals. But she doesn't particularly like them, and makes this opinion clear at every opportunity.

          Image
          Tidir is an elderly-appearing woman, perhaps in her late 60s or early 70s. Her hair is close-cropped and curly, and entirely white with age now. Her skin is the deep brown of many people in the South, though with piebald white patches, including a large one over one eye. Her wrinkled face is often twisted in a scowl, and only rarely shifts into a smile. Her iconic anima is a swirl of yellow that shapes itself into a vision of a long, long road through a desert of yellow sand, under a harsh yellow sun.

          Intimacies
          Defining: "Suffering builds character"; "I've built more than enough character already";
          Major: Chejop Kejak (Exasperation); Circlemates (Tolerance and acknowledgement of usefulness); Creation as a whole (Determination to protect);
          Minor: Mortals (Mild contempt); Mortals who endure suffering silently (Respect); "I deserve as easy a life as possible, within my duties."; Florivet, the Whim-of-the-Wind (Fondness mixed with suspicion)

          Secrets
          As an elder in the Fivescore Fellowship, Tidir knows many secrets, of Heaven, Creation, and stranger realms. When it comes to using them, she tends towards a light touch – she lets the people concerned know that she knows, and then simply casually drops suggestions for what she would like to see happen, rarely making any sort of overt connections between following her wishes and the preservation of her silence. She is also willing to give someone multiple chances if one attempt to leverage a secret fails. She will find new blackmail information, drop hints that she now knows more, and then repeat her mild suggestions about supporting her goals. However, if one or two iterations of this tactic don't bear fruit, the target will find all their dirty laundry suddenly exposed at once, with little indication of who could be revealing it. She rarely has to go this far.

          On her own account, Tidir has been involved in numerous Sidereal ploys over the years that the Bureau of Destiny and Heaven as a whole would probably not approve of, starting with the Usurpation. She takes care to not be the only agent involved in such activities, however, so that if they are revealed, she has others to share the blame with. Since the Contagion, however, she has been making small adjustments to the Loom from time to time, unauthorized by any higher authority. They haven't been tracked yet, but if they ever were, they would be found to never directly support Lunars, Exigents, Liminals, and other Exalts opposed to the Realm, but often to push useful minions or allies into their path, or ensure that helpful artifacts, spells, or other supernatural resources make their way to places where they could be claimed.

          After the Jade Prison was opened, Tidir was one of the very first Sidereals to suggest that now was the time to begin grooming the returning Solars into a better, more stable path that would help ensure the safety of Creation, rather than threaten it with grandiose conflicts. Her opponents among the Sidereals, particularly Chejop Kejak, wonder if the speed she reacted with was more than simply being unusually willing to quickly change position, and not aided by foreknowledge of events…

          Equipment
          Tidir usually wears robes and an overlayer, if necessary for the climate she finds herself in. Her clothing is simple and unadorned, but this is deceptive – she makes a point of wearing finely spun, expertly tailored garments that are as comfortable as possible for her in all circumstances. She often wears an amber pendant concealed beneath her robes, with a scorpion encased within it. Often, her only weapon is a walking stick, though anyone attacking her will find it is harder and tougher than it appears (effectively a baton).

          Roleplaying Hints
          Tidir is very interested in finding Exalted who can be relied on to defend Creation as a whole, and to work together with others to accomplish this. In order to find such individuals, she tracks down rumors of Lunars, Solars, and other sorts that the Realm wouldn't necessarily approve of, and quietly observes them for several days or weeks (using a variety of short-term resplendent destines to avoid being noticed as the same person). If she thinks someone has potential to serve her goals, she then subjects them to trials of her own devising, usually involving travel of some sort, which test their resolve and decision-making skills. If these trials are passed, she will begin providing covert aid to the targets, though she almost never directly uses Sidereal charms or astrology on them, instead arranging circumstances so that they have opportunities to benefit, if they see the possibilities and seize the moment appropriately.

          Personally, she is very, very grouchy. She has very little patience for what she sees as foolishness, and no fear of expressing her irritation. Her tongue is razor-sharp. If one of her potential targets for assistance manages to anger her, she sees no contradiction between her goals and putting every bit of bad luck and disaster possible into his path – suffering, after all, builds character, and if he survives the gauntlet, he will emerge stronger and even better-suited to her purposes after all.

          Despite her willingness to subject others to discomfort and danger to prove themselves, she avoids such things as much as possible in her own life. She shamelessly uses her own Sidereal powers and sorcery to travel quickly and painlessly, ensure her own comforts, and otherwise make her life more pleasant.

          Excellencies
          Add up to (Essence) dice to a Dodge, Integrity, Larceny, Linguistics, Occult, Resistance, Ride, Sail, Survival, or Thrown roll at a cost of one mote per die.           Life Gets Worse Approach (3m; Supplemental; One scene; Decisive-Only): If a thrown weapon inflicts any health levels of damage, it becomes stuck in the wound (treat this as a crippling injury). After this, every time someone misses with an Archery or Thrown attack within Medium range of the target (either the attacker or the original target of the attack can be within Medium), Tidir may reflexively spend 2 additional motes to cause the attacker to re-roll their attack (all charms and other effects used for the original attack still apply) to instead attack the target of this charm. Tidir gets 1 initiative, plus any bonus initiative if the attack crashes the target, but no additional initiative.

This does not apply in crash.

          Someone Else's Destiny (4m; Reflexive; Indefinite): At the moment she is affected by a drug, poison, disease, or other effect that calls for a (Stamina+Resistance) roll to mitigate, activate this charm. The substance or condition has no effect. Later, as a special gambit with difficulty 4, Tidir may transfer the effect to another target. The target gets any (Stamina+Resistance) roll that would have applied at the time she originally suffered the effect, and experiences the effect just as if they had been the original target. Tidir can only hold one effect in abeyance this way at once – if she uses this charm to resist another effect before the original is transferred, it returns and affects her instead.

          Storm's Eye Stance (10m, 1 wp, +3m, 3i per target; Simple; One Scene; Decisive-Only, Perilous): Requires a prayer strip (can be attacked as a Difficulty 5 gambit, success destroys and ends this charm). Once this charm is active, whenever a decisive attack is made against her, Tidir may choose targets she can see, up to long range, reflexively spending 3m and 3i per target, after damage has been rolled but before it has been applied. Divide the health levels inflicted equally between Tidir and all targets (round health levels inflicted up). This effect cannot be used in crash.

This doesn't use up her reflexive movement, and doesn't require disengage rolls if she moves away from other foes.

          Glory Path (8m; Simple; One Scene): Roll 5 dice. For every success, the mount currently being ridden gains +1 to its Speed bonus. The mount can now cross difficult terrain without spending extra movement, and can even run over 1 range band of empty space without falling, as long as the difficult terrain or empty space are natural, not created by humans or other intelligent creatures.

          Yellow Path (2m; Simple; One Journey): Declare a destination and roll 5 dice (in combat, a destination must be a location, not "an enemy"). For one success, gain +2 non-charm dice to all rolls required to move towards the destination. With 3 successes, reflexive movement also moves 2 range bands rather than one, and any non-obvious barriers (hidden pits, invisible force barriers, etc.) that would obstruct the path are revealed to Tidir. With 5 successes, a miraculous route is revealed that takes only a single reflexive movement to cross any number of range bands. Out of combat, this charm reduces a journey to 3/4ths normal for 1 success, reveals barriers and reduces journey time to ½ on 3 successes, or reduces journey time to only a single hour for 5 successes (or a single day if the journey would normally take a week or more).

          Social Charms
          Death-of-Self Meditation (2m; Reflexive; One Day): When entering a decision point to resist successful social influence, or when subject to a psyche effect that allows spending wp to resist, Tidir may instead choose to adopt a new personality that would find the original influence or command unacceptable. Any intimacies that supported the original influence are totally supressed, and she gains a new Defining intimacy opposed to the influence or psyche effect that would have affected her. She automatically rejects the influence or psyche effect without spending any wp. The new personality lasts the entire day, and this charm cannot be ended early.

If she forgoes this wp gain and instead spends 1 wp, double her mote regeneration in the next hour (or at the end of the round, while in combat).

          Sorcery
          Tidir is initiated into sorcery at the Sapphire Circle level, via a now-lost initiation. Her two shaping rituals are as follows:
          Walking the Earth: Whenever she visits a location that she has never seen before, or one that has changed enough to be completely unfamiliar (this usually requires at least a century, though it can be longer in particularly unchanging locales), roll 8 dice. For every success, gain one sm that lasts for the rest of the story. She can hold no more than 50 sm this way.
          Traveller's Courtesy: When she begins shaping a spell that will aid a traveller to reach a declared destination, immediately gain 4 sm that can only be spent on the spell.

          Allies and Associates
          Tidir maintains virtually no mortal associations. Certainly none in her true identity as a Chosen of Journeys – her Arcane Fate makes that virtually impossible – but she also doesn't bother much these days to craft the long-term resplendent destinies that solid associations could be attached to. Mortal lives are just too brief to be worth investing the time in, from the perspective of someone who has seen multiple millennia pass. When she does need a long-term resplendent destiny to accomplish an assignment or one of her own goals, she deliberately sets it up to not involve much socializing.

          She uses her sorcery extensively to ensure she always has a retinue of beings at her beck and call, ready to support her in various endeavours. She usually has access to enough combat-ready demons to form a Size 3 battlegroup (typically some variety of anhule, baidaks, or tinsiana), a peronelle or two for defense, an aalu or naneke for a secretary, and an angyalkae or other artistic demon to provide entertainment. She summons other First Circle demons to meet specific needs. If she anticipates a specific need, she summons a 2nd Circle demon with appropriate skills, such as a general like Octavian or Sigereth to command her lesser demons when she intends to fight a large battle. She doesn't generally trust 2nd Circle demons and mostly maintains a strict distance from them, but she has grown somewhat fond of Florivet, the Whim-of-the-Wind, having had cause to summon him multiple times over the centuries, and enjoying his hedonistic approach in spite of herself. She hasn't yet summoned him simply for companionship, but that day might come.
