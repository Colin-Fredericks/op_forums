    Before Creation, the only order was that imposed by the entities that willed things into being. Princes of Chaos - unshaped monsters and worse - fluctuated in power from below the smallest mite to towering above He-Who-Would-Become-Malfeas himself. At nearly random.

    The Primordials were ordered beings, comparatively. Their world-jouten had physical laws and consistent cause and effect...at least as far as the Primordials willed it. Their created beings were native to the world, and worshipped the second and third circle Devas as gods.

    Xen Mu, the first playground of the Primordials, was created from their cooperation at the behest of their bodiless King. Yet, the fae wyld energies of the Princes of Chaos could inpinge and encroach. The weak ones could be kept out, but the wyld power of the great ones warped the rules around any defenses. The Primordials could combat them with order and will and law and rule, but the Princes always had those who could cheat. Change or ignore the rules so that they would win, would kick over the Primordials' sand castles and trample them to mud. Indignity of indignities, it was always possible that the Primordials might have to flee.

    The Primordials - rightly or wrongly - felt like the small kids on the block. It didn't matter that the Chaos Lords were, by and large, no stronger than the created denizens of their world-jouten. The few that erupted to great might were terrible threats, and there was ALWAYS such a being out there, if not multiple. It doesn't matter that the Primordials maitain their power level consistently, while the Princes of Chaos could spend most of their individual time as the weakest of the weak, only spiking to power over the Primordials occasionally; there always is at least one Prince of Chaos out there that is greater than they.

    So the Primordials determined to make something that was ordered and rooted in rules, which could NOT permit cheating.

    They built the Games of Divinity. The Games have profound echoes of law, rooted in the carefully-selected shinma which underpin Creation. It is impossible to cheat; it is impossible to play it other than by the rules. But because of this, everything done in the Games is of real consequence. It is caused by your choice and action, and its effects cannot be undone by anything but another play. The rules are complex and rich and deep, but they cannot be violated.

    These rules enforce themselves by preventing any protoshinmaic alterations to local reality which would violate them. The games were designed to be played by the Primordials, and so they are the greatest players thereof. The Fae were invited to play, but most hated them, because they could lose by something other than their own rules. They couldn't alter the rules to let them win. And yet, the sheer consequence of each move, that it matters because it cannot be washed away, fascinates them as much as it horrifies them.

    This utter, supreme consequence of each move is the source of the heady, addictive sense of power that comes with holding a Game piece and making a move.

    So powerful a device requires tremendous power and maintenance; this is what Creation and Yu Shan exist to provide.


    Now, the Unconquered Sun and the other Incarna are truly amongst the greatest creations of the Primordials, and they CAN play the Games. But they are not for whom the Games were designed, so even if the Sun might play better than He-Who-Would-Become-Malfeas himself, he is still not the being for whom the Games were designed, and thus unintended effects impact him.

    The Games remain the central purpose of Creation, and at the same time the Games' existence ensure Creation's stability against the Wyld. Creation powers them, but the Games enforce the rules which permit Creation to declare to the Chaos that none shall enter with arbitrary power.

    The Games truly are important, because they create lasting consequence. In a sense, playing a move of the Games of Divinity is more important than anything else one can do...but still, other things are essential to maintain and power them. The Games are the most essential, important thing one can do...but they are not enough alone to preserve and grow that which is important.

                        In the period of time just after the Primordial War but just before the First Age, the Eclipse and Journeys Caste went and bound all of the various geography gods in to an agreement that would allow for special paths that could be taken through their domains. These paths would go out of their way to make the journey much faster on the traveler, with things like "always on a downward slope" and "the wind constantly at your back" being just a few of a dozen small accelerants that add up. It's possible that with proper planning and offerings made to the right gods, you can go from one end of Creation to another in the span of a few days.

                        During the First Age advancements in travel technology made these paths mostly obsolete, but they still exist in the Age of Sorrow for any Exalt learned enough to rediscover them.

                                            I stole my headcanon on the Emissary from game someone posted about on the old forums a long long time ago.

                                            He is a ghost. Not a deathlord (probably), just a ghost. Technically.

                                            See, he's the ghost of Thrice-Damned Gorol, and the prototype version of the Akumatization process, applied by the Demon Emperor himself, was powerful enough to warp and control and empower him even beyond death. This means that although he is a ghost, he is an Essence 9-10 ghost, with Malfeas Charms, Sapphire (and a very small amount of Adamant) Sorcery, an anima banner mixing the powers of Night and Slayer, and full mastery of both Solar Hero and Infernal Monster Styles, including the truly ridiculous number of expansions he developed for both. And that's not counting whatever he's picked up after he died.

                                            Why is the First Akuma protecting and guiding Nexus? Think about it. What place in all Creation is more similar to the Demon City him/itself... what place would be easier to nudge into merging with the Emissary's true master, and give him just the tiniest toe-hold in Creation...
