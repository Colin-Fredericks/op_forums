    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
    The average legion has eleven Dragon Blooded in total.

    I have to reread the Thousand Correct Actions and see if the Realm organizational doctrine allows for high ranking officers to serve certain other roles, but even so...

    I wouldn't think it's much of a stretch for a legion deployed for war to typically have another dozen Terrestrials to serve vital auxilliary roles. In my mind, they're not quite formal units in the military organization (but then, neither are the rank and file; most of their cavalry, archers and skirmishers, say, or drawn from subject populations, even probably sometimes mercenaries; recruits and commissions are in the medium and heavy infantry phalanxes, which form the heart of their tactical doctrine). Like, I've said before how I think most retired Dynasts have engaged in some form of military service at one time or another even when it's not the focus of their career and lifestyle, I imagine that most engage in such by lending their actual talents to various support roles (with an understanding that they'll possibly get in a scrap while on the march, or if a camp is struck during a flanking action, or the whole legion is overrun a bit). It's a bit of a voluntary thing; like, Dynasts who hear that a legion is being deployed for war will usually volunteer their support services for the prestige or adventure, and when they don't there'll be somebody in the legion command structure tasked to go recruiting.

    But they're not a permanent fixture, not career soldiers.

    EDIT: Oh, I meant to say, part of my attitude about specialists is that I wouldn't want it to imply that mortal experts don't have their value to Dragon Blooded commanders, including them being in their own positions of command.
    Neither would I. The dynastic Lunar I mentioned on this thread was a heroic mortal talonlord before Exalation, and most of his family are in legion posts. So this is something I've thought about a lot, and I would never say mortal soldiers don't have value in Realm legions when obviously they do; they're most of the legion's strength and skill. So no problem agreeing with you there. But just on the basis of arithmetic, I think it's better to just take what the books say about the number of DBs in a legion and throw it in the garbage. Because - at least in the more martial Houses - if there are 11 Dragon-Bloods in a legion, and maybe 60 or 70 in all the family's legions combined, then what the heck are all the rest of them doing?

                    Originally posted by TSk View Post
                    Because - at least in the more martial Houses - if there are 11 Dragon-Bloods in a legion, and maybe 60 or 70 in all the family's legions combined, then what the heck are all the rest of them doing?
                    I'd first think the more martial Houses are a bit smaller than the other ones; Cathak particularly is described as having a problem with their members dying glorious deaths.

                    I'd say that when it comes to the more martial Houses, they provide the majority of career officers for the Imperial legions, but the majority of actual House members prefer to go off on their own military ventures, where they can be their own bosses and fight for their own glories rather than the advantages of the state. They take relatively small companies of of the family troops around to pillage beyond the edge of the Threshold or act as elite troops in foreign wars, in contrast to typical House legions who basically just do security detail on the collection of tribute.

                    Then this makes for House legions that are a bit too elite and experienced, and need to be taken down a peg.

                    EDIT: Wait, I think I came up with the numbers for this once...

                    If I assume that it's reasonable for about a thousand Exalted to typically serve in the legions at once, then that means you can get 20 legions with the usual complement of eleven Exalted, four particularly elite ones with sixty, and the remaining eight are somewhere in between, varying according to legion...

                    Then if it's about... Cathak, Tepet and Sesus are liable to provide 250 each at any one time, the other Houses make up a hundred between them, and the rest are Found Eggs who accepted the coin...

                    If you average about a thousand per House, but assume that the militant ones are a bit smaller, then that means ones serving in the Imperial legions make up better than a quarter of all members of the House? Combine that with assumptions that many of the others engage in more private enterprise forms of military venture, and that not every member of those Houses can be a soldier, and...

                    I think that's consistent, at least.
