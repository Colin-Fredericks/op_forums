                                                Arms of the Chosen inspired in me an idea for a moonsilver ring that would let Lunar Exalted use the Heart's Blood of living victims against them in a way that would be utterly broken if it were done through Lunar Charms.

                                                It amuses me that my other idea, a Warstrider that borrows Lunar's Heart-Blood for shapeshifting into behemoths, represents the complete opposite side of the scale. One tiny artifact and one huge artifact.

                                                A perfect Changing Moon and Full Moon pair of artifacts, really.

                                                The No Moon artifact would be one that takes advantage of moonsilver's sorcerous potentials; a sorcerous phylactery geared for spells that transform targets.

                                                I don't know what physical shape this artifact would take, but I like the idea of a Lunar using it to countermagic a spell to turn someone into an animal, and then using the stolen motes and an Evocation to turn around and curse the other sorceror into an animal's shape. Or to use the motes to fuel her own Heart's Blood transformations.

                                                    If I ever get a chance to play another Supernal Thrown character (I probably won't, they're silly), I'll take the Frost-Thorn Knuckles on them. They're fantastically mean, and I eagerly anticipate the look on the Storyteller's face when he or she realizes that wound penalties, Joint-Wounding Attack, and Glacial Beast Claw has reduced an antagonist's Dexterity + Dodge pool to zero.

                                                    Unfortunately, I'm not sure what to make of Ravening Fimbulwinter Approach. It's a neat charm, but it treats the results of a rush roll as a reflexive attack in a way that seems to be unprecedented. Here's an example, as I understand it. The wielder of the Frost-Thorn Knuckles rushes an enemy and rolls seven successes to their three successes. They activate Ravening Fimbulwinter Approach, and attack (unrolled) with the four remaining successes. If this exceeds or matches the defender's Defense, they roll the relevant decisive damage dice.

                                                    The ambiguity comes from adjudication of the defender's charm use. When does the defending character declare parry or evasion enhancers? Do they have to declare charms like Dipping Swallow Defense or Dodge Excellency during the initial rush, or can they spend essence after the rush is successful? If it's the latter, they receive the benefit of being able to spend precisely enough essence to avoid the attack. If it's the former, they're forced to spend essence defending against an attack that might never happen. What's the correct play here?
