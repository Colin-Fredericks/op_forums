                  I ran an extensive Dragon Blooded game that took about 3 years to finish and had numerous timeskips and arcs. It started with the characters in Secondary School, and then involved a 20 year time skip, an 80 year timeskip and finally a 150 year timeskip ("We've got to get the gang back together to handle this big threat...")

                  The first thing I did for the Secondary School game was come up with a large cast of NPCs. One of the issues is that with a school setting you are going to have a lot of characters (all of the various classmates), and the players are basically stuck in one place for a very long period of time. It's not like the players can decide to just pack up and head to Nexus one day. As a result, it helps to have a fairly strong, vibrant cast of characters to serve as the players classmates, rivals, members of other classes, teachers, etc. That was probably the most work, coming up with about 30 different NPCs with vague background stories that I would be able to use and introduce as the campaign went on.

                  Fortunately there are plenty of "high school" focused movies, games, anime shows, novels, etc that it shouldn't be too hard to crib for ideas and then come up with different names and alter a few details of the character stories. My campaign took place in the Heptagram is probably the easiest one to work with. It's basically Exalted Harry Potter, so younger people would already have a pretty good idea of where to slot into it.

                  When I began the campaign I first came up with the "end game." The major endgame plot was that one of the students was working with an Abyssal (his older sister who had been sacrificed as part of a magical ritual and both them carried a grudge). Their goal was to open a massive shadowland on the Heptagram, bring in an army of warghosts and murder everyone. In order to open the Shadowland he needed to get access to various books, destroy some number of magical warding sigils and perform a few rituals. I decided it would take him just until the PC characters were getting ready to graduate before he could pull everything off (and all together I was planning to have the Secondary School part of the campaign last about 6-8 months in real life).

                  Then I came up with about a dozen or so different mini-arcs (some comedic, some serious) that would serve to help fill out smaller stories that would help fill sessions and give the players a bit more time exploring the various inhabitants of the Heptagram as well as their instructors and classmates. I tried to occasionally have those stories brush up against the major plotline so that the players would get a feeling that there was something big going on, but without having any ideas to what it was.

                          Originally posted by Daredevil View Post

                          Could I ask how you run the PC's family & the type of antagonists you throw at them?
                          What I did was focus on events almost day by day. They had a number of plots they were simultaneously dealing with. On day 7, they might deal with plot items 1, 2 & 5, then on day 8 (a seperate adventure) they would deal with plot items 3 & 4 & 5. Then there would be some time down time, and events would become "hot" again and move quickly. They dealt with Technocrats trying to take over their school and the goths being infiltrated by Nephandi. (Very 90s!) They came to attention of a local vampire and had to hunt them down and kill them. I ran the game 20 years ago and don't remember all of it anymore. I didn't do much with the parents or teachers.

                          I more recently found the M&M Heroes High supplement and quite liked it and thought "I should have had this for that game."

                                      This thread is very interesting ! I'm planning to launch a secondary school game next month myself, so feel free to have a look at my campaign wiki over there.

                                      For the Blue Tree (a secondary school I wrote) I've got a map (currently being done), timetables and a full curriculum for the students, clubs, and most importantly a full cast of characters, (the teachers already appear on the wiki, if without their description for the moment, and the first year students should be up soonish). Each of them or nearly so have a plot idea attached*, and I'll see what the players grab onto to develop the subplots. I think that's the best way of playing a game in a "closed" system. Start with the cast and see what interactions pick up naturally with the players.

                                      The main plots for the first year will include a secret demonologist planning a murder on the school grounds, and an interschool gateway tournament at the end of the year, and all the secondary plots around lessons and school life will make up the rest of play.

                                      *For exemple, one of the teacher is a former senator of the Greater Chamber, who lost her seat when she pressed her hand on her abolitionist beliefs, and might try to push that part of her ideals onto her students, one of the students is already being seen as a potential ideal marriage prospect for those who wish to have a powerful political alliance with his own ideas about it, etc...
