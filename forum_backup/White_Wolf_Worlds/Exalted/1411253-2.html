    Mortal martial artists, meaning "people who can perform Martial Arts Charms," do not and cannot exist in the current edition of Exalted. Period. Therefore, "Martial Artist" is a Merit of no value for mortals. Mortal practitioners of the forms of various styles can and do exist, but do not and cannot have Charms; in mechanical terms, they have specialties and stunt like crazy.Thus, the Martial Artist merit, the sole purpose of which is to grant access to Martial Arts Charms, is of no value whatsoever to mortals.

                        Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
                        Often Wuxia is about demonstrating the superiority of the protagonist's signature style ("Japanese karate is no match for my kung fu!")
                        Not.. really? While "dojo rivalries" are certainly part of Wuxia, Wuxia generally focuses on that to set up a villain's motivations ("Our sifu wouldn't teach me the ultimate secrets of his kung fu, but I found a new sifu...") than the straight up style-offs of modern martial arts action movies.

                        Wuxia wouldn't have genre tropes around the heroes earning permission to learn from new masters to expand their training if it wasn't something the genre supported happening: it's more about those who do it with respect and humility vs. those that do it for lesser reasons. Wuxia is about the triumph of heroes because living well is more powerful than mastering techniques, more than it is about proving which styles are superior.

                                    I'm not sure the question can be answered directly, esp. as there's other options.

                                    Frankly, if I'm looking at making a character that's going to invest in MA, I want all of the following, in no particular order and in variable levels of focus depending on the character and game:

                                    - Yes, to be more powerful, even if through versatility over raw power. I don't like it when games make you pay XP/creation points just to add flair. Buying Charms is about making a character more powerful on a fundamental design level.

                                    - Yes, the appeal of MA is increasing your options in combat, letting you have more options than "I hit better/harder." That's part of the appeal of MA in that they frequently cross into things like Stealth, Athletics, Resistance, and so on: you get a lot more things out of one investment instead of having to branch out into lots of different Abilities.

                                    - Another appeal of MA is built in combat approaches. MAs can be fun because they're really good in some situations and really bad in other, letting you be really awesome without the lack of weaknesses expanding your native Charms does.

                                    - Another appeal is flavor outside your native Charms. This doesn't invalidate my first listed point, but as noted above, making a character with a specific theme is frequently better supported in combat by MA than general charms .
