Since it's converting non-charm dice into successes, I've seen a lot of people decry it as useless, because the automatic successes would count as two dice added by a charm towards your limit, but I noticed this:

                        Does this mean that the successes count as one charm dice per success, or is it like other auto-successes and count as 2?
                        Originally posted by Exalted 3rd Edition, pp.251
                        Unless specified to be non-Charm successes, treat each success added by a Charm as two dice for the purpose of dice-adding limitations.
                        The rules only specify that adding an automatic success counts as two dice toward the cap, so I would say each converted die counts as one Charm die per success; you're reducing the size of your maximum potential dice pool to guarantee a degree of success. Otherwise the Charm really is useless, since the same 4m could be used for four extra dice through an Excellency on top of your Appearance bonus, which might not be worth 4m to begin with.

                        Originally posted by Leetsepeak View Post
                        Another question: If I have a Yasal Crystal, and I have a demon or a god inside of it, can I use their power to dematerialize?
                        Originally posted by Exalted 3rd Edition, pp.601
                        Instead, whoever touches the stone can use a captive spirit’s powers as if they were his own, if the holder of the stone has the spirit’s permission.
                        It looks like the RAW answer would be yes, but look over the character profiles carefully: most spirits have the ability to materialize from a dematerialized state. The only one with a dematerialize power as written is Fakharu (pp.523), and he's definitely not going to fit into your standard yasal crystal. In general, though, I think any power written in their profile is fair game.

                                                08-22-2016, 08:59 AM
                                                Leetsespeak suggested earlier that breaking an object could be handled as an extended action. It doesn't really make sense to perform a withering attack on it (any more than it does to attack one's allies) because those represent gaining leverage over the target. Breaking an object can be a more involved endeavor, and oftentimes serves as its own reward (breaking open an escape route, collapsing an enemy fortification, etc.). In terms of mechanical precedence, Sledgehammer Fist Punch supplements an attack on a object that is neither withering nor decisive, so your ST can use that as a guideline. Alternatively, your ST might just decide that it's a special kind of gambit with a difficulty equal to that of an equivalent feat of strength.

                                                I don't have access to the backer Charms, but a quick look at the core book revealed this:

                                                Originally posted by Exalted Third Edition, pp.562
                                                Pack Hunting: When the hound or wolf attacks an enemy, it adds one automatic success to the attack roll for each allied pack member in close range to that enemy, to a maximum of three successes.
                                                Emphasis added. Given that a battle group of wolves would already get bonuses for size, it's up to the ST to decide whether pack hunting is redundant. It might be that wolf battle groups would get the bonus when attacking with other wolf battle groups, or they might get the maximum of three bonus successes automatically as long as their Size stays above 1. I'd lean toward the former, personally, to keep the scale consistent; the latter seems particularly unbalanced.

                                                Going back to your previous question about War Charms, most are probably compatible, but it's difficult to make a blanket ruling, especially without knowing the specifics of that battle group Charm to begin with.
