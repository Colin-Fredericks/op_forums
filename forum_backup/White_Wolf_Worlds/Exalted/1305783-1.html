                            Nope. Love the fictional setting, interested in how the system plays out and like talking about both (subject to constraints on my understanding how the system plays out!), but play Ex3 I do not.

                            (This probably comes through in my preferences in discussions for "Characters as compelling fictional characters and the setting as an compelling fictional place for an intelligent, well read fantasy audience that knows a thing or two about real world history and economics, and mythology " rather than "A setting that facilitates adventure and characters that can fit being played and STd around a game table and have it be fun and not degenerate into a toxic situation". Probably a useful disclaimer on some of what I post!).

                            Re; Exalted popularity, my opinion is pretty much that the popularity was gonna decline with the popularity of the main influences that were about when Ex1 was first published, as geek culture moved back towards more Anglo-American (though not necessarily Tolkien-esque) fantasy and sci-fi, and to Marvel comics. (Like, I'd go on a tangent here and argue about how the '90s jrpg and anime and wuxia influences, and their parent cultural influences, in Exalted are more than just surface influences and substantial to how the setting is politically and substantially construed and constructed and have most appeal within a base of familiarity with that (which is less common in the marketplace since the mid '00s). But that would be a major tangent.

                                I think it's a very good point though. Maybe that's a bigger point than the lack of printed books (which is an issue for many games).


                                I was looking at this thread on RPGnet: https://forum.rpg.net/index.php?thre...n-2019.840578/

                                There's a lot of games mentioned (so you can also see the explosion in games available), and it seems there's quite a few games that are quite popular which I'm not really familiar with, as they're fairly new, like Coriolis, Savage Worlds and Masks.

                                But looking at the games, they do mostly seem to be DnD-fantasy clones (different rules, but a similar aesthetic) or sci-fi, and a bit of horror and superheroes*. And in horror games, Vampire is still one of the big ones.
                                And there are other Onyx Path games mentioned, like Pugmire.

                                *Which maybe is unsurprising, considering the Marvel films, as you mentioned.

                                Exalted is barely mentioned (it comes up a bit on RPG.net's discussions, but not a ton), but neither is Legends of the Wulin, Anima, and other Asian-inspired games. Even L5R isn't nearly as popular as it used to be, when I put "L5R RPG" into google trends, it's searched for less than 25% of the amount it was searched for in 2005, and that's despite recently releasing a new edition and having a popular card game advertising it.

                                DnD's never tried doing another Oriental Adventures since 3rd ed, and none of the popular settings are East Asia-inspired. So presumably they don't see a market.

                                So I think you're probably right Ghosthead, the interest in Anime/Wuxia-fantasy rpgs is probably much less than it was back when Exalted 1st ed was released.

                                My group of players, who have an Exalted game every Sunday, include two people who've lived in China (me for 4 years, another guy for 1 year but also his wife is Chinese), and some big anime fans (they're currently watching every Gundam series in order). We used to often hang out and watch Kung-fu or East Asian historical action films, most of us have read the Chinese classics (Romance of the Three Kingdoms, Outlaws of the Water Margin, etc).

                                Whereas, despite some of my DnD group being into anime, none of them have ever said "hey, we should play an anime-inspired game". Nor are any of them into Wuxia. And I don't think any of the games being run in my city's rpg club are anime-esque. Dunno why.


                                Obviously, Exalted has moved slightly away from anime towards classical Graeco-Roman myth (swords and sandals stuff), but that's not a genre that seems really big either.
