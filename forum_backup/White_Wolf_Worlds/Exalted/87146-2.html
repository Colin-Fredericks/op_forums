        I generally agree with this, and the reason I'm constantly irritated at the Great Curse as a setting element is how it's a slap in the face to that idea. Though even if Solars aren't meant to be People Who Set Things Right and are instead people who make human mistakes with superhuman power, it would still go against that thesis by absolving them of guilt for their mistakes. Thankfully the 3E previewed charms for Solars majorly toned this down, so I have hope that the rest of the Solar presentation will do a lot to help alleviate my distaste for them.

                                            Originally posted by UltraFennec View Post
                                            I'm advocating that for the purposes of the setting and making the game unique, that making the Solars inherently 'good' in some way detracts from the experience.
                                            Lunars, as a group, are driven to protect Creation. Some do that by fighting Wyld-spawn, some do it by striving to strengthen societies, some do it by trying to put the Solars back in charge, etc.
                                            Sidereals, as a group, are driven to uphold Creation. Some think that's best done by keeping Solars from fucking it up, some think that's best done by supporting the Solars and making Creation a place of virtue and strength, and some think that's best done by addressing threats to Creation directly.

                                            Do these default group characterisations detract from the setting, or from Lunar and Sidereal gameplay? Neither of these types of Exalt is Chosen for their "goodness", and yet both seem to have a predisposition toward altruistic behavior. If that doesn't detract from the experience, I don't see why it should be a problem for Solars to also take the "good by default" characterisation.

                                            Come to think of it, how could the original Exalted Host possibly have beaten the Primordials if they weren't "good" in some sense the Incarnae understand? There is zero reference to Celestials of the Primordial War era defecting to the Primordials' side. Why? Surely there was some opportunity presented to them. If free will is encoded into Exaltation, and the Incarnae wanted the Exalted to fight for their side only, then wouldn't it be an intrinsic part of the process of Exaltation that it's only awarded to people who are at least initially motivated to bring about the vision the Incarnae had for Creation?
