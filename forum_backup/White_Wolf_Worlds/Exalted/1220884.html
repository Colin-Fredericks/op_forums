          What kind of elements are they to represent?

          IMO elementals - especially low level ones - are just marginally more sentient than a magical golem. They are living forces of nature after all... its first if they reach maybe essence 3 that they would reach full sentience.

          While essence 2, it would be on par with a wild animal - but with a twist: it would be an 'animal' expression of the element they're embodying.

          So... water elementals of a raging river with strong currents? strong but mindless wall bulls that seem impossible to physically stop. summoning a couple of these and chaining them to a ship with sturdy black jade-steel chains can allow for a ship to be pulled along as if flowing downstream in a river, anywhere in the water.

          wood elementals that effect plant-growth in a forest? Their level of activity would depend on how much wood essence is in the area for them to work with - they could seem very dormant if operating in a shrub desert, where plants grow slowly - or be manic buzzing bees in the east, making things grow all the time. In the realm these are constantly summoned to improve farm yields.

          their bodies would be informed by their purpose and their element - of course - so an earth elemental that consumes fallen leaves and whatnot to produce dirt would be a wolf-sized earth-worm that just mindlessly and constantly gobbles up organic matter and poops out highly fertile dirt. (never mind that a lot of sorcerers summon these things to do away with corpses quickly)

              Cloud People are Essence 2 and they seem fully sentient, quite smart in fact. In the Core, Tidemares (Essence 2) are capable of speech but they're not too bright and tend towards inane gossip. Meanwhile, young Greenmaws (Essence 3) are much more feral. I think elementals are probably less likely to be of animal-level intelligence the higher their Essence rating, but it isn't absolute.

              As for how to come up with cool elementals, a few suggested methods...

              1) If you're better at coming up with interesting characters than magical species, try to create an interesting character who is an elemental, and reverse engineer their species from there, taking into consideration how they embody or differ from each aspect of their species archetype so that you don't create an entire race of "this guy".

              For my young Zenith sorcerer, I came up with an earth elemental companion called Aiax (that's his given name, not his species name). He's a small pangolin made of rough stone, and can burrow through earth as fast as a fish can swim. He can roll into a ball and become much more durable, but can't attack and can only move by rolling clumsily. He used to have a humanoid form that looked like a stone warrior in pangolin-scale armour, and was quite the swordmaster, but he recently lost the ability to take that form for some reason and doesn't want to talk about it. He's generally a creature of few words, but wise. Also lazy. And keeps demanding shoes even though his feet are made of rock. How am I going to explain to a cobbler that I need two sets of sturdy boots for baby-sized feet, Aiax?

              2) Straight-up steal. Trawl folk tales for creatures that could be adapted into elementals. Trawl video games like Monster Hunter, Final Fantasy or Pokemon. Trawl popular culture in general. Import them into Creation and knead their concept a bit until it blends with the setting. Do some palette swaps or rename the creature if you want.

              Cambodia has legends about a deer that breathes underwater through gills on its muzzle. Filipino/Spanish myth has Ibong Adarna, a magical bird whose songs can heal and whose poop can turn a man to stone. China has the Shangyang or rainbird, whose dances herald heavy rains. There's also a Chinese tree-spirit called a Penghou, which looks like a black dog with no tail (and possibly a human face) which apparently tastes like dog meat when cooked (seriously, every source mentions that you should eat this thing). Hey, how about centaurs, right, but with their back-legs replaced with fish-tails! Too silly for Exalted? Not too silly for Greek mythology! Meet Ichthyocentaurs! South Africa gives us Grootslang, a massive elephant-serpent with keen intellect and cunning, a taste for cruelty, and a lust for gems. The basilisk turns creatures to stone, pick your favourite interpretation of its appearance. Have we mentioned naga yet?

              I'm not even going to start on Pokemon. Find an online pokedex and browse. If the pictures don't spark ideas, the pokedex entries will. Search the wiki of your favourite monster-fighting game if you're not into Pokemon.

              3) Take an animal. Add an element. Now add a twist or cool feature not directly related to the element, something you could use as a story hook maybe.

              Fire-horse ... crap that's a Pokemon. Oh well, let's forge on and make it our own. Let's see, a hook. If you stare ahead into its fiery mane while riding a wild fire-horse at breakneck speed, you will see true visions about past and future events in your life. Surviving this ordeal is the only way to begin taming the beast, after which its fire will no longer burn you, but will also no longer grant you visions. Needs a name. Pyremane? Sounds cool but has deathy connotations not otherwise present in the creature's themes ... although it will absolutely burn you to death if you can't resist damage from magical fire, so maybe it's not too inappropriate. Kludging together some Persian names gives Azaroya (fire+dream/vision). That'll do.

              4) Take a manifestation of the element, and turn it into a creature. Something about the element that people fear or find wondrous is particularly fertile ground.

              An earth elemental based on a geode ... wait, no, fuck, that's a Pokemon again, I'm not doing this a second time. Hmm, how about a fear one? The way the wind howls through the trees sometimes and makes your lizard-brain crap itself with dread. Make the beast that makes that sound. Like ... a pack of ephemeral wind-wolves, barely visible in the oncoming twilight, their bodies too long and fluid for real wolves, their fangs as cold as ice. You mistake the first bite for the cold nip of the wind, but then you notice your suddenly numb hand is red with your blood, and see the glowing points of their eyes surrounding you ... shit guys, I've gotta go to bed soon. Look at what you made me do.

              It's winter here, too!
