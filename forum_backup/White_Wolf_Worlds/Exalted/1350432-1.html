                    I think every Exalted character I've ever played is both evil and ignorant. They've (mostly) been human after all.

                    The Realm has prevented the total destruction of all Creation. Every Lunar and Solar fighting against the Realm is fighting for the destruction of Creation.

                    Every Sidereal, Gold or Bronze, sees only part of Fate which itself is only part of what makes reality tick.

                    Are the police officers who work in your city evil or ignorant? I can absolutely guarentee that whatever country you're in, you have at least one unjust law and one criminal those police officers haven't stopped. I can also guarentee your local cops haven't led a perfect life without sin.

                    But chances are (most of) your local cops are doing the best they can with the society they were born into. Yes, the government is funding terrorists to overthrow middle eastern states, and yes they're also selling the weapons being used to commit genocide in Yemen. But your local beat cops are putting their lives on the line, walking the streets to stop murderers and rapists.

                    Same for your Dynasts. They're part of a system. That system objectively has aspects which are not good. But it also has parts which are good -- like stopping armies of cannibals descending on the Satrapies and eating everyone. And what you play is your choice. You can be the heroic beat-cop, helping Old Man McClusty cross the road and rushing into danger to free girls from sex trafficing. Or you can be a racist, abusive, powermad ass.

                    But there's nothing inherent to being a Dynast that makes them evil or ignorant compared to any other PC. The Bull of the North rules a tribe of murderous barbarians. The Queen of Fangs literally eats babies.

                    You don't play The Realm. You play a Dynast.

                    My 3E Dynast character, Cynis Ganan, is a pretty bad example. He's deliberately evil and ignorant. But he's not without redeeming features. He's a hero in the classical sense, a champion who selflessly throws himself into danger to protect others. He loves his children. He wants to provide genuine guidance for mortals on how to improve their lives. He's not deliberately cruel to people or animals. He (basically) never lies. He's brave. He puts his wife's happiness before his own.

                        Cynis Kuran, who grew up surrounded by wealth and luxury and slaves who he was encouraged to use for whatever he liked, and to whom peasants bow down and do whatever he says, and who was constantly told he was better than most people, and that his country was better than other countries because the people in those countries are evil and backward.                             I think there's a degree of 'should be' in this argument. Like, to be fair to OP, his question makes a lot more sense if you ignore/downplay the even potentially redeeming aspects of the Realm... which I feel Ex3 has done. MoEP Dragon Blooded opens with the story of a Dynast fighting back cannibals and defending mortal troops at the risk of her own life. WFHW opens with a Wyld Hunt willing to put villages to the sword, and The Realm opens with banditry on the Blessed Isle itself. I can't recall offhand any examples of the Ex3 Realm doing stuff that's even marginally benevolent. Immaculate Order aid is focused on 'you should obey the Realm' more, Satrapy status is focused on 'what stuff can we take from you'. There's a feeling of less nurance to me, of less room to defend/support the Realm without being ignorant or malicious.

                            Which to me undercuts some of Exalted's central themes. It's hard to question 'would the Solars be better' when the Realm is both crumbling and malevolent.

                            (My PoV on where the scale of ambiguity for Exalted should be: if you put Chejop Kejak and Ayesha into a room and had them argue Bronze vs Gold as relevant to the current time period in game, neither should feel clearly right or wrong. Ayesha's got all the issues, all the problems of a Realm that's both crumbling and corrupt. Chejop has a combination of the risk Solars impose, and enough stability in the Realm to make it a credible alternative, if not a good one. Hope vs certainty. That should by its nature be an unwinnable argument, falling to personal preference.)
