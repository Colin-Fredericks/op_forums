    Frankly i'm not gonna miss 2e's "Solars can chump everybody" attitude with a heaping side of "The Dragonbloods aren't even REAL Exalts if you think about it". And i'm saying this as someone who likes Solars. Most of my concepts are Solars. But i'm okay with them being amazingly good at what they do without also going so far as to make everything that isn't a Solar a chump.

    I don't need my Solars to be better Sidereals than Sidereals. I don't need them to be better Dragon Bloods than Dragon Bloods. I'm good with them having edges in efficacy on their own terms (We'll see how their Advantages shake out but Free Excellencies and Wins Ties sound like nice power of theirs)

    I'm fine with Solar Melee letting me parry a volcano erupting without slipping in "This attack is unblockable to anybody who is also not a Solar". I'm okay with Sorcery and Craft being how Solars can build and maintain the miracles of the First Age. I like the universal Resonance that Solars have, for Evocations.

    I am not okay with other Exalts being kneecapped for the sake of Solar Supremecy. It doesn't feel like I won if a baked in mechanic on my foe is "Your defenses don't work on Solars". Like yeah, if I challenge someone to a sword fight but they immediately get paralyzed because i have brown hair, that's not really a sensation that makes me feel powerful.

    That's how you get things like people saying "Oh the Dragon Bloods aren't real Exalts" or the Alchemicals book saying "Well technically the Alchemicals are PRIMORDIAL Exalts, rather than Celestial or Terrestrial, but for effects that care they cound as Celestial"

    2e's Solar Favortism made Terrestrial a borderline dirty word and made non-Solars a joke, if less of a punchline than Dragon Bloods thinking they mattered.

    So yeah. Take the idea of widening the power gap again, set it on fire, and i won't cry a tear over it.

    Disclaimer: In favor of fun and enjoyment, but may speak up to warn you that you're gonna step on a metaphorical land mine

        Something worth noting too is that 3e and to some extent ExEss kind of go with PC-NPC asymmetry. This is kind of important too. A DB player need not be much weaker than a Solar one, while NPCs need only be as powerful or weak as needed for the kind of games being run. For a group of Celestials, the DBs need to be enough a challange to be mattering, but that depends on how many you want htere to be for that. For a Dragon-Blooded, the Anathema NPC should be a credible thret, but something in the boundaries of what you try to run. ANd since NPCs need not follow PC rules to the letter, this means that from game to game, what even the power leell of an Exalt might be is probably pretty different depending on how they're constructed. Especially if (like the game kind of is decding on now and with Essence) go all in on QC-style write-ups for NPCS.

            Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post
            I think it could work, but you'd need to rework the setting and be up front that. So you might have the Realm show up with 3 legions of soldiers, and the appropriate thing is for the Dawn to say "Excuse me a moment." and Horizon Severing Slash all of them to death with a back hand swing before returning to her conversation with the Abyssal.

            Other systems/settings work like this. Godbound does it, you literally have a player resource that you can spend to just topple an empire without rolling anything. Legacy: Godsend does it even harder, no resource required you just tell the GM you want to decimate a nation and it just happens. Those systems and settings are built around that though, they don't go into great detail about the ants that are crushed beneath your feet, and they have big, rich settings filled with a thousand things that are on your level, as you move around continent-leveling forces like pawns.
            And this is where I gesture again at my argument again that Godbound is genius because Kevin Crawford took lots of the main beats of "forum-meme-Exalted" and "Exalted-being-cool-but-probably-veering-away-from-its-core-coherence" that emerged during 1e and 2e and then built a world and system from the ground up that totally, directly leaned in to all of that, and marketed it directly to Exalted fans who'd bought into the game on that basis.

            As much as, without re-litigating edition drama, I think it's great (from my perspective, ideal) that Exalted got its groove back as being "Kung-fu anime Sword & Sorcery with personalities and politics" (there are better descriptions, but that's fairly succinct and hopefully not too horrible).

            ....

            Perhaps more constructive and useful comment for conversation : I would kind of expect it to make sense for a mechanically more simple implementation of Exalted to lean in to cross-splat play. Seems like a relative strength of a simple system?

            That is, it seems like in big, full mainline Exalted, it's often probably too much to handle for an ST to manage a group who are exploring the full diversity of splat mechanics across a huge variety of splats, and also the richness of any given splat will lean players towards feeling satisfied with all playing a group of the same splat. In a simplified system then, your design incentives are going to be serving cross-splat play as a niche that's less easy to run in the main game, and where intra-splat play is a bit less rich niche to explore.

                Originally posted by hurum View Post
                I would think their are other ways of "evening up" mixed groups than an all-round equaling of the power levels (what about bonus XP or increasing the cost of charms for the more potent Exalts when in a mixed group?)
                Originally posted by Blaque
                A DB player need not be much weaker than a Solar one
                So I was thinking about the fact that DBs in 3rd ed start at E2, with the same number of charms and more merits than Solars. Now it's not like DBs actually start with E2 or that many charms, but that by default you play an experienced Dragonblood.

                And so, in terms of fluff and PCs/NPCs, I wonder if you can use that. Maybe DBs have charms that are just a bit weaker, and both DB and Solar PCs start with 10 charms, the same stats, and the DB gets more background-type stuff. Or maybe the DB even gets more stats and charms (but their charms are weaker). But a DB PC represents a Dragonblood who's exceptional or experienced (say, in their 50s), while the Solar represents a very new Solar.
                (And Lunar PCs, Sidereal PCs, Alchemical PCs, etc, represent something a bit more experienced than the Solar PC but not much more.)

                And so, in the setting, a Solar who's been exalted for 20 years is far more powerful than a Dragonblood who's been exalted for 20 years, but in terms of PCs there's only a bit of a difference.


                And you could also include rules for less experienced DBs that are not designed for mixed play.



                So to expand on this a little, maybe your mixed game consists of:
                Solar Shonen Protagonist, 17 years old, exalted two months ago
                Lunar bro, 25 years old, exalted a few years ago, explains the Anathema stuff
                Dragonblood air-wizard, 100 years old, gives advice while stroking his beard
                Dragonblood kung-fu sifu, 60 years old, she teaches kung-fu to our shonen protagonist.
                Alchemical robo-nerd, she was made 20 years ago and met the group in their adventure

                Or some such.
                Most stories of bands of heroes, after all, don't actually have them all be the same level of experience.
