                Originally posted by Khazadir View Post
                This means that SAM couldn't be used if there was no hidden harm, but it would automatically trigger even if you were already in a fight and someone tried to make an unexpected attack, correct?
                Its first effect applies only when you make the Awareness roll to detect the hidden threat - so, in this case, not when the guy makes the unexpected attack against you, but when you roll opposing his Stealth to enter concealment.

                You make Awareness rolls to detect hidden threats normally, without needing to use a Charm. Surprise Anticipation Method just enhances those rolls.

                                                        So, I got pointed here from RPG.net, and I am still unsure just on how to view you two...

                                                        So, I guess I have a question in regards to that. One that, I suppose, I have to be a bit rambly about, as I am not sure how to properly put it. And want the question to hold the proper weight it should. So, Second Edition took a very... mechanistic view of the Exalted, and was often written by those who seemed to have grown too familiar and contemptuous of them, as well as often taking the memes of the once-thriving community to heart. You had Solar charisma turned into invasive mind-control, you had their Exaltation conflated with military service, out-of-fiction comments about them being the Sun's Space Marines, and so on. You had Dragon-Blooded mocked at the start of each of their Charm sections, hated by the Gods, seen as not real Exalted. You had Sidereals viewed as terrible by Heaven itself, disliked by their environment, and by the person writing their Charms (who found the Maiden-linked stylization of them as unnecessarily fruity). You had Abyssals seen as just lesser corruptions of Solars whose only path was redemption (and man, I like redemption, but...); Solars themselves already viewed in a dim manner.

                                                        Then you had that which was created after them, by the same people who had such views on the core Exalted. The Mountain Folk were a Primordial's favored children, whose powers existed to create, rather than destroy as with 'The Sun's Space Marines'; The Alchemicals were heroes of the people, the true chosen of Autochton to whom the Pattern Spiders, the Jadeborn and the world would bend to; and Infernals... well. Infernals have been well-discussed. Snead said they were well-written, just not a good fit for Exalted as they were sole protagonists, and that is a decent way to put it.

                                                        It often felt, interacting with Infernals fans, that they were fans of Infernals, and not of Exalted, in any way.

                                                        As Morke and Holden took the reins, this was, in fact, what was pointed out. Morke compared Infernals to The One Ring, before which all of the other Exalted looked like lesser copies. He identified this as a problem, and set out to push against it. When an user on this forum thought of making a third edition introdutory webcomic in the vein of the sadly-deceased Keychain of Creation, the Infernal character in the outline was the clear focus and protagonist, and when the user noted that he did not like Solars, Morke's reaction was 'Good luck with this thing that resembles Exalted, then.' When I was linked to that, it made me start following the community again. It made me hope that there was something there I could engage in.

                                                        That was the clear thoughtline in most of the other decisions they made; to make the Exigence connected to the Sun so him returning his gaze to the world has an appreciable impact; that the Getimians exist to contrast and enhance Sidereals, rather than the way Alchemicals and Infernals were just able to ignore and laugh at them. Morke was upfront on Getimians being a way to make the Sidereal millieu more interesting, rather than to detract from them. All of the new Exalted seemed to be made with the intent to enhance the primacy of the Core Five. Which through themselves, describe the core pillars of the setting.

                                                        But, well. Morke and Holden are gone, now.

                                                        And so, I have to ask, in what I imagine will be the only quoted part: is this concerted effort to re-establish the primacy, importance and mystique of the Core Five still a major part of your agenda? Or is it something you do not feel is as important as your predecessors did?

                                                        And can you tell me how you view the five and their place in the setting and game?

                                                        The way I put it, the answer seems like a no-brainer, but Infernals fans will likely be quite disappointed at no longer being the greatest masters of Sorcery, greater existences than the Yozi, and greater in freedom, agency and metaphysical grandeur than anyone else in the setting; Alchemical fans will also likely be quite disappointed that they can no longer faceroll Creation and no longer hold the true allegiance of most of Creation's apparatus; and I have met too many people who thought the Core Five should not be viewed as any way special, and that their primacy over the setting should not be treated as anything other than a historical accident. Certainly several very influential writers who worked on Exalted to this point believed that.
