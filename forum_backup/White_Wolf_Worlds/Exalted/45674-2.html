    Yeah, I like Creation's medical stuff working the same way as ours, with the spiritual side as an add-on rather than a replacement. It makes Creation more intuitive for players portraying characters who've always lived there. Sure, you should be able to treat Malaria by doing a ritual and praying to the Mosquito-Goddess...but the ritual involves brewing the bark of a tree to make a potion of sacred quinine water, most of the time. I don't want you to be able to cure a plague by calling up the plague-god and beating him up, and I don't think treating individual patients should look like an exorcism (most of the time-supernatural diseases like Green Sun Wasting or the Contagion might be different). I want it to involve steaming cups of herbal teas and wrapping patients in prayer strips soaked in water from a sacred fountain or ritualized bloodletting or acupuncture. I want my Exalted Healer characters to feel like doctors, not just magic away the problem.

    I'm fine with using mystical forces to weaken the sickness and oppose its spread and strengthen the patient so he/she can fight off infection, and I'm fine with spirits inflicting illnesses as a curse or punishment as an additional vector for the disease...but I want dealing with spirits and magical energies to be a thing where you increase or decrease the odds of survival/infection, not something that cures/inflicts the condition, y'know?

    Anyway, back on topic? Pretty sure Lunar "I am super-attractive charms" plus changing into plants can lead to pregnant trees. Good way to seduce forest-gods or wood-elementals in particular, I feel, might make a nice backstory for a godblooded character. Being born from a tree is a nice mythic sorta backstory for a character. Sorta Monkey King-ish. I'm probably more comfortable with tribes of humanoids with plant-features being the result of patronage by forest spirits or wood elementals and lots of godbloods over many generations, but I could see Lunars doing this as a one-off trick. Wouldn't want to make it a character's whole schtick though.

    So I'm making God-Kicking Boot, an Exalted webcomic, now. Updates on Sundays. Full-color, mediocre but slowly improving art. It's a thing.

                    "Only stuff visible to the naked eye matches our modern knowledge base" is a good bright line for players and Storytellers. Once that line is crossed, the resulting changes in setting flavor -- and possibly gameplay, depending on the interests of your players -- can shift the game into an undesired thematic space full of smallpox vaccines, weaponized anthrax, homebrewed C-4, and suitcase nukes. A lot of traditional pulp fantasy did run with the idea that magic was purely an add-on to Scientific Knowledge As We Now Know It, but not all, and the resulting flavor is not to everyone's taste.

                                                This thread started with the idea of "virusmen." Now, viruses don't work that way, but choleramen and plaguemen and whatever... Urgh. Of course I'd shut that down, but it's not a singular point, and it's not about inventing modern medical science either. It's about the fact that if the setting runs on cellular biology and bacteria, people will treat it as running on cellular biology and bacteria, and come up with solutions and applications pertaining to cellular biology and bacteria. The alternative is to say "the world runs on cellular biology and bacteria, but you can't treat it as such," which is a lame answer. If it has no relevance to the game, it shouldn't be there, and the setting should work in a manner that correlates to the mood and themes it is supposed to carry.

                                                That is to say, if you shouldn't be inventing antibiotics, then make it so you can't invente antibiotics.
