    What Would Your God Do?

    07-12-2020, 06:37 PM
    Hello, I'm looking for sub-plot ideas for my campaign.

    The deity in question is a god of a local strip mine, one of two important features of a southern town (the other being an important crossroad of caravan routes.)
    The mine makes it a strategic location for the Realm, and is a stable job, making him important enough for the locals that they don't really have a separate god for the town itself.

    The problem is, the mine is getting depleted, and nobody among the other gods care. So, our god needs to either do something to extend its usefulness (get some extra resources assigned to it by the offices?), or get a new form of sustenance (somehow becoming important enough for the locals that he becomes the city god after the mine is no more) Problem is, he is a big fish in a small pond, important locally, but not really connected in Yu Shan or anything, and not super smart either.

    What I need for him is some sort of schemes he'd try to rope the player into in the effort to save himself, preferrably flawed ones, so he is forced to go through several through the campaign (possibly culminating in making some shady deal with Abyssals after everythging else fails)

                      There's a few avenues here.

                      Solar vs Realm. The god wants Immaculate backing. The Immaculates want a tighter hold on the town. The Solar wants Realm stability... but does want a Wyld Hunt. Left alone... the god is going to put the town firmly under heel. Does the Solar now have to reevaluate her support of the Realm? Does she directly intercede to make sure the god gets what he wants but the town doesn't suffer? Does she keep her head down as her hometown turns into some kind of Orwellian nightmare?

                      There's Solar for God. I'm assuming your Lawbringer is a Twilight, not an Eclipse diplomat or Zenith priest? As a Twilight the realm of spirits sits within her purview. Maybe your god enlists her to do the negotiating with the Immaculates on his behalf? After all, the Immaculates don't know she's a Solar, but she's bound to be more competent (in the god's mind) than a mortal (this could be a gross miscalculation).

                      There's Solar vs God. The Immaculate Philosophy has pretty strict rules about gods not interacting with mortals. How does a god, then, parlay with the Immaculates? Does he manifest, breaking faith, and speak for himself? Does he converse with his priests, breaking faith, and send them to speak on his behalf? There's plenty of room for misunderstandings and wilful interpretation here. How does your Solar feel about treating the broken legs of the mortal sent to go negotiate with Peleps Delad? Or about this god clumsily bargaining with the Dragon-Blooded when he knows her secret?

                          -An elemental court is simpler than a divine court. Use a tribal system. They are usually reactive to problems caused by other elementals, Out of Fate beings or irresponsible deities.

                          -Work in Bureaus of Seasons (mainly) and Nature. They monitor living beings, material components and/or natural phenomena in a region. A court linked to the Bureaus of Seasons is usually a military reserve force (useful charms for mass destruction).

                          -A Cselenine pact (payment in Jade) or worship can convince an elemental court to include other matters as their function. The nature of the majority determines the policy of court, Wood elementals are meritocrats, Earth elementals do not tolerate Shadowlands and Wyld, Fire elementals defend marriages or duels, Water elementals participate in rebellions and Air elementals accept complex systems of organization..

                          NOTE: Sorry for my mistakes. English is not my native language

                              The Council of Winds is the most powerful elemental court in Creation. They control each court in the north and west. Its headquarters are on Mount Meru. They secretly hunt Water elementals for 1000 years (Yu-Shan ignores crimes in exchange for cooperation). Its role is to oversee the climate for the Bureau of Seasons.

                              The most common is Mineral and the Mountains. The large number of them and many members do not guarantee influence because they are recluse. They inspect the soil components of Creation and hunt down hostile darkbroods.

                                          Originally posted by Braininthejar View Post
                                          Please educate me on what an elemental court actually does. I mean, I've heard of them, but have always seen them as a bunch of disgruntled fire dudes sitting in a cave. What is their actual political influence and how do they do it?
                                          The basic format of a spirit court is that it's how spirits form into a local hierarchy and socialise. The main thing of them is establishing an order of precedent so that spirits can co-exist without stepping on each other's toes in terms of their worldly purviews and any worshippers they might accumulate (even if they likely scheme amongst one another to extend their power within protocol). It also lets them form a collective of protection against other spirits that might muscle in on their territory. They can tend to form around a spirit who either has the strength to offer that protection and keep everybody under the umbrella playing nice, or is respectable, charismatic or well-organized enough to be a stabilizing influence whose interventions are agreeable to all.

                                          Courts have significance in a world where the Terrestrial gods are often ruthless and the authority of Heaven is limited and disinterested.

                                          You do have a few more specialized courts with more abstract mission statements. The Court of Orderly Flame is an organisation of Southern elementals (mostly fire) dedicated to the memory of an exceptionally upright and honourable dragon Censor who was lost to the Fair Folk invasion. They try to incentivize other spirits to live up to those high standards and promote decency and justice. The Court of Seasons is simply the collective of the gods who represent each calendar month, although they're also charged with the responsibility of managing the Carnival of Meetings, the grand festival that takes place in Yu-Shan each Calibration. This gives them a popularity out of proportion with what they actually do, and they're pretty big-headed over it.

                                          Previous Editions have written Yu-Shan as considering disbanding the Bureau of Seasons (which governs Creation's weather and climate) and giving those responsibilities to the Court of Seasons, unaware that they are woefully unqualified.

                                          The funny thing to me is that while the game uses the term 'court', evocative of the attendants of monarchs and powerful aristocrats, they're a lot more reminiscent of the ancient Roman concept of the collegium.

                                              If OP is looking for plothooks centering on the god trying to keep his job, then I can think of several things. These two ideas can both be applied, one as a starter adventure, the next one as the big main campaign.

                                              1) Recruit the players to strong-arm local earth elementals into submitting to him. Basically have the players become the god's leg-breakers as he builds his own elemental court up.
                                              - this will of course lead to future conflict with local elemental courts, but that's just more leg-breaking
                                              - that will in turn lead to conflict with sidereals as this god suddenly starts wielding a lot more power than he was meant to have. Not sure you can leg-break your way out of this one, perhaps try some heavenly diplomacy?

                                              2) In the gods search for ways to make the mine a more permanent location of interest, he asks around and learns of the legend of the Drop That Contained A Sea, a first age artifact used to instantly conjure up vast amounts of water. He wants to turn his strip mine into an oasis, capitalizing on the traffic that comes via the trade road. A massive water supply would draw in caravans from all over, making him far more powerful, especially if he can be sure that the locals are thanking him for the water
                                              - This means getting the players to quest for this ancient and lost artifact
                                              - It was used by the solars back in the first age, but... apparently hasn't been used since - so where'd it go?
                                              - the players can ask around and learn that it was last used to create the lakes and waterways that define the Harborhead territories, back in the late first age, about four hundred or so years before the great contagion. It was of course lost after that.
                                              - poking around black market artifact auctions in Chiaroscuro, the players learn that in the immideate years after the great contagion, when her Redness took power and formalized the immaculate monks, a lot of first age artifacts that had previously been used by the DBs in the late first age were hidden away because they bore too many marks of their Solar origins.
                                              - so... find the hidden artifact?
                                              - the central administration for the immaculates in the south is in the Lap, via a monestary located behind the central grand immaculate temple in the city. It's administrative library contains the ledgers that detail what was hidden where. Good luck finding that ledger, because book-keeping from 700 years ago on hidden artifacts is very frail and very secret.
                                              - once they find the ledger, crack the code and find the location, its off the temple where the drop is hidden.
                                              - the temple turns out to be a very remote monestary for asshole DB monks in the south that nobody else really wants around. So they're all assholes.
                                              - the drop is locked away in a magical vault manse under the monestary. The password is a special prayer known only to the abbot of the monestary... or, abbots, because up until recently it was run by two DB brothers.
                                              - problem is that one brother wanted to be less of an asshole and left the place, so even if the players can talk the remaining abbot into telling them his half of the prayer, they need to find the other guy
                                              - The lost brother travelled to the east
                                              - got captured by Lookshy
                                              - commited suicide, to prevent the secret prayer-password from falling into non-realm hands
                                              - shit
                                              - except... the abbot(s) apparently undergo a ritual when they learn the prayer, marking their souls. It makes them guaranteed to return as ghosts, to prevent the prayer passwords from being lost forever if they die without teaching someone else the prayer. But the ghost is nowhere to be seen.
                                              - and where to ghosts of people who commit suicide while in prison go? the players will have to ask around
                                              - time for an adventure into the Eternal Prison in the labyrinth of the underworld (see Compass: Underworld p 122)
                                              - if the players can bust in, find the ghost of the DB, get the info, and get out again, then... the strip mine god gets his oasis and throws a nice beach party for the players
