            (Oh god I did not expect this response).

            I'm thinking of massive prayer forges. Now, it's pretty difficult to make artifacts or anything magical. There's a lot of facilities needed to make the bare minimum for the weakest artifacts (at least for mortals). There's an entire religion, with a smattering of Dragonblooded and god-blooded, all of them joining together in a religious ceremony, all of them to create artifacts and materials of higher power.

            Farming communities have wide-scale rituals and festivals. These aren't just for pleasing the gods and social harmony. There are actual hard benefits there, designed and created by priest societal-engineers who alter the culture, rituals, and social norms so as to create wards, cast blessings onto the fields, and protect against disease. Instead of working with the magical materials and crafting artifacts, these priests use human beings and the very fabric of society the same way a smith might hammer at metal.

            One of the things that broke down the first age was the destruction of social norms. Solars could use wide-scale, massive social charms and their incredible intuition on the occult as well as coordination with Heaven itself to turn entire societies into massive scale rituals to create prosperity, harmony, and happiness. Such an effort is highly difficult, prone to failure, and only the usage of Solar social and bureaucracy charms let them be done at all. When the solars fell and the First Age shattered, life became harder, sometimes literally because there weren't society-wide thaumaturgies to make life easier and warp probability.

                                                Blaque, Hmm... Well, I would say there are a lot of great touchstones to the idea. It touches on contemporary concerns of civilization being bound to complex financial structures which no individual merely human person really understand, yet which are far Too Big To Fail; in what remains of it in the Second Age to the post-apoc genre's language of post-apoc humans conducting empty and garbled simulcra of past culture that they dimly understand; and on fantasy genre tropes on the "solidity" of money and commerce as a counterweight to fairydom and chaos (with a subtle inversion of where this trope is used in a sense that 'This Is A Bad Thing That Robs The World of Evanescent Wonder').

                                                It also meets my thumbs up test for a Good First Age Stuff, where it's undeniably the stuff of "greatness" in a way that places the accent on grandiosity, but has some elements pointing in a disquieting direction. Like, should people really be subtly compelled to live their lives conducting what is in a sense arbitrary economic activity for the purposes of extending Creation and the size of the Solar Exalted's domains and "greatness"? Isn't it hubristic to make oneself so central to a fragile system on which millions of lives depend?
