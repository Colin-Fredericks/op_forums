    I posted something about this a long time ago, in another thread, and I don't remember the exact wording I used then, but I'm going to try to replicate it now.

    The First and Forsaken Lion was not a great, amazing general in the First Age. He was not the preeminent general or warrior of the Solar Host, and in fact was not particularly well known at all.

    But he wanted to be.

    In life, he wanted nothing more than to be considered the greatest, finest general in all Creation. Unfortunately for him, he came late to the party, and there were far greater generals than he. Jealousy burned in him, and he swore he would be known, by any means necessary. When he died, atop the mountain formed of the bodies of his slain army caught in a trap during the Usurpation, the whispers came. As the light faded from his eyes, the whispers promised him an army that would black out the sun, if only he would serve. The man that slew him stood before him, spear in the Lion's chest, and heard the words he spoke as he died, and it is said that they haunted him to the end of his days. He never repeated them, but on the day of his death, he too heard the whispers, and even now he serves his master in death.

    The Lion died young, never having a true chance to grow into the Golden General he always swore he would be. Now, he grows his might, day by day, night by night, and swears he will see himself at the head of the greatest army Creation has ever seen. As the Underworld turns, he has necromantic smiths toiling endlessly, developing new weapons, new creations, in his drive to have the most unstoppable force in any realm of existence. He aggressively recruits the dead into his ranks, and draws forth plasmic horrors from the depths of the twisting underlayer of the Underworld to shatter the wills of his enemies to fight. He burns with the need to be able to project force to any corner of the Underworld, overwhelming enough to be truly unbeatable.

    But the Lion is a warrior, not a general, not a leader. He does not understand that the greatest army is not the largest, with the strongest soldiers, but the victorious army. And so his grand army of the dead labors under the yoke of his obsessions. Unfortunately for the Underworld, and perhaps for Creation, that army is indeed mighty, and that might accounts for a great deal, even if it is often misapplied.

    He is, however a very capable warrior, and his rage at being forgotten burns like a smoldering ember awaiting the lightest touch of kindling to burst into a terrifying flame. When provoked, he can shake the very pillars of the Underworld with his personal acumen in battle. But this is not enough. Never will this be enough. No, indeed, for he craves the recognition he never achieved in life. The Lion will burn his name across the very skies themselves, if that will make Creation remember him forever.

    And those whispers in his very soul mock him endlessly, stoking that ember and keeping it ready…
