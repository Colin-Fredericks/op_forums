                Originally posted by Flare
                I don't think Rakan Thulio has to micromange, but I do know that it was said before that a big part of a Getimian's story will be 'escaping' from Thulio- or it was before-, which is why he's important; in terming it as 'escaping' or similar, there are apparently deliberate similarities invoked to Renegade Abyssals and Renegade Infernals; escaping from a massive power.
                Okay, I'm going to break down why I think Rakan Thulio exists in the game.

                First of all, when he was first mentioned, I found that he basically existed as a footnote in the wider description of the Getimian Exalted, basically just a plot device for why they exist in the setting. As time went by, he had to accumulate more details as a character in his own right, because that's kind of helpful for any character in the game; that they're well-rounded, including qualities that might associate them with conflict separate from their main political interests.

                The function I see Rakan as originally serving is providing the Getimian Exalted with an elder Exalt mentor, when they otherwise wouldn't have one. I think it felt necessary to give them one, because something that sets basically all of the Exalted apart from the core Solars is in them having some already established society amongst themselves, which even the Abyssals and Infernals get; Liminals don't, but their manner of alienation is its own thing (Exigents don't because they're almost all a community of One). In that he needed to accumulate a few details to explain why a Sidereal would be with these people antithetical to Sidereals, so it would appear that, following the cosmological basis for Getimians being a thing, he's a Sidereal who is in opposition to the rest because of matters pertaining to Destiny rather than Solars.

                I think that's an interesting concept. I think there's compelling drama in the idea of a Sidereal who is broken from the Fellowship owing to matters of Destiny in its own right, and particularly as a basis for what connects him to the Getimian Exalted, in which having been screwed over by Destiny is their whole thing. Noting the likely fact that Rakan's ideals exist for the sake of Getimians, and not the other way around.

                Because Exalted generally need some friction with their elders, it would appear to me that the intent was that Rakan presents a bit of a problematic mentor for Getimians; he has a lot of experience and insight that is very useful for them, but it may also prove that he's an authority figure and control freak (still a Sidereal) in a manner that might grate with their issues with how Destiny has used them, and that some may feel as though they're not really being trained to shine in their own right, but exploited to fulfil Rakan's vendetta. I think these points were and are intended to be written ambiguously. I think the idea of escaping Rakan was not in a literal sense (although doing so probably isn't the hardest thing in the world, since we've got Lunars and Solars to attest to the point that Sidereals with more resources than Rakan can't track down everybody they want dead all the time), but a question of emotions (he probably ought to come across as compelling and helpful to Getimians to fulfil his narrative function), and uncertainty about whether they can hack it on their own (yet).

                If, when the Getimians are written, Rakan is meant to be a side character for them, I find it a lot more likely that he's written as a target or complication to deal with than a cage they can't get free from.

                Originally posted by Flare
                While Dynasts escaping from their parents to rebel against the Realm may be similar, I feel that the scale is different in what they can accomplish. It may not be actively different, and may have more similarities then differences, but this sort of rebellious break from society seems easier for a Dragonblooded, who, while maybe not liked in all corners of the world, can generally find a place that will at least accept them. A Lunar never has to 'escape' their teacher. A Dragonblooded might have to escape their family, but the implication also seems to be if the Dragonblooded is rebellious or otherwise a troublemaker to a point that they can't be fixed, they're generally going to be put aside rather then pressured into a mold. A Dragonblooded is presented as a hero who can do great things, even if their family is problematic or a struggle.
                If I take your implication correctly about the problem being that Getimians have trouble fitting in, that seems a lot more about creating drama for them than anything to do with Rakan.

                why do games featuring Infernals need to almost always end up focusing on the Yozis?
