            The most successful ship based RPGs I've ever seen have been Star Trek Adventures and Rogue Trader, and they both operate almost exactly the same way, with different ship positions that all get to go once per turn if they're crewed, and all give either some benefit like allowing for communication, or buffing someone else's action like by using sensors to increase the accuracy of the weapons fire.

            I'm not sure how well that will translate to Exalted, partially because there might be less fun obvious things to do without the sci-fi angle, and also because unlike those games Exalted might not have the right attribute/ability spread to fill multiple roles with characters with a variety of skills.

                    In terms of non-Sail things to do in different positions... the naval technology of the setting plus magic actually has a lot of options here because you can't just input commands into a computer and have the ship do stuff.

                    Since Savage Seas is considered a lore appropriate book for 3e, we have a list of positions already (going to skip the under positions since I doubt you'd have enough PCs and important PCs to need all of those secondary positions having places to give bonuses):

                    Admiral: The leader of multiple ships. War could be used for Command Actions and Strategic Warfare per the existing rules for those; maybe modified slightly.
                    Captain: In many cases they're not actually sailing the ship, but organizing the crew, making their function more Presence or War oriented depending on the situation.
                    Kubernetes: The actual pilot of the boat, this is where your best Sail person goes.
                    Proreus: This is probably the most flexible of the command level part of the crew and oversees whatever the captain and kubernetes don't. Awareness (if the kubernetes is the conn, the proreus is sensors), Investigation (is the captain's main enforcer), Presence, or War (is responsible for crew discipline) depending on the skills of the rest would be important.
                    Keleteus (only if the boat has oars): Performance and/or Presence to keep the rowers operating together; vital for getting the most speed you can out of your boat.
                    Arms Master: Another War position that's done by the Proreus in smaller crews anyway, should also be the best fighter as the direct unit leader of armed forces in combat.
                    Purser: Bureaucracy and Socialize primary as the one responsible for ship supplies. Navies sail on their stomachs too. Lore and Occult if the ship can't afford a specialist to negotiate with spirits.
                    Carpenter, Weapon Smith, and Sailmaker: Crafts obviously.
                    Surgeon: Medicine obviously.
                    Master of the Tops and Master of the Bow (only if the boat has rigging; Master of the Bow would still exist on a rowed vessel but wouldn't be actively important in tense situations): Athletics as the crew that handle the rigging in a sailing vessel means a lot of agility and strength to ensure the sails are positioned properly, though both positions should also have good Awareness as they are relied on as spotters (esp. on large ships) due to their vantage points.

                    And while not given any official title in that book:

                    Ritualist: Occult and Lore. Ships in Exalted have lots of strange things to worry about that real world ones don't. Someone needs to know the way to appease the various spirits and gods the ship might encounter. Having an actual Sorcerer is even a bigger boost of course.

                    So there's room for a lot of other Abilities to come to bear in ship operations. Though they overlap a lot in practical function, I think it's more than enough to fill out something like STA's base concept. Ignoring admirals:

                    The CO stuff goes to the captain and proreus.
                    The Helm and Navigation stuff goes to the kubernetes, keleteus, and masters of the tops and bow.
                    Sensors go to the proreus and masters of the tops and bow.
                    Security and Tactical go to the proreus and arms master.
                    Comms goes to the purser and ritualist.
                    Internal Systems go to the purser, craftsmen, and surgeon.

                                    The thing I would be careful with, is that boats big enough for serious naval combat need to function as well-oiled human-powered machines. However well the intention, an Exalt archer taking pot shots at enemies ships/crew could mess up their whole ship's operations.

                                    A Solar archer not working in coordination with the arms master (assuming they aren't the arms master and then need to be in coordination with the proreus and/or captain) can easily screw things up by "helping." One of the watchers needs to spot this, and relay it to the kubernetes. The kubernetes needs to toss out a bunch of orders on the fly. The proreus needs to make sure all those orders get to the right people. Those orders need to be executed perfectly in sync with each other. If a single step gets screwed up, the boarding action keeps derailed as the ship is nowhere near the crippled enemy boat instead of pulling up next to it. All because one super-archer decided to act on their own volition.

                                    I'm pretty sure this is why DrLoveMonkey was pointing towards something like STA's starship battle rules. You put that archer in as the arms master of the ship. They're normally busy running all of the ship's ranged fighters, with whatever bonuses that could provide. One of the CO positions could use their bonus to give the archer a bonus action, which they could use to do a big personal effect with; putting the coordination into the system and avoiding the chain reaction of events above.
