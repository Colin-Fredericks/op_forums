                                        Originally posted by Epitome View Post

                                        Good points. It's kinda counter-intuitive to me that you can be 2 different people to 2 different observers but not 1 person to 2 different observers, but I can see how that can be a trade-off for being able to automatically take on the &quot;right&quot; appearance.
                                        I want to be clear that to my eyes, the primary justifying element isn’t in balances but in that narrative of being concealed by a reflection of the desires of the beholder.

                                        I think it encourages engaging with the disguise in a very intimate, personal manner that I think creates unique narratives, plays off of other qualities of Lunars in general and Appearance in particular, and gives more weight to this form of disguise distinct from their actual shapeshifting. I would have reservations in the first disguise becoming applicable to all later observers because I feel it weakens the sensation of the Charm if at any point it makes you look like somebody other than who a given observer most expects or wishes to see.

                                        I’m biased in its favour because it reminds me of the plot of an episode of Stargate SG-1: a protagonist tries to infiltrate a criminal organisation by hitting the leader with a drug that will cause him to see the good guy as a specific other person. That person so happens to be only identifiable by the boss, so he’s the only one that needs to be fooled like that, and everybody else goes along with it.

                                        A complication later ensues when another criminal who is already familiar with the guy and knows that he definitely isn’t one of the crime lord’s captains enters the scenario unexpectedly.

                                        I guess what I’m saying is that I think the limitation is compelling and dramatic, presents a particular ethereal and personal quality distinct to Lunar magic, and that logical complications encourage finding interesting solutions (without completely compromising the power).

                                        Still, you’d be better off asking the developers if what I see as valuable in it was anything like the actual intent.

                                                        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                                                        If a character can't read, and has a letter read out to them, are they still affected by Linguistics charms in the message?
                                                        From "Written vs. Spoken Effects", Exalted p. 322: "A Linguistics Charm using the written method must be read to be effective..." I'd say that that means they have to be read, i.e. comprehended. Though, as Epitome says, stunting can affect this. I'd allow Linguistics to cover picture-books, for instance. It already covers other information-conveying images like maps. So if you stunt your document as being a series of unusually accurate and message-relaying images, someone who can't read might still be affected by that. I'd definitely say that such a document was longer and took longer to compose than a standard text, for the same quantity of information, though (I think pictures are worth 1000 words only when you have at least a few words to give them context).
