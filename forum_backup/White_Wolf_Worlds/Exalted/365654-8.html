                                                    Originally posted by Astral View Post
                                                    Would it help to stop trying to measure essence in Joules and look at it more like Chi? Something that can be understood, cultivated, manipulated, and used, but cannot be definitively measured? The very thing that people who practice Fung Shui, Kung Fu, and Acupuncture deal with all the time?
                                                    I'm not sure this is a useful framework in that the things you mention are very scientific in their outlook and presentation, and would not characterize themselves as "anti-empirical" or out of sync with scientific rationalism. There are lots of things that we can only measure or detect by their echoes or ripple effects, but we do not consider those things to come from a non-empirical paradigm and we understand that our failure to directly measure them does not have to do with them being outside the framework in which empiricism operates. For that matter, "The Way that can be expressed is not the true Way", for all its antiintellectualist gloss, is a concept with considerable resonance to and history among scientists.


                                                    Part of the problem, I think, is that there's a dichotomy between "scientific empiricism" and "spiritualism" being drawn here, but those things aren't opposite or even incompatible. There are scientific empiricists who view their work as contemptuous, mechanical mastery of nature and there are scientific empiricists who view their work as communion with transcendental cosmic mysteries, and the fact that what they're doing is scientific empiricism is largely irrelevant to the distinction. Scientific empiricism has deeper roots in classical spiritualism than it does in Enlightenment-era industrial rationalism. So a spiritual-empiricist dichotomy is something that needs to be put into a setting for thematic purposes, rather than something that occurs accidentally, and it seems like a weird thing to put into Exalted. Empiricism is not a thing unto itself, it's a byproduct of the fact that the universe "has a way about it", so a universe where empiricism is not applicable at a fundamental level -- rather than one with insurmountable but comprehensible barriers to certain applications, which is what we have now -- tends to scan as a universe that lacks meaningful qualities or relationships, which feels excessive as a way to re-mystify Essence.


                                                    I get that ultimately this is all about trying to avoid things that seem prosaic in the way midichlorians made the Force prosaic, but I think "Essence can't be approached or understood in a scientific way" is kind of an awkward, brute force way of doing it, as opposed to simply being cautious about how these things are presented in the text. If the Jedi had been legendary builders who created giant infrastructural projects powered by the Force, the Force would need to be presented something very different from the amorphous mystical power that it is. On the other hand, this wouldn't require making it safe, trivial, or fully mapped-out, either -- without midichlorians.
