                    I suspect that Lyta came across as bad to the people that first read her due to her professed desire to ritualistically execute her enemies, which... has been fairly standard throughout human history, but I'd say that a lot of fiction up to that point would have been more likely to attribute to villains.

                    Mind, if I had been there at the time, I imagine that I would have been saying such right alongside everybody else, for a couple of reasons. But my current world view is one in which I don't really see any value or sense in condemning ancient world politics and morality, nor do I see the purpose of fiction depicting such to be reinforcing the rightness of contemporary views by having a representative of them righteously opposed to things as they actually are.

                    (I don't exactly apply that lens to matters of rights and representations for traditionally marginalized people, for reasons of deconstructing the sense that such marginalization is the naturally encoded default)

                    Besides which, it helps that Lyta's ire is directed towards the corrupt and powerful in her world view, rather than punching down. Really, the same goes with Havesh (including the point that I'm really convinced that there's a not particularly subtle subtext in his story that he kind of hovers on the verge of convincing himself to be a champion and emancipator for the oppressed underclass from which he originates).

                    As for the significance of being Chosen by the Unconquered Sun, I would frame it in terms of him being compelled by people that are passionately motivated, and if not particularly good, then at least not heavily inclined towards utilising their power to contribute to the suffering and exploitation of the already oppressed.

                    In that regard, one could read Arianna as being something like among the worst of them, as her attitude towards most people as I recall it was somewhat cold and elitist, but not necessarily in a manner that is going to make life worse for the majority of people.

                    They might not be exactly nice, but people have often found cathartic value in stories of heroes who were not particularly nice to those perceived to be the source of their woes, and practical value in using such people in capacities such as revolution.

                    I have approximate knowledge of many things.

                        Since the subject of whether or not Lunars can form mystical bonds that endure over lifetimes at will to anybody has come up elsewhere:

                        My read on the situation is that it's not exactly a property of Lunar Exaltation that they can just harness as they please, and that it's more like remaking their Castes; that it requires drastic, dramatic circumstances, something that collectively resonates through many if not most if not all of them, and to have a certain foundation in their underlying mythology.

                        I think that it's supposed to be something that worked because political marriage to end a harmful conflict with what were not only fellow Exalted, but the Chosen of the Sun, who possesses a dualistic relationship with their Moon, as well as being heroes mighty enough to temper and focus them in their own right, is something that had a powerful enough weight to leave a lasting impression upon their Essence.

                        One thing that I suspect will be ambiguous is exactly how many Lunars and Solars got that deal; it can't be more than three quarters of the former, and I suspect that there will be room for their to be fewer even than that, that there are Solar Essences for which there is no waiting Lunar.

It's arguable whether it's good (and, honestly, while Havesh may murder people, the Bull of the North has probably killed far, far more innocent people than Havesh has in his imperial war of conquest), but they are the sort of people you might choose to recruit in a war against the god-worlds who made the universe.

                                    I think the reason people bring them up with the Unconquered Sun choosing Solars is because of the logic chain:
                                    The Unconquered Sun is good;
                                    So he'd choose good people;
                                    He chooses bad people;
                                    This makes no sense.

                                    But I don't think the problem is him choosing people, I think the problem is assuming he's good, from the perspective of a 21st century player. He's Zeus/Ra/Jupiter/Huetzilopochtli. Sure, he's better than the Titans, but he's not perfect. So sometimes he chooses people who seem to others like villains.*
                                    (And Luna... is insane. And the Maidens are inscrutable and weird.)

                                    *Lyta literally was the villain for our Dragonblood game, but then, we were Dynasts, so maybe we were the villains. And I used Havesh as King of the Casteless in Varang, someone the PCs cooperated with as his revolution distracted the authorities from the PCs plans to rescue all Djala slaves, and later killed because he was also cooperating with people that Sidereals+Kes had manipulated them into fighting. Well, actually they killed him because he was a suspicious foreigner in their enemy's palace who was trying to leave and wouldn't answer their questions. That's PCs for you.

                                    Saying this, I was totally fine with the 2e thing of the Exaltations choosing people themselves, and I preferred that to the UCS choosing them. But I don't think it's a big problem.
                                    (As long as there's some mechanism for the Exaltations to keep choosing people after the UCS dies, of course, otherwise I'd have had to stop people making new Solar characters in my game. Or Lunar characters.)
