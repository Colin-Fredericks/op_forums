                    Alright, looking at the panoply section, I'm seeing one-issue so far.

                    One-handed (and to a much lesser extent, paired) are included in amongst the weapon tags. That's fine on its own, but we're told that mundane weapons can have up to two tags, and then the examples seem to include (or mysteriously exclude) these tags in addition to the others. A knife, for instance, is listed as having the concealable and thrown tags, but then also sometimes the paired tag. Mysteriously absent is the one-handed tag, something I'd definitely expect a knife to have.

                    It really seems like it ought to be 3 tags for most weapons, or 2 if you really want to differentiate things. I like the general idea of a tag system and a "build your own weapon, here are some examples" approach, but this seems to be applied inconsistently from the sentence at the beginning of the section. If the intention is that the "sometimes (tag)" is a suggestion that might replace one of the others, that isn't super clear in this draft.

                    EDIT: Looking at it more thoroughly, it seems like every weapon should have either melee or ranged. Then every melee weapon ought to have one of one-handed or two handed, and then two additional tags on top of that. Honestly, I'm not sure I like that. I think it would be far more elegant to simply remove the two handed tag and only include a one-handed tag. It might seem odd at first, but it then allows one-handed to simply be the bought advantage of getting to use an off-handed weapon/have a hand free. Two handed weapons then naturally have the advantage of getting to have more tags because they aren't "taxed" by taking one-handed. I like the basic idea of this system a lot, but it seems messy right now.

                                                06-22-2021, 07:44 PM
                                                I got around to reading a bit of the previews and just skimmed a few of the charms, the sorcery system and the antagonist section.

                                                I can't agree with most people claiming that the base system got somehow significantly simpler. That may change when I playtest it at some point after the official release, but my first impression was that in most parts the options increased thus adding depth. I like this, but this does not make the game simpler in any form.

                                                How the split between power and initiative plays out I can't say, but my prediction is that combat isn't significant faster or easier. I may be biased in that case since the proposed rule of "everyone decides when they act" for initiative is a no-go at my table. They will talk endlessly about the most optimal course of action instead of doing stuff dragging a single action over an hour or more. Having one side act and then the other I as a GM dislike heavily. So I'd go with regular initiative, which is just an additional value to keep track of compared to vanilla Ex3 combat, since Power/Initiative aren't the same value anymore.
                                                I like a lot of the changes, I'm interested in the new way hardness/soak are applied, gambits look way better, rush and aim are actually usable and very good and I will try it out expecting it to be a fun system to play with. But -again- I fail to see how it is easier. I suspect that the soak/hardness together with the powercap of 10 makes balancing for the ST a lot easier. So that would be a very big plus for me.

                                                Ability/Attribute reductions are not simplifications in my book. I never had a player that complained that there are too many or that they are somehow bad for the game. This is a solid "meh" from me. Doesn't make transitioning from one version to the other easier when everything on a base level is different.

                                                What I really like and appreaciate is the project/venture system. That may change ingame but one uniform system for crafting, travel, bureacracy, etc. is good stuff. There are a lot of examples in the book so comming up with new stuff/complications will be easy enough. I don't know how good the system will be in the end, but I believe it will be a big improvement. And I think I will test it out in my regular Ex3 game.

                                                Charms are drastically reduced in number and the design is also simplified, reducing complexity. This is where Essence really shines. Everything is a lot more accessible. Concerning balance I can't really speak with authority as I haven't played it and only read a few charms, but the power level seems to be all over the place. I believe the very unclear written Step based attack resolution is also part of that. There is a lot of weird interactions or applications that are not clear or borderline broken, which may or may not be fine. Some charms are just written in a very confusing way. Some charms are just useless.

                                                I despise the "rest" like DnD mechanics for recovery and will rip them out as soon as I got a grip on the system.

                                                The social system is a big question mark for me right now. I honestly don't really understand it, but from what I could gather is that I will probably ignore it and do it the Ex3 core way. I dislike virtues. That they can also add to social influence in addition to intimacies makes things more complex, no? The way to "succeed" at a social action is confusing, why would I need to persuade someone with additional successes if my original goal of persuading someone has been met with the difficulty in step 1? This feels like a bad adaption from the Scion 2nd Edition Stunt system.
                                                Also, isn't this the only part of Essence where this mechanic is used? Wasn't the goal to reduce the amount of sub systems instead of increasing them?
                                                Most of the dynamic of 3e Core social influence will still be there I guess (reading and then influencing the opposition) but everything is done in a confusing manner.
                                                As I said I don't really understand it (weird for a simplified system...) so everything above is even more subjective than everything else.

                                                Can't say anything about naval combat.

                                                From what I've seen I love the antagonist section. I love the qualities. Very good stuff. Exalted needs more like this.

                                                What really bothers me. And I mean it bothers me A LOT. Even more than the "rest" mechanics is the new difficulty scale. 3-10 seems absolutely absurd to me. I really hope that somewhere in the preview will be advice for Ex3 Core STs to convert difficulties from the 1-5 scale to the new one. On mechanical level this is a major nerf to everybody. A 5+5 mortal was really worth mentioning, being able to do almost inhumane stuff with a good chance. Now the almost impossible stuff is really almost impossible even for the best of the best and not achievable without the use of divine power.
                                                This is probably the most impactful thing in the whole book.

                                                I talked about it with my players and we pretty easily agreed that the focus of narration shifted with that change. Before we treated Exalted as a game where failure was the exception. The fun stuff is dealing with the consequences of being successful in almost everything. This changes completely in Essence. Failure becomes a lot more real and common. Especially if my concerns that the boosted dice pools and the frequency in which those can be rolled or be completely ignored through auto-success charms by the players without completely alarming everything in their direction of their presence are a lot less compared to the core become true.

                                                Our group just had a moment where an Ess4 Solar (280xp or something) failed a Stamina+Resistance check against Arrow Frog Venom causing a chain reaction into crippling injury (death) against a few hundred mortals. Then I think about the new scale, think back and realize that everyone would have been crippled more than once or twice with all the stunts they have pulled. The charms I have yet to read need to do a lot to make an Essence character even comparable capable to an Ex3 Core character. Even more so since Exellencies are now purchases.

                                                With all that in mind I think Essence is really a whole different game. It makes regular stuff a lot more challenging and I think the designers had a completely different vision of what Exalted should be compared to the authors of the core.

                                                I'll probably do a little 2-3 session one-shot in the essence 3 area. I'm not convinced yet that more will be fun. For me it sounds less like walking gods trying to save and restore the world from certain destruction or doom it and every other world alltogether and more like "Storyteller DnD but always 2 CR less for the enemy encounter".

                                                The base system seems more complex with more fun options, the ST probably needs to put in a lot less work which is really nice, that the whole cast can be played is awesome, charms seem weird on first glance but are a lot easier for players and most of those that I read had really good effects and seemed fun.
                                                But I don't think that the kind of players that really like Essence will like the Core.
