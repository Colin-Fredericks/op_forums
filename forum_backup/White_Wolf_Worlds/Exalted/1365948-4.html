                                I'm a bit sad because I seem to arrive after the war, but I'll post some of my comments on the discussion nonetheless.

                                ​DrLoveMonkey JohnDoe244 I don't think we agree on what is asymmetric gameplay ? (I'm not saying I have the good definition, just we don't seem to think about it in the same way). To me, asymmetric is when the ST doesn't work the same way as the PC's. It doesn't mean the ST is stronger, or the ST is weaker, just that they are different, and use different tools to function and represent their action on the game. The games of the Apocalypse have asymmetric gameplay because the ST rolls no dice and only functions through narrative opposition and calling rolls on the players. DD is actually mainly symmetric because most NPC's have the same functioning as the PC's (AC, HP, scores based on their statistics, spells, etc ...) but have asymmetric elements as well (DD5 has lair actions, epic actions, powers recharging on a d6).

                                In this perspective, to take an example (that I don't think this is necessarily the best one as said Lanic, but it's just an example), what are these X tracks of initiative per turn ? Should the PC's be angry because Deathlord Kejak of the Changing Moon do it and the PC's don't ? The answer is no, and here is why : it's not a "Charm" in the game, it's game mechanic of making Kejak works to explicitly portray the "long complicated battle against a master that can challenge 5 heroes alone" (if you buy into that trope). This does not mean that Kejak always has 5 initiative tracks, this means the way of representing his prowess through this fight against the PC's, and make it closer to the mentioned trope, is through this mechanic. Why the players do not need it ? Because when the players are masters at Essence 5, and they want to crush some newbie, the system already supports that : they play normally and crush them, because they are master at Essence 5, and with the Core Rules, except if the ST is an asshole that makes the "newbie" be 5 Dawn supernal in brawl, they do it.

                                Then your next line is "But there is already a way to abstract the power of the characters in the setting and these are Charms why invent something that does the same thing but different" and I will answer, as usual, by "Yeah but symmetric gameplay is bad and asymmetric gameplay is good" (personal opinion of course).

                                Concerning the example with Aang : The only difference between a DB or a Solar would be the narrative. I think the trope that Aang is a Solar is the most obvious one because he is literally the chosen one of the prophecy that will bring balance and peace to the earth by overthrowing the Evil Empire that rule the world with an Iron Fist, returning from a slumber of a hundred years. You can't make it more Solar than that. Even if Aang didn't have any superpower, you can't make it more Solar Exalted than that. We could of course think of Aang like another splat (a Lunar rallying different frightened faction against the Empire around Creation, a lost egg that rebels against the unfair new order of the Empire, etc ...) and it would be just as interesting, I just think it's less obvious at first.

Unless you have some knowledge they want, you don’t even factor into their day planner. Which is pretty cool, you’re starting out lowly heroes, but then as you progress from there and through the Dungeon of the Mad Mage you grow and learn and eventually become a power that can rival those titans who’s shadow you used to stand in. That’s not what I want from my Solar campaigns in Exalted, for the most part.

He was someone that even the greatest sorcerers in the world had to take seriously. Not quake in fear and surrender to, but somebody who would definitely factor into their plans. If there’s a big meeting of the south-eastern sorcerers, I get an invite, and when I speak at it, the people present listen because I am a Solar who has poured his soul into the occult, and that makes me their equal. Or at least almost their equal. It’s not like I can cast Burning Hands and they can cast Meteor swarm, so I have to sit at the kids table for now.

Which for DnD or even other Exalt types is fine. I’m good with being granted an audience with the Blackstaff and have her just totally not care about us at all really, just offhandedly dealing with us as she goes about her day. If I’m playing Solars it’s because I don’t want that.

                                                            Mortals and Sidereals can't (without great personal danger) fight five Solars at once. They just can't. And it's bad for the setting if they can. And, fundementally, that's not what Exalted is about. They can fight Solars 5 on 5. They can fight Solars One on One. They don't just get to beat Fice Solars because they are elders.
                                                            If you don't want your Elder Exalted to fight a circle of Exalted with house rules to make the fight resort to a certain trope some of the other players want to, it's fine.

                                                            I agreee that we're "talking past each other" (as the saying is in English) and that it's fine to disagree.

                                                            But why should Kejak get to be a Big Bad Boss who can fight five Solars at once, but Blind Frog not get to be a Big Bad Boss?

                                                            Why is it ok for Kejak to break the rules, but not a mortal?
                                                            Because the rules represent a certain narrative. Kejak is a Big Bad Boss because he is literally a TV Trope (https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/OldMaster) that totally fits the genre, and so we try to find tools to do it in different ways because a lot of people has not been convinced in practice by the functioning of fights against a Single Big Boss (see this thread for example). Blind Frog could I guess as well be a TV Trope (The lucky guy that beats the odds), and if you want to houserule for him go do it, but you'll notice I haven't seen tons of threads on this forum about "How do I make a fight against 1 very lucky guy ???", but I have seen quite a few "How do I make a Big Boss that challenge a group of Solar ?" and so we are looking for solution for this. It happens that a lot of these solutions comes from different other RPGs, because they have been tested and useful over the years, like for example multi-actions from DD. It doesn't mean the character is "beyond the power ceiling of the world", it means that the way of representing his power through a specific encounter against the PC is through this mechanic, to give a different dynamic to the fight.

                                                            To took one of your good examples : You taught me (truly a good advice) to ignore enemies that were useless in fights when they were not good enough for fight, in order to simplify combat. It's a good advice, and I take it. It's of course inspired, for example, from the rule that says "If a character is too weak just make a roll of (Dexterity + brawl) against him and be done with it". So obviously, when a Solar optimized PC encounter a DB freshman with 7 in fighting abilities, I won't spend my time doing a real fight and apply your advice. But what happens the way around ? Would you just look at a player with a freshman DB encountering a Solar that wants to fight him, tell the player "Ok roll (Brawl + Dexterity) ? 6 successes ? Well you're dead you can roll a new character." I tend to think you wouldn't do that, and instead try to let player escape or find a way around his imminent death, because the narrative is not the same from the player perspective (disposing of weak enemies vs surviving a killing machine). Does it mean that suddenly "You broke the rules and the setting doesn't make sense anymore ?". Well obviously no, you just adapted the mechanic at your disposal to fit the narrative going on.

                                                            So as I said, if you don't want the narrative to represent of the Old Master Sideral challenging a group of Solar, then don't adapt the rules to the narrative, it's fine.
