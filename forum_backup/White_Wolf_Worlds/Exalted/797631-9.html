        Now I have some time to spare after my first session, I can sink my teeth into this! Apologies if I repeat anything, I haven't been through the thread, and this is from the perspective of not having played through the crafting rules in-game, and with only having built one crafter PC.

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        Fixing Craft

        The tweaks:
        Mostly onboard with these. Exceptions and comments to follow.


        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        4. Expand your definition of a "Project" to include anything that might provide inspiration for crafting. Discussing the design of a castle with fellow architects can be a Basic Project. Making a scale model of a manse can be a Major Project. Experimenting with chemicals, teaching an apprentice, studying a behemoth whose powers you want to emulate...they can all be Projects.
        I'm at a bit of a loss as to what this is meant to help, particularly as architecture is potentially now shoehorned into Basic Projects. I don't see designing large-scale structures as being that simple. Maybe there's a category mistake in my thinking - that structure design IS simple, it just needs specialised knowledge.

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        5. Craft is one ability, divided into areas of expertise the same way Lore is.
        Makes total sense, as Craft is otherwise a huge XP sink. Apart from the "like Lore" bit. Going on Stephen's comments in the Not-Quite-Newb thread, Lore gets a specialisation to reflect the person's education, with areas of expertise represented by specialities. For example, a graduate over here might have Law (Harvard), with specialities in Criminal Law, Copyright Law and International Human Rights Law. Things like Lore (dynast education) is apparently a thing. I can't see how craft could work in the same way, unless it's simply "you get a free speciality when you purchase Craft dots for the first time, and need a relevant speciality to attempt a Craft roll at all".

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        6. First Age wonders are just really powerful artifacts. Repairing them will often take odd components, but that's because you need appropriate materials to repair anything.
        I... took this as read anyway. Or are you explicitly ditching any reference to First Age stuff in-system? Again, I almost took this as read, but the core seems to be building them differently.

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        7. Steal the "assess item" action from the full rewrite below. (You can probably do this by the book, but I want to put some emphasis on it.)

        8. Look over the Charms I've written and take some that have the Agnostic keyword. Maybe change the names, if necessary.

        9. Look over the Charms in the book and ditch any that you don't want to play with.
        My first thought on reading this was "clever, a gateway drug into the hard stuff you're offering". But I'm overly cynical, I feel.

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        10. Replace the "When finishing your project upholds, furthers, or protects one of your character’s Intimacies" objective with "When finishing your project involves roleplaying your character in an entertaining and appropriate way".

        11. Replace the "When finishing your project produces a clear in-game gain for your character" objective with "When finishing your project moves the story forward or opens up interesting possibilities for future play".

        12. Replace the "When finishing your project causes another character to gain or strengthen an Intimacy toward you" with "When finishing your project provides character development to another character or illustrates an interesting detail of the setting".
        I feel like this is kind of the intent of the original text of the game as written. RAW just implies that the plot of the game should be about adding roleplaying depth and motivation through a character's intimacies, whereas your adjustments make that more explicit. I guess I just see much of these as being almost two sides of the same coin, with your rules being less about systems engagement and more about direct engagement. But that said, I hugely dislike broad principles as intimacies and prefer to load up on ties, so I guess it's quite abusable in its way.

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        Craft can also be used to assess crafted items. This is resolved with a (Perception + Craft) roll, with success allowing the crafter to determine how old an item is, how well it's made, what it's made of, and what condition it's in. A strong success may also allow the crafter to identify the maker if their style is distinctive.
        This makes sense, but it makes Frugal Merchant Method pointless.

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        A character making a painting, a sculpture, or another emotionally-impactful object may attempt to encode a social influence attempt in it with (Charisma or Manipulation + Craft). Such influence must be vague, and must play on an Intimacy to do anything other than inspire emotion. Intimacies exploited by art are always considered to be Minor, and characters who don't possess the targeted Intimacy may react unpredictably; for example, a painting that uses love of the Realm to make people like you will likely make you unpopular among people who hate the Realm.
        Really like this.

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        A character can work on (Craft/2, rounded up) projects at once, making progress on them all at once by using the downtime required by one project to work on another. An Artifact or Manse counts as two projects, so Craft 3 is required to attempt one.
        I love this simplification, but it still enables mass production. I really don't like the "work on everything at once" mechanic, which this doesn't fix. It's still better than keeping track of vast numbers of slots, though.

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        Only essence-wielders may attempt to create Artifacts and Manses. Doing so requires an extended roll. The difficulty, interval, and goal number are set by the rating of the Artifact or Manse, while the terminus is set by the crafter's available resources.[/FONT][/COLOR]

        Making a X-dot Artifact has a difficulty of Y, a goal number of Z...
        This is lovely. Nice and clear, with ACTUAL TARGETS! Although I'm still curious about non-artefact projects. Is a town a four- or five-dot artefact, for example?

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        I made the difficulties increase so that Terrestrials and other weaker crafters could use magic that adds intervals to the Terminus. It seemed like an elegant way to push them towards smaller projects.
        Although that assumes people will follow the logic of the system and not be "I want to build THE BESTEST MANSE EVER, SCREW THE SYSTEM!" as a Dragon-blooded. I'm always a little leery about systemic encouragement, despite my above comments on intimacies (I think that's one of the few examples where it actually works).

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        There are no temporary or single-use Artifacts. However, there are Artifacts which serve only to create temporary or single-use magical items. For example, the recipe for a magical injury-healing soup could be an Artifact. The rating of such an Artifact depends on the power of the item created, how easily it can be used to create that item, and how often it can create that item. It's possible for this type of Artifact to take the form of pure information, which can easily be copied into any number of books. Information Artifacts are obviously more useful than physical objects, and as such they have higher ratings than similar non-information artifacts.
        This makes sense I guess...

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        Groups that intend to use these rules should discuss beforehand whether they intend to pace Artifact completion rolls by time in setting or by time spent playing. The two methods roughly equalize if there's a story per season and three sessions per story.
        These feel like REALLY quick stories. My groups (regardless of game) never finish a plot in three sessions.

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        These intervals don't assume any specific amount of work per day, but a character may only accumulate rolls towards one Artifact at a time and may only spend their rolls when they have a chance to sit down in their workshop.
        Isn't this contradicted by the "make progress on them all at once" during downtime? Or if there is a difference, in that you only focus on story-relevant artefacts during stories? If so, this needs to be clarified.

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        Moreover, a character may only accumulate rolls if they're making a good-faith effort to work on the Artifact when they get the chance. A character who spends two sessions (using the story-based intervals) or two months (using the time-based intervals) in prison can make four rolls on a two-dot Artifact the next time they have a chance to work on it. This represents the character thinking about and planning their creation while unable to work on it properly, and it's up to the ST how far it can be taken.
        *muttermuttermutterandthereIwashopingforsomethings ensiblelikehoursworkedsuggestionsmuttermuttermutte r*

        This isn't a problem with your system as such, but I cannot for the life of me understand why every craft system out there is allergic to the idea of concrete time requirements for projects. Hand-waving "oh you spend a few hours a day on this and that" just makes my skin crawl and make me want to hit things. And now I will stop before I start foaming at the mouth and ranting.

        Also, isn't the allowance of spending time while in prison or whatever a bit generous? I don't expect that Creation's prisons generally let you have exercise times, visiting hours or other such things of the modern prison system, including writing materials for the design process. I would expect a Craft equivalent of Mind-Scribing Method to enable that sort of thing, not for it to be assumed that people can just think things out.

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        Having a bunch of unfinished Artifacts sitting around is cool, but you can't advance them all at once.
        Explicitly contradicted by your downtime rules.

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        To find the terminus for an Artifact-creation roll, add together the factors for their workshop and their materials. If you're missing either, you can't even start. Then add bonuses for extra time taken, the help of others, complementary abilities, relevant magic, and anything else that seems appropriate.
        Nice, but I got really confused by its positioning. This would flow better if placed under the costings, rather than going on a tangent about the kinds of thing that can and can't be created; finish the general system before discussing caveats.

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        Workshop & Material termini stuff
        Beautiful. Actually gives meaning to the terminus in craft rolls, rather than making an arbitrary roll.

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        If you use up all of your rolls, all is not lost. Just ask the ST what you have to do to earn another roll, and they'll tell you. Chances are it won't be easy.

        Is there actually a fail condition here? I feel like that should give the chance of one, but then again "ask the ST" is a plot hook generator, so I'm not sure what removing or lessening the options to carry on the project would solve.

        Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
        Notes On Crafting Magic

        These make nice thematic sense, but I have no idea what Dragon Kings are. I also get the sense that "like Solars but deathier" won't quite be the whole of the Abyssal wheelhouse.

        Will post up thoughts on the charms soon.

                I finally have some time to look at the charms! Note that in the interest of post length, I'll only be calling out things that I am uncertain about, disagree with or answers a question that you put in the post. So apologies if this feels all confrontational - silence is agreement.

                Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
                The Agnostic keyword indicates that the Charm with it functions equally well under the original rules or my rewritten rules. Most of them are aimed at giving people something non-Artifact-related to do with Craft.

                The rest of this post is now taken from the updated version.

                Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
                Supreme Masterwork Focus
                This Charm may be repurchased at Craft 5 Essence 2+. This allows the Solar to spend an additional point of willpower to double 8s on a Craft roll instead.
                This may be because I didn't read your complete rewrite afresh, but I did a double-take thinking "what's happened to the gold point?" On rereading your rewrite, you don't actually mention anywhere that you're ditching craft points. Or are you? I assume so, but the rewrite doesn't say either way.

                Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
                Even with the merit, each success counts as two dice from Charms.
                Hmmm... any reason for making this count as two charm dice? I guess that the most common thing to do with this is to have mortals use it, so it probably won't come up, but is there a reason for effectively penalising Essence-users for using the example?

                Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
                I wanted to come up with a method of mass production that felt Solar.
                The pedant in me is saying "well, it's probably more batch production or an assembly line really..." Nevermind, ignore me.

                ​
                Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
                Shattering Grasp
                This Charm supplements a feat of strength to break a crafted object or structure. It allows the Solar to use Craft in place of Athletics. If the attempt is successful, the Solar may take the target apart neatly instead of smashing it. In addition, if the Solar spends a few extra minutes performing the attempt, she doubles 8s on the roll and may attempt feats with a Strength minimum up to twice her Strength score.
                This Charm is fully compatible with Athletics Charms.
                Lovely system adjustment. But I'm not entirely sure why it should be compatible with Athletics charms. If disassembly is done through knowledge and not strength, why do things that bolster feats of strength give bonuses to that? And if that's the case, are there any that would give bonuses to the roll at all? I'm struggling to think of things.

                ​
                Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
                Master Craftsman's Eye
                This charm has no duration. Given Gaze of the Great Maker (the point where I noticed this), I'd imagine it's around one action or so.

                Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
                Arete-Shifting Prana
                Upon purchasing this Charm, the Solar gains two additional Craft specialties for each Craft specialty they have. Further specialties may be purchased for 1xp, and take one third of the normal time to train.
                This a little too good on a first read, as it's effectively an xp multiplier, which isn't an effect I've seen anywhere else. Although limiting it to specialities shouldn't make things too terrible, as you say. It might just be a matter of taste. I can however see it being a problem if you have 2 crafters in a group - that sort of set-up kind of needs niche protection.

                Regardless of my liking of it, I think it needs renaming - it's not so much Arete-Shifting as Arete-Multiplying now.

                Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
                Awe-Inspiring Feats Of Craft
                A character who wishes to resist this may do so at no cost, but most people don't.
                This feels fuzzy. Leaves the question "why would anyone want to?" unanswered. Perhaps rephrase to "A character with a reason to resist this may do so at no cost", or "A character with an existing intimacy that would contradict the new intimacy does not form one". That shifts the reason for its dismissal from ST fiat to character motivation.

                Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
                The Craft Of War
                While this Charm is active, the Solar may use Dexterity + Craft to "attack" when attempting gambits. The Solar doubles 10s on the Initiative roll when doing so. Gambits enacted with this Charm represent battlefield applications of the Craft skill, and as such some gambits are not possible with this Charm while others are not possible without it.
                Really like this idea, but I think this language could be a bit tighter. Instead of " the Solar may use Dexterity + Craft to 'attack' when attempting gambits", change to "the Solar may make craft gambits using Dex + Craft in place of their initial decisive attack dice pool". A bit more wordy, but it feels clearer to me.

                Words As Workshop Method

This feels lean and, although not quite intuitive (not sure any craft tree could be that) like it serves the narrative more than it does bean-counting. I will be endeavouring to persuade my wife to use it and rejig her character sheet around it for our current game.

                        Originally posted by Xerxes View Post
                        Any reason for this exclusion? Part of the return of the wonders of the First Age is better sorcery, surely?
                        Sorcerous Workings are unique in Exalted system design. Most systems in Exalted (past or present) have been splat-neutral, and between-splat variation has been handled through Charms that interact with the system (combined with normal dice caps). For example, Craft is heavily balanced in this fashion; the difficulties are the same for everyone, and the primary way the game expresses "what feats are Solars capable of doing?" is through the Power line of Charms that are effectively capbreakers.

                        Sorcerous Workings are different; the system itself encodes "what can DBs do; what can Lunars do; what can Solars do" through the Circles and the dice caps. If you run the math you can actually see stuff like "oh, a Dragon-blooded attempting an Ambition 3 Celestial working will need X means to succeed and will take Y months", and how this corresponds reasonably to what you would want the actual probabilities to be to be consistent with the setting as written*. You can see how the Lunars crafting their Destiny-warping-thingy over the Caul was a project they could plausibly do but only with a lot of time and effort: this is probably an Ambition 3 Solar Working, which would probably require about 8-10 Means and several years for an experienced Lunar sorcerer with maxed dicepools and applicable stunts on every roll.

                        As a result of this, the actual design space of "Charms to boost Workings rolls" is not a good one.

                        *they are a little too easy to be perfectly compatible but that's mostly due to the benefits of stunting, which I chalk up to PC exceptionalism
