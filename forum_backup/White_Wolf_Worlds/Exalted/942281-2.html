                        I would just chime in to say that, in my experience, people can have very, very different experiences with game mechanics. So, I would take with a grain of salt any claim like "this system is phenomenal" or "this is unplayable". For example, a lot of people found the Fair Folk 1E book unplayable and impossible to understand -- with the exception of the Mountain Folk chapter, which they praised highly for clearness, cleverness, and coolness. I found the Mountain Folk chapter one of the dumbest, least original, and most broken pieces of work in 1E. But the rest of the Fair Folk book ... oh, what a work of genius: beautiful, clever, well-written, easy to understand, and incredibly fun to play. The best product produced by WW, ever!

                        That said, my group and I found the 2E mechanics painful -- overly complicated, and not well balanced. So much that we never made the switch from 1E except for a handful of "trial' sessions. With the possible exception of the massive re-write known as 2.5E (with which I have almost no familiarity), I would recommend 1E over 2E.

                        As for 3E... Well, I have played three sessions of 3E so far, but I can say this. It's nice, I like it better than 2E. However.
                        First, while you *can* play with just the corebook... I really miss the other books. From gods and demons, to other Exalted, to the Fair Folk... a handful of QCs only go so far (though I can happily live without any "books of artifacts" or location books -- for the latter, I'll just use Scavenger Sons, which I think beats handily any other location product of the Exalted line).
                        Second, mechanically 3E is nice, but it's not perfect. It feels as if it is based on very sound design principles, with some rather shoddy work on top. It's not just the craft charmset. It's a lot of little things, the way charms are written in a slightly ambiguous fashion that makes people ask "does "until next action" include next action" or "how are clashes and counterattacks dealt with" etc. Then again, the fact that the principles are sound means that it's not too hard to rebuild the top layer to one's liking (in fact, some fans have already done great work in this direction, so you might find the work done for you).

                                                    Originally posted by Lundgren View Post

                                                    The problem is what that leads to when you also have cheap, spammable, perfect defenses.

                                                    ST: The Lunar attacks you with his grand goremaul which can kill you in one hit.

                                                    PC: I perfectly defend for 3 motes, because if I don't, I'm dead. This leaves me with 57 motes of Essence.

                                                    ST: Okay. Your turn.

                                                    Player: I make an attack against the Lunar with my grand grimscythe which can kill him in one hit.

                                                    ST: He perfectly defends for 4 motes because if he doesn't, he's dead. Your Essence Sight tells you he has 63 motes remaining. His turn now. He attacks you with his grand goremaul which can kill you in one hit.

                                                    PC: I perfectly defend for 3 motes because if I don't, I'm dead. This leaves me with 54 motes of Essence.

                                                    ST: Okay. Your turn.

                                                    Player: I make an attack against the Lunar with my grand grimscythe which can kill him in one hit.. Since he used Charm X which has Y weakness, I will attack him in a way to take advantage of Y and bypass his perfect defense.

                                                    ST: He perfectly defends by using Z Charm which doesn't have that weakness for 4 motes, because if he doesn't, he'd be dead. Your Essence Sight tells you he has 59 motes remaining. His turn now. He attacks you with his grand goremaul which can kill you in one hit.

                                                    Well, you get the point.

                                                    This is what combat turns into - a slow slog to see which person can bleed the other out of Essence first. Sure players and the ST can try to stunt and spice things up for the attack (which is meaningless since the attack will just be perfectly parried), but at the core, this is what high lethality coupled with cheap spammable attacks leads to.

                                                    Basically it's just kind of boring for everybody involved. If your group managed to avoid that happening, cool. But a lot of people ran into this problem, which was a core facet of the Exalted game engine, and one of the big reasons why they decided to alter it in the new edition.
