                                            Since he can't jump and dodge like he could before his injury, his new armor is better off being heavier.

                                            3) Kasai is ........ mostly healed from the Lunar fight, in the sense that he can use his hands and walk. He still has a few weeks until everything is "back to normal", or as normal as he is going to be, however; some fingers on his left hand are stiff and nonresponsive (and will be for quite some time), his right arm still aches, his ribs still hurt (and twisting and turning his upper body is painful, meaning he needs help rising from bed in the morning, much less fighting), his concussion gives him headaches occasionally, and his dislocated hip troubles him on inclines and in cold/wet weather.

                                            He can fight, but it will be with decent penalties, at least -2 in game-terms.

                                            Note that Kasai's days of fighting directly are...... well, I'm not going to say that they are over, but he has people to do that for him now. While he is still Oathsworn, and therefore expected to fight when the situation calls for it, as a commander he isn't expected to charge into battle. And, while he is expected to fight in his own defense, he technically is "too important" to fight things like honor-duels anymore, although he certainly could if he wanted.

                                            Most Lords have retinues of Oathsworn retainers and armsmen for stuff like that, as well as general work. If/when Masanori swaps sides, that will be his position.

                                            Lord Iysen doesn't fight her own duels, for example. She had Nori for that, as well as her unlanded Oathsworn retainers in Teriake Castle (which is what Kasai was)

                                                        It would appear that most of you want some combination of 1 (push ahead) and 2 (protect flanks), on top of at least seeing what the track-gods are. Coolio

                                                        -----------------------

                                                        You take a long moment to ponder the best approach to this problem. Then, turning to the assembled Tribesmen, you ask,

                                                        “< Just who, or what, are these ‘track-gods’ of which you speak?>”

                                                        The sub-chiefs glance at each other almost incredulously. “< Are you saying that you Vanehani do not know of the black spirits of the deep woods? We knew you didn’t worship them, but forgetting them….>”

                                                        “<No, most Vanehani settlements are in the lowland river valleys. Most people stay out of the woods, for many good reasons. Tell me; what do these spirits do? How did they move your warband so fast? What did you do to appease them?>”

                                                        The Onset war-leader shrugs, “< They patrol the deep wilds, the lands untouched by humankind. If someone disturbs them without the proper ceremonies, they tend to retaliate violently, as they hate civilization and what it stands for. Whenever a new steading or path is built, we make offerings so they stay calmed. To appease them….. well, it depends on what we are doing. Constructing a new farm or clan-stead involves some lengthy ceremonies anyways, so we usually just fold the appeasement into those. For new trails or paths, we usually leave offerings of grain-spirits, bread and flesh at the edges of the forest. I don’t know if the gods themselves take them, or other animals, but so long as the offerings are sufficient, they tend to leave us alone.>”

                                                        “< As for how they moved us so far, so fast….>” He chuckles uneasily. “< The Tribes might be more familiar with the actions of gods than you Vanehani, but that was …. Something else. All the bushes and trees and rocks in the path of our travel moved out from before us, then moved back into their old positions behind us. We basically traveled as the crow flies from our new steadings to here, through the heart of the forest.>”

                                                        You turn and look back at the Akibara again. The darkness under the trees has taken a decidedly unfriendly turn. “< These spirits…. Are they everywhere? If I walk under the margins of the trees right now, will I meet one?>”

                                                        The Nemasket subchief shakes her head in affirmation. “< A possibility. At the very least, they will know you are there. You know how, when you go hunting in the deep woods, you sometimes get this feeling like you are being watched, like eyes are fixed on you, yet when you turn around, there is nothing there? That is the spirit, watching you to see what you do.>”

                                                        “< Will they respond to a summons?>”, only to get a shrug.

                                                        “< We usually just leave offerings out for them, meeting them is rare. Generally, if you meet one face-to-face, they are unhappy about something. I don’t think that has happened in living memory.>”

                                                        You draw yourself upright, pulling Moonlight Over Water from its sheath. “< Well then, we had best get started. Will you join me in meeting with these spirits?>”

                                                        The warleaders agree, albeit reluctantly, and you call up one of the militiamen to fetch you “lunch” from the camp canteen; a large kettle of noodles in broth, a jar of ale sweetened with apples, and a rack of pork ribs.

                                                        It occurs to you as you walk into the margins of the Akibara, burdened under your load, that you might not be the best Immaculate.

                                                        Almost as soon as you pass under the branches of the trees, the light dims substantially, and you have to squint to see more than a couple feet in front of you in the gloom. Unlike other forests, which are usually filled with the sounds of birds and other animals, interspersed with clearings and sunlight, the Akibara is …… dense. Light struggles to penetrate the canopy above, and the thick turf and moss swallows the sound of your footsteps. In the gloom, there is little underbrush, and the trees and bamboo grows close and thick. The only sound you can hear is the whispering of wind, passing through the upper branches of the forest.

                                                        Only a few moments after passing into the forest, you start to feel a crawling on your skin, like someone is watching, with great interest as to what your insides look like. You curse, drawing your lips back from your teeth in a snarl, and the Nemasket warchief lays a hand on her axe almost instinctively. She removes her hand, embarrassed, but the feeling doesn’t abate.

                                                        “< Well, they obviously know we are here, Chieftain.>” She says in a whisper.

                                                        “< Indeed.>” Lips still drawn back from teeth, as you glance around the trees. You’ve been in plenty of forests in your time, but never felt anything like this before!

                                                        After a few more moments of struggling through the trees, you come into the closest thing the Akibara has to a clearing: a falling tree cleared a path through the canopy, letting some small amount of sunlight through. Instead of trees, the space is filled with a dense thicket of brambles, thornbushes, and small saplings, intertwined and struggling for the limited sunlight.

                                                        A good a place as any.

                                                        Setting down the packed offering, you spike your spear deep into the turf and, going down on both knees before the trees, speak loudly.

                                                        “I am the Koshaku Qianke Kasai, Diamyo Jianghu-han, the legally-recognized ruler of these lands. I call upon the spirits of the forest to hear my pleas, and appear before me now!”

                                                        The words are swallowed up by the woods without even an echo, and the feeling of being watched only intensifies. Regardless, nothing else happens, and you are about to stand up before the wind starts blowing. At first, you think it is ‘just’ the wind rustling through the exposed branches of the clearing, but as you listen, the rustling and creaking of leaves and branches starts forming words, a toneless voice coming from nowhere and everywhere at once.

                                                        “I care not about your name, titles or laws, mortal, but I will hear your words. Leave your offerings and say your piece.”

                                                        Mouth suddenly dry, it takes you a moment to find your words.

                                                        “In these woods, there is a grand encampment, of bandits and other law-breakers, murderers and criminals. I seek to wipe them out, in effort to protect my people. To that end, I need to move troops to their location, an action greatly hindered by the forest surrounding the hills. If you would help me, our presence in these woods would be minimal, and over-and-done with quickly.”

                                                        There is no answer from the whispering wind, and if it weren’t for the skin crawling up and down your back, you would have thought the god left. Instead, the wind suddenly picks up, whipping the trees and leaves into a flurry of movement, shrieking and moaning in the gale. You lift an arm to protect yourself from the wind-raised leaves and detritus, but stop and stare in awe at the sight taking place before you.

                                                        In the center of the clearing, the wind condenses, drawing scattered leaves, pine needles and twigs to itself. As the cyclone gathers strength, it pulls in black bog-water, larger sticks and branches, and the skull and antlers of a long-dead stag into itself.

                                                        After but a few moments of shrieking wind, the gale subsides, and before you stands a large (easily horse-sized!) dog-like thing of the accumulated forest detritus. Long and lean, its ‘fur” is made of pine needles, its flesh of rotten leaves and bog-mud, its bones of white-and-black-mottled decaying branches. Its head is crowned by the skull and antlers of the deer, and as you watch in mounting awe (and not a spot of terror), the eyes of the skull blaze with a deep green light, the color of bamboo or oak leaves. Even ten feet away, all you can smell is the ripe odor of rotting vegetation and stagnant water long cut off from sunlight. It…… doesn’t look healthy (can gods even get sick?), with its ‘ribs’ clearly outlined under its ‘skin’, even exposed in some spots.

                                                        The forest god huffs, stalking around you on silent footfalls, before sitting back on its haunches in front of the offerings. Silently, not from an unmoving mouth, but through the rustling leaves, it speaks;

                                                        “Well then, mortal. I accept your offerings, at least for now, and show myself before you. Your words, and your intended actions, are of great concern to me. Long have I watched these interlopers, who cut down my trees and shit in my rivers. Due to ancient binding agreements with the place-gods and ancestor-ghosts of Dun Nashuon, as you call it, I have not been able to remove them from my forest.”

                                                        The god’s lips pull back from its teeth in a feral grin, exposing the straggly-teeth of white-bleached branches, and you are blasted by it’s fetid breath. It takes all you can to not show your disgust on your face, much less pull back.

                                                        “You are not bound by the same agreements as I, are you?" It was not a question. “You can freely enter the boundaries of the walls, forcibly remove them from the fortress…….”

                                                        The god looks away from you and starts feasting on the offerings, ravenously devouring the food and beer.

                                                        “Fine, I will allow you to move through my forest unhindered. Although…….” It pulls its head from the kettle, noodles and broth running in rivulets down through the muck.

                                                        “I will make you the following proposition.” The blazing green eyes turn from you to the assembled Hill-Tribe chieftains, who all-but wither under its gaze.

                                                        “These mortals made great sacrifice to me, so that they may pass through my territory at great speed. I would make you the same offer, if you sacrifice to me as they did.”

                                                        You gesture to the food and drink, confused, “Forgive me, spirit, but as far as I know, these foodstuffs are the correct offering. What, pray tell, did the Tribes offer?”

                                                        The god snorts, resuming its feast. “This offering was sufficient for me to allow you to enter these woods unmolested , nothing more. For anything else, I require a sacrifice with a bit more ….. body.”

                                                        You shrug, still confused, “I have draft-animals, oxen and donkeys. Would those be sufficient?”

                                                        “No, but they would be most welcome.” It grins again, then continues “ In order to move the forest around your path, I require …… more. More breath, more blood, more body. I require Essence.”

                                                        It’s jaws snap shut, shattering branch-teeth and scattering droplets of broth and bog-water over you.

                                                        “ As even you mortals can clearly tell, I am …. Unwell. My physical corpus reflects the health of the Akibara, which is polluted and unclean with the spirits of the dead. The rotten stench of the Underworld permeates my soil, pollutes the water and kills the trees.”

                                                        You swallow, mouth dry. “Spirit, what caused the Underworld to enter the lands of the living?”

                                                        The god snarls, “The spirits of the restless dead draw the Underworld to them, like moths to a flame. While the spirits walk, the Akibara is unclean, and I remain weak. Until I regain Essence, I cannot help you, not truly, as much as I would wish, for I want the mortal interlopers gone as much as you do.”

                                                        “So, what does this have to do with sacrifices, spirit?”

                                                        The god grins again, “The mortals behind you offered me the sacrifice of a still-living human, blood and bone, body and breath, so that they might move quickly and unhindered through the Akibara. If you make sacrifice of five mortals, that will be enough for me to move the very land itself, at least for a short while.”

                                                        In sudden horror, you recoil, then glare at the assembled Tribesmen behind you. “<What the fuck did you do?>”

                                                        “<What do you mean? None of us speak Riverspeak, so we couldn’t follow along>”

                                                        “< The god says you made human sacrifice to it!>”

                                                        “<Oh, that.>” The chief shrugs, “< Yeah, we gave the spirit a bandit that was captured. They were going to be killed anyways, so we figured we might as well get some use from them.>”

                                                        Disgusted, you turn your face away from the Tribesmen. Human sacrifice is Anathemic to civilized society.

                                                        After regaining control of your emotions, you speak again to the god, ‘Spirit, is there perhaps any other way we might come to this deal, so you might regain the necessary power?”

                                                        It rests its head on its forepaws, staring balefully at you, “Hmm…. So long as the spirits of the dead wander the trees, I cannot regain Essence at a meaningful rate.”

                                                        “Just….. where are these spirits of the dead coming from, exactly?”

                                                        “The tombs under Dun Nashuon groan with the spirits of the dead, mortal, disturbed from their slumber by the interlopers. Other than that, for the last few years, mortals have been leaving their old and weak in these woods to die, especially during the cold of winter. Most of those ghosts pass on quietly, but several have stayed here, protected from the light of the Unconquered Sun by the shade of the trees. If anything, those ghosts are more troublesome than the ones in Dun Nashuon, as they ceaselessly wander in wrath and sorrow, and spread the Essence of the Underworld wherever they travel.”

                                                        You sit, lost in thought. You’ve never dealt with a ghost before, but the priests in the Immaculate Shrine in Liangzhou should know funerary rights. Or…… the Akibara is one of the main places you planned to expand to, when the population of Jianghu grew too large for the river-valleys to hold. Maybe….. if you offer to build this god a shrine, and dedicate the farms and fields of the new homesteads to it, it would regain power that way? But those would take time.

                                                        After a moment, you speak to the god.
                                                        1. I will offer you human sacrifice, as distasteful as I find it. You will have your ‘Essence”, as you put it, and in return, you will move the very land itself out of the way of my army. I need the fortress of Dun Nashuon in my hands within 2 weeks!
                                                        2. I will put these spirits of the dead to rest, so you might regain “Essence” with the healing of the Akibara. In return, you will allow my army to build a road to Dun Nashuon.
                                                        3. I eventually plan to settle these lands, spirit. If you allow my army to build a road to Dun Nashuon, I will make you the patron god of the new settlements, and of the Akibara Forest as a whole. I will build you a shrine in the town of Liangzhou, with regular worshippers, as well as shrines along the road. You will gain much more power through worship, as well as regular sacrifices of beer and grain, as much as you can eat!
                                                        4. Yeah ….. no, I’m out. Human sacrifice and ghosts are too much for me.
                                                        5. Something else? (write in)
                                                        OOC:

                                                        The Akibara is based largely off the Aokigahara Forest in Japan. It isn't a Shadowland yet, not quite, but things are ........not kosher.

                                                            A combination of 1, 2 and 3. I reworked this once, so I hope on rereading later that I will think it came out okay:


                                                            I will offer you human sacrifice; although it is against my upbringing. You are part of Creation, so I will abide by your ways inside Akibara, and help you as I can. I do this partly because I plan to settle these lands, spirit. For now, with these who have already know your ways and have already sacrificed. Later there will be other setters. I will make you the patron god of the new settlements, and of the Akibara Forest as a whole. I will build you a shrine in each settlement. You will gain much more power through worship, and I will seek the aid of priests and shamans to send the wandering ghosts on. The abandoning of the sick and old will be forbidden. I would build a road to Dun Nashuon as well, so people could travel without disturbing you. But all of that is for later. For now, the other part is that I would have Dun Nashuon in my hands, as soon as possible. Speed my troops and the interlopers will be routed. The wakened spirits there will be put to rest.

                                                            I would be your ally, I and all of my line, as long as it continues. I will make binding pacts, if you wish it. And if you prefer to wait and see how things turn out, I will also wait, upon your pleasure.
