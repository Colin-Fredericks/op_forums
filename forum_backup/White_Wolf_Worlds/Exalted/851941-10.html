    Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
    We're not in the setting, though. And we're supposed to take the "it was necessary" line seriously. It's not supposed to be the obviously self-serving crap that that line pretty much always is in real life.
    Are we? Says who? I mean, I could say a lot of the books push the view that "it wasn't necessary and led to the Great Contagion; Solars are totally awesome and rad".
    I also don't think you can say "obviously self-serving crap that that line pretty much always is in real life." When the Bolsheviks murdered the Russian royal family, children and all, most of them really believed it was necessary to help end the civil war (with the right side winning: the right side obviously being the communists). They didn't do it just to steal their jewels or something.

    The books present some bad stuff first age Solars did. You can decide if it was necessary yourself. Or, more importantly, characters can decide. So Solars are likely to say it was unnecessary, while Dragonblood are likely to say it was. And this is just the same as in real life, where people talk about ancient rulers and argue about how justified their atrocities were. As a British person, I hear this all the time about British history. "Oh, sure, our Empire killed some people, but overall it was a good thing in the long run." That's what I'd expect Sidereals to say.

    Personally, I'd much rather have the books say "Chejop Kejak and co did this because they believed it was necessary" than "Chejop Kejak and co did this because they're evil dickwads who live evil" because I've already got the Deathlords for that (and that's boring too).

    Bad people often believe themselves to be good people. They justify their bad stuff not only to others, but also to themselves. And I think that makes them more interesting villains for the game.

                                            One of the ways I would shift the roles of the Deathknights and Deathlords is to say that you can't actually serve Oblivion, and you certainly can't derive power from it. Oblivion doesn't put anything in, only remove it. The essence of the Dead, then, is the stuff of preservation, stagnation, finality, and endings that are not endings. That is to say, it is the Essence that maintains the perpetual Underworld.

                                            That's not to say that the Neverborn don't crave Oblivion, or that Nephwracks can't work towards a day (or lack therof) where everything (or lack thereof) is annihilated in Oblivion. But those are their motivations, and their essence itself carries a different allegiance. The secret that the Deathlords might not know (given their embrace of melodrama) and certainly don't tell their Abyssals is that the choice to end the world is not made once and regretted forever after, it is a constant thing made moment by moment that can always be turned away from.

                                                    I think any change to the Deathlords or Abyssals is always ultimately going to be strained because the setting still demands they're bound by the will of the Neverborn, which demands they work towards the overall Big Bad. So I think the Abyssals, and even the Deathlords, could be made a whole lot more relatable, and even playable with a clearer conscious, by making one simple change to the Neverborn:

                                                    Switch "The Neverborn want to kill off the the entire world so they can truly die" to just "The Neverborn want to truly die".

                                                    Take away the requirement that everything else has to die so they can go away, and everything about them and those they empower changes. What if the Neverborn simply don't know how to finally end their existence? Suddenly the setting changes from Lovecraftian entities trying to bring about the end to all things to Lovecraftian entities using formerly human entities as imperfect intermediaries in a search for their own personal oblivion. They were pseudo-killed during the Primordial war, cosmic beings forced into a state of existence so alien to everything before this point that they created a whole new realm in their wake. Now they're probably in a state of constant agony, completely unable to connect to the world they once ruled, and want nothing more than just to end their own pain. Suddenly the Usurpation happens, and the new realm they created is suddenly filled with the ghosts of the beings that were the closest to them when they 'lived', the ones that 'killed' them. But hey, water under the bridge, if anyone can figure out how to finish the job they started, it's their killers, so they reach out, and a few of the solar ghosts hear them, make some sense of their agonized whispers, and are sympathetic, or see an opportunity for power, or see some moral necessity in the eventual death of the Neverborn.

                                                    So now the Deathlords aren't harbingers of the death of everything, but rather more priests, scholars, and conquerers trying to root out the secrets of life and death so that they can bring about their masters' desired end. Of course the whispers of pseudo-dead primordials filtered through their constant agony is anything but clear, no one Deathlord can agree with any of the others how exactly the Neverborn want them to go about finding the secret to their ultimate death. This introduces the potential for a lot more variety in the Deathlords' aims. Sure, some can interpret the whispers as a desire to end the universe, and seek it for reasons ranging from suicidal nihilism to the moral conviction that the Neverborn were wronged and deserve their release even if it means the death of everything else. Others might believe releasing the Neverborn involves recreating the Primordial War that created them in the first place, and see the amassing of ghosts and eventual creation of the Abyssals as steps towards a great but necessary battle to truly 'kill' the Neverborn. Still others might think that finding and control the very nature of Essence might allow them to forcibly alter reality and create new laws that allow for the death of the Neverborn. Or maybe some think the answer lies in subjugating the Wyld and forcing the agents of chaos and potential to create a way for the Neverborn to die.

                                                    Most of the history of the Underworld and its interactions with Creation can remain the same, the intentions merely change. The conflicts between the Deathlords are now over philosophical/theological differences. The release of the Great Contagion could possibly be an attempt by the Duchess to open up a passage to a new reality for the Neverborn to travel to and actually die gone awry, or possibly an experiment by her to create a sickness that could kill even undead gods that got released into Creation. Now the Abyssals can have any number of drives and moral standings and still be bound to the Deathlords and the Neverborn. They could believe the deaths they cause are necessary to finally release the Neverborn from torment. Or they act against a Deathlord who follows the annihilation-of-all philosophy and believe any number of deaths is worth averting the end of everything, much the same as the Bronze Faction just in a different context. Or maybe they believe the Underworld is full of sentient beings who deserve the right to continue their existence, and seek a way to balance the needs of the living and the dead together.
