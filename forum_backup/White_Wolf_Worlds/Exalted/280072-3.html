            I like them because I feel that they encompass "Doing the Impossible" far more than a Solar ever could. For a Solar, nothing IS impossible or even - after a certain point - all that difficult. Terrestrials are faced with far more challenges with less power, and they face them anyway. After the Usurpation, Terrestrials held the world together. They messed up a lot, sometimes even outright lost, but they did it. When the Contagion swept across Creation and Heaven itself recoiled in terror, the Terrestrials maintained order and tried to cure what could not be cured. When the Balorian Crusade threatened to consume all of Reality, it was the Terrestrials who fought them back. Battling unending hordes of nightmares, against which there was no hope or chance, the Terrestrials fought tooth and nail for every bloody inch of Creation that was lost. Even knowing that there could be no victory, they fought until one of their own did one more impossible thing in a long list of impossible things and took control of a weapon noone alive could safely weild, and ended the endless swarms of the wyld.
            They are flawed. They are weak. They are decadent, exploitative, petty, tyrannical and bound by dogma. They are also the true guardians of Creation. They are the ones you can count on to save the day, when nobody else can or will.

                Originally posted by Jen View Post

                Yeah seriously, why do people think that working together is something special that only DB can do anyway.
                I think it doesn't help that the Solar examples and the text in general tends to point towards Solars having to be generally the kind of people who honestly can only be conceived as working together because of enormous need or plain OOC conceit.

                As for why I like Dragonblooded, well, I think part of it is my liking for family themes (I'm one of those people whose characters actually have parents they are in good relations with), and the other part is their underdog status. I came into the game in 2E, and the general assumption by everyone always seemed to be that the Realm is absolutely boned and the Solars are going to knock it down like a sand castle the moment any of them bother, and there's nothing the Dynasty can do. It's very hard to not feel a pang of sympathy for the guys relegated to props by the unstoppable invincible protagonists.

                                Re: teamwork and cooperation (or sorts) and the Dragonblooded vs Solars (and others)...

                                When I think of people who distinguish themselves and lift themselves up through their willingness to stand out beyond the human norm, to be champions and leaders beyond the norm, I don't really think of people who really *need* to work together and for who mutual support is really important.

                                I mean, clearly champions and paragons and people who are above the norm *could* achieve more through teamwork than they could without it (although maybe they'd get on better alone as well - it's not like some people don't). And no man is an island, etc.

                                Cooperation just isn't what I would think of for them compared to a group of people whose motif is fundamental primal matter which must come together to create all the complex and wonderful nature which exists in our world. And I wouldn't expect people whose nature is being champions and paragons to have magic as dedicated to it as people whose motifs are being like the elements and being plebian amongst the Exalted.

                                (And yes Solars probably should be able to have people amongst them whose mythic special, "lifts me up above the norm", paragon and champion talent *is* their ability to cooperate and be part of a team, it's not really what binds them together as a splat in the same way. Its a direction they can take, not essentially part of Solar-ness.).

                                                            I'm thinking more about the idea that, at least within the Scarlet Dynasty, there's a certain balancing act between maintaining bonds of personal loyalty from child to parent, and having an awareness of their abilities and accomplishments and proclivities, while also maintaining a stoic patrician stand-offishness and clear lines of hierarchy. Not so much impersonal as often aloof, and where jockeying for power with fairly concrete and pervasive people is a frequent thing.

                                                            When I think about how the numbers and nature of the Dragon Blooded lead them to cooperative action, I don't think so much of Planateer-type groupings, nor of a big impersonal machine that diffuses the individual, I think about how, if you're a Terrestrial and you need some Terrestrial magic type action taking place in any major city in the country, it's actually possible for there to be some there. Or for the idea that when some big technical project is going on, you can have a large pool of colleagues and peers to all concentrate in one space for it. It's like... because I've been watching The Good Wife lately, I want to say it's like having high-powered lawyers; between their career paths and their education background, a lot of them will have extensive personal relationships with one another that make for various sorts of coordinated activity.

                                                            It's a thing, and a thing of relevance, but I don't quite think needs to be a thing in their Charms. It's an emergent property.
