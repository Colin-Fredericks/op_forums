    Hmmm….

    Several of you want to go attack the Lunar, and save Corbitant at the same time

    Several others want to break for the blessed weapons ASAP

    Others want to attack the Lunar in order to save Corbitant, then go for the weapons
    …. Very well. I will work with what you’ve given me.

    --------------------------------------------------

    Hurriedly, you snatch at the dagger bound up in your sash. As the snake-tongue flashes forwards, you throw it. You’ve never had much skill at knife-throwing, and your dagger isn’t weighted for throwing anyways, but you can’t help but feel a small glow of satisfaction as the dagger impacts the body of the snake.

    Distracted, the Lunar whips her head about, hissing at the interruption. As you make for the altar, sending bones skittering in front of you, you draw your bow from its cover. Mid-stride, you draw and loose an arrow, not your best shot! Loosed quickly, and scarcely aimed at all, the arrow zooms across the chamber, and as you reach the altar, you hear two screams of pain from behind you!

    Chancing a glance up from the altar, you see that the arrow, as poorly aimed as it was, somehow managed to connect not with the Lunar, but with her snake-tongue, uncoiled in mid-strike, and the serpent flails around wildly, spewing blood and venom in its death-throes. The Lunar dropped Corbitant in her pain, who is curled up against the wall, wailing in fright. After but a moment, she yanks the arrow out from the snake, and turns to you, eyes blazing with fury and a froth of blood and saliva heavy on her lips. Another arrow loosed in her direction merely shatters, deflected off her skin as easily as harmlessly as a reed.

    In near-panic, you quickly scan the slate tablet, heaped with votive objects and weapons, for something, anything, you can use! A bronze dagger… no. An iron spearhead, bent into a zig-zag…. Useless. A flint spearhead…. Maybe….. there! Arrows, of flint and bone and bronze, fletched with eagle plumes! You snatch up a handful of the projectiles, and hurriedly fit the nocks of one to your string.

    As you draw the arrow back, you look up and see in terror that the Lunar is all-but 10 feet from you. She draws back her claws to strike, and as the razor-sharp talons descend, you loose. At this range, you pretty much can’t miss, yet she twists and flows like a drop of quicksilver….. not enough! The arrow impacts her shoulder with a wet thok, and the scream that leaves her throat makes any earlier cries of pain sound gentle by comparison. She drops to her knees, grasping at the arrow-shaft punched through both sides of her shoulder, and to your surprise, the wound (and the blood running from it, down her body and dripping from the point) is sizzling, smoking, burning. The sight is so much, for a moment you forget what you were doing, bow-and-arrows held numbly by your side

    She cries out in agony, and with titanic effort, yanks the flint-tipped arrow from her body. Unlike the rest of the other wounds you have inflicted over this battle, this one doesn’t close. She screams again, in pain and fear, argent corona condensing into foxfire tails spread out behind her, yet the wound remains fresh, bleeding. She turns to you, eyes filled with tears, and cries almost piteously,
    “What have you done to me!? WHAT HAVE YOU DONE TO ME!? Why am I bleeding, I’m not supposed to bleed! Luna, why have you forsaken me?!”

    Your only response is another arrow, drawn once you collected your wits. She makes no effort to dodge this one, in the middle of her panic attack, and the arrow punches into her thigh easily. With nothing more than a soft grunt, she sinks down to the floor, lying deathly still.

    Shocked, you remain still for a few moments, your heartbeat pounding. It…. it couldn’t have been that easy? Yet it looks like the Lunar isn’t even breathing, and she is lying so still……

    Cautiously, to avoid disturbing her if she is only unconscious, you make your way to within a few feet before drawing back your final scavenged arrow. You want to be able to put an arrow directly through her black heart, and if she is just unconscious, you might not have time to search for more, what with her Anathema powers and all.

    This close, the stench of burning flesh is overpowering, and you can see the flesh blackened and charred where the two arrows penetrated. Ordinarily, you would be confused and fearful at the sight of something so overtly supernatural, but at this point, you will take whatever help you can get!

    As you draw the arrow back to your ear, the bow-lathe creaks, and right before you loose, you suddenly catch a glimpse of her face, silver light escaping from the slits of her eyes. Before you can jump away, she rises to a knee, and you scream in pain as a line of fire traces its way across your chest, slicing through your mail and underpadding as easily as a knife through paper.

    Staggering back, hands to your chest, you are in no way capable of stopping the blow to your face, were you even capable of seeing it coming. Your head snaps around, hard and fast enough to make the ligaments crack, and you hear your teeth and nose break before the pain comes.

    You don’t even feel the punch to the ribs, nor the impact with the wall across the chamber. The final impact, with the stone floor, is like something out of a dream. Nothing even really hurts, just…. numb, like an icy winter fog, and your thoughts are slow and sluggish.

    After what feels like an eternity, feeling comes back to you, and the first thing you can feel for real is something resting uncomfortably under your head and neck. Feeling around clumsily (your left hand doesn’t want to work, for some reason), you discover the uncomfortable pillow is nothing other than your spear, laid on the floor at the beginning of the fight. With immense effort, you manage to get yourself up on your knees (your entire right arm went icy-numb again, when you tried to use it to lift yourself), keeping one hand on the spear, and spit out a torrent of blood and shattered teeth.

    Across the chamber, you see the Lunar on her knees, trying to pull the arrow from her upper thigh. At the slightest touch, she groans in pain, yet after a moment, she yanks this arrow free as well. Just like before, this wound remains open, and even from this distance, you smell the blood. She screams in frustration, silver disc and corona flaring, making the chamber as bright as day, but to no avail. With the crack of bone and wet sound of tearing flesh, she transforms, from a woman to a fox, which immediately drops to the ground, two legs of four useless and limp. With another flare of light, she changes back, sobbing, dropping back against the wall.

    She is startled from her breakdown by your laughter. In a voice, hoarse with pain, you call out, “Looks like ‘your patron’ doesn’t agree with the whole ‘living god” part, Anathema!”

    “Shut. Up.”

    Instead, you laugh again, “You know, I know where I shot you. If that bleeding continues, you only have a few minutes before you bleed out and die. I suggest you stop crying and make your peace.”

    “I’m not the one who is going to die, mortal. I am a god, I can’t die from mere blood loss….”

    “You don’t sound too confident, Anathema! What is your problem with dying? Are you a coward? You must be, considering how you are here, hiding in the mountains, away from the Wyld Hunt! I find that immensely funny. You see… here I am, a mere mortal in your terms, yet I came to my death with no fear, while you weep and scream at it! Hypocrite! That makes me, in a way, ‘better than you’, doesn’t it?”

    “Stop talking, STOP TALKING, STOP TALKING!”

    You grin, grasping your spear in preparation, “…..make me, Anathema.”

    With a scream of fury, she wrenches herself upright, and charges into a shambling run. Stumbling through the bones, her claws raised, her teeth bared, she staggers towards you. A few feet away, she pounces, all grace and fluidity gone.

    At the last moment, you grab your spear and brace it against the wall behind you. With the backspike firmly set, you lift it and hold the point out in front of you.

    Directly in the path of the Lunar.

    This close, you can see her eyes widen, and she makes an effort to twist and avoid the glittering edge. Inexorably, the point connects with the base of her throat, right between the collarbones, and punches through her marble skin. You feel the spearshaft shudder as the point, a full foot of Vanehani steel, cracks her spine in half and cuts through the back side of her neck.
    Due to her momentum, she almost pulls the spear from her grasp when the two connect, and she shaft snaps under her weight. She impacts the wall beside you, breaking something else (the spearhead or her neck, you can’t quite tell), and she slides down next to you.

    At this close range, you can see her eyes tremble and her lips quiver, and she gasps for air. The silver disc on her forehead burns, and before she can heal from that final effort (you are certain you won’t be able to do that again!), you raise the broken spearshaft in two hands, and with a cry of effort, bring it down on her sternum. The bronze backspike punches through her skin and sternum, and for a moment you can feel her heart trembling on its point, fluttering like a bird. With a savage wrench, you tear it open, and the silver-and-purple corona surrounding the Lunar Anathema cuts out, covering the chamber in a layer of dew and hoarfrost.

    You gasp for air, all strength suddenly gone, and with the weakness comes back the fog, creeping over your senses and slowing your thoughts. Finding it hard to concentrate, you shake your head dumbly, and go to take your feet. As soon as you put weight on your left leg, the numbness changes to cold fire, and all goes black.






    It is dark and cold, and for a moment, you don’t remember who you are. You are afraid….. and wet. Very wet. Why the hell are you so wet?

    After a moment, cold water dribbles on your forehead, and your eyes snap open. Almost instantly, you close them in agony, groaning. Your head throbs, and you feel like you might vomit. With your eyes clamped shut, the nausea slowly passes, and you carefully crack your eyes open.

    It is dark in the chamber, the only light coming from the full moon above, beaming light down into the pool, and it takes a few moments for your eyes to acclimate to the darkness. Looming over you is a human figure, features obscured by shadow, and you start in terror. The figure hears you move, and shifts back into the moonlight. It is Corbitant, kneeling by your side.

    Relief plain in his voice, “< Good, I was starting to wonder if the two of you killed each other.>”

    Confused, “<What do you mean?>”

    He laughs, “< What do I mean? What do you think? The Lunar is dead, that is what I mean!>”

    “<….. really? Huh>”

    He nods, “< Yes, cold and dead, the best kind of dead.>” He turns his face from you, “< I am guessing you passed out, too? I just woke up a few minutes ago.>”

    Your only response is a groan of pain, as the fog starts to get replaced with embers. It feels like your entire body has been beaten with a big ol’ stick, a wet one.

    “< Wh…. Why am I wet?>”

    “< I was dribbling water on you, to see if it would help you wake up.>”

    Giving a grunt in response, you gingerly start feeling your body, to see just what the hell happened to you. It hurts to breathe too deeply, and your side aches, both your ribs and the slash on your chest. Your nose is definitely broken, but the bleeding has stopped (and the clots are inside your nose, ow!), and your entire left jawline is a mass of splintered teeth and agony, and all you can taste is blood. Your right arm is almost-certainly broken as well, telling from the pain, and based on that some fingers on your left hand seem to be broken as well; at the very least, two of the fingers don’t want to bend, and hurt then you try to make them. Your neck aches, and it feels stiff and weird when you try to turn your head. There are many other more minor cuts, scrapes and bruises, too many to count, but they appear to be minor upon cursory inspection.

    No, the worst of your injuries appears to be your left leg (damn, that was the ‘good’ one!) and your head.

    Gingerly, you feel around the back of your head, stomach sinking when you feel a mass of matted hair and clotted blood. The bleeding has apparently stopped, but your coif and underpadding is absolutely soaked, and upon thinking on how much blood you may have lost, you feel weak and sick. Exploring around the wound some more, you come across the edge of the wound, and even when applying the gentlest of pressures, you feel the plates of your skull suddenly shift, and the nausea rises up, hot and acidic. You vomit, spewing breakfast (that was so long ago!) across the floor, and Corbitant gently passes you his helmet, filled with water from the pool, to rinse out your mouth.

    After rinsing out your mouth, you still feel weak and sick, and the room spins around you. After a few long heartbeats, the spinning stops, and Corbitant leans over.

    “< What is wrong?>”

    Blood acrid on your mouth, “< I…… I cracked my skull. It must have been when the Lunar threw me into the wall.>”

    “<….. Damn, there isn’t much I do about that. Anything else?>”

    “< Yes, my left leg. It hurts really bad.>”

    Feeling up and down the limb, it is readily apparent that the leg isn’t actually broken, but instead the ball of the hip is projecting forwards, straining against the skin, burning with cold fire. You tremble and shake, and not just from the cold.

    “< I think my hip is dislocated.>”

    “< Let me see>”

    He leans over you, and gently pokes at your left hip. Even the gentle touch, little more than a fingertip, makes you groan in pain and break out in a sweat.

    “< …. Yes, sorry Kasai, but your hip is popped out of place. You said she threw you into a wall? You must have hit wrong. Fortunately for you, I know how to put it back in, but…… well, it is going to hurt.>”

    “< How bad are we talking?>”

    “<Likely worse than anything you have ever felt. But, if you want to walk, it is going to have to happen.>”

    You nod, but he doesn’t respond (odd). “< I guess you might as well go ahead, then.>”

    In response, he takes a deep breath, and shuffles around you, feeling his way with his hands.

    Confused, you ask, “< Corbitant, can’t you see? Is it too dark?>”

    He laughs darkly, “< No, Kasai, I can’t see. The Lunar bitch spat some sort of venom at me, and it got in my eyes. >”

    The blood drains from your face, and you feel immensely guily. “< I… I am so sorry Corbitant, but I think that was my fault. I shot an arrow at her, and I hit the snake.>”

    He is quiet for a moment, then shrugging, “< Eh, I would rather be blind than dead any day of the week. I came into this fight expecting to die, so anything other than that is a plus.>” Grabbing ahold of your foot and placing his foot into your crotch, “< Are you ready?>”

    Taking a cloth from your sash, you stuff it between your teeth, “< About as ready as I will ever be.>”

    With that, he pulls, and the cold fire burning in your hip turns hot, arcing up and down your body like hot sunlight on steel. Your spine arcs involuntarily, and your scream behind the gag. Just as your vision starts to blacken around the edges, the knob of your leg pops back into place, and the white fire subsides to a deep, aching throb. Still painful, of course, but far preferable to what came before, and after you catch your breath, you find you can move the leg.

    “<…. Thank you.>”

    “< No problem. I owe you my life, and the spirits of my dead people thank you.>”, pressing his forehead to the floor.

    “< Corbitant, if it wasn’t for you, I would have gotten killed in the first 5 minutes of the fight! I should be thanking you!”>

    “< Ah…. What is a ‘minute’?>”

    “< …… never mind.>” Sitting up, wincing at the pain in your ribs, “< I guess the right thing to ask is, ‘what are we going to do now’?”

    He sits back, kneeling, “ I think we should sacrifice the Lunar to Luna, and consume her heart. If She accepts the sacrifice, the sacrificed’ strength will flow into us, healing our wounds.>” Slowly, cautiously, “< Your grandfather did that, 30 years ago during the Feast of the Moon. Look at him now!>” Then, viciously, “< Plus, it would be retribution for my slaughtered warriors, my people who were killed by this monster! Giving it a taste of its own medicine would be karma!>”

    You balk at the suggestion, “< Corbitant, that is ……. well, cannibalism, man! Isn’t that ritual done on animals?!>

    He snarls, “< Yes, but what this bitch did makes her an animal! She killed children, Kasai, and ate their hearts!>”

    He takes several deep breaths, to control himself, then “< Of course, you were the one to strike the final blow, so you get the final say in what we do.>”

    After a moment of thought, you suggest you;
    1. Take the head and leave. You don’t know if the Lunar has friends or allies, and you definitely need medical attention! Let’s not hang around any longer than we have to….
    2. Cut out the Lunar’s heart and ritually consume it. Getting the strength of a demigod sounds…… pretty good, actually. But…. do you want to commit the sin of cannibalism?
    3. Re-consecrate this shrine of Luna by sacrificing both the heart of the Lunar and your broken spear. She undoubtedly helped you in this fight, and you would be amiss if you didn’t show the proper respect in turn……
    OOC:
    1. This was the golden ending, believe me or not. Everything else would have resulted in either 1- Kasai losing something, 2- the Lunar escaping, and swearing revenge, or 3- some combination of the two.
    Kasai and Corbitant were actually extremely lucky the Lunar was a Full Moon, not a Changing Moon. If she was a Changing Moon, Kasai likely wouldn’t have been able to resist her charms (and Charms), and would have fought Corbitant. Even still, it was close.

    Here is a list of Kasai’s injuries.
    1. Concussion
    2. Cracked skull
    3. Moderate blood loss
    4. Dislocated left hip
    5. Several broken fingers and bones in left hand
    6. Broken right arm
    7. Several broken ribs
    8. Broken nose
    9. Broken/knocked-out teeth
    10. Claw-slash across chest
    11. Numerous other minor scrapes, cuts, bumps and bruises.
    Yet again, he will be out of the running for a while.
    1. The weapons were indeed blessed by Luna, and they are basically the sole reason we didn’t get even more fucked up.
    The Hill-Tribes usually sacrifice weapons after a successful hunt, but a wise hunter might also want to be on the Moon-Goddess’ ‘good side”, and ask Her to bless their weapons ahead of time by leaving them in a shrine.

    In that case, Luna might send visons of things she really would like to see dead in the dreams of the hunters, as well as blessing their weapons against that particular target.

    That is, by and large, what happened here. Functionally, the weapons acted much like Holy weapons vs Creatures of Darkness, and in game terms they would have dealt Aggravated damage, as well as causing bleeding (aka, for all intents and purposes, the Lunar was mortal-and-then-some, so long as she was struck with the weapons from this shrine)
    1. If we consume the heart of the Lunar, we will indeed be healed……. But we will also take on other aspects of the Lunar’s personality.
    By ritually consuming the heart, you take on all of the strength of the creature, not just the “good” parts.

    For example, look at Kasai’s grandfather. His behavior isn’t……. natural, for lack of a better term. His snarling, growling, bearing-of-teeth, all of that is bestial behavior, ingrained as a result of partaking in the Feast of the Moon. And that was from a deity that is largely benevolent and arguably, the most human of all the Incarnae. That was a reward.

    Now think about what would happen if Kasai consumed the heart of the Lunar. What sort of crazy, sociopathic tendencies would he develop? Now, of course, Kasai wouldn’t do a complete 180, changing from a morally-upstanding individual to a man-eating insane monster…….. but there would be a personality change, a marked one. More bestial, more savage. Quick to anger, slow to forgive, that sort of thing.

    So, he would gain power, but at what cost?

                                            In my mind, his most serious injuries are the concussion and the dislocated hip. Everything else is manageable, to a given extent: Kasai is young, and in excellent physical shape. While he won't be running around and fighting for the next few weeks, he will bounce back from the various broken bones pretty well, barring chronic pain and stiffness.

                                            -With regards to the concussion: He is actually lucky he cracked the skull open, as there is less chance for blood to put pressure on the brain. Most of his "issues" will come from the concussion itself, not from the skull fracture. Remember (and I don't want to reveal too much), he didn't pass out from the concussion itself, but from pain due to putting body weight on the dislocated hip, and he can remember before, during, and after he got thrown into a wall. His head was protected, after all, by the padding of his armored coif, the Lunar just threw him into a wall that hard.

                                            He is going to be confused, irritable, nauseous and have trouble balancing (as well as other symptoms) in a few minutes, however.

                                            - Dislocated Hip: This is the big one. As Oathsworn, he is expected to serve as a cavalryman, or at least be able to. He already has issues riding, as a result of the injury to his right leg. He can walk (hobble, really, but over time the deep-tissue bruising will heal), but depending on the treatment he receives, this will plague him for the rest of his life. Chronic pain, stiffness, the works.

                                            -ribs: he actually isn't too concerned about these. So long as he isn't coughing up blood, and can still breathe, he assumes he is OK, relatively speaking. Remember: he wears a thick and tightly-woven sarashi about his midsection almost-literally for this, aka supporting the midsection and limiting the effects of injury.

                                            -bleeding: Kasai has lost a decent amount of blood, not enough to make him pass out like last time, but enough where he is weak and woozy. The good thing is: his bleeding wounds have already closed, mainly due to the pressure of his armor underpadding, so as long as he doesn't break them open, he won't lose any more.

                                            shattered teeth: meh. Both the Hundred Kingdoms and the Hill-Tribes know what dentures are. The stumps are certainly going to have to be pulled out, and that will be fun ( no solid foods for the next month or so!), but he can have dentures made in a few weeks.

                                            broken bones: as above. So long as they get set, not too big of a concern. He just won't be capable of fighting for 6 or so weeks.

                                            Treatment is key, here. If he gets good enough treatment, he will be fine, relatively speaking, even regarding chronic long-term effects. It is just, well, he is a couple hours away from the closest village, at night, cold and wet, and he doesn't know the way, and his companion is blinded, etc etc etc.

                                            Lord Iysen's chirugeon is very capable (and will almost-certainly want to give him a physical when he gets back to Teriake Castle, regardless of what happens), and the Hill-Tribes aren't chucklefucks either. The main issues he is facing at this point isn't the fact that he is injured per se, more that he has to actually get to someone who can treat him.

                                            +++ Spirits:

                                            Getting help from some form of spirit or deity is ...... possible, even plausible, depending on how we play our cards. Most Essence-using beings within a couple miles felt the Lunar go Totemic, then cut out, and probably recognized the implication.

                                            The Hill-Tribe animistic totem spirits, which are worshiped in various ways through the East and Northeast (and have corresponding levels of power), are probably going to be fucking ecstatic this Lunar is gone. They certainly didn't appreciate her killing their worshipers in particular, and resent/resented the presence of Exalted in general. The thing about them is: their domains, and corresponding powers, are largely limited to what their animistic qualities are. They probably aren't going to be able to heal Kasai (or Corbitant, for that matter), not in any major capacity (jolts of Essence akin to adrenaline, maybe?), but they could certainly offer other forms of aid, largely limited to what you fine people come up with, if that is what you wish.

                                            Of course, Kasai would have to make contact with the totems first, and he has no fucking idea how to do that. Corbitant has some semblance of what the rituals entail, but he is no shaman. Traditionally, the shamans of a tribe would be the ones to handle communication with the totem spirits, and they guard the rituals very closely.
