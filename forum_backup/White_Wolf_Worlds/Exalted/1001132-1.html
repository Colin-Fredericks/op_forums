            Duel it is!

            After a quiet moment of deliberation, you decide that challenging the bandit-leader to a duel would probably result in the least amount of bloodshed. Charging into the bandit camp, sword raised to Heaven, was all well-and-good in the stories, but was stupid in practice; the bandits would probably fill you full of arrows before you got to the camp. And leading the peasants into battle was risky, as is all battles. You doubt there would be 40 peasants willing or able to fight, and going up against experienced killers meant you either wanted a skilled band (not happening), or force-of-numbers. No, fighting the Oathsworn-bandit face-to-face was the best possible choice, given the circumstances.

            You sigh, " Ten-Sheaves, if I challenge the leader to a duel, would the bravest of the villagers have my back? It is traditional for a duelist to be surrounded by their seconds and retainers, as insurance against interference."

            The headman scratches his chin." There are some of the more rowdy boys who were always willing to fight, and your victory out front might have started a fire in their bellies. I'll put the word out, but what does this involve, exactly?"

            Pushing back from the table, you pace back and forth, gathering your thoughts." Usually, both fighters meet at a predetermined place and time, usually somewhere where neither party will have an advantage in terrain or elevation. They are surrounded by a circle of their seconds and retainers, both to mark out the boundaries of the dueling ground, and to ensure either one of us won't catch an arrow in the throat from the treeline. Generally, most duels go to either the first blood, or the third blood, but in this case I am going to challenge the leader to a duel to the death". You smile grimly.

            Ten-Sheaves stares for a moment." Isn't that ...... a little drastic?"

            "No. The situation is dramatic enough that it warrants it, it is rather ...... dishonorable to refuse a duel to the death, without at least showing up, as it implies cowardice on their part, and besides." Your lips pull back from your teeth, gleaming in the firelight, making your look remarkably similar to your namesake. " Their refusal to heed their Oath offends me, on a particularly deep level. Their denial of their vows sullies the honor of the Oathsworn as a whole, disgusts me personally, and needs to be amended as soon as possible".

            You stop pacing, and walk over to the door of the common-house. From here, you can look out over the village plaza, and to the river. On the other side of the river, there is a small bit of earth, between the shore and the scarp, that from here looks relatively flat. Without turning your head, you address Ten-Sheaves.

            "Headman, send out your notice, and if you will, please bring me paper and ink, or some other writing material. I have a challenge to deliver."

            Ten-Sheaves bows formally, forehead touching the floor of the house, then scurries past you to address the crowd of gathered peasants. Ignoring them, you walk down to the riverbank, then, grimly ignoring the ice-cold springmelt (gah! And you just got dry and warm, too ), stride across the gravel riverbed to the other shore.

            From there, you can clearly see that the flat land you saw from the village is perfect for dueling. Flat, large enough to fit a band of people, relatively free of rocks and large hummocks, and within bowshot range of both the village and the treeline, so no side could claim unfair advantage. Deciding on the spot, you splash (Damn!) back to the village in order to compose a letter of challenge.

            The peasants have no paper, but have ample supplies of paperlike birchbark, which is almost as good, and ink made from soot and pine resin. After scraping the birchbark smooth, you sit down and compose a letter using the graceful characters of Dragontongue, whose brushstrokes are something all "proper" Oathsworn learn to write, alongside the sword, bow and spear. Your armsmaster actually started you on calligraphy before you ever held a blade, only deeming you capable of swordplay when you could make brushstrokes with a brush 3 feet long and 10lbs in weight.

            In your letter, you "call out" the Oathsworn bandit, calling them dishonoured and dishonorable, their behavior with the bandits a shameful display of character, and that they were not even fit to care for pigs. In the closing statements of your letter, you formally challenge the Oathsworn to a duel, held tomorrow at noon, on the field below the village. After the ink dries, you curl up the birchbark and bind it tightly to one of your arrows. Upon leaving the common-house to go check on Xuansu, you get buttonholed by Ten-Sheaves, who informs you that the aforementioned rowdy peasants would be more than happy to back you up, and that the bandit camp was at the top of the falls, about 3 miles downstream. With that in mind, and partially due to the fact that the rain has actually let up for once, you saddle up Xuansu and proceed up the riverbank.

            Like most peasant trails, the going is rough, with the trail between the hillside and the river more suited for foot-traffic than a mount and rider, so it takes you a couple of hours to make the distance between the village and the falls. There, a stream tumbles down a rock face, joining the river with a roaring crash and spray of mist. At the top of the falls, about 30 feet above the surface of the water, a bandit leans up against a tree, watching the trail both up and down-stream. You know he sees you, because he ceasing leaning back and takes a bow in hand, watching you to gauge your intentions.

            Upon seeing you raise your own bow and draw back an arrow, he ducks behind a trees in a sudden panic. Amused, you loose the arrow so it thunks into one of the tree-trunks, sadly not the one the ruffian hid behind. With that, you turn around and start to ride back to the village to prepare, not heeding the angry yells of the embarrassed bandit.

            Once at the village, you are informed that a meal has yet again been prepared for you, but you are unable to enjoy the food to the extent it deserves. The tenets of your Oaths, as well as your personal honor, demand that you be unflinching in the face of death in the acts required to uphold said Oaths, and you are....... technically, in the sense that you have weighed the choices and accepted the consequences, but that doesn't mean you are happy to die. You still, for a lack of a better term, fear death, at least the painful part that usually precedes death. Dying as a result of your Oaths has never actually been this close in the forefront of your mind before. You don't even understand why you are so nervous: it isn't like you haven't been in a fight before, or even killed. Your first battle was at age twelve! Regardless, your stomach is in knots, and you push away the food and drink brought before you. You need to find something to occupy your mind.

            Reaching over to your saddlebags, you pull out the bits and pieces of your armor, checking them for damaged laces and rusting metal. Now, your father definitely cared for you, and tried to provide the best equipment he could, but his lands were high in the mountains and far from a large town, making him relatively poor in a land where most wealth is caught up in agricultural production. Your weapons, your horse, and your armor are literally the only things you own, in all of the world.

            Making lamellar-style armor is more of a Lowlander thing, with access to large deposits of "iron sand". Up in the hills, the people have to make due with smaller deposits, meaning their main weapons are spears and bows, and metal armor (when they even have it. Hill-tribespeople mainly protect themselves with shields, and armor made of leather and bronze), is instead made up of hundreds and thousands of small rings, linked in a fine mesh and backed by a quilted cloth padding. In your case, the mesh is sewn to the padding, in a 6-in-1 pattern, meaning the mesh is very fine and resists slashes and stabs just as well as lamellar armor, and there are no laces to cut or absorb water or blood. It has served you perfectly well so far, and is actually relatively comfortable to wear for long periods of time. Composed of a jacket, a coif/hood, sleeves, thigh protectors, and greaves, all bound together with loops and ties and buttons, the armor is light enough for you to put on on your own, always a plus when you lack retainers and pages.

            Now preoccupied, you go over your armor, piece by piece, checking each lacing, each ring, each piece of padding, to make sure it is functional and not compromised. With that finished, you inspect your weapons, examining your sword and spear for nicks, then whetting them on a fine piece of slate until they are capable of shaving hair.

            Thus preoccupied, you are startled by the sudden opening of the door. On the other side is a peasant girl, who is very surprised (and none too happy), to open the door only to get a dagger held up to her face. She jumps back with a yelp, falling to the floor in a mess of skirts and ribbons. Amused and partly embarrassed, you help the girl up, apologizing for startling her. She quickly offers a bow, stammering pleasantries.

            "O..Our humble village would like to thank our Lord for aiding us, and I offer your Lordship suc...succ..... assistance, if he so desires"

            What the hell? It isn't likely that she would know how to care for, or put on, arms and armor, and you have already stacked the dishes the farmers brought for you, so she can't really be referring to food. And, why is she so dressed up? Ribbons in her hair, powdered chalk on the face, fine (for rural peasants) clothing .....

            Oh. OH.

            The girl looks up from her bow at the sound of your face meeting both palms. Your are, to be entirely honest, mortified. Based on her tone, she was probably put up to this by Ten-Sheaves, the bastard, out of some misplaced sense that you would appreciate the gesture. She is obviously embarrassed, color showing through the chalk on her face, though for your continued lack of approval or for this farce as a whole, you are unsure.

            "Listen, girl, come over here...... quickly"

            "A ... at once, my Lord" She scurries over, reaching to the back of her sash and beginning to untie it. You cut her off hurriedly

            " No, NO, not like that!" Almost immediately, you regret it, as tears of shame start to well up.

            "Am .... am I not pretty enough? I ... I could get one of the other girls ...."

            "No, listen, you are fine, plenty pretty enough. I just....... what I want right now, is a bath. A nice, hot, bath. With soap. And something to drink. And more blankets. And maybe, after all that is over and done with, a nice, pretty girl like yourself to talk to. But not ...... that.", you say, waving your hand as if warding off flies.

            She stares, shocked, then smiles, relief apparent in her eyes. "I'll see what I can do", wiping the tears and chalk from her face. Without it, she looks much better, not so doll-like.

            She goes to leave, only to turn around at your question," If you are going to be helping me, I would like to know your name."

            She smiles," My name is Saiko, my Lord". She pauses, then impetuously , "And if I am going to be helping you, then I would like to know your name!". She gasps, then covers her mouth and bows. To be so forward with one of the nobility is to presume to be close to them, which is extremely rude if one isn't actually close to the noble, and a good way to lose your tongue with less-benevolent blue-bloods. Instead, you chuckle, and reply," My name, miss Saiko, is Kasai, of Clan Harutane." With that, she grins, bows again, and leaves.

            In the end, you get your bath, with pine resin-scented soap, a jug of apple cider, plenty of quilted blankets, and Saiko, who returns wearing much more comfortable clothing. The two of you stay up late into the night, talking about your respective backgrounds ( She is fascinated by your stories of growing up in a noble Manor, with horses and hunts and things. You, on the other hand, agree with her that watching potatoes grow sounds, quite frankly, boring as hell) By the time the fire dies down, the cider is gone, Saiko is out cold on the pallet set out for you, and you have to wrap yourself up in a blanket in front of the hearth. So much for a good nights sleep. At least, you were distracted from the looming possibility of your own pending demise, at least for a few hours.

            ---

            The next day dawns bright and early, much to your displeasure, while Saiko (who drank most of the cider, if you remember correctly) mumbles something incoherent and pulls the blanket over her head in a pile. Leaving the common-house, you hold your hand to the sun, taking note that you have a few hours until noon. Almost immediately, you get blindsided by Ten-Sheaves.

            "Was the girl to my Lord's satisfaction?"

            "Yes, for what it is worth." (You decide to not lay into the man for his moral shortcomings this early in the morning).

            "Good, good!", rubbing his hands together. "It is good that Saiko can actually be of us.."

            You cut him off with a wave of your hand, not wanting to discuss this any farther. " I want to see the fighters that will be supporting me, Headman".

            "Of course, my Lord", he replies, leading you out to the terraced fields, where the population of the village is hard at work planting various crops. He calls out several names as he walks.

            Several of the peasants put up their tools and make their way over to you. All young men, they all appear to be sturdy, strong from working in the fields and at crafts. They line up at the direction of Ten-Sheaves.

            You walk down the line, looking each of the men in the eyes as you pass. To their credit, none of them look down or away, indicating strength of will and purpose.

            "Now, as I am going to be dueling the bandit leader, there will not be much fighting on your part. However", you state, holding up a finger. " We are dealing with bandits here, so they might be a little ..... less honorable , and not uphold their end of the bargain, if you understand. Are each of you willing to fight, quite possibly to the death, if that happens?"

            One of the men stirs, then states," To be honest, m' Lord, we've been wanting to fight back for a long time. Letting them walk all over us was a bad idea from the start. Makes us look weak, makes us feel weak. Not nice, if you get me."

            You nod in understanding. Exactly what you wanted to hear. " Sounds good. There is a few hours left before noon, so go get your weapons, relax, hang around until I call for you." With that, they disperse back to their houses, and you head back to the common-house ..... only to be met by Saiko, who has your armor and weapons spread out across the room.

            "Please be careful with all that, girl. The sword alone is probably worth more than your whole village", you say, half-jokingly.

            "Oh, I was, Lord Kasai, I just ...... wanted " she replies, flustered.

            You chuckle at her embarrassment," Do you even know how to put this stuff on"?

            "No, but I can try, it doesn't look too hard", chin jutting out.

            You sigh, then hold out your arms and spread your legs," Might as well get started, this is probably going to take a while".

            First, you strip down to the waist (to Saiko's immense embarassment), then pull on a jacket up undyed linen. Over that, around the midsection from the waist to the upper ribs, is wound a length of tough hemp cloth, to reinforce the stomach. Then, greaves, bound to the calves. Then, the thigh guards around the waist. Then, the armored jacket, which buttons up the side and is bound on the inside to the thigh guards. Then, the sleeves, bound to the jacket, and finally, the coif, which buttons on the side as well. Over that, another sash of hemp, through which is thrust your fighting-dagger and from which your sword sheath is suspended. Once everything is in place, you wriggle and jump in place until everything is comfortable and "where it is supposed to be".

            You grunt in mild appreciation," You aren't half bad at girding, Saiko. That took much less time than it would on my own, you wouldn't be looking for a job as a page, would you?", you joke.

            She flushes, beaming, " It's just like putting on regular clothes, my Lord, only .... heavier, and ....... made of metal..." she trails off, lamely.

            You grin, and leave her on the veranda of the common-house, to head down to the field to prepare it for the duel. Mostly, said preparations involve kicking flat any clumps of grass and dirt, and kicking out any large-ish rocks. Most of this is to keep your mind occupied, as the "knot" is growing again in your stomach, and you are rather desperately attempting to stay busy.

            Eventually, there is nothing left for you to do. You go over any possible strategies (there are none, you don't even know what weapons this bandit uses, you really went into this one blind), you go through various weapon-forms and techniques to keep yourself limber, panic some, pray some, and, finally, fall into the last refuge you can think of: meditation. Your armsmaster, a devout Immaculate, attempted to teach you meditative techniques, but you never really took to them. All they did was make you sleepy and bored. Now, in your last moments (HAH) of possible peace, you sink to your knees in the damp grass, close your eyes, and attempt to empty your mind. At first, it is rather difficult, what with the undercurrent of nervous tension running through your body, like a strung bowstring gently plucked. Eventually though, your breathing steadies, the sound of the river rushing over the gravel bar fills your ears, and the smell of the wind rushing down the slopes of the hills fills your nostrils, bringing with it the smell of wet, green growing things. You focus on these, and your mind empties.

            ---

            "You know, I thought it would have been someone older, or taller at least. This guy is just a kid!" Brash laughter shatters your empty mind, and your eyes snap open.

            In front of you, in a circle, stands a motley collection of assorted bandits, thugs and ruffians, armed with an equally-mixed collection of weapons; spears, clubs, axes, a sword. There are about 12 of them, spread out around the outside of the ring you flattened out earlier.

            Closer, in the foreground, about 10 feet away, stands the bandit-lord, the Oathsworn. She stands rather nonchalantly, glaive thrown over both shoulders, sheath still over the blade. She is dressed in a lamellar vest, lacquered black against rusting, with a single lamellar thigh plate and shoulder pauldron, both of different colors and styles (indicating they were looted from different corpses) on the left arm and leg. On her head, she wears not a helmet, but a conical hardned -leather cap, like what a peasant Man-at-arms, would wear. At her waist is a long, single edged slashing saber, not the straight sword of an Oathsworn. From her face and figure, she could be anywhere between 20 and 50, but she definitely carries herself as someone older, rather beaten down and weatherworn. She smirks coldly at her joke.

            You rise to your feet easily, with no cramps. Surprising, since you have been kneeling in the damp grass for over an hour. The knot in your stomach is gone.

            Sounds of running come up from behind you, and the peasants from before fill in the rest of the circle. The outspoken one steps forward and taps you on the shoulder,

            " Sorry, my Lord, they just came in so fast, we didn't see them until they got here....."

            You dismiss the man with a shrug, and he steps back into the circle. The peasants come equipped with mainly spears and shields made of planks, and two have crossbows. Ten-Sheaves joins the circle, armed with the chopping sword from the bandit you killed yesterday. At your raised eyebrow, he replies ," I am the Headman of this village, and I would think less of myself if I couldn't bring myself to defend it". You nod your agreement.

            With that, the circle is complete, and you are technically ready to duel. You turn to the Oathsworn, still smirking like this is a party of sorts.

            You reply to her snipe," You know, I am actually rather surprised that you had enough shregs of honor to actually show up. Here I was thinking you were going to back out, and that I was going to have to hunt you down like some kind of rabid animal."

            She snorts. " Speaking of animals." She gestures at your hair and eyes. " What, Daddy or Mommy got snagged by the "forbidden fruit of the forest", and thought to raise some pups? HAH". She shakes her head. " Regardless, you show your age and lack of experience, kid, by bringing up honor. Honor. Honor is just a pretty word, one that is as empty as the words we say. Can you eat honor, burn pride to keep you warm at night?" She sneers. "No. No, no you can't. I've tried. Believe me, kid, "honor" won't keep you alive when you get abandoned by your lord in the middle of winter. Want to know what will?"

            She flicks the sheath off the blade of her glaive, the curved blade gleaming in the noonday sun.

            "THIS will. Trust me, all those pretty Oaths and vows and promises we make, kid, all they are are ribbons, ribbons wrapped around a sword. Sure, they look nice and make the sword beautiful, something you can show off to the rest of the fops in court, but they really just make the sword hard to use. That is what we really are ..... swords. The second you realize that, and stop trying to uphold some ultimately meaningless words said to someone who really doesn't care about you, the happier you will be. A sword is just a tool, and tools are made to be used. Discard all that useless shit that is holding you down, and realize what you can truly be."

            She raises an eyebrow," That is, of course, if you survive this, which I doubt. I've been doing this for almost as long as you've been alive. This ain't my first swordfight, kid".

            You ponder her words for a moment.

            ".....You are right".

            Her other eyebrow shoots up. "Really?"

            "I AM a sword, I will not deny that. But ....... " You look her in the eyes, staring her down " I am a sword raised in the defense of the weak, those that cannot defend themselves. I am a sword, aimed at destroying those that oppose the righteous. Yes, a sword is "just" a tool. A tool made for killing and blood. But, what matters is how you use it. I am the ribbons wrapped around the blade. I am Oathsworn, and that is why I am here today, here right now, and that is why one of us is going to get laid out on the ground in this circle, and it certainly isn't going to be me. Without my Oaths, I am nothing ........ but that is what you already are, isn't it? A twisted little ball of hate, filling the void with bloodshed?" You shrug

            "Go ahead, preach to me some more, about how my Oaths are meaningless and how we are so alike. By all means, fill the day with talk, but ...... I am going to have to "interrupt" you in a minute or so!"

            She snarls, whipping the shaft and blade of the glaive up and around. You snap your shield up, by your face in a high guard, stepping forward to catch the shaft of the glaive in early-swing. You slide your shield down the shaft, directing the blade of the glaive out of the way, and stab your spear in an overhand grip aimed at her face. She jerks her head back, and your speartip snags in the underside of her pauldron, With a push, you cut into the flesh of her upper arm, drawing a gout of blood and a cry. She drops the glaive with a curse, and makes to draw her saber. You, in turn, drop both the spear and the shield and step in close, close enough to be face to face, Grabbing ahold of her wrist and upper arm, you prevent her from drawing her blade, and try to wrestle her to the ground, only to receive a knee to the gut for the effort.

            The wind is blown out of you in a rush, and you stumble back all-but-helpless. In your stumble back, your foot catches on a tuft of grass that you somehow missed (although you swear you went over this ground like, 30 times). It ended up being a blessing in disguise (and you darkly promise to offer sacrifice to the God of Grass later on), as your tumble to the ground caused a two-handed swing at your face to miss.

            She steps forwards, raising her blade with a cry of victory, and drives the blade towards your face in a glittering arc. You manage to grasp the edge of your shield, and bring it up in time to stop the blade punching through your teeth. The tip of the blade skates off the chamfered wood, and she snarls in frustration, bringing the blade hacking down in great slices that knock chips from the wood only inches from your face. On the third strike, she hits the edge of the shield, rather than the face, and the tip of the blade punches through, splitting the shield down to the reinforced ridgeline and scoring a line across your mail coif.

            You wrench the shield back to your side, jerking the blade in her hands to the side and throwing her off balance. There is an opening, if only for a moment! You scrabble for the dagger tucked into your sash, then, almost in slow motion it seems, pull it back and drive it up into her midsection, up under the edge of the lamellar vest.

            She coughs, releasing her blade and grabbing hold of the dagger. You lift up and give the pommel of the dagger a good kick, to drive it deeper in and to drive her back, then roll over and jump to your feet, drawing your sword as you go.

            She remains standing, but doubled over the dagger embedded in her ribcage. She coughs again, then falls to her knees, blood running over her chin. She straightens out, armored cap falling off as she does so, and you stride forward to give the finishing blow.

            She holds up a hand, and you stop. She raises her head and smiles, looking you in the eyes. She whispers something through blood-stained lips, and you lean forwards (not too close, mind you), to attempt to hear it.

            "Let me ..... do it right..."

            She looks you in the eyes again, and you come to an understanding. Almost imperceptibly, so only she can see, you nod, and stand beside her, sword raised in a two-handed grip. She closes her eyes, draws in a long, shuddering breath, then pulls the dagger upwards, cutting up into the stomach. At the same moment, you drop the blade, drawing it back with a twist of the hips.

            You don't completely sever the head from the body, but you do cut into the spine and the jugular, killing her almost instantly. Her body topples forwards, blood seeping into the grass, and you bow deeply, respecting the Oathsworn she was again in her last moments. Every Oathsworn makes the oath to destroy those who oppose the weak, and, in a way, that was what she did.

            In the middle of your bow, you hear the beat of feet rushing up behind you, and you snap up straight, your sword into a high guard, to see a bandit rushing you with axe held high. Evidently, you were right, and the bandits wouldn't uphold the agreement you made. You sidestep the downswing, then open his throat with a drawcut that sends him tumbling to earth.

            It is 11 vs 11, and the odds are not in favor of the bandits. None of them have ranged weapons, for starters, and two of them go down almost immediately with crossbow bolts buried in their chests. 11 vs 9. You stand up tall and roar," ON ME!".

            The peasants with spears and shields rush forwards, forming a rough shield wall with their plank shields. With that, they are able to ward off and harry the bandits with spear thrusts, all while staying safe behind their shields. Meanwhile, the crossbow-users reload, but it is an unwieldy process at the best of times. The remaining bandits turn to run and flee.

            You, however, have other ideas. Picking up the slain Oathsworn's glaive, you twirl it around, then charge home into the mass of confused ruffians. Separated and confused as they are, there is plenty of space in between them to swing the glaive in mighty arcs, opening up chests and slicing into limbs. 4 go down before your onslaught, and 1 more takes a crossbow bolt in the throat.

            The remaining four bandits panic and break, some fleeing down the riverbank and some trying to scale the escarpment. You direct the spear -wielders to chase down the river-runners, and the crossbow-wielders to take potshots at the villains trying to scale through the scrub and gravel, while you gather up your equipment and head back to the village.

            Pressing a hand to your side, you feel wetness, but on further inspection, the mail and padding on your side is un-compromised. You assume that you just popped your stitches again. Regardless, you would have the villagers take a look.

            When you get back to the village, the whole settlement is in an uproar, with cheering, yelling, clapping and dancing. Evidently, everyone in the village watched the fight from the banks of the river, and now everyone wants to slap you on the back, shake your hand, or some odd combination of the two. The celebration goes on late into the night, with singing, dancing, booze and food, brought out from the farmers hidden storehouses.

            Damn, it feels good to be the hero.

            Early the next morning, when everyone else is still sleeping off the libations, you bundle up the armor and weapons of the slain Oathsworn in a burlap sack, to either be brought to market or to be worked into something you can use. That, along with a bulging pouch of food (dried oats and corn, a rasher of smoked pork, a jug of cider, dried apples, aka enough food to ensure you could eat well until the next castle-town, and then some) that is your "payment", gets lashed to Xuansu's saddle. You start walking him through the quiet streets of the village, only to be met with Saiko, dressed in travelling clothes and equipment bundled up in a blanket, and with a determined expression on her face.

            You stop, and raise an eyebrow, without saying a word.

            "I'm coming with you". No flustered words, no bowing; just determination.

            2) Fighting in armor.

            This is something many movies and suchlike fail to portray. Armor, when compared to weapons from a similar "era" is largely invulnerable to said weapons, meaning combats between two heavily-armored opponents usually doesn't involve swinging/stabbing away at armor, but instead devolves into "who can sneak a blow into a space not protected by armor"?.

            When this happens, you could do one of two things:

            - Use a heavy weapon to just bust your way through the armor.

            With a weapon like a mace or a hammer, armor becomes largely irrelevant due to concussive force. Even using a large slashing weapon on armor, like the Oathsworns "glaive" aka a Naginanta, would be reasonably effective due to the force impacted. Kasai would still be getting whapped in the head with a heavy weight on the end of a long stick, which would probably kill him outright, mail or no.

            - Go through the unarmored spots.

            There are almost always unarmored spots on a warrior, even ones in Full-plate. When facing a heavily armored warrior, one of the more effective ways to take them out is either wrestling them to the ground then slotting a knife through their eye, or stabbing them under the armpit or in the inside of the thigh. This was what Kasai was attempting to do, first with his spear-to-the-face, and then when he tried to manhandle the Oathsworn to the ground, to slot his knife through her eye. In the end, he still went around her armor, just up-and-under as opposed to a place that was unarmored to start with.

            Another thing, and a personal pet-peeve of mine: shields

            You know how in the movies, when you see warriors "block" blows with shields and take the full force of the blow perpendicular to the face of the shield?

            Yeah, no, that is how you break your arm and the shield, unless your shield is domed/lenticular, which most personal shields ended up becoming. Kasai's shield is lenticular. Rounded shields deflect blows along the surface, as opposed to taking the force directly.

            And, even with lenticular shields, you still kinda want to use them to "parry" blows, not merely "block" them, as it tends to be more effective.

                                        There is a topic about quests on the forum - http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...cussion-thread

                                        I was thinking back to my own quest and considered all the decision points I use. I am all about moral/character defining decision points.

                                        1. Climax of a chapter should always the most frequent decision points. I always have one climax decision point in mind when I am writing a chapter. In Chapter five, it was whether the quest character would keep his agreement with the Fair Folk. In Chapter Six, it was what the quest character should sacrifice for their sorcery trials.

                                        2. Combat decision points. Violence in Hero is not taken lightly. I let my players decide if they want to join combat or, if there is a surprise attack, I let them decide how to react to it. Later, when Svante is older, they will have to decide whether to spare a combatant or kill them. I also give them decision points over the goals of combat.

                                        3. Info Dump decision points. After a long explanation, I usually have some "not really important" decision point to give the players a break and to see if they understood the info dump. This also give my players a chance to catch me in any plot holes.

                                        4. Character Introduction decision points. I try to have a decision point after I introduce a new and important character so the players can define their relationship to that character. I just introduced a Neomah and am trying to gauge how the players are reacting to her.

                                        Though for some characters, I heavily influence the PC's relationship with the NPC. A lot of the players have strong opinions about Chejop Kejak due to OOC knowledge. Thus I declared the relationship as a tense mentor student one. As the game goes on, I am letting up on the control and letting the players decide how to proceed with Kejak.


                                        There are also things that should NOT be decision points.

                                        The Decision point to avoid plot. Early on, I made the mistake of giving the players decision points that basically avoided plot. Chapter three was suppose to be the "Svante runs wild around Yu-Shan" plot. I seeded that plot among the other decision points....and the players choose to do something else.
