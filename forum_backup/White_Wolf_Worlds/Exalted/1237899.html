    Describe Your Present Campaign (All Editions Equally Welcome)

    07-23-2018, 02:42 AM
    So,
    Was interested in the games my fellow fans are running. Would love to hear about the happenings in your games, wildest moments, favorite resolved/upcoming story arcs, characters/NPCs you're most proud of etc. All Editions Welcome, but if something you're describing hinges on something that others might not be up on, elaboration appreciated I realized I'd been coming on a bit strong in some threads, so wanted to do something fun

    Since I started this, here goes:
    My present game is four years, ten months old. Mainly 2E. Game began in RY 768, and is presently in RY 914.
    The Cast:
    Davion Chance/Murr-ya Wyldwraith, Full Moon Lunar, Gallant, Motivation: Protect the Domain of the Eventide Pheonix and its people. Defend the Northeast of Creation. 368yrs Exalted, Essence 7

    Lireal Vendra, the Eventide Pheonix. Twilight Solar, Paragon, Motivation: Restore the Glories of the First Age, Protect and expand the Domain. 145yrs Exalted, Essence 6

    Viridian Strix, Heretic of the Razor's Edge. Slayer Infernal, Paragon, Motivation: Destroy every influence of the Yozis upon Creation, Urge (Malfean) Destroy the Greyfalls Dominion, 145yrs Exalted, Essence 6* (Note: Viridian Strix has mastered the Heretic Charm, Swallowing the Scorpion. He is believed to be the first true Heretic.)

    The Onyx Stiletto Rends All Life, AKA: Osral, Day Caste Abyssal, Conniver, Motivation: Cast the Mask of Winters into Oblivion, Annihilate the Duchy of Winter, Murder the other Deathlords like he murdered the Walker in Darkness. (Monstrance destroyed by detonation of R.I.D acquired from the Warrior-Smiths of Iza. Wanted throughout the Confederation of Rivers for Crimes against Humanity and Creation. Red Listed by the Bronze Faction Chair of the Committee on Entrenched Anathema.) 145yrs Exalted, Essence 6

    Eventide Seraph, Chosen of Secrets (Ronin), Architect, Motivation: Unearth secrets of the First Age, Prove or disprove veracity of the Theory of Historic Anathema Instability Syndrome. 388 years Exalted, Essence 7

    Timeline of notable events:
    RY 768: Remnants of Arczecht Horde flee northeast, ignoring Metetha. Cross the Sandy River, south of Cho-Holuth.
    Calibration of 768, Legions of the True Blooded Shogun besiege Sperimin, lead by the Daughters of Iza. Raksi's Ape-men and outlying cannibal tribes pushed back into the city before the month is out. Raksi summons Octavian to disperse invaders. Octavian battles noble warstrider-equipped unknown champion of Shogun's forces. Octavian gains the upper hand, but is forced to withdraw by concentrated shelling from Essence cannons brought to the fore during the "duel." Raksi summons Gervisin to assist Octavian, but reinforcements continue to enter theater of conflict.
    Descending Water, 769: Sperimin completely encircled by Shogun's forces. Hundreds of First Circle Demons reinforce the ape-men defenders, but cordon continued to tighten.
    Resplendent Earth 14-21, 769: Marukani cavalry rides in support of Lookshyan expeditionary force, against Arczechti to prevent horde from crossing the Grey River. After heavy casualties, Arczechti disengage and turn northward. Lookshy and their Marukani allies read map, laugh maliciously, withdraw back across the Grey, and shadow the Arczechti to ensure Nexus's safety.
    Ascending Fire 16th, 769: Thousands of goat-wolf men detected by Shogun's forces approaching from Southeast, True Blooded air assets attempt to disperse beastmen, but find themselves engaged by a huge Strix, Steelbeak, and Warhawk, in addition to several elementals. True Blooded air assets withdraw with minor damage, after inflicting minor damage on advancing beastfolk horde. Shogun's reserve forces deployed to southeastern portion of cordon.
    Ascending Fire 16th, 769: Under cover of darkness, hundreds of Xaalite spider-folk infiltrate northeastern portion of True Blooded lines, as Sperimin Demons go on the offensive. Forewarned of the Lunars' plans, Xaalite spider-folk are slaughtered, many of Raksi's demons are banished or destroyed, and Xaal himself is gravely wounded and captured. Dark Eyes appears in the True Blooded war camp, claims Xaal, and carries the badly wounded Lunar away, even as Xaal damns him as a traitor.
    Ascending Fire 17th, an ear-splitting scream resounds throughout Sperimin, carrying miles beyond the besiegers lines. True Blooded air patrols spot the huge goat-wolf beastman horde withdrawing back the way they'd come.
    Midnight, Ascending Fire 17th, 769: Raksi appears atop the sorcerously fashioned earth-works surrounding the inner third of Sperimin's ruins, before the massed concentration of True Blooded forces to the Southeast. A great ball of green fire appears between her delicate hands, but something goes terribly wrong. Raksi is consumed by eldritch emerald flames, as the resultant shockwaves shatter the southern earthworks, killing hundreds of True Blooded legionaries. Ten Izan sorcerers give their lives diverting the devouring fire, saving the bulk of the southern cordon.
    Lytek receives Raksi's Exaltation, finds it wiped utterly clean of memory. The Right Hand of Power sheds a single tear, for what might have been.

    The Shogun's forces mourn their dead for a full day, while the names of The Ten, as the sorcerers come to be called, are inscribed on the warstrider of the Shogun's Champion.
    Ascending Fire 19th, 769: The Legions of the True Blooded Shogun invest what remains of Sperimin, claiming The Book of Three Circles. Expert scavenger-teams are deployed with the remaining sorcerers to loot the city. By Descending Fire 10th, the little which remained of Sperimin is stripped bare. The Legions march north. Honoring their bargain with Dark Eyes, the loggers of Far Hold, man, woman, and child are put to the sword. Elder Oak is beaten savagely, clapped in Manacles of Endless Night, and imprisoned. The legions depart Farhold, leaving the tribes to loot the city under Dark Eyes.

    Ascending Fire 20th, Lireal Vendra Exalts as a Twilight in Halta's capitol, Chanta, treating cases of Green Sun sickness.
    Night of Ascending Fire 20th, Elements of Arczechti horde pillage and burn the town of Rainbright, despite the best efforts of its small guard. As his comrades stand and die, the son of their Captain and newest inductee breaks ranks, and flees into the night. Later that night, the Agatae Ephemeral Sting visits the remorse-wracked warrior. Viridian Strix accepts Malfeas's offer, Exalting as a Slayer on Ascending Fire 26th. His brother, town sneak-thief and ne'er-do-well, Stooping Direhawk, dying from an Arczechti lance in the gut, is visited by the Walker in Darkness. Exalts as a Day Caste the night of the 20th.

    Descending Fire 28th, 769: Eventide Seraph, having failed to convince Ayesha Uru that the sudden unraveling of the fates of Sperimin and Farhold are indicative of an event at least as significant as the Solar Return, defects from Heaven after two hundred and forty-three years faithful service to the Bureau of Destiny.

    Ascending Air 10th, RY 770: Xaal, recovered from his injuries, contacts his mentor Wyldwraith by Infallible Messenger. Details fall of Sperimin and Farhold, describing the unprecedented level of First Age technology possessed by the invaders, and Dark Eyes collusion. The older Lunar bestirs himself from the Argent Redoubt and goes to investigate.

    That was basically where everything began. I riffed off of the First Age Dragon Blooded Gen Iza, who Mercury instructed to gather up tons of disaffected and orphaned Terrestrials, pile up the resources, and depart into the Wyld. After creating a huge fiefdom out there (with the help of the Emanation of one VERY peculiar Unshaped), they were finally returning to Creation.

    So, anyone else?

      Out of interest, what XP level did you start at, and how do you handle such long downtimes? Since the game has been like 150 years.

      So, I play two games currently, both have been going at least 8 years, not sure exactly. One I used to run.

      I'll start with the other, the game of some of the most ineffectual Exalts ever. It's 8-10 years of game, so I'll try to make it readable.

      We started on the great steppe of Mon-Gul, in the north-west of the Scavenger Lands.
      We were investigating a ruin the Realm was also interested in, and we wanted to stop local Realm-aligned tyrant Timojin from uniting the tribes.
      We fought him and his army. They shot our Zenith warlord to death, and beat up my Twilight. So we flew away to another country before the Wyld Hunt arrived.
      We went to the river kingdom of Sukhmet, and investigated caves of magitech. But our Dawn got in a fight, flared, and died. So we flew north before the Wyld Hunt arrived.

      We arrived in the Haslanti League and were hired on an airship expedition to the West. While we were there, the teenage Night caste and teenage Changing Moon got drunk and ended up in bed. After that they started dating.

      The core characters by this point were:
      Meek Proudfoot, 15 year old Night Caste street kid from Sukhmet... with a panther familiar;
      Lysa Bloody-Claws, 16 year-old Changing Moon from the slums of Nexus;
      Shadow of Kings, Twilight sorcerer of the steppes;
      Cid Hawkwind, Twilight Haslanti airship engineer.
      Other PCs came in and out of the game.

      The expedition flew west, coming across a variety of strange islands where many sailors were killed by monsters, etc. The realm also sent a fleet, who we clashed with.

      We eventually reached an ancient Dragon King city, woke the sleepers, and crushed the Realm army.

      We then flew to Coral, but on the way Shadow of Kings accidentally blew up the airship, so we had to go on a raft. Slowly.

      In Coral, we joined a revolution that overthrew the government. We were largely ineffectual. Also, Lysa, now pregnant with Meek's kid, forced Meek to marry her.

      We stayed in Coral for a while, and then found an ancient manse that seemed to transport a consciousness es back a million years into the bodies of some Dragon Kings in Rathess. We explored the city, but when we tried to go through the gate to Yu-Shan, we woke up in our normal bodies in Harborhead.
      We helped rebels kick out their Autocthonian masters and free Harborhead. Then Ahlat asked us to come stay at his heavenly palace for a while.
      After about a month, Sukhmet, crocodile-god of Sukhmet, asked us to come help stop a war.

      We went to Sukhmet, now somehow moved to next to the Summer Mountains, and stopped a war with another country.
      However, Shadow of Kings lost all his Solar powers somehow. And his Raksha girlfriend was kidnapped. We investigated, and eventually discovered that the bad guy was... Shadow of Kings! He'd got sick of the party, replaced himself with a mortal clone, and gone off to become a mad, evil sorcerer.
      The circle defeated him, but he escaped.

      For the last few years, we've been protecting Sukhmet from threats internal and external: invasions, Dragon King cults (plural), an Abyssal, etc. Various Exalts have joined or left the party, some left and came back.
      Thanks to Lysa's political manoeuvres, Meek is now son/son-in-law of the recently deceased Pharoah.

      However, currently a Nephwrack and his cult are causing chaos, and in our efforts to defeat him, Meek and Khalum (another Night) stupidly released a horde of monsters and forbidden gods from beneath the earth. This also killed the Pharoah and all the county's exorcists, as well as tons of regular people.

              XP Level:
              The Full Moon and Chosen of Secrets (the Full Moon I ran as a quasi-NPC, as something of a safety net because I had two relatively new to Exalted players to begin. The Chosen of Secrets was the PC of my one veteran, and I just used the XP table in 2E Core for their respective ages. The age table builds, with the proportion requirements for what gets spent where is really very, very balanced IMO) I padded them both just a little, but then I simply didn't give them XP for awhile. My veteran was fine with that.

              Downtime was not as big as you might think. We tend to play three times a week, but more important is one of the major setting-pieces. Gen Iza built essentially their own Shogunate out in the Deep Wyld, and had eventually allied with the Autochtonians once the Locust Crusade stuff got done with. Izan agents had recovered Filial Wisdom, and brought him back to their territory. To limit my PCs from running off to the magitech-advanced civilization out in the Wyld to solve their problems, I installed an Unshaped across the Waypoints that must be trod to reach Shogunate territory. Shatters Infinity With the Hourglass was promise-bound not to use it's time-flow-manipulating abilities in a destructive manner versus the True Blooded Shogunate and Shogunate natives, but it was only constrained from active hostilities against non-natives bearing passage-tokens. (The Unshaped was not so much an "ally" as a non-enemy who provided minimal assistance to the Shogunate in exchange for a safe harbor to scurry into if a Haanya pack came unusually shallow. In my campaign, it was cosmologically understood the "Traditional" Unshaped had abandoned "Pure Chaos proper" for the border-edge of Deep Wyld and Pure Chaos. The Haanya preferred to stay to the "middle" and further out into Pure Chaos, but the more complex Unshaped could tolerate the "shallower" portion of Pure Chaos, and even the "deeper" Deep Wyld without discomfort. Whereas a Haanya in the Deep Wyld was akin to a a killer whale surfing up the beach for seals. In any case, the Unshaped wanted to continue existing a great deal more than they wanted to wipe out the irritating chunk of metaphysical grit the Shogunate's 25-ish Waypoints represented.

              Time distortions INVARIABLY nailed the PCs both coming and going. I had been of the opinion the first time the PCs lost twenty-four months, relatively easy access to two-dot and very very expensive three-dot magitech would lose a lot of its luster. Especially since I was merciless about their enemies advancing plans in their absence. Instead, my PCs simply learned to adapt to enemies who now had tons of prep-time. I tried widening the time-differential once, costing the PCs an outright twenty-five years, but the general consensus was (Out of Character) that once was novel, repeats would kill their connection with their own agendas in Creation. I also decided I didn't really care for the extensive book-keeping on "lived versus actual" time Exalted I'd have had to keep, since we were nearing the beyond-a-mortal lifespan mark than denotes de-gating higher Essence. (Which, BTW, I do not treat as anything REMOTELY as easy as navel-gazing for six months like Core describes) I keep the age-limits, but it's Beyond Epic Enlightenment Quest-level if you want to exceed Essence 5.

              There was also the factor the Domain of the Eventide Phoenix was located north-northeast of Halta, while getting into the Shogunate required heading into the deep Southeast, negotiating (later sometimes fighting past) the Opal Court, and then slogging through three Waypoints of Bordermarches, 1-6 Waypoints of Middlemarches, and 1-6 Waypoints of Deep Wyld, just to reach the Waypoints that made up the Shatterer (who the PCs eventually proved clever enough to begin propitiating). Travel was a big, big thing for the first three years. Obviously, increasing PC power-levels lead to faster travel...

                                      Alrighty.

                                      Currently I'm running a game called "Immortals of Stone and Sacrifice".
                                      It concerns a city at the edge of the civilised world, fittingly dubbed Exile. Here, thousands of different cultures mix together in a mélange of tongues and customs.

                                      Into this mix? Throw five Solars.
                                      We have:
                                      • A Nexan mercenary captain, named "Wolf of Veiled Vengence", who is a womanizing scoundrel, trying to forget the fact that his bonded mate is a monstrous pantherlady with an appertite for human flesh. He's the Dawn, although he behaves like a Night Caste in a lot of ways.
                                      • A Vanehan ronin, on the run from the Sword Prince and the Clans that he spited. Hachidori is a Night Caste swordsmaster, practicing the Single Point Shining in the Void, and uses both Ride charms and Athlethics. Yet, his keen Investigation skills has made him the Magistrate of Exile.
                                      • A young woman from the Air People with a talent for engineering, Silver Wisp is the Twilight of the group. She's the henmother-like inventor, quite ditzy, but scary comptent at times. And insecure as all hell, as she doubts that she is even worthy of being a Solar.
                                      • A former monk turned beggar, Indomitable Boar is of immense girth and even grander insight into the ways of the world. He was once an Immaculate Acolyte, but his master casts him aside, and the Zenith Caste became far greater than his master ever was. Integrity is the core of this one's being, and he strives to bring his Circle to their destined mastery of all there is.
                                      • A plantation owner and Guild Merchant, Aliyah was originally a courtier in Great Forks, but was sent packing due to a scandal. She fled with the wealth of her family, but finally Exalted when she tricked a Demon into giving her audience with Mara, The Shadow-Lover. Today, she seems like a benevolent and endearing young lady, but the will of iron and ruthlessness is hidden beneath her Black Claw style and Socialize charms.
                                      And, well.
                                      They're doing a lot of stuff.

                                      To recap.
                                      The circle came together in several adventures, almost all of them centering around the machinations of Indomitable Boar who wanted to bring them together.
                                      They then formed a Circle, and stopped a soulsteel-clad juggernaut from claiming a place of power, by dropping the entire fortress atop of said juggernaut.

                                      After finding a immense Manse capable of drifting on the Dragon Lines, The Circle defeated a horde of wicked godeating Beastmen who were ravaging the spirit courts of the countryside. But, there was still a single godblooded daughter of a slain forest god missing, perhaps she could tell them why these Godeaters had been so successful.
                                      Following that, they attempted to place the city under their influence, but realized that such a chaotic city needed a firmer hand. Especially when a Deathknight known as the Scarlet Balladeer arrived with his harrowing music, almost sending the entire city into a black depression.
                                      Unable to kill the Deathknight, but strong enough to chase him away, the Solars tried figure out who had sponsored the Godeater Hordes, and sought out a ruined city at the edge of a collossal shadowland.
                                      Here, they discovered that the area had once been the staging ground for a rebellion against the Exalted by the Terrestial Gods, and five weapons had been forged to help the gods overcome the Solars in particular. Each blade meant for a individual caste.
                                      At this point, every clue they discovered pointed the Circle at the Deathknights as the hand behind the Godeaters. They've wanted to lay claim to another old Artifact from the Divine Revolution, a Crown meant to control a behemoth large enough to swat mountains like flies. Now, as the behemoth had been slain in the First Age, the Crown of Maggots could bring the creature back to unlife and serve as the Abyssals tool. The crown had been split apart, scattered to three different places in the region.
                                      The fortress that the Circle had encountered the juggernaut-like deathknight, He Who Sows Fear and Reaps Despair, had been one place.
                                      The god-ruled city of Moku was another.
                                      And finally, the last piece was hidden in the Changeling-ruled Kingdom of Blossoms.

                                      When the circle returned to Exile, they found that a messenger had arrived with a call for aid from the Kingdom of Blossoms. The Kingdom's own calender had stopped in the middle of their biannual Calibration, leaving the Kingdom in the hands of the madness-bringing Calibration King. (Think a Dementation-fuel Sidereal...)
                                      Through many trials, the Circle managed to put things right, but not before Limit Breaks ended up nearly tearing the Circle apart and the Day Caste known as Sanguine Scorn managed to pilfer the third piece of the Crown.

                                      From the Kingdom, they once more returned to their own city, and Aliyah solidified her hold, making her the High Despot of Exile, while the rest of the Circle began preparations of their own. Finally, they decided to embark on a quest to Moku, the City of Eternal Spring, where they saw what wonders a city ruled by unhindered Gods could be. Even though it was hedonistic and debauched, culture and craftsmanship thrived here.
                                      In Moku, they learned of the city's defenders, four powerful entities armed with the Sunkiller Blades, to secure Moku from Solar dominion. In Moku, they navigated politics of both Gods and the diplomats send by the Lunar Elder Raksi. Here, Wolf met his bonded mate again, and nothing was settled except more aches, as the machinations of Sanguine Scorn used the feuding as a cover for her theft of the third piece of the Crown.

                                      Enraged that they had been tricked, the Solars did manage to find certain clues to the identity of the Dusk Caste, He Who Reaps, and the composition of his seemingly impenetrable armor, and to do so, they continued into the wastelands of the north, to the City of the One-Eyed.
                                      Here, they found the knowledge they sought, and managed to get an ally in the form of the One-Eyed King, a mortal who fused with a shard of the Balorian Eye.

                                      Having gathered their allies in Exile, the Circle is now nearing Calibration, where a prophecy claims that the Brobdingnagian Worm will rise in the Canyon of Shadows. They march to face the Deathknights, and whoever those deathknights serve.

                                              Speaking of mind games ...

                                              My campaign has been going for over three years now. It's 2.5 edition with some minor rules tweaks. The setting is a homebrew modern shard, and the PCs are (or were) all Abyssals.

                                              I've gotta say, I've really enjoyed playing with Exalted in a modern setting. This is a world where the Meteorological Bureau retains political analysts to report on conflicts in the elemental courts. Where daring mortal hackers trap data spirits in yasal-drives and use their powers to get past the magical firewalls of dragonblood-run companies. Where satellites orbit the axis mundi, just below the ever-turning wheel of the constellations. Where a gang of street-ninjas runs illegal drugs (promising an easy path to enlightenment) from the back room of Thanh's Tengese Takeout in downtown Nexus.

                                              This is a world where the dragonblooded rule through corporations, courts, and every sort of political system from dictatorship to democracy to hold-out monarchy. Where Lunars are rumours, and Sidereals aren't even that. Where Solars haven't been seen or heard for a thousand years, passing from public consciousness to be remembered mostly by religious scholars as an allegory for humanity's hubris.

                                              The world's two superpowers are the Realm Eternal, occupying the Blessed Isle and a few tributary states, and the United Protectorates of the River Province (UPRP), occupying the central East. Both are successor states to the Shogunate which collapsed under its own weight after years of infighting and bureaucratic ineptitude hundreds of years ago. As it broke up, leading to the patchwork of nation states that exist in modern times, technology advanced to fill the gaps left by slowly failing First Age infrastructure. There was no Great Contagion, no Balorian Crusade, no apocalyptic event to cast humanity into a Dark Age. Not one that humanity remembers, anyway.

                                              The other entity that might be considered a great power is the Wyld Hunt, which in this world is a secular organisation focused on combating supernatural threats to Creation. It works with national governments through the League of Nations to co-ordinate responses to these threats and provide expert support to their military and police forces. It has a permanent force in each Direction roughly equivalent to the military of a small nation, plus a large list of reservists and volunteers who might be activated to assist with a crisis.

                                              The League of Nations has another military arm, the Iron Shield, an international military effort to keep threats from the Wyld away from the civilised lands of Creation by patrolling the borders of reality.

                                              Into this world, quite unexpectedly, in the Calibration between 767 and 768, strange new exalts started appearing. They weren't even sure they were exalts at first, because after all, that word was synonymous with dragonblooded, wasn't it? Every one of them had experienced something that should have killed them, only to wake up strong, vital, and brimming with dark essence. No deathlord offered them a bargain, no liege commanded them. But across the world, a handful of Abyssals nonetheless came into their power. Wisely, they tried to keep their power hidden from the authorities which would no doubt brand them dangerous. And they were dangerous. Some of them heard nonsensical whispers in their minds that nevertheless sometimes revealed horrible truths. All them found that their new natures demanded they align themselves with the power of death to an extent, lest their very essence rebel and produce spectral phenomena that might give them away, or overwhelm their minds with madness. But each of them was also a person with their own ambitions, their own morals, and their own heroic will, determined to make something of their new gifts, even in the absence of guidance.

                                              At the time, only a few scholars marked the fall of several Sentinel Stars, the bright lights that remained fixed in place in the night sky while all the other stars turned. But this phenomena lent the story its name ... Starfall.

                                              The PCs:
                                              Besserack Cardun: A teenage Day caste Abyssal from An-Teng. The son of a noble family that got shafted in its business dealings and was gutted by corruption charges and scandal. He died starving in a gutter (turns out being a rich kid doesn't prepare you well for homelessness). He has a familiar, the ghost of a rat that choked to death nibbling on his un-moving body as he lay dying. He started out pretty much just wanting to watch the world burn (especially the other rich families that screwed his over), but over time has developed more constructive ambitions, including overhauling the political system of An-Teng and finding a way to destroy soulsteel to free the souls inside (both still works in progress). He has few scruples, but to the relief of his circle-mates one of them appears to be loyalty. In addition to being sneaky, he is also devilishly persuasive. He is also a maker of fine poisons. He's pretty intellectual for a death-ninja, and has branched into thaumaturgy and artifact crafting recently.

                                              Betahl Altensand-Berg: A Dawn (former Dusk) caste from the Delzhan Republic. The daughter of a junkie mother and an absent father (Delzhan Army Lieutenant, MIA). She died in a cage fighting match against a dragonblood after refusing to go down in a previous match when instructed to by her crimelord boss. She got up and beat the other exalt senseless before stumbling home with her prize money. Her girlfriend broke up with her in the immediate aftermath of the match and she spent a lot of time wishing to be rid of her 'curse' while also reveling in her newfound power. She found her way to the Chiaroscuro criminal underworld and started taking odd jobs before meeting up with the PCs. As the game progressed she found her direction in life and came into her own as a champion against the (other) powers of darkness. She cleaned herself up, started a military career, and eventually redeemed into a Solar. Mostly fights with her fists. She is also a very accomplished sorceress specialising in defensive and war magic.

                                              Kaiwa Valko (nee Shiruba): A former PC as the player left the game last year. Midnight caste from Chiaroscuro, in the Delzhan Republic. Lieutenant Shiruba Kaiwa was a Wyld Hunt specialist, the face of her special forces fang. She died on a diplomatic mission to the Realm when a dragonblooded diplomat grew enraged and attacked her. Her fang covered for her after she killed the diplomat, and she was quietly retired from the Hunt. She ran an underground organisation aimed at overthrowing dragonblooded hegemony and putting more power in the hands of mortals. While she retains this goal as a focus, she has also taken on the tasks of learning as much as she can about the various threats to Creation at large, in order to combat them, and of building connections to help the group achieve its goals. She has an obsessive personality, and an arrogant, abrasive demeanour when she's not trying to butter people up, but would do anything for the few people she lets close. Very persuasive, specialised in breaking through brainwashing. Over time, she has become an incredible teacher and multidisciplinary researcher.

                                              Reynard Fox: A Twilight (former Daybreak) caste from the streets of Nexus. He was a sorcerer-detective who died after chasing a suspect onto a roof and losing his footing (with a little help from an Air Aspect hurling lightning at him). Fox joined the game later, when the PCs were already established as a taskforce. He has a familiar, a fox spirit called Zorro, who is a little innocent ray of sunshine in a dark world. Fox's life has been plagued by a bad case of Sidereals, as he was once fated to receive the exaltation of Chejop Kejak after the elder Chosen of Secrets passing. That obviously didn't work out. After finding out about the existence of Sidereals he spent a lot of time resenting their influence on his life, but has made peace with it over time. After his knack for investigation and his powerful sorcery, Fox's greatest gift is sarcasm.

                                              Dr Ashyria Jaya D'Mophry: A Daybreak caste from the upper crust of Nexus society. Jaya (she goes by her middle name most of the time) also joined later in the campaign. She escaped the stifling world of Nexan high society by studying medicine and volunteering with an aid organisation in Halta. The Realm Eternal and the UPRP were having a proxy-war in the region, backing Linowan and Halta respectively, and she was unhappy with the way foreign interference was affecting the people and landscape of Halta, so she joined a group of rebels resisting the exploitation of their country. She died in an ambush set by a corrupt general. She has used the connections she has made over the course of the game to bring an end to the war (if not the animosity) and curtail her country's interference in Halta. She is blunt and straightforward (really, she made a terrible socialite), but an excellent healer (with access to Solar healing Charms), markswoman, and wilderness survivor.

                                                          One thing I've really enjoyed about Storytelling for our present game, is its the first time I've had a group really want to "go the distance" with the same group of PCs. Everyone really seemed to dig in and get invested in the character they were playing, and anyone who plays much Exalted knows how rare it is for four people to all decide at the same time they're in love with their characters. Ironically, it was the Infernal that started it. Everyone got so invested in how Jacob roleplayed his break with Ligier, subsequent struggle to break away and forge a new and independent persona after being Hell's darling for so long (I didn't have Ligier get all wrathful and send death-squads or anything boring when Viridian Strix told him to fuck off, he wasn't killing anymore innocent people, and he understood why all of reality had risen up to throw the world's makers into prison. Instead, every time that things got tough, there was one of Ligier's agents, offering to grease the skids and ice the roads. The relapse-pull away cycle went on for awhile...until the other PCs sort of staged an intervention, and really got serious about helping Viridian Strix "stay clean.")

                                                          It wasn't until Strix had about five-six months where he'd stayed completely away from demonic influences, that he was able to realize just how much of his difficulties in staying out of Hell's hands were due to the carefully-placed insinuations and doubt-farming of his unwoven coadjutor. I know this might seem obvious, but I'd cultivated this "No, I'm not on Hell's side. I'm on your side, dummy" persona in the Agatae Ephemeral Sting coadjutor (Yes, I KNOW that unwoven coadjutors are supposed to be First Circle Demons descended from the Infernals Yozi, but I'd long since house-ruled it that it EITHER had to be a demon descendant of the Caste-Yozi, OR the Infernal had to take their Yozi's Urge. Some people might think a Slayer-Malfean Urge is a killbot, but not when done well.)

                                                          Anyways, by playing up to Viridian Strix as being on his side first and foremost, Ephemeral Sting had broken down Strix's natural suspicions and conviction that the demon was on the side of Hell. It was really long-game work here, but it broke down in the face of the party keeping Strix away from Hell in much the same way as concerned family members or friends might work hard to keep a junkie from slipping off to score a fix. Ephemeral Sting got a little more obvious as its desperation built, and...finally, Strix twigged to the fact his unwoven coadjutor had been deeply-truly playing him like a fiddle. It was beautifully, beautifully done, and the pendulum swinging between righteous indignation and raw anguished betrayal ...if I could've given Jacob the Academy Award, I would've. As it was, he was scoring the bonus XP point nearly every session by group-consensus. Now, you might think THAT's where his whole "I'm going Heretic" began, and in a way that's where the motivation began, but there was no cheesing knowledge of Infernal Heresy into existence. Strix really started digging deep for options in how to make a permanent break with Hell. Exploring leads indicated by knowledge his Past Life possessed, and there were so very many entertaining dead ends.

                                                          Finally, we're about...Oh, nearing the three year-mark I'd say. The quiet, thoughtful wallflower Twilight, whose been sweet on Viridian Strix like FOREVER without saying a word about it. (He'd totally friend-zoned her unconsciously) Just LOSES HER SHIT! The Limit Break fallout was epic, and pretty much rehabilitating the party's image in the minds of the people of the Domain that bears her name/she's the queen of took much of the next 12-13 sessions...Anyways, with that out of the way, and with not a drop of prior warning OOC from Lireal's player, we get in-game "Eventide Seraph, you're going to have to cover for me at the capitol's Calibration festivities. I have....something important to take care of."

                                                          Now, you can imagine what's on the mind of the centuries-old Chosen of Secrets, and he correctly deduces what Lireal's planning. To his surprise, he gets nowhere with the normally agreeable, quiet Twilight. He wants to be there, but she's neatly trapped him as her second in the running of the Domain...so off he goes to handle politics, sweating bullets while the Twilight retires to summon Ligier.

                                                          I swear, you could've heard a pin drop around the table. The IC tension created by Seraph tattling to the other party-members on Lireal morphed into OOC tension right-quick, because it was well-understood by then that Ligier is FURIOUS at this point about their persistent, determined, dedicated and most importantly, effective efforts to keep Strix on the straight-and-narrow (or as straight and narrow as a Slayer with a Malfean Urge gets). There's been quite a bit go down where his subtle and sometimes not-so-subtle machinations have been indicative of a progressive loss of patience with their interference, and now the Twilight wants to call Ligier up, bind him, and grill him on how to break Strix free of Hell forever. The context couldn't have been plainer that Ligier was going to rip their souls out and puke spit-pea soup down the holes in their Exaltations if he got loose if I'd spray-painted it on the wall. It also bears remembering that Ligier does not in fact HIMSELF know how this might be accomplished, as more than the vaguest theoretical.

                                                          I've gotta say, hands-down, this was the most fun I'd ever had with anything Yozis-related in a game of Exalted. Dawn (Lireal's player) has brought her A-game, and she's doing this whole "Trying to be big and bad, but really scared shitless" schtick that's dead-on....So, up comes Ligier. He's so furious, he brought flash-cards made of malfean brass (with impeccable Old Realm calligraphy, of course), because he's punctured his own ear-drums while crossing Cecelyne. He isn't having any "Even an offered deal the demon rejects makes it easier to bind." He's out for blood, and he's doing his absolute level-best to ensure Lireal can't offer such a deal before it's time to get to the clash of wills. Fortunately, Lireal just HAPPENED (and I mean this in the sense she'd asked the powerful Garda-bird she has a Celestine Pact with to be there, to help as combat-support if Ligier gets loose) to have a fire-elemental on hand. Thinking fast, she starts ordering the Garda-bird to burn her deal-terms into the wall behind her.

                                                          Problem: The Garda-bird speaks, and can read Old Realm, but has never had cause to WRITE.
                                                          Solution: The Twilight stunts teaching the Garda-bird to write on the spot. Legendary Scholar Curriculum + 1st Lore Excellency. I set the difficulty high (9). She's attempting to impart an entire body of knowledge, under pressure, to a sufficient complexity it qualifies as sorcerous bargaining during a demon-summoning rite. While the elemental in question is terrified almost beyond the capacity for rational thought. I give her two dice for the stunt.

                                                          Int 5 + Lore 5 + 1 (I decide her demon-summoning Occult-specialty applies here) + (2) Stunt + 3 dice due to 1st Lore Excellency (She's already blown a healthy chunk on Summon Demon of the Third Circle, and she's trying to hold something back if Ligier gets loose. Like that's going to matter, hah)

                                                          It was the first and only time I've ever seen more than four 10s result in any rolled die pool. She gets a couple successes, SIX 10s, and 3 of those rolled over into successes. 11 successes. The Garda bird has a migraine now, but it's been flash-prepared as a journeyman scribe, just about ready to leave its master and strike out as a fully-trained scribe. So here we have the Garda-bird burning Old Realm characters into the walls, the floor, eventually the ceiling...and boy is Ligier even madder at this turn of events.

                                                          Dawn finally twigs to the fact Lireal's an Exalt-stain on Ligier's boots if he goes free, so she goes all in on dumping Essence into the clash. So, on top of everything, we've got a huge white-gold-and-sunset hued phoenix with a chained orichalcum tome in its claws raging in this underground summoning chamber from her anima blazing totemic (You can imagine Ligier is even, if that were possible by this point, MORE furious at the blazing anima in his face)

                                                          Clash of wills. Now, she's managed to knock a -1 off trying to bargain and Ligier basically "Haha, you've obviously lost your about-to-be-damned mind, Copper Spider, NO" her, but here's where Lireal gets clever on me. She details how she's well-aware Ligier will go on a rampage if he gets loose. That once he's done with her, he's obviously going to go Exalt-hunting to squish the other Interventionists who've been relentless in keeping Viridian Strix from falling back into old habits, and how the ensuing battle will most assuredly devastate Sunset (the capitol of the Domain). Now, I handle Immanent Solar Glory and (for Lunars) Silver Lunar Resolution Charms a bit differently. I long ago ruled the Peripheral Essence gained can only be used on the same sort of focus by which said Essence can be regained, to prevent the Charms from being turned into simple Essence-pool-expanders. (I created two Charms that use these as prerequisites, with steeper requirements to do that, that go after these Charms in their trees)...Lireal doesn't have that one, but she's got Immanent Solar Glory, and she explains to me how this situation applies to her queenship, because Ligier is a direct, existential threat to the Domain's continued existence.

                                                          Fortunately for her, the requirements are silent on the fact she's the one who CAUSED said direct, existential threat to be...but I find this both clever and in keeping with the Charm's intended purpose. She dumps the extra twenty motes (she bought it twice) into the binding-attempt....and ::drum roll:: Takes Ligier by one success. Kid you not, totemic anima, four peripheral motes to her name...and a murderously unhappy-but-bound Ligier to her name. It was everything I could've asked for in a first Third Circle Demon summoning. I'd kinda thought I woulda been jealous (none of the games I'VE gotten to play Exalted in have things progressed to the point of me having a sorcerer PC who could summon a Third Circle Demon), but I had a BLAST roleplaying Ligier. Even the sullen-but-drawing up what remained of his dignity about himself during the Q&A which followed was great...

                                                          Ligier's theoretical notions DID, after about another year, lead to the concrete knowledge Viridian Strix needed. More importantly, Lireal was so relentlessly exacting in her Q&A, she managed to uncover the 150 year lifespan-limit that'd been put on the Infernals. (One of her Perception + Investigation rolls beat Ligier's Wits + Socialize, and she realized he was trying to hide something)...So ensued Viridian Strix's sudden burning interest in Enlightenment.
