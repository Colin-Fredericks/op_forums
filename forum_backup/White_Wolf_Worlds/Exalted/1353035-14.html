                I do think that the two mote pools are a bit of an artefact from First Edition wearing its World of Darkness lineage a bit more on its sleeve. That core book reads to me as placing a good deal more emphasis on a tone of the returning Solar Exalted needing to keep a bit of a low profile, and that is the context in which I read the idea of choosing between a smaller more pool and one in which you risk giving yourself away with a Super Saiyan aura. Others (mostly Dragon Blooded) build on that foundation with novel takes on the dichotomy, but I think that was the fire concept.

                I like it as a bit of a legacy thing, and discretion undermined by anima banner is not without significance in the setting, but its definitely something I could see being trimmed in the interest of simplicity.

                            Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post
                            But if that's not actually how the game is played these days, and actually games don't spend that much time in that and it's not what Exalted fans are really seeking from Exalted, in favour of a more "I am the Bull Of The North! See me declare my rule!" vibe and experience (or a bunch of other scenarios that inherently more open and challenging and immediately upsetting to the status quo), maybe that's something that can be simplified to reflect less focus?
                            I find that unfortunate for a lot of reasons, that and how many people just handwave or ignore limit break. Solars are the greatest and mightiest of the Exalted, with no real exception in any area. On the other hand they're also among the most hunted along with Lunars, and have the least support and are the least able to hide and flee intrinsically compared to any of the other celestial exalted.

                            They also live in the shadow of a curse that twists their hearts. There may be a time when your general is sitting there weeping for his lost honor while his soldiers are being slain by the enemy, unable to bring himself to stand and defend them.

                            They're both big things that you have to work with, and it frames the setting less as "You've got all this power, who could stand against you?" and more as "Thank the Sun you have this power, without it you wouldn't stand a chance. Just make sure your passions don't turn you against yourself." Which is good, it makes the Solar games just more epic, with greater challenges and greater risks, without making it just like every other splat except easymode.

                                You could also cut down (keeping the personal/peripheral split or not) by having more of the bread-and-butter (esp. non-combat) Charms be indefinite with motes committed to them.

                                That would let you have a bunch of Charms you keep running most of the time, and are more than enough boost to handle most situations, and then you bust out the bigger Charms and anima with them.

                                So if there was a universal Excellency that you committed 4m (in the current math, less if motes are made more valuable in Essence), and you get +2 successes, and each splat gains a unique bonus, you're running around with 2-4 of these on, anima not an issue unless you activate them that scene, and you can generally get past at least mortal opponents by just being better than them. A fight breaks out and there's some combat in there, you basically have mote regen covered, and you can focus on spending stuff on your more potent Charms that will risk anima.
