        One step would be getting rid of Triple Attack Technique as an independent Charm (and it broke the rules by being an off-shoot, anyway), and just make a universally accessible effect of Double Attack Technique.

        If there's really so little chance of Terrestrial Exalted achieving a Triple Attack Technique against other Exalted warriors, there's no point in gating it off from them.

        A storyteller could also ditch the Terrestrial nerf, and give the Charm the Mastery buff of Triple Attack Technique.


        Double Attack Technique
        Cost: 3m; Mins: Martial Arts 3, Essence 1
        Type: Simple
        Keywords: Mastery, Withering-only
        Duration: Instant
        Prerequisite Charms: Naked Fang Draw

        Moving with deceptive speed and feinting strikes, the Exalt slashes with one blade and strikes with another.

        This Charm creates a normal withering attack that has a chance to land an additional second strike. On a hit, if the threshold successes meet or exceed the target’s Defense, this is treated as landing a second attack. If both attacks land, however, only a single damage pool is created and a single damage roll is made. On a successful double attack, add (the Exalt’s Dexterity + [Charge/2, rounded down]) to the attack’s base damage.

        Note that both attacks incur onslaught penalties (p. 194)—a strike against a Parry of 5 would only need to generate four extra successes to penetrate the resulting rating of Parry 4. Likewise, the successes used landing the second attack are not calculated as extra successes when determining damage.

        If the Steel Devil has a minimum of 5 dots in Steel Devil Style Martial Arts, and if extra successes from the second blow would roll over the target’s Defense again (remembering the -2 onslaught penalty, and inflicting a -3 penalty afterwards), then the Steel Devil proves swift enough to make a third attack. If this Triple Attack Technique is successful, the bonus to the Steel Devil's base damage is instead equal to (Dexterity + Charge).

        Mastery: If the character has a minimum of Essence 3, her player may reroll (Essence) non-successes on the attack’s damage roll.

The rules absolutely allow this, but it is not intentional game design, and you shouldn’t expect everything to work if that’s how you’re playing.

                                                There's a few things DAT can do to be functional against strong opponents.
                                                • Enjoy the onslaught bonus of the attack that generated it. So you DAT against def-1 and def-2.
                                                • The secondary attack adds damage without sacrificing the hit threshold's bonus. Could be +dex for example, which is in line with other styles' damage adders, or +charge which starts low but overtakes if you maintain pressure
                                                • Allow you some benefit on fail, like a partial refund.
                                                • Allow you to attack secondary targets (seriously, look at its attached art!) in a limited fashion.

                                                Other techniques to maintain and exploit that onslaught, like spending charge to lock onslaught and applying the target's onslaught penalty (that they just refreshed) to their attacks against you.
                                                There's a good feel to making the style's defenses rely on incessant aggression.
