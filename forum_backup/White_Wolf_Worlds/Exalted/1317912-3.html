                I stand corrected on a few things. Fair enough.

                Vanelith and Florivet I suspected were for 1e, but that's still their presentation in 2e. Like that's what and where they are. Personally I considered the Rolls to be a big source of the OTT craziness of 2e, but if others didn't that's fair enough....
                I think it depends what you are counting as gonzo, which is a bit of my pushback with the OP. What he's considering gonzo looks to me to boil down to a few things, for me at least:

                First, they seem to be based on whether there is any sort of "inappropriate for what I consider sword and sorcery" technology, which is less about gonzo and more about a view of genre. Which, mind is also not how the genre actual operates and I think is part of the push-back.

                The second is kind of one of scale, wherein apparently if you aren't personally abile to single-handedly battle the Demon City to bypass the millions of demons there, then it's not "big enough" I guess, and that powers don't exist to do this is a sign of toning things down. This is in the end, completely arbitrary. As I note, there are still miles-high behemoths, raksha armies ala the Huns, and cities built on statues. The thing is that the world itself is big enough that these things are something PCs can do and the world is probably not going to melt the moment it contacts one of these things. Tied with this is power level, where I notice a lot of it is "But I could fight the Ebon Dragon or the Sun in last edition". Which well, it never asks 1) Why is this relevant to actual stories besides sheer ego-boost of beinga ble to kill the Biggest Thing and 2) Even then, they shoudl be tailored to the system of 3e with its assumptions of power, not with 2e's. The whole "You're Achilles" sticks out ot me on this as it ignores other obvious inspirations for Exalted, and assumes the Ebon Dragon in 2e should indicate what shoudl be done wiht it in 3e, or how interactive its houdl be.

                Another to me seems to be this idea that the setting has arbitrary magical shit at every step and the way, resulting in aw orld which is much more speculative fiction/weird for its own sake than our world. The "sorcerer in every village" line is this to me. I think that this is a trend 2e had where the gameline was trying to be so "Let's make random thing different" it got up its own ass, for lack of better term. Many authors got into basically arbitrary world building, without really thinking through plotlines or whether that would change the setting. For me a big one is that if every village has a reliable thaumaturge, can there be snake oils salesmen of some of the things people actually consdier in that context? To a point towards the end of 2e, it's kind of hard to actually know what folks thought at times as their lives were so disconnected from normal Earth experienc, past or present, that it became to say much reasonable about them. 3e dialing such things back is in part, so that when you deal with a person in the world, they are actualy like a person you can interact with and gets oem idea ofw hat htey want or do, rather than some alien living in some weird sci-fi world where even your god-damned bricks are being forged by little spirits.

                I actually think if stuff were gonzo in 2e, it's kind of these points. It's not that there was magical technolgoy, it was that there was so much that it was practically like one of those fantasy battle academy animes. It wasn't that there was large scale things, is that thsoe large scale things were often on topics which were written for setting details that in the end affect mostly anyone who actually lives in the world as a whole and where 99.9% games take place (shinma as real, the Daystar) or the presence of dozens of threats that each one if it fully manifested its plan would wipe the whole setting out of existance, thus making any wordcount about things whicha re still grand but not existential fade away. (Yozis escaping were this: Who cares about a Realm Civil War if there's a giant shadow dragon that wants to ruin the life of literally everyone forever?) And kind of tied a bit with the two before, this being done in ways that were "different for its own sake", often to a point which undermined even what was original stated purposes of things. (Gods doing jigs to kilm bricks is pretty much flying in the face of "gods aren't their domain and administor, not do". The 25-hour day is another one I bitch about since it's an arbitrary number made for cutesy 5x5 memes, and generally useless as a time keeping device for anyone who actually cares to subdivide a world into hours.)

                Sword and sorcery is allowed to have cool machines, a lot of its source material is that. It is allowed to be of grand scale, a lot of the source material is that too. And it's allowed to be weird, as it's always been weird, and again, a lot of the sources it builds on are weird. The foundational piece of fiction for this particular game is Night's Master, which has a blind musician defeating snake monsters corrupted by addictive jewelry through kindness and then travelling to the underworld in his dreams to rescue a woman made from a flower by the demon lord. The thing about these is amount of them. It's kind of like cooking. A bit of salt enhances flavor, too much and it becomes un-palettable.

                    Firstly, to OP, you do you, totally fine that you're into all those elements, no problem at all. (Some of the degree to which 3e has been inspired by Tanith Lee style Sword & Sorcery actually isn't my jam so much either. My preferred imagined Exalted vibe tends to be more JRPG+Ghibli inspired with a bunch of extra political/economic/historical grit, more so than actual proper S&S, which has a distinct feel to me. Maybe that's an excessively subtle thing!)

                    Secondly, though, given that, I would still more actively defend 3e's change in emphasis. Part of that is that it is closer to the way Exalted launched as a game in 1e.

                    But the other part is that the 2e shift (towards massive magitech and powerful monsters from outside reality) was one where character types that weren't Solars (or 'Solaroids') couldn't follow on.

                    To be true to the setting ideas of the Usurpation and the setting changing power of the returning Solars, Dragonblooded+Sidereals+Lunars had to be sharply limited in their ability to engineer magitech (because they idea was that they couldn't recreate a "grand" magical / technological society), and they also had to be limited in the scale of threat that they could offer to starting Solars, which meant that they couldn't scale up to participate in huge Primordial type boss fights very well. Hence, it seems to me this means a setting which tried to do both - really massive, really accessible magitech and very present, threatening Primordials, etc. and also the Usurpation and Paths of Bronze and Gold and hosts of non-Solar Exalted that matter - was kind of underselling both or caught in an internal contradiction.

                    I do think that if you wanted to really do just to the characters of Exalted and include all those themes, it would be a lot better served to pretty much abandon canon Creation's setting history, and start from the ground up with a setting where you could have Lunars dueling Kimbery and Dragonblooded pulling together huge magitek cyberpunk corporations and so on were all viable, without messing about with the setting history or crowding most of the Exalted host out of the setting.

                                        Originally posted by Lanic View Post

                                        Third edition while retaining lots of elements individually, works hard to change the general feel of the setting from admittedly cheap anime to pulp fantasy/classic mythology/wuxia. The developers are rather explicit about their preference to tone things down. However, I feel that it creates certain intrinsic inconsistency in the feeling of the setting. I understand what setting 3rd edition tries to create and I like it, but this is simply different from 2 ed setting, not 2 ed setting dialed back.

                                        Moreover, I think it creates certain tension in the seetting. Take warstriders: in 2ed we had cool giant robots. People liked them, so they have been ported to third edition. However, tthey clearly do not fit with the general tone of third edition, so the writers tried hard to present them as not-giant-robots and something that generally aways was unique and never was a standard part of warfare. Even more so with airships, where the whole aesthetics tries to strongly convey that they are not airships but rather flying chariots. Essentially, what we currently have is a mix of different settings. Each of them is cool, each is very different.
                                        Ancient and powerful relics from the Time Before are good, interesting things to include in a pulp/swords and sorcery/weird fiction game, particularly one with powerful, over the top characters like Exalted. Warstriders advancing alongside ranks of Realm Legionaries, and Implosion bows pulled by teams of draft animals are good exciting visuals.

                                        - make third edition explicitly sword-and-sorcery. Remove any mention of warstriders, power armors, implosion bows, as they do not seem to fit anymore. Make artifacts even rarer.
                                        - make an official alternative setting for Exalted which retains gonzo cheap comic book feel of second edition.

                                        I personally think that I would enjoy both of the settings. I liked more toned-down stories as well and I really understand the basic intent of the developers.. What is really painful to me, personally, is the discontinuity. Over-the-top setting was something completely unique to Exalted. I really do not understand why so many people seem so actively rejective about it. I would be extremely happy to see it ported to the third edition, retaining just the things which are unctroversially improvements: core mechanics, more open and bigger Creation. In practice, I imagine that it could amount to releasing a supplement with an official second-edition shard and certain mechanical modifications to support it.

                                        What do you think about it?
                                        I think you're suggesting getting rid of the good, unique Exalted setting and replacing it with two less interesting settings.
