    Here are some possible situations I'd like to see :

    1. Idealistic Bronze faction abolishionist neglects to report a Solar Exaltation for a few weeks in exchange for help destroying a child slavery ring.

    2. Pragmatic Gold will sometimes ride on Wyld Hunt when the Solar in question has murdered, tortured, etc. Someone has to take the dangerous guilty Solar down.

    Kejak may be a true believer, but every Sidereal has thier own reasons for supporting Gold, Bronze, or none at all. Those reasons may be pure, like "Those Dragonblooded did alright during Baelor's Crusade" or "Killing innocent Solars is wrong" Other reasons could be less pure, "The Bronze Faction never gave me a promotion, so fuck them." or "I got a pretty sweet gig at the Imperial court. Putting Solars in charge may threaten that."

    I write things.

                Nah, I don't mean I'd wish to see them physically fighting each other, I think that would make for a poor game. I like the suggestion that the Bronze get their dominance partly from being good at realpolitik (though having massacred the entire Gold Faction during the Usurpation and having 100% of elders on their side has got to be significant, too.)

                But you haven't actually changed the Factions here: you've eliminated them entirely, made everybody Bronze Faction, and then recycled the names for comparatively small differences in philosophy.

                I think there would be disagreement and dissent about policy on the largest scales, not just small local issues. People don't line up unanimously to support the status quo unless it's going really well and there are easy answers for everything, which it hasn't since at least the mid-First Age. I think it's just not plausible that for 1500 years, all the Sidereals were 100% unanimous about exclusive (and quite oppressive) Terrestrial rule*, even in the immediate aftermath of the Contagion. That even right after the apocalypse not one of them started to think that Terrestrials+Sidereals were inadequate to protect Creation without Solar or Lunar help, and they needed a change of plan. There are reasons for both idealists and pragmatists to argue against the Realm.

                That's what I mean by hive-mind. My opinion may be uncommon on this forum, but if even one Exalted player can have a differing opinion, surely so would at least a one or two Sidereals. They are intelligent, very highly educated individuals who grow up in widely different circumstances, not robots. There are no easy answers for the biggest problems, so people won't all agree how to solve them. 100% agreement is really incomprehensible to me. Humans don't function that way.

                *I think they all would agree on just 2 things: the death of any Sidereal is a bad thing, and that actually Sidereals should rule the world and everybody else is a puppet or an enemy.


                And like Wonder said, there would likely be pragmatists and idealists on all sides. Bronze Faction idealists who believe the Realm is the best option to defend Creation but try to reform it and end the mass slavery because they want the DBs to be just rulers, not cruel tyrants (and point out that rebellions and kingdoms unwilling to ally with/submit to the Realm would be less common if the Realm was less oppressive). Gold Faction pragmatists who see Solars or Lunars as the most likely way to keep Creation safe, and are willing to let them become as awful tyrants as the Empress is and exploit them ruthlessly, or manipulate them to spend their lives fighting Fae instead of Sidereals (and die frequently). Independents of both philosophical stripes -- pragmatists who will use whoever is most useful at the moment to accomplish whatever goal and betray them later, idealists who believe Sidereals' sole responsibility is Fate (and maybe ____ other thing) and them playing kingmakers is what's wrong with Creation today. That creates 3x as many options for characters as you've got here.


                You don't want them defined solely by opinion about who should rule the world... neither do I. Their hyper-focus on [killing or saving] Anathema is my biggest criticism of canon Sidereals.

                So give them more to care about! There are Exigents and Getimians and who knows what else in 3E. What are their ideas for interacting with those? There are 3 realms outside of Fate which pose significant and unknown threats to Creation, one of which somehow caused the Great Contagion, which smart Sidereals would pay a lot of attention to and spend some resources to try to watch/spy on/maybe fight; they probably disagree about how best to do that, for each of the 3 realms. There would be Sidereals who are either Independent or nominally part of a Faction, but who focus on one of these other issues instead. With so few Sidereals, there might not be any two who agree about all of the big issues facing them... so maybe there aren't 2 Factions, as such. But there sure would be disagreement about the big ideas, and coalitions of the like-minded around specific major 'global' issues.

                        Alternate courses they could have tried that some of them want to try, but don't have enough political support to accomplish. I just can't wrap my head around unanimity among humans. Even in totalitarian societies which strongly devalue individualism, there's some dissent about big issues.

                        Plus it's boring if PC options are so limited. You're always required to do and support XYZ, because there's never been dissent. Like when anyone suggests all Lunars are united about X and Y even though they're spread over the entire Threshold and stuff. I think Lunars are boring anyway.


                        I have also had a lot of thoughts yesterday about the Great Curse which I was pondering the whole time I was away from home, which I let myself get sidetracked from talking about. The idea of Paradox as the micro, PC-level manifestation of the Curse seems to fit well with my own vague suggestion of how it could work on a large scale. Having Paradox be the micro-scale form of the Curse (instead of having... a vague lack of mechanics, or putting it down to ST fiat) keeps control of the immediate manifestation in player hands. It also gave me the idea of broadening what Paradox can look like. Sure sometimes you lose the ability to interact physically with solid objects... but sometimes everybody you meet is irritated with you for no apparent reason. Subtle Paradox effects that aren't "physics break down". I admit I have a slightly silly view of Paradox, though.

                        I had an idea of how relations between Sidereals and other Exalted started to go wrong in the First Age. Solars and Lunars gradually stopped listening to Sidereal advice. The Sidereals thought that was arrogance, and arrogance was certainly a big part of it... but the other Exalted also started to distrust Sidereal advice because their plans kept backfiring! Yet there was always an external cause that the Sidereals could point to that did it, because their own decisions were often only indirectly, vaguely, subtly at fault.

                        Make the Curse subtle enough, and it's no longer necessary to say Exalted are incapable of noticing the Curse. They haven't yet because... it's just legitimately really hard to tell. That accomplishes two things: for STs who want the Curse to be incurable and/or not focus on fixing it, it makes it harder to notice, and "Duh, it was right in front of you!" is no longer a thing. For those STs and players who want to tell a story about breaking the Curse, it's possible for any type of Exalt, including Sidereals, to finally realize something funky's happening and try to do something.

                        The only difficulty I have with the Paradox approach is that somebody would have noticed when suddenly these fate glitches started happening after the Primordial War, which had never happened before. There needs to be some wrong-but-quite-plausible explanation for that change that First Age Sidereals settled upon. (The easy one is that without the Primordials and Maidens directly controlling the Loom, it just isn't quite as smooth. Hey, Sidereals and Spiders aren't perfect.) Not like Paradox is a super-big problem... by itself.

                                        Like, what does he want to replace the Loom of Fate? Anything? Would physical laws just break down without it, or would they just continue because they're built into Creation?
                                        I have no idea what the Devs are planning, mind, so this is purely my speculation.

                                        Premise: Desiring a course of action that would result in the destruction of Creation will paint you as a crazy villain.
                                        Premise: Rakan Thulio's perspective will not paint him as a crazy villain for wanting to destroy Fate.
                                        Therefore, his desire to destroy Fate cannot result in the assumed destruction of Creation.

                                        Premise: A status quo in which the freedom, happiness and dignity of the people are significantly constrained or reduced is going to make those who support that status quo look morally questionable, at best- and more likely like quisling villains.
                                        Premise: The default Sidereal perspective will not paint them as quisling villains for wanting to preserve Fate.
                                        Therefore, the default Sidereal perspective to preserve Fate must be sufficient to make their desire rational.

                                        My speculation is essentially that the Loom of Fate and Heaven's Five Year Plan is sort of a compromise arrangement.

                                        Once upon a time, the Loom didn't exist, and the Gods walked Creation freely. They were charged with the preservation of Creation from external threats, but otherwise given dominion over the world. The role of the Maidens, at this time, was to direct the Gods to where they would be most needed to preserve Creation, and means that when Heaven dictates something to happen, it becomes more likely to happen.

                                        Unfortunately, while the Gods are less alien than their creators, they are still far removed from humanity, and their causes and cares could casually crush common folk. Many were benevolent, but many would also slack strange hungers and alien lusts upon mankind, forces against which humans were largely helpless.

                                        Then came the Exaltation, the war, and the dawn of the First Age. The Maidens desired to join the rest of the Incarnae in playing the Games of Divinity- but their prophetic insight was still needed to keep Creation safe. The Small Gods were eager to dominate the world- but the Sun had given the Creation-Ruling Mandate to the Exalted for their service. The Loom of Fate was created as a compromise. Heaven plans the weave of destiny, which is half-description and half-prescription. Where the pattern corresponds to Creation, the Gods are limited in the degree to which they can interfere. The greater the deviations, the more freedom the Gods have- nominally to repair what has gone wrong, but there's little hard enforcement on that front- and see previous comment re: 'strange hunters', 'alien lusts'.

                                        Creation would continue without the Loom of Fate. Fate does not represent natural law, and Destiny is the proper term for "Heaven's intentions towards Creation". Because the Loom of Fate is, to a degree, pre-scriptive as well as descriptive (to be self-healing from small deviations), the existance of the Loom allows for Paradoxes to occur.

                                        The Sidereals are troubleshooters who go to fix the weave when it's gone wrong, to act as the direct agents of Heaven on Earth, and to punch Gods that have gone rogue and criminal.

                                        According to the Sidereals, Creation with the Gods unrestrained would be an age of savagery such has not been known since before the First Age- and it is the function of the Loom of Fate to act as that constraint.

                                        According to Rakan Thulio, humanity would be better served in an age of savagery than one in which a corrupt Heaven dictates who lives, who dies, who loves, who hates. Men suffer in bondage now- at least then they would suffer in freedom.

                                        According to the Sidereals, Rakan Thulio doesn't give two shits about the suffering he would impose on humanity; he would burn down the house and salt the earth because the man he loved loved another.

                                        According to Rakan Thulio, the Sidereals are complicit in every atrocity of Fate's design, every innocent suffering, every wicked deed which is rewarded.

                                        Some Sidereals can look at Rakan Thulio's arguement, look at Creation, and conclude that he's right. Maybe he *does* have some other way to control the Gods. Maybe he *can* get rid of the influence of a corrupt Heaven. Things are so bad, maybe any alternative is better.

                                        Some Getimians look at the Sidereal's arguement, look at Creation, and conclude they are right. The Gods unrestrained would be a never-ending disaster for humanity. Better to reform Heaven, to soften the blows of Fate, than to drench their own hands in the torrents of blood that unleashing that hell would cause.

                                        At least- that's my current line of thinking.
