    Originally posted by habitableexoplanet View Post
    Is it worth trying to run Solar Brawl vs. Solar Single Point? Are there other reasonable guesses for the second-best combat tree?
    Solar Brawl will yeet Single Point into the atmosphere no contest. SP doesn't have any defense or accuracy enhancements beyond the excellency and can't negate penalties to parry, and Dodge at essence 1 doesn't provide enough since it caps at the same place as parry with a medium weapon. The first time SP attacks just use Blade Rebuking Wrath into Thunderclap Attack Rush + either a grapple doomcombo or Hammer on Iron Technique and the martial artist is d e a d.

    Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post
    Thank you BadassOverlord. I was hoping Brawl would do better.

    You don't need to run it, but do you think the outcome would meaningfully change as Essence 5?
    It might. Sally gets a bunch of offensive improvements, most remarkably Increasing Strength Exercise x5 and the Melee clash suite, but her defense doesn't meaningfully improve other than raising the cap on Hail-Shattering Practice: she already has pretty much all she needs at chargen; Fivefold Bulwark Stance and Victorious Wreath x2 are crazy good against multiple opponents but in a duel they aren't worth spending the motes, especially against a grappler.
    Her main strength is also her weakness: since her build is just Melee charms on top of more Melee charms, she suffers diminishing returns as she grows in essence. Awareness is a good candidate for spending some xp just to join battle harder and cheaper, but outside of that I don't know what off-Melee improvements to give her. If I wanted to cheese it I'd probably go into Ride and Survival and switch her horse out for a shieldback lizard or something since it's tanky af and they can reflexively defend each other with War Lion Stance and Worthy Mount Technique. Plus it can join battle with Ambush Predator Style and then immediately give half that initiative to Sally with Immortal Rider's Advantage for extra alpha striking. But I don't know if the side-grade into mounted tactics dilutes the pure-Melee premise too much, so I'd like to hear people's opinion.

    Bobby though gets a LOT better at essence 5. His defense, which previously was just having high soak, has been massively upgraded with Resistance charms all the way up to Aegis of Invincible Might. Against withering attacks, he can now get up to 10 motes each time he's hit, and Iron Kettle Body + the upgraded effect of Spirit Strengthens the Skin make it trivial to reset it every time. Against decisive attacks, any single attack gets massively reduced by Adamant Skin Technique + Ruin Abasing Shrug, and any followup attacks in a flurry will get negated by Aegis of Invincible Might unless Sally can somehow get past 20 hardness. The mote regen from Essence Gathering Temper makes it easy to maintain for several turns. To top it off, with Ox Body Technique x5 and Iron Skin Concentration he has 32 health levels in total, so I'd add Bloodthirsty Sword-Dancer Spirit to negate all wound penalties and add extra essence Regen.
    Offensively, Dragon's Coil Technique becomes a full excellency for both the attack and control roll for only 3m, making his grapple game incredibly efficient. He can ditch the artifact smashfists to free up some motes since Adamantine Fists of Battle ensures that even if Sally were to switch to a soak-heavy build he still has an overwhelming of 6.
    In addition, if it is allowed, then Incomparable Body Arsenal is a huge boost in power, providing heavy artifact armor traits with no essence committed and giving double 9s to all grapple attack and control rolls thanks to Devil-Strangling Attitude. He can also use God-Weapon Armament to create artifact weapons with any tags he wants, such as reach for getting Sally off her high horse or balanced to up his overwhelming value even higher.
    Even if Sally chooses to go the enhanced mount route he can Use Dragon's Coil Technique and just yeet it away; he doesn't have to kill it, just Crashing Wave Throw it 5 range bands away while farming a ton of initiative.

    So tl;dr I think essence 5 Bobby with a full Resistance suite could beat essence 5 Sally, but I think that showcases the strength of Solar Resistance moreso than Brawl. So if you want my nominee for second-strongest combat tree then there it is; Solar Resistance doesn't get mentioned nearly often enough.

                                Warning: Lengthy, rambling post…

                                This was the culmination of a ‘cooperative’ prologue session, the idea being to get myself and my fellow player/ST back up to speed(ish) with actually playing 3e while engaging with weapon tags and gambits more than we did in our previous game.

                                Harnos is going to be an NPC in the game proper, which is aimed to build towards an investigation into his (pre-ordained) disappearance. We played this with the intention that this could be the fight that he loses to explain his disappearance, so the criminals were played to win, burning willpower and stunting throughout. We explicitly didn’t want to run them as a battle group so it was a harder fight and we got to try out different tactics.

                                Write-up below, but fair warning, it’s a long ‘un that may simply make folk go, ‘Gah, what the hell is this?’ If you just wanna skip to the musings on tags, tactics and Harnos as a character, I’ve stuck them in the spoiler at the end – basically interested in musing/learning about fun ways people have combined tags and gambits and other not especially charm focused tactics.

                                Also, apologies also for the corny intro text below…

The archon’s sharp eyes would have been welcome now but he could never have made that climb, certainly not in this storm. Better he secured their passage to Arjuf. Harnos would join him – somehow – once he finally had proof. Proof as to what was behind all those disappearances. Proof that Teruk and Jald hadn’t died in vain.                                 Scenario



                                Four criminals in the pay of House Ragara, aware the magistrate is closing in on their trafficking operation and fearful their Ragara contact may have them killed to tie up loose ends, attempt to set up an ambush at the cliff-top warehouse they use to access the tunnels underneath Jau Dei. Harnos’ charms and senses allow him to scale the cliff and spot his would-be ambushers – a fight ensues inside the crumbling warehouse (i.e. the ambush failed, everyone Joins Battle normally). Beyond medium range towards the back of the warehouse are the dimly lit walkways leading down the mineshaft, which counts as difficult terrain with a -1 environmental penalty to vision (not really used in this session).

                                Harnos has used All-Encompassing Earth Sense (3m), Observer Awareness Method (1m) and his Athletics Excellency (2m) so starts with 13p/25ph, 5wp. He begins with an improvised ‘hammer’ (medium weapon with the Smashing tag) ready.

                                The criminals use the Grizzled Mercenary stats from p.497 of the core, except with tweaked weapons/tags (we didn’t bother varying their parry between those with light weapons and those with medium for sake of simplicity). They have names but I’ve just called them by their weapons for the sake of making this write-up a bit easier to digest.

                                Axe, badly wounded and abandoned, surrenders. The fight ends as the spent magistrate stops to interrogate his new prisoner as to where exactly the tunnels lead.



                                Witterings



                                ^ Harnos got real lucky on Turn 5.

                                ^ Axe (in-game name: Joru) felt spectacularly inept throughout this fight after Turn 2. We were really pleased that things ended with him captured and potentially spilling the beans to Harnos.

                                ^ We were reminded that being able to negate onslaught somehow is, right after Excellencies, pretty much the thing that makes you go, ‘Yep, you’re a setting badass’. If Harnos was, say, a melee fighter with Flame-Born Interception and Graceful Flowing Defence this fight would have been a cinch.

                                ^ Harnos was a deliberate attempt to play someone fun to fight with who wasn’t a powerhouse (Will, who played him, made utter merchants of death in 2e. Physical tertiary was a new one on him, although he couldn’t keep himself from adding Pain Tolerance to the initial build). He felt Harnos was a lot of fun (thanks to DrLoveMonkey for the suggestion) – having grapples and smashing available to him made it feel like he still had combat choices to make even though he didn’t have many combat charms. We’ve been spitballing ways to make him more important to what we want to do down the line.

                                ^ Dex 1, even with the great fun Flotsam-and-Jetsam Approach from Heirs, is unsurprisingly a real kick in the nethers, it affects too many important things like disengage, rushes and preventing people from disengaging with you. Will was paranoid about being knocked prone (he’s had irritating experiences of that in the past), and as a result Harnos ended up with a lot of Excellencies (and had to burn motes in earlier scenes) that another starter character might have foregone in order to be able to do what Will wanted him to do. As mentioned above, a 2/3/2 melee-ist with the starting melee charms and artefact weapons and armour instead of Pain Tolerance and Quick Draw is going to be more effective in most scenarios. But Harnos felt characterful...

                                ^ On the actual fight, we couldn’t decide if Javelin aiming then moving to close range next turn with the bonus intact was kosher, but it felt right so we let it go. Not sure about aura of Join Battle either...

                                ^ Harnos really should have clashed some of those attacks to save motes, but given we had a clash-happy fire aspect in our last game we wanted to see what a different approach would do. The lesson? Should have clashed more…

                                ^ Mixing ranged and melee fighters is a really obvious way of making a simple fight harder against Exalts with weak ranged options, need to do that more in the future.

                                ^ We wanted to use more gambits than we were ultimately able to, often the timing or initiative pool wasn’t there. I was a bit obsessed with trying to find a point to use Distract. Looking back, Axe maybe should have used it in Turn 1 to boost Javelin (as the person most likely to preserve initiative), but then he was the one who actually dropped Harnos into his -1s in Turn 2 which made a difference, and 7i against a difficulty minimum of 3 isn’t great. Distract feels like something that’s tricky to use well unless you’ve got two cooperating fighters already in a strong position. Has anyone else used it well?

                                ^ Disarm = good and fun – although Harnos was spec’d to negate it (we ruled Quick Draw and the surroundings meant there was plenty of stuff to hand) – and is a pretty handy tag for making not-that-threatening mooks matter in a fight – thugs armed with fighting chains are probably going to crop up in our games down the line.

                                ^ Chopping, as mentioned elsewhere many times by EVERYONE, is so niche that you’d pick almost any other weapon over a chopping sword as far as I can see.

                                ^ Smashing is still the bestest tag for my money, being prone is such a nerf.

                                ^ If you’ve read this far – thanks! And apologies for length.
