            Originally posted by Crowley View Post
            Two, why resisting Mental Influencia generales Limit Break?
            Because resisting UMI means opposing that influence by focusing solely upon your own identity, beliefs, Motivations and Intimacies. A priest, a thief, a sorcerer and a soldier, all attempting to resist Husband-Seducing Demon Dance, would probably focus differently in their efforts: priestly vows, deepest desires, will to power, and survival instincts seem likely. But all of those things are elements of the resistors' core identities--they have a chance to deny the force telling them "you want this," or "you will do that" by reinforcing their integrity of self. In effect, they are saying, "I am {Exalted-stye name}, and what I want is {motivation}!" Or, "I am {a title, office, or role essential to their sense of self}, and a {same title} would never do that!"

            The trouble for Solars, Lunars and Sidereals is that the Great Curse is, itself, a metastasizing magnification of the Virtue most critical to each of their true selves. The Curse isn't an infliction of any Neverborn's particular obsession or nature, it's a decree that "You, who have undone your betters, will be yourselves undone, and the same strength that has been magnified by your Exaltations shall be the means of your undoing."

            So when Thrice-Radiant Misho resists a Bloody Hand's UMI attempt to make him kill a Wild Hunt leader in his sleep, he does so by anchoring himself to his Compassionate nature, though that could manifest as his current Motivation (to ultimately prevent the First Age superweapon hidden in his tomb from falling into Abyssal hands) as easily as his overall dedication to preventing unnecessary suffering, his grudging respect for the courage and honest decency demonstrated by the Hunt leader's previous actions, or his desire to set a good example for his Abyssal Circle-mate, Secret. But no matter what, he has to reinforce his own innate Misho-ness, embracing the fact that he is Thrice-Radiant Misho, goddammit, and he will not kill at the command of another.

            Unfortunately, it's that same Compassion at Misho's core that is pushes him over his Limit when his Circle arrives too late to prevent his Deathknight enemies from slaughtering an entire city. And that's why Misho collapses to his knees and wails in grief and sorrow. That's why he remains wrenched with despair, even though the Abyssals are still lurking amidst the blood and gore, and the Wild Hunt is hot on their trail, and there are only 12 hours before Calibration grants the Deathlords access to his tomb and the superweapon. That's why it takes every shred of Will he has, even with his Circle aiding him, to even attempt to help them fight for their lives against overwhelming odds. Because he had found strength and resolve in his identity as Thrice-Radiant Misho the Compassionate, the archetypical Misho, Misho in his purest form. And the needs of the Misho of the present, the Misho who needs to get his shit together and lead his Circle to victory (or even escape) has been overshadowed by the Misho who always abhors cruelty, the Misho that will mourn every death in the fashion that their lives deserve, the Misho whose Misho-ness cares not for courage, or self-discipline, or dedication to a cause-- not when the important thing this tragedy, this suffering, this grieving at hand.
