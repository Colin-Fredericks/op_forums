        Originally posted by autXautY View Post

        I'm not the person this is asking, but my analysis of the corebook sidereal excellency and clashes:

        Sidereal excellency is 1m per die or target number reduction, max of (essence) dice and 3 target number reduction. 2m per static number increase, max (essence).
        At >5 die pools, target number reduction is more efficient than dice adding, and for most possible essence/attribute/ability/other sources of dice the cap is higher than solars. (At e3+ the sidereal always has a higher cap for expected successes, at e1 or e2 a near max attribute+ability can beat the sidereal if there aren't many dice from stunt, speciality, accuracy, or similar coming in.)
        The static excellency, on the other hand, has the same efficiency as everyone else, and at low essence a pretty poor cap.

        This lets them do much better on rolls than other splats before charms, and often even after charms, especially on things like withering attacks with high-accuracy weapons. Clashes put a lot of focus on winning a single roll, can get a lot of non-charm dice from accuracy and dual wielding, and compensate for the sidereal's poor Defense cap.
        An e1, dex 5, ability 5, specialty sidereal dual wielding light artifact weapons in a clash with a 1-point stunt and max excellency averages 16.8 successes. And only spends 4m for this
        A solar under the same conditions averages 15, excellent strike brings that up to 16.556, for 10 or 11 motes.
        At higher essence this gets even more extreme - e5 gives 20 average successes for 8m.

        Because of this, a clash-specialized sidereal can win lots of clashes, even against other clash-specialized enemies, and won't need to spend as many motes.

        On another note, do people think Snake Form's penalty to attacks applies to clash attacks?
        Basically this, but with more emphasis on how weak PC level Sidereals are defensively without it.

                            Okay. A breakdown of the Dreaming Pearl Courtesan's clash potential:

                            Things don't look good at the start. No armor to help you survive failed clashes, and no weapons at all. If I didn't mention it before, Robert clarified that bare hands do not enjoy a clash bonus for being paired. The Style's very first Charms do something to make up for that.

                            Pearlescent Filigree Defense is cheap and reflexive, but only makes mundane light armor without the Form, which we'll address later. Just on its own, though, the Charm lets you spend further motes to increase soak against a successful withering attack, and if your clashing opponent used a decisive attack (and it's the first decisive attack that hits you in the fight) you get a great boost to toughness.

                            Elegant Weapon Repertoire is a supplemental Charm, but since it supplements a draw weapon action, and everyone is allowed a reflexive draw weapon action at Join Battle unless they were ambushed, I would rule that you could use this Charm then to have a fully-readied weapon before anyone acts. I would also rule that you could ready two objects at once, so long as they are functionally identical enough to operate as a pair and thus enjoy the clash bonus. This lets you enchant BOTH sleeves of your long-sleeved robe, or two fans, or a pair of hair-pin sticks. As paired light-weight weapons, they now offer you the second-best clash potential in the game. The Mastery effect and alternative effect don't help win clashes, but do help take advantage of success.

                            Okay, it took two whole Charm purchases/activations, but now the DPC is armed for battle when everyone else around her might have thought her completely unequipped and unready to fight. No one can make that mistake now, since her clothes shine with magic and she's brandishing a pair of broken glass-bottle shivs in a fighting stance.

                            If she faces one opponent who beat her Join Battle roll and delays, but she's desperate enough to try clashing him anyway, she's got decent equipment for the attempt.

                            If she faces one or many opponents and beats them all on the Join Battle roll, but did not ambush them, she could delay her action and wait for one of them to attack her, so that she could clash them. If none of them attack her, she could use the reprieve to assume the Dreaming Pearl Courtesan Form, or perhaps attempt disengaging, which could put her at a safe distance and also possibly let her assume the form reflexively. Using Demure Carp Feint and Seven Storms Escape Prana helps with the later option, especially if the martial artist invested in the complementary Ability of Dodge, along with any Disengage-assisting native Charms.

                            Rather than clashing or delaying at all, she may be best off simply Disengaging at the start, if necessary, or using a Full Defense. It depends on how her attack pool compares to her Defense values, give or take the assistance of Demure Carp Feint. As always, the secondary effect of Demure Carp Feint can help off-set the Initiative cost of a Disengage action to help her avoid losing the Initiative advantage, especially with the Mastery effect.

                            None of the weapons she can prepare with Elegant Weapon Repertoire will give her a +1 to Defense, and the Style includes no Charms to assist with parrying. Parrying is only an equal or better option compared to Dodging if you've invested more dots in this Style's fundamental Ability than in the Dodge Ability.

                            The Form Charm helps with clashes, as it not only allows you to improve the basic traits of your armor and weapons, it allows you to clash at range. If you maintain your distance and your opponent is left with no better options than clumsily throwing sundry objects at you, if it comes to a clash attack, your dice pool is probably much higher. But if they have no ranged option, then they're not likely to give you an opportunity for clash, preferring to Full Defense or attempt to escape to more favorable ground.

                            If, on the other hand, they are equipped for ranged attacks, and you find yourself in a clash, you're much better off than naked, even if you're only wearing a bedsheet and holding two pillows.

                            Additionally, your ranged weapon attacks are not treated like Thrown attacks, so your accuracy bonus at short range remains unchanged from 4/5. Meanwhile, anyone forced to use Thrown weapons against you only has an accuracy bonus of 3/4 at short range, and Archery weapons can only match your 4/5. Also, don't forget your +2 clash bonus for dual-wielding pillows. Enjoy.

                            Flurry of August Leaves and Vindictive Concubine's Pillow Book can reward you for winning a clash, but won't help you succeed at one. Though, you definitely could set yourself up for a better clash result by using Flurry of August Leaves to help disarm someone before the clash, leaving them without their best attack pool.

                            Fragant Petal Fascination Kata's ability to give you a higher Initiative total than any number of your enemies can put you into a position where you can delay to clash, or alternatively protect you from clashes by denying them the choice.

                            Seven Storms Escape Prana helps the martial artist avoid clashes where she has the disadvantage, by denying close-range specialists the use of their best weapons and by avoiding grapples which would penalize her attack rolls. The Charm also makes it even easier to reflexively assume the Style's Form Charm, and thus gain the clashing potential of light artifact weapons, and the failure-insurance of light artifact armor, as well as the ability to clash projectile attacks.

                            After invoking the chimera's coils...

                            You can fucking fly. Maintaining your distance just got a lot easier, which really helps for only needing to clash projectiles if you have enough room to ascend even one range band above the ground.

                            Then there's Demure Carp Feint and Seven Storms Escape Prana.

                            If you didn't already have a weapon readied with Elegant Weapon Repertoire, you automatically gain a light artifact weapon with any of the four associated tags that you want. So, even if you already have a specifically-tagged weapon readied, you can reflexively ready any permissible weapon you can imagine.

                            Any armor you've enchanted or will enchant with Pearlescent Filigree Defense gains the traits of medium artifact armor, with no mobility penalty. Much better for failed clashes, with no downside.

                            Capitalizing on a successful clash attack gets better with Flurry of August Leaves and Vindictive Concubine's Pillow Book .

                            Fragant Petal Fascination Kata now it works even better at helping you gain the option to delay and clash, but remember that you can't use it if you've already used it once in the fight.

                            However, if you activated this Charm because you lost your last -2 health level, now you've got an even higher risk for failing a clash. If your opponent can sufficiently overcome your attack dice and your armor, succeeding with a decisive attack lets him inflict one automatic level of health track damage after damage is rolled. So he can utterly fail his damage roll and you will still automatically die, instantly.

                            Thankfully, that clash bonus specifies "rolled", so if your Hardness is good enough, it's good enough. Also, there's the option of post-roll damage reduction effects, like Snake Style's Countless Coils Evasion, or one of those native Dodge Charms you are encouraged to complete the Style with.

                                                        Originally posted by Simon Darkstep View Post
                                                        My general interpretation is that the winner of [whatever you're doing] determines the effects.

                                                        So if you're receiving a multi-attack and Clash it successfully, you'd have stopped the remainder of the attack by pulling it into your Clash.
