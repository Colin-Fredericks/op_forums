    Originally posted by Lundgren View Post
    Nope, I haven't played 2e RAW. As playing RAW is a gaming style I'm not interested in, even if I had I would never have played it long enough to run into the problem some people had with it. Now it sounds like we are heading into the Strict Kriegsspiel vs. Free Kriegsspiel argument, which goes back to the 19th century, and I feel like I'm going on like a broken record (hm... is that phrase making any sense to people who grew up with CD or mp3 players?) on everything else.
    I mean, I'm over 30; I understand the Strict/Free Kriegsspiel debate, even if I wasn't around for the D&D 1e incarnation of those arguments. But, again, when people criticize 2e, they're criticizing the game that was written, and not the hodgepodge of "rulings not rules" that exists in any particular GM's head. I have no way of determining whether 2e As A Hypothetical GM Might Mentally Rewrite It is a better game than 3e AAHGMMRI is, but the 2e rules I actually read were bad.

    (I also think that 2e had some of the most brilliant and beautiful system-and-setting marriages I've ever seen - which 3e pointedly and deliberately does not. Nephilpal's work on Infernals and Glories is a master class in rules that are also stories. But those marriages were built on a foundation of boring, unfun, and spontaneously lethal combat. These are problems that 3e does not inherently have, and the problems 3e does have - its poor keywording, ambigous resolutions, and unclear verbiage, for instance - are not hard-coded into the game in the same way that lethality was.)

        Originally posted by Drascin View Post

        Yeah, characters in Exalted 2E were just incredibly fragile. It was one of the first things that struck me, and I actually went to my ST asking if I was understanding something wrong.

        A normal Exalt has 7HP. A normal attack with basically no enhancers nor super amazing weapons (just basic artifact weapons) nor anything special, just plain "I hit the dude", in a fight between Essence users can easily be around 15 damage dice. Substract a little from Soak, and you're left with a system where, without any resources being spent, people die in two hits in all probability. Especially because getting hit once reduces your ability to defend to such a segree that you are not going to dodge the following attack.

        Now add the myriad things Exalted allows for offense. Post-soak damage adders. Poison. Ambush. Shaping. Directed environmental effects. Excellencies. Grand artifact weapons, all of which hit for ridiculous damage and ignored half of soak. Damage doublers. The list goes on and on. Anything, if it spends any kind of juice, can kill you in a single hit. So people grow paranoid and abuse perfect defenses. And the rest is history.
        Just throwing this out there:

        the average mortal also has 7 health levels.

                A wargame for training Prussian and German officers created in 1812.                                                 Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

                                                ​There are issues with vagaries of chance throwing a whole game into tumult and possibly excluding one or more of the players early.

                                                Not really, in my opinion.

                                                Many players forget that they will almost always have the option of "fleeing" from a lethal/possibly lethal situation. Running from combat, backing out of a bad social situation, etc, I almost always include some sort of "out", but the PC's have to initiate it.

                                                In my games, you get one dice fudge, usually when the players are new or the campaign just started, but after that, that is it. The dice fall where they may. NOTE: I roll all the dice in my games, behind a screen. The Players handle the RP, while I get everything else.

                                                If a PC dies in my games, it is usually pretty much their fault. Either they did something stupid, got into combat with something that was way out of their league, or refused to pay attention to "the way the wind was blowing". Get surrounded in combat? Your fault. Piss off the local authority figure? Your fault. Not recognize that the BBEG is probably gonna take you out if/when you cause a ruckus? Your fault.

                                                I don't treat my PC's any different than I do an NPC. They don't get any slack, either crunch or fluff wise. I find the whole idea preposterous, and vaguely patronizing, as a matter of fact.

                                                Asides, it takes about 5 minutes to make a new character.

                                                It also probably helps that I actually take the recently-deceased player aside and explain to them what they "did wrong" that led to character death. See if they understand the mechanics, or understood where a characters opinions and decisions were based.

                                                But, other than that, it is my story. The PCs are their characters to play, yes, but everything else is my decision. If the PC does something that would logically result in their death, then that is on them. I just run the world like it is "supposed to". Don't want to get ambushed or assassinated? Don't mess with the wrong people, or hire counterspies/bodyguards. Don't want to get surrounded in combat and get ROFL-stomped? Don't fight as an individual, fight intelligently and bring backup.

                                                It is very simple. Don't do stupid stuff, and you will almost always survive.
