                                    Originally posted by Elfive View Post
                                    Long-lived exalted and gods and shit probably put the brakes on linguistic drift.
                                    That may be a little true, but it's actually mass literacy that reduces linguistic drift, and nationalism (nationalism of course can increase linguistic differentation between countries, but most countries are too big to naturally have one language; nationalism forces them to have one language).

                                    Blaque mentioned Arabic*; the reason Arabic* has one of the smallest linguistic drifts is because of the Qu'ran (and Egyptian TV).

                                    *Arabic is of course, a macrolanguage, not a language family. It's a Central Semitic macrolanguage, a subset of Semitic (along with Hebrew and Amharic), which is a subset of the Afro-Asiatic language family, the 4th biggest (in population). The largest is Indo-European (which includes everything from English to Russian to Iranian to Hindi to Bengali), followed by Sino-Tibetan, followed by Niger-Congo. After Afro-Asiatic you have Turkic and Dravidian. You can see a nice map here.
                                    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langua...milies_Map.png

                                    The Age of Sorrows doesn't have mass literacy, sacred texts (except in the Realm... which probably has a reasonably realistic level of linguistic drift), or 19th-century levels of nationalism. So long-lived Exalts aren't going to make much difference to peasants. They're just going to sound weird and old-fashioned when they do talk to peasants.
                                    (Though Old Realm probably won't change nearly as much, as that is mostly spoken by gods and long-lived Exalts, rather than peasants)

                                    I mean, I'm totally fine with saying "it's way too much hassle to do it otherwise, we'll just assume these language families are much more mutually intelligible than real-world ones". But I don't think there's much point pretending its realistic.
                                    I'll just say a Sidereal did it.

                                                Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz
                                                Old Realm probably won't change nearly as much, as that is mostly spoken by gods and long-lived Exalts


                                                Considering the fact that we don't really have actual immortals on which to base things, I'd say that's a subject that depends more on the priorities of the writer and developer than any kind of independent logic.

                                                Look at JRR Tolkien, a man who probably forgot more about linguistics than many of us will ever know, and what he did with the multiple and evolving languages of his immortal Elves to reflect his own delight in the intricacies of how languages develop and function. The premise he gave behind that wasn't even limited to the fact that the Elves splintered into multiple groups that lived in significantly different contexts and were often separated by wide distances; the language of the Eldar of Valinor was supposed to continue evolving in its own right in a manner that moved it even further from their Sindar cousins, on the basis that a combination of Elves having a natural predilection for language and that unburdened eternity gives them both the time and a certain requirement for novelty meant that they took a delight in coming up with ever more elaborate forms of vocabulary and terminology and methods and arts of speech.

                                                From my own priorities, I would be inclined to think that there are distinct, if mutually comprehensible variations of Old Realm simply because I think it would make for more interesting characterisation. Especially considering the divide in speakers between gods, demons and Fair Folk (the latter in particular the types I would readily characterise as using the language in such a flowery and overwrought manner as to often be as readily comprehensible as a Lewis Carroll poem, and that many other speakers find this obnoxious in the extreme, to the general delight of the fae in question).

                                                Plus my idea that there are certain Lunar elders that are difficult to speak to even for people fluent in Old Realm because they rather insistently use the language in a manner archaic enough to actually make figuring out what they're saying a bit of a slog.

                                                Although that runs into the question of how much an Exalt can effectively retain archaic speech if they have a need to communicate with anybody other than fellow ancient Exalted, but I'd say that's one that is often effectively sidestepped by Linguistics Charms allowing people to generally get the gist of what you're saying, and vice versa.

                                                            Wow, this really exploded! I eventually found what I was looking for - a 2nd Ed paragraph in Lords of Creation, gods help me, that confirms that the High First Age Exalted created the directional languages as a structural support for the Order-Conferring Trade Pattern. However unrealistic that is, and it's pretty bloody unrealistic, it does answer the question I was asking.

                                                            For myself, I think keeping the directional languages unrealistic serves simplicity - I don't want characters to have to spend a bunch of XP every time they go somewhere new just so they can speak the language, and unless it's a specific plot point, I don't want to juggle language barriers. There's probably a fine House Rule somewhere that would accommodate both extremes, but I haven't found it yet.

                                                            In the Exalted: Modern shard I created on my blog, I dealt with the modern world's languages by letting players roll (Intelligence + Linguistics) to learn a new language, with the difficulty set by the ST depending on the complexity of the language and the speed of learning it dependent upon the net successes rolled. (Yes, this meant some Exalts could learn a new language instantly. I find this in-genre appropriate.)
