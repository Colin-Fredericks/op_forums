                                    I've just come up with a really simple system tweak that I think puts the numbers closer to what is described in the books and/or is sensible.

                                    What we do is keep the target numbers as-is, but instead of rolling just one die, we roll a variable number based on the parents involved, and need to get at or below the target on all of them. Dragonbloods add a die to the pool each, mortals with terrestrial bloodline add 2 and regular mortals add 3. This has the advantage of only dropping the dragonblooded odds a bit whilst really kicking the ones involving mortals in the balls. For comparison it makes the kid of two dragonblooded of breeding 3 or lower have a 36% chance of exalting instead of 60, but makes the kid of two good blooded patricians have a 0.16% chance of exalting instead of 20%.

                                    Thoughts?

                                            Originally posted by Elfive View Post
                                            If you assume about half the kids of dragonblooded exalt, then obviously the number of unexalted dynasts is going to be the same as the number of exalted ones. Less, because they don't live as long.
                                            It's not that simple - you have to consider what kids the mortal kids are more likely to have. More to the point, the books have always presented the Dragon-Blooded as the elite of the elite of dynast society, not a half-and-half mix.

                                            Also people without any terrestrial blood exalt on storyteller fiat alone, so if your estimate of the percentage produces numbers too high then it's [I]too high itself.
                                            When describing the world, rather than a game, it's not helpful to talk about storyteller fiat: in general, if we wanted to summarize the way Realm society works, what are the numbers? That's the question of interest here. And the answer to it matters, because a Realm where 10% of non-DB Dynast children Exalts is, shall we say, rather different from one where one in 30,000 Exalt, and your Exalted kid is the most important person that anyone in your social circle will ever know.

                                            Also, a good chunk of realm dragonbloods are in the order and don't breed.
                                            This is also true of human society; average birth rate figures take that kind of thing into account. (Real world birth rates range from .7% in Japan, which is "SHOOT SHOOT EVERYBODY PANIC" low even given modern lifespans, to about 5% in less-developed countries. If you're going to take the book's numbers on DB pressure to have children, you actually get a birth rate quite a bit higher than 3% - so again, you have to throw out something.)

                                            And they are noted to have a bonkers mortality rate.
                                            Originally posted by Omicron
                                            Dragon-Bloods tend to die.

                                            A lot.
                                            In the example I gave earlier, I worked my way up to 750 DBs/year; for a balanced population, that implies a 7.5% mortality rate. I'm going to run with that number for what follows, because I have to run with some number; if you think this is the wrong one, that's fine, but treat it as a rebalancing point to figure out what number does make sense. From the fact that people are objecting, I'm going to assume that, on the surface, 7.5% doesn't sound that bad to at least some folks.

                                            So, let's talk about bonkers mortality rates. Africa has a pretty bonkers mortality rate, wouldn't you say, of modern countries? South Africa has the highest - and it's 1.9%. Iraq, if we want to add an active war zone to the picture, has a mortality rate of 0.4%.

                                            7.5% is stupidly higher than that. Let's talk about what 7.5% mortality rate means: it means that every nine years, half of everyone you know is dead. The time from entering high school, to shortly after graduating college? Half of everyone you know is dead. Your ten-year college reunion? Half of your graduating class is dead. Reached age thirty? You're a lucky guy, because 90% of your fellows won't. This is a mortality rate that might make you envious of the longevity of your mortal peasants.

                                            The Black Death, in Europe, was a time of pretty bonkers mortality, wouldn't you say? Wikipedia estimates that maybe 45% of Europe died during the Black Death, over the course of several decades. If we concentrated all of those deaths into just the 7 years when the plague was worst, that's a 7.5% mortality rate.

                                            Is that the mental image we have of the Realm: seven hundred years of Black Plague-level mortality? Because that's where these numbers go.

                                                    Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post
                                                    Hmmm what if only a small percentage of DBs get married? With most dying in the threshold or living out their days without having kids? It might be an okay explanation if you ignore all the dynastic pressure to get a husband and start cranking out children.
                                                    If Realm society was rewritten so that most Dynasts are basically sex-averse and birth rates are closer to modern Japan, sure, we could probably make this work, as long as we also are all clear that non-Exalts have teeny-tiny fractions-of-a-percent chances of having Exalted kids. That would be a huge rewrite to Realm society, though.

                                                    ***

                                                    Look, the big thing is: don't try to headspace this. Don't just think about numbers and go, "Eh, that sounds right"; as humans, we're really really terrible at statistics. Run a model! Figure out which of the given population figures you have to ignore (the sizes? the longevity? the birth rate? the Exaltation rate?), what real-world benchmarks those things correspond to, and then how the Realm has to look different as a result. You can run the Realm as "Black Death Europe," but how does it shape society when you're probably going to be dead in ten years? How does it shape things if most DBs are born to unExalted patricians? To peasants? How are things different if you had a graduating class with two Exalts, as opposed to (as the books seem to presume) several hundred?

                                                    But you can't just uncritically take the book's figures and go with them. That way lies madness.

                                                        I understand that not everyone sees it this way, but I don't really feel any compulsion to make the numbers make sense. Exalted is a world that operates on drama. An arrow fired from a bow will literally fly in a straighter trajectory if released in a dramatic fashion. An object at rest will remain at rest until drama acts upon it. An object in motion will continue in motion until acted upon by drama.

                                                        The numbers given are only there for the purposes of story. The real reason there are supposedly 10,000 Dragon-Bloods in the Realm is because someone thought 100,000 would make the game too difficult for Solar PCs. The reason there are 11 Great Houses is because (I assume) someone decided that roughly two houses for each element was a good number. And the rate of Exaltation is pretty much dictated by the story. But the actual numbers in various books don't really add up when you look at them and to my knowledge they never have, so I pretty much just disregard numbers entirely. How many Dragon-Bloods are in the Realm? "A lot." A more precise estimate? As many as the story demands.
