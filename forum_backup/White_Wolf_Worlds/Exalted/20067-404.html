        How does Guard-Breaking Technique work? For context, the relevant part of the charm reads as follows:

        "When using defend other, the Exalt may use this Charm to use a distract gambit (p. 200) to benefit her charge, with double 7s on the Initiative roll."

        Say Bob has initiative 10, Jack has initiative 5, and BBEG has initiative 8. Bob wants to use Defend Other on Jack.

        1) Does Bob have to declare Guard Breaking technique when he declares his defend other, on tick 10, before anyone attacks Jack? Or does he declare it on tick 8, when BBEG declares his attack on Jack?
        2) Say BBEG attacks Jack on his tick. This makes Bob's defend other come into play. How exactly does the charm work? Since it allows the use of the distract gambit, does that mean that now Bob has to launch a reflexive decisive attack on BBEG and hit to be able to roll initiative and transfer the successes? Or does he simply get to roll initiative and transfer the successes?

        I lean towards the first option, but it seems too complicated; on the other hand, by RAW, a gambit is a decisive attack and thus has to hit before its effect takes place.

        3) If Bob uses War Lion Stance and has access to reflexive defend other actions, when does he declare the use of Guard Breaking Technique? Does he have to declare it at the beginning of each turn he plans to invoke it, or whenever someone attacks Jack?

            I would run it as if Guard Breaking Technique was a counter attack, except since it doesn't have the keyword that means you can 'distract' someone who has counter-attacked your charge.

            Your charge is attacked, your defend other activates and you then declare Guard Breaking Technique reflexively, make your decisive attack and pass over successes of initiative to your charge to use as a decisive attack against that same target as their next action.

            It doesn't actually specify though, so by RAW you could created a whole bunch of reflexive attacks on enemies to screw them with onslaught assuming you had enough initiative to pay for all those gambits.

                                                            Originally posted by Handigar View Post
                                                            Stupid question most likely, but can you generically spend Initiative you don't have, thus crashing yourself, like the Disengage example in the combat chapter?
                                                            The system penalizes you heavily for crashing yourself, which implies that yes you can do the action, but you probably shouldn't.

                                                            Quote from page 194 of the core: "If a character enters Initiative Crash as a result of his own actions (such as by using a disengage action, which costs 2 Initiative to attempt, with only 2 Initiative remaining), he immediately loses another 5 Initiative."

                                                            Additionally, "If a character forces himself into Initiative Crash (such as by using a Charm which costs Initiative to activate), then the Initiative Break bonus is awarded to the opponent most directly responsible for provoking the action which caused the character to Crash, at the Storyteller’s discretion."

                                                            So if you Disengage from a foe and that Disengage crashes you, not only do you lose 5 extra initiative, but the foe you're Disengaging from GAINS 5 additional initiative. Yeouch.
