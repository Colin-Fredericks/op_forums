        In the early Shogunate, the Immaculate school (a system of ideological teachings spread through the Dragon-Blooded military, not an organized religion) used the term “Immaculate Dragons” to refer to veterans of the Uprising (aka the Usurpation), and those who died fighting Anathema - the idea being that their deeds had purged them of karmic sin and debt, allowing them to become one with the Elemental Dragons in death, thus leading to the the actions of different Air DBs to be referred to as “Deeds of Mela”, the actions of different Fire DBs as “Deeds of Hesiesh”, etc.

        By the late Shogunate, these concepts had given rise to archetypical Dragon Blooded figures used as parables, combining the “deeds of Mela” into one Air-Aspected archetype, and so on.

        After the Contagion, the Immaculate Order is born as an organized dogmatic religion, and the previous parable archetypes are canonized as “historic” divine persons.

        The 7th Legions of course still follows the Immaculate Faith of the late Shogunate, however with over seven centuries of removal from that era, the distinction between literal divine persons, and parable archetypes has been largely blurred to non-existence. One of the biggest gaps in the ideologies though (besides the 7th Legion's far greater religious tolerance for the rest of the Riverlands than the Immaculate Order's behavior toward things like the Hundred God Heresy, the Immaculate Faith is about proper behavior for Dragon-Blooded, not a proper order to all the world) is that the 7th Legion views the Immaculate Dragons as having begun as "normal" Dragon-Blooded who achieved unity with the Dragons through their deeds, while the Immaculate Order views them as having always been the Dragons Incarnate, made manifest to liberate Creation from the Anathema and guide the Dragon-Blooded to righteousness.

                                This is one that came to me randomly as a fun way to mess with my Solar PC cult leader on his first trip to Heaven.

                                It is a well know, and oft-repeated rule in the Five-Fold Fellowship that Sidereals are not to create cults. This rule, however, says nothing of the other types of Exalted. In the days of the Old Realm, Solar and Lunar rulers with vast flocks of worshipers would sometimes visit Yu-Shan to exploit this loophole, walking away with huge armfuls of divine goods. While this was largely frowned upon by the Viziers, no regulation stood that could forbid the action (which was generally seen as harmless laziness by artificers looking for quick access to materials anyway). The true danger of this practice came from unscrupulous Sidereals who would use the grand displays of their fellow Celestials to pull similar stunts on a smaller scale. After the Usurpation, the Celestial Bureaucracy devised a fool-proof plan to deal with such embezzlement. Since the Solars were no more, and the Lunars driven to exile would not visit Heaven, the Bureaucracy wrote a rule that any Exalted who entered Yu-Shan with the prayers of a cult would be showered in rainbow Quintessence. While harmless on the surface, this would instantly mark any Sidereal as a rule-breaker, making them a target for Censors, or in this more lawless age, the target of thieves and murderers. As an unintended positive consequence, it also happened to out infiltrating Infernal and Abyssal Exalted, as their depraved followers screamed their names. This rule stood for many centuries and worked perfectly fine, but the return of the Solars has caused a bit of consternation. Now, once again, Exalts walk Heaven's streets with the wealth of worship. However, no matter their type, they are marked by this law, and thus as criminals in the eyes of every god nearby. This has caused more than a few scuffles and cases of mistaken identity. After a particularly nasty incident involving a Zenith bishop, his Circle, and several hundred Ambrosial coins worth of property damage, an addendum was added to the law. Now, Solars (and the odd Lunar) are rapidly directed to the Golden Barque, the heart of all commerce in Heaven, and are registered as legal cult leaders. This ends the humiliating showers and causes prayer to them to manifest as Ambrosia blocks nearby, as it does for any other god, and lets the Exalts of Sun and Moon once again bask in the endless wealth born of mortal praise. That is, provided they can make it there without being accosted by slumming divines. After all, walking around with a shower of Quintessence over your head in the poorer districts has been likened to walking the streets of Nexus with a loudly jangling coin-purse; in other words, simply demanding trouble.

                                                            Something fairly small, but here's a headcanon for one small aspect of Dynastic Manners

                                                            When a younger Dynast meets with an older family member of theirs (who may be a century or more old, because Dragon Bloods), proper decorum insists that when the younger Dynast first speaks to their family member, they address them precisely in relation. However, after the initial introduction, the older family member may insist on dropping the 'greats' from the term of address, at which point it's considered acceptable for the younger member to do so for the rest of the meeting.

                                                            However, sometimes the older member may be feeling vindictive, and in such a case will insist on the proper address the whole time.

                                                            For Example:

                                                            If a promising young Dynast in the House Cathak Legion were to meet a hero of House Cathak, manners would insist that the younger Dynast address the older one as "Great great uncle Narak", or whatever. Narak, being in a good mood and having heard that this new soldier has a promising career, responds with "For you? Please, call me Uncle Narak", and for the rest of the lunch it's acceptable to do so.

                                                            However, if an aspiring Sorceress of House Mnemon caught Mnemon in a bad mood (that is to say, MOST days), then Mnemon is within her rights to insist that the Sorceress keep referring to her as "Great great great grandmother" for the rest of the family reunion.
