            Originally posted by TSk View Post
            I think there's probably some number between "ten thousand" and "tens of millions", isn't there?
            Depends - how low do you want to set the growth rate? Couch gave a (birth*exaltation - death) rate of 0.9%, and while my numbers for that calculation aren't quite the same as his, they still give you a population of ~10 million. If you want something more like 100,000 over 768 years, you're talking about a (birth*exaltation - death) rate of ~0.3%.

            "Growth rate" is a dependent variable, of course, so the question is really, "Where do you want to set the birth, Exaltation, and death rates?" If the total difference is only 0.3%, you still can't justify the books' seeming opinion of "Children of Dragon-Blooded are likely to Exalt, and Dragon-Blooded have lots of kids."

            And the Wyld Hunt is understaffed largely because, in the last five years, the number of Anathema in Creation has doubled, and because a political crisis has thinned its ranks still further. You don't need that much to explain it.
            Right, which works fine with a stable DB population, but less well when there are a thousand times more Dragon-Blooded now than at the Realm's founding. That's what I mean - the presentation of an empire in decline gets harder to justify when the DBs are increasing and the Celestials are a static number.

                                Actual aristocratic families, historically, limit their own reproduction, because you don't want too many heirs, family members you can't support and won't inherit. And for the same reason, their growth does not pose such problems to the monarch.

                                        In the case of the Empire, the pressure actually comes from the Empress rather than the great ouses themselves. I think the idea was a purely selfish one - the Empress did it in order to limit the power base of her potential rivals and keep them easier to control or at least keep tabs on. Left to themselves, the great houses would surely want to expand, but those who do so beyond a limit the Empress set would have to pay for it... either fees or no further subsidies, I don't remember which one at the moment, but either are good for the Imperial treasury. If you are not worried about an external foe - and the Empire during its heyday had no reason to be - then ensuring internal stability and safety becomes a top priority for the ruler. It definitely sacrifices a fair bit of the possible effectiveness of the Dragon-Blooded host, but I think from the standpoint of someone who made the working of the empire pretty much contingent on herself it makes sense.

                                        Incidentally, the two avenues open to Found Eggs - and the system, by making it undesirable to have a large number of legitimate heirs, tacitly encourages at least some extramarital procreation - herd them into the organisations that the Great Houses would (at least imo) have the hardest time controlling. The Immaculate order is strictly neutral but the Empress is probably the outsider with the most sway, and Pasiap's Stair prepares them to be officers loyal to the Realm, not to a great house. To me, this is a further indication that the system was planned in a way to limit those whom the Empress saw as the most likely potential rivals and solidify her own power base. It does mean sacrificing the overall potential of the Empire against an external threat, but for the vast majority of the Realm's history, there simply was no (credible) outside threat to its existence.
