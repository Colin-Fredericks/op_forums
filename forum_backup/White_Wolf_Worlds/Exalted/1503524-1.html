                            Originally posted by Erinys View Post
                            I have nobody to play with and no idea whether or not my favorite splat will be recognizeable to me, so I would rather know the basics rather than spend my very limited money on an unillustrated draft and then spend more again on the finished version.
                            As noted by Blaque, this is the fully illustrated, properly laid out, ready-to-go PDF. There will be one more set of edits based on the community proofing, but it's basically done. (And if you buy it now, you'll get the updated version when it lands at DriveThru anyway.)

                            I don't want to buy a new version of my favorite splat with NO info on the risk I may hate it and also have nobody to play it with? Plus pay for a bunch of other splats I can't use either.

                            I am reading older threads about Essence at the moment and they don't have any concrete information about Infernals either. I have seen references to podcasts and videos on Discord, which are useless to me because I'm hard of hearing.​
                            I am a huge Infernals fan and I'm very happy with ExEss. The castes feel very flavourful, even in short write ups, and the Charmset lets me take all the cool stuff I wanted in 2e but couldn't have because of Patrons.

                            For example, is anyone willing to please share a basic description of the neo-Infernal castes, more than just their names? (themes and favored Abilities, sobriquets, who they are Chosen from, differences from corresponding Solar and 2e castes) The sort of things that used to be posted for free on the wiki?
                            Azimuth: War, destruction and punishing the corrupt are key themes for the Azimuth. These guys are like the Slayers or Malfean Urge Infernals of old. Powers include adding your Essence in successes to intimidation, killing your Essence in extras, and assuming the Devil-Body Incarnation (2e Shintai, of course) for free. Very cool.

                            Ascendant: These "charming priest-kings" and "blasphemous missionaries" proselytise about the hypocrisy of society and undermine its values in the hearts of others. They are the anti-Christs and serpents of Hell, chosen from among those who were cast out and despised. These are your Malefactors reborn, who easily seduce others to give in to their darker urges, and to rise up in anarchy or rebellion. They also desecrate the ground by literally going all scorched earth.

                            Horizon: These Infernals are brilliant, powerful and sorcerous. All of them denied their potential in life due to inequality or enslavement. These are your Defilers 2.0. They find it easier to engage in ventures (projects/fancy extended tests) with their cunning and strategy, may add Essence successes to Craft and Sagacity (lore) rolls, and can do some cool stuff involving others' Essence and Willpower (increasing others' Charm costs or stealing their WP).

                            Nadir: These are your "rogue class" Infernals, chosen from those who were imprisoned or oppressed and who now seek to liberate others. They are the Scourges of old, and can be more discreet (dampening their anima), moving silently in plain sight (bonus successes to Athletics and Stealth), and can do the Assamite/Quietus 1 thing of creating a zone of silence in which they can murder their targets.

                            Penumbra: These are the spies and ambassadors of the Infernals. They rejected wealth after discovering how corrupting it was, and spread paranoia and distrust among the powerful. They are the Fiends of Essence, and get to perform the usual Eclipse shenanigans, gain respect from spirits and demons, and can instil certain insidious Intimacies or break oaths.

                            Or just, what are the bullet points version of how Infernals differ from the 2e version? (since I can barely remember the old 3e preview other than disliking it)
                            They're still Hell's rockstars and royalty. They're more like the second half of 2e Infernals than the first half. So none of the weird squick about demon wombs that could've been ripped from Urotsukidoji, and a much stronger focus on "you've been wronged; now it's time to take revenge".

                            Essentially, the Yozis pick people who've been oppressed, downtrodden or abused, and they whisper in their ear, "Hey, do you want to make it all right again? Do you want to show those d*cks what you're made of, and let them know how it feels to be helpless? Do you want to punish injustice and wreak vengeance on all the people who've exploited others and got away with it? Yes? Then here are the keys to Hell, kid... *and even quieter* Oh, and if you can blow the cosmos up while you're at it, that'd be great. Ta!"

                            Key themes: righteous indignation, glorious rebellion, delicious vengeance, and all the power fantasies you ever dreamed of as an outsider kid in the 1990s.​

                            Does Mind-Hand Manipulation exist? Is it availabe at character creation?
                            Yes and yes. And there are some other fan favourites in there, to boot.

                            Or, do the 3 new types of Exalted (Exigents, Liminals, Getimians) have any sobriquets or favored Abilities or Attributes as a whole, or for individual castes and aspects?
                            They all get their own write ups with castes, anima powers and exalt advantages, exactly like the other exalts. But the above took me quite a while to summarise, so I won't do it for three more exalt types just now. They are quite interesting, though, and add a lot.

                            Exigents are your "Build Your Own Exalts" splat, representing all those exalts created by minor deities and other forgotten/less well known beings. Liminals are creepy, undead, Promethean-esque exalts, who are more body horror than the Abyssals. Getimians are like Arnold in Terminator: Dark Fate. They come from a future/present that never happened, and so are like debris cast out of the space-time continuum.

                            Basically, these three splats are designed to mop up some of the "kitchen sink" qualities that were added to Infernals and Abyssals, and to add a foil to the Sidereals (respectively), so that those splats each become clearer and more focused as a result. They're neat, although I probably won't play one, as I'm Infernal all the way.
