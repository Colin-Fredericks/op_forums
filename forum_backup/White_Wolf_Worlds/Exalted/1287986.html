    A Philosophical Question concerning Artifacts

    02-14-2019, 01:25 PM
    So one of the biggest, and in my opinion best, changes from 3e was the addition of Evocations, giving artifacts a lot more personality than they used to. The evocations of artifacts are related to their personal Narrative, especially the legacy artifacts for the Dragonblooded. However, you can also create new artifacts even 5 dot ones, which presumably don't have a story related to them as of yet, but you are still able to get evocations for them which do all follow a theme. Should these evocations be related to destiny, in anticipation of a story yet to come? In this case does the dot rating of the artifact merely describe the scale of the legend it can reflect?

    Second, since they are now more based on Narrative, does that make them inherently irreproducible? Could you produce an artifact that can make copies of artifacts?

      Narratively, yes, you charge as many dots as the artifact will have if its story were fulfilled. Forging a Solar a daiklave because he wants a weapon is a small narrative, but forging Solar Optimus a daiklave so that he can sunder the Underworld is a far larger narrative, and should have more Evocations.

      Second, because they are narrative driven, making an artifact to copy artifacts would copy the physical copy, but because the narrative of why the artifact was made isn't there any more, the Evocations would be lesser, or maybe even practically non-existent.

              Duplicating the general form and base powers of an artifact is easy. There are tons of daiklaves, winterbreath jars, audient brushes, magic suits of armor, folding ships, etc.

                      When it comes down to it also remember the components are going to be a huge factor in how an artifact is created and its subsequent evocations. Winterbreath jars and such can be recreated with fewer problems than trying to remake a specific skycutter, or goremaul.* Gorgon and Stormcaller both have specific ancient monsters forged into them, or the weapon forged around them, meaning any attempt to recreate one is going to inherently be different because either you have an entity like that to use or you don't. Of course said entity's own will, personality, and abilities would also influence the resulting artifact. So Gorgon the second might end up lacking the ability to turn those its killed to stone as a base evocation, but instead turn any injury to stone with that spreading as a poison, due to that being more the new entity's way of operating.

                      Then you have artifact creation intent. Beloved Adorei and Volcano Cutter were mostly constructed to fullfill a specific purpose and that grew into their influence. Adorei was meant to be a partner to and keep a specific solar out of trouble and Volcano Cutter was meant to handle regenerating foes by burning them to ashes. Attempting to recreate those artifacts would likely be incorporated into the new one's legend and consequently how it responds when it starts developing evocations and potential personality. So the new Adorei has knowledge of a predecessor and a challenge that a being like it had been made to accomplish before, and failed. Volcano Cutter literally wants to burn the world the cinders, what the heck comes out of something created in as close to the same way as possible when it also has the knowledge that it is an imitation?

                      Oh I'm not saying it couldn't be done, I mean easiest method would just be Wyld Shape a new one with the same narrative, and maybe a bit tacked on or edited about how it fell into the Wyld when it was lost to the world all these centuries. I just feel like there's a bit more fun to be had in the idea that recreating powerful artifacts comes with unforeseen complications and results as opposed to just you know having half a dozen wrackstaffs that are completely identical to Tailon wandering Creation smacking people about to appease grievances.


                      *Granted they'd theoretically have their own evocations if you managed to unlock it, but its noted that winterbreath jars able to gain evocations are anomalous enough not counting people who're able to bond with it well enough to unlock one.

                                      First the legend is an important thing an artifact, but it wouldn't create one just because there's a cool legend at play.An exalt who defeated a thousand exalted foes in single combat would be impressive, but it would be the exalt who is considered spectacular in this story not the artifacts.* As snakeandsuns mentions though artifacts with evocations do end up with their own personalities and the indication seems to be they are slightly emphatic an example being that Sesus Choran became convinced that his work as an assassin severely cheesed off the Black Wind, artifact skycuter, and that it was going to try and kill him when he used it one day.** Leading to the possibility that these thousand artifacts would probably view the exalt in question in ways ranging from dislike at their own treatment if the exalt is just using the new toy, to just viewing the exalt as a temporary set of hands they passed through if the exalt stuck to a preferred weapon and only used the others when needed. In any cases it would be an anomaly for an exalt to manage to unlock the powers of all 1000 artifacts as opposed to a given.

                                      The ashes of the exalt would make good material components for an artifact, or if the exalt was performing some sort of ritual after each battle then the final product from those thousand battles and rituals could also be used as a component. Spontaneous artifact existence due to a really cool legend is not the most reliable method for acquisition of a 5 dot artifact.

                                      Now the forging of a 5dot artifact requires a few things.( Baring charms screwing with time scales) About 2 years for a 5 dot and 1 year for a 4 dot artifact and a number of rare material components and various complex forging processes and sort of magic.(Sorcerery isn't strictly required, but if your project is going to require crystallized rage then it helps for acquiring it.) The specifics will vary based on the desired outcome for the artifact in question. Remember though mistakes can happen, and it is entirely possible to get unintended effects from a mistake made late in the process.

                                      Now as to the thing of carrying an object through later events and it improving. First a quick thing "unremarkable" isn't really a term to associate with artifacts anymore, nothing big just a thing to remember that officially there is no such thing as a generic diaklave or goremaul, etc. Now onto the fun part. It is most certainly not unheard of in legends or stories for weapons and armor to gain special or mystical abilities as a result of their journey. Volfer's sword is described as "insane, twisted by the blood of a great demon it once struck down," so it most certainly is not impossible for a weapon that has been part of great things to end up altered and more mystical than they were when they began. A favorite example I've made before was the idea of a weapon used by an exorcist clan and consequently regularly anointed with potions, or holy oils to be able to harm spirits may one day end up just having that function due to countless applications. Granted there is no hard and fast rule for this sort of thing beyond discuss it with the ST and your group.


                                      *Heck for that particular story I'd just have the legend be a thing debated about by those seeking to acquire some of those weapons since it would be helpful to know if the Chakram that cut down Raiton of the Granite Abyss was Vainglory, Sunflash, or something else entirely.

                                      **Granted Sesus could have also been insane, or suffering a crisis of conscious, but it is notable that all of the evocations associated with Black Wind are unlocked via killing for ideals or killing murderers and Sesus being an assassin is very unlikely to have ever unlocked any of them.
