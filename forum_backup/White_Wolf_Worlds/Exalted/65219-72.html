                    Canonical writing is what I use as the basis of my game. It keeps things consistent. But yes, the Akuma basically get their traits overwritten (though to be honest, they don't have to be overwritten). You get a pile of bonus points or XP and spend them tailoring the Akuma to the Yozi's whims. Its not that hard a process to understand... "Here's more points to spend, spend as you see fit". How is that hard to work with? All your doing is adding a few Demonic Wyld Mutations and bumping stats.

                    As far I see things, an Exigent is just a divine version of Akuma. Now a God might not have the power to overwrite someone's DNA, they're not the Primordial Creators of the World after all. But they can grant boosts to stats through charms as training effects normally, this is known from their standard Charm set in 2e. Gods may have more unique powers in 3rd edition, which would be nice, but honestly thats not hard to create anyway. Basically to create an Exigent, add XP or bonus points just like an Akuma would, taylor the champion to the god's needs, grant charms and powers based on what the god has access to and you're done. It's quick, dirty exigent creation, and it works for now, until the actual official book is finally released, whenever in 2018/19 that happens... As I said in a previous post, my faith in the writing team isn't very high right now. Especially considering Paradox Interactive (White Wolf's new parent company) is putting greater emphasis on the World of Darkness line than it is on Exalted. To me, this emphasis means the writers at Onyx Path will likely be derailed into more WoD content and Exalted is likely to continue its already four year delayed release schedule...

                    Also, to the 3rd edition people, nothing against you, but my group has agreed we aren't switching to 3rd until enough books are released to make it worth doing. The fact only the Solars book is out doesn't make us want to shift gears. We've been watching the painfully slow release of 3rd since the kickstarter first launched, many of us were backers. We just have no desire to play it with limited Storyteller options when we already have the full 2e line and I can create NPCs using that line. If I'm going to have to spend time converting material from the previous edition, its just as easy to continue using the previous edition until the new edition is finally chugging along and the books are actually out.

                    Epee102: Regarding Headcanon... I don't mind the idea of making changes to canon, if they make sense. However, as a Storyteller, I have to keep a degree of Consistency or players get upset. If I said, ok lets change Calibration to happen on the 20th day of the 4th month of the year, Call it Ramudan, and everyone smokes Hashiesh on that day to Celebrate I'm sure a few players might laugh but someone is likely going to disagree with this notion. Especially if I dropped it in the middle of an ongoing series. Now if you want to do Shards of the Exalted Dream stuff and just do something that changes the setting in a new way, great have fun doing that if it works for you. Part of the reason Storytellers, Dungeon Masters, and Game Masters (whatever you use to describe us) like having pre-generated settings is because someone already did the work for us. If I told you I was running a Forgotten Realms campaign and gave you an alternative calendar when you showed up to play and showed you 100 changes I made to the setting you'd probably be upset over it, because you expected something and what I'm giving you isn't what you expected. As the Storyteller for my group, I try to limit headcanon to reasonable things, like Behemoth building. Sure Nexus can evolve over the Series to become a steampunk capital of the East over time but that's not something I'd drop on my players who may have only just read Exalted and expect the setting to be whats in the book. Gods and Monsters, Weapons, Artifacts, Manses... all great for Headcanon, but if you are going to change Calibration you need to make sure that kind of change goes with your player's expectations up front. Its not something I'd just do and drop into the game without players knowing about it up front. If I was running Shards of the Exalted Dream, players would know this so they can expect a variation on the basic theme.

                                                            This depends on how your Storyteller handles it. The Return of the Scarlet Empress is one possibility of many. You are not required to treat that adventure as canonical, why? Because nothing in it was really set in stone. Even the prelude module leading up it the storyteller had to do all the work building the module, then they dropped "The Scarlet Harlot Returns" and basically did the same thing... here's some possible ideas for a storyteller to use... but the storyteller has to flesh out everything. I would not consider that canonical at all. Any storyteller can rule that the Empress is missing because someone assassinated her. Truthfully if Adorjan found out about the Ebon Dragon's plans she would most certainly intervene in such a plot. Infernal exalted are not always working toward the same ends, their own paths can be blocked by other infernals. Lytek forbid should regent Fokuf have actually killed her and be something more than just a schmuck sitting on the throne letting people push him around. It would be a damn shame if he was actually an Infernal exalt much like Manosque Cyan, plotting for a different Yozi patron. It all depends on what your Storyteller decides to do with the dangling story threads. The reclamation could just as easily fail on its own due to the natures of yozis not trusting each other. These are plots that can come into play or not. The storyteller can decide night one as she writes her notes that the Empress, already well past her prime for a Dragon-Blood died of Old Age and someone hid her body intending to start a civil war and just let things go from there. Maybe the Realm collapses under its own weight in the civil war and is reclaimed by Solars in the Chaos. Maybe the Lunars Lay claim to it and chase the Dragon Blooded and Solars to the corners of Creation and force them to live at the periphery of the map as they once endured for centuries. Who knows, its really up to your Storyteller to decide these things. Nothing in Exalted is truly set in Stone. If I buy a module there's nothing stopping me from changing or editing it. The endgame that Onyx Path published was just one possible ending. Autochthon could return in the sky, tainted by darkness and become a deathstar after being reshaped by infernal essence and Creation could be the new Alderaan. If you really want to start getting outlandish we can crack open Shards of the Exalted dream. The empress can be come Empress Palpatine, the Roseblack can be Darth Vader and every one has blaster rifles... Then we can get Michael Bolton singing about Jack Sparrow in this new Star Wars universe we're playing in while everyone else is back in the Nightclub on Nar Shada partying with the Twi Lek hotties on the dance floor...

The map given alluded to it being a sphere, so I don't think its a far stretch to say that his body may actually look the death star. Especially given infernal influence or full blown gremlin syndrome taking control of him. One man's Engine of Extinction could be another Emperor's Death Star...
