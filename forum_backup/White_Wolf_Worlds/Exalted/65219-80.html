                                                    I've always been a fantasy of pourquoi folklore. Allegorical tales about such things as "why do bears sleep in winter?" or "Why are the constellations in these shapes?". Also included in the genre are myths of the creation of the world, or the founding of nations or peoples. The Tower of Babel is one of the better known examples in real life. Given that Creation has very active supernatural forces, I imagine that these sorts of stories would be even more prominent , if that is even possible, than they are in the real world. Not myths and legends formally taught by priesthoods and cult leaders, but stories a peasant farmer tells their children. Or a wetnurse for a dynasty might entertain their young charge with. Maybe some are even true? Or were planted by gods looking to increase their stature? Or weren't true, but the gods and heroes featured proved popular enough that the divinities took notice and changed themselves to better reflect them? Or some may have been changed from prior forms, such as the Immaculate Dragons being subbed in for the original folk hero? Here are some of my offerings:

                                                    -------

                                                    This tale is told among tribes living in the Far East of Creation.

                                                    Long ago, when the world was young, the soul-eaters were able to slip freely into the world. Sometimes one would appear as a great stag and eat the dreams of hunters wishing for glory. Sometimes one would take the form of a handsome youth and devour the dreams of those who lusted for them. Other times they would shape themselves into a storm or a blizzard and consume dreams of warmth and safety. Those they ate of would be nothing but bones or a grey shell of life. Powerless were the people against these monsters, for even heroes would have their dreams of courage eaten. The people called to Luna, whose light the soul-eaters could not stand. Luna looked down in pity and resolved to enact a great hunt. In their Hall, they called all the beasts and creatures of the world.

                                                    "I will go and hunt the things beyond the world! The soul-stealers and the dream-eaters. Who will come and join me?"

                                                    Bear shook it's head. "My cubs need me to show them the secret places to sleep through the cold. I cannot hunt." Hawk raised it's beak. "I do not care for those humans on the ground. And I cannot spread my wings where the hunt will take you. I cannot go." Wolf whimpered, "My pack's fangs will grow dull biting the endless monsters. We need them for our own. We cannot go."

                                                    One by one, each beast and creatures gave their excuses and left. Soon, only a butterfly remained, weak and frail. "I do not know what did my clan can give", said she, "But we will fly with you on your hunt Great Luna." Luna grinned, donning the mask of the Huntress and blessed the butterfly and her kin. "I name you now, Actia. Moonlight Butterfly. Luna-Moth. You will be my worthy shield-maid." By Luna's blessing, Actia and her kin grew wings the colour of moonlight and became mighty against the monsters beyond the world. They flew in Luna's wake as she went on her bloody hunt. Any monster who thought to creep around or behind the Moon was riven by the Spears of their Tongues or made dust by the dust off their wings. And the greater monsters beyond the world could no longer pass into Creation as long as the People remain true.

                                                    All beasts remember their shame and so none hunt the Luna-Moths. Any that sin so are struck by madness and are soon dead. The dust off their wings is used by the wise in powerful magic that Ward against the Wyld-things. Moonlight Butterflies flock to Luna's wake when they walk the world. And Actia is honoured still as the bearer of Luna's Shield.

                                                        In the South, when all you see is desert and the dunes rise high, walk carefully. When you see tiny crackles of lightning building up at a distance, be wary. If you see a cresting of sand, the way a large fish just below the surface disturbs the water, walk away. Find whatever rocky area is nearby and pray you survive.

                                                        These are the heralds of the Grandfather of the Desert; Old Man Eternity; in the original Flametongue, Shai-Hulud. Some languages have seen Shai-Hulud and give him the name 'sandworm'; using this or its bastardised Flametongue rendering is bound to earn you dirty looks from those Southerners who know of him. Disrespect him at your peril, for he can sense the rhythm of your steps, and most outsiders to the deep desert are unfamiliar with the odd, shifting gait that natives adopt, so as not to attract his attention. Shai-Hulud does not often take notice of individual travelers, but if he decides to make a meal of you, pray that rock is nearby...and that Shai-Hulud is not particularly inclined to churn up your plateau.

                                                        Shai-Hulud is massive; those smaller examples that range furthest north are still tens of yards long, and those travelers from the deepest deserts say that he is often much longer. His maw is large, even the smallest capable of swallowing camels whole. They say that he leaves blessings in the sand, that if sifted through, yields a most wondrous spice. Whether this is true or not is yet to be determined; some of the more enterprising Southern traders do sell what they call 'melange', and it makes for a quite exotic addition to food. As for if it lives up to its claim of prolonging a lifespan or sharpening the mind or enlightening the Essence...none can say.


                                                        Yes, I put sandworms in the South. The very first game I ever played was Dune 2; the thought of wormsign still sends a reactive tension through me; and thus, given the deserts of the South to play with...why not Shai-Hulud?
