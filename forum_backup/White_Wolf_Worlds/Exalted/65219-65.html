            While I can appreciate the symmetry of the Blessed Isle being a reflection of the Celestial City, and casting its shadow into the Underworld as the Stygian Isle, I never really liked how Yu-Shan is presented, so...

            Yu-Shan is a massive hollow sphere of pearlescent Adamant. There is no land mass within; instead there are enormous masses of solid clouds drifting about. The five largest clouds support the five Bureaus, while many of the smaller ones bristle with the towers, roads, plazas, bridges, and other dwellings used by the godly inhabitants. Mortals who have seen this marvel and returned to Earth to tell of it sing hymns to The Five Heavens, which are also often sworn by in casual conversation.

            As in canon, a network of canals contains the Quicksilver Highway enabling high-speed transport throughout Yu-Shan. However, it also courses beyond each cloud, through the air itself with no visible means of support, a silver-and-gold ribbon that serves to bind the cloud masses together. Some of the smaller clouds support private gardens, parks and Celestial Manses, some of the most prized of which are not bound to the Quicksilver Highway and float freely around the rest.

            There is a gigantic tree at the center of the B. of Nature's forested cloud, about which an avatar of the Dragon of Wood himself can sometimes be seen coiled about, and where Gaia's Jouten sometimes can be found. This is the Tree of Worlds, and it is metaphysically one with the World Tree at the Elemental Pole of Wood in the Far East, and it's roots even reach down into the Labyrinth of the Underworld itself. One of the secret entrances to Yu-Shan is here, and it is the source of the rare Peaches of Immortality that grow once per century (see previous headcanon), along with other rare and powerful magical bounty.

            The shell of Heaven is translucent, and the keenest eyed of the gods can barely glimpse the maddening impossibilities of the Beyond Without, although most only do so once in their immortal lives. They prefer to focus on the gigantic murals etched into the diamond shell, illustrating the constellations making up the Houses of the Maidens. When one of them is ahead in the Games of Divinity, those figures shine brightly in their signature color, casting their light and fortunes across the heavenly clouds...

                    In ancient days, the primordials built the world, and filled it with life. but their failure to understand or care about that life led to their overthrow, and at the moment of their death, some of them cursed their murderers to failure. then the mighty sought to build their own, better world, but they failed, not because of the curse but because of their own assumptions. they built a world in which only the exalted mattered, they tried to distance themselves from the very people they were supposed to rule, to declare that the very exaltation itself made them qualified to rule the world, to build a better one. the exaltation bonds to vision, to strong moral stances and goals, and gives the power to potentially achieve that goal. It was designed to find people who would wage war against the invincible creators of the world, and overthrow them. It does not select for the wisdom needed to make a better world, or the empathy to recognize that all men and women have importance, have value. Then they went further, and declared that the type of exaltation made one better than the other, that someone who is exalted by birth is less than one exalted by deed, even if the child of dragons might have been exalted by deed thrice over had they not been born with the the blood of the dragons. instead, 300 men were placed above all others because of a meeting of deed and chance. Their will was declared greater than all others, regardless of what that will was directed towards. And men who, through poor luck or one moment of failure, did not gain the power of the gods, were declared lesser, with no regard to what they might bring to the future. And it fell into despair, and the world was nearly destroyed in the attempt to save it. The strongest of the chosen were locked away, others cast to the edges of the world. If a better world is to be created, we must learn from the past. A world in which chance and power determine leadership can never create an ideal society. Sometimes, the strongest among us are also the wisest and kindest, but that is not guaranteed, and any society based on that idea will fall. Exaltation is not a declaration of character or wisdom or moral standing; it is a declaration size, of being larger than life. It is a sign of greatness, not greatness of character, not the greatness that creates a better world, but the greatness to change the world, for better or for worse.

                        I have quite a lot to catch up on, but...

                        Celestial Exalted - The numbers of existing Celestial exaltations has never been completely known. 300 , 300 , and 100 is probably grossly under-reported. The Exalted themselves could never get an accurate census, because many of them were displaced well beyond Destiny - some by the actions of the enemies of Creation, some by misadventure, some by going Faraway or into hiding, and some by exertion of their own will.

                        A truth that has been deeply suppressed is that powerful Exalted may also create Exigents. The Exalted of the First Age insisted that this was sheer impossibility.

                        Miracles of the Chosen - Another deeply suppressed truth is that the Celestials of the First Age fell to envy and sabotage much earlier than anybody realized. The great workings and discoveries of the Age were often made at the expense of the "others" as the Chosen grew paranoid and scheming. Nothing intrinsically limits any Exalted from the highest circles of Sorcery or the highest blossom of Martial Arts, except the Workings of the other Exalted who made it so. Many Exalted were betrayed and killed to cover all of this up, well before the Usurpation was even an idea.

                        The Enemy - At least one of the Deathlords was a Lunar, and hidden signs point to at least one existing who was a Sidereal. The formerly Lunar is in disguise as "simply" a Behemoth, hiding by rampaging in plain sight, yet always working toward it's hidden goals. The formerly Sidereal, if he can be said to exist at all, has escaped even the gaze - and memory - of his Neverborn master.

                        One of the Primordials allowed itself to be defeated, but in so doing exchanged Fate and Destiny with the Exalted who struck the final blow. Both the Exaltation and the now-Neverborn have memories of this swap. The imprisoned Exalt refuses to give up, lest doing so set the Primordial free, but constantly tries to send messages asking for help. The Primordial has lived as so many Exalted now, that it believes itself to actually be Exalted, but once subject to a mental influence in a past life, leaving bad memories and nightmares. A Bureau in Yu-Shan once existed to keep the two of them separated, but it fell into neglect after the Usurpation.

                        Yu Shan - Yu Shan itself was the greatest Unshaped ever believed to have existed, and in its annoyance at its rivals, embraced the idea of Creation with a mad glee. It increased its own power by magnitudes simply by basking in the hatred of the other Unshaped, and again by emanating the Loom of Fate to become part of every single story within the Creation project. Even today, it weaves itself through every drama and conflict and betrayal taking place in its heavenly body. It weaves stories and drama through the Games of Divinity, entertaining itself and the Celestines - who know exactly what it is. The Exalted of the Primordial War had to sever the Underworld and drop it beyond Destiny, and do the same with Malfeas, because otherwise Yu Shan would have created twists of fate to keep the war going eternally for its own amusement.

                            In my headcannon:

                            The Green Sun Princes were created by the Yozis as a response to the acceptance of a cold hard truth, they were NEVER going to break free from their prison. The Infernal Exalted are the Yozis final middle finger to all of Creation and The gods. The Yozis cannot and do not control or direct their exalted champions. They give them aid and support in their goals of spreading chaos, madness, and suffering across Creation. If the infernals do manage to find a way to break their primordial patrons out of their prison bindings, so much the better.

                            Autochthonia plays a very significant role and the Octet nations develop municipal charms that let the poli expand upward and outward across Creation's surface.

                            The Immaculate Styles are quite common among dragonblooded in the threshhold. Lookshy teaches the Immaculate styles as do many other dragonblooded dojos across Creation. Immaculate Monks do not take their vows for life but instead for twenty five year periods. The majority of dynasts spend at least a portion of their lives in the monastery in their lives either before or between marriages (dragonblooded marriages often end in divorce as living with the same person for a century give the exalts a desire for something, and someone, new). Blood purity is not a thing, the breeding background is completely random and a five generations removed lost egg could easily have equal or greater breeding dots as a dynast who can trace her pureblooded linage all the way back the the Shogunate.

                            Mortals who devote their lives to a particular spirit finds their soul bound to that spirit. When the mortal dies and the god chooses to claim their soul the dead mortal will awaken as a spirit in their patron god's sanctum or celestial abode and shall be bound into the deity's service for eternity. This practice is discouraged by the Immaculate Faith. In the First Age the gods required permission from the Solar deliberative before allowing a god to claim a soul.

                            The Solars of the First Age were a representative Republic. Every twenty five years the solars would gather in Meru and choose which circle would take the mantle of chief executives of The Realm. The elected circle assume the titles of Supreme Commander, Holy Hierophant, Arch Magus, Grand Inquisitor, and Prime Minister respectively. The solars would then choose to either stay in Meru and vote in the Deliberative or depart the Exalted City and leave their proxy vote with one of their solar peers or an exalted representative.

                                A lesser known variety of flame weapon, after the firewand and flamepiece, is the humble torchpipe. You make one by taking a small metal tube, filling it with firedust, capping one end of it with thin cloth or paper, and capping the other end with a carefully-shaped piece of flint. When you push on the flint, it sparks against the inside of the tube and ignites the dust, which will (usually) spill out the front end, burning through the cover. Reloading these weapons is frequently impossible, because the tiny flint can break or wear down after only one or two shots. Aiming from further away than-point blank is partly down to luck. If you're not wearing gloves, the pipe will likely singe your hands. Also, if the flint-cap isn't set properly, you may well incinerate your own arm. Despite these drawbacks, torchpipes are popular weapons in the criminal South, partly because of their power, partly because they are easy to conceal, and partly because (unlike a proper flamepiece) a torchpipe can be improvised from scavenged materials.

                                        Headcanons regarding firedust:

                                        When it burns, it explodes with a modest amount of concussive force, which is why most of the dust gets blown out of a firewand's barrel as it ignites. This is not enough force to drive any projectile, however - you will note that dust-blooms do not travel far (Short range!), even though firedust grains are very light. That said, it's still enough force to generate firey Hollywood-style explosions if a lot of dust is involved, or blast small holes in things if the flamepiece or firewand is at point-blank range. And you absolutely should avoid stuffing anything solid inside a fire weapon before firing it, or it may burst apart.

                                        Firedust smells kind of like charcoal and sulfur. That smell gets a lot stronger when it is burned.

                                        Firedust does not cling like napalm. But it burns so hot and so quickly that there's no real hope of scraping it off before 2nd or 3rd-degree burns set in. Secondary fires from burning hair or clothing are almost inevitable.

                                        It's actually quite difficult to kill someone outright with any fire weapon. The nature of firedust is such that most of the damage is confined to the skin (epidermis, dermis, and hypodermis), and crippling injuries from burned flesh are common. Actual death may result suffocation from secondary fires consuming all oxygen or, more rarely, a body can ignite due to the Wick Effect - ( https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wick_effect ). But most often what will happen is that someone will become temporarily disabled or pass out from pain, making them easy to finish off by other means. Or, if they do not recieve adequate treatment for their burns, they can easily die from infection. Protip: in Creation, adequate treatment is rare and infection is common.
