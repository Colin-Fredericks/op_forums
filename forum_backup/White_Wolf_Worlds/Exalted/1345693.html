    Alchemicals vs Solars and other Creation-exalts

    11-03-2019, 11:24 PM
    So its something I saw on the 'Ask the Unconquered Sun' thread.

    Autobot asked if he could go to Creation and asked to watch out for the alchemical exalted.

    Sol asked why he would worry about the alchemical exalted, since each exalted cost vast amounts of resources, could only use a set number of charms at a time, and took a large amount of time to replace.

    So here's the thing. I kinda agree. And yet, it irks me. I love solars, don't get me wrong. But Alchemicals are incredibly fascinating, both in the fact that they're far more dependent on human intervention than other exalts, their ability to switch out charms, the magitech and steampunk aesthetics, as well as their focus, not on ruling humans, but facilitating them.

    So on this comparism of Alchemicals vs Solars... what advantages do Alchemicals have?

              Personally, I tend to view the "load-out" mechanic as more of a disadvantage than an advantage. I mean, sure they can swap their Charms out so one day they can be loaded out with social Charms and the next day they can be loaded out with combat Charms, but a Solar doesn't have to worry about that. After they spend exp to learn a Charm they just always have access to that Charm. They're loaded out with their combat Charms and social Charms and craft Charms and every other Charm they've learned every single day of the week. Meanwhile (at least in 2nd edition) the Alchemical spends slightly less exp per Charm, but if he doesn't have those Charms equipped than he simply doesn't have access to them at all.

              This also came with the drawback that Alchemicals needed to spend exp on "slots" in order to equip the Charms they spent exp on, and then needed to commit Essence on top of that to actually equip the Charms, which functionally put two caps on how many Charms they had access to (I remember Holden mentioning he wanted to change or get rid of one of both of those mechanics in 3rd edition when he got around to dealing with Alchemicals, but I don't know what the current writers and dev have planned.) And, of course, there's the issue that it's very difficult to swap Charms out on the fly - if you want to change your loadout it generally requires you to go back to a base of operations with a vat complex, which could take days or weeks or might not even be possible at all (if you find yourself in the Far Reaches of Autochthon, in Creation, or somewhere else).

              I'm not saying is a bad mechanic, I think it's actually pretty cool and it makes the Alchemical play experience very different from a Solars, but overall it's one that I definitely consider to be much more disadvantageous than advantageous.

              They also turn into giant living warstriders and eventually cities, which is really fucking cool, but I don't know if I would necessarily consider that an 'advantage' over a Solar (who has a much easier time operating on a normal human scale at high Essence).

              Ultimately I think Alchemicals are really fucking awesome, but I don't know that they necessarily have many mechanical advantages over Solars. That's okay though, they don't necessarily need mechanical advantages over Solars. Their "advantages" are more a matter of roleplaying (similar to Dragon Blooded) and generally rests more in their place in society. They're respected heroes who are cheered on by adoring crowds whereas Solars are generally hunted and forced into hiding. They also have more easy access to artifacts and whatnot.

                  In general, you won't tend to find any exalt with a real kind of advantage over Solars. They're the most powerful, it's in the description.

                  A Dragonblooded general can summon a thunderstorm, call down lightning, and raise the earth into fortifications in an instant, but they're not actually better than a Solar general for it. Lunar shapeshifting is probably the closest you'll ever get, and it's only because it gives Lunars a really wide variety of base abilities that they can really easily get without focusing.

                  If you just want to win a fight, cure a disease, convince somebody of something, the Solar who's good at that thing will do it better than anyone else. On the other hand Solars can't become living cities with badass steampunk avatars walking around either, or casually engulf an entire battlefield in fire, or shapeshift into a hundred different animals. You'll notice that the list of things they simply can't do though has nothing to do with the result, just the way they do it.

                  There's a lot of really fun and super cool ways of doing things that Solars will never get to do, but they'll never be better than doing it the Solar way.

                      I mean, a good thing I think is when Alchemicals actually have forewarning on what they're up against. You get a bunch of alchemicals, all kitted out for social, and you drop invincible sword princess on them? Dead alchemicals.

                      You get a dozen alchemicals, kit them out with combat charms, and throw them at invincible sword princess? You've got stuff working out for you. And because you can make more alchemicals (while solars are capped at 150/300), eventually, a solar gets mobbed.

                      Things also come out for rulers, too. A ruler may distrust a solar. After all, their charms are all self-contained, solars may eventually learn charms to mind control and whip up the masses, and basically may supplant the ruler. No such thing for alchemicals. I mean, they may be dangerous, they may be powerful, but they're capped by the fact that they need mortal industry. A ruler, forced to choose between a solar/ dragonblooded and an alchemical, may simply opt to have the alchemical and ply them with a vat complex and various artifacts/ charms and resources to improve them, reasoning that an expensive and hard to maintain vat complex and alchemical charms is a better and more reliable use of their resources than a solar or dragonblooded that may take the money and turn against them anyway.

                                          I think the main distinction was supposed to come from how it's more expensive for a Solar to diversify to that degree. And not just in terms of the costs of their Charms, which was higher than those of Alchemicals; a Solar that wants to go deep into any of those cascades is going to run into needing a maximized Ability very quickly.

                                          Alchemicals technically need high Attributes as well sure, but a thing that I feel that people often forget was how they got an Augmentation Charm that could increase their Attributes. Alchemicals weren't just about changing loadouts for their Charms, but for their Attributes as well to ensure that they had the minimums for those Charms. It wasn't unlimited, but I think that on the whole it would work out as less expensive for the Alchemical than buying the Attribute dots wholesale (at the cost of taking up some Charm slots), the Attributes also not being too expensive to buy.

                                          Between that and the fact that there are fewer Attributes than Abilities, in practical terms I think it was generally supposed to be easier and more expected for an Alchemical to have a more diverse set of options than a typical Solar would have, just constrained within the terms of not being able to do all of it at once. I expect an Alchemical to switch between a high level of capability in combat, social and creative endeavours than I do a Solar.

                                          That being said, sure I wouldn't regard this as an advantage as such, it's just an element of characterisation. You express a lot of a Solar through their Abilities, and even a Lunar through their Attributes, but for an Alchemical the minimums they need for Charms aren't really a big deal and aren't the primary way of depicting them; this would be why their Charms aren't even organised by Attribute cascades. Alchemicals are expressed through their context and function, and their personal decisions in how they pursue these things.

                                                          Hypothetical design idea: What if every Alchemical Charm in the book were always completely available to every Alchemical? Charms then become the product of state industry rather than things individually attuned to particular Alchemicals (and thus no longer an XP expense for characters). Now, what they use is a matter of personal preference, planning, available Charm slots, and Essence and Attribute ratings.

                                                          If that wouldn't produce enough mechanical differentiation from other Alchemicals, you could also flag certain Charms with tags like Moonsilver or Jade, making Essence rating requirements for Charms that particularly resonate with certain magical materials more easily available for some members of an Assembly than others, sort of like a Supernal function for Alchemicals. Then the Soulsteel Caste could more easily pick up Personality Override Spike while the Moonsilver Caste gets first access to Magnetic Joint Bearings.

                                                          Also, mobile vat complexes need to be a thing, either something that can either be moved about with vehicles and powered with elemental cores - probably requiring days of assembly to be functional and the same to be able to be packed for moving - or else a Charm that would let Collossus Alchemicals service their Champion kin. Otherwise, the character refit logistics of wars between the Eight Nations become ridiculous for PCs, who would end up trapped for extended periods without ability to swap around their Charm load outs.
