    Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz
    Well I suppose the issue is that an Imperialist with a caste-based religion is a lot more complex and nuanced than a champion of "let's kill everything" in black armour, so allows multiple interpretations and sympathetic traits. Essentially, the Abyssals need to be linked by a more complex philosophy/milieu, to give them multiple motivations.
    Yes that's certainly the precise thing that I said.
    Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz
    "he wants to kill everyone to stop their suffering", but it never felt very believable
    I can see Abyssals and the Underworld including a thing of life being suffering when compared to the experience of Oblivion or even just the power and luxury that can be afforded to ghosts. To actually kill people for this is something I see as crude. Honestly, I could do without revising Abyssals in terms of some overall purpose regardless of what it might be, some dubious and convoluted argument in favour of active destruction; I'm more interested in matters of perspective and experience, closer to the Solars. To whit:
    Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz
    he absolutely had to be all about ending the world
    I actually would like Abyssals to still be a thesis on doom for the world, just without any need for mass murderers or Earth-shattering kabooms or anything so simplistic and ornery. To have it feel like something that might organically emerge from the powers or attitudes of them in terms that are subtle enough to warrent immediate response or indicative of a cartoonishly villainous character. To not be generic doomsday villains but heralds of the grave. And also just a bit of obtuseness and projection on the part of the Deathlords (between several things that John has said, I like this image of the Deathlords as figures who had their lives taken and so despise the living, and erroneously assume that if somebody loses their life and surrenders their name and destiny in order to become a herald of the grave, they must hate the living as well, combined with a bit of self-loathing and appropriation in the idea of "of course Solars would bring about the end of the world"). Things that can feel like distinguishing aspects of the Abyssal without feeling like manacles for character concepts or actions.

    I have approximate knowledge of many things.

            Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
            Do people use signature characters much?
            I certainly have. I've used quite a few of the NPC's in the gameline over the years. All of the Dragon Blooded signature characters (Mnemon, Tepet Arada, the Roseblack, etc) have all played huge roles in my campaigns. Signature characters from other splat books might crop up depending on where the PC's are and the needs of a particular story.

            I'm not shy about including pre-generated NPCs in the games, especially if the players find some of them to be kind of cool. My players would read the Aspect or Caste books, and then I'd include some of those NPC's in my games, simply because the players already have a good understand of many things about those NPCs (that characters history, desires, goals, regrets, etc) so that character comes off as more rounded and memorable in our games than if I had introduced an entirely new character (which I certainly do, but sometimes it's nice to drop one of those pre-made characters as a cameo or such).

            Ultimately I view the signature characters as no different from any other NPC - they just happen to have a bit more spotlight in the artwork than the rest.

                                        The reason I asked, was that I wondered how important it is for the characters on the front of the books to have well-rounded backgrounds, rather than just look inspiring.

                                        It came out of a conversa​tion I had with one of my players a while ago: I said I didn't like the characters on the front of the Core because they're too stereotypical and their backgrounds are rather dull and clichéd. There were more interesting (and, frankly, likeable) Solars in the Caste books they could have used instead. It also encourages people to make very stereotypical characters.
                                        He argued that's good: their purpose isn't actually to be an interesting character, it's to give new players (whether that be new players of the game, or new players of that splat) an immediate idea of what Solars are and what each caste represents. You look at Dace, and you know that a Dawn is some kind of fighter. You look at Harmonious Jade sneaking and jumping around in the comics, and you know that Nights are good at Athletics and Stealth.

                                        This is why I was asking about the sig characters themselves (as in the ones on the front of the books), rather than canon NPCs generally. Do people actually use them in their games, or not. Do they need to have good and interesting backgrounds, or just look inspiring.

                                                            Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                                                            Do people use signature characters much?
                                                            I see the Maiden of the Mirthless Smile feature constantly, but that's pretty much it. Oh! Manosque Cyan got a huge facelift in a high-power game I'm in and is basically De Facto Queen of Infernals What Care About Creation (they have a more or less de jure king, who is the first Infernal, but he basically only cares about Malfeas any more). Never seen sigs as good guys, ever.

                                                            Honestly, you can't really take loyalist abyssals very seriously so long as you adhere to the following assumptions (as laid out in the books):

                                                            1. Abyssal endgame is blowing up the space station after blowing up the escape pods, followed by the sun
                                                            2. They all know this
                                                            3. They're all on board

                                                            Just changing 2 and 3 just gets us the Loyal Infernal Problem (where a loyalist is just someone who hasn't been screwed over enough yet), so, I figure it's on us to make a better endgame. There are people in the real world who are pretty stoked about the idea of the world ending, but there are a lot of assumptions that go along with it (like "get a better world in its place" and "everyone I don't like will finally get theirs"). SO.

                                                            Idea 1. Broken Windows, Cosmic-Tier

                                                            People will cling to a corrupt system to have any sort of system at all. Start tearing it down and you'll be amazed at what solutions they'll come up with!
                                                            Oblivion exists as written. Creation is a shithole. Abyssals and Deathlords are privy to some First Age secrets and the existence of their kin Exalted Solars and Infernals, who are both possessed of the ability to create worlds. Rather like a surge in interest in coastal engineering when the storms start getting more intense, serious threats to the stability (and continued existence) of Creation are liable to start the Powers That Be looking into that much more seriously. Heck, a sufficient amount of access fae shenanigans (and the connections between faerie and Guild) means that even mortals could make it happen. A meschlum-esque Road Into Forever sounds crazy when the world isn't under threat or when the worst threat anyone faces is just another conqueror.

                                                            They're deathknights. Making things better by providing the best opposition is what they do, right?

                                                            Idea 2. I'm a Final Fantasy villain. Good thing it means I get such nice hair.

                                                            Oblivion's whispers drive ghosts mad. That's pretty much a given. People have been trying to interpret the ramblings of nephwracks for ages without much success. However, none of them were necromantic geniuses who had the benefit of actually being alive, capable of holding the raw data-stuff of Oblivion in their minds (ghosts are excessively self-referential, and will get stuck in recursions, becoming trapped in an endless programming loop; "going mad" is just a side effect). Abyssals, when they try, actually get real patterns and programming instructions (that manifest themselves as superpowers or the ability to turn into a swarm of bats or kung fu, but what are you going to do). Oblivion isn't so much alive as it is the precursor to something that intends to 'be.' A human would probably classify it as a primordial but it is to a Primordial what a Primordial is to a human.

                                                            If allowed to escape/manifest/ascend/be born it will consume the remnants of its former kin. It will consume universes (the infinite worlds of the Yozi) and herald a new era of darkness - but one in which it's liable to be pretty damn sweet to be its avatar. The influence that it has on the world defies description; cults pop up seemingly out of nowhere. It has prophets and actual, real prophecy due to living 'outside' the normal flow of being. It is the closest thing that the Exalted universe has or will have to a proper God. (Too bad that having been suckled on the suffering of living universes and born by eating them, it's probably evil.)

                                                            (Obligatory HE IS ALREADY HERE)

                                                            Meanwhile they also need to help the Infernals hellscape enough of Creation that the entrance to Malfeas is "wide" enough for a newborn void-god to get in and eat the Yozis, too. (The Yozis, meanthile, support the Abyssals because they think some prominent Abyssal successes will let them offer the gods a devil's bargain - put us back in charge and we'll stop the destruction of everything; refuse and you and everything you care about will get oblivion'd.)

                                                            Idea 3. Sometimes you gotta tell the world "get out of the way," this time it was a little more literal

                                                            Similar to 2, except that inside the black hole is an infinite universe compressed into a single point through denial of the concept of "distance." (Like a protoshinmaic vortex, but times a billion.) The Abyssals' powers are derived not from death but from entropy; dust, death, decay, degradation (and free cosmic power to the first one that figures it out). It could unpack into a brand new universe, except that Creation is quite literally, quite physically and metaphysically "in the way." Like a dam, they need to crack it enough to get it to bust, but the old thing is remarkably well-built! Through their Fetters, the Neverborn are some of the most effective tethers that it has, whether they know it or not. They want to join with the new universe, but what they really need to do is get flung off into the Wyld somewhere so they'll stop keeping Creation intact so well!

                                                            What happens when the point is allowed to expand? Well, they might call that a "big bang?" At which point the Abyssals emerge from their perfect defenses and step out to become gods of their new world.
