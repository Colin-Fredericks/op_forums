    Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post
    Yeah, I may be underselling the raw powah of some SF characters here (particularly their "stuff I can do only in cutscenes and OVA!") and also there could be other matchups in that analogic frame that would better illustrate the point. General point being that some crossovers do things to the worlds being crossed over to kind of "balance" them, while ExVWoD really doesn't - it's very much "The Exalted against the entire World of Darkness" and not "Exalted meets World of Darkness".

    (The latter being a thing which Holden could've written but I guess didn't because 1) he didn't want to and 2) the sheer obscurity of rewriting the mechanics and universe to affect tweaks rather than just plonking the Exalted into the WoD - as an Exalted fan I am of course into writers deciding to write *extremely* obscure and niche stuff because its an idea that must be written, goddammit, but that definitely means a lot of uncompensated labour among writers that absolutely no one is entitled to.)
    The "only in cutscene" clause probably wouldn't hold if the characters made an appearance in a different play genre - say, a large scale brawler a la Dynasty Warriors instead of in a fighter with a mostly static 2.5D background. Then cities would know the unbridled fury of the Satsui no Hadou!

    Not just slam-porting the Exalted of old in is likely because of a similar issue, along with issues that crept up during the presentation of higher-Essence play - the fight would be really one-sided. During the height of 2e, Solars, Abyssals, and Infernals could double-break their Essence cap and acquire Charms that devastated an entire countryside in one attack. Sidereals have long been known to possess the broken Creation-Slaying Oblivion Kick thanks to Charcoal March of Spiders. Lunars can turn into giant, city-destroying kaijuu... though, in a hint of terrible irony, Mokole can as well, and are arguably more capable of doing so because that's a potential chargen outcome, not something that they have to wait centuries to acquire. In a one on one fight, most of these broad-scope attacks aren't more terrible than the single-hit attacks that a power made flesh could inflict, so the tight focus akin to a fighting game wouldn't necessarily change, other than for turning into a giant monster. That tends to change the fight into a whole stage or series of stages.

                    Incoming Beans Textwall, spoilered for courtesy. EXWOD does some things I like and a lot of things I wildly do not like.
                    On one hand, Holden is trying to patch up some of the more abrasive parts of the OWOD rules in EXWOD; Exalts' areas of expertise ignore the "1s subtract successes" rule, the Ability list is meant to be unified rather than variable, and combat uses the DAV20 initiative system instead of the more finnicky standard one. Also, boiling down Charms has some nice effects, and in some ways it's got shades of an easier Exalted system. The translation of Caste/Aspect Abilities goes nicely in some places, like giving Firearms to Air and Journeys, or Finance to Wood.

                    On the other hand, do you like OWOD? Do you like the OWOD engine, and the OWOD combat system? You'd better, because you're still stuck with it, Holden's bandaids aside. Meanwhile, while EX3 is in some ways shaving down some of the power-gaps, EXWOD exacerbates them. For instance, a standard starting Solar at Essence Rating 1 has a total mote pool of 10. A starting Dragon-Blood at Essence Rating 1 has a total mote pool of 5. The highest a Dragon-Blood's mote pool can ever go is 10. This is irksome to me, as are about a thousand other little things, like kind of clumsy writing that makes it hard for me to understand very important details of things. Then there's also the fact that, given the lack of a social system in OWOD, most social charms are either "your roll is gooder" or "MIND CONTROL LASER". Then there's Infernals, where he seemingly tries to balance out overtuned charms with undertuned Excellency coverage and just makes two wrongs instead of a right. Annnnnd there's the way Alchemicals share the lowest starting Attribute dots with Dragon-Bloods (which itself is a thing we left behind in 3e), which is pretty unpleasant given they're Attribute-based Exalts. Also, remember those Caste/Aspect Ability spreads? Well, sometimes they mismatch badly with Charms. Eclipses and Moonshadows get some vehicular charms, including one to summon a magic car/motorcycle/whatever, but they don't get Drive while Dawns and Dusks do, which strikes me as a bit odd.

                    My TL;DR for this is essentially... EXWOD deserves credit for making me and probably like five other people want to convert it to something else, and having some legitimately good concepts in it. It then, in my opinion, burns that credit as the initial "oh, that's neat" gives way to the disappointment of finding the whole thing is covered with a host of jammy hamster pawprints. If you love OWOD, you're probably fine. If the OWOD engine irks you, grab the PDFs to loot for ideas for some other thing, they're free.

                    Oh, and as much as the referential header fonts are clever, I also dislike them.

                                                        I've played it. It's very nice, but I'd like some more stuff to it--I'd really like full Alchemicals, and I think they need to be empowered a bit. (but I'm an alchemical fanboy).

                                                        It's important to note that while you can exalt as a Kinfolk or Ghoul, the default assumption for the setting is that you exalt with no idea of what is going on. This is a big deal, because it's going to inform a huge part of your game. The assumption for most settings is that say,
                                                        Catch Vamp snacking on boyfriend.
                                                        INSTANT SOLAR POWER!
                                                        Totally murderize vamp, his friends and likely anyone else that looks vampirish...
                                                        Realize that you have no idea what is going on, and every vamp in the city is now hunting you.

                                                        Which is fun, But...

                                                        Okay, one thing I really would have liked would have been at least a few suggestions for... Six months later, a year later, etc. What happens when you start learning about the WOD? When the Abyssal has had time to sit down with some ghosts and chat about the mages and vampires and werewolves, oh my?

                                                        I mean, I can see why not, given that part of the fun of Exalted is that there isn't so much a metaplot as "what am I pointing my howitzer at today!" But it would have been interesting, especially suggestions for higher power exalts.

                                                        For myself, I'm getting ready to put together a one year later campaign set on the "Free City of Detroit" where no less than twenty of the Chosen have established their domain. It looks to be interesting...

                                                        One thing I will say, is that I like it, because it's not as complex as 3E and I'm seriously considering, at least until Exalted Essence comes out, just using it for any Exalted games period.
