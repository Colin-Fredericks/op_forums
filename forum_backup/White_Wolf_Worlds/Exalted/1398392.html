    Is Usurpation the right term still in 3e?

    06-23-2020, 02:41 PM
    Disclaimer: This is designed to promote a discussion and any assertions made are just my opinion not objective reality.

    One of the more interesting (to me) lore bits that was dropped by the devs have been changes in presentation of the First Age. In particular, the idea that there was not a unified state as depicted in previous editions.

    In particular it is no longer the Solar Deliberative (which was ruled by solars through what amounted to Divine Right over all of Creation) but instead the Second Deliberative (where Solars has outsized influence but no longer ruled through divine right).

    The other bit is that even the Second Deliberative did not rule over creation as a unified state (central, federal, or even a confederate model of governance). This implies that there was significant land either ungoverned by the second deliberative or rival polities.


    This leads to an interesting question: is the term “the Usurpation” even still the right term? A portion (To me) of the previous thematics in Exalted had been that there was a cyclic nature to Creation’s history, with the rulers of each age finding their rule usurped as they grew abusive or corrupt with their power. In previous editions, the term usurpation (which has the implication of actively taking power from the previous regime illegitimately, but retaining a semblance of the status quo regime) followed the form and function of the events that had transpired.

    My question is: is the term Usurpation still the right term for what transpired? Revolution would work (as the Second Deliberative was replaced with a new status quo). It feels like Usurpation is an artifact (heh) of a bygone era that that no longer fits with the history of Creation as presented.

              It has never made sense in any edition for Realm scholars to call it "the Usurpation." It's most likely described as "the fall of the Anathema," "the purge of the Anathema," or the like; we certainly see such naming in such material as the 1e Aspect books.

              Despite the changes in 3e, it's still reasonable to speak of it as an usurpation. The Dragon-Blooded may have had more of a role in the Second Deliberative than in its 1e/2e equivalents, but the end result was the same: With the Solars dead, the Lunars driven out, and the Sidereals withdrawn to Heaven, power that once resided in other hands was consolidated — indeed, usurped — by the Dragon-Blooded alone. And if the term is used primarily by the Exalted whose power was taken in this way? From their perspective, their power was usurped.

              (There is ultimately a certain amount of legacy text here that might have been changed if the core book developers had been so inclined! But the fact that certain things could have been changed does not mean that keeping them the same was necessarily wrong.)
