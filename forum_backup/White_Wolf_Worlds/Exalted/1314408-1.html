        I tend to look at Wuxia/Xianxia Novels for inspiration such as Emperor's Domination, King of Gods, Invincible/Invincible Conqueror, Dragon Marked War God, Martial World/True Martial World, etc...

        Curoch, Iures, and the Ka'Kari from the Night Angel Trilogy by Brent Weeks
        Holdrun Cube, Kanai's Cube, Sword of Justice, Scroll of Fate, Black Soulstone, Spear of Valor, etc... from Blizzard's Diablo
        Chain Veil, Immortal Sun, Blackblade, etc... from Magic the Gathering
        The Dragon Amulet of the Spirit Monks from Bioshock's Jade Empire
        Vault Hunter abilities, most notably the Sirens for different Evocations, from Gearbox Software's Borderlands
        The Three Heavenly Treasures from Martial World/True Martial World (Magic Cube, Grandmist Bead, and Purple Card)
        Ring of Lucis, Royal Arms, and Armiger from Final Fantasy XV
        The Masks from Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask (Deku, Goron, Zora, Giant, etc...)
        Create a Daiklaive based on the Forgotten Realms Moonblade that can only be wielded by a Specific Exalt and his subsequent reincarnations with each Incarnation that wielded it bestowing a Power
        Pieces of Eden from Ubisoft's Assassin's Creed
        Three Headed Monster, Three Winged Staff, and Three Lobed Burning Eye that form the Scepter of Power from the Black Trillium (Julian May, Andre Norton, Marion Zimmer Bradley)
        Ribbon Device, Eye of the Gods, and Sarcophagus from Stargate
        Primal Stones and the Sunforge from Netflix's The Dragon Prince


        ::The Radiant Pagoda of the Uncrowned King::
        Floor of the Eastern Wood
        Floor of the Western Water
        Floor of the Northern Air
        Floor of the Southern Fire
        Floor of the Central Earth
        Floor of the Many-Faced Moon (Lunars/Luna)
        Floor of the Star-Cloaked Vault (Sidereals/Maidens)
        Floor of the One True King (Solars/UCS)
        Floor of the Kings in Hell (Yozi/Infernals)
        Floor of the Kings in Shadow (Abyssals)
        Floor of the Forge-born King (Autochtonia)
        Floor of the Kings from Beyond (Wyld)

        Each "Floor" is a separate Artifact that will automatically merge with additional Floors found. Each Floor can be banished Elsewhere and re-summoned to your location like a Cache Egg. The Floors will only merge when you have attuned to multiple Floors and have them Elsewhere. Once they merge they cannot unmerge unless they are unattuned while Elsewhere, in which case they will leave Elsewhere and randomly appear anywhere within the bounds of the Exalted Universe (Malfeas, Yu-Shan, Wyld, Underworld, Autochthonia, Far Shore, etc...).

        Altogether, the Pagoda is a portable NA Manse independent of Leylines and Demesnes. Each Floor grants it's own Hearthstone and share of Powers. Without obtaining multiple floors, one might assume the wondrous treasure is nothing more than a portable manse of the relevant Aspect for the Floor they possess. If the Pagoda is fully assembled, who knows what power it might possess in addition to the benefits of a Manse untied to Geomancy.

            Some examples from previous editions of N/A artifacts were like, the Five Metal Shrike, a nearly indestructible hypersonic intelligent star fighter with a primary weapon cable of destroying a city in one shot. The only personal scale artifact N/A I can think of is this thing:

            https://campagna-exalted.obsidianpor...kis/aidenweiss

            Those aren’t even all of its powers. It also forms a unique N/A hearthstone for its bearer, custom tailored to that character, which they get to keep for all time. Having a campaign where your PCs are cranking out like 10 of those things so everyone gets 2-3 would be complete insanity. That’s when I’d start instituting hard rules about the exotic components required for it. You might have all the magical materials that you could want, but for X thing you’re making you’re going to need one of Mela’s actual scales, freely given. I don’t want to punish my players or stop them from feeling epic, but going from zero to everyone walking around with the Aidenweiss as a weapon, a similarly legendary set of armor for protection, flying the Five Metal Shrike with the Eye of Autochthon and the Mantle of Brigid rattling around in the cargo hold is just way too much.

                In my game Paths of the Maker, the whole second 'season' was devoted to the creation of an N/A artifact called the Maker's Flame. I'll use my player's words here:

                At rest, the Maker’s Flame is a thin, vertically symmetical, palm-sized pentagonal prism of unnaturally clear and smooth diamond-like crystal. The eminent craftsmen of the First Age called this synthetic godmetal, ‘Adamant’ - the purest of its kind.
                Within it is suspended a perfect white flame that dazzles and bewitches those that look upon it with its unearthly beauty. The ethereal matter in which it floats is a resurrected solution of a dead element that was slain and perverted by the Celestial Exalted at the end of the Time of Glories, much like those primordial demiurges that fell to them. It is an element of glorification, and it only remains inactive within its container because the surrounding flame and crystal alike can be refined no further.
                When the crystal is placed before a character’s brow and attuned for ten motes, it dissolves into a burning sigil - The Word of Making, once spoken at the birth of the world. From the Word, a crown of white fire encircles the wearer. Its flames are difficult to gaze upon directly, not because they are bright, but because within them are contained the potentials for impossible things - every spark and tongue is a thought of splendor, and they promise that a future of miracles made real belongs to the one who wears them.


                The Maker's Flame can initiate those who wear it into sorcery, provided they've constructed many artifacts. On top of that, its evocations supercharge the wearer's Crafting abilities, basically turning them into a demiurge who can perform crafting projects without the need for raw materials, or wrench a whole city out of the ground. They can transform matter into other types of matter.

                One notable weakness of the Maker's Flame is that it cannot conjure raw magical materials. No conjuring Jade or Orichalcum or anything like that.

                        Originally posted by Lioness View Post
                        Not sure how helpful this will be but here goes.

                        When I introduce N/A artifacts I'd rather my PCs be intimidated and unsure how to use it than unimpressed. I favour uncontrollable power that the wielder learns to use efficiently (or safely!) through Evocations rather than efficiency building towards raw power.

                        Though my N/As tend to be things my players found rather than made, hence its easier for me to give them a monkey's paw.
                        So Saber's Excalibur from Fate/Stay Night would be rated N/A. Since, if I remember correctly, using that thing at all always meant a torrential outpouring of destruction, and using it for anything else required mastery.

                        An N/A rating would actually makes much more sense for its lore than a 5-dot, really. Since it was supposed to be a divine weapon made by the spirit of the world itself from humanity's collective dream of "Sword", and possibly humanity's wishes for victory. Since the weapon's title is "Sword of Promised Victory", and all humans would instantly recognize the sword's identity upon seeing it, even if they had never heard of Excalibur before.

                        And while it can't raise those it kills as zombies, it can in fact slay an entire army in one attack. As well as the castle they might be hiding inside. And any dragon that might also be inside the castle.

                        Its not the sort of weapon that any player should be able to start with, like a 5-dot sword.

                                        N/A Artifacts can do many a thing. I wrote the below as inspiration, not limitation.

                                        An N/A Artifact would not break the attacker's blade parrying in protection of the player's ward, it would break his will to fight. An N/A Artifact would not strike true from afar when thrown but would disappear from the player's hand, reappearing in inconspicuous places, tripped over and forgotten, preventing the fate the player's aggression seeks to halt.                                                     I recently remembered the reason why I originally never considered Saber’s Excalibur to be a N/A artifact.

                                                    Gilgamesh’s Ea, which objectively outclasses it. Whereas Excalibur is classified as an anti-fortress weapon, Ea is classified as an anti-WORLD weapon, allegedly capable of destroying entire planes of existence if given enough time to charge. Certainly capable of destroying the temporary worlds created by Reality Marbles (if he doesn’t hesitate to draw the thing, because of pride).


                                                    But I suppose that both can be NA artifacts if Ea’s least output has certain drawbacks compared to Excalibur’s best output.

                                                    For one thing, Saber’s master was struggling to provide her with enough energy, whereas Gilgamesh was consuming an entire orphanage’s worth of children’s souls for fuel on top of having a better master, so Ea might have been far more expensive and Gilgamesh simply had more energy to spare.

                                                    For another, Ea’s least output might still inflict far more incidental damage to the surroundings than Excalibur. Certainly, the weapon’s attack wave seemed far less precise.


                                                    But perhaps most importantly, Ea can only ramp UPWARDS, and when you already start with more than enough power to destroy fortresses, anything more quickly becomes diminishing returns. Especially if destroying the world (or even just one whole Direction) requires such a long and alarmingly obvious charge-time that Heaven itself can and will intervene to stop you. Which turns Ea’s later Evocations into plot devices more than character tools.

                                                    Meanwhile, Excalibur could theoretically scale DOWNWARDS, so that it becomes more versatile to use in a wider variety of situations.
