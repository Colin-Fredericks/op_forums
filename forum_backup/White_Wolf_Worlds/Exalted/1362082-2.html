    Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
    It really depends. If you're regularly opposing Exalts with massive dice pools, they're worth the xp. Otherwise, your free excellency is fine. In fact, the excellency is a better charm than a lot of them (not all), and you should only use them if you've already maxed out your excellency.

    There are others that are basically just discounted excellencies, and I suppose saving motes is worth a charm for some characters.

    I do feel a lot of them are marginal enough that they should really not be at the base of trees though. If you're the sort of player who carefully keeps their essence expenditure down, then a charm that gives Diuble 9s for 4m is something you are not going to use (as you could instead buy 4 more dice, which on a 10 dice pool is better).
    Similarly, charms to reduce mote costs of your excellencies are something that you don't need until later, because at character gen you may not have many charms to use.
    I'm not asking only from an "statistical efficiency" point of view, but also from from an easier management from the player's perspective : so many dice trick Charms have so many different dice trick rules it makes them impossible to track quickly, wether they be efficient or not.

    Here's a suggestion of what I would like people interested to help make me build :

    Solar mastery

    Essence : 1 (Repurchase at 2 and 3). Abilities : Those concerned by annoying dice tricks, Socialize, Presence, Performance, Linguistics, Lore also I believe, but some others are offenders as well.
    Cost : 2m

    Blabla Lawgiver blabla truth of the sun blabla shining light. You gain Double 9 on the roll. You get one free Full Excellency once per scene.

    Repurchase (Essence 2) : You gain cascading 10s on a roll. You can reroll once per scene and keep the better result.

    Repurchase (Essence 3) : (Permanent) Your maximum Excellency cost is capped at 6. You can double 8s once per scene.


    Please keep in mind the purpose of this Charm would be to replace every dice trick Charms in a given tree. The only Charms left would be either those with a direct effect, or those whose subsystem is so specific they cannot me changed (like Athletics and Feats of Strength, or the Crafting system). The purpose here is to consider that Excellency is actually the base Mechanism for dice tricks, because it literally allows more dices to be thrown, which I believe is enough for the players to care about.

    They are in 3 tiers, they unlock 3 different and generic powers that are kinda recurrent across the Solar Charms tree : Double 9 and Free Full excellency, Cascading 10s and reroll, Mote economy and Double 8s.

    The idea is, you know which tier you have then you know your exacts dice tricks, and there are no others. I tried to mix effects that were weak but core to solar ideas with other stronger power so the overall cost of (re)purchase is worth a Charm.

    Obviously, you pay this Charm for each concerned ability.

        I tend to write seperately on my sheet some common ways to make rolls, like so (this is from my Full Moon):
        Combat tactics (Stunt bonuses are 1pt, and included)
        Join Battle: 8 dice
        Boosted Join Battle: 5m for 13 dice
        Ultimate Join Battle: 10m for 13 dice, reroll 1s and double 9s. (The re-roll 1s is until they no longer appear.)
        Ultimate Join Battle with a stunt: 15m for 20 dice, re-roll 1s and double 9s. (The re-roll 1s is until they no longer appear.)

        Withering Attack: 3 motes. 15 dice, re-roll up to 5 failures. +12 damage (14 with Essence-Fangs active).
        Mighty Withering Attack: 11 motes. 20 dice +1 success, re-roll up to 5 failures, roll an extra dice for each 10. +14 damage (16 with Essence-Fangs active).
        Ultimate Withering Attack with stunt vs an enemy with lower initiative or who hasn't had their turn: 19 motes. 27 dice +1 success, re-roll up to 5 failures, roll an extra dice for each 10. +19 damage (21 with Essence-Fangs active).
        Add 5m, 1wp to ignore soak from armour.

        If necessary, add 3m to re-roll up to five 1s and ignore 1pt from medium parry/cover/full defence.

        Poison decisive attack: 8 motes, 2i. 16 dice. With a stunt, 10 motes, 2i, for 23 dice.
        Poison damage 2i/round (A in Crash), Duration 6 rounds, –2 penalty. Or 1i(A), -4.
        For each 10 on the damage roll, add 1 to either duration, penalty or damage. Duration can't be reduced below 1.

        If necessary, add 3m to re-roll up to five 1s and ignore 1pt from medium parry/cover/full defence.

        Poison attack vs a crashed enemy: 14 motes, 3i, 1wp. 16 dice. With a stunt, 19 motes, 3i, 1wp. for 23 dice.
        Does +1 auto-success on the damage roll.
        Poison damage 1 agg/round, -2 penalty. Duration is 1 per 2 dice of raw damage rolled, enemy doesn't get a roll to resist.

        Defence (Enemies take -1 or -3 penalty to hit when in Snake form):
        Parry 6
        4m for Parry 8.
        10m for Parry 12 with a stunt.


        ... Yes, I have found that as annoying and complex to run as it is to read. I'd like to avoid dice tricks with my next character, I think.

                    Originally posted by Chausse View Post
                    but Winning Stride discipline that gives you 2 motes or 2 initiative only for Athletics when you successfully win a test of speed but that is not the same Charm that Onrush Burst Method which gives you 3 motes (and not initiative) for Athletics Charms but only on a Rush action and not in a test of speed and bzzzz. I can assure you when you play someone with Athletics supernal these Charms are the worst to be paid, you just forget them because meanwhile you have 8 different other Charms with real effect on the game that you have to take into account when you rush and attack someone.
                    That's not the only instance of the problem in Solar Athletics, either.

                    Demon-Wasting Rush and Godspeed Steps both advance from Racing Hare Method. GS requires an additional Charm, but it's largely the superior Charm. Demon-Wasting Rush lets you rush from medium range, and gives you two reflexive movements. Godspeed Steps (which is actually slightly cheaper to use) not only allows you to rush from farther than Demon-Wasting Rush (especially if you're already using Racing Hare Method), except instead of rushing you automatically enter close range with your target, rendering the need for reflexive movements largely moot. Also, it removes the flurry penalty for ALSO attacking the target that turn.

                    This seems to be an error of conflicting drafts. DWR is weak enough in comparison that it seems like it should have been a prerequisite for Godspeed Steps, but GS is so obviously superior that you'd never use Demon-Wasting Rush again except in very niche situations (such as your target having a Charm to reflexively escape close range without a disengage action). In being far more niche, DWR should follow after GS, but it's still not powerful enough within that specific niche to be worthwhile compared to Godspeed Steps.

                    I also hear that Nine-Aeons Thew doesn't work as advertised, but I haven't experimented with Solar power-lifting Charms.


                    So I might be slightly accentuating the problem of dice tricks (because they are not the majority of Charms, thanks God), but I was really glad when I played Dragon Blooded and felt much more ease to use their Charms, except for the excellency that I think they should have all unified to avoid checking "What's the special effect of Athletics Excellency already ?" with something like "Dragon Blooded always reroll 6" or something like that.
                    Dragon-Blooded Excellencies have specific effects because not all Abilities are made equal. Some benefit more from adding dice, others benefit more from adding successes, and some benefit too much from one effect, and others are so niche that they deserve to have effects which would be too powerful for more popular, frequently-used Abilities.

                    Indeed, I think that a lot of Solar dice-foolishness might have been avoided if Solars approached Excellencies the same way, the way they did in First Edition.


                    Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post
                    Part of that is probably the size too. Solars have like 1,000 charms or something and with so many it’s hard to make each one a big unique memorable effect. It’s easier to make a character that has a super hard focus on one ability though.
                    Solars being able to focus super-hard into one Ability was definitely a design goal, considering Supernal Abilities. It's a way to reinforce "Solars have unparalleled talent within their areas of expertise", a way to distinguish different Solar Castes from each other, and a way to distinguish individual Solars from each other without elemental Aspects or Totem beasts.


                    Originally posted by Chausse View Post
                    Yeah I think as well that the number of Charms, coupled with the absence of a Charm tree to allow the player to easily visualise what is going on, is a lackluster.
                    The staggering page-count of the book, together with the staggering number of Charms, might have made adding more pages for multiple Charm trees for every Ability somewhat unfeasible.

                    But yes, I was also disappointed by the lack of that visual aid.


                    But for Athletics, I think they managed to make really unique memorable effect, when they are not about small dice/mote tricks : Graceful Crane Stance, Monkey Leap, Soaring Crane Leap, Foe Vaulting, Increasing Strength, Thunderbolt Attack Prana, Feather Foot, Spider Foot, and they all are only Essence 1 Charms, and every one of them is either simple, effective of use cleverly the established mechanics of the game to put a twist on them.
                    "They managed" is perhaps inaccurate. These effects are a legacy of First Edition Core Book, not an invention of the men behind the Third Edition Core Book. They are instead more responsible for the Athletics Charms which tend to give us problems.

                    Even the new version of the the old Monkey Leap Technique is not as neatly and clearly designed as I would like.

                    I don't know what their circumstances were, so I don't wish to judge them based on the results of their work. In any case, it's in the past, and assigning blame doesn't fix the present problems.
