        Oh, something else I should say that you have to account for here is, like, if this character is meant to be the group's primary fighter, there's two ways that can go.

        The first is that, Charms notwithstanding, no one in the entire group can fight better than an experienced mortal warrior. As Exalted circles go, these guys are just not playing in the god-killing side of the pool. This is fine! It means the party is going to have a different approach than many parties do in practice, because they're likely to face physical threats with something closer to a mortal mindset than the traditional "just assume you can kill anything you want", but that's a completely viable approach that makes for good games. As the countless "my fights aren't challenging" threads attest, fights are often more fun if you're weaker.

        The other possibility is that one (or all!) of the other PCs is playing a wilderness guide or something and goes "I have some points left over, I guess I'll also be one of the greatest swordsmen in the East" and they totally outclass you in a fight. People complain about this a lot, but my experience is that it's basically OK as long as the other person actually wants to be that good? Like, the arc you get here is extremely hype: your character starts out totally outclassed and awestruck by this incredible warrior, which makes sense because she's an inexperienced farm girl, and then they train together and gradually over the course of the game she matches and then outclasses him, because she's very focused on Melee and he's spending a lot of his Charm picks on unrelated wilderness-guide shit. I love that sort of thing, so the fact that the game makes it happen where you'd expect it to happen seems more like a feature than a bug to me.

                            I had some time to kill, so I tried running the build that habitable exoplanet proposed against a squad of elite troops --Size 2, so we'll say there's around 30. In a real game I'd be using the environment as much as possible and maybe doing some intimidation or using a horse, but here I just had them stand in place and beat on each other, pretty much.

                            I made a couple changes to the build which are worth noting:

                            Worse attributes, since six dice to attack might seem "too good":
                            Str 3 / Dex 2 / Sta 4
                            App 4 / Cha 5 / Man 2
                            Int 1 / Per 4 / Wits 4

                            The only nonphysical one she uses here is Wits, for JB. Aside from maybe moving a point from Wits to Int, I think the only way you could get worse attributes than these without betraying the concept or taking fewer dots than you're allowed is to take physical tertiary or give her Dexterity 1.

                            I replaced Iron Skin Concentration with Spirit Strengthens the Skin, as you can't learn the former without the latter.

                            It is perhaps worth noting that the only non-Excellency Charms she actually got a chance to use were Excellent Strike, Spirit Strengthens the Skin, and (very rarely) Dipping Swallow Defense, as none of the others were relevant vs. a battle group.

                            Caste: Melee (Supernal, lol), Awareness, Brawl, Dodge, Resistance
                            Favored: Survival, Integrity, Athletics, Ride, Presence

                            Willpower 8, because it seems in-character and she needs to spend her BP on something. This does matter though, as she tends to spend a lot for this sort of thing.


                            She uses a straight sword and plate-and-chain, because there'd be no real reason for this character not to have heavy armour. If you're wondering where she got it, well, it's from doing stuff like this. She'd be pretty tough without it, but probably not enough to have good prospects vs. Size 2 elites. I did spend the battle group's WP, for what it's worth.

                            I gave her a minor stunt on everything and none for the BG. I guess some people would consider that "unfair", but in my experience, actual STs tend to be more generous, in the sense that they very rarely stunt mook piles but will usually award a major or two to a PC.

                            Anyway, the fight is excruciatingly long so I'll spare you the details (unless you really want to see!), but I did it twice. The first time the battle group had dismal attack rolls but still won after 30+ rounds; however, she got it very close, down to its last two points of Magnitude on its last point of Size. Late in this battle I realized that she was far better off alternating between aiming and attacks than just attacking every turn, so I tried that the second time and she won very handily (basically her entire peripheral pool full) even though the BG got better rolls; having the aim bonus means she's overwhelmingly favoured to win clashes even when the BG spends WP, which helps enormously. I'm pretty sure that if you do the aiming thing, she's strongly favoured to win even if she's not willing to manifest anima.

                            So that seems...pretty good to me? Like, if she had two other people in this fight who had even worse stats but could stick onslaught on the battle group, it'd be enormous. If she so much as took a Melee specialty in swords, she'd be vastly better. By Exalted warrior standards she's a joke but she can, in fact, beat a squadron of the Realm's finest without significant luck involved, which is pretty damn good for a starting character with a four-die attack pool. I feel pretty happy with where that's at.
