    39 Steps' Deathlord Headcanon

    The Eye And Seven Despairs is doubtless the most brilliant theorist extant in the Exaltoverse, and his unmatched knack for extrapolation is the cause of his erratic behavior. His obsession with torturing the Exaltations of his former Circle is a derangement he consciously instilled in himself, for alone among the Deathlords, only he understood the inescapable insanities that his kind inevitably develop, and he chose to limit his obsessions to an area that would not interfere with his assigned tasks--all they cost him is time, which Deathlords have in abundance. The obsessions of the Lion, the Dowager, the Lover and the rest cost them perspective, or flexibility, or focus, or contingency planning. The Eye And Seven Despairs may look the ineffective fool, but he alone retains the trust of the Neverborn, having shrugged off the punishments and accusations of both his dead masters and his fellows.

    The Eye and Seven Despairs' arm was the first thing he created, and most other Deathlords think its creation was due to the Eye's lack of combat skills. In truth, he built it so that, should the Neverborn ever choose to rip their ghostly essence from him, he would still have power unconnected to them. If the Neverborn were to rescind their gifts to the Deathlords, only the Eye and perhaps the Bishop would be left as more than quaking ghosts.

    The Eye never told the Lion the true reason he urged them to invite the Raksha to invade at the height of the Great Contagion. The Lion believes it was to strike Creation's deathblow, and the Dowager thinks it was to steal her thunder. The truth is that the Contagion was too good at killing. The Eye realized that it would only be a matter of time before it began "killing" ghosts as well. In fact, his calculations showed that everything sentient, or alive, or capable of creating anything else would have, in time, fallen to the Contagion. That which never lived, however, was never affected by the Contagion. That means that, while elementals would have become extinct, leaving Creation a sterile, exhausted husk, Creation itself would have endured, after a fashion. Even the Neverborn would have "died" again, but since the infinite Fetter that is Creation would still exist, they would remain in the Abyss; dead, yet ever-dying, forever bound to a broken Fetter that they would no longer have the might, or the servants, or any hope at all, of destroying. Of course, explaining this to his fellow Deathlords would have been an exercise in futility, so he introduced the warping, ever-changing element of the Wyld to Creation, and those chaos energies proved able to mutate even the Contagion into something that spared one in ten of its victims, eventually forcing the plague to infect itself.
    On some primal level, the Neverborn understand what the Eye did, and his continuing favored status, so puzzling to his fellow Deathlords, could be said to represent what passes for gratitude for the Eye's compassion in sparing them what the Contagion would have done to them.

    Finally, when presented with the stolen Exaltations and the Monstrances that applied them, the Eye And Seven Despairs almost immediately intuited the (relatively) simple process by which an Abyssal could become a Solar once again. That is the other reason that he keeps his Deathknights mired in romantic underworld intrigues. Ever the researcher, however, he has been unable to resist setting his servant, Red Famine, on the road to rebellion and (he hopes) redemption. The other Deathlords (except for the Dowager) have invested heavily in their Abyssal Exalted, and a string of redeemed Deathknights should keep them busy enough for the Eye to continue his infinite researches without interruption.

            The giant orgy that we're told created the Dragon-Blooded legions never actually happened. Rather, it was a joke that sprung up in the mid-to-late first age due to Solars' tendencies to form harems of Dragon-blooded, a joke that later became a legend after enough people had forgotten it was a joke. What actually happened was that the Dragons and the original 10,000 DB used a secretive ritual now lost to the dust of time to change mortals into DB. These new DB only had 3/4ths of the dragon's blood, meaning that their children wouldn't necessarily Exalt. While this was deemed a problem for Creation long-term, it was considered a manageable one at the time, and a necessary risk given the threat the Primordials posed.

                The Western Star of Calibration / The Nova of the Deep Wyld & the Remnant Archipelago

                During Calibration there are a number of astronomical items that cannot be seen at any other time (See my previous post for more information). In the west a bright wide star sits forever in the same spot. It has been measured by astronomers and astrologers in the both the first and second age, and to their amazement its essence reads identical to the Daystar but an indescribable distance into the Deep Wyld. Heaven is silent what it is, or as to why it is seen during Calibration. Theories abound. Is it afterglow from the Daystar's western landing birth? Is it the Unconquered Sun's hidden retreat? It is impossible to know.

                The Nova Barrier
                When the Mock Creation of Cemunian (the Constellation of Bone and Sinew) was destroyed by the Daystar's one and only use of his ultimate weapon during the Twin Centers dilemma. It left an ever expanding hollow sphere of Solar Flame in the deep deep Wyld. This expanding fireball burns through way-points, the unshaped, and anything else that strays too close to its boundaries. The Nova Barrier impacted all of the Mock Creation, burning it away but it is not perfectly symmetrical. It is conceivable that either some of the structures or powerful beings left "shadows" or "holes" in the barrier. These are likely a thinning of the barrier, so rather than diving directly into flames of eternal solar judgement hundreds of leagues thick they maybe only a few miles thick, or perhaps an ever shifting passage way as the flames eddy and shift inside the barrier.
                -- To the Fey it is a constant reminder of the horrors that are the Primordials, their pet gods, and their Creation.
                -- To Creation it is a curiosity. The Twin Centers Dilemma has long been lost to history, and those that do know of it, do not speak of it for it embarrasses the Unconquered Sun's sensibilities.

                Inside the Nova is the remnants of the Mock Creation. A world burned to ash eons ago, but not everything was destroyed (yay perfect defenses!), but that which remained was not untouched by the experience.

                The Remnant Archipelago: The central element of Mock Creation was turned to Ash, stability crushed to powder, what remains is an Archipelago of islands dancing in a cloud of ash. It is unbearably hot and knows no true night as the Nova casts light inward toward the Archipelago as much as it does outward and from all directions. The closest these island worlds knows to night is when a dense potion of the Ash Cloud or Mock Water Clouds envelopes an island and blots out much of the light. The remaining elements are similar to Creation's but made in mockery of them, sickly, strange and foul.
                -- Mock Water, what little can be found, is undrinkable poison, quenches no thirst and can unpredictably change states (liquid, gas, or solid).
                -- Mock Air, in the Remnant is stifling, stagnant, filled with Ash, and devours sounds.
                -- Mock Wood, within the Remnants grows plants of a sickly pallor, with strange shapes, covered in thorns, filled with poisons, and are nearly all predator.
                Lastly Fire comes in two forms. Solar Embers left behind by the Unconquered Sun, and the Remnant Flames of Cemunian.
                -- The Solar Embers burn bright and true, with a furosity that lets them devour anything native to the Remnants. These dangerous seeds of destruction are greatly feared and occasionally used as WMDs by those foolish enough to try to tame them.
                -- The Mock Flames devour heat and light, as well as whatever they are burning. This produces an unnatural darkness and cool that most of the denizens of the Remnants cultivate to make their citadels and dwellings more comfortable than the unbearable solar heat of the Nova Barrier.

                The Survivors of the Archipelago: All of the mortal races, and many of the enlightened ones were destroyed by the Fires of the Daystar, but some souls of the Primordial Cemunian, as well as his Mock Gods and Mock Elementals did survive the Nova in one way or another. They have created their own societies, and some of them have created their own servitor races as well.
                -- The Mock Gods which Cemunian created were divine bureaucrats filled with hate and enslaved with chains of fate. Unlike the Gods of Creation which served helping to create a balance and fulfill a role in the perfect machine, the Mock Gods constantly fought with their equals, connived against their greaters and tortured their lessers. The concepts of Mock Creation existed at a constant stated of war, its hierarchy one of strength and strength alone. How much would Cemunian laugh at the current state of Yushan, and how much must his own bureaucracies revile the Unconquered Sun as the continue to drift towards the Mockery that Cemunian created. Only the stronger or most protected of the Mock Gods survived. Though lesser gods may have been created by Dark Stars in intervening eons.
                -- The Mock Elementals are beings of great elemental mockery /"purity" but unlike those of Creation they are rarely if ever intelligent, almost entirely beasts to be tamed and enslaved by the Mock Gods. Mock Elementals do not tend toward a draconic visage as they gain strength. There are no Mock Elemental Dragons, either lesser or greater. Instead they become a Demense and spawn more of their own kind. These living Demenses are still capable of acting and must be physically subdued to attune to.
                -- The Servitors: These can generally be anything that the Mock Gods or Dark Stars decide to create. From mortal slaves, to enlightened warriors, to sorcerously constructed constructs.
                -- Dark Stars, the Lost Souls of Cemunian: The souls of Cemunian are as the stars in his constellation, each burn with an unearthly light and like the souls of other Primordials are varied and manifold. The souls of Cemunian that stood in Mock Creation to direct its defenses against the Primordial Forces until the collision destroyed Creation and the Mockery with it. These souls were amongst those that felt the full power of the Daystar's ignition. The vast majority were annihilated as was the greatest amongst them a 3rd Circle Soul of Cemunian which stood with its greatest guardians in the Mock Sky over the desolate hellish mockery of creation and its central elemental pole. Those that did survive, in a shelter of somekind or due to mystic puissance combined with not being the focus of the attack. However the Daystar's assault did not leave any unscathed. It burned away their primordial connection to Cemunian. This extinguished their lights making them appear much like the Mock Element of Fire, devouring light and standing as solid shadows. Dark Stars, come in greater (2nd Circle) and lesser (1st Circle). These Dark Stars control vast realms and often rule over Mock Gods, Mock Elementals, or their own created Servitors.
                -- Fairfolk Explorers: It is conceivable that some of the Fairfolk may have made their way into the Remnant Archipelago through holes in the Nova Barrier or mystic trickery. These Fairfolk would need to assume a body or form compatible with Mock Creation (via Mock Elements, or the Hate, Sorrow or Darkness that Cemunian imbued it with). If such Fairfolk exist they would be few and far between as solar flames destroy fairfolk as readily as any other Creatures of Darkness. Additionally since there are no Wyld Zones in the Remnant Archipelago, they would be forced to take a Demense or capture Mock Elementals and Servitors to devour their essence to survive. This likely makes them reclusive Lords constantly fighting their own Demense for control, or desperate raiders constantly looking to take new acquisitions for their larder.

                Why go to the Remnant?
                Well in my story hook it is "By Accident" but there are a number of reasons that one could go to the Remnant. First it is an invaluable place for studying the might of the Daystar, after all it is the only place its greatest weapon was ever used. Second, it was created by a Primordial, and that means it may have all sorts of treasures and useful materials found no where else.

                Story Hook (How Mine Went)
                In my own adventure, a pair first age solars (a Sagely Twilight Craftsman, and an Eclipse Explorer Prince) created a Reality Engine protected Generation Vessel and sent it out into the Deep Wyld on a millennium long trip to reach and investigate the Novas flames, and the Daystar's devastating power. In addition they wanted to explore the Mock Creation (if any of it did still exist) in order find anything of value there as well (and to sate their curiosity). When the Vessel finally reached the Nova Barrier the 2 solars were supposed to use a transfer gate to board and protect the ship. However they had died some 900 years earlier during the usurpation and without a solar on board with protective Charms the vessel struck the Nova Barrier and barely survived. The crew was not so lucky. The Hulk has now been drifting in the Ash Clouds of the Remnant Archipelago ever since, its gleaming hull of first age materials corroding in the Mock Elements. The PCs in my game discover that the essence of their Manse and many others in the area have been redirected to a previously unknown Manse beneath a first age ruin. They may also discover that there are reports of demons in the nearby landscape and that countless families have gone missing in the area. When they investigate they find the portal to the wreck of the Generational Vessel inside the Remnant Archipelago. The when they investigate the ship a battle with Remnant natives causes the Passage to close. The PCs must find a way to re-power the passage to go home, or they can explore the Remnant Archipelago to their heart's content.

                Note: It is important to remember that though some of Cemunian's souls were in Mock Creation when it was hit by the Daystar's mega-weapon, the vast majority Primordial's Jouten, and Souls (2nd & 3rd) had already made their escape. Cemunian is potentially still wounded and wandering the Deep Wyld.

                                        There is a good reason that the Golden Lord and the Pale Mistress are so strongly similar to the Unconquered Sun and Luna; it is a family resemblance.                                         The First Infernal

                                        One of the first things that anyone "in the loop" knows about the Green Sun Princes is that there are fifty of them, ten of each caste. This precise number was the Ebon Dragon's price for teaching the Neverborn how to capture the Solar Exaltations, and they were quickly parceled out, an equal number to each of the Reclamation conspirators. This story, however, is only half true. It is a secret known only to the Yozi conspirators themselves and a few of their highest souls that there are ten Fiends, ten Scourges, ten Defilers, ten Malefactors... and eleven Slayers.

                                        Even the Yozis, puissant and brilliant as they are, could have performed so extensive a change to the most powerful and intricate things ever created on their first try. The Ebon Dragon and She Who Lives In Her Name agreed (for once) that any failed attempt could risk the Exaltations escaping. No, they needed to be sure that they had a working process before they carried out their grand scheme. Fortunately, there was a subject ready to hand.

                                        In the middle days of the First Age, one of the greatest Solar generals, called Sun Dragon, had come to Malfeas to find a new battle, tired as he was of endless peace. He raised a great army of dispossessed demons, and no force save the Yozis themselves could stand against him, and they were wary of breaking their treaties that kept the Deliberative from waging full scale war upon them. In the mass confusion and terror of the Usurpation, however... who would notice if one more Solar went the way of his peers, and his Exaltation did not join them in the Jade Prison?

                                        Sun Dragon's last battle was a glorious one, to rival the days of the Primordial War itself. The Demon Realm shook with it, as the Dawn's blades and fists wounded the Yozis themselves. In the end, however, the War had been won by the entire Exalted host, and he was only one. And his enemies were legion. Inevitably, he fell, his Exaltation captured in Malfeas' grasp and his tattered soul discarded. Where it went, none can tell; perhaps Sun Dragon's hun made its way to the Underworld, perhaps it became a demon, or perhaps it languishes in a prayer mill to this day.

                                        His Exaltation, however, was experimented on endlessly, for centuries. First, they managed to contain it, in a small box alloyed of Creation-mined Jade and Malfeas' basalt bones. Numerous human denizens of Hell were Exalted, experimented on (including being Invested with Infernal Glory, or horribly failed attempts to do so), and murdered at every stage of the process. Until, finally, something emerged that had never been seen before. An Exalt who wielded the powers of Malfeas and his brethren as if they were his own, with no visible trace left of the Unconquered Sun's glow shining through. The Yozis declared their project a success, and moved on to the next phase of acquiring more Exaltations to repeat the process.

                                        That first Green Sun Prince, Sun Dragon's heir, was sent out into the world to aid the hekatonkhire who broke open the Jade Prison. Perhaps if he had not been there to lend his power, all of the Exaltations would have escaped... or perhaps, if he were not there to sabotage the process, all would have been captured and tainted. For in their glee at finally creating their champion, the Yozis had neglected to add the same leash of fate that would shackle the other fifty Infernal Exaltations. Though lavished with only the best that Hell has to offer, many think that this prodigal son chafes at the tight restrictions upon him. With such a weak metaphysical hold, a first hand look at his so-called masters' cruelty and insanity, and a head start in developing his powers over his younger brothers and sisters, who knows what this Green Sun Dragon might dare to do?
