    Worried about scale and expectations of Exalted

    01-21-2024, 09:29 AM
    I initially bounced off Ex3 a long time ago, but Essence has reignited a want to dip my toe into the setting and give the reworked system a shot. I have access to players who I reckon will jump at the chance to play a streamlined version of Exalted, but there's a specific issue that's holding me back from jumping straight in with full confidence:

    I'm worried I'm not going to be able to match the expectation of scale and grandiosity that comes with Exalted.

    I hear so many people recall so many stories where their circle accomplished things like stormed Yu-Shan, threw down with Yozis in Hell, changed the face and culture(s) of Creation in fundamental ways at its foundations, etc. Granted, I'm not sure if new players to Exalted are going to want (or know they can) accomplish these things at the start, but the idea of keeping up with bigger and bigger conflicts and ventures makes me worried that a campaign will start to feel unfocused or suffer narrative creep.

    I know the phrase 'big things start with small beginnings' exists for a reason, and let me set the record straight that I think these are good and cool things to strive for as aspirations under the belts of literal demigods and that this is sorta the point of Exalted. But my players and I are coming off the back of stuff like mid-level Pathfinder 2E and stuff in that vein, where expectations are still fantastical but comparatively more grounded or small-scale in my experience, so I worry that we're all going to be at a loss of what types of things are appropriate for their PCs to be doing at their levels of essence.

    I know Essence has some advice on scaling ventures, battles, arcs etc. around the relative essence level of the PCs, but still, any further advice?

      As long as you clearly set expectations up front, you can stay small forever.

      You can have a dynastic secondary school game that's about the school, the students, the drama, and maybe an occasional minor escapade or incident nearby.
      You can have a city-focused game where the PCs keep dealing with local threats and drama without ever shaking the pillars of heaven.
      You can have a band of wandering heroes solving small, important yes, but small problems for various cities, minor nations, or lesser spirits.
      Those, and many other such small-scale games, are great Exalted games. I ran one, it was extremely well received.

      The only thing I'd suggest is to keep a finger on the rate at which power and Essence is gained, so the PCs don't outstrip good local-scale threats too soon.

          Originally posted by The Firebrand View Post
          I'm worried I'm not going to be able to match the expectation of scale and grandiosity that comes with Exalted.
          Don't worry, relax. Know your player's expectations, their characters' capacities and start from there. You have the ST hat now, so you have a solid hand on what pacing to give or not to things.

          Pick a place and situation for starting points, delve into the books and what options & tricks they may offer you in fleshing out the scenery and cast.

          The PCs by virtue of being Exalted are more than a cut above most people - the nature of its charms will on themselves make a young Chosen a match and probably the better of any demon, elemental, fae, ghost or small god of equal Essence, what to say of god-bloods, beastfolk, mutated people and the whole expanse of mortal people across Creation, that do not wield either charms or essence.

          For every Dynast, Lunar warlord or Lost Egg with a city-state or kingdom, there are lots of places ruled by princes, jarls, senators, autocrats or what-have-you of no inherent supernatural might of their own, counting on a combination of treasures (magical or not), connections & favors from sorcerers, spirits or more and their own cunning to keep their thrones and head above the shoulders.


          Shape the locale, give the PCs situations to play to their strengths, to shine strutting their brand new tricks and pace things to your leisure through what sort of opposition you pick with each story arc.

                          Originally posted by The Firebrand View Post
                          I know Essence has some advice on scaling ventures, battles, arcs etc. around the relative essence level of the PCs, but still, any further advice?
                          The Dirtiest Little secret of every single RPG under the sun is that you, yes you; the GM decide at what scale the game is played at and what powers are and aren't allowed.
                          Look over the charm section of your book(s), identify which ones will take the game places you don't to go; ban them, and inform the players of the bans prior to character creation. If one slips through during the course of play, tell the player you're disallowing it (post-game) but will refund the exp costs and allow them to take another charm of equivalent costs and pre-reqs. You can do this during the game, but things tend to get a bit more heated when that happens. Though this ideally doesn't go beyond some grumbling while they erase the offending charm.
                          Exalted has a long and storied history of charms that absolutely break the game balance and game elements that squick people out and are thus ruled away by the ST, so a ban list won't be too unheard of, even with fans of older editions. Especially them.
                          They remember the dark days, the days when the Prophet Chung heard the whispers of Scene Long Perfects and Mote Regeneration to discover the terrible charm combinations that render you entirely impervious not only to damage; but to exhaustion of your motes rendering the defeat of a character utterly impossible. And it drove him mad.

                                      So another thing is that things are going to be possible in Exalted that are not in other games by the nature of the different settings. For example when we were essence 1 in our game we were investigating a weird cult dedicated to like a milling wheel in the middle of the town. Not getting any help from the cult in an explanation on the hell was going on or why this was something our mentor wanted us to look into. I just set up a thing and summoned the local spirit court, essentially the gods in charge of the island. And they answered, they took their time (one threw a fish at me because they don't like being on dry land and that's where I set up the meeting area.) This was incidental to the scale because its a small island in the middle of nowhere with the only notable thing being said cult, so these gods weren't that powerful or high in the celestial bureaucracy. But when I told this to a coworker of mine who mostly plays Pathfinder because the idea little gods that you can just walk up to or call out like trying to talk to the mayor or something is that just wouldn't happen there.

                                                  It's not worth worrying about.

                                                  There's a lot of "epic" Exalted chronicles that when you scrutinise them are basically the tabletop game equivalent of games loaded up with cutscenes and quick time events kinda like how the Locust War and Return of the Scarlet Empress would probably play out if you ran them faithfully.

                                                  "This week in our session Ragara died"
                                                  "oh wow, he's pretty scary for a Dragon-Blooded did he get to use Blood Zenith to restore his youth?"
                                                  "nah, he died off-screen shortly after his ship ran around after escaping the Autochthonian invaders who razed the island he was retired on but house Ragara is swearing revenge!"
                                                  "okay and where were your PCs in all this?"
                                                  "An-Teng"

                                                  No shade for people who like the epic narratives, but when I want to hear about someone's game I want to know what the player characters did and its consequences.
