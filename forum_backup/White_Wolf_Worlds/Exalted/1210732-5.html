        Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post

        The second point for me is difficult. If you’ve got a party of 5 Solars the fights that lead up to the climactic battle need to be at least challenging enough to make them spend more than they respire. They need to keep getting harder too or else subsequent action scenes will just be opportunities to regen motes.
        My biggest success so far is presenting fights where "mashing the enemy into pulp" is not the only goal (or sometimes not even a primary goal). Spreading the focus gives the the nondawns more space to align themselves as part of the fight and gives the players more considerations to make, which can dramatically increase the tension of the scene.

        Even the most straight up dungeon crawl fight scenario i've seen in play, wyld shaping encounter rolls, there is an element that adds a LOT of tension to the fight: Waves. Those encounter rolls go bad real fast, and you will likely pit yourself against several if you're doing more than just dispelling the chaos. How do you fight a raksha hunting party and stay efficient in case you fight some hannya? And then are you risking low essence or a couple hits, knowing a behemoth can spring from anywhere?

        Battle Groups are really, really good to play up the wearing effect of wave encounters too. You don't gain initiative from them past the ping and crash rewards, Their soak tends to be high and decisives put you at risk despite being extra powerful against them. Add a commander and a couple heroes and you have to burn hard.

Dragonblooded are never at their best alone, and they know it.                     Had to take a break yesterday to actually sleep, time to make up for it today though.

                    Craft

                    The 3rd edition craft system has a lot of knobs and Solars have a fist wrapped around each one. I think the Solar charm tree is nearly 50 charms or something. Slots, craft points, terminus, they've got a few charms for each. They shuffle around points, negate the need for tools and do things way faster. They've also got some ones that just take the system beyond and do things like let you retroactively and instantly finish a hidden project.

                    The Dragonblooded don't have half as many charms, although a few common themes are still there. They can negate the need for tools when crafting within an element, they can speed it up as well. The other charms that interact with the actual system are straightforward, but powerful. Stuff like giving a discount on how many points you need to finish a project, and a gambling system to try and get more points.

                    The thing that they seem to focus on here is repairing and breaking. They can actually add up to, I think 4 dots of strength for Feats of Strength that involve demolition, or 1 for repairs, and any excess strength that they have gets added as a non charm bonus. They can also speed Feats of Strength demolitions, using their craft speed enhancer. So they break things good and can be really strong when their craft is on the line. It gives me the image of a terrestrial planting her feet on the ground and with great heaving effort raising a roof on a building by herself, just hand over hand on the pully system.

                    This one I was very worried about but it turned out better than I expected. A lot fewer things to keep track of than the Solar version, which is good for an already complex system like craft. It's also got a whole lot of cool effects like the strength boosters that guide your character to epic actions, rather than just epic dicerolls. Nice.

                    Dodge

                    Solar dodge is one of the best defenses in the game, and it's full of tricks. Things that manipulate initiative, things that give you motes, burst your dice cap. It's strong in a lot of different ways with a lot of answers to any threat, including a true perfect defense.

                    Dragonblooded dodge seems way more aggressive by comparison. There's 3-4 counter attacks of some fashion or another in the set. It definitely feels like DB dodge is all about punishing your foe for attacking you.

                    My favourite combo also made it over from the past edition. There's a teamwork charm that lets you leap in front of an attack, reflexively using evasion as a defend other. There's another charm that lets you dodge an attack and then redirect it to another one of your enemies. Put that together and when an Abyssal anathema swings his Grand Daiklave at your hearthmate you streak over to them, catch the blade, push it around your sister and then send it spinning into the zombie horde like a blender. That abyssal just blew all his initiative and motes on a decisive attack that wiped his own army.

                    So yeah, that's all you need to know, you can do that with Terrestrial dodge. Tree done.

                    Integrity

                    Solar integrity is kind of an odd duck. It accomplishes the theme of "Being amazing with integrity" in the sense of social system power pretty quickly and then branches out into things that just broaden the the idea of protecting your character from change.

                    After the DB one is done protecting your intimacies it gets busy, uh, protecting your intimacies. Okay let me explain better. When somebody tries to convince you to betray your hearth, DB integrity is there to make sure your intimacies on your sheet stay safe. When an anathema comes for your hearthmates, DB integrity is there to help you protect the things you care about physically. That's what's happening with charms like Immolating Phoenix Soul, which allows you to respond to a persuade attempt to make you betray your allies by going berzerk. And when you fail to protect your family? Unflagging Vengeance will make sure you don't have to stop for anything on your road to revenge.

                    Terms of enrampagement indeed. I touched on it a bit what it can do before so I'll leave that there.

                    Investigation

                    The Solar one here doesn't differ all that much from the DB one. It allows you to more easily case scenes and profile people, they just do it in slightly different ways. The DB one maybe focuses on emotional attachment to the act, possibly.

                    Some cool things that DB investigation can do though are give yourself basically an eclipse protection if you're invited into a building by somebody who has the authority to do that. There's a cool power where you can tell who's a stranger in a town and if they're not where they live. A lot of little tricks like that which don't technically boost case-scene rolls but are generally really useful. They also get this really cool ability to lock onto an arch-nemesis and gain a suite of permanent powers that help them in their cat and mouse game, and stopping them from being deterred.

                    Overall some cool distinct effects and otherwise somewhat similar to the solar one, which isn't really bad.

                    Larceny

                    On to larceny! It too is similar in the themes, probably because these last two abilities are more about becoming a character archetype than adding a specific skill. They can both retroactively see a crime scene, pickpocket, pick locks, ect. Of course every time I say that the DB version is similar, it usually comes with some restrictions, which can be fun. Like the anti-pickpocket charm for DBs which pierces the palm of the would-be thief with an essence thorn.

                    It gets a few cool and unique things though. Like the ability to submerge an item into stone, a social attack to encourage more people to break the law, it's also got a few ways to disarm opponents. It also seems to have a lot more of a focus on specifically fouling up crime scenes and messing with investigators, whereas the Solar one...well it's got that too but there's just so many other charms that do other things.

                    Linguistics

                    Okay now HERE'S one that I can't say resembles the solar one very much. Solars have the market cornered here with language learning charms, and written and spoken social attacks.

                    The DB one's got half that, with a focus again on passions, and half Seal Team Kicks. It has charms for discreet information gathering, communicating instantly over long distances, communicating silently with your allies, and disguising missives. The capstone makes you and your allies fight as though they had one mind and will, seeing through eachother's eyes and defending eachother. That charm also gives double 7s..DOUBLE 7S...on a distract gambit's attack roll. My god this has the potential to make a group of terrestrials so scary.

                    Overall this is good. It has absolutely enough support for characters who just want to be great calligraphy artists and writers, and apart from that they're so useful to have as support on a mission they're borderline required.

                    Lore

                    Yeah, lore. Solar lore has a ton of charms for training, introducing facts, discovering facts, manipulating essence and forging the wyld into a design by force of your very will.

                    The Dragonblooded version is a really cool foil for it. It technically has a lot of what's above, but it does it in such a different way. Where Solars do things in a kind of secular way, it almost seems like lore for DBs is a religious experience. There's a certain reverence of knowledge, and linking it to the elements. Not only can DBs manipulate and transfer essence, but aura as well, and there's this big running theme that the lore master of the group is guiding and bolstering his allies. You don't have to take it this way, but a monk with a lot of lore is PERFECT.

                    Mechanically there's a lot of playing around with specialties and, of course, elemental manipulation for attacks. Bolt, Burst, Dragon Vortex, it's every Avatar's dream. Dragon Vortex alone deserves it's own paragraph. It's essence 5, but it lets you combine your powers with your ally's and become the ultimate embodiment of elemental fury. It's the kind of thing that can end an army or a city if used right.

                    I'm quite impressed here with Lore, I never would have themed it like this myself and I'm so glad they did.

                    Medicine

                    Thumbs up on Solar medicine, I've used it on many characters myself and have rarely been disappointed. Reattach limbs, treat disease, poison, damaged health levels.

                    The first thing you might notice about DB medicine is that it's not just about healing, it's about hurting. Using medical knowledge and essence to poison, numb, and infect your enemies. It's also got a great charm to treat mental illness, which, as Isator pointed out in his thread, his magical and useful, without making mental illness seem like a simple disease to be killed. You've also got an awesome charm for fixing crippling injuries, if your patient wants to their injury can be repaired by plant matter. This ranges anywhere from a bark patterned scar to basically a prosthetic grass arm.

                    I'm now picturing a formerly blind character with brilliant eyes that change colour with the seasons to match blossoming lilac flowers in the spring, brilliant leafy green in the summer, fiery reds and golds in autumn and deep brown bark in the winter.

                    That actually runs into a theme with medicine as a whole. Unlike with Solars, who tend to boost their own ability and use that to heal their patients, Dragonblooded just heal them by magic. This actually has a mechanical benefit even, the first couple wound restoring charms in both trees actually favour the DB for speed! Imagine that. You don't help them get better, you MAKE them get better, and now damnit.

                    It's got all the base competencies, and a lot of flavour besides, and how can anyone complain about having a charm which is in some ways more powerful than the Solar version?

                    Melee

                    We've talked enough about Solar melee.

                    Dragonblooded melee however, that needs more words. Starting with it's banishing to elsewhere charms, they let you pull a weapon out of the same element you stored it in, as though all fire in the world is spiritually the same to the Terrestrial, and even form a weapon out of pure stone or water or what have you. The teamwork is also pretty strong in this ability, using the finesse of melee to interpose for allies and even counter with a distract gambit. It might kind of relate to the subtle idea that the swordsman is also traditionally the leader character in mashup gangs like this. Just look at any sentai series.

                    It's also got that thing, which is a theme for fire in general, where it really likes having higher initiative than its opponents.

                    Mechanically it's pretty solid, with a few effects having some cool overt elemental manipulation like a large cloud of steam that gives allies initiative or grasping roots that make your weapon awesome. It's also got a really nice wide spread of aspects within it, letting anyone be a swordsman if they want to.



                    I think I might leave that there. Performance is going to be a doozy to read through and I probably won't even have all that much to say on it. I like how the DBs have enough options that relate to their element as to grant a boon while fighting in it, but not so many that you feel dumb not doing it all the time. It's also just an aggressive charmset overall, with trees like dodge dealing a lot of damage, integrity making you a lion to defend your intimacies, and medicine aggressively healing and hurting. I'll get to the rest of it this weekend though, this has been fun!

                        Going to do a quick on here just so I keep up with it

                        Occult

                        Now I played a Supernal occult Solar and as I recall it's really focused on a few things. It has an entire tree branch dedicated to this mandala of sealing/warding charm, a few charms to manipulate and fight spirits, and a couple others that boost sorcery, particularly mote collections.

                        The DB one dues have that, but they go about it in a different way. Whereas the Solar charms feel like you're just exerting your willpower over spirits, with charms that just bind spirits or allow you to see them, the Terrestrials feel more like they're using some kind of universal sorcery laws. Like instead of just warding a place against spirits they need to ring it with salt or germinated grain, or how they need a mirror to see spirits and a prayer strip to bind them.

                        It's also got this kind of interesting theme of connecting you more deeply with the world. It lets you sense expressions of the elements nearby, summon spirit dragons in the water, all sorts of stuff. Even if you're not a sorcerer this tree definitely makes you feel like one.

                        Their sorcery is also really cool, it's only two charms but they're much appreciated. Especially the one that allows them to gather sorcerers motes faster, in the case of Aura much faster, for an elemental spell. With this and one of those sorcerous rituals to draw more motes you're easily snapping off an attack spell every turn.

                        Overall I'm impressed, for all the mechanical overlap it does a great job of making it feel unique.

                        Performance

                        You ever play DnD? You like it? Like the Bard? Me too. Solars get some cool stuff that improves their ability to convince people with song, to make phantasmal backup dancers, and to improve their companions in combat.

                        Dragonblooded take the cake here though, they can cure disease, hide in plain sight, sing realistic illusions into existence. Solars might be really good at performing but terrestrials are straight up magic. The there ARE charms in here that boost performance rolls, usually related to either passion, authority, or shared intimacies. This would be another great ability for an immaculate monk character. I'm seeing a kind of a trickster monk who uses his song and speech to enchant a forest and the people who live there.

                        The thing is the actual performance rolls are kind of overshadowed by the cool other stuff. I don't even mind, it's awesome. You can rock out on a violin and shred physical attacks out of the bow, you can float through a forest. I mean it's just great. You can grab a bit of foliage and magically shape it into any instrument you want. I can't even list how many sweet sassy things you can do with this ability.

                        Final verdict? A+, but to be fair this was basically tailored for my sensibilities. I'm pretty sure Solars wish they had this tree though. Teamwork in this tree too numerous to mention.

                        Presence

                        Solar presence is interesting, it not only boosts your ability to personally affect people with the social system, it kind of lets you just empower them to do anything better. More courageous, more skilled, more loyal, more fearful.

                        The Dragonblooded themes of passion come to a head in this tree. It's got some pretty cool abilities to just skip a lot of the normal processes with this one, like turning lust into rage into giddy laughter. Which doesn't actually just let you skip the social scene, it just gives a fun shortcut if you can pull it off. There's also a lot of combat charms in here. There's ones for enhancing join battle, for giving yourself more soak and health levels, and even just straight deal damage in an area, among others. I think with presence alone you could almost be a decent combatant, almost.

                        There's also a kind of a running theme of loyalty here too. You see it a bit in the Solar tree but it comes out quite a bit harder here. It's actually to the point where some of these effects rival Solar charms. It's good though, you can take this in either a despotic forced Darth Vader enforcer route or a brotherly Captain America and Bucky style bond.

                        Resistance

                        Solar resistance has very little surprises. Apart from a berzerker rage section of the tree really everything is just about resisting damage and poison and stuff.

                        Dragonblooded do that too, but also their charms to stave off hunger and sleep and stuff are here, instead of Solar's integrity. I've also been waiting this whole review to link this, and here's the most appropriate time. It's got the 5 elements right and everything. It's also got some really cool mechanical effects like gaining hardness equal to your current initiative. It's also got more of that instant healing that we saw in DB medicine that Solars don't really get. Again I feel it's a virtue of rather than being a human ability taken to an extreme it's an utterly inhuman magic.

                        Short, simple, straightforward, but with some cool effects to set it apart.


                        I think that's all for today, but I'm continuing to like these comparisons. If all you knew about the terrestrial charmset was that they were weaker than Solars you might not even bother. Which is really too bad, considering DB performance lets you pick a dandelion, shape it into a giant yellow drum with leaf hammers, and beat out a tune of shock waves to defeat an army. Something else I'll notice is that the Solars tend to have a lot more dice trick charms, where as the DBs tend to just have charms that add more unique effects.

                        Also there's just a lot of incidental flavour to them. One of the resistance charms that heals you also bursts forth and heals all the wilting plantlife around you, the occult charms require sacred scripts to function, stuff like this really helps bring the charmset alive.

                        I might try to finish it off in the next one.
