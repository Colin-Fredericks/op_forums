                              Originally posted by Megapope
                              Because cowboys are preeeeeeeeeeetty played out in general.
                              Really, in cinematic terms, I think we've moved past the point of the Old West being played out, and there has recently been some interesting postmodern stuff that alternately analyses and deconstructs attitudes around it, or uses it as the setting of more contemplative, bleak, or just plain weird narratives. The kind of thing where the so-called frontier spirit is not depicted as a theatre for the American can-do spirit, of manly men and sassy women taming the wilderness, but of an edge of the world to which people who can't cope with society flee, and in which isolation breeds madness.

                              So I guess that, in the end, the Reavers were less of an analogy for Native Americans than they were the actual frontiers people.

                              (The Reavers as actually written are still totally a problematic analogy for Native Americans as narratively employed in older Westerns)

                              But that's all tangential to Exalted (except insofar as it might be inspirational to some stories in the less populous Directions, or just the rim of the world in general). The main thing is that the cowboy elements are really only cosmetic, and don't retain much of the cultural context, which I stated why I think it's distinct and interesting.

                              Really, in a fantasy fiction context, I think that part of why Mongols feel played out is because they're so often the go-to reference for a society of pastoral nomads associated with horses. Horses being basically the only thing that the Marukani have in common with the Mongols, and why I don't find them a good fit for overlaying.

                              At least insofar as Internet discussions go. That you're having fun with what you're doing in your game is the most important thing, practically speaking.

                                                  Originally posted by Lioness View Post
                                                  I like the aesthetic for individual characters, but when you're presenting an entire nation or culture drawing from such an well known and influential source leads people to half-read the text then fill in the blanks with what they're familiar with.

                                                  The Marukani soldiers in Compass Scavenger Lands look nothing like cowboys, but who honestly remembers that when everything about them otherwise screams cowboy?
                                                  Honestly, re-reading the description, there's bits where it's very cowboy ("they're masters of the whip and firewand, and never go anywhere without their cowhide hats"; "each town has a general store full of knick-knacks").

                                                  And then there's other bits where it's not all. Their towns have amphitheatres, their soldiers fight with lances or bows and wear Lookshy-made (so Japanese-looking) armour. Their elite soldiers are heavily-armoured cataphractoi with hammers. Their social organisation is all about clans.
                                                  And the Marukani girl in Castebook: Eclipse doesn't look like a cowgirl at all.

                                                  Also, cattle are mentioned 3 times... but it also mentions sheep, pigs and wheat.

                                                  I think the idea was, you know, not to make a sphaghetti culture of spaghetti Cowboys/Mongols/Samurai, but to take bits from all over the place and make a unique culture (which is a good idea).
                                                  But, as you say, as cowboys are a big influence on them, people take that and assume that's the whole thing.

                                                  Also, tbh, they don't have a lot of other interesting features to get past that, IMO (but maybe I'm biased; I consider Compass: Scavenger Lands the worst Compass of Terrestrial Directions).

                                                  I guess the same way as other places get labelled "Japan-land" or "Viking land".

                                                  (At least Medo was described by Holden as "Mongol Transylvania" so that's not just one thing.)

                                                  I don't know about Scavenger Sons (I don't own a copy), but Outcastes just calls them "horselords" and talks about their alliance, and how they provide horses to Lookshy.
                                                  This mostly makes me think of the Rohirrim, tbh.
