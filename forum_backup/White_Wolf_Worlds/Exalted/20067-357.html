        We're talking about those single story, boxes with a flat topped roof not that much higher than the top of a doorframe, right? Usually with rather smooth walls, but those wooden ceiling support beams jutting out around the eves.

        ​As a rule of thumb:
        • If you are able to do it without strain but it still takes time, it's difficult terrain.
        • ​If you are able to do it only with a bit of successful exertion, it's an Athletics check.
        • ​If doing it makes you look cool, and gives only a tad much of an advantage; it's a stunt.

        ​In this case, I'd be inclined towards the third since it doesn't give much of an advantage save perhaps for cover and high ground.

        ​Though if it's well-built villa rather than crappy shack, I might lean towards the second, as those might be a span taller and adobe walls are sturdy and smooth; so there's not much foothold beyond a small window square.

                            I would disagree with your gut there. There is no clear definition of the term spell effect, which leaves us with simple semantics. Both the hit and damage rolls are effects of a spell such as FotBR. In terms of the world, they are one and the same because even a spell that misses presumably does damage to something. The flames don't die down because they fail to hit the intended target. The distinction between the two is purely mechanical and Heaven turning Calculations has zero indication that it would apply to only one or make any distinction between different kinds of spell effects.

                            Considering it's a seven charm investment to get there, and none of those charms help you with being a sorcerer, I don't see any problem with it applying to both.

                                                            Originally posted by Maudova View Post
                                                            What breaks if all charm repurchases that relied on an essence or ability threshold for repurchase no longer required repurchase and were given at the target threshold?
                                                            A lot I'm afraid. There is no clear distinction between prerequisites and repurchases in terms of power. A few could as well be mini-trees, for example Destiny Manifesting Method or Supreme Masterwork Focus, because they essentially give you different charms, complete with cost and different effects. Others such as Ox-Body Technique are straight upgrades of the original in the sense that you simply gain more of the same. Yet others, such as Accuracy Without Distance, simply expand the scope of the original charm.

                                                            My impression is that, barring a few exceptions, repurchases were mostly used to save space. In the base game, there are only 27 charms that have one or multiple repurchases and a lot of abilities don't have any at all. I think it's best to think of repurchases as functionally different charms that were simply too similar to waste word count on.
