    Originally posted by Raemus View Post
    In my headcanon....

    The Sidereals are a mysterious group of NPCs that have some fate based charms, wield incredible Martial Arts, and a form of astrological sorcery that works via fate, but one of their most spectacular abilities is to borrow the powers of sponsoring Celestial Gods that sign on to their plots/designs. There is no "Forgetting them" nonsense, but they can weave new faces/lives/fates like the Resplendent Fates in the book. Though there is "red tap" it is no where near as restrictive as it is described. The Sidereals do work for the Bureau of Fate, but only about 50% of the time. The rest of their time is spent as one part Divine Socialite, one part Heavenly Mercenary, and one part Concerned Citizen of Creation (in which they engage in faction and personal activities). Thus whenever a Sidereal is discovered, it is difficult to know their motives because they may be working for the Bureau of Fate, themselves, or on behalf of a random celestial god or even the terrestrial hierarchies/courts.

    The Sidereal Factions of Bronze (55% though only 15% are true die hard supporters), Silver (25% they fully support the Lunar Exalted working the Thousand Rivers Method) & Gold (20%) all exist and Bronze is roughly equal in power level to the other two. Bronze & Silver are both losing members to the Gold, but the Bronze has the most senior positions of the Bureau of Fate, and the Greatest Masters of the Sidereal Exalted. The Silver Faction has been heavily oppressed since the Usurpation and though they have never been 50%, in the tumult before the release of the Solar Exalted their numbers swelled to the point where they could no longer be easily suppressed. The Gold Faction is very new, as the original Gold Faction were Usurpation protesters, but when the Solar Exalted never returned, the faction dwindled into a theoretical political science squabble until it practically disappeared.The resurgence of the Gold Faction has happened only since shortly before the Release, and is almost entirely made up of idealistic young Sidereals that don't know enough to be quiet or are too enthusiastic to care. They have begun to truly organize with one of the few Idealist Elders. They are heavily discriminated against by the Bronze Faction as the Bronze attempts to manage the "Solar Problem." This has allied the Silver & Gold Factions in many situations.

    I like this better because a less restricted Sidereal Exalted make for better antagonists or allies and when the same sidereal shows up working motives at right angles from the last time they were encountered has been fun. Also when they start whipping out the powers of Gods that the PCs have met, it gives me a good Club-by-Four that I can hit the PCs with if they haven't figured out whom the Sidereal was working for/with or have too many theories.
    Is there a reason a sidereal can only side with one particular faction? ... Like is there a reason that you can't side with the silver faction if you're also part of the gold? These aren't strict clubs we're talking about, these are differences in philosophy... though admittedly my information on Sidereals comes secondhand.

                Originally posted by OpMegs View Post
                When a mortal is fed upon by a member of the Fair Folk, most result in Dream Eaten, the listless, soulless husks that have little left of the humanity they once had. But there is arguably worse alternative that some of the Raksha Princes use. Rather than draining out all of a given Virtue, the clever Raksha removes only one and places a fragment of his Essence in its place. The resulting individual gains powers akin their sire, but is forever consumed with a hunger for the Virtue they lost. If they do not feed on this, they will slowly crumble away, too human to calcify, too Fae to become Dream Eaten, consumed by the inherent contradiction of their existence. These "changelings" are often used by Raksha Princes to uplift valuable mortal servitors, or simply to have an opinion at hand that comprehends the madness of the Creationborn as one native to it, yet is conveniently leashed by their hunger.
