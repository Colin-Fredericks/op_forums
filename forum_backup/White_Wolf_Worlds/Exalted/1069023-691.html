            Originally posted by I'm Ferrinus View Post
            I haven't read this whole thread, so sorry if these are repeats of other questions. Regarding the latest DB backer update:

            1) One of the Air anima powers lets you spend 3m to put a "-1 environmental penalty" on an incoming ranged attack. As far as I can tell, "penalty" means a change to a dicepool, not the number of successes, but other 3m powers for other elements generally allow you to swing some contest by one success, rather than one die, in your favor. Is the Air power meant to be weaker (maybe because "ranged attacks" is a broad category or something) or is it meant to be the equivalent of an "external penalty" from back in 2E?
            It subtracts one die.

            2) A number of DB combat charms use a single attack roll to determine the accuracy of multiple successive attacks, like that one that launches a arrow at one target which has the chance to explode and strike neighboring targets. What's the timing on secondary targets using reflexive defenses against this effect? Since there's no separate roll for the followup, it'd seem that they either have an extremely easy time using defensive magic to evade (e.g. you know the attack will have 7 successes exactly, so you just boost your Evasion to 8 exactly) or that they'd have to make some kind of blind bet as to whether the secondary will go off before the primary is even rolled. Or maybe they have to decide on their defenses ahead of time but only actually spend the resources and activate the charms if the explosion fires?
            With Blazing Phoenix Pinion, I'd have them declare it after the AoE triggers, but if lack of transparency becomes an issue, the ST could go with the latter approach.

            2a) The signature Fire Brawl charm uses a single attack roll to determine the accuracy of (1 + number of 10s rolled) attacks, and doesn't reset your initiative until all the attacks have been resolved, but reduces your initiative by one per point of damage the attacks do. I THINK I've understood it right, but I just want to make sure:

            I make the attack with 15 initiative and roll 5 successes and three tens. That means that I effectively strike four times for five successes each. My enemy has Parry 7, so my first attack misses, my second misses, my third hits (thanks to onslaught having built up to -2) and rolls 15 dice to deal let's say 4 damage, and then my fourth hits and rolls 11 dice to deal however much more damage, and then my init resets. And my target has two ignition points that'll detonate for 1 followed by 2 dice with later decisive strikes. Right?
            You've got it.

            If that's how it's supposed to go, does the target of such an attack have to use defensive magic on a per-attack basis?
            Yeah.

            Does magic that enhance my damage need to be activated separately for each attack?
            Yeah.

            Can I wait between the results of each attack to decide what I'll tack on to the next one, even though I know exactly how the roll will come out?
            Yeah.

                                Originally posted by Murcushio View Post

                                I second this question, although I'd clarify it more along the lines of "when, if ever, is clearly using your essence, whether it be Charms or Sorcery, to influence the situation a social faux pas?"

                                It's a widely held conceit that many "Charms" can function or be engaged without conscious decision-making on the part of the Exalt engaging them. That's always made a lot of sense to me and is super cool, and it makes even more sense in the current paradigm where "Charms" are much less of a thing anymore in-setting. A Dragon-Blooded with a ton of Socialize or Presence "Charms" might not know what they're doing; they just know they're very, very good at getting their way.

                                But you can still tell when someone is using their essence even if they haven't decided "I will engage this Charm I know that makes people do what they want." Among other things their anima will start to flare if it goes on long enough. Maybe that matters; "this guy is using his tricksy Exalted ways to fuck with my mind-meats." Maybe it doesn't; "this is a thing Dragon-Blooded do, of no more comment than them being able to jump over a house."

                                So I, too, would like to know if, say, you're given an impassioned speech in front of the Deliberative, or attempting to seduce a new lover, or close a deal, if "this person is flaring like a lighthouse" is regarded as a bad sign, a good sign, or a neutral sign.
                                As in all social interactions, the answer boils down to "it's complex, and that depends." Flaring your anima is an indication of significant effort—and, just like in the real world, the amount of effort that it's socially acceptable to put forward in a given situation is highly context-dependent. Letting your anima flare as you're giving an impassioned speech on the Deliberative floor might be seen as laudable; doing it while you're making eyes at a stranger from across a teahouse is probably gonna be seen as coming on too strong.

                                On top of that, there's the reputation of the individual Dragon-Blood in question. The Realm doesn't have a codified list of Charms, but a Dragon-Blood who uses Smoothing-Away-The-Past Technique at every possible juncture and isn't particularly subtle about covering it up is probably going to eventually develop a reputation as a socialite so skilled that her words can twist your memories. Folks are going to take that into account when the anima flares.
