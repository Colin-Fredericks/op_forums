    Combat Variant-- Players Roll All the Dice

    06-01-2020, 09:53 PM
    In my never-ending quest to make the game easier to GM, I found myself looking at NPC die pools. I've played around with reducing their size-- from 10d to 4d+3 successes-- but this time I found myself wondering "what if they never roll at all?" The combat system as it currently stands features an awful lot of back-and-forth, with opportunities for the player to interject at every stage. It seems to me that it might be simpler if players did all the rolling. Something like...
    • NPC declares an attack; player rolls Dexterity + (Dodge/Melee/whatever) against their attack DC. Effects that would boost defense by a flat +1 instead grant one auto-success on this roll. If she succeeds, she avoids the blow.
      • If the withering attack hits, the player rolls dice equal to her soak, minus one die per threshold failures on the previous roll, against the NPC's damage DC. She loses one Ini per threshold failure on this roll, to a minimum of the attacker's Overwhelm.
      • If the decisive attack hits, the player takes one level of damage per three Initiative the attacker had accumulated, rounding up. (Unless Hardness would block the attack)
    • For things like Rush and Disengage, the player would roll their pool against the NPC's combat movement DC.

    And... that's pretty much it. Players attacking NPCs would work normally. (Or you could have the players roll damage + threshold successes, subtract half the NPC's Soak, and that's the Ini damage). My feeling is that re-framing attacks this way would help new players remember when they can use Charms, simplify the back-and-forth involved in Soak, make it easier to coach new players through their turn, and just... overall feel smoother and more under the player's control. The main complication I can think of is dice trick Charms like Hail-Shattering Practice that depend on the foe's roll, but you should be able to apply them to your own Parry roll with no ill effect, I think?

    Anyway. Thoughts? Issues? Opinions? Angry shouting?

          You'll have to figure out how to deal with clashes though.
          For the soak:

          Let's say that Three Silk Stars is fighting a Blood Ape.
          • She has Dexterity 5, Stamina 3, Melee 4 and is wearing light armor for a final soak of 6.
          • The Blood Ape's die pools, converted to static DCs, would be Attack (Claw) 6 and Damage (Claw) 8

          The ape attacks. Silk rolls 9 dice to parry (Dex+Melee) and gets 4 successes. Compared to the ape's attack DC of 6, that's two failures, meaning she just got hit soundly. To determine how much withering damage she takes, she rolls (6 base soak - 2 threshold failures)=4 dice for soak. She gets 3 successes, not nearly enough-- she missed the damage DC by 5, meaning she takes 5 points of withering damage.

          Does that make more sense?

          -----

          NPC charms can be tweaked to either work based on the player's roll (instead of double 9s, maybe the player's 7s count as failures-- that works out the same, I think), or just by flat bonuses. I'm inclined towards the latter-- the aforementioned Blood Ape's Rending Claw Slash can become "+2 to the attack's damage DC, +3 if the target rolls 6 or more failures on their defense roll."

          Clashes shouldn't really need anything? The player rolls an attack against the NPC's attack DC; if she wins, she lands a hit and gets the extra damage.

                                  Honestly I don't think this would take too much conversion work as some others may think, but the reason 1e moved away from contested rolls was speed. Part of the problem in that is that battle lasted a long time until one side got really unlucky. My worry is that in 3e, which combat takes longer than the past editions on average, will really compound this with withering and decisive.

                                  Aside from making the resolution slower (more rolls) you also run into the problem of damage. Soak in Exalted has always been an issue. One can trivially outpace the damage one can belt out with soak in all but the most extreme cases (EX: Rampage Berserker Attack). This isn't the case in some issues, such as DnD where 99.9% of the time how well you hit is irrelevant outside of crits. Even in 2d20, a success based system, the *vast* majority of your damage comes from your base damage + attribute.

                                  So while you can do this, it would involve some final eyeballing of the math to get things right.

                                              3e has already moved away from PC/NPC rules symmetry more than previous editions with QCs, so I think something like this is a natural step further if you're into it. Treating the NPCs exactly like PCs (except run by the ST) is a convenience for the writer as much as anything, so they don't need two sets of mechanics. But if you're willing to put in the work there's no particular reason not to just turn NPCs into sets of static values and put all the randomness on the players.

                                              4e D&D embraced this asymmetry idea in a BIG way, and I think it was a strength in that sysyem (there were things I didn't like about that edition, but this wasn't really one of them). Fair warning though, the further you go down that path, the harder it is to do PVP effectively. Also it might require some extra suspension of disbelief in a world like Exalted where your most potent opponenrs are other Exalted and so, presumably, are more like yourself.
