            Originally posted by habitableexoplanet View Post

            Probably part of the problem is that those kind of narratives just don't grab me - at least not right now.

            This makes me feel like the solution to this part is very simple - just don't summon demons (at least not unless there's a very good reason...)
            That's certainly fair! Note, though, that by no means all demons are going to have the same kind of narrative difficulties. An angyalkae, for instance, has a much less violent problem than a blood ape: if it stops harping, it dies. If you summon one, you'll need to deal with constant background harp music if you keep it nearby, or put it somewhere and not have it as handy. Hopping puppeteers are pretty much fine as long as there are absolutely no babies near the construction site you want them to work on. And so on. The key to demon summoning, both in- and out-of-character, is to be aware of the limitations and quirks of the individual demons you're calling up, and only summoning those that you're prepared to deal with.

            Originally posted by habitableexoplanet
            Do I want to have my blood transform into giant scorpions that attack whoever shed it?
            Well, it's an example. It's handy for a sorcerer who's not very combat-focused, because it will make most opponents think twice before hitting you. But there are always other options. The point is, they all have complications and side effects, it's just a question of choosing the ones you're okay with.

            Originally posted by habitableexoplanet
            For instance I was wondering about the dry well, which already seems on the low end for the difficulty and danger of problems for a group of Solars.
            Sure, for a Solar Circle, that's probably a bit trivial. On the other hand, Terrestrial workings are pretty easy to toss off for even a starting Solar sorcerer, and it's worthwhile to consider all the tools in your toolbox. And another good thing about workings, particularly Terrestrial ones, is that they're an openly supernatural way of problem-solving that doesn't announce your nature as a Solar Exalted. There are lots more sorcerers in the world than there are Exalted, and while a sorcerer coming through your town and doing something for you is by no means an everyday occurrence, it's got to be more common than any Exalt except possibly a Dragon-Blood doing so. If you want to go around helping people or solving long-term problems supernaturally, but don't want to broadcast "I'm a Solar, bring on the Wyld Hunt!" to the winds, workings are damn handy.

            Originally posted by habitableexoplanet
            Theoretically whichever complications arise from the working should be less difficult than the original problem, but still significant enough to be worth spending time on.
            Yeah, that's definitely true. As an ST, my approach is always going to be the least dickish one possible, so making the complications and consequences worse than the problem the working solves is out as far as I'm concerned. They're there to lead to interesting stories and provide cool flavor for sorcerers.

            That said, another useful tip for creating interesting complications is to make the potential problem fairly serious, but the probability of that problem actually manifesting quite low. Using the well example again, if the working binds a water elemental into it to provide an endless supply, that might work fine, with no drawbacks at all - but if the arcane binding sigils you carved into the stones at the bottom of the well to permanently bind the elemental are ever seriously damaged, the binding could break, and the elemental rampage through the village. But who's going to swim to the bottom of the nice fresh well and chip at some markings in the rock?


            Originally posted by habitableexoplanet
            If I think being a Solar is superpower enough, am I missing out on something?
            As others have pointed out, there are quite a number of superpowers that simply aren't within the power of the Solar Exalted, but that sorcery can provide. If none of them interest you, fair enough, but I do feel there's a lot of cool stuff that workings can give you.

                Originally posted by habitableexoplanet View Post
                Should I want to do those things? I feel like doing those is mostly going to give the ST trouble not to make challenges that are rendered irrelevant by those powers.
                One piece of advice I suggest for STing Exalted, but that is also very relevant to players, is not to worry so much about deriving narrative tension from whether you will accomplish your goals or not. You're Exalted, you are probably going to achieve most of what you set out to do. Instead, try to get narrative tension from the consequences of your success. The question is not "can I do this thing?", it's "given what will happen if I succeed, should I do this thing?" Ending slavery in your city frees the slaves - and now what happens when the economy needs to pay wages for work previously done for free? How will the Guild react? What happens to the economies of the settlements that were providing slaves? All of those are things you now need to deal with.

                To that end, gaining new cool powers from sorcerous workings (or summoning demons and elementals) aren't really making it more difficult for your ST, because you were probably already capable of stomping whatever problems they cared to put in your way. Instead, you're just giving yourself extra ways to stomp the problem, with different, interesting possible consequences for stomping the problems in that particular way. Really, you're actually opening up more useful sources of narrative tension by using these powers.

                That's how I look at it, anyway.

                                                            Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post
                                                            The biggest problem with just picking up a ton of unrelated and epic powers from all over the place, I think, is that you make anything that's not sorcery a bit more lame.
                                                            It's important with sorcery, and the powers granted by it, to note that they do come with costs and drawbacks of their own. A supernatural power granted by a working isn't necessarily free to use - it can have costs in terms of motes, willpower, health levels, or other resources. Or, in the case of passive abilities like the "blood turns to scorpions" one, the potential for accidental dangerous activation exist. And sorcery in general is weird. The more you use it, I think, the more the sorcerer and her targets should change, in ways that tend to be unsettling and off-putting for most inhabitants of Creation. I think that by the time you get your third or fourth supernatural ability via working, you should probably be making children cry, dogs bark, and peasants reach for their pitchforks and torches when you walk into town. And, of course, every working has an XP cost, which means that a sorcerer doing this will be spending less on other stuff (I generally let a PC who benefits from a working be the one who pays the XP cost of the working, but that just shifts the cost to them from the sorcerer, not eliminates it).

                                                            Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey
                                                            You could play a Sidereal to get cool fate powers, or you could just be a sorcerer and grand yourself fate powers. You could be an epic infiltrator, or you could sorcerously give yourself the best infiltration abilities there is in a shorter time.
                                                            In the first case, you probably could give yourself Fate powers, but they'd never be as versatile and flexible as a Sidereal's, and I would certainly have a consequence of such a working be that you're now a big red flag on the Loom and to general Sidereal tracking abilities. Expect a lot more visits from the Wyld Hunt, backed with fate ninjas. In the second case, I generally wouldn't allow supernatural powers to completely supersede a niche, without spending similar amounts of XP. So the ability to perfectly disguise yourself (equivalent to Flawlessly Impenetrable Disguise) would probably require at minimum two different Celestial Ambition 2 workings to achieve (since each of those will cost 4 XP, and thus total the 8 XP that buying a single charm at Favored cost would).
