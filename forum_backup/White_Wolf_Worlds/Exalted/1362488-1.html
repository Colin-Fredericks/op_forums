                    And also to get back on topic:

                    We have a tiered system for Craft in our games. If you just want to craft just one thing, like swords then you follow the rules as written. If you want to be a more general crafter you can pick something more encompassing for x2 cost per dot. If you want to be the omni-crafter you can take Craft: All for x3 cost. This rule came about because Craft tends to be enough of an XP sink as is considering the bloat of its charm tree.

                        Retroactive Dragon Experience.

                        So no character can have more than (Sessions played ×4) Dragon Experience. But sometimes you just can't shoehorn an Aspect bonus in, and other times you might hit every point in the Aspect box, and play up the Great Curse and cede a scene. So if you don't earn the full amount of Dragon Experience one session, you can make it up at some point in the future.

                        This is especially noted for Air Aspects who get DX for achieving long term plans.

                                            And to get in topic:

                                            Doing it from 2e I forgot to add my voice to all of the people that just use XP instead of BP at character creation, and use flat costs for everything. We also generally ignore training times... just makes all that easier.

                                            Something that's not quite a full house rule, because it's still in flux: When using the full sail rules, PCs and major NPCs all get one action before the captain of their ship makes a roll.
