        My minimal math method would be to get a quick stateline from each of your players that has their attack, damage, defense, and Join Battle at minimum, but you might as well get their social influence stuff with their resolve, guile, appearance, per+Socialize, and whatever their best social pool is.

        Using the “2 dice = 1 success” rule of thumb, you can use those numbers to compare to the attacks and defenses of an antagonist and go “On average, they’ll be able to hit this guy no problem” or “they’ll need to pump essence to hit this guy” or “ok, they can hit this one no problem, but she has enough soak they’ll only do their Overwhelming damage” and so on. Same for their defenses.

        Granted it’s more work than the challenge rating system, but you really can’t just go “oh they’re essence 3 now, I better get a group of essence 2 opponents together.” Exalted is highly customizable. I’m actually working on an Essence 5 Solar who can successfully take out an army but will get spanked in single combat with a skilled mortal. That’s just not something you can do in Pathfinder, because of how the base attack bonus works.

        I’ll grant that there are lots of ways you can get various bonuses and methods for leveling the playing field in Pathfinder (I had a level 4 Ranger with a +17 stealth bonus) but that game is set up so that the playing field can only be leveled so much. A low level Rogue just really can’t deal enough damage to one shot a high level Fighter and, once they’re out of stealth, the Rogue is dead.

        Conversely, in Exalted, if you have a high Join Battle and Stealth pool and your opponent’s Awareness sucks, you stand a decent chance of assassinating them with a single big Decisive on the first turn, regardless of who’s Exalted and who’s not, let alone your relative essence levels. There are enough ways of approaching Exalted and methods to force your opponent to fight you where you’re strong and they’re not that you can’t just assume “Oh, I’m an Essence 5 Solar therefore I win the game.” That line of thought will get you killed.

                    Surprised PneumaPilot didn't mention that Liminals made their Debut in the penultimate 2nd edition book, Masters of Jade which featured a Liminal on the cover, with additional art and a namedrop on page 50 for Dame Crimson. She and her Guild associate Casim Thurat can also be seen in the Ex3 core on page 86.

                    There's a passage referencing them on MoJ page 43, in regards to fighting a Lunar, in a classic comic book case of confused heroes fighting each other for the same cause-
                    "Eventually, the Guild, citing the power of the
                    ghosts and their champion, enlisted the aid of one of the
                    Liminal Exalted. As the two champions did battle under
                    the darkness of a moonless sky, neither could gain the
                    advantage. But the Liminal soon realized that it was the
                    Guild that had brought death to the region, and it was
                    only by the Guild’s defeat that the ghosts would return
                    to the lands of the dead. Showing no signs of flagging,
                    the Chernozem withdrew from battle and returned to
                    the Underworld to face the judgment of his dark mother."

                    While looking through the Core for additional appearances, I finally realized one of the art pieces I wasn't really fond of on page 343 actually has Shen using Solar healing charms on Dame Crimson. I'm no longer convinced that is her own arm he is sowing on to her. This art piece has now endeared itself to me a little more.

                    I also hadn't realized until now that their excellency cap jumps to Attribute+Essence when their anima is flaring.

                    Another fun little quirk I think is of note, is that the process of creating a liminal distinctly parallels to the process of making an Alchemical exalted, what with the mortal labor of creation, and needing the divine intervention of a higher power (Dark Mother for Liminals, while AutoC doles out some Demiurge).

                    Just to be clear, I'm not trying to be the forum canon police, just wanted to further the conversation.

                    Really enjoyed this episode, and I'm glad you guys got on the Liminal band wagon. While it may seem like all the new Exalt types can feel shoe-horned or extraneous, I really like that it opens up the setting even further. Things started to really feel paint by numbers in late 2e, where you would just spin the wheel or reference a random table to decide what the powerhouse in the area was, but breaking up the rigid pre-ordained cosmology has felt so freeing.
