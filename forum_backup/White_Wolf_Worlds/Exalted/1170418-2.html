    And multiple different ones in the same charmset. My players found remembering 35 charms hard enough as-is, but they found the dice trick ones by far the hardest to remember.

    This is the sheet I wrote for the Twilight when he was making artefacts:
    20 dice

    Flawless Handiwork Method (re-roll 10s)

    Bright-Forging Prana (1/story):

    [Supreme Masterwork Focus x3 (double 7s)

    Experiential Conjuring of the Void (1 success and 10 extra dice, all non-charm)

    Unbroken Image Focus (5 extra successes plus 1 per success rolled before double 9s, all non-charm)]

    So, in conclusion:
    Roll 20 dice

    7s, 8s and 9s are each worth 3 successes, 10s are worth 4. For each 10, roll another dice.

    Then roll another 10 dice with double 7s (so they're just worth 2 each this time). For each 10, roll another dice.

    Now add 6 successes

    Now take away 4 (because the roll is difficulty 5)

    You need 100 total, and can't roll more than 6 times.
    And that's not even working out how much gold and silver xp it cost to do that.

    Way too complex. Double Xs is fine, but when you've got a whole bunch of different effects on the same roll, the players just don't remember.
    I'm not going to be upset if DBs have a few charms that say "double 9s on such-and-such" or even "re-roll 1s" as long as they just stick with 1. Or 2.



    Ironically, in some ways, I understand why they did so many different ones in Solars. First, Solars are supposed to be the most powerful in their area of expertise, and are simultaneously most human, and thus are most likely to be rolling an attribute+ability really, really well. Whereas DBs or Lunars are just going to roll it a bit better than a mortal would (but maybe do something inhuman with it, like throw fire at someone).
    Also, of course, it was the first book, so they probably wanted to try them all.



    Some people have suggested cutting half the charmset out. I can see the logic, but I'd rather just move all the dice tricks into their own trees. Then people who like them, like Blaque, can still buy them, but you don't havee to.

The whole concept of conveyance in design is to provide the players with simple concepts they can easily internalize, so that they can more easily engage with the complexities of interactions of those simple concepts.
