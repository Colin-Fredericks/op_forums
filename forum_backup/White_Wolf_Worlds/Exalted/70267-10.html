                I meant to quote Kunoichi earlier, in my big post, but the forum software wisely decided I was quoting someone else. Anyway, this was for you, Kunoichi:
                Why thank you.


                It seems many people want to be able to treat larger groups. Just remember that execution will be limited to how it pushes realism in Creation. There won't be a Charm to make your nurses just as effective as you, or a Charm to wander across a battlefield Essence-zapping people back to life. Solar Charms thrive on a logos of action and dramatic effect. This is what allows them to be so powerful. Taking away the need for logos does make them more powerful, but it also makes them into miscellaneous wish-powers, which by turns makes them uninteresting, boring, and disappointing. So my challenge here will be to make it so that you can luxuriate in the extremely difficult challenge of triage, rather than just hope your Athletics and Awareness Charms can get you where you need to go fast enough and your Medicine Excellency will help you when you get there. A large part of this is going to be on you, and the Storyteller. Making things interesting requires a little elbow grease and creative use of the system and Abilities at large. No Charm, no matter how well written, will ever suffice.
                Yes, I totally agree that just using a charm to clap your hands and sunburst-heal a battlefield would be an impressive power, but a kind of dull one. I can think of a couple different ways off of the top of my head (maybe the middle of my head since I've pondered this before).

                1) The Solar has her patients gathered and continually moves between them, both observing their conditions and intervening medically when necessary. It could be limited to a number of patients or could be limited geographically (as in, anyone within your move distance). Heck, if you did it the second way, then the Solar could keep it up for longer than the typical hour and have new patients brought in once the previous ones are stabilized or cured. Granted, this could leave it open for abuse by untiring Solars becoming a medical factory.

                2) Second idea, one I actually just thought of would be to have the Solar begin treatment of someone, then they have an assistant take over and the Solar's healing continues (for the hour or whatever) while the Solar can leave and do other things, potentially visiting others and healing them. I imagine it would work via committed motes for the assistant and the Solar could keep visiting new patients and assigning "nurses" to them as long as she wants to keep motes committed (assuming committed motes still exist). I actually like this idea quite a bit as it ensures that the Solar sees each individual patient and it sort of seems like reality with magic.

                            Originally posted by Limited Reagent View Post
                            It might be cooler if there were Charms that many not perfectly cure a patient instantly and right then, but always told you a way how: a special plant, a secret technique, whatever -- something out there, if put into the supernal hands of a Solar medic, would have the capability to cure what would otherwise be incurable. Better adventure fodder that way.
                            I have a major problem with these kinds of effects.

                            Here's the issue: You've basically just described a "find plot" charm. That is to say that the only real effect a charm like this has is to ask the ST to give you the plot.

                            In practical terms it does nothing. If nobody in the circle had any medical knowledge, and a vital character was sick, the ST would give us a way to go on a quest to save them regardless -- we wouldn't be using our own personal medical knowledge in any way, but if it were important to the players or the overarching plot, we would be able to go somewhere and find something or someone that would at least give us a chance at success. Don't say that it shouldn't work like that, or that the problem is STs who run things like that; that's how it does work and everyone knows it. Saying "no, what you want is totally impossible" is boring when you can say "here is an epic quest to attempt that!" And because setting you on an epic quest is something that every ST is going to want to do and is going to always be trying to do, you should never charge XP for a charm whose only effect is to provide the initial direction that sets you on a quest.

                            If you doubt it, consider this: What would a non-Solar circle do -- a circle of some splat that lacks these sorts of medicine charm -- if they had someone with a horrific disease who needed curing? I can tell you what they would not do: Give up and shrug because you need Medicine to even attempt an epic quest to cure someone. So... what exactly does a Twilight doctor contribute to the situation, then? You might tell the doctor ,in my previous example, "well, you didn't buy the plot-hook finding charm, so Tiny Tim dies", but you obviously wouldn't say that for a circle that never had the option to buy that charm... and since most players are going to be aware of this, it makes the charm feel like a waste of XP at best.

                            In my experience -- I've played games with powers like those before, powers that are basically "summon plot hook." And every single time, even when I used them, it left me with a very unsatisfied taste in my mouth. Why did I take that charm? Everyone at the table is aware that I just wasted XP and motes (or whatever the resources were in that game) to get what the ST was going to give us for free anyway. Why didn't I spend that on something useful? Leaping over mountains and cutting the tops off mountains are cool because they are tools that I, as a player, can assemble to find my own solution to tasks; they interface with the world in a dynamic fashion, in a way that leapfrogs off my other powers (and the contributions of the rest of the table) to face and overcome challenges.

                            "Enable epic quest" charms are worthless. You don't need a charm for that.

                            (This doesn't mean that your medicine charms have to be able to cure everything, of course. I'm just saying that "point you in the direction of a plot device" is not, itself, a meaningful power worth spending XP on; it could be a flavorful side-effect to a charm whose practical mechanical applications are elsewhere, maybe when you activate it for something it can't cure. But it shouldn't be considered a meaningful part of a charm's value, because practically speaking most of the time it just reflavors the source of a plot hook you would have encountered anyway.)

                                            02-13-2014, 12:26 AM
                                            Regarding some of what people are saying about Medicine's "scale", it might be helpful to think about how other abilities can have a major impact on the world first.

                                            War can dramatically reshape the direction of nations. Personal combat abilities can contribute to that, and can also be used to shut down almost any other magical power by murdering it at its source. Diplomacy and persuasion powers can change the fate of the world by altering the minds of leaders, or by (if more slowly and with more difficulty) influencing crowds and populations and so on. Even if 3e is scaling things down from the ridiculousness of Taboo-Inflicting Diatribe, I imagine that it will be quite doable to start a riot that can change the face of a city overnight.

                                            Crafting and sorcery are infrastructural powers whose scope doesn't really require any elaboration.

                                            So what about medicine? Well, in previous editions (excluding a few oddball charms) it's mostly been "you can cure an important person if they happen to be sick" -- which is much, much more situational (and tends to fall slightly into that 'solution looking for a problem' thing, in that even if the ST throws you a bone and gives you an important sick person to cure, you're probably going to be well-aware that this is just them deliberately creating a problem that wouldn't exist otherwise just for you to solve it -- which is never as satisfying as using words and warfare to carve your own legend on the world according to your will.)

                                            Medicine and disease are hugely important things in terms of shaping human history, but the problem that I'm seeing (based on what hatewheel is saying about the direction he's looking for above) is that most of the really major medical effects in the real world are infrastructural. Alexander Fleming and Jonas Salk may have saved more lives than even the most brutal of generals has taken, but practically speaking their advances were able to spread because of a huge and intricate social infrastructure... and if the game treats it like that, then most of the major wide-scale impact Medicine has had in the real world is outside of the reach of Medicine charms.

                                            Which is, I mean -- well, obviously "cure polio" and "invent penicillin" can't be available as one charm activation. But at the same time, when a few War and Melee guys can win a major battle, or your Zenith, Night, and Eclipse can work together to start an uprising that overthrows a major city -- the Medicine guy needs powers that contribute.

                                            Maybe part of this is that Medicine doesn't automatically play well with other powers. When there's a major battle, the Dawn does tactics and the Zenith and Eclipse do propaganda / morale and the Night caste assassinates enemy generals... sorcerers hit them with sorcery and anyone with information-charms (lore, investigation, awareness, etc) can get information. What does the Medicine guy do? Medicine has, up until now, been mostly calibrated at a level where it cannot contribute meaningfully to a major battle like that (yes sure you can say you're healing people and the ST will nod and this will, compared to the dramatic efforts of everyone else, accomplish exactly nothing.)

                                            Pretty much any other major task that isn't directly about healing the sick nobleman is going to play out exactly the same way. You want to overthrow the city government? The Night sneaks the Dawn in to murder key people, the Zenith and Eclipse do propaganda on crowds or key officials, sorcery and the things made by a crafter are still good at everything, information from Lore / Investigation is still vital, and the medicine guy... hopes someone has a sick daughter they can cure?

                                            Medicine is narrow and specific and mostly limited to small numbers of individuals and, collectively, this means it usually has very little to contribute compared to most of the other abilities. So I would tend to say that Medicine charms ought to scale up to a certain extent -- enough to influence a noticeable area in a city or a battlefield. The mechanism doesn't have to be (and shouldn't be) "wave your hands and cure a bunch of people", but something like recognizing key vectors that can be used to cause or prevent outbreaks of--

                                            Bleh. You know what? Even when I put it like that, that still doesn't look like enough. Even with the broad application of Medicine where, I don't know, your medical guy figures out how to poison a well, or... keep your well from being poisoned? It's still a very narrow and limited contribution compared to pretty much everyone else in the scenarios I outlined. And it already feels a bit forced, in the "let's find something for the medicine guy to do" sense.

                                            I know that this isn't in the cards, but I am honestly, seriously wondering if maybe the game would be better without Medicine as an ability -- rolling it into Lore (yes, I know it does too much already) or Occult or Craft or something. I know there are people who enjoy focusing on it, and I'm sure it can be fun in the right game, but it is too specific and too narrow at what it does, and this is a fundamental problem with its definition, not something that can be solved by introducing clever charms.

                                            Anyway, sorry I couldn't be of more help, but I hope I at least shed some light on the issues Medicine faces and why so many people are desperately begging for a way to apply it on a larger scale.

                                                Having said that, I can contribute a little bit: Rasputin managed to almost take over a government using medical knowledge. The medicine peddler from Mononoke (who used his knowledge of medicine to do things like win the trust of nobles and the like so he could carry out his mission) also comes to mind. A good doctor in a circle can be useful to build up a reputation that will give the Eclipse access to places where they wouldn't have before -- running a scheme where the Twilight Doctor takes on increasingly high-class patients and earns reputation as a miracle worker so the Eclipse and Night caste can infiltrate their families, for instance.

                                                So perhaps Medicine would benefit from charms that allow you to find things that are wrong with people at a distance (by glancing at them or even just hearing accounts), charms to help convince people to accept treatment, charms that make patients more dependent on you and which attract more patients in turn, charms that allow you to invoke your reputation and authority as a doctor...

                                                I mean, is it treading on social charms a bit? Sure. But I think there's justification. Being a good doctor (especially in a setting like this) is not simply about surgery. You need to be able to project a sense that you know what you're doing when it comes to medicine, and you need to be able to make people go along with it. The Ultimate Doctor might not be able to work a crowd like the Zenith or negotiate a treaty like the Eclipse, but they should be able to convince a noblewoman that her daughter needs treatment right now and that only he can cure her -- that's the kind of social authority that, I think, someone who is playing the Greatest Doctor Who Ever Lived can reasonably expect to be able to invoke without needing too much from other abilities.

                                                Anyway, I think looking at Rasputin as a Twilight miracle-worker who used his capabilities as a doctor to win the trust of the royal family might help -- give Medicine charms that can actually force that situation to an extent, rather than just hoping the royal family's son is sick and that they'll maybe be grateful to you for saving them.

                                                            Originally posted by Aquillion View Post
                                                            Many wonderful things about Medicine and scale.
                                                            All of these are great points! I don't know if getting rid of medicine completely is the best idea (or even possible at this stage) but what I take away from this is that Medicine Charms should be useful in more situations that mending a random diseased jerk, especially when the circle tackles a big problem. Something like a patch healing charm that gives a buff when that patient acts on an intimacy (possibly one given by the circle's eclipse/zenith!). A charm that turns a disability, like a magic penalty or a disease or a missing arm, into a short term bonus to combat. Drugs or treatments that calms breathing and heartbeats, allowing the patient to be sneakier as well as preserving her life.

                                                            The Circle should FEEL your contribution to your goals. Medicine charms should have just as much impact and weight as combat charms. As a bit of a sketchy example, I have a 4eDND druid who's primary role in combat is to drop enemy defenses by 7 and give them vulnerabilty to damage. Debuff, debuff, debuff! My party members love to hit when they roll an unlucky 6, and their faces light up when they get to add more damage because of me. I don't do much damage myself, but when I'm not around my party's tactics and strategies change. Likewise, out of combat, I am able to sneak into places that the rest of my party can not because of my shapeshifting. Much of the time I can take them with me. Again, how my party deals with their problems changes. More brute force, less finesse. They feel it when I'm there and feel it when I'm gone.

                                                            As of 2.5ed Exalted, not having someone with Medicine charms around means that they heal slower and lean heavier on defensive charms. Maybe they have to dip into resistance a bit. If somebody loses a limb, the Circle can probably go to another God or Exalt to fix the problem. A healer does not impact a Circles plans like someone who can summon demons or sneak does. Sure, they get to shine whenever a plague or poison shows up, but the ST has to go out of their way to make that happen. I would like a Medicine specialist to have as much impact on how a circle deals with their problems as the Awareness specialist, or the Melee specialist, or the Presence specialist. They should feel it when the healer is there and feel the sting when she is gone. They should branch out from just fixing injuries and curing diseases whenever it is plot appropriate and have the possibility to contribute meaningfully (but not overwhelmingly) to more situations. The Sail charms seem to have the right idea, imo.

                                                            Again, not too sure how feasable this is for the core, but this is my medicine structural wish list.
