                                            04-30-2018, 02:25 AM
                                            Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post
                                            First Age artifice isn't necessarily overwhelmingly powerful. Something like power armor, that's probably Artifact 3. The reason it's First Age artifice isn't because it blows regular artifact armor out of the water, but because Creation isn't a setting where major powers field legions of powered armored troops.
                                            Sorry if this sounds lawyer-y, but I'm trying to lay most of things down nice and sort of pretty.

                                            If I get things right, First Age artifice is not a power consideration to limit game-breakingness, but a setting one to limit setting-breakingness. So technically, we could First Age Artifacts at low dots for relatively pedestrian Artifacts that would corrupt the setting by being widespread among Exalted and assorted Essence-users (like say, some of the real world equivalents that cropped up in 2e), the same way we have non-First Age Artifacts at 5 or even N/A for insanely powerful things that would make sense to be re-created in-setting by a sufficiently prepared and gifted artificer (thinking of LotR's One Ring).

                                            Kind of like rows and columns in a matrix: of course most First Age will be in the "very powerful" column, but it's not a necessity.

                                                            04-30-2018, 09:38 AM
                                                            1. If gods do not gain power from worship, why do they want prayers?
                                                            A) They do gain a measure of power, as depicted in the Cult background.

                                                            B) Receiving worship is an extremely enjoyable experience for gods.

                                                            C) In the event they lose their position in the celestial bureaucracy and then get killed, having a cult can allow them to reform after death.

                                                            2. The Lookshy section of WFHW describes their airships as decrepit and mothballed. That seems to imply neglect. Is that the right impression?
                                                            Not willfully neglected, but worse for the wear of centuries, which is beyond the prowess of Dragon-Blooded artificers to fully mend.
