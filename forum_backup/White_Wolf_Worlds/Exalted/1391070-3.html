    Yeah, definitely. Non-combat Solars tend to have very high combat dice pools but hardly any combat charms beyond the excellency, and as they have very little else to spend motes in combat on, they throw out big excellencies on all their rolls/defences.

    This isn't always the case, but I find it to be the case 80% of the time. When it's not, it's a deliberate choice.
    I deliberately played a character with Dex 2 and 1 dot of Brawl, who then learnt Martial Arts up to 5 over the campaign as a character arc, and that was kind of fun, but even considering that our ST tends to throw very easy combat encounters at us (well, for our level), he was a total chump at combat compared to his companions. And that's why he lost an arm, an eye and several toes. It would have cost very little in the way of starting resources to be way, way better.

Basic supernatural opposition. I don't know about you, but that suggests 5 combat Charms makes for a generally capable mid-range combatant, not an OP Warrior-King.

        Let's look at the example character, the "terrifying assassin." According to the text, the player's intention is to make him "good at sneaking around and killing, as well as competent in a straight-up fight (emphasis mine). At the end of the process, he has Dex 5, Melee 4, a Melee specialty, a third of his Merit points spent on combat traits, and 6 combat Charms (8 if you count Blinding Battle Feint and Stalking Wolf Attitude). This is identified as a "stealth-based fighter--an unusual approach." Not a master martial artist, not a samurai champion, not a barbarian warlord. There is, in fact, nothing to suggest that Otso's 2 attack, 3 dodge, and 1 resistance Charm will make him into a creation-shaking warrior god. Can you really blame me for pointing my players to similar spreads?

        But look-- the problem I've run into isn't in the power of individual Solars. Hell, just yesterday, I was testing a potential houserule by running a Fair Folk Cataphract against a fresh Solar with very similar stats to Otso (Dex 5, Martial Arts 5, and 5 combat Charms-- 2 offensive, 2 defensive, 1 dual-role) and ran him with 30m to simulate a character who'd done some stuff before fighting. And it was a real fight, and if I'd continued for more than a few rounds it might have been a losing one. My guy smacked the Cataphract a couple times with Motion of Myriad Legs for some real pain, but burned through his motes real fast trying to keep up with his foe's 16d to attack and Parry 7.         The problem is that three copies of my character verses that same Cataphract make mincemeat out of him without being scratched. That five copies can splatter Ahlat without feeling pressured. That's not an issue with character power; that's an issue with a system that assumes a fairly large number of players (a five-Solar circle) will generally be outnumbered by foes with real mechanical complexity... and without offering so much as a scrap of advice hinting at that or suggesting ways to build significant encounters.

                            Then you'll be not very good at combat. If you want a strategy on how to do that and not feel bad, first take every non-combat charm and ability you can, until all your points are spent, and then start cutting things out in order to take combat stuff. Even then, consider taking things like Call the Blade/Summoning the Loyal steel. Also spend all your merit points before taking combat artifacts.

                            That way you actually have to tell yourself "No, my character does not have the ability to run straight up vertical surfaces like an epic fantasy ninja, I choose to cut that power out in order to take Excellent Strike." then you go "And, neither does he have the ability to run across water, or up falling lotus leaves as he dashes through the moonlight, instead he has One Weapon Two Blows." and you might end up saying "Okay, I could take Peony Blossom Attack, but then I don't even have Graceful Crane Stance to let me perfectly balance on ropes or tree branches, and that's really cool so fuck it, Melee 2 and two melee charms are where I'm at."

                            Also consider diversifying your combat ability. If your character is a knife fighter, and you only have melee, you can't do any throwing knife stuff. Also if he's supposed to be a street fighter, what happens when you have no knife, or get disarmed? Brawl 0 makes you worthless in the fight without a weapon, is your character the kind of fighter that surrenders as soon as they get disarmed? If not, get at least a bit of Brawl.

                            Likewise look at things like Integrity. Is your character easily swayed? If not, get at least some integrity. Is your character really open for having their intimacies read? If not, get at least some socialize. Does your character have any hobbies like playing an instrument or riding? Well get at least a dot or two there. Is your character even a little bit personable? Probably grab a dot of presence then.

                            Of course, two dots in something represents a professional mortal, but at the same time your Solars are not going up against professional mortals. Integrity/socialize 1-2 might be okay if a minor mortal courtier is trying to sway your character, but a Fair Folk Lorelei will read you like a book and have your intimacies as putty in her hands.

Remembering everyone who didn't take dodge and is wearing heavy armor is making that roll of straight (Dexterity - 2) dice.


                                                At no point there are you running a combat with more than three Storyteller characters, and never more than two with motepools. It's only three scenes which is relatively short, but you'd still need to be pretty darn optimized in your whole group if you get to that final fight with enough resources left to just clown it. Especially if your characters just get fed up with those battlegroups and blow a ton of motes just to burn them down fast.
