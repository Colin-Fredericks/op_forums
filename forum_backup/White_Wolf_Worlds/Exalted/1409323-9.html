                                    09-04-2020, 05:40 PM
                                    I honestly never envisioned I AM being a computer as such. I AM is a person, who speaks out of objects like someone on the other end of a two-way speaker. A person with vast knowledge of almost every subject, perfect memory, and the ability to hold an absurd number of simultaneous conversations and relay information quickly between one conversation to another. He makes a great telephone, except a telephone conversation routed through I AM is going to be like those conversations on sitcoms where two people are mad at each other and not on speaking terms, but are each speaking through a friend who can still speak to both of them. "Tell Hellen to pass the salt." "Tell Peter he can reach for it himself."

                                    What he doesn't make is a good graphics card. There aren't I AM websites. There's just a voice that knows a ton of stuff, is good at doing math and reciting encyclopedia articles, and who knows everyone in the world like, at the very least, a familiar acquaintance and sometimes like a close friend.

                                    There's probably screens and a text interface only inasmuch as there would need to be to let him communicate with folks with hearing or speaking impairment.

                                    So, like, he's definitely social media! You can use him to keep up with what your friends are doing, as long as your friends are telling him what they're doing and have told him it's okay for him to relay that info to you. And he knows almost everyone so he can definitely introduce you to someone on the other side of Creation who you'd probably really like, or who has a shared interest, and then you could talk to them through him. But he's not the Internet. He's something that is being used in a very similar societal niche as the Internet.

                                                        Put that way, IAM sounds more like GPT3(+Siri+Google) than internet.

                                                        (On a tangent, GPT3 and this discussion has made me think again about an old post of mine on here in where we discussed constructed intelligences in Exalted, where I thought that they owed a lot to the assumptions of early to mid 20th century sci-fi about what would be easy and hard - that matching basic sentience and sapience to a roughly human level would be easy, but exceeding it from then on would be hard. IAM has a little of that quality perhaps, in that it is a little reminiscent of something like Ship's Computer in '60s Star Trek or HAL 9000, which could easy vocally interact with the crew, but their vid interfaces were pretty primitive. In the same spirit, GPT3 seems to offer some indications that, where we thought that coding AI to generate new expertise might be easy, but new creative output would be hard, it seems that there are some indications that it's the opposite. It seems relatively easy to prompt GPT3 to generate, for instance, new Cowboy Bebop episode plot outlines - if ones you'd probably reject as a series planner! - or Neil Gaiman parody... but hard to get it to develop any novel unguided expertise that isn't just a reprint of a text that it's effectively found through search.)

                                                        On another point I'm a bit surprised that Exalted's First Age development ever considered Exalted Modern as an option - the Records of the Before and all the developer commentary was pointing to them having mecha, sky carriers, automata, and generally stuff that was beyond late 20th century ("modern"), or if beyond per se, not exactly the same. Or maybe I'm not getting what Exalted Modern means in that context!
