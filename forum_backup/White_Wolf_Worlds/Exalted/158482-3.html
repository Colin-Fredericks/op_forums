                                Originally posted by Drascin View Post
                                Gee. That sure doesn't sound horribly self-satisfied and smug. Thanks for reminding me of my wrongness.
                                Sidereals have the best-designed powerset in 1e. Maybe the best-designed powerset in the history of the line. It is a major component of what people who like Sidereals like about Sidereals. Presenting it as though it's random and aimless is insulting to those people while also giving the sense that you don't really grok the splat. It's difficult to be wholly inoffensive about expressing that, and given the tone of your original post, probably not worth the effort.

                                Originally posted by Drascin View Post
                                I understand the powers plenty, don't you worry. They are not in any way complex.
                                You are not doing a good job of convincing me of this!

                                Originally posted by Drascin View Post
                                But without the background of being part of the godly hierarchy and envoys of the maidens and agents to make things go as they should and so on? A lot of them would make a shitload less sense. Like, let's take something very simple to not get lost in particulars and pedantry. I dunno, Medicine. Sidereal Medicine kills zombies instead of helping keep and continue life (which is what Medicine is ostensibly for) as an expression of Saturn's purview of Endings - an Endings Sidereal was chosen to make sure that things die in the proper way, he was not chosen to keep life, that is not what his basic job is, and Saturn's magic is not going to help him do that, so deal. You remove "being designed to do the job for Saturn" from the equation, and what you get is a bunch of guys who seem to be unable to use medicine to actually help anyone for no real reason other than, ostensibly, some designer feeling like being cute and making the Charm tree a nonsequitor from the Ability.
                                You're correct that Sidereal Medicine is clearly filtered through the lens of Endings, but it's a mistake to infer that it's because of the "job". Notice that the closest charmset to Sidereals' in terms of what goes where is the Dragon-Blooded one, which is the absolute antithesis of "you were chosen as this caste in order to do your patron's job for them." Dragon-Blooded Thrown doesn't give you a tornado shield because Mela wants you to go around tornading things. But the dragon-blooded charmset is also full of "non sequiturs", by which you seem to mean "effects that don't flow logically from the mundane application of the Ability". It's more noticeable in Sidereals because they get fewer general-purpose effects, but that's part of the unrelated design goals of giving them a small number of charms and ensuring that their access to core meat-and-potatoes effects is limited. Once you rule out Sidereals and Dragon-Blooded, the only Ability Exalted left over are Solars (for whom "fairly literal application of the Ability" is a core thematic), and Abyssals (whose charmset is a very close relative of the Solars'). From the 3e Infernals preview, it looks like Ability Infernals lean a lot more in the Sidereal direction.

                                If I were going to try to put my finger on the essence of the esoteric nature of Sidereal charms -- thematically, I mean, beyond just the "they're kind of scattered to force you to tap-dance", which you're absolutely right about -- it would be down to two things:
                                • Unlike most Exalted, Sidereals get different portions of their charmset from different gods. The Endings charms aren't Saturn-themed because they're hints about what Saturn expects you to go do, they're Saturn-themed because the charms of Saturn express the nature of Saturn, much like the charms of Luna express the nature of Luna.
                                • Sidereals develop in power by building an enlightened understanding of the cosmos, as filtered through the Colleges (and, implicitly, through the minds of the Maidens). Their powers are often metaphorical because each one is representing 1/25th of the entire nature of the universe, as embodied in the associated scripture. A Sidereal who progresses through the Medicine tree uses her knowledge of Medicine to develop a transcendent understanding of the purview of the Corpse, because secret cosmic wisdom and enlightenment are Sidereal themes.

                                It's a disservice to the charmset and the people responsible for it to suggest that this was an arbitrary or careless decision, or that the choices made were not made carefully.

                                        Over in Spacebattles, I and a poster called Ryuugi got to talking about, "In Kingdom Hearts, what if the original Keyblade went to Riku as it was intended, and Sora instead received an Exaltation?"

                                        Ryuugi decided to explore the idea of Sora as a Sidereal Exalt. These are his thoughts.


                                        Premise
                                        The Destiny Islands are being torn apart and consumed by Darkness. Without any kind of weapon to fight the Heartless, Chosen of [Journeys / Battles / Serenity] Sora beats the ever-loving shit out of them with his bare fists, until there's no more world left to stand on, and he falls through a dark void lit by all the stars of the universe.

                                        Afterwards, he awakens in a strange place. Moreover, something strange is going on; most people barely notice him, and when he departs their presence they forget him entirely. In time, he learns his fate -- to be forgotten by the world, forgotten by anyone he meets, forgotten by the very friends he strives to protect, but yet possessing the power he needs to save them all. He finds paths between worlds in a realm they call Heaven, learns of the fate that awaits the ones he loves if he does nothing, and travels the universe to save them.

                                        Kairi is awake and alive, but hunted in worlds far from her own. Riku is walking a path fraught with dangers within and without, against which his Keyblade is as much bait as defense. So Sora, whom neither remembers, chases after both knowing that its worth it as long as he can keep them safe.


                                        Arcane Fate and Kairi
                                        I prefer to retain the Storytelling potential of the Arcane Fate, due to the effect it has on the character dynamics. Not only is it a tragedy for Sora--one Kairi doesn't even remember--but its not enough to stop him from aiding her however he can. He'll protect his friend, follow her to the ends of the Universe and aid her silently, even if she doesn't and might never remember who he is.

                                        Why? Because she's his friend and he loves her.

                                        There's also Kairi's side of things, with a part of her life so cruelly torn away and she can't even remember how. Have you seen...what was it? Season Five of Doctor who? When Rory vanishes and Amy can't remember him and yet sometimes someone would ask something and she wouldn't remember but she'd cry? And what Rory did when he returned.

                                        Kairi doesn't remember Sora--can't remember him--but she keeps meeting this boy who wants to help her and though she can't recall his name and never things of him when he's gone, he's...

                                        There's a lot of potential on both sides in the face of what's been forgotten.


                                        Being A Sidereal
                                        If Sora became a Sidereal, right off the bat that means a bunch of different things. It means everyone he knows--and almost everyone he meets--will forget him. Evidence and memories of his existence would fade away like dust in the wind. He could walk up to Kairi or Riku and they wouldn't know him, baring use of magic. But it also means he'd have a lot of power, most of which doesn't involve fighting at all, because a large part of being a Sid is avoiding combat or making it so you don't need to fight. It gives him a diverse set of useful powers to take advantage of, but also imposes a lot of limits as he has to act within the bounds of his Charmset. There's also the involvement of enhanced knowledge such as from the various prediction effects Sidereals have and their roundabout way of doing things.

                                        To be a Sidereal is to be forgotten. Even his closest friends forget him, and though massive time and effort could leave impressions, even they won't think of him when he's gone. Those he meets, fights for, and saves--they forget him when he leaves. He has no Keyblade to save the worlds, no destiny there. He is a hero, but no one anyone knows about. And yet, he devotes himself to saving them anyway, to do as much as he can to bring happiness to the world, and trying with all his power to help and support Kairi and Riku.


                                        The Plot
                                        My rough picture of things going to relate things broadly to Xehanort's plan, perhaps taking place in a situation roughly analogous to Hollow Bastion in the First Game and the whole Riku fight/freeing of Kairi's heart and so on. Xehanort's canonically spent plenty of time in the Dark Margin and found the Jade Prison in his efforts and began to slowly work to extract them from it one by one and slowly converting them into Abyssals. He then plugs and plays with eligible targets who fit specific criteria--such as the Organization Members, I was thinking, though with a number of design changes from the canon model, a number of which Xehanort couldn't predict and isn't aware of.

                                        After Riku got sufficiently corrupted by the Darkness but after he still got kicked to the curb in a climactic fight with... I'm thinking Kairi and her friends (maybe or maybe not including Sora), a shard is attracted to him and he makes the deal with Ansem and becomes an Abyssal. Insert Round 2, but this time it's against a Solaroid who chose to be a huge dick and dump everything into Melee so he could block all your fucking attacks and hit like a motherfucker and thank god for Olympia--ahem. So round two with Riku--or Riku suffering from a sort of Past Lives/Urge/Resonance hybrid, at least--starts and it doesn't go so hot. Fighting a Solar-tier Melee focused Exalt in a sword fight, even a young one, is an enormous pain even with a sweet Keyblade--especially since Riku also has a Keyblade of his own*. The good guys get the ever loving shit beaten out of them until Sora steps in to fight directly, at which point Riku starts remembering him directly, but the fight still doesn't go so well, because Keyword defenses fuck with a lot of Sora's non-lethal Martial Arts and while he can fight him and hurt him, he can't bring himself to go far enough to risk killing his friend--and Abyssals can be tricky bitches about going down besides, especially while on an Exaltation high, in armor, and being fed Xehanort-brand steroids.

                                        The bright side is that seeing Sora and Riku fight finally begins wearing on the Arcane Fate some and Kairi finally manages to get that first or second dot-worth of Acquaintance and remembers Sora's name. Insert emotional reunion, insert Abyssals being dicks and ruining parties they didn't get invited to, insert return to action sequence. Kairi's wondering if there's any way to win with her lacking an Exaltation and Sora admits to having had visions (Source: Sidereal Lore/Investigation/Awareness/a prayer roll to the Maidens) of someplace he knew it was his destiny to travel to. Kairi opts to face Riku alone while Sora runs past him to do Sidereal business, Yellow Pathing his way to timeliness. Kairi opens the Keyhole and Sora rushes through the Door to Darkness and then into the Realm of Darkness. He locates the Jade Prison and opens it.

                                        Up top, Kairi gets a major dose of shine goodness. Down low, Sora finishes grinding to the next level and levels up to Essence 5--though I've also been contemplating Oathkeeper/Oblivion given their thematic importance to Sora by representing Kairi and Riku, the dual nature of his heart, and their general coolness. Excrement becomes actualized and the Keyhole is closed with Sora on one side and Kairi and Riku on the other, ending the arc.

                                        Vague thoughts following that: Sora's locked in eternal darkness. Are you a bad enough dude to rescue the Chosen of Fate?

                                        *And tell me Souleater isn't an Abyssal weapon?


                                        Sora's power-set
                                        The Charms of Serenity are especially well equipped for a role of shadowing and supporting someone else--especially Craft, Dodge, and Linguistics, though Performance and Socialize are by no means lacking either. With MA favored and some of the other vital parts of Sidereal Wise Mastering--like Lore, for instance, and Awareness--and he'd be in a great position to guide his friends through the minefields that are their lives, while also having to make things work.

                                        Add in some astrology...

                                        I have a good idea of which Styles I want--Throne Shadow for obvious reasons and probably Sapphire Veil later on, also for obvious reasons*. My main question is twofold: Does he need any other styles? Beyond some dabbling, which is periodically useful; a number of Styles have some useful tech hidden in them that can be used. But any specific styles you think should be dabbled in or etc. In all honesty, I avoid Scroll of the Monk and the Dragon-Blooded book like they'll give me testicular cancer by proximity.

                                        And second of all, I wanted to get a take on what you guys think about Essence? I'm sure about him either starting at three or reaching it very quickly, but because of the pain in the as of raising Essence, I'm wondering whether to just start him higher (between the...god, three Hearts inside him? It's not hard to hand wave up to 4), whether I should start at three and sort of handwave some of the rules, or use the 3E version of appropriate moments. I'm leaning toward the later, though I suppose a mix of options could work, too.

                                        *But just in case it's not obvious: Throne Shadow is the Sidereal's Hero Style and is the only MA style they can make Charms for. Which also makes it the only part of their Charmset they can make Charms for, period. Sapphire Veil of Passion Style, meanwhile, is a Sidereal MA Style that's 1) not mechanically broken, 2) not broken mechanically, and 3) at a low enough Essence that it actually has a place in the setting since non-Elders can by it. Even with the Charm that allows Sids to buy Essence 6 MA Charms I think there are, like, two other SMAs that can be completed without being a hundred years old. Which is a big deal, because you need to complete SMA styles to get the Master Sutra that lets you not spend your entire motepool in one Charm.

                                        But actually, I'd given thought to the Virtue Styles and they do fit. They're good styles--showing their age some and missing a few major bits that have sense been added, but again, that's every MA--and they also make thematic sense. It can be easy to miss, but Virtues are supposed to be very central to Sids; while they don't have an mote-pool expansion Charms, this was by design. They were supposed to use (Virtue) Essence Replenishment as a replacement; having a technically lower mote pool but being able to potentially generate a theoretical max of two hundred additional motes via obeying their virtues--which is also why they're paired with the Virtue Keyword, that makes a lot of Charms open to Virtue channeling.

                                        (Of course, this got messed up in practice because the writers of Sids didn't know what they were doing, but...)

                                        It's also a very good match for Sora, who's a very Virtuous character. While Compassion, Conviction, and Valor are obvious, he even shows a lot of temperance, such as when he refuses to go back to the Islands--what he wants most--until he finds the King and Riku and the many other times he resists temptation.

                                        Given the importance of Virtues to both Sora and to Sidereals, playing with that could work well, and the Virtue Styles are very powerful.


                                        Yu-Shan
                                        Though speaking of Heaven, I've also be thinking about where it fits. My take on it was to merge the cosmologies--Creation was the original world that was broken apart. Heaven still exists though, connected to all the other worlds via it's original connection to Creation. Something I've been pondering, though, is how it's changed? Certainly, there would be a lot of new gods for the new cities, worlds, places, and things, but how many of the Exalted Gods should remain approximately the same? It makes sense in a lot of ways, as you'd figure that being in a position of strength before the fracture would equip them to better wrangle for a new position, but what do you guys think?

                                        If it came up, I could see Kairi and Riku Exalting later in the story as a Solar and Abyssal respectively. The Abyssals would need to get a different background, of course, but they actually work pretty well with the mechanics of the Heartless, allowing for sufficiently special circumstances. That could be pretty cool, potentially, but I'm not sure if it'd be a good idea; while by the time it came up, Sora would be Essence 5 to their 2 or 3, there remains the potential for overshadowing. On the other hand, that could be cool in and off itself--Sidereals work best in the shadows and he'd play the role of the wise old master, while also protecting them from the shadows and guiding them. And really, the more I think about it, it kind of seems fitting, though it might just be me.



                                        formerly Tornado Wolf, formerly Inugami
