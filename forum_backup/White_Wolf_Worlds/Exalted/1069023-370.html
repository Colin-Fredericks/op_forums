    First, I just want to share a little story from the most recent player to join my game: He had never even heard of Exalted before listening to me talk about the skype game I'm running with some friends from high school, so he jumped in to our vacant spot largely to hang out with the lot of us again. I tried explaining everything as best I could, but there's a lot to take in and some stuff just inevitably doesn't quite sink in until you've played a bit. So, four sessions in he asks if it'd be okay to write up a new character since he had some things he'd have done differently, and I was real flexible about that stuff for the rest of my players (who were new to the game as well) so I told him to go ahead.

    He started reading into sorcery, and saw the Workings system, and as we were talking I could hear the gleam start up in his eyes. "Wait, I can give myself mutations?" "Yeah, totally!" He went deep. Unusual Hide 5, wings, claws, poisoned body, and even wrote intimacies into this new character about being some kind of mad Wyld prophet seeing mutations as gifts as well as a custom sorcery archetype to go with it. When the figurative dust settled, he asked me to check to make sure everything was legal and my main question to him was 'Everything looks good, but why would this guy join the rest of the group?'. We discussed some potential changes to his intimacies to make the character slot neatly into the group, but after a while my friend just said 'I don't think I can play this character'. I'm given to understand that enabling the Conan-style mad sorcerer-king who chases blindly after power only to discover too late what it's cost him has long been a goal of Exalted sorcery, but I find it remarkable that this edition managed to do that to my player. Mission Accomplished.


    Now for some actual questions!

    First, Fulminating Word starts off with "When the Lawgiver argues from one of her defining principles, it is nearly impossible to disagree." Are we to take this at face value and require that the influence in question line up with one of the acting character's defining intimacies?

    Next, Steel Devil Style's double and triple attack techniques both refer to the opponent's Defense taking onslaught penalties for the threshold successes required to activate them. How does this interact with single-attack onslaught negators like Dipping Swallow Defense? If they were truly separate attacks, you'd need to activate it once for each strike, naturally, but given the psuedo-flurry nature of those charms, the timing doesn't seem to have a clear timing. The charms don't activate until after the attack roll is made, so making them pay 9m up front for 3 activations of Dipping Swallow Defense seems weird, but the normal window for activating it has already passed by the time you know if double attack triggers. Does this mean that single-attack onslaught negators actually can't help you against them?

                    Originally posted by shkspr1048 View Post
                    Then is it possible for someone to recover an artifact, storied in it's legend and powers, only to find that none of said powers are available?
                    Evocations are evocative (!) of the bond between weapon and wielder. They are rooted in the history of the weapon, but also in the power and personality of the wielder. Evocations are theoretically unique (though it's something you're encouraged to handwave and treat published Evocations like any other purchased Charm). A saint who wields the blade of a demon may have a disharmonious relationship, corrupting or purifying one or the other. Those legendary powers may be similar or substantially altered.
