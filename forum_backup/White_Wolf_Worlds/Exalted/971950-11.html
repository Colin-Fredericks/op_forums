    I think some flexibility could be in Lunars, but distinct from how Solars do it. That Solars can be good at basically anything, so long as they actually develop in those things, while Lunars might have some smaller number of things that can be developed which can be applied to numerous things. As an illustration rather than suggestion, the idea that a Lunar could develop down one branch that can include good sword fighting in addition to several other activities, if not exactly as good as Solars.

    I think the priority there would still be making the experience of playing them feel different, rather than niche protection.

                Not sure if this came out upthread already, I would certainly still expect the Lunar Charms to be focused and structured around activities, a la 2e Alchemicals, and not Abilities.

                So if you want to play a Lunar with combat focus, then there are tree(s) for combat which will let you do that, rather than sort of oblige you to go and buy Charms that have secondary functions outside of combat (or whatever activity of interest) that you're not interested in playing a character with, etc.

                But if you want to be a character who's like all "Well, I'm great at combat, 'cos I have tons of Melee enhancing Charms, but very little in any other combat enhancing Ability, and it shows in how I fight and approach problems" then Solars will still be your best bet, and Lunars won't really have much space for that.

                (Some activity focused trees will inevitably overlap with Ability based trees - Alchemicals 2e had a tree about survival, for instance, even though their trees are based around activities and challenges and not Abilities, because survival is an activity and a type of challenge equally as much as a capitalized Ability. In a way, yes, this is kind of on a theoretical level unsatisfying but I would think it's a necessary step to having characters that people actually want to play when rubber meets road.)

                    Oh, I remember now; it was about the idea that something closer to fickleness in Lunars could be found in the fact that they have no role, rather than in them periodically switching between two roles. I was thinking about how their current Castes are described not in terms of things that they do, but the manner in which they overcome obstacles and assert themselves against the world, and the idea that such a Lunar who can operate without constraints somewhere could cycle through a whole bunch of roles and ambitions in a manner that could be construed as fickleness.

                    ​I wouldn't exactly define Solar Castes as inescapable or inflexible, but that there tends to be a consistency to them through means of the kinds of people the Unconquered Sun selects for them, and the feeling one gets from pursuing them. I could read the acquisition of Solars XP via Caste as representing the idea that Caste represents a role and purpose that is reliably spiritually fulfilling for a Solar, not as something that turns them into a stereotype, but as part of the basis for why a Solar might repeatedly engage in functions of the Caste over the centuries.

                    ​I could see Lunars as getting things differently in a manner that means that some of them might frequently switch their interests.

                    ​I have this idea of a Full Moon Lunar who started out in the far North living in her ruined mountain fortress manse, going out to get into fights with the local gods and elementals and Fair Folk and stronger, more aggressive tribes, then became a kind of wandering beggar warrior-for hire while travelling down along the length of Creation through the East, and finally wound up in... well, it was originally the South, but now I find the political situations of the Southeast to be more fitting, and she became a champion of a resistance movement within one of the empires and ultimately, after some encouragement from her son, who tracked her down for one of his visits every fifteen years or so, opted to try her hand as a monarch for a while.

                        Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post
                        I would certainly still expect the Lunar Charms to be focused and structured around activities, a la 2e Alchemicals, and not Abilities.
                        Well something that struck me about the way Alchemical Charms were organised there was that they basically weren't arranged by Attribute at all. That it went beyond just cascades that would occasionally branch through different Attributes and into the sole focus of their arrangement being on their utilities. It finally helped me make sense of their descriptions in the Wiki, I'll tell you that.

                        ​And it works for them because part of the concept they had there was that the Exalted could buy the Charms for cheap, and that even Attribute requirements could be handily fulfilled by Augmentations, and the limit was on how much of one's arsenal could be accessed at any given time. It's part of why I've considered that version of Alchemicals to not really even have Attribute Charms; Attributes work more than Abilities, or even just Essence, as the prerequisites, but a given Charm is expressing a concept other than the Attribute associated with it. Really, the expression of the whole Alchemical is coming through in something different from their Charms or traits, because those are things that can be modified with relative impunity.

                        ​I may be biased by familiarity, but when I think of Lunar Charms, I still think that the expression of Attributes by them is important. I would think of a Lunar Strength Charm as an expression of strength, compared to an Alchemical one that is more a case of a given capability requiring a modification to this part of their makeup.

                        ​The fact that some Solar Charm cascades have been divided according to certain expressions or utilities would make me think that something like the Second Edition Lunar arrangement could still be the result, but I think that for something closer to the Alchemicals would entail a significant overhaul of them.

                        ​I'm not necessarily arguing against such an overhaul, I'm just laying out some of the premises that I see.

                                        Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post
                                        @ Isator, some stuff to think about here. I actually didn't know there was route whereby Alchemicals would buy Charm based Attribute increases that count as prerequisites
                                        ​Yes, the Fourth Attribute Augmentation. Has no prerequisites and minimums of Essence and Attribute 1, can be purchased for any Attribute, once purchased can be installed up to (Essence/2) times per Attribute, and you only need to commit Essence for the first installation, which costs one mote. It has the effect of increasing your Attribute cap by one and granting a free dot of the Attribute per installation. The Attribute boosts do not count towards your experience costs to raise Attributes, but count as your real value for all other purposes, including raising the cap for other Augmentations.

                                        Originally posted by Ghosthead
                                        which I would presume are balanced by being cheaper in experience vs slots and committed essence?
                                        ​Just about. Alchemical paid (current rating) x 3 to raise Caste and Favoured Attributes, while all Charms cost a flat 6xp.

                                        ​As far as I can see, there was also no limit to the number of Charm slots you could buy (with those also being cheap), and although this would typically gain diminishing returns if they outstripped your personal Essence pool, I could see somebody dedicating a few Charm slots just for this Augmentation considering how its commitment worked.
