                I changed the Dominion of the Silver-Crowned to make them more usable in my game.

                The Silver-Crowned believe it is their duty to protect all of An-Teng and practice a rite of passage in which their youths are exiled from the dominion and unable to return home until without proof of their worth.

                The preference is for the exiles to earn their return home with proof of military service or trophies from dangerous beasts of the land, but the elders can be pragmatic about this and will accept other offerings that can help make the domain stronger. Its simply a different type of prowess.

                Unfortunately, without oversight these exiles often look for easy ways to return home and many of them conduct themselves like glory seeking mercenaries rather than protectors. Some even resort to kidnapping and outright banditry reasoning that their victims deserved it.

                            A new thing for a game I thing I may run on Roll20 in May...

                            The areas in the River Provence around Nexus, Great Forks, and Nechara south of the Yanteze. I've given a each one a different name and assumed there are dozens of small kingdoms in these areas.
                            The Trader's Plain or Merchant's Lands: The land immediately around Nexus and stretching out and southward until the choke point between the rivers. These nations of the 100 Kingdoms are heavily influenced by the Guild (to varying degrees) and the politics/culture of Nexus, as well as the Poor Man's Breath for the nearest kingdoms. The Eastern half of the Region is also heavily influenced by the Culture if not the Politics of Great Forks. The Hundred Gods or Local Gods are the common form of worship. The Trader's Plain is swampier near Nexus but otherwise dry and fruitful. Wars in this region are rare, but do occur but they are usually more of border disputes than actual battles. Kingdoms here may be a few days ride across.
                            The Champagne Beatific or the Divine Gift: The lands between the two "lesser rivers" including the Great Forks and extending down terminating between the headwaters of the two "lesser" rivers. This land is politically, & culturally dominated by the Hundred Gods and Great Forks. The lands of the Champagne Beatific has been blessed hundreds of times by the sorceries and charms of the powerful gods and their servants worshiped by the Hundred Gods. Each giving whatever blessing their purview would allow to increase the bounty of the land. These blessings have made the already bountiful region even more so. The Champagne Beatific is one of the most densely populated regions of the East. Here each kingdom is dominated by a small city and fast riders could cross several kingdoms in a days ride. The people of the Beatific are soft, haughty and overly concerned with appearance, personal/family honor and status. Wars within the Divine gift do occur but conflicts are more often solved with personal duels or tournaments. When wars do happen they are either more of a "large scale duel" or the armies are merely spectators for a duel between champions.
                            There is one exception to the culture of the Divine gift and it is found at its southern terminus between the headwaters of its border rivers. Here lay the so called "Grass Kingdoms" rise and fall with regularity. The usually 5-7 kingdoms protect the rest as its southern bulwark and buffer. These kingdoms are generally a mix of the Beatific and Crimson Fields cultures. These kingdoms like the Crimson Fields are directly open to outside attacks by barbarians and nomads thus they rise and fall with a regularity. None of these kingdoms are treated as anything more than cultural backwaters at best by their northern cousins, and weaklings by their eastern relatives. Most of the Grass Kings are ambitious mercenaries that are attempting to create their own kingdom but are constantly dealing with raiders, rogue elementals, and similar threats.
                            The Crimson Fields or Red Plain: The lands surrounding Nechara, the city of Calibration. These lands are filled with relatively low populations controlling large territories with little in the way of natural defenses. This means that they are often invaded by barbarians, nomadic tribes, and worse (yearly raids and a full invasion about once a decade). These intrusions are usually repulsed within a season or leave of their own accord. They do war with each other but this is dwarfed by outside threats.The People of the Crimson Fields are a hard people skilled in arms almost to a person, and many mercenary companies can be found or recruit from within.


                            The game I plan to run will be a sandboxy/themepark game centered (at first) around the Grass Kingdoms.

                                            The skies of Yu-Shan reflect the overall stability of Creation. When Creation is at it's safest (relatively speaking) the Sun, Moon and Stars of the Maidens are arranged in a prismatic, ever shifting rainbow reminiscent of an aurora borealis.

                                            As creation's stability comes under threat, the celestial bodies begin to fade away, distorting the colors in the sky in to more and more dire hues. Each phase reflects how bad things are getting.

                                            First the Sun and Moon vanish, robbing the sky of the comforting gold and soothing silvers. This reflects some noticeable but still manageable creation-wide problem, such as a food shortage, or economic recession.

                                            Then the Blue of Venus vanishes, and soon after the Yellows of Mercury. This represents a whole direction in serious trouble, such as a plague sweeping the east, or a massive drought in the south.

                                            When Jupiter vanishes, leaving only the blood red of Mars and the violet of Saturn, things have destabilized to the point of total anarchy across the whole of Creation. The Usurpation and The Great Contagion were prime examples of this.

                                            When Mars leaves, leaving only the light of Saturn, Creation is one bad day from breaking utterly. The Primordial War was the only known events to push things this far.
