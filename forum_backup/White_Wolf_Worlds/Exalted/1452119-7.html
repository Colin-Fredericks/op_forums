        I haven't read MAs & Sorcery yet, but I do think that the intention is for multiattack Charms to occupy Step 1 of the attack they create.

        The text never says this, but it fits with the pattern: all Charms which do not specify a Step are Step 1 by default, and lots of them create new actions without specifying another Step. For consistency, whenever it's ambiguous, I will be assuming that all Charms which create actions work that way.

            Okay, Martial Arts time. Sorry, all you splats I keep putting off! It's that I dislike you (well, okay, I do dislike some of you), just that looking at a charm set in this level of detail, with stops for cross-referencing and errata reports, takes like two hours. I'm hoping to do it for all of them, if only because articulating my thoughts well enough to write them out here is a great way to figure out what rules and I do and don't get, as well as what seems outright incorrect.

            (I started on Liminals yesterday, but that will be an even larger investment than average.)

            I think I like the overall approach to MAs here, with Complimentary Skills and weapons defined by tags rather than a list (no more "why does this style allow Seven-Section Staff, but not Fighting Chain"), though it is a little odd that I can now use Close Combat Charms to enhance ranged attacks, but still care about Form weapons. I hope that armor isn't a huge deal for moderately-invested combatants here, the way it is in 3E - massively penalizing random MAs based on what appear to be aesthetic rather than balance concerns is a pet peeve of mine.

            We start with my old archnemesis, Crane Style. It immediately justifies my opinion of it by falling into a version error: the intro talks about the Celestial and Terrestrial tags, but Crane actually uses Mastery (Celestial) and Mastery (Terrestrial). If that means anything, a search cannot tell me what.

            Fluttering Cry Of Warning reveals a baffling detail of the Defend Other rules. It already bugged me that EE was following 3E's lead by making "Defending" an ally actually refer to redirecting all attacks on them towards yourself with functionally no counterplay. But now, I learn there is one way in which this isn't true: Decisive attacks hurt the defender and apply the defender's soak, but apparently they still check the original target's Hardness. Why?

            And while I'm on the subject, what happens the Celestial version of this reflexively raises the target's Hardness high enough that the attack on them is illegal? The Alchemical Charm Reinforced Chassis forces the attacker to switch to Withering, and that's probably what I would do in other cases, but there's no general rule here - one could assume that action is just "wasted," or that they get to declare a new action with no penalties instead, and those are pretty wide extremes.

            Crane Form does the Crossed Wings Denial thing in a ruleset which actually supports it, which is nice, and I really like that both Modes are such cool wuxia tropes that it makes me sad to not get both - nobody is being shafted here. However, as usual, I am dubious of "this gets massively better when your opponents are opposing your Virtues" stuff. That looks to me like a clause which encourages bad character design - like Alchemical advantages, it pays a player off for picking broad "Path of What I Was Going To Do Anyway" stuff. Almost any Ambition will be "opposed" by anyone who dares try to kill you, but almost no Courage will be ("How dare you run away - my Defense increases by a further point!"), for example.

            Um, Humbling Enlightenment Commentary, you do know that running out of HLs doesn't kill named enemies by default, right? What are even the rules for this "unconscious" situation? Can allies wake them up with a single action, like from the Gambit? Does it have some limited duration? Probably yes and no, respectively, but why do this at all in a world where Incapping is cast in such loose and narrative terms?

            Ninja time!

            Should Nothing But Shadows stack with regular concealment? As written, I think it does not.

            Throat-Slitting Shadow Strike is the first Charm that I've seen where the power difference between Modes is immediately clear. One is a 2-mote swing, which looks pretty huge in Essence... the other is a cool stunt.

            Mantis! Huh, both "defensive, grounded animal Kung Fu" styles in the core.

            Some of Iron-Arm Block's effects last for one attack, some last until next turn, and one of them (regarding flurry penalties) my be literally impossible to use. Even if I can declare Charms on my own Step 2 (and I sure hope not), that's not when a flurry would be declared.

            I wonder what (Celestial) Mantis Form thinks it means in game terms if it "inflicts a wrenching injury that keeps the opponent from using one of their limbs"? Almost all movement is unrolled, so unless they're using a very two-handed fighting style, the effects seem like they would be entirely narrative (or incredibly harsh for something being made up with no textual support). Maybe they also reduce the Attribute they care about the least by 1, Dramatic Injury style?

            Unfolding Retribution Strike is making me confront the distinction between "contested roll" attacks in a grapple, and clashes. There admittedly is such a distinction, and it seems clear enough to apply, but now I'm wondering why there needs to be. It's kind of quirky how the Celestial mode expects to slowly wear enemies down with a pack of allies, and the Terrestrial one expects to quickly drop the target outright, then gain Power to help with the next one.

            Righteous Devil without having seen the Flame tag seems potentially hard to... ...judge. I outright refuse to believe that this Martial Art associated with a single lore character and a single concept has ever had the 4+ practitioners required for "Creation-spanning rivalries," plural. Interestingly, its only Modes are for Solars and Solars alone.

            Wait, if a character uses (Solar) Blossom of Inevitable Demise, can their side just have her act last in the first round, making interrupts totally impossible until round 2?

            Being stuck in melee against (Solar) Righteous Devil Form looks miserable - they Aim, use their free Excellency, and then blast you with +11 dice, for a total of 22 (plus whatever Accuracy ends up looking like). Against the Charmless maximum Defense of 5, this is a hit with +6 damage dice. They're not even spending motes. Pray they didn't bother with the Close Combat 3 for Excellent Strike.

            I really appreciate the Burning Judgement Halo actually specifies that it doesn't harm the Exalt (and so, by extension, we can reason it's deliberate that it doesn't mention allies being immune to this literal friendly fire), though I am a little surprised by the Solar mode, which appears to easily be the most complex area-based effect I've seen in the game. Hope you brought plenty of post-it motes for those index card "range band" trackers!

            Snake Style, explicitly called out as The Martial Art You Find Everywhere.

            Snake Form actually has rules for disabling parts of the body! Take note, Mantis Form.

            Martial Arts effect sure do seem to love skipping directly from Short to Long Range, between Burning Judgement Halo and Uncoiling Serpent Aura. I was going to say the Celestial Mode looks insanely deadly, assuming that it (and poisons in general) means levels of damage, rather than dice. However, re-reading the rules for poisons, I noticed that "When a character rolls to resist a poison’s damage, she also reduces its duration by the same number of successes," and now I have much bigger questions about that. The same as what, Essence? Extra successes, extra successes plus one for success, or total successes? The example seems to imply the second option, because it says Mercurial Iris "reduces" the poison's duration, but actually just has it tick down by 1 like it was going to do anyway, and then seems to think that "another successful attempt" will plausibly involve 8+ successes on 8 dice, which is more than a little optimistic.

            My point is that a poison with Damage 3/round, a 3-round duration, and Difficulty 4 is brutal for anyone who didn't build heavily for Fortitude + Physique - 5 levels of damage if you're reasonably lucky, and 9 is a real possibility. And poison resistance rolls seem hard to stunt.

            Tiger Style. It lets you treat Light Form weapons as medium, which could be interesting with some other MAs - most of the ones so far share either Concealable or Disarming.

            I'm honestly a little confused by what ends up Celestial versus Terrestrial. Overall, it seems like Terrestrial ones have larger statistical bonuses, and Celestial ones grant over overall control over the flow of combat. That's already an unexpected place to end up, but then there's cases like the Celestial mode of Tiger Form being almost identical to the Terrestrial Mode of Ebon Shadow Form. What does that mean? Is there any pattern beyond legacy code?

            White Reaper,able for having Lunar modes, and for being not the Martial Art That Is In No Way A Martial Art.

            Oh, you don't believe me? Consider this: White Reaper Form appears to lock Sidereal Characters out of accessing the Greater Signs, since you can't go above Anima 10, and cannot spend Halos on Anima effects. A Martial Art which has anti-synergy with the Martial Artist splat is surely on somewhat shaky ground.

            On the other hand, you can finally raise your Resolve in combat, and all you had to do was rip people's souls out with your bare hands.

            (Lunar) Bleeding Crescent Strike doesn't say anything about dividing Power, so can you really use 10 Power against each of four different opponents? I don't know how often you'll ever have that many enemies in melee, but it's still a pretty wild possibility.

            This doesn't have much to do with Martial Arts, but it only occurred to me at this point in my reading that there's no general rule establishing a maximum Hardness. As written, I see nothing to stop you from focusing on defensive tech until you reach Hardness 11 and become immune to Decisive attacks.

            In any case, that took even longer than I expected (we're already at something like 1.5 normal Charmsets, so that may not be too shocking). Next time I'm free, it'll be the Five Dragon Styles, plus the much-hyped one Sidereal Style.

                    I don't know that it's bad design or anything, but it does reinforce my stereotype that this heavy armored, self-healing, silver aura thing is just the Lunar Melee tree which they normally don't receive. The only things I've ever used it for were as a pre-Fangs Lunar charmset, and to beef out Ahlat.

                    Though, typing all of that, I really can't shake the feeling this is an Abyssal thing, with the soul stealing and all.

                    But I don't have time to be ambivalent about White Reaper being a corebook style again, because I'm too busy being ambivalent about the Five Dragon Styles being in the corebook. Sure, they're iconic, DBs would feel a little incomplete without them, and some of the most interesting MAs in the game are Dragon Styles... but they're also really heavily pitched towards Dragonblooded and nobody else. I can't quite shake the feeling that if we're going to have 20 pages of Martial Arts, we maybe shouldn't spend one third of them on one tenth of characters. Yes, I know, lore-wise these make some sense for Sidereals, and somebody always wants to play the ex-Immaculate Solar, but Exalt-only Modes feel to me like they would discourage that.

                    Mixed feelings, for sure.

                    As ever, we start with Air Dragon. Alphabetization at work: we always begin with the oddest, least-representative Style, just because stupid William the Bastard had to get Lyft replaced with some Latin word 700 years ago. Boo.

                    Um, anyway. Aid Dragon shows off its oddness right off the bat, as a Style which lets you fight unarmed, but with a special tag effect which only works on their chakrams. Neat.

                    I may have to walk back (heh) a little bit of my questioning how many characters will want these, because even modeless Cloud-Treading Method looks like a pretty strong dip for any combat character who meets the prerequisites. A ranged character can kite people all day, a melee one can hit somebody then go two bands away, leaving their target with no appealing options for fighting them.

                    The downside is, I thought this was something which could be done already. I went back to movement actions, and Sprint doesn't mention any limitations on flurries... but the section does mention that "some movement actions can be used as part of a combat flurry, which will be stated in the action’s text." So, "normal" combat action descriptions will only tell you if the cannot be flurried, but movement actions apparently cannot flurry by default, and so only mention if they can. Dammit, Essence, just pick one default case, and mention exceptions to that! Why do you insist on changing the baseline all the time? Why is there a different general rule for counterattacks than for other reflexive actions? Why do you hate straightforward communication?

                    I have seldom been more curious whether a Charm was doing dice or levels of damage than I am when reading Wrathful Winds Roar. This can quickly become a Difficulty 5 roll against taking 7+ [somethings] of damage - no Soak, no Defense, if you don't have an Excellency in... whatever Ability you resist this thing with (it's not specified, and there doesn't seem to be a general rule for environmental hazards), you take it. heck, even if you manage to hit Difficulty 5, you're not safe, you reduce the damage by one, then one more for each extra success. If those are levels (and the rules seem to imply they are), this is a death sentence which can easily be dropped on the first or second round.

                    (I'm mostly reserving judgement on balance until I actually get some play in, but honestly, this looks insane even if they're damage dice. Maybe Soak somehow applies?)

                    Earth Dragon Style next. Interestingly, its weapon tag effect is strictly worse than that of Ebon Shadow.

                    The actual Charms of Earth Dragon all seem clear and straightforward. Come to think on it, I feel like that's been true across the editions. It truly is the element of stability.

                    Fire Dragon brings back an age-old question: are you dual wielding when you fight unarmed?

                    Searing Edge Attack seems pretty bad most of the time. I spend a mote so that someone else might lose one? If you have the mote economy to make this look good, surely you're already way ahead, especially since this is Essence - unlike 3E, a Celestial mote isn't just worth more.

                    Blah blah, Fiery Blade Attack, blah blah, want to know about levels versus dice and the resistance pool. I know that in the unlikely event that anybody is reading this, you know the drill.

                    Water Dragon implies that Tiger Claws might have the One-Handed tag, which is surprising if true. Also, in the first real surprise of the Complementary Abilities, it uses Sagacity. Sure, What Fire Has Wrought lists Occult, but they all do that.

                    I was surprised not to see Bottomless Depths Defense, one of the most famous MA Charms, adapted here, but on reading closer, it looks like that's what the Dragon-Blooded Mode of Water Dragon Form is doing. That's really clever!

                    Foe-Restraining Whirlpool marks the second time that the book has talked about losing your Reflexive movement as meaning you "must flurry" rolls to move. So, are you not allowed to just Sprint? Do you have to make a Difficulty 3 roll, even though combat movement in Essence mostly involves no dice rolling? I don't really know whether this is a versioning thing, or I'm just being obtuse. Does anyone have a theory here?

                    Wood Dragon sticks to 3E's decision to let them hit people with bows, but not use their Charms through arrows. This, once again, is an edition where I see no obstacle to using Excellent Strike or Ready In Eight Directions with a bow. I do not understand the choice, and would surely houserule it in a game I ran.

                    Unlike Water Dragon, "esotericism and medicine" Style getting Sagacity makes sense to me.

                    Wood Dragon Form looks unspeakably beefy. Comparing it to, say, Mantis Form, this might be better before you get to healing a level every round.

                    "Opponents with Power lower than the Exalt’s Finesse plus one," Unbreakable Fascination Exercise, really? "Equal or less" seems clearer to me, but okay.

                    Soul Mastery looks like a pretty good effort at the eternal challenge of making an instant kill attack neither useless nor broken. I do have to wonder about the "cannot cost more than 10 Power" clause, since the only way I can see for that to happen is if your opponent somehow has Defense 11+, and if that problem comes, that would an issue with or without Soul Mastery.

                    I feel like I have a few more doubts about the Immaculate Styles than the earlier ones, often due to inheriting odd legacy ideas like "these styles for a group of martial artists to overwhelm one strong opponent are loaded with area effects which cannot tell friend from foe." As always, I want to run off and build an Air Style expert who no other player would want to work with (lots of long, grindey fights, lots of running away which would end up abandoning most other PCs), and I think that Earth looks solid but dull, Fire looks gimmickey and useless, Water looks strong but reliant on facing small numbers of "elite" enemies, and have mixed feelings on Wood. The fact that I have the same core reaction to each of these styles as I have in the past definitely shows that Essence remains great at evoking core Exalted themes in a very stripped-down format.

                    But, hey, enough about that. We're really here to see how they could have possibly pulled off a Sidereal Style!

                    First off, it's a little surprising that the book says Getimians cannot develop these Styles and need direct Sidereal tutelage to learn them. Add that to the list of lines which will be extensively litigated between now and whenever Timmies get their own book.

                    "All other targets must flurry an Integrity roll to attack" an Exalt with Games of Divinity Form active, eh? If I'm reading this right, they don't need to succeed on that roll, it just taxes their dice pools and action economy (and makes clashes and counterattacks outright impossible), so maybe that's also the idea with the "no reflexive movement" stuff.

                    Prismatic Arrangement of Creation Form definitely does not make it clear to me whether the writers are expecting multiple Forms to stack with each other, or what kind of action they're supposed to take. It doesn't even give me any idea of where "extreme range" ends - I somehow doubt this Charm is meant to let Getimians sense all Essence use in Creation.

                    Interestingly, this MA is completed at Essence 3, meaning there are still no high-Essence Charms for Sidereals in Essence.

                    Sadly, I'm having some trouble separating my feelings on Essence's take on this style from being reminded of the reasons why I never liked Prismatic Arrangement in the first place. These effects do look powerful, though, and a Getimian mode which explicitly exists to mess up Sidereal abilities is neat.

                    Regarding Martial Arts as a whole, I don't know. It's a classic part of Exalted, so there was likely never any question whether it would be here. Most of these Styles are exactly the ones that everyone would guess (I might have expected one of the animal styles to trade out for Dreaming Pearl Courtesan). Even so, I was mostly left with the feeling that these Styles were less widely-useful than the Universal Charms, and less flavorful than the Exalt-specific ones. There are some which carve out unique niches (Righteous Devil, Air Dragon, Crane) or have some degree of minigame (Wood Dragon, White Reaper) that makes these Charms feel like a package deal apart from everything else (unfortunately, most of the ones which stuck out at me are only aimed at a single splat), but overall, I am less excited about most MA Charms than I was the ones which came before, and find myself wondering if they're the reason why some plats' combat suites (especially Getimians) felt so perfunctory. I think I'd probably use them because a character concept called for it, rather than construct a character to justify playing with these cool toys.

                    "For me, there's no fundamental conflict between really loving something and also seeing it as very profoundly flawed." -- Jay Eddidin

                                                            3E's All-Encompassing Sorcerer's Sight is pretty narrow, and I've seen people repeatedly be surprised when it doesn't do things they expect it to. Not detecting Exalts is especially baffling, given how trivially low-Essence Spirits can do that, seemingly infallibly unless you use a perfect effect against them.

                                                            That said, I can see the Essence version as being too much of a temptation to just leave on. I think my preferred fix would be to leave the full functionality (Essence is big on both giving PCs information and not letting NPCs play by the same rules as them), but make it spend a mote to turn on AESS for one scene, rather than committing. That way, you only use it when you have a reason to.
