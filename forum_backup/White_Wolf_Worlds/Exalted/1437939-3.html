        Being pursued and cornered by an Abyssal hunter's bloodthirsty hounds is absolutely an Exaltation-worthy situation. Monkey Leap Technique lets you jump clear over a twenty foot wall. You can literally jump over the wall you were backed up against.

                            Sorta? It's not like I was giving examples of people that would have any widespread recognition of their talent.

                            Given that Abilities are trained skills that require time and effort to develop (however accelerated for Exalts), it's very difficult to say that you developed a 3+ in something before Exalting and somehow nobody's noticed this. Recognition isn't inherently global or anything. Being the best tipped tea server at the best tea-house in Nexus doesn't mean everyone in Nexus recognizes you, let alone Creation, but your employer and/or fellow employees certainly know you're the best they know off. Some of the major players in Nexus at least know your name and face (because it's Nexus and it pays to be aware of the people around you even if you don't actually care about them emotionally). But you can easily walk down the street and nobody would know who you are, or what your greatest talents are. Nobody in Gem is aware of your existence.

                            The way the book describes traits, characters that go under the radar completely unintentionally are best represented by high Attributes and low Abilities. An Intelligence 5/Bureaucracy 1 character could easily be missed as a server that helps the owners with the books a bit, even though their genius is what actually keeps the business in the black because they easily outperform an Intelligence 2/Bureaucracy 2 manager who's better trained and more experienced in running a business.

                            When you try to say that somehow you have a 3+ and nobody at all realizes it, it quickly strains plausibility (unless you hand-wave away how the books describe the traits, which is sensible but not really the point). At 3 you're better than the vast majority of people that will ever rely on this Ability for a living.

                            A shepherd that's developed Thrown 3 with a sling fending off predators might have done so via innate aptitude and all, and nobody saw the real world experience training happen, but people are going to notice that shepherd hasn't lost an animal while taking them grazing in years, while everyone else loses a few each season. They're going to be curious, even if they don't buy the modest answer of "I practice with my sling a lot," and if they try to spy never see more than... skill with a sling and attention to the animals.

                            Recognition isn't binary. The higher the Ability rating, the harder it is to say there's zero recognition at all.

                            And, of course, Creation has things watching and recognizing that aren't just other humans... or these people wouldn't be Exalting.

                                                        The problem in question isn't, "can you make a character that through a combination of previous life skills, Exaltation, and some time as an Exalt, that justifies having high stats?"
