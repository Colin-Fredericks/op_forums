                                        Something I think has been deliberately left ambiguous is exactly how and why Nadia began feeding that tree wives. I see two possibilities:

                                        She tried ignoring it until the torment of its constant plaintive cries became too much for her and she finally tried to feed it just to get some relief, and the resulting fruit is a significant part of what allowed her to take her place as a victor of the Universal War, creating a vicious cycle.

                                        She neglected it until it sprouted the fruit as a way of compelling her, and in the face of the Universal War, the fruit represented an advantage that she felt she couldn't turn down, and so she continued to feed it just to maintain that supply.

                                        I think either way might suggest why she could never really adopt the methods the other Six use to cheat death (well, Four or Five technically; Mammon is definitely a Servant, which means that he is immortal by default); it's not in her nature or her will to utilize such methods. Nadia is a person who lets herself be shaped by the world, and so lacks the Want to seize and wield such a name for herself as might make her more enduring.

                                        It's an interesting contrast between her and Allison.

                                        Nadia started herself along the path to Royalty when she asserted her own will against her husband by killing him (also committing regicide, which is the only true path to sovereignty), and this put her on a course to great power, but she ultimately failed to continue asserting herself in the face of obstacles such as the sprouting corpse of her husband or the guilds and kings she acquired dominion over. These things created satisfaction in her already attained victory, and so she stagnated. That is why Mottom wields the false name of Glory rather than the true name of I; she let her acquisition of power be something that shaped her into a figure of glamour and ceremony. That is also why, when faced with the opportunity, she tries to foist that position off on somebody else, which is not even as good as abdicating to try and discover herself. She is no Wielder of Names; she refuses to name the world as other than what she first sees of it, and refuses to name herself as something other than what Hastet Om and her subjects see of her (and what they made her see of herself). She has no self-love.

                                        Allison has had power thrust upon her by a dying king (possibly accidentally), and was thrust into a terrifying world of forces trying to command or exploit or destroy her beyond her understanding or power, and yet even from the very beginning, even while scared out of her mind and completely powerless, she kept asserting herself in small ways, demanding answers, demanding respect, demanding that the lofty powers debating her fate actually ask what she wanted. Think about it; while she was held captive by Delicious with Preem Vash and the opposing guild leaders parleying over her fate, she plaintively asked if anybody was going to ask her opinion on any of the proceedings. She knows many qualities of herself that she doesn't like, and thinks herself vastly underqualified for this journey, but she undertook it anyway, and she's had several points in which she's actively trying to be better than who she is, to not be constrained by the narratives of others, not only of herself but of themselves; she wouldn't let herself be completely beholden to Nadia's self pity. She even draws certain strengths from things that she hates about herself; she acknowledges that changing herself into a sorority sister didn't actually help with her esteem, but it also gave her a lot of drinking experience (although I'd warrant that she beat Vladok more because of the perspective of drawing strength from a hateful attempt to change, rather than the literal resistance to alcohol). Allison thought herself powerless, but she didn't revel in that, and it's what is leading her to this point of resolving to try and be powerful instead. She is on the path to self-love; she rejected the false name of Glory.

                                        I'd say that's a quality at the heart of why she was capable of masking that unbound demon (even if the Key was part of the actual power, the Will and Want is what is necessary to wield the Key).
