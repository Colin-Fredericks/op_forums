    Originally posted by Guancyto View Post
    Eh! My thesis was more or less a hypothetical of, "can you design a martial art specifically such that Solars are not better than you at it?"
    I mean.

    It's trivial if that's your only goal. Just take what would usually be the Terrestrial effects, and make the whole style purely Terrestrial, with no Celestial or Mastery benefits. Done. But unfortunately there are some other issues here.

    The Immaculate styles need to be:

    1. Effective for Dragon-Blooded, but not so effective that they're a dominating option. My understanding is that in 3e, DB MA isn't supposed to be dramatically better than Melee. This means you can't just straightforwardly give the DBs the style's Celestial benefits unless you make them a bit weak (or limit the way the DBs access them in some way, which potentially breaks your requirement that Solars not be better at the style than DBs.)

    2. Powerful martial art styles. They're supposed to be legendary. Oops, if they're Terrestrial for everyone, they're instead the worst Marital Arts in the game for everyone except DBs. You can say "well, the style is just for Dragon-Blooded", but... players are still going to notice and understand that the vaunted Immaculate styles are in fact the worst Martial Arts ever created.

    There isn't any easy way around this. Notice that the problem isn't even really just Solars, it's Celestials. Should Sidereals be better at Immaculate styles than DBs? Should Lunars? Alchemicals? Abyssals? If you go by the default way 3e styles are made, the only way to avoid this is to deliberately spike the style by removing the Celestial benefits and forcing everyone to take Terrestrial penalties (well, baking them into the charms with no Terrestrial / Celestial divide), which puts us back at "the vaunted Immaculate styles are actually the weakest martial arts ever created."

    There are a few ways to finesse the problem, of course. The Immaculate styles could do weird stuff that doesn't fit into the normal power structure; this allows you to somewhat ignore the usual Terrestrial / Celestial / Mastery issues. There's certainly effects out there that are equally useful to all splats and which could be given to everyone evenly (even with some small cosmetic advantages for DBs) which wouldn't necessarily make DB Martial Artists dramatically better than DBs who focus on Melee, while still making the Immaculate Styles genuinely impressive... but I'm dubious that you could construct an entire style around this, let alone five, especially when you're also laboring under the requirement that these be some of the most impressive Martial Arts in the game.

    But I suspect what you're going to see instead is that the Immaculate Styles will look like a typical martial art (in terms of having the Terrestrial / Celestial divide and perhaps even some Mastery effects), plus some flashy-but-ultimately-not-that-powerful DB-only advantages and possibly ways for DBs to situationally boost to the Celestial or even Mastery advantages for those styles at some significant cost, so DBs don't feel like they're strictly worse than everyone else at their signature martial arts. This would mean that a DB who pulls out all the stops and pays whatever cost is necessary to boost all the way in the style could match a Sidereal or Solar at it (even if they're paying more for the privilege), and even exceed them in certain ways due to the unique benefits... while balancing both the costs and the available benefits so they don't break the game or render DB Melee obsolete. It'd still be a tricky thread-the-needle exercise, but it'd at least be possible to satisfy all the various goals that way.

    (As an aside, though, regarding whether the Immaculate Styles are just for Dragon-Blooded: They aren't. The Dragon-Blooded already have charms that are just for Dragon-Blooded, they're the Dragon-Blooded charmset. The fact that Sidereals, Abyssals, Lunars, and, yes, Solars can learn and excel at the Martial Arts that Dragon-Blooded consider iconic to their religion is a feature, not a bug; if they wanted the Immaculate Styles to be "only for DBs", they would be sets of DB charms, not Martial Arts. The purpose of Martial Arts is to be something a wide variety of people can learn and excel at.)

It's a concrete step to dovetail with why Immaculates learn them (other than just handwaving "It's their culture") - if they just get Terrestrial level effects for the Immaculate Styles, period, and that's all you get, why not just learn Five Dragon Style or something like that? Why is there this cultural tradition of learning the Immaculate Styles?

                (And on a tangent to that, I think thinking about other elemental and Terrestrial styles from 2e definitely makes it clearer for me about why it doesn't necessarily feel great for Solars to have Mastery effects, whether it's mechanically necessary or not.

                It's not because they are, as styles, "for Terrestrials" or "their thing". It's because they represent a kind of spiritual connection and essence of the Dragonblooded.

                It's like if you had Lunar Hero style coming back... or better let's imagine there was a good style for Lunars called "Five Faces of Luna" or something like that, a style which lovingly fitted techniques to different phases of the moon and incarnations of Luna. And it's clearly meant to represent a path where Lunars get spiritually closer to Luna. And the books say that this is what Lunars believe. Then then you said that Solars get the highest benefits, and they're actually totally inaccessible to Lunars. Doesn't feel good, right?

                Whereas with something like Five Dragon Style, iconic to a splat and just as much for that splat, but not with any spiritual undertones of perfecting their essence, I'm happy to see a Solar punch another Solar in the face with some extra Mastery effect that pushes it up to Solar level, all while screaming "Five dragons fight as one!", or "The Dragonblooded host united shall never be defeated!" something like that. It's not like anything is getting lost in the same way. Or Falling Blossom Style or Jade Mountain Style, even if they're sort of elemental, it's fine, because there isn't that idea that there is a deep connection to the essence of the Elemental Dragons there. Go wild.

                It sort of has me pondering whether it would be best to just drop the Immaculate Styles and that whole tradition, if they have to do them with Celestial and Mastery effects inaccessible to DBs. Might be better just not to have them, than have the tradition ring false and hollow because the kind of spiritual union that is represented by the highest levels of the style does not actually unlock for Dragonblooded. Then just have the Immaculate Order practice more generic elemental styles that are not in themselves a "gate" and just something they do in their monasteries to focus their mind and soul).

                    Like, how do you think the Dragon-Blooded should react to an Anathema who has learned the Immaculate Styles? Outrage, sure, but should this Anathema be less dangerous than an Anathema who practiced any other martial art? That doesn't make sense. Fighting an Anathema who practices the Immaculate Styles should be an epic fight as you put down the would-be usurper who has profaned your most sacred techniques. You can't credibly run that if the Immaculate Styles are, for most people, the worst martial arts ever printed.

                    I would also add a note as well that the reason for the Immaculate Styles (if they're kept in the game!) to be stronger for Dragonblooded isn't *just* a sop to the feelings of Dragonblooded players or anything.                     I doubt Five Dragon Style will exist in 3e.

                    If your question is "why learn the Immaculate Styles and not something else," though, let me flip that around and ask you this: Do you want a game where the situation is "why learn anything but the Immaculate Styles?" Do you want a DB who learns Melee and not the Immaculate Styles to be noticeably weaker in combat than an Immaculate stylist?

                    This is something the game did in the past, yes. I don't think it was a good decision (in practice it resulted in players contorting themselves to explain why their DB had Immaculate MA even when it made no sense; and thematically it lead to players viewing the Immaculate Styles as the "real" pinnacle of DB power, which I don't think they were ever meant to be.) The Immaculate Martial Arts are supposed to be a big deal, but they shouldn't express this at the expense of Dragon-Blooded Melee.

                    It's like if you had Lunar Hero style coming back... or better let's imagine there was a good style for Lunars called "Five Faces of Luna" or something like that, a style which lovingly fitted techniques to different phases of the moon and incarnations of Luna. And it's clearly meant to represent a path where Lunars get spiritually closer to Luna.
                    The Hero styles have been dumped from the game precisely because that was a bad idea. Creating martial arts and then trying to turn them into native charms special to that Exalt type is completely pointless; that's not what Martial Arts are. Martial Arts are a universal charmset; they represent talents that the Exalted share among themselves and compete with each other at. They're a common ground between splats. The Sidereals are partially an exception (they have Sidereal Martial Arts as their "secret techniques"), but even then it isn't totally unique to them.

                    It sort of has me pondering whether it would be best to just drop the Immaculate Styles and that whole tradition, if they have to do them with Celestial and Mastery effects inaccessible to DBs. Might be better just not to have them, than have the tradition ring false and hollow because the kind of spiritual union that is represented by the highest levels of the style does not actually unlock for Dragonblooded. Then just have the Immaculate Order practice more generic elemental styles that are not in themselves a "gate" and just something they do in their monasteries to focus their mind and soul).
                    That would be a mistake. As I said above, there are ways to express the idea that the Immaculate styles give DBs a sort of unique enlightenment and understanding (expressed as DB-only benefits that are flashy and impressive but which don't give you overwhelming raw power) while still letting Sidereals, Solars, etc. eke more raw power out of the style in certain ways.

                    Fundamentally, if you want to play a character who is all about being the best in terms of raw power, and who judges their competence based on being the best in raw power at their specialty, DBs are not the splat for you. People hate Solars for expressing that theme, but then they turn around and try to measure Dragon-Blooded by the same yardstick, and it's a mistake. That's not what the Dragon-Blooded are for (and that's, therefore, not what they'd get out of the Immaculate Style -- although it should of course still be one of the best options for them, just not head-and-shoulders above everything else.) Their connection to the Immaculate styles is, partially, spiritual in nature, so the benefits they get with them are not necessarily going to be of the same sort of raw power that a Sidereal or Solar who practices the styles will be able to express.

                    I will be pretty blunt here: I believe that part of the reason DBs have the Immaculate Styles is so they go up against Anathema who have learned it (which means sometimes losing and shouting "it cannot be!") Like, that's literally the main thing the Immaculate Styles offer over other charms. It's not the only reason they exist by a long shot (the monastic structure is important), but if you didn't want that, you could have the Immaculates teach secret Dragon-Blooded charms or somesuch rather than Martial Arts. If you're playing a DB, you get to turn this around and beat down the upstart Anathema who profanes your sacred techniques -- but it's supposed to be hard. It's not supposed to be "haha, thank goodness this idiot Anathema wasted his time learning the Immaculate styles; that makes him much easier to beat down than other Anathema!"

                    The idea that the Immaculate Styles are just for Dragon-Blooded is an in-setting conceit. In terms of the stories they're meant to tell, they are intended to be sometimes used in ways that violate this.
