        Something I find useful for sourcing boss mechanics. Looking at 2nd edition charms (ideally 2.5 since it's mechanically much closer) which haven't been included in Ex3 or were changed significantly and trying to explore the though process of them not making the cut.

        i.e. Adamant Skin Technique (2.5 version) lets you reduce the damage of an attack to 0 after it hits you for 8 motes, it has a built in weakness that can shut down when you use the charm but it's a pretty permissive one. In a 3rd edition combat environment that's nuts because free excellencies are gone and damage is now built up via successive actions via withering attacks, the ability to just take a successful decisive to 0 after it hits is huge!

        So a character with this charm and no other considerations would have an absolute game changing power. You'd basically have to keep making cheap/low damage decisive attacks to try and mote tap them. There's two easy says to way to weaken a defensive charm from a previous edition, the first is to apply the Perilous keyword, which would kind of turn it into an all or nothing version of Heavenly Guardian Defence promising invulnerability when you had even 1i but vulnerable in initiative crash with no scope to add something like Protection of Celestial Bliss. I don't like it, there's too much incentive to crash your opponent and dismantle them and this makes it mandatory. The other would be to make it once per scene with a reset condition. I like this far more. Essentially the proposed charm would be the best parts of Ruin Abrasing Shrug and Seven Shadow Evasion (it's a bit stronger than that but we're still in broad strokes here).

        There's still good reasons to turn this down as a custom charm. It would fufill the role of two charms in different trees and notably stealing Dodge's big panic button charm for Resistance in the process.

        For a boss though? That's a handy way to condense their charms and reduce your mental load as a Storyteller. So I'll give it the same reset condition as Ruin Abrasing Shrug and since having no way to resist your initiative being rest to base is both really powerful and extremely annoying to the person you're doing it to I'll add the initiative loss clause from the Dragon-Blooded Resistance charm Perfected Scales of the Dragon.

        Perfected Skin of Adamant (8m; Reflexive; Instant; Decisive-only): After being hit by an attack, but before damage is rolled reduce all damage to 0. Attackers lose two Initiative instead of resetting to base. Once per scene, unless reset by taking no withering or decisive damage on three separate damage rolls.

            Originally posted by Lioness View Post
            There's still good reasons to turn this down as a custom charm. It would fufill the role of two charms in different trees and notably stealing Dodge's big panic button charm for Resistance in the process.
            I generally agree with what you wrote, but I don't think that two panic buttons with slightly different functions are bad design (the hypothetical Resistance one doesn't prevent other possible effects of being hit, like flying a couple of range bands or being poisoned; the Dodge one doesn't protect you against Enviromental Damage), quite the contrary. I could easily imagine even five or six different panic buttons in different places. It shouldn't be to easy to get two of them.

            Generally, attrition gets boring mostly when the pattern is clear. If you can perfectly dodge an attack using willpower, you could easily skip next [willpower] attack and start watching the fight again when it gets interesting. On the other hand, complicated attrition in which you have several distinct factors to follow and individually they can go up and down (on of which is "can I use my panic button") makes for a tactically deep game.

            Currently, we already have two different panic buttons; one in Dogde and one in Occult (that charm that makes you impossible to kill, unless you are at you last health level). I think that another one with Resistance with slightly different effects and rather deep in a tree could be an interesting addition.

                                    There is the old 2e adage of "Don't fuck with perfects until you fix lethality."

                                    One thing that I like about 3e that I wouldn't want to change is that, usually, an enormous effort brought into an attack requires a likewise enormous effort in defense. Either that or likely that it will still punch through and do something. If you get attacked by some giant enemy who's got 35 initiative and double 9s on the damage roll something is still going to happen. If you use Heavenly Guardian Defense to nullify it you spent some initiative to do it, if you use Adamant Skin Technique some of that damage is coming through, if you use Uncanny Shroud Defense you probably still lost some health levels from it.

                                    Seven Shadow Evasion is about as close as it gets, because for 4m 1wp you force them to waste all the motes that they put into the attack, but they also only count as having missed, and it's not really able to be spammed much. With really generous level-2 stunts I guess you could safely use it every fight, but that's about it.
