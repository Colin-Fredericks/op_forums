                        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                        And this is why running Lunar or DB games is so much easier...
                        Yes, but Lunars are consistently present as something very different narratively from Solars. Solars are humans, but jut that good. Lunars are human, but ... just with tentacles, weird wall-crawling abilities, and most likely wings?

                        Ok, I guess there are two discussions here which would be great to disentangle:

                        1. How powerful Solars should be, given their narrative role?
                        2. If we decide that a circle of 5 Solars should beatable by a single (human-type) foe, could we make such foes (possibly stepping outside the current design paradigm)?

                        I think that the answer to (2) is simply "yes". If you think that a group of five Abyssals can beat your Solars, then just make an elder Abyssal with five Initiative tracks and possibly some extra shenanigans with mitigating decisive damage by spending motes or Initiative. That's actually simple. I don't want to make too strong claims how it works, but I tried to prepare solutions in this vein and I intend to playtest it.

                        The answer to (1), I guess, is an irreducible disagreement between us. I don't really think that the basic premise of Exalted is that you can literally be the best warrior rules-wise. For me the basic premise is that you are returned hero, completely surpassing everyone around, of the kind that used to rule the Creation, but you're hunted, and now you want to change the world. (A premise very different from Lunars'.) For that premise it's really not necessary that you are literally the best rules-wise. It is important that you are way above average folks though.

                        If in your group you play Solars to be literally at the top of the food chain, then yes, I guess that it would be bad for you to have human or human-like opponents which are fair match for five of these. I definitely see people here that see Solar exactly like this. But I don't think I'm the only one playing Exalted that is more compelled with "returning hero narrative" and doesn't really care about being literally the best. I know that this is how Exalted is often sold on forum, but I don't really see it in corebook of any edition (including second which comes closest to that).

                        Ideally, I would love the Solars to start somewhat weaker and grow significantly stronger, stretching up to uncharted territories past Essence 5 with potential for truly cosmic scale over there. Generally, I'd love to see the games with wider power level spread, and the power level being generally more dependent on Essence rating than on splats (I fully endorse flattening the gaps between splats) and to see more differences in potential rather than raw power.

                        Concerning the Witcher (literal spoilers):


                        Well, a group of competent characters: Geralt, Milva, Angoulême, Caehir, Emiel, faced Vilgefortz and defeated him, but at great cost. He is a good example of Nemesis-like character.

                        The same with Bonhart and Ciri's gang -- consistently depicted as comprising of very strong individuals.

                        But mostly, in books you rarely find the kind of protagonists we see in rpg: five equally super-competent people working together.

                                    I think some of this shades into our previous discussion about how much progression Solar Exalted should have, whether they should start fairly near their peak, and be a break from the whole zero-to-hero thing in favour of players getting to start out playing "Mightiest Returned Heroes... Right NOW!" and dealing direct with being the strongest (which is what 3e devs thought would be a great USP for Exalted!). Or it is more satisfying to have them have a longer Hero's Journey with some degree of building up even before they can have much chance of deal with Second Circle Demons alone on an equal basis in an area of focus (DrLoveMonkey's example on first page being Octavian).

                                    I would tend see Solars as having to be the most generally *skilled* humans and the most *skilled* supernaturally empowered beings, and the magically the strongest of the Exalted. That's a pretty non-negotiable core part of the setting. But I don't tend to see them as requiring of being the strongest, as such, things in any category an absolute sense.

                                    Where there are beings flat out stronger than the Solar Exalted, though they probably should be constrained by being not able to apply it in an easy "human hero" esque manner of course, or a manner which is replicable or lends itself to massive coordination with other beings in the manner that the Exalted host can coordinate. Like, their power's confined to a particular lair or location, it is connected to destabilizing effects on reality (introduces baleful effects of the Wyld, etc), it requires mass sacrifice, they're a one-off behemoth / monster that has inhuman personality and can't be replicated, etc.

                                    But going back to my first paragraph, this topic is all bound up again with how much journey Solars should have, and how much focus is on them having individual power against power to lead a circle in a particular area. (The OP question - now and in pre modified form ; ) - is all kind of bound up in "Which Solars?".)

At least they are explicitly something a Solar could become.

                                                I believe it is reasonable to assume that if something popped up in two editions (or really was mentioned more than once in a single book), then it qualifies, as a thing that could reasonably exist in Exalted. So powerful elder exalted, even if we don't have them as of now, and probably won't see them anytime soon, are not something totally out of scope of how setting works in Exalted broadly construed.

                                                EDIT: Just as an illustration: I could easily imagine that developers decide that in the Exalted: fourth edition there will be no such thing as Caul. If someone were homebrewing a continent-size island at roughly the same place, I would never say that big landmasses on the Western Ocean are completely out of the scope of Exalted. Hypothetically, I could dislike it personally. I could even point out that it distorts the pure symbolism of map-as-governed-by-elements. (Hypothetically. Caul is cool!) But since we've definitely seen such things at some point (actually in two editions), it wouldn't automatically be out of the scope of the game.

                                                        In my opinion: A Circle of Solars should be powerful enough to be able to challenge their elders. Not ridiculously curb stomp, but challenge.

                                                        An elder (whether Solar, Sidereal, Lunar or Other) should be wary about taking on an entire Circle of fresh Solar Exalts (or experienced Dragon-Blooded), but should be able to beat them if they employ a clever strategy (like enlisting the help of a bunch of mortals, or practicing a specific type of Martial Art, or investing in Charms for fighting groups).

                                                        Is giving an elder five Initiative tracks a good way of doing this?
