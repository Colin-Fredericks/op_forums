            Yes... ish. Most everything, up to and including the ground and water, is either a Demon, a piece of a Yozi, or both. Plants are most often part of Second or Third Circle Demons, like Szorny's forests. Compass of Celestial Directions: Malfeas, from 2e, would give you a decent run-down.

            A lot of Infernal Exalted charms seem to mutate people as a price for helping them. Is there a particular point past which one's patient would become demon blooded?
            ST-Fiat, mostly. Being a Creature of Darkness doesnt make you a demon, but being a Demon (or Demon-blooded) almost always means being a Creature of Darkness.

            Do demons have something that marks them as apart from the various divinities in Creation?
            Metaphysical and political ties to the Yozi, mostly. Gods and Elementals largely arise naturally from the Essence of Creation, or were shaped for a purpose before the Divine Revolution. Demons are either Yozi loyalists during the war that were thrown into He'll alongside their masters, or more commonly slave-species created by a Yozi's sub-soul. One could make the argument that Elementals are to Gaia what Demons of the First Circle are to the Yozi; the distinction is largely political, with differences enforced by the Yozi's surrender oaths.

            Is there a list somewhere with a summary of the various martial arts in previous editions?
            A neat one? No. The Ninjasensei wiki kinda has one, but most are very scatter-shot.

            For 2e or 3e? Crane would be your go-to for 3e, where anyone can learn any Style, but in 2e is was a Celestial-tier Style.

            Assassin-y styles like Throne Shadow, Charcoal March of Spiders, Laughing Monster, and Dreaming Pearl Courtisan can use whips, lassos, or rope darts, depending on the style. Ebon Shadow can use fighting chains.

            For weird, Silver Voiced Nightingale turns your voice into a literal weapon. Like, you can Fus Roh Dah at people, it doesnt cost Motes or anything.

            Game isn't published yet, unfortunately, so speculation off of Exalted Essence is about all we can go off of.

            Yes, it's just going to be sickly, spooky, or both.

                The light of the Green Sun causes the metals of Malfeas to spring up in forms of flower and fungus. The demons of Hell can process these into alcoholic beverages.

                Originally posted by Cauthon View Post
                One could make the argument that Elementals are to Gaia what Demons of the First Circle are to the Yozi; the distinction is largely political, with differences enforced by the Yozi's surrender oaths.
                I think an argument like that would be predicated on an assumption that Gaia is equivalent to Creation itself, which is largely a subject of fanon, not generally supported by the text and often contradicted in it.

                Gods, elementals, and demons have different origins that push them towards different functions in ways that often make their appearances and behaviour distinct. A god is a spirit that has responsibility for some aspect of the world. If it's a Terrestrial god it often embodies some local feature, a Dog of Unbroken Earth to preside over some section of trackless wilderness, a field god to watch over a cropland, a Bloody Hand to witness all murders within a city quarter, while Celestial gods are provided with purviews as a function of a grand bureaucracy (and tend to be influenced by their responsibility over the centuries or millenia). The nature of what they're connected to or some of the ways it can translate through symbolism can render a god a bit alien to humanity, but there's a tangible, concrete connection to parts of the world.

                Elementals are more primal, more visceral, in the way they emerge directly out of the substances of the world and participate in their functions being carried out. You can have a god of avalanches who has responsibility for recording and reporting all avalanches that occur on a mountain, and ensuring that avalanches occur when Heaven decrees it, but an Earth elemental is more like a personified avalanche and a likely being to cause avalanches to occur (even if the usual forces that can do so allow them to happen without intervention). Elementals can evince and promote the symbolic connotations of their element, things like passion for fire and stoicism for earth, but on the whole they're less focused on the abstract than gods tend to be.

                Demons are... they could be seen more as personifications of abstract principles that are fully detached from the world. Or more than that, they can be seen as entities focused on particular obsessions. First Circle Demons have a tendency to be very function oriented, and given to a specific function; blood apes are violence, agatae are steeds, neomah are courtesans. But they often don't arrive at such things in a direct fashion, they're composed of strange form and express their function through odd quirks of behaviour; an agata is a remarkably beautiful wasp given to incomprehensible philosophical musings that occasionally fill them with a rapture that causes them to buck their riders to their deaths, a neomah takes payment for services from taking bits of flesh from each client that are cobbled together into strange hybrid children (that are characteristically abandoned).

                Second Circle Demons are built in much the same way, just that their purpose and function tends to be more elaborate and nuanced, or have greater scope or entail deeper skill. They're like hero figures of Hell, tending to conduct themselves in adventurous fashion to acquire what is needed for their purpose or pursue their wants. And the principles they embody tend to not be things that you could reduce down to one word. You have a Second Circle Demon who is a blacksmith, but not as an essential, archetypal smith ala Hephaestus; she reforges living beings into objects that can more effectively carry out their dreams and ambitions.

                And Third Circle Demons... in some ways they're back to being more single-minded in the manner of the First Circles, but with far more vast scope or harnessing greater arcane force. The Third Circle Demon Erembour does not scheme, she does not have elaborate plans or varied relationships; she wanders Hell (and occasionally Creation), sounds her horn, and all who hear it are called to the night. What that actually entails can be varied (and possibly even applied at random), being anything from inducing people to engage in activities that might be seen as relegated to the night (violence, conspiracy, theft, lovemaking), it can literally summon people out to admire the sublime beauty of the night, or it can warp them into monsters that will never again suffer the light of day. In a sense, they each have a particular way that they warp the substance or inhabitants of the world, and simply go about doing it as they please. The only thing that gets much difference out of them are things that act as specific obstacles to their endeavours.

                It's a bit more tricky to convey demons through summary than it is for gods or elementals, since the Exalted idea for what they are is very particular to this property; it works better to see them firsthand. Still, I once saw a good point from the game's original developer, Geoff Grabowski; where the stories of Michael Moorcock portrayed demons as personifying chaos, in Exalted that role belongs to the Wyld and the Fair Folk, so demons instead are functionaries of a kind of Order. It's just not an idea of order that humans would necessarily arrive it, and it's often dangerous to be around.

                                    Some more stuff on spirits to remember, and this is taking into account all editions:

                                    Gods
                                    These are the beings created to manage Creation from gods of rivers to the Incarna. Generally charged with managing their domain accoridng to a general dictate of Heaven to help reinforce Creation from becoming the Wyld. 3e goes into most depth on this process, wtih destiny-forging and stuff. Gods arise either through the weirdness of their specific species of god (like bloody hands), apotheosis of a mortal being into godhoold, or gods just going Greek on things nad having kids who happen to be gods too, even if in some cases it might look llike say, Athena or Aphrodite. This siad, gods are "fungible". A bloody hand could in theory become a celestial lion if that is where they're trasnferred to, but might keep some of their prior job's elements with them as they move along.

                                    The gods that sided wiht the titans are not demons. They are a category knwon as forbidden god, which is something that 3e has kept as a term as applied to Nebiru. Basically rogue gods.

                                    Demons
                                    Demons are the spirit compositon of a Yozi pantheon. Third CIrlce Demons are the souls of the Yozis, Second Circles the souls of the Third Circles. First Circle Demons are the deliberate, instincutla, or unconciosu creation of demons of thigher circles and usually represent species of demon. Demons are not fungible like gods, and don't have the same powers or portfolio as them. That said, magic exists that can transform a demon into a god.

                                    Elemental
                                    Naturally material spirits composed of the elements of Creation. Generally different species of elemental exist with degrees of power within them like wood spiders, garda birs, and gem lords.

                                    As I note din the prior post, their origins did shift a bit between editions. In 1e and 2e, they're the remnants of Five Elemental Titans destroyed in the Primordial War. These took care of the elements before that and now the elementals that boil-up in Creation as needed fulfill that role. In 3e, elementals appear to just have always been there, and they are part of the underlying machinery of Creation's ecosystems.

                                    When an elemental garners enough power they become a lesser elemental dragon. In 1e and 2e there was also a category of greater elemental dragon for ones who became big apoclypse dragons, but 3e runs wiht those still being lesser elemental dragons. The term for that I bring up is that "Greater than the Five is oxymoronic" seems to be the goal.

                                    Ghosts
                                    The souls of a dead person. Normal ghosts represent a person's higher soul and represent memory, attachments, personality, and so on. The lower or bestial soul represents cravings, passion, and all that, and is what becomes a hungry ghost. While the term hun and po from Mandarin were used for these in 1e through the 3e corebook, I think the plan is to just use higher and lower souls for 3e as it's trying to not appropriate non-English unless that is the best term, which the Chinese ones werne't quite analogous enough.

                                    Misc. Spirits
                                    This is a thing that 3e has more of, or at least, points out more of, than had been before. These always are implied, but never been talked about in-depth. The big examples of these right now i would say are:

                                    The Five Elemetnal Dragons - These are Gaia's children. They're beings independent of her which seem to represent or embody the Five Elements which comprise Creation. We honeslty don't know a lot about them besides thier creation of the Dragon-Blooded and thier being Gaia's kids, and bieng biggo dragons that the term "lesser' connotates. The retiring of the term greater elemental dragon is in part I think because these are meant to be the Platonic Elemental Beings. One might argue that elementals have them as a template in a simlar way the Titans did in 1e and 2e I guess, but different relationship.

                                    Gaian Spirits - 2e had the term deva for a non-bound Primordial, but 3e is probably just going to call them well, souls of the respective titan since we don't need a term for something so rare in Creation. Gaia has some souls lingering in Creation: the Worm-Eaten Woman in The Realm is apparently a Second Circle soul of hers. She is noted as having 20 hidden souls in the 2ebook Lands of Creation, and while I tend to not take that into 3e, it's a neat idea t ome I stick with.

                                    Autochthonian Spirits - Autochthon's souls were pretty different between 1e and 2e. In 1e, you basically had the Core + Eight Divine Ministers, their sub-souls, and then various machine spirits and elementals that filled in for gods and elementals of Creation.

                                    In 2e, you had this weird soul-hacking thing which made the Core directly Autochthon, and the Ministers having to collaborate for souls and all this. And also apparently some thing where each elemental was made as-neededb y one of them. And machine spirits also spawned out htere. Kind of a mess. The Compass tried to simplify it but yeah.

                                    We don't know 3e yet, but my personal guess is something more like 1e's, where it's a pretty clear titanic soul patheon, with machine spirits taking a role similar to First Circle Demons, and elementals just being elementals ala Creation in that they're just natural outgrowths of the elements of Autochthon, however thsoe are done.

                                    Pattern Spiders - At least in 3e. They're notably never called gods, but are still like, spirits that do their little task even if it's not god-like. They kind of feel at least to more in the "misc." category now.

                                    Five Days Darkness - Not been confirmed either way, but in 3e's context he might be an example of a "None of the above" spirit rather than an unemployed god.

                                    Plasmids - There's all sorts of just fucking weird monster-spirit things that are dead but never alive down in the Underworld.

                                    And stuff.

                                                            Clay and a truckload of Magical Materials, if I remember right, which undergoes a transmutation process as part of the creation of the Alchemical's body. Unlike most Exalts, Alchemicals are a body built around a soul in such a way that they are exalted from their first breath, rather than being Chosen for their Second Breath. Most of which has to do with the... atypical nature of Autocthonia's cycle of reincarnation.

                                                            How does being a Green Sun Prince work, like politically? Are they under the direct command of the Yozis, or like, given a large degree of freedom so the Yozis can claim innocence of their actions? Does a Green Sun Prince swear themselves to a particular Yozi, or is it more just to the cause?
                                                            Only speaking to 2e - all fifty are nominally a part of the Yozi hierarchy, just below Third Circle Souls but closely on-par with Second Circles. They answer to their "Favored" Yozi usually, who is the one who actually chose them, and have ties to their "Caste" Yozi, who contributed their specific influences to that Caste. OOC, if you chose your Caste Yozi as your boss, you'd just choose a Favored Yozi that you resonate with.
