    Okay, show me what Falcon Style’s high-flying kung fu antics can do.


    Oh, actually uses the term “high-flying”, that’s cute. It’s all leaping into people and working one’s way to a high spot to launch down from, plus a bit of grappling to position them and knock them to the floor for that drop. Also described as being very showy, of a kind liable to draw a lot of people to watch the fights… hmm, suddenly want to rewatch Jet Li’s Fearless. Probably popular with children; children are pure, they know who’s the strongest. It’s all about the arms and headbutts, accommodating cestus, boots, and the slightest concession with tiger claws and improvised weapons, although it gets to use light armour, and obviously benefits from having Athletics and Dodge.

    So first, that Falcon Takes Flight, allowing them to make grand leaping manoeuvres in the form of adding half their Martial Arts rating as successes to disengage, rush, and rise from prone actions when they’re stunted to include jumping and flying motions, or else make reflexive move actions straight upwards away from an enemy following attack (I suppose that’s something beneficial for when there’s a tree branch or ceiling fixture or something to grab on to… or maybe it is just valid for getting to a balcony or hill top). Mastery allows either movement to higher elevations to let the successes not count towards the dice cap. This sounds like something that could be fairly useful even for just dipping into the style.

    They get to start actually attacking with Swift Talon Strike (which is another root Charm), letting an attack made following moving into close range with an enemy get an automatic success on attacks, that reroll 1s on the damage roll or to control a successful grapple, with 2s also given that bonus if the movement consisted of descending from a higher position. I’m seeing now that the descriptions of flying aren’t merely metaphorical, it just happens to be a style whose method can be readily complemented by a human that can actually fly. I’m okay with that; I don’t want Styles that could only be used by such a person, but something where it incidentally complements is fine, even desirable. Mastery is similar to the last one, success is non-Charm.

    Raptor’s Fatal Descent allows decisive attacks at a lower enemy (one either knocked prone or at a point beneath the stylist) to add Athletics or Strength as extra successes to a damage roll, and drains Initiative for every 10 on that roll; it comes with the slight drawback of being left prone, but one doesn’t get penalties for being so (and can’t us the attack again until risen, although I would take that as a given). Dropping down usually counts as a movement action, but not for Mastery.

    I’ve got to say, not as a criticism but just an observation, for the way the style is described, it does seem to go straight into the aerial attacks. Mind, given the description as being showy, I guess I can see the idea of going for the more big ticket stuff early and then working in the tactical setups deeper in.

    So what does the Form Charm allow? Spreading out their arms to assume a nimble stance from which grapple control rolls get double 9s and Athletics added to rounds of control for the sake of throws and slams. They also get reflexive move actions without needing to roll for ascension, and falling damage gets halved, as well as improved Defence against anybody at a lower elevation who doesn’t have Reaching attacks. Reflexive activation comes from succeeding with a high threshold over opponents in movement rolls and grapple controls. Does definitely feel like a good tactical crux for the surrounding attacks.

    Hmm hmm. Hmm.

    God help me, the entire time I’m reading all of this I have this theme song stuck in my head:


    Tell me it’s wrong!

    Okay, Grasping Raptor Claw allows one to make nasty grapples in which a restrain or drag action gets to be followed by a decisive savaging attack, throw or slam, both on the same turn. It costs Initiative, but that gets refunded if the opponent is rendered unconscious or dead. This feels like a good setup for those who can’t fly, pulling an enemy into range of something that can be climbed and then immobilising them. Terrestrials are limited by needing to treat the two actions as a flurry, with appropriate penalties.

    Talons Greet Prey improves their defensive options a bit, giving bonus Defence against an attack at close range, and allowing a counterattack grapple if the assault fails, with 1s on the original attack added as non-Charm dice to the attack and control rolls. Can only be used once a scene, but gets reset by incapacitating a grappled enemy (especially helped by some of the prior Charms) and then getting to Initiative 12, but it also has the unique benefit of not having its limit used up at all if the grappled opponent is immediately thrown or slammed.

    Hero-Killing Death Dive, that’s a rather threatening name for something in a Style with this character. Ohhh, it’s for that cool Black Widow manoeuvre, with the head grapple and the pulling somebody down; those are always a treat to see people actually do. I need to look back at that martial arts blog… anyway grapple gambit gets double 9s on attack roll, and winning control immediately renders opponent prone and takes Initiative equal to any rolled numbers that provide double successes for the attack roll. Sounds very helpful for resetting Talons Greet Prey.

    Neck-Piercing Hook is a much flashier rendition of the idea, with its thing of grabbing them in the legs and then spinning while up in the air; that lets them have a longer ranged throw from a grapple, and on impact the opponent is forced to make an Athletics roll against a difficulty based on surrendered rounds of control, and if they fail they get to suffer a second damage roll; it’s limited to dice equal to successes on the first one, but gets to ignore soak or Hardness. It’s also a Charm that gets stronger at higher Essence, being able to increase the throwing range in exchange for halving the second damage roll. Terrestrials are limited in dice on the second damage roll, but not too severely.

    Raking Talon Kick has a nasty sounding strike, with the spinning from behind to catch an enemy in the face with both legs. The mechanics look a bit intricate… has to be used on an enemy that was thrown or slammed immediately before, letting it be a decisive attack that gets a damage bonus from the rounds of control used to make the preceding throw or slam. A proper stunt to use the environment to make the elaborate manoeuvre allows the kick to benefit from surprise attack rules, and Raptor’s Final Descent can enhance it without that Charm’s usual restrictions. Terrestrials don’t get to add more bonus damage than half of their Initiative.

    And finally, Doom Plummets Down. Needs a grapple with at least five rounds of control to give up, and throwing an enemy into a hard surface, upon which a reflexive decisive attack is launched. Player gets to come up with their own format for a signature move, and stunt dice for describing it get added to the damage roll as well, and they get to make the initial throw decisive and let the reflexive attack go before Initiative is reset. It can be enhanced even further by using Raking Talon Kick… ah, that Charm and Raptor’s Final Descent are normally Simple Charms, getting to combo them with these things is a big deal. And as a last normal benefit, they get to leap into range with an enemy thrown further with Neck-Piercing Hook reflexively, although it uses up the movement action. Mastery allows a successfully struck enemy to be reflexively rushed or disengaged with automatic success, without movement being used up, and Terrestrials can only use it on crashed enemies and don’t get the option to use it with a withering throw.


    Not much to say in summary of Falcon Style, it’s another fairly solid thing. Does seem like it would be a useful thing for dipping, in order to get some handy supplements to other Styles.

        All right, a solid foundation was built with more grounded Styles, now let’s indulge in some esoteric weirdness with Laughing Monster.


        The implication that the actual Style as a whole was originated by Fair Folk, developed by their dream-stealing antics, is interesting. I have a lingering affection for the old descriptions of Fair Folk limitations with Martial Arts, but I can see how that might be contrary to the interests of making them a lot less arcane and convoluted. And hey, it lends itself to a background in which a Lunar discovered a Style by coaxing it from a raksha’s remains.

        As for the Style, it’s described as being all irritation confusion and humiliation to put enemies off balance. I like the description of the training methods, internalizing a bit of discord and lack of shame. I also enjoy the idea that it’s primarily a style for people on the margins. It employs things like slaps and leg sweeps, wields the staff, war fan and whip, can’t be used with armour, and while its footwork naturally employs Dodge, it’s also distinct in benefiting from Abilities such as Presence and Socialize, and even a bit of Occult for the more unusual mystical elements.

        Begins with Furiously Stalling Destiny. Stylist gets bonus Evasion against an enemy, and the first attack made on the one who did it before the end of the stylist’s next turn gets a die of post-soak withering damage, or die of regular decisive damage, while gambits get an automatic success to attack and Initiative rolls, all to represent the idea of somebody taking in violence directed at them and pointing it back at an opponent. Its bonuses can also be stacked repeatedly for the same opponent attacking repeatedly, a chain that breaks if the fighter goes for somebody else. Masters get that option familiar to Lunars to activate the Charm after an enemy attack has already been rolled, while Terrestrials are limited to their Essence in number of times the Charm can stack.

        Deeper-Into-Trouble Technique allow one to infuriate an enemy by enhancing a decisive damage roll with double 10s and the level of an enemy’s negative Tie as bonus dice. The resulting fury means that the enemy can get Willpower if they immediately attack and successfully crash or incapacitate the stylist, and gives a decent penalty to attacks made on anybody else. That penalty actually lasts the whole scene, unless the infuriated opponent successfully hits the stylist, although having gone for somebody else first forfeits the Willpower bonus.

        I’m getting a good feel for this Style; feels reminiscent of Black Claw, but with a very different emotional tenor.

        Hmm, Form Charm already. Laughing Monster Form allows one to assume an obscure and unpredictable stance, with a different take on being surrounded by illusory arms. Distract and disarm gambits are enhanced with dice equal to the stylist’s Occult, and when an attacker from close range misses and loses Initiative, the fighter can make a decisive counterattack for dice equal to the lost Initiative that ignore Hardness, and don’t reset Initiative. Or they can counter with distract and disarm gambits, that use Initiative as normal, but get bonus attack dice equal to lost Initiative. Terrestrials need to spend some of their own Initiative to make counterattacks, and the Form can be entered reflexively by either provoking somebody into battle against them with influence, or use dodging to make an enemy’s Initiative drop below their own. While I imagine that there may be later Charms to help on the dodging front, there are definitely ways in which a Solar or Lunar’s own Charms could help them harness the benefits of this Form.

        Inauspicious Moment for Attack allows enemy assaults to be clashed with a slightly enhanced distract gambit, which if successful counts as a dodge for the user, and creates an opening for a reflexive decisive attack for the beneficiary of the distraction. It can also capitalise on a later Charm, Unitary Being Forge, to allow the clash to be made with a decisive attack, with a special source of base damage and ignoring Hardness, which can also be enhanced by powers to improve distract gambits. Terrestrials have to treat the action as their attack for the round, meaning they can’t use it if they’ve already attacked.

        Using Subtle Hammer lets a stylist follow distract or disarm gambits with reflexive decisive attacks that ignore Hardness, rolling for their damage the extra successes on their gambit’s roll, and getting back the Initiative spent on the gambit if the enemy is incapacitated. It can only be used once per scene, but has the straightforward reset condition of successfully clashing an enemy with the prior attack. Mastery allows the Form Charm’s bonus dice for gambits to be added to the attack rolls of this Charm. There’s a good image going, of wearing an enemy down with lots of small decisive attacks while continuously messing them up with distraction, disarming and enough annoyance to keep their attention focused on the stylist. Furiously Stalling Destiny probably helps a bit with continuously building the stylist’s Initiative to help those attacks and gambit.

        Right, the style was described as having been employed to turn a Raksha court upon itself, and that’s coming in with Thieves Fall Out. A decisive attack can be rolled against two enemies close to each other, Initiative divided evenly between them; if they both take a hit, the disrupting impacts cause them to stumble over one another whenever close by, meaning that for the rest of the scene they both lose Defence if they’re in close range, as well as losing a bit of Initiative at the start of each of their turns, which go to the stylist. It can also be used to attack battle groups, enhancing the attack’s damage dice, causing the group to have a persistent Defence penalty, and to lose a point of Magnitude (pop pop) at the start of each turn in lieu of the smaller scale drip, that the stylist won’t get as Initiative.

        That starts getting worse with Friendship-Dissolving Venom, through which dodging an attacks allows it to be redirected against somebody else within its original range; the attack has all of the power directed at the stylist when it resolves against the new character, and if Initiative is lost for it being withering, the stylist gets that. The redirected attack can also get bonuses from Furiously Stalling Destiny. If the roll for the redirected attack is also greater than its victim’s Resolve, they form a Minor Tie for the attacker to represent their outrage, with contrary positive Ties being suppressed for the scene. The stylist even gets to make a reflexive Presence or Socialize roll to persuade the newly angered character to act in a manner antagonistic towards the one who hit them. The Charm has a Willpower cost, that is waived if attacker and victim have already been affected by the prior Charm. Masters can further enhance the redirected attacks with supplemental Laughing Monster Charms, while Terrestrials only get to redirect attacks at non-trivial enemies when they’re crashed.

        You know who this reminds me of? Heysha Black Asp. And, while I’ve always been kind of dubious about almost everything about her schtick, I did like the way that the comic in Manual Lunars portrayed her fighting style. I like something that I feel would more effectively portray that than Lunar Hero Style really did.

        Right, that thing referred to before, Unitary Being Forge, errr… bit complicated. So the stylist hits an enemy who possesses a negative Tie of which the stylist is aware with a decisive attack, getting to reroll failed dice equal to the strength of the Intimacy. If the damage is at least the target’s base Resolve, the Intimacy detaches and becomes embodied as a semi-tangible imp, a mental shaping power that appropriate magic can defend against. The imp remains by its originator’s side constantly and indelibly, in manifest form for the length of a scene; during that, the victim can’t benefit from the Tie, but still takes its drawbacks, and their base Guile is halved by the imp giving away their feelings. Once per round, the stylist can have the imp grant the lower of Intimacy strength or Essence as extra damage dice to decisive attacks against the victim, from any source. A gambit with a difficulty of just the Intimacy’s strength can be made to destroy the imp, but the victim’s Guile is the basis for its Defence, and its only vulnerable to magic that can hurt the dematerialized; magic to permanently slay spirits weakens the Intimacy by one step. This one is some carp dragon weirdness to me, and for a Martial Art that came from the Fair Folk that’s a real boon.

        Shuffling the Pieces harnesses that power further, by allowing the stylist to take hold of the imp when close enough and wrapping it around their weapon, a state in which the creature can’t enhance attacks against the victim, although the other effects remain, and while the imp is still vulnerable to destruction, the stylist is always considered to provide defend other. The benefit for having done this is that the imp can be imbued into a decisive attack, to allow the Intimacy’s strength in damage dice to be rerolled, and if used on somebody besides the imp’s originator, and attacks of damage at least the target’s Resolve embed the imp in the mind, which causes the Intimacy to move from the prior character to the new one as a mental shaping effect. If the new Intimacy fundamentally opposes a strong existing one or is generally incompatible with their identity, they develop a Minor Derangement appropriate to the contradiction in their personality. Terrestrials can never fully transfer an Intimacy, only imbuing it in a new character as Minor, and weakening it by a step in the originator. I guess this is useful for spreading promoted bad Intimacies around, to create further group discord.

        And finally, the thus far uncommon Essence 4 Martial Arts Charm, called Dancing Wind-Monster Transformation. A seven mote, one Willpower surcharge can be added to Laughing Monster Form, allowing the user to diffuse themselves as an immaterial body in the form of an inchoate cloud of mocking madness. Allies within short range will gain the benefits of Laughing Monster Form, and their attacks and dodges can benefit from the stylist employing their own Reflexive and Supplemental Charms in the style for them. The stylist can only normally attack immaterial characters within short range, but can also go for physical characters being followed by imps, and can use Shuffling the Pieces to discharge imps through the attacks of allies. The character pays for this by becoming vulnerable from a slightly longer range to attacks on the immaterial, and the Charm automatically ends if they end a turn with no enemies within attack range, only being able to resume it with Laughing Monster Form again. I’m not entirely clear on whether the immaterial character can still move. The limit to Terrestrials is that their cloud form is still vulnerable to material attacks, but with better Evasion if they’re not enhanced by magic to target the immaterial.



        I think Laughing Monster strikes a good balance; it includes elements for attacking in a more social, esoteric fashion, but isn’t strictly reliant on them, being perfectly serviceable as a distinct and stylized method of fighting in a straightforward manner. One thing I’ll say is that, to my eyes, it’s an odd case of an Art that might require some complementary Charms to maximize the value of some of its powers (including its Form), but I might just be missing something.

                    Okay, finishing off the Martial Arts in reasonably short order with Swaying Grass Style.


                    The image of this one is interesting to me, being a more conceptually grounded one that is elevated a bit not so much by Essence but context and legacy, basing itself on the apocryphal origins of capoeira as it does. Even out of character, it has a bit of an interesting history, having been an Ink Monkeys product during a time of a bit of game drought, that drew some attention for its ideas but was hampered a bit by being Terrestrial; I’d say it’s among the more prominent to benefit from the overhaul. In any case, its internal history expresses itself in the thesis of using a lot of feint and misdirection to leave enemies open to heavy kicking attacks, as well as some more discrete weapons such as baton, boot and knives, although obviously not compatible with armour that would not have been available to originators. It’s complements of Dodge and Performance are really a given.

                    First Charm is Jubilant Battle Proposition, through which a character gets to open a fight in a suitably discrete fashion; Join Battle gets rolled with Appearance or Manipulation + Performance, which also gets to do double-duty as an inspire roll against all observers without multiple target penalties, and gets bonus dice under conditions where observing the stylist’s feet would not be possible. To model the idea of the dancing covering the fighting intent, characters whose Resolve was beaten by the inspire roll get to treat the first attack against one of them in the fight as unexpected, with Mastery allowing attacks that would have already been unexpected to be ambushes.

                    Reed-Breaking Banda allows a withering or decisive attack to take the form of a leg sweep, that in it does sufficient damage forces a check against falling prone, or else to make a smash attack with no Initiative cost and Defence penalty, as well as forcing the enemy struck that rises from prone or makes a disengage action to forfeit one Initiative to the stylist. In addition, Mastery allows attacks that are unexpected or stunted to have concealed footwork to impose a penalty to the roll to rise from prone, and automatic failure if it was an ambush. Then Sweeping Meadow Awareness Practice is used to half penalties to Evasion as a result of onslaught and unexpected attacks, with a successful dodge reducing the Initiative costs for disengages, gambits and Style Charms by one for a turn, although it can be stacked a few times. Those Charms with the Perilous keyword whose Initiative costs are reduced to zero this way can still be used in crash. Terrestrials get a different metric for their penalty reductions, only being able to relieve as much as half of their Performance.

                    The Form Charm uses swaying rhythms between combat stances to protect from penalties while fighting prone, doubling 10s on damage rolled for decisive attacks made against enemies that are prone and didn’t expect it, and there’s no cost for attacking with improvised equivalents to the style weapons. Performance can be used in place of Athletics to perform rushes, and in place of Stealth for establishing concealment. It gets to be entered reflexively when an enemy’s Resolve is overcome at close-range by a dance-based Performance roll.

                    Rolling-with-the-Wind Method lets one match the rhythm of their movements to those of enemies, by way of reflexively disengaging in response to taking a hit, before the damage is rolled; if they depart successfully, post-soak damage is halved, and decisive attack damage is penalised by extra successes on the disengage roll up to a limit of the stylist’s Performance, moving a range band away once the damage is rolled. It uses up movement action, but if such has already been used that round, it’s paid off by removing a movement action in the next one. Terrestrials reduce withering damage the same way as decisive, and the Charm can’t be used at all if the turn’s movement action has been used.

                    Teeth-in-the-Grass Strike is a major feint manoeuvre, allowing one’s Performance to be added in dice to decisive attacks and distract gambits. An attack whose successes beat enemy Resolve becomes unexpected, but can also be an ambush the first time it’s used in a scene, if benefiting from a distract gambit. It’s limited to once per scene, but is reset by beating an enemy’s Resolve with a dance Performance action, and Mastery allows a surprise attack to add half of Performance in dice to the damage or gambit Initiative roll.

                    Whirling Rhythm Revolution evokes the fighter’s passion, allowing a successful rush, disengage or dance Performance roll to be followed with a reflexive decisive attack, that doesn’t count as the round’s attack. Excellencies and Style Charms used to enhance the attack get discounted by the cost of Charms used to enhance the action that allowed for the attack. It might have one of the harshest Terrestrial drawbacks yet seen, withholding all bonuses aside from just the reflexive attack (to the point that it counts as the round’s attack). Then Grass-Reaping Posture acts as a Permanent enhancement, allowing a stylist in the Form to use the previous Charm whenever a close range enemy tries to rise from prone or fails to disengage from the stylist.

                    Last two Charms: Inescapable Tumbleweed Prana is Instant, but provides a benefit that conditionally lasts for the duration of a scene, that being that successful rushes allow the fighter to cross a range band towards an enemy every time they move away. The benefit is lost if an attack or rush is directed at a different enemy, the fighter moves away from the enemy, or experiences crash. The Charm can be used when rushing an enemy from concealment, with reflexive movement provided by such not requiring new Stealth rolls to be made.

                    And finally, the Instant Charm Serenading the Reed. Feeling a source of inspiration and rhythm from ambient noises, the stylist is able to add the lowest out of their Appearance, Manipulation or Performance to their Evasion, unless they’re actually in the presence of music, in which case they add the highest. If the dodge is successful, and the attacker rolls fewer successes than what is added to Evasion from the Charm, the enemy is forced to reset to base Initiative. As an alternative, it can be used to reflexively clash using a decisive attack, with the presence of music allowing 1s on an enemy’s roll to be added as dice to the stylist’s damage roll. Mastery allows the latter effect to make clashes against ambushes, while being Terrestrial means that clash counts as one’s attack for the round.



                    Swaying Grass Style is another that feels decent enough, although its synergies don’t stand out as much to me. That’s not to say I doubt they’re there, I’d just need to look back over the Style one or two more times to have a full sense of how it builds up and slots together.


                    Having looked at three of the Styles today, gotten an earlier start than I have for a while, and not expecting Sorcery to take very long, I’m going to leave it here and then try to get through the rest quickly tomorrow.

                        From Essence 4, Chains-of-Silver Geas provides yet another path for dealing with truculent spirits and fae; they just need to overcome the being (which seems to need to present a meaningful opposition) in some challenge, physical, spiritual (i.e. exorcism) or social, which then becomes irresistibly bound to perform an inconvenient or serious task, or even a life-defining one if the Lunar’s Essence is equal or greater. It’s even a very compelling binding, from which the being can only meaningfully deviate to attend to another threatened Major or greater Intimacy, or use them in Decision points to win short reprieves from pursuing the task. It can only made to last more than a year if it entails a task that the being could meaningfully conclude, but automatically prevents them from attempting to harm the binding Lunar in any capacity but self-defence. And it can only be used against a character once per story; you can’t readily put multiple bindings on the same figure, but you can hand out geasa left and right to many. This one looks really good on a number of counts, such as mystical binding rooted in a specifically Lunar method, engaging the function of guardianship, giving a bit more substance to how often the fiction has referred to them as bending gods and Raksha to their will. Good stuff all round.

                                                        Warning: this one is especially long. I had underestimated the length of what to say on sorcery.

                                                        Okay, up fairly early. Break me off a piece of that Sorcery, and then give myself a break for a little while.


                                                        Sorcery opens with some new shaping rituals, and of course one of the premier figures of Lunar sorcery is going to feature heavily there. I like the idea that Raksi’s teachings can be somewhat eclectic and expansive because she’s providing knowledge cribbed from the breadth of First Age knowledge. Plus some nice hallucinatory visions. What of the actual shaping rituals…

                                                        Balancing on the Edge: once per story, a player is allowed to suggest a twist for their spell, something that doesn’t undermine its power but relinquishes a bit of control over its effect in a manner that inconveniences the character, and for which they’re allowed to supplement a shape sorcery action with a reflexive repeat of that same action, benefiting for free from an Excellency spent and (if the control spell) any other enhancements. I like the sound of this one; I enjoy anything that can deviate spells a bit or make them somewhat wild, the means of reaping more sorcerous motes sounds particularly potent, and it has the feel of something with the Raksi backstory.

                                                        Sigil-Skin Geometry: once per day, a character gets to make a difficulty 2 Intelligence + Linguistics or Lore roll (which, if working on the control spell, get at least three bonus non-Charm dice) to spend a few hours carving the formulae of a specified spell into their skin, creating a pool of motes equal to extra successes that can be spent towards shaping that spell. This one is interesting for rewarding the sorcerer that prepares ahead of time, possibly with a fairly large bank of sorcerous motes, and for a sense of playing off the imagery of the Lunar tattoos. Also marries together Raksi’s schtick of elaborate knowledge of sorcerous theory with some of her more brutal experience.

                                                        Terror-Relishing Art: once per scene, a character who successfully threatens or inspires fear in a narratively significant character gets sorcerous motes flat equal to the Attribute used in the roll, in addition to the difference in Essence ratings if the influence was on a higher Essence character. This one is just neat for harnessing a key quality of Lunar power, and how it encourages one to act in the presence of higher status figures.

                                                        The initiation can also provide Merits for extra Resolve against fear and sorcerously produced influence, to represent conditioning from the experience of Raksi’s tutelage, and a once per story capacity to cast a spell they haven’t learned yet that they can later learn without tome or teacher, to represent the impression left by having read the Book of Three Circles (with an encouragement for Storytellers to adjudicate spells that logically wouldn’t be in the book to be off the table).

                                                        All of this together makes for a very compelling picture of why one would want to study under Raksi.


                                                        The concept of the Jasmine Gems of Mishiko makes a return, albeit with a significant overhaul; I’m pretty sure that the lore of previous Editions made Mishiko an outright Lunar (and somebody the Crossroads Society claimed as the actual first sorcerer), rather than one of their children (checks back} nnnnno, she was always the child of a Lunar, and that guy was referred to as the sorcerer, Mishiko was just very significant to his legend. The actual gems are very different, though; where once they were five, had a specific origin in the sorcery origin story, were kept by the Lunars, and needed to be gathered together and arranged in innumerable geometric formations to bring forth a simulacrum of her to recite mystically insightful poems, here it’s a terms for spontaneously generated sorcerous relics that bloom forth from any number of liminal places and summon her literal ghost. Contrary to a prior notion in which their possession was closely guarded and let elder No Moons gatekeep sorcery a bit, this comes with the idea that young Lunars are gravitated towards the gem by unknown forces. She still has some of that same teaching method (albeit seemingly with more latitude) and inclination towards exploration and discovery. I think it overall makes for one of the more esoteric and mystical initiation processes. The shaping rituals are:

                                                        Remembrance of Mishiko: the sorcerer gets to make an offering to Mishiko once per day, using mechanics very close to old prayer rules, and getting the threshold successes plus their own Essence rating in sorcerous motes that can be held until the end of the story, or reset with another offering. This just seems like a very straightforwardly potent ritual, probably commensurate to the implicit difficulty of acquiring one of the relics needed to perform it, although it can also be worth considering the hefty material and financial burdens of keeping it at a high rate.

                                                        Sorcerer’s Quest: learning of a location to which one must go to achieve a major character or story goal, or succeeding on Survival rolls to get there, immediately grants a sorcerous mote that can last for a story, and can be stacked with a bank of up to ten motes. In addition, once per story when upholding an Intimacy by entering such a place for the first time, the level of the Intimacy is multiplied by the sorcerer’s Essence and granted as sorcerous motes, that can be held on to until departing from that place. I like the way that this one alludes to and incorporates Mishiko’s old story, as well as the encouragement to follow in her foot steps, at least in principle.

                                                        Twilight Koan: Mishiko’s ghost reliably appears to offer strange questions or riddles, and players get to stunt an action with realization of its meaning, granting the better of Intelligence or Lore in motes that last for the scene. Doing so requires waiting for Mishiko’s next appearance and new koan before getting the opportunity to do it again.

                                                        And the Merits are for being allowed to confer with Mishiko once per story to introduce a fact without applicable background and with at least three non-Charm successes, and suggestions are given for Evocations for the jasmine gems, such as deeper communion with Mishiko, as well as themes of mystery, discovery and questing to enhance sorcery.

                                                        Mishiko’s place in Lunar story has always been at the margins of my awareness; looking back on it, I would judge the story to be fairly interesting, and the idea of how the gems were used was a bit cool, if a little limiting. I think this initiation does a very good job of taking the best parts of all that, and converting it into something a player can become really deeply invested in; a character’s whole storyline can revolve around the guidance of Mishiko. It’s actually one of the more vivid sorcerous prospects.


                                                        And going even deeper down the rabbit hole of the esoteric and just damn weird is the simply named Root-Lore, by which one derives sorcery from deep insight into the patterns within trees, roots and flowers. The rituals are:

                                                        Master Apothecary Enlightenment: a Lore roll to introduce a fact about herbs or Medicine roll to treat somebody using them grants sorcerous motes equal to Intelligence + extra successes on that role, which last for the rest of the story. Furthermore, basic or major crafting projects to create concoctions from plants allow a quantity of motes equal to Intelligence + awarded silver points + twice the awarded gold points, which also last for a story. It’s limited to only being able to hold onto motes from one use of the ritual at a time, but still… I’ll admit that I’m starting to feel a hint of power creep in these rituals. Learning from Raksi and acquiring a rare relic was fair enough, but this one… either root-lore is supposed to be distinctly difficult and unusual to acquire or I’m not fully appreciating the imposition of having to make a craft roll for herbal drugs, medicines and poisons that accomplish the objectives necessary for getting craft points.

                                                        Sorcerer’s Harvest: shape sorcery actions taken within medium range of significant plants such as trees or large gardens get to draw additional motes equal to the shaper’s Essence, with any such source limited to once per day. It can also draw power from wood elementals, directly tapping the same quantity out of their own mote pools, although the shaping action needs to exceed their Resolve, and still limited per day. Powering control spells with the motes doesn’t count towards the daily limit. This one feels more reasonable, and I like the impression it gives of how such sorcerers live and associate.

                                                        Thousand Blossom Arts: with a few hours foraging for an efficacious plant, character can make a difficulty 2 Intelligence + Survival roll (with difficulty adjusted if appropriate to local verdency, or straight raised to 4 for foraging enough to provide a basically unlimited stock of material), and once per scene the foraged plants can be incorporated as reagents into a spell, contributing sorcerous motes equal to the highest of Intelligence, Lore or Survival, limited by the week-long shelf life of the plants for that purpose (as well as being useful only to the foraging sorcerer). This one I also think is neat and fair, for how it incorporates external materials directly into sorcery, at the cost of some tedious and inconvenient prep.

                                                        This one has three Merits, one to let the character treat wood elementals, intelligent plants and any similar creature as though they have an affectionate Minor Tie, one for discounted cost on spells to create or manipulate plants or similar spirits (with the control spell getting the added benefit of a once-per-day waiving of the Willpower cost), and the last to let the character add their Occult to Survival rolls to forage for plants, Lore rolls to introduce facts about them, or Craft rolls to makes chemicals out of them, albeit only once per week and they count as Charm dice.

                                                        I just like this whole thing for having a very earthy feel, in contrast to a lot of more lofty initiations.



                                                        And now let’s round it all out with these spells, starting at the Terrestrial Circle.

                                                        First up is Blood Lash, which is exactly what it sounds like; a wounded sorcerer can draw their own blood out and shape it into a red whip with a black-barbed tip (in the dub we’ll call it Crimson Lightning). The whip has decent attack traits, and for actions to attack or defend using it the wound penalty is halved. The actual cost of the spell entails taking a lethal wound, but that’s waived for a sorcerer already wounded from another source. The whip also gets additional powers for the sorcerer with an Essence pool, as their wound penalty increases; at the lowest level, they can reflexively threaten enemies after damaging them, a roll in which wound penalty is waived and inverted into bonus dice, at medium it can extend the whip’s range and, if grappled, has the option to pull them closer or leave them grappled at range (which leaves the grapple vulnerable to a simple gambit from the enemy’s helpers), and the worst wound penalty allows the sorcerer to make a decisive attack in which the last develops a vampiric maw, allowing non-aggravated damage to be healed for every three levels dealt to an enemy, with a minimum of one, and the prior powers get to be used in combination with this for no cost; it can only be used once per scene, and is reset by taking at least 7 levels of damage in a decisive attack.

                                                        I think that the interaction with wound penalties gives even the base spell something a bit more than just being a slightly odd way of summoning a weapon, and the powers from escalating wound penalties really sells it.

                                                        Ah, right, control spell. Sorcerer is distinguished by really freaky veins, and gains a general capability to control the motion of their own shed blood, which can function as exceptional equipment, and at Storyteller discretion may be sufficient to create actions; it can be done at any range, but doesn’t inherently convey awareness of the blood’s situation or proximity. And distortion of the spell causes the whip to coagulate, reducing it to inflicting bashing damage and removing its Flexible tag, and treating the sorcerer’s wound penalties as a level lower for the sake of accessing powers. Nice.

                                                        Flight of Separation allows a sorcerer to convert oneself into a flock of tiny birds, guided by the caster’s diffused consciousness, that will migrate to a designated location within ten miles. The birds use the sorcerer’s traits, but are only capable of moving and dodging, for which they gain a +2 Evasion bonus against attacks that don’t cover an area, and bonus dice for disengage and withdrawal. The top flight speed is designated as Dexterity + Athletics and then multiplied by two miles per hour, and the spell can be reflexively ended at any time, naturally ending with the journey or incapacitation. Having it as control spell allows one to communicate with birds (many of whom aren’t good conversationalists), all of which (plus any similar spirits) treat as having a Minor Tie of fondness. This spell also has a special activation condition; upon gathering the necessary motes, the sorcerer can hold off rather than cast immediately, although doing so imposes a -1 penalty to everything as the sorcerer strains against the continuous effort to not explode into birds. It can be cast reflexively at any time, although close range enemies require disengaging first, or else the spell is lost. Distortion causes the flock of you to become disoriented and uncoordinated, forcing a Wits + Integrity roll every turn when fighting to prevent some of the birds from fleeing independently, represented as rolling a die of Hardness-ignoring bashing damage as they become too diffuse; when not in combat, the roll is for each mile of travel, with failure making the next mile half the speed.

                                                        It’s a functional spell, nice and weird image of a means of travel, and compelling distortion effect.

                                                        And the somewhat infamous Peacock-Shadow Eyes, now rendered as a Psyche effect, and cheaper in sorcerous motes than the norm (although costing two Willpower). The sorcerer’s psychedelic and hypnotic eyes allow an Occult roll to be made against the Resolve of a target at up to medium range; if it wins, the target is put into a suggestible trance for a scene, in which their Resolve is penalised by 2, and they generally function as though sleepwalking, having no memory of time in the trance and rationalizing influence acquired under it as self-determined, although the trance can’t withstand being attacked. It also fails outright against those without sight. Willpower can be spent to resist the memory suppressing effect if lacking such information could endanger a higher Intimacy, although social influence endures under those circumstances. Observers whose Resolve is not beaten can tell that the sorcerer is employing some kind of supernatural chicanery with a successful Perception + Occult roll against the sorcerer’s successes. If the control spell, the sorcerer’s eyes are permanently died with iridescent colours, with which they can treat their Appearance as one higher for rolls to inspire fascination with oneself or for any rolls against the hypnotised, although effectively disguising oneself becomes harder. Distorting the spell (which can target the ongoing trance effect) allows the target to experience cryptic flashbacks of their experience, which after a few days permits spending Willpower to resist the memory suppression even without concern for Intimacies.

                                                        I’d say that the effect is less directly powerful than it was in the prior Edition, but is both more involved and potentially more rewarding. I think it feels both distinct as a hypnotic sorcery effect, and as something that might complement other social powers, rather than compete with them.

                                                        Okay, onto some shiny new Celestial Circle Spells.

                                                        Starting off with Cloud Trapeze, through which one can draw together vapour and Essence to manufacture a big cloud boat, which is sufficient to lift the sorcerer, cargo and willing passengers that are within long range with a combined mass equal to ten times the sorcerer’s Essence in tons, and which can then fly at the same number in miles per hour, possibly helped or hindered by prevailing winds. Wits + Occult can be rolled to navigate obstacles or perils if necessary, and in combat the whole mass can be moved across range bands as a miscellaneous action, counting as the sorcerer’s own move action for a turn. It benefits its passengers in numerous ways: they’re protected from weather, temperature, and any other travails of aerial travel, and imposes a penalty on vision based rolls to find them (or the cargo) for anybody observing from the outside, an imposition not made on the passengers. The Trapeze can actually rise to the level of existing clouds, among which it is concealed to the point where it takes a difficulty 5 action of Occult or Survival or magic that can predict weather to make it out. The sorcerer has to stay aboard the ship to guide it, although they don’t need to remain fixed to that task, and departing from the boat causes it to quickly descend, just carefully enough to minimize risk to those aboard before it vanishes. As a control spell, half of the caster’s Intelligence is added to Essence for the sake of determining carrying capacity and speed, and bestows a capacity to make an Intelligence 2 Occult roll whenever in up to medium range of bodies of mist, for or water to take an action to shape vapour into structures like bridges or ladders the length of a range band. Distorting the spell causes it to be open to outside observers, while obscuring the vision of those within.

                                                        I think this spell is interesting in the image and the kind of transportation capability it provides to a sorcerer, although I must admit that the control effect stands out a bit to me; I keep picturing a sorcerer turning a fortification’s moat into a liability.

                                                        Insidious Tendrils of Hate is a Psyche spell cast with a Ritual cost. The sorcerer spends a couple of feeding their malice into a fire until it is rendered as green as Ligier, and then adds something with a symbolic connection to an intended target, such as a body piece taken within three days or a personal item. Upon which the target will, no matter how far aware, be enveloped in acrid smoke that will seep into their soul, causing a roll of sorcerer’s Manipulation + Occult against victim’s Resolve (and anybody else within short range) to render anybody it overcomes paranoid and hateful, taking everything they hear in the worst way and suspicious of everyone they meet; it functions mechanically as an inspire roll for hatred that can’t be resisted with Willpower, which lasts for days equal to the caster’s Manipulation, and suppresses a victim’s Major and Minor Ties by one level. As a control spell, the sorcerer is marked by such smoke being exhaled constantly, and they get to add their Essence as dice to rolls for inspiring hatred (including the spell) and influence targeting hateful Intimacies. Distortion targets a cursed individual, and allows them to spend Willpower to resist the hatred, although their Intimacies remain diminished.

                                                        I think that it’s an effective spell for sabotage, particularly with the range, and find the imagery and effect to scratch my itch for some really dreadful magic.

                                                        Now, what would Prince of the Fallen Tower do… sounds kind of reminiscent of some demon titles. Creates a door of foul water, out of which emerges one of the titular creatures, a long ago sealed ogre of massive size, a rusty exoskeleton, and weeping sores, that is not very smart but serves faithfully and has a mystically bound Defining Tie of loyalty ala demon summoning, which remains for (Essence) hours. It can be summoned directly into combat, immediately rolling to Join Battle, and while more than one Prince at a time can be summoned, it can’t be more than the sorcerer’s Essence. As a control spell, a Willpower surcharge can be paid to summon a prince permanently (not counting towards the limit), which allows it to be treated as a familiar, and expands its powers by one Charm at the end of every story it’s in. Only one prince can be held permanently at a time, but if it dies, a new one can be called with the increased powers. Distortion weakens some of the Prince’s powers for a scene.

                                                        Before reading their stats, I’ll say that I’m always happy to see some sorcery that can summon more distinct and idiosyncratic beings, and find it particularly interesting that the sorcerous mote cost gives this a more flexible range of conditions than calling elementals and demons have, particularly the application directly into ongoing combat.

                                                        Okay, what can they actually do… decent mote pool, fifteen health levels, immensely strong, some good social stats and defences… not the fastest, but hits hard, has a good Parry and that chitin makes for a fairly hardy body. Huh, thing gets to have Special Abilities, Charms and Merits, let’s see what they can do… its corrupting touch allows it to destroy ten cubic feat of mundane material with a simple miscellaneous action (or not very hard gambit for when it’s something being worn or wielded), as well as being able to ignore some points of soak and Hardness from non magical armour (which also does aggravated damages to wood and earth spirits). Charms can allows its sufficiently heavy decisive attacks to waive some damage in exchange for imposing a corruption on target’s Artifact that makes it a bit heavier and increases Evocation costs (requiring a repair project to dispel), double 7s on feats of demolition, and increase its Hardness by its soak, which destroys any mundane weapon that strikes it without sufficient Initiative to roll for attack. In addition to Legendary Size, has a Merit to completely disregard any influence that would make it stop destroying, even from its sorcerer. Between the name and some of the powers, these things feel as though they have an association with Octavian, but I think they overall distinguish themselves as their own nasty little buggers.

                                                        Thorn of Cold Rebuke, lets you go all bad guy from Final Fantasy VIII (I never played it) by summoning a javelin of ice… ahh, it’s not actually to impale people, it’s thrown out to break into a cloud of sharp blue particles, although they still lacerate with an undiscriminating unblockable Thrown decisive attack, that gets Occult bonus dice and hits everybody within medium range of the impact site. It rolls the caster’s Intelligence in Hardness-ignoring lethal damage, in addition to divided Initiative, while battle groups take a straight Intelligence + Initiative roll. On top of that, the area is rendered dangerously cold for the next several hours, creating an environmental hazard, causing difficult terrain from the slick frost, and if made by bodies of water, generating naval hazards of grinding ice, which can be sufficient to block up all but the widest rivers. The sorcerer even has the option to hold onto the javelin and wield it as a normal spear, and thereafter can elect to make a decisive attack to shatter it and unleash its cutting and freezing mist. As a control spell, its effects go from hours to days, the ice unnaturally resistant to melting for that time, and causing the sorcerer to become an object of Minor fear for spirits connected to wood and water, for which carrying the spear gets the benefits of exceptional equipment to intimidate them.

                                                        I like it when sorcery provides unusual projectiles, and think this one does a very nice job with the combination of the scope and picture of its original attack, and its enduring after effect. Personally, I also like to imagine an Artifact that could be made around the core of this power (in and out of character) and the workings that might employ it as a Means.

                                                        And finally there is Torrential Cascade. The sorcerer invokes something called the Storm Who Drowned the World in order to conjure a churning pillar of water, drudged together from any nearby bodies of water, which is then unleashed as a huge wave in a large arc in any direction from the caster, out to short range. It resolves as a not very high damage environmental hazard, but one that can put battle groups to rout, obliterates mundane buildings, and possibly tears up environmental features such as trees and crops. Being caught in the water also imposes a penalty on all actions besides resisting the hazard, and puts them at risk of drowning (with success against the hazard letting them get their face clear of the water enough to breathe effectively). What really makes the spell is the fact that it can be sustained after the initial burst, so long as the sorcerer devotes shape sorcery actions to it, requiring no rolls or cost. Doing so allows it to keep expanding its range, until it reaches long, and dragging along people that failed to resist the hazard (which gains additional damage dice if the wave is filled with debris). In cases where the Storyteller determines it encounters an obstacle it can’t overwhelm, it becomes redirected. When the spell ends for whatever reason, the water discharges to create mucky ground that qualifies as difficult terrain, and might have other logical consequences. As a control spell, the shape and flow of the water can be more finely guided, able to change direction mid-course or go around troublesome obstacles, as well as avoiding things the sorcerer doesn’t want to strike, as well as giving them a general quality of nearby water responding to their more extreme moods. When distorted, the opposing sorcerer and anybody within close range of them becomes protected by an opening in the water.

                                                        I like this spell for some real wrath of God kind of destruction, and the more indirect ways it can be harmful to enemies.


                                                        So the sorcery generally comes off well. Some very interesting additional rites and initiations, and good updates or expansions to the canon of spells.

                                                            Overall I really liked the sorcery section; cool stuff, generally balanced, and while some of it is quite subtle and restrained, rather than big and flashy (for example, Mishiko), I think that it does so in an evocative way.

                                                            I was a bit bored of the Celestial spells in the book; I've played a Celestial Sorcerer and seen two others played in 3rd ed, plus used an NPC with it as a recurring villain, plus our party has Mara as an ally.
                                                            So it was nice to see some new spells, and I like all of them.
                                                            I also liked that they had a nice variation in terms of theme, element, and type. They also seem balanced.

                                                            I have to say though, I didn't notice the balance on the summoning rituals, but I have to agree with you now you point it out, a couple of them are getting quite a lot of sorcerous motes. Master Apothecary seems like maybe it should be limited in how often you can do it.
                                                            As I discovered when I let a PC do a working to give themselves a magical heart of sorcerous flame that gave them sorcerous motes (hey, the concept was cool, so...), plus of course their rituals, letting sorcerers start a scene with a lot of sorcerous motes is actually a bit overpowered. So it's something to watch.
