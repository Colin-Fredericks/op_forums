                Don't pity the horse.
                She was the Lunar all along.
                The rider who died was just one of her clones. Like a worm who leaves behind its tail to fool predators into thinking they'd eaten it.

                Originally posted by Blaque
                Why would it be? QCs should be designed to be interesting challenges to PCs and express their role in a story well. Their stats reflect their narrative importance, not some statement of their actual relative power or traits in the world as some measure of their real traits.
                I kind of agree with you, and kind of not.

                I'm quite happy for a Solar QC to have charms that don't actually work the same as ones in the charms section of the Core, for the sake of simplicity and power (Solar charms are designed to work in aggregate, but as ST I don't have time to assign and remember 30 charms for every Solar NPC). For example, I don't care if they have one Melee charm which is just "double 8s" or something, rather than the first 5 melee charms of the Solar Melee tree.

                But not of course, if they're charms like "you shoot fire from your eyes" or "you summon an army made of sunlight" because those aren't things Solars can actually do.

                Abilities/attributes do represent something. There's a whole chapter on that. A person with Dexterity and Melee 5, and a Specialty in swords, is explicitly one of the 5 greatest mortal swordsman in the entirety of Creation. And the greatest swordsmen in all of Creation have Parry ([5+5+1]/2+1)=7.

                So if the PCs run into a hot-shot mortal immaculate who fights with a staff and has Parry 8, they're going to ask "wtf".


                But that isn't actually the point I was making.

                The question is, "why, fluff-wise, is the practicioner of an esoteric and bizarre unarmoured and unarmed fighting style just as good as a professional soldier?"
                Now, in real life, the answer is "they're not."
                And in Exalted, when talking about mortals, they're also not.
                Even if they have the same stats, the soldier is better because she has armour and a better Parry (because of a medium weapon).

                In 2nd ed, the answer was "actually, the practicioner of an esoteric and bizarre unarmoured and unarmed fighting style has unlocked the secrets of Qi, so can rival or even beat a soldier."
                Which isn't realistic, but it is appropriate for the genre.

                If your answer is "just give the Martial Artist higher Defence", well, why can't the soldier have that? Why does the Martial Artist get better stats?
                In 2nd ed, kung-fu was magic. In 3rd ed, it's not. It just allows people who are already magic to use a wider variety of magic.

                Now, there are some game balance reasons for this, of course. And in terms of Exalts, I think it works fine.
                But that's why I said I did miss enlightened mortals a bit. Not enlightened sorcerer-engineers or thaumaturgists, but enlightened martial artists.

                Now, the only people who can really power up via kung-fu enlightenment is Dragonblood.

                            I run strictly-mortal campaigns in 3E, and so long as the mortals are well developed and intelligent, they can stand up to many things.

                            A farmer that has never held a spear in his life organized into a poorly-led mob? Yeah, no, sausage for the grinder for the 3 seconds they decide to stand and fight

                            A well-led, well-motivated, well-trained and well-equipped mortal can kill plenty of shite.

                            My players have killed ghosts, demons, Fae, monstrous animals, armies, and even a few Exalts. Even a Prince of the Earth wont survive a pike through the heart, much less 6.

                            Most non-intelligent beasts and monsters would prefer to avoid injury, and can be driven off if a potential target is enough of a PITA. My mortal players organized a village to drive off a hungry Tyrant Lizard by slinging stones at it and poking it with long sticks until it fucked off in search of easier prey.

                            Most intelligent "monsters" can be bargained with.

                            Finally, even with all of the monsters and madmen and supernatural shite Creation has on display, the most common problems a mortal settlement is likely to deal with is wolves (or some other predator) taking their livestock and bandits shaking them down for goods and deterring away traders. Both can be dealt with with a little steel-in-the-spine and steel-in-the-hand

                                        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                                        But that's why I said I did miss enlightened mortals a bit. Not enlightened sorcerer-engineers or thaumaturgists, but enlightened martial artists.
                                        As it happens, I've been working on a few ideas for mortal characters (aiming at putting something up on the Storyteller's Vault), and this is one of the things I'm looking to capture. My current approach is to allow mortals to follow a specific code of conduct (I'm developing several examples) to earn "Enlightenment XP" (by analogy with Solar or Dragon XP for filling character roles or playing the limitations for Flaws). But rather than being used to buy Charms or abilities or the like, Enlightenment XP can be cashed in for motes to power MA charms.

                                        So, for example, a PC mortal MA following the Path of Temperance can earn Enlightenment XP for being restricted by her ascetic principles in play, thus gaining a pool of points that can be expended to power her bought-with-ordinary-experience Golden Janissary Style charms. Obviously, that means she'll have to husband use of those charms in ways characters with actual mote pools don't, but when the situation merits it, she can use her "secret technique" of Golden Janissary Form to kick ass.

                                        (So far, my rough-draft paths are Temperance, Compassion, Valor, Filial Piety, and Blood Sacrifice. The Immaculate Order strongly favors Temperance; Compassion is uncommon and often considered socially subversive; Valor is popular among those who focus on martial glory; Filial Piety is most common in places with strong ancestor cults; and Blood Sacrifice is popularly associated with Yozi cults and Anathema and seen as "cheating the system" by followers of other paths.)
