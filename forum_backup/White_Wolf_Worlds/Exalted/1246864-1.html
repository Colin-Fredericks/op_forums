                                I wonder if Rakan Thulio's story will be one in which he's been fighting Destiny since he acquired control over his own thread, and discovery of the Getimians escalated that and made it more efficient because he gained access to soldiers who can more effectively fight on those terms, or if it will be that he mostly kept to himself after seizing his own Fate and the Getimians catalysed his convictions by representing an example that people being unjustly screwed over by Destiny was a more widespread problem.

                                Or possibly a case that the kinds of people that can become Getimians have always existed, and Rakan has tried to be an advocate for them with limited success, and the importance of discovering and reviving Getimian Exaltation is that he now actually has a means by which such people can be empowered to take their own vengeance and seize their own destinies.

                                After all, one take on the idea of puppetry could be that Thulio radicalizing the Getimian Exalted along an ideology of the unjustness of Heaven and the rightness of opposing it is something that he sincerely believes will be helpful to them, and only wants the best for, but it's still directing them into a life of endemic violence and possibly a general experience of hate and anger, which might not be the best thing for many of them. I think there can be a perspective on charismatic leaders in which they're bad when they're insincere and only using people for their own agenda (whether it be a general power grab or the actual ideology without consideration for human costs), but there's something to be said for the idea of a leader who genuinely wants to improve the lives of the people that they're inspiring and organising, while still not critically evaluating the costs of it.

                                Overall, I think that there's fertile ground for discussion of the subject, and that we've not yet gotten any detail about Rakan Thulio that is a basis for writing him off.

                                            Originally posted by Frostav View Post
                                            My only problem with them is that they were started/created/whateverthefuck'ed by an incel.
                                            I do think this is more of a problem than people are giving it credit for. Part of the issue behind the whole incel thing is that traditionally stories have portrayed excessive fixation in the face of rejection (and finding the "real" reason you were rejected) in this romantic / heroic light. It's only more recently that we've begun to step back and look at that and realize the serious problems with it, both in terms of the stories themselves and what they teach people who take them seriously. The disaffected men you're referring to are often ones who are rejecting that cultural change.

                                            So Thulio's backstory is a bit awkward in that it's written in that traditional "rage against the heavens for denying me my love" thing, whereas most modern-day readers are going to stop and say "wait, no, this guy rejected you. Accept it and move on." And to the extent that it's about fate, it's sort of picking at the scab of free will and the issues players are already going to have with those themes - ie. either Thulio's lover had free will, in which case his story makes him look like a worthless loser who deserves the laughed off the stage, or he didn't, in which case... the entire setting comes into question. Even if you thread the needle and say "well, Thulio's lover was mind-controlled by heaven shenanigans, so they weren't rejecting him of their own free will!", it leads to serious problems - I'm not really OK with the implications of a story like that.

                                            If he was a minor side-character this wouldn't be a problem. Picking at cultural or narrative scabs, so to speak, can sometimes be a good thing, and if he was a minor side character an audience reaction of "he's a worthless shitlord whose 'concerns' are total crap" wouldn't necessarily be a bad thing - he could even be seen as a deconstruction of that sort of romantic story, making him as a skeevy monster who doesn't want to accept he was rejected. With characters like that you can also take the risk that your story won't click with some readers, or won't be read the way you intend it to. But as the head of an entire faction, that isn't an option - the game seems to be trying to insist that his story has some grandeur or depth to it, and I suspect a lot of players are going to hard pass on that.

                                            Basically, a lot of readers are going to have zero patience for any attempt to portray Thulio's story as sympathetic or as anything but a story of how broken this loser is, and that makes him pretty hard to use as a faction leader who needs to have depth and multiple facets. Making such an unsympathic story (and one with such unfortunate implications when viewed in light of real life) central to the main example the game seems to be using for raging against Fate and the heavens seems like a really bad move.

                                            I suspect that some of this reaction is intentional, but that the authors misjudged the intensity or firmness with which many people are going to... basically, view it as non-heroic, is I think the best way to put it. Thulio comes across as pathetically grasping at straws after a failed relationship, in a way reminiscent of far too many problems in the real world. And once that real-world connection is made, it's going to be very hard to slot him back into a heroic fantasy role in people's minds. Sort of how the Games of Divinity started to lose their grandeur once people view it as a Cosmic X-Box. There isn't really any going back from that - all you can do is tone it down and focus on stuff that works with the audience instead.

                                            Basically, if I were writing the books, I would nix the story of Thulio's lover hard and it would never appear or be referenced again - I'd come up with some other motivation if necessary (technically an additional motivation, since the lover was mentioned in the core, but only in passing, so it's easy enough to explain as a minor bit of backstory trivia that never came up again, even if that obviously wasn't the original intent.) It's not something that is worth potentially poisoning people's views of Getimians over. Will some people be fine with it? Sure, but the fact that it keeps coming up shows that it's at best an unwelcome distraction.

                                            (Another option would be to have his lover die, but this would flatly contradict the core.)

                                                            Similar things less succinctly and well than Levi:

                                                            I'm not sure our ideas about love have changed that much. Or at least, I hope, as a culture, our capacity to deal with the human experience of heartbreak and take it seriously and sympathetically and to understand how even great people can be profoundly affected by it, has not been reduced and coarsened that much. (My read of pop culture has not been that people are suddenly blase about heartbreak, and unsympathetic to people who remain attached after their relationships break down, but I could be deeply wrong.)

                                                            On a more specific level to the audience for this game, historically (and perhaps human experience pre-2017 is almost irrelevant to today's reality, and society has completely changed in a very short space of time) the audience for Exalted has not really had a problem with the idea that Heaven can and does break people's love lives, and that them doing this both doesn't detonate the setting and is still Heaven's responsibility.

                                                            (Like, "I use Shun The Smiling Lady on the target!" equals the audience enthusiastically agreeing "Get over it!" towards the target? Indeed, the objections usually tended to be that this is overpowered and not fair to be able to target a PC! Not that it ever broke the setting or that targets should just brush it off emotionally as "He's just not that into you".)

                                                            I'd also say, to use a more general case, we know that the Sidereals engage in complex interventions into Fate, to achieve a given ends, without stripping people of their free will. If a Sidereal really did engage in one of these complex interventions to say, strip a person of their kingdom and raise another in their place, would we then be sitting there going "Well, everyone still had free will, so..... I can't even sympathize with your rage against Heaven"? Or take another example, perhaps of a woman who had been stripped of the love of her beloved child by the machinations of a Heaven that intended to set her down a more useful path? If not, why does romance become something different?

                                                            But, really, this is all in the realms of "I do/don't think people at large would think this way". This seems mostly like a sort of a useless thing to argue about, as anyone involved in the conversation can simply flatly declare how "society" or "the audience" would react and we have no way of breaking deadlock and going further with that conversation. None of us can really own or prove what we might believe about how "the audience" would react. (Or if the authors should even care that some people will have bad ideas that will stop them clicking with what they present!) Unlike simply exchanging on our personal views and experiences, or on the text or rules, etc.
