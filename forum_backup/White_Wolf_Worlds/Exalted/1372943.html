                  Ones I have read that were suggested in books or otherwise I thought were pretty good for exalted:

                  Night's Master by Tanith Lee, as well as pretty much the rest of the Tales from the Flat Earth books. It's a suggested reading in all the Exalted coreoboksf or a reason.

                  The Black Company by Glenn Cook and all the rest of thsoe there. LIke Night's Master it's one of the books suggested in the 1e corebook.

                  Lord of Light by Roger Zelazny for awesome pros, really cool setting, and exotic weird shit. I think i'ts my favorite Zelazny book, which helps it a lot there.

                  The Grace of Kings by Kim Liu to me is a really good exmaple of doing a political intrigue fantasy setting that also doesn't have to be entirely existential in its nature. THe follow-up book, Wall of Storms is solid and again, not as existential as it first appears. Although I think it does dirft a bit away from what I would consider good Exalted inspirtaiton exactly since it goes more into the silkpunk aesthetic as it goes along.

                  The Gods of Pegana by Lord Dunsany are big inspiration for the setting and generally just cool and interesting. Recommend for good example of the storyteller angle to present the setting.

                  The Compleat Traveler in Black by John Brunner is effing weird fantasy. It apparently was a big inspiration for the Sidereal Exalted though, and I think Getimians get a good chunk outta it too. Hard to describe, but look it up and see if it is up your alley.

                  The Dying Earth by Jack Vance is pretty old school fantasy, but I think it is from a time where fantasy and sci-fi weren't quite as distinct things as they are today. This is important for Exlated to me since I think a lot of the magitech aesthetic from 2e draws on the idea that they were separate genres in a way I don't tihnk Vance or Zelazny did. It's got some pretty old timey technobabble I find amusing too.

                  Shadow of the Torturer by Gene Wolfe and the rest of The Book of New Sun. Really dense, kind of weird, but I think there's actually somer eally cool ideas in there for Exalted like artifacts, the way communiteis are isolated, the long forgotten past of the world, and the nature of the Autarch are all things I think that do well to inspire Exalted stories perosnally.

                  Things Fall Apart by Chinua Achebe is a weird one since it's more historical fiction/drama, but I think the big thing to me that stands out is it shows inside the life of some folks in a part of the world not a lot of people see, and I think it illustrates well how people can grow up in a culture with things assumed perfectly normal and with their own issues and failings. And how that can be completely ruined by colonialism, exploitation, and pride. I think really worth reading just to get ideas of such things. The follow-up book No Longer at Ease I think is less relevant to Exalted specifically, but I think is something to think of when deisgning a character that has a lot to them. The book is strongly about what the MC is in context of the start of the story and someone not really knowing or caring the whys of what he did.

                  I'll post some of my favorite non-fiction in a bit.

                                              Another vote for Lord of Light-- it's great for the "supernaturally powered beings struggle with each other using the lives of mortals as pawns" side of things. The Chronicles of Amber are another good Zelazny series.

                                              Brandon Sanderson's Stormlight Archives are good too-- both in the general "these are awesome books" sense, and in that the central plot involves the return of incredibly powerful heroes who used to rule the world and are now seen as monsters.

                                              Kill Six Billion Demons is probably the most Exalted thing I've ever seen, and that's including some Exalted games I've played.
