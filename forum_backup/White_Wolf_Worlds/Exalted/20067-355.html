Death Ray says that you get (Essence) automatic successes on the Intelligence+Occult roll that functions as a Decisive Attack roll.                 However, the rules for charm dice say that one automatic success granted by charms is equal to two charm dice.                                             Originally posted by RCa View Post
                                            It's at the start of the Charms chapter, in the definitions of supplemental and reflexive charm types.
                                            Thanks for that.

                                            Originally posted by Ryoki View Post
                                            In our group we are always struggling with the decision what social ability should be used. Performance is the easiest one, but even the oratory part can easily go with Presence. And Presence evolved to our got to ability for everything else that isn't musical or artistic.

                                            So when is it better to let players use Socialize for Social Influence instead of Presence? Let the player decide?
                                            Warning, personal interpretations ahead:

                                            The way I understand the social system, it's about two things, what you are trying to do and how you are trying to do it. The first defines what action you take while the second can define what ability to use. My personal mindmap looks as follows:

                                            If your action is built on captivating your target(s) through your actions to distract them from thinking things through clearly, you use performance. Passionate speeches that grip the heart more than the mind, music that makes you feel more secure than you are or an impressive ballet that draws on your memories to make you draw (not necessarily valid) connections between then and now all play on the illusion of truth. The action doesn't really convince you on an intellectual level but rather mutes your doubts or better judgement.
                                            "I am aware that many object to the severity of my language; but is there not cause for severity? I will be as harsh as truth, and as uncompromising as justice. On this subject, I do not wish to think, or to speak, or write, with moderation. No! no! Tell a man whose house is on fire to give a moderate alarm; tell him to moderately rescue his wife from the hands of the ravisher; tell the mother to gradually extricate her babe from the fire into which it has fallen; — but urge me not to use moderation in a cause like the present. I am in earnest — I will not equivocate — I will not excuse — I will not retreat a single inch — AND I WILL BE HEARD" -William Loyd Garrison

                                            The speaker's (writer's) goal here is to convince your of the righteousness of his cause not by logical argument, but by conjuring images of personal fear and then connecting them to the subject matter while at the same time, pointing out the double standard in dealing with the subject matter (slavery). He doesn't speak of the subject matter directly, but instead connects it with emotions.


                                            If your action is built on social conventions, laws and institutions to convince your target that your interpretation of them is more valid than his, use socialize. Your target needs to understand the rules and conventions as well as the stakes perfectly and you are basically trying to beat them at their own game. Convincing a guard that while he could certainly report your minor transgression, he shouldn't because doing so would cause a scene at his Lord's ball that his Lord would not appreciate is a good example. Another would be to convince a guard to let you into the ball because you clearly belong there, your lack of invitation is an oversight and not letting you in would insult your house, which his lord surely doesn't intent. Basically whenever you try to rules lawyer your way into or out of a situation based on the social or cultural norms, you're using socialize.
                                            "Save it for Geoffrey, he loves a good grovel. Tyrion Lannister [Spoiler Warning]

                                            The speaker evokes one social convention after the other and time and time suggests that the reasons given for his targets behavior contradict the social assumptions about his person. Eventually he traps him in the social dilemma between doing what he doesn't want or accepting the consequences of his actions. Note how Tyrion doesn't actually do anything except pointing out the obvious. The force of argument comes from the social conventions of the world they live in and not the speaker.


                                            If your action relies fully on your force of personality, your stature or your status in the world, use presence. You get people to do what you want for you and only you. Whether you make someone lust for your body, pretend to be their best friend or worst nightmare, your goal isn't so much to get someone to do something specific but to get them to do something for or with you. The emphasis is not on the what but on the because. Threatening a guard to let you in because you'll bad mouth them to their lord if they don't, bargaining for a better price with the claim you were in the army too or persuading someone to help you out just this once is the field of the presence.
                                            "I don't know who you are. I don't know what you want. If you are looking for ransom I can tell you I don't have money, but what I do have are a very particular set of skills. Skills I have acquired over a very long career. Skills that make me a nightmare for people like you. If you let my daughter go now that'll be the end of it. I will not look for you, I will not pursue you, but if you don't, I will look for you, I will find you and I will kill you." Liam Neeson in Taken

                                            The force of argument here resides entirely in Liam Neeson's character. Everything he says is basically cheap talk and backed up by nothing. The recipient can't even see him. But the delivery is so grounded in his character that it becomes both awesome and horrible at the same time. The threat is believable not because of what he says, but because it is him saying it.

                                            Finally, Integrity is, in my opinion, a passive ability that can help you resist social influence (or yourself) but doesn't really lend itself to incite others into action. At least I can't come up with a way to do.

                                            Again, this is my personal interpretation and not really a mechanical answer. It makes sense to me, but your mileage may vary.

                                                        Originally posted by Beaumis View Post

                                                        "These limits are absolute unless a Charm explicitly contradicts them, and apply to any magic used to enhance a Solar’s dice pools or static values [...]" (P. 251f)

                                                        "Step Three: Subtract target’s soak from the attack’s raw damage. This cannot produce a result lower than the weapon’s Overwhelming rating (see p. 580). Roll a dice pool equal to whatever damage remains after soak." (P. 191)

                                                        "Step Two: If attack is successful, roll your current Initiative value as a dice pool." (P. 191)

                                                        While I personally think it's weird and counter intuitive, yes, dice caps do in fact apply to damage rolls by the rules as written. Thanks for asking that question. I didn't actually realize this until now.

                                                        While this is technically correct (which as we all know is the best kind of correct), it leads to some dumb results. First and foremost is that any charm that adds either damage dice or successes to a decisive attack and does not expressly call out that damage as being non-charm winds up being completely unusable (for decisive attacks) since there are no Attributes or Abilities that factor into a decisive damage attack. So essentially, this ruling would be taking every damage increasing charm with the Dual and Uniform keywords (Fire and Stones Strike and Hungry Tiger Technique) and making them "Withering-Only" and completely doing away with damage increasing effects that have the Decisive-Only keyword.

                                                        Likewise, withering damage bonuses from charms would be limited to +5 dice or + 2 successes unless they were used on a weapon that had the Crossbow or Firearm tag because those weapons do not use Strength in their damage calculation. In those instances, those damage increasing charms would be completely useless for those weapons as well. That means that any damage increasing charms in Rightous Devil Style would be useless.

                                                        So while Beaumis is technically correct, it leads to results that make me feel that the charms were written in such a way as to assume that damage dice pools are not included in the dice caps. That's something that should get some errata, IMO.
