    I feel like Culture Minds *could* exist in Exalted. If anything like them did exist, I feel like they'd be far beyond the scale of assumed play, or they'd be weird in terms of being actual Culture Minds if they actually did try and exist in the space of "This should be impossible to counter, but it isn't because Solar is always better" (the point of them is to be transcendent AI capital G God things).

    If you had beings like the Minds they'd have to be rooted in Essence and magic, not motonic physics, if you get the distinction I'm trying to hint at. In a world that is not rooted on the primacy of technological essence.

    I can't see anything with a lot of Autochthon's Essence being like a Culture Mind - he's all wounded craftsman, dirty industrial, self consciously mechanistic in deliberate contrast to natural and biological, religious-technological, Clarity. Different from the underlying Essence to Culture Minds and vessels.

The Culture is very much an a-religious society, though.

                    Originally posted by wulf View Post
                    Would Twilight Solars really be content with turning out barely-sentient machines for thousands of years or would they challenge themselves to create artificial sentience and then see how far they could push it?
                    Maybe as a thought experiment; a thing to publish speculative essays on.

                    I don't see much practical use in the thing, so I don't really envision Solars as pursuing it.

                    I am not compelled by an idea of Twilight Caste Solars who create things for arbitrary or egotistical reasons in the manner of the mad scientist.

                    Originally posted by wulf
                    Hell, Twilights were rumored to be looking into ways to carve up Creation into its own distinct worlds, and we know they created a potential secondary Elemental Pole of Earth just in case.
                    Twilights in the milieu of Second Edition in general and Dreams of the First Age in particular were doing that. Take them out of a context of apathy and regret and go with the premise that creating wonders to sustain the infrastructural and intellectual needs of a constantly expanding world, and it is unnecessary.

                    I can see the Realm as having made increasingly superior Thinking Automatons still based on practical scenario modelling and problem solving, because I see those things as quite noteworthy and challenging on their own.

                    I'm not really concerned with the premise of a "self aware" machine in the manner of A.I. science fiction being pursued or even possible, because I don't tend to find the premises of such fiction very compelling.

                    I would see something like Eyem was not a "barely sentient" machine; sentience or sapience weren't even on the table in its production. It was an artificial intelligence in the actual meaning of the word; not an intelligence created artificially, but something that, through complex mathematics and a comprehensive set of premises and interactions to process, is able to provide the superficial appearance of intelligence. I just see Thinking Automatons as portraying this premise through a fantasy lens, where its workings and results were a combination of mathematics and sorcerous processes, imagery and terminology, and the interface with its input and output being a matter of ritual and magic.

                    That's the way I interpret Eyem as trying to take over the Shogunate and the Realm; not because it had aspirations, but as a long-term emergent property of how the Exalted who were using it didn't really know how to work it properly, meaning the premises it was given, the processes it used to calculate them, and the way its outputs were interpreted gradually shifted in the direction of "take over everything".

                    This is a way I maintain my interest in Thinking Automatons as something distinct and noteworthy in a setting where people are able to mystically animate golems or conjure spirits or various other things of that type.

                                            The Culture wouldn't lose any physical conflict because the Culture just effectors any Solar it recognises as a threat into non-existence within a fragment of a fragment of a nanosecond. The Culture can't really lose any mental conflict because Exalt Charm capability tends to result in them being able to throw so many dice at a problem that it becomes trivial, which is how a Culture Mind operates too, except Culture Minds are lots of orders of magnitude more intelligent than any organic can possibly really be. The Culture doesn't really need to worry about Culture Humans becoming Exalts and fucking everything up because Culture Humans are notably balanced and psychologically stable individuals, raised as they are in what is a no shit actual utopia. Culture Humans wouldn't Exalt and suddenly decide that they are going to become conquering God-Kings, that fact that they have no interest in hierarchies of that kind and such is why they are the Culture.

                                            But the whole "who would win in a fight" argument is silly. I think it is fair to say that any kind of conventional problem is going to be easily solved by a Culture Mind, and if you're talking about Minds being unable to deal with out of context problems, they totally can do that. They'd adapt their understanding of how things work very quickly, and then devise solutions. They're from a really high concept sci-fi setting about a utopia with a population in the trillions run (without any real effort or trouble) by intellects so far beyond us that we can't even really comprehend how far beyond us they actually are. Exalts are not from a setting like that at all. I love the Culture books and I love Exalted, I don't think either gets much at all from a crossover, other than certain character concepts (Gurgeh would make for a really good Exalted character concept, but you'd have to take the bare bones core of the character and translate him across).

                                                    I'll answer the "Who would win - Exalts or Minds?" question that way I answer all "Who would win in a fight?" questions - the winner is whoever you want. Just pick and choose setting assumptions until the side you favor is the logical victor.

                                                    The question isn't really relevant to the topic, though - the OP asked "Could the Culture emerge in Creation and/or Autocthonia?" My answer is "No, because the social context and potential resources available in Creation aren't anything like those in the pseudo-real Milky Way that the Culture novels are set in." Creation has or can make things that are Mind-like, and post scarcity is theoretically possible with incredible abuses of magic, but even if you managed both of those things you'd still live in a world where extreme power disparities between individual people make the formation of an egalitarian machine-managed anarchy unlikely.
