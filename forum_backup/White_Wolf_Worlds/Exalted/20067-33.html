    A query of medicine, disease, and rolls. Specifically, do we have a "Book A said this, Book B that, nary the twain shall meet" situation?

    My lovely wide got me MoEP: Infernals for my birthday, (mwaha mwahahaha!) and I noticed in the Malfean charms a little sidebar regarding Green Sun Wasting. (pg 110). Nasty little piece of work, but the Morbidity/Treated Morbidity caught my eyes specifically. To quote: "These both begin at the Ess rating of the malady's source rather than 1 like mundane infections, but continue to increase each day normally."

    This got me over to the Core book, pg 350, were I saw plenty about virulence, morbidity, and difficulty to treat, but nothing about them starting at 1 and building over time. Next stop, Core pg 129 Avoiding/Overcoming Disease. Apparently Exalts just never die from any disease, ever, (GC excepted, of course) but there is still nothing about the difficulty ramping up over time, the morbidity always sits at max for whatever un/treated state the victim, er... patient happens to be in.

    Checked the Scroll of Errata, nothing on either book's sections, soooo, yeah, I'm assuming the Core is correct, because otherwise ALL diseases become basically trivial, even to mortals, but at the same time, whut? Infernals was one of the later books to come out for 2nd, did I miss something in one of the earlier books, or was it just something copy-pasta'd from 1st, which I don't have?

    As a corollary question: Seriously? Exalts can NEVER die from any disease less than the Great Contagion? They just suffer a -2 penalty on all rolls until they shake it, even if they take very literally years to build up enough Stamina and Resistance to roll high enough? Okay, sure I'd absolutely count all that time as Training time for both, but seriously? Some Sta 1, Res 0 any-kind-of-Exalt-you-want-to-name (rare, but it can happen) gets him/herself infected with Plague, and they can survive until they have they have trained up say, Sta 1, Res 2, and gotten lucky on a roll? Need an Excellency too, but Plague. For no less than 2 and a half weeks (Solar, non-Zenith), and probably longer due to being -2 dice and needing to be really lucky on the roll anyways, even with a medic dedicated to curing them 25/7?

    Has anybody house-ruled that in a logical manner? I'd love to hear how others have made disease relevant in Exalted games. Mortals games, it's frigging scary save-or-die stuff. Exalts? "Meh, I've got a charm that'll mean I don't even notice I'm a walking Plague bomb!"

        Originally posted by Hand-of-Omega View Post
        New Q: I know that Creation's currency is based on Jade, Silver, and Cowries, but is Gold just not valued at all?
        It is, in fact, valued. More highly than silver, even, but less so than orichalcum; observe the Panoply chapter in the Ex2 Core - there's a section on clothes and jewelry and such, and gold fits right between the two.

        Presumably it's just not something that gets minted into coins for various reasons - perhaps there was a scare early on where a sorcerer did a Working, creating a bunch of "gold" coins that turned other gold it came into contact with into lead after a week or somesuch.

        Re: Exalts and disease: Exalts caring one whit about non-Exalts (or even caring one whit about Exalts who care about non-Exalts) is probably a good start. They might not be able to die from the plague, but being covered in highly-contagious sores while constantly dripping disgusting fluids is gonna hurt you socially. Others won't wanna hang around you for fear that they'll die… and they probably will die if they hang around you.

        That said, I'd probably increase the penalties if they continue to go untreated and the disease is severe enough, but it honestly doesn't bother me that they won't die from a disease. If having the plague stops people from interacting with them, and they lack food-growing or food-gathering skills (let alone food-preparation - stacking botulism on top of plague makes it even more unpleasant!), starvation might even be a factor.

                                    Well...

                                    The Neverborn want to die, properly this time, because being undead hurts a hell of a lot and also it sucks. Destroying Creation seems like an excellent way to drag down everything into Oblivion and unclog the toilet. Or possibly it's revenge. Or they're insane and nobody knows why. Point is, they want to destroy the world. The Deathlords are the ghosts of dead Exalted, mostly insane First Age Solars but possibly a few other types, who want ... stuff. I prefer to think that only some actually want to destroy Creation, that the rest want to conquer the Underworld or get revenge for their deaths. I'm not entirely clear on that. Well the Neverborn turned them from regular (if knowledgeable) ghosts into insanely powerful (and insane?) ghost-monsters and told them to blow up the universe. They got awfully close with the Great Contagion, but couldn't quite finish it off, and then lately got their hands on ~95-100 Abyssal Exaltations.

                                    Meanwhile, Bob the sort-of heroic but unlucky mortal gets gacked by pirates, or murdered by Exalted, or devoured by cannibals, or trampled by a mob, or eaten by a bear. As he lays dying, a creepy voice that definitely isn't a normal person offers him life, and incredible power, if only he'll serve it... I'm not sure if Bob is told right there that he has to give up his name and help conquer/get revenge on/destroy Creation, or if his obligations are nebulous. If Bob refuses, Alice 32353 miles away gets offered the Black Exaltation. If she accepts out of desperation or misanthropism or whatever, she is now an Abyssal. Her Deathlord sends other deathknights to collect her, train her, equip her, and inform her what her new duities are to her Liege. And this stuff about sins of life and death which are punished with Resonance. She is a vassal of her Liege, which means she does what the Deathlord wants to advance their agenda, and in return she gets artifacts, and undead slaves, and possibly gets to rule or help rule a shadowland. Ideally, if her Deathlord is planning to destroy the world, then she has to die eventually too. But that doesn't need to be soon, because she's literally immortal unless somebody kills her.


                                    Meanwhile the Yozis really hate being locked up inside their own and each others' mutilated bodies, and they hate the gods and humans and want revenge. Or to reshape Creation to make it more pleasing. Or just to get the fuck out of there. Or in Adorjan's case, to enlighten everybody with the gift of murder. Six of the Yozis have a sort of deal with the Neverborn, kind of, that gets them 50 Green Sun exaltations. And they have this plan to conquer/get revenge on/reshape Creation, which they hope will also allow them to escape from prison. And these Green Sun Princes are going to be the tools to make it happen.

                                    So, Millie the mortal is trying to help defend her village from faeries/cannibals/soldiers/bandits/tax collectors/slavers, but she's not really a warrior nor particularly good with combat thaumaturgy (which doesn't really exist) and it looks like EVERYONE is going to die. So she runs away into the woods and everybody else is dragged away to be eaten/enslaved/raped/mind-wiped, including her family, possibly including her. Or she's trying to search the dangerous wilderness for the last Pink Orchid of Life to save the town from plague, and loses confidence in her ability to jump over the 20-foot chasm to get to the slippery hill where the Orchid might grow, so she fails. Or she is a shaman trying to negotiate with the local spirit court to not bilk her people out of everything they own and just doesn't pull it off, and her village is wrecked. Her people, in outrage, brand her face and exile her into the wilderness to die.

                                    And then as she wonders what the heck she's going to do now that her life is utterly, completely ruined, and possibly contemplates suicide... this weird spirit appears and gives her a second chance. You don't ever have to be weak again, you don't ever have to fail again. You can get revenge on whoever betrayed or hurt you or ruined your life, or you can save your people and make them great again. There are great beings who understand pain and failure and betrayal and they can give you power beyond your dreams if only you will help them in return. (If she refuses, the demon kills her and finds somebody else.) If she accepts, the demon gloms onto her and turns them both into a giant coccoon, which other demons drag into Hell. She sees bizarre visions from the Yozis in her dreams, and wakes up 5 days later in Hell. And they give her training, and artifacts, and equipment, and slaves, and drugs, and hookers, and whatever else she wants, and they also plant a compulsion in her head to go do XYZ. XYZ is probably related to her personality or interests or inclinations in some way, but it isn't necessarily a very good fit. But the point is, if she goes and does XYZ, she gets rewards! And also a new Urge, because she agreed to serve. Meanwhile there are zillions of demons who treat her like a rockstar celebrity and fulfil her every wish (well, they can fulfil many different wishes). Life is actually pretty cool as long as she does what she's told.


                                    In both of these cases, you start with a mortal who had some spark of heroism in them, but for one reason or another did not Exalt in any normal way. They were unlucky, or they lost their courage/conviction/temper/compassion at a critical moment, or something. They weren't good enough, and the gods did not Choose them. And as a result, their life was taken away from them. Every Abyssal came to the very edge of death. Every Infernal failed so terribly that their life was ruined. Each of these people loses their place in human society through exile, or being presumed dead, or just having their society killed out from under them. Everything is taken away from them.... and then a new world is given to them. They're alone, but they are offered a new place to belong, amazing magical power and luxuries and wealth and artifacts and servants and possibly a manse. They have a place where they belong where they don't have to be a failure or a misfit or weak ever again... and the price to continue to belong and be accepted in their new world is to serve their new society.

                                    For most human beings, that's going to be hard to reject. Make war against outsiders... well most humans already do that. But then again Abyssals and Infernals are both asked to do things that become onerous, or incredibly dangerous, or that they sit up at night and wonder why they agreed to do such terrible things. They were desperate, but are they willing to continue doing it? Some are, and some aren't. So there are some who take their incredible power, and give up the community and infrastructure and support that their rulers make available to them, and go rogue.

                                    Oh, and in both cases there's a lot of political rivalry. The 13 Deathlords aren't friends and sometimes attack each other in various ways (that meanss, often, sending their Abyssals to mess up each others' stuff). The various Third Circle demons of Hell, whether or not they're part of the Reclamation project, don't necessarily have the same short-term agendas and are often willing to inconvenience, oppose, or betray each other in pursuit of what they want. Exalted are going to end up enmeshed in these schemes, so even if they serve loyally, some other Deathlords or Third Circles may want to gack them. Or recruit them. Or trick them into agreeing to unfavorable alliances.


                                    Aside from goals, there are significant differences in aesthetics (Hell vs. the Underworld, demons vs. undead) and thematics.

                                        I'm not very clear on the themes of Abyssals. They share with Infernals the idea of being something unholy, of making morally questionable deals with nasty, nasty entities on the brink of desperation and ruin. They are entities of death instead of life, designed to bring death to everything, and so metaphorically and narratively, and to at least some extent mechanically, everything they do brings death to the living around them, and resounds towards the death of the world. They can kill who their Deathlords want them to kill, or become rogues and spread death according to their own scruples. Or they can instead become champions of the dead people, making their afterlives better, even if it's at the expense of the living. They can set out to conquer a piece of the Underworld to rule for themselves. But in all things, what they bring is death, and undeath, an unquiet and incomplete ending or, if they are ambitious, the ultimate and final ending of Oblivion itself. Only by seeking to become something fundamentally other than what they are, that is Solar Exalted, can they cease to be creatures of death and harbingers of the grave. They don't have to be evil, but they are inextricably linked to death and estranged from the living world that they lost when they exalted. They are held back from death, but they are not returned to being entirely alive in an ordinary sense (although they aren't undead).


                                        Infernals begin as people who made a mistake, or weren't quite virtuous enough for the gods to Exalt, or just had a single moment of failure ... or perhaps lived a life of failure. They may be misfits in their society, mentally unstable or disabled or cowardly or weak-willed or too willing to question the gods, or else upstanding members of demon-worshipping cultures, or just unlucky people who end up in desperate circumstances. If they are from a privileged background, generally they are offered the Exaltation after all that they had is taken away from them. They are remade into beings alien to Creation, but not alien to life itself. They become demonic, and partake of Hell. Hell isn't a place of inherent or absolute objective evil, and demons aren't inherently evil, so neither are Infernals. But it is a place ruled by the Yozis, who by most human (and even some demon) standards are incredibly cruel, tyrannical, unjust, and certainly insane rulers. And generally their souls share these traits. Which means that demonic society is full of misery, and pain, and lies, and hate, and betrayal, and almost completely lacking in things like love and trust and kindness and equality. The music is AMAZING, however. First Circle demons are like space aliens, utterly inhuman and often inimical to human life, and living in an exceedingly brutal society in which they're the slaves. But not necessarily evil in any objective sense.

                                        Green Sun Princes are made into celebrities, lords and ladies, of this brutal and beautiful and alien world. They are gradually transformed into something less human and more alien... and they tend to lose their sanity, as they take pieces of the insane Yozis into their minds and bodies and souls. Their metamorphosis and powers often take the form of permanent mutations that change how their bodies or minds function, not just magical abilities that they can turn off at will. So they gradually become less human, though the degree and ways they change is largely up to them. As they become something radically other than human, they also start to take a more demonic or Primordial view of Creation and may have really bizarre, inhuman ideas of how to make Creation a "better" place. Some of those ideas might even be beneficial for humans! (Depending on your point of view.) But they achieve these things in very strange, disturbing ways, and their thought processes can seem rather nonsensical to normal human beings. For GSPs there is no path to become a Solar Exalt. They're stuck with what they are, however they can choose to take that in a lot of different directions, all of them bizarre. And if they decide they don't need the support of Yozis to make it big, they can take their toys (and maybe their demon worshippers) to Creation or the Wyld and build a kingdom there, and do whatever. And possibly turn themselves into weird Primordial-Exalt hybrid THINGS that take the bizarreness in directions no Yozi or god had conceived of, because they made it up in their own twisted heads.
