    If we go back to 2e lore on the topic of First Circle Demons and their culture, most first circles are lacking in the area of distinct identity and behavior. I believe developing a distinct identity and personality was actually illegal for first circles and ones that did were hunted and killed.     So can a first circle be teacher, sure. However, for anything that a player might be interested in a second circle is a far more reliable option.

                    First of all, basic teaching isn't that hard. If you're a human that knows their subject well, you can probably teach it. Teaching well is a different matter.
                    But for the basic teaching rules of "half the training times" then it's fine.

                    The difficult thing with basic (as opposed to good) teaching is class management. A lot of people get really annoyed at student's bad behaviour, as well as impatient with stupid students, students who forgot what you taught them last week, students who are uninterested, etc. It's also easy to just accept it, fixate on the small number of good students, and end up basically not teaching the students who sit at the back of the class and go to sleep/play on their phones.


                    So the key points you need for a demon that's going to teach, say, kids, is:
                    Good charisma (so they pay attention)
                    Decent mental scores.
                    Decent Lore and Linguistics
                    Not going to flip out on the students.

                    The last one of course, is why Blood Apes are rubbish at teaching Brawl, even though they're very good at Brawl.

                    A demon with Int 3, Charisma 4, Linguistics 2 and Lore 3, is going to be a better teacher than most humans.

                    The other problem though, is that you have to understand the students. Some human teachers are better than others at this, but for demons it's a big issue.

                    Like, a Gilymne could probably teach dancing... except dancing is so natural, and they're so inhuman, that they might well find it very difficult to explain to a student how to do the dance moves.
                    And a Neomah could give tips on seducing people... but they'd have no idea how to explain how to make yourself look seductive, because they just use a charm.


                    This of course, is talking about a pretty "normal" teacher, as demons go. The "stick knowledge into their head via proboscis" is exactly the sort of thing a demon might do.

                    It's probably also important to remember that Neomah weren't designed by the Yozis to help sorcerers conceive, Passion Morays weren't designed to cure PTSD, and Sesseljae weren't designed to cure people of poison. That's just what sorcerers use them for.

                    There probably isn't a demon that was designed by the Yozi to be a teacher for humans. There just might be demons who happen to be good at it (like Isator's example), and so are used by sorcerers for that purpose.

                            There are, incidentally, some example demonic teachers in the books. Here's one from each level:

                            Lucent Wing is an Agata who was the steed of a sorcerer at the Quicksilver Queen's court. As he had a lot of free time when the sorcerer didn't need to fly anywhere, he learnt Lore and Occult at the court. When he returned to Hell, he set up a small trading stall of thaumaturgy and alchemy. Later he opened a small school, the Tin Orchard Academy.
                            He's essentially just an Agata with better Lore and Occult, and some thaumaturgy and (secretly, as it's illegal) sorcery.
                            (Agata are not actually good teachers; they have the stats, but their brilliant obsessions are pretty untranslateable to human minds.)

                            Mara of course, is famously summoned to teach sorcery and martial arts. But these can cause the summoner to fall under her dark sway (and indeed, those who learn Black Claw from her will fall in love with her).

                            Suntarankal, the Crucible of Brass and Iron, is both a school and the headmaster of that school. This wizened old sifu with brass eyes and a sadistic smile likes teaching, and wishes his students to spread his glory... but his teaching methods are callous, and brutal. He'll teach a summoner ancient martial arts, but hints they could learn more at his Dojo in Hell... where he'll put them through a series of tests so brutal it will probably kill them. Normal students are easily accepted, but cannot leave; those who fail to match his exacting standards are kept as fodder for better students to practice on.

                            (If you look at p.96-98 of Malfeas, it mentions a few schools. There are demons who are professional teachers)

                                            Thinking more about it...

                                            Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                                            It's probably also important to remember that Neomah weren't designed by the Yozis to help sorcerers conceive, Passion Morays weren't designed to cure PTSD, and Sesseljae weren't designed to cure people of poison. That's just what sorcerers use them for.
                                            Yeeeaaahh that's kind of the worst thing to remember, because it's worth noting that the neomah, the passion morays and the sessljae weren't actually designed by the Yozis, or even the Second Circle Demons; they were designed by Jenna Moran, who absolutely was thinking first and foremost of how they would be useful and interesting in Exalted games, especially as things for sorcerers to call up. The in-character reasons for a demon courtesan to have things like shapeshifting and weaving flesh into babies are mostly just to give some colour to the concept, and exactly where they fall in the demon hierarchy is really just a background detail that is kind of fudged (even if the manner in which it is comes out as kind of neat, it's just not the point).

                                            Make up a First Circle Demon 'cause it's interesting and can serve a useful function to the magician that summons it, not because you can justify why it exists. I can't really see any way that getting too hung up on the diegesis is beneficial here. That's how you end up with sidebars talking about how Alchemical Exalted can't become akuma because bpup bup Primordial Essence.

                                                    I wanna say that Compass: Malfeas had these mantis headed scholars who were passionate about learning ...because they wanted to learn/figure out something forbidden that would cause Orbilis to fling them into the sky. I feel like they would probably be good teachers for most kinds of Lore or other academic abilities.

                                                    As for the original poster’s question, I guess my feeling on the subject of teaching charms is that, reducing the time required to learn things is baked into the rules as is, so it’s probably ok to mess with that. Messing around with xp totals is where I draw the line. I’m of the opinion that charms really shouldn’t mess with xp in general and, if they give you something free it should either be temporary or you should need to pay for it later.

                                                    Like, the demon that sucks out ignorance might grant +1 to Int rolls for a scene, but if they grant you another point of Int, the character should need to pay the xp for it as soon as they could. Alternately I suppose that losing a dot from another Attribute might fit, but that might be a perverse incentive in some cases.

                                                    Anyone trying the hypothetical “So, I can trade my second dot of Wits for a fifth dot of Int and then buy back the second dot of Wits I had with my accumulated xp” trick should probably have the training times for Attributes heavily enforced in that case, so that having Wits 1 is actually a problem for awhile.
