                                            07-26-2017, 11:13 PM
                                            Most of you want to accept Three-Leaves into our command, so long as we divide his forces among our own, to prevent any undue plotting or scheming, as well as placing Three-Leaves himself under Masanori’s heel. Others suggested various tests of loyalty.
                                            We shall see how things play out.

                                            -----------------------

                                            After a long moment of thought, you call out, “Very well. I accept you into my service, pending an oath of loyalty. Once you are inside, come to my pavilion, and we will discuss terms.” Turning to Sg. Oxblood, “Open the gates.”

                                            With a short shrug, he gives the order, and Masanori steps forward, “Now, Lord Jianghu, I do not wish to tell you what to do, but….. do you really think this is wise? Three-Leaves is about as trustworthy as..”

                                            You cut him off, firmly yet politely, “Masanori, I can’t very well offer you absolution, and not offer it to others who wish to submit. That would make me a liar and a hypocrite, both epithets that I do not want, nor can afford. I intend to be known as a fair ruler, and that means keeping my word, even if it would seem foolish. Besides, even I know that killing those who surrender will do nothing but make it less likely in the future.”

                                            He stares, “… I can understand that, my Lord, but …… Three-Leaves, really? What, exactly, do you expect him to do? He is barely a leader, and will almost-certainly betray you as soon as it is convenient….”

                                            You raise an eyebrow, “I expect him to follow my every order to the letter, never faulting or shirking. If he does, then our agreement is off, and I will punish him for his past crimes. As will you.”
                                            He looks confused, “….say again?”

                                            “I am placing Three-Leaves under your command, Masanori. You are my first retainer, and as such, you will have superiority over the other retainers under me. You will manage him, and if you find him wanting…….”, you raise your hands in a gesture of finality. “Just come to me first.”

                                            He looks shocked, then amused, “I must admit, I am a mite touched you trust me so early on, my Lord.”

                                            “Just an appeal to your better nature, Masanori.”

                                            “My better nature?”

                                            “Sure. You are a good man at heart, Masanori, even if you yourself don’t believe it. Your ‘defection’ wasn’t entirely pragmatic.”

                                            He rolls his eyes in response, yet a small smile remains ghostlike on his lips.

                                            Turning to Sgt Oxblood, “Sergeant, please ensure our most recent recruits….. shall we say, start seeing new people?”

                                            He nods, “Good idea, M’Lord. Whenever there is a troublesome group, it is best to split them up before the trouble gets worse. Any preferences for how you want them divided?”

                                            “Not really, so long as they can’t collaborate effectively with each other. Splitting them up between the regiments Masanori brought over would be a good start. Other than that….. keep the night-watch on high-alert for now, for a few more hours at least. I would hate for this to be an ambush in disguise.”

                                            The Sergeant knuckles his brow, “By your command, M’Lord.”, and starts dragging men from the line walking into the encampment. As Three-Leaves passes by, you and Masanori bracket him, with you up front and Masanori taking the rear.

                                            As you limp back to your pavilion, you feel the frost ebb from Moonlight Over Water, until all that remains is a slight tingle in your fingers. Evidently, the weapon responds to your emotional state, or, at least, that is what this has been happening thus far. Suddenly, you think back to when you fought the Lunar, and used the weapons blessed by the Horned Huntress. Granted, they smoked and burned instead of freezing and gleaming, but…… could this be the same thing, or something similar?

                                            Perturbed, you wonder just what this could mean. Supernatural stuff like this is supposed to be the purview of the Dragon-Blooded Princes of the Earth, but ….. this spear was a gift, from one of the Most High Rulers of Heaven! It can’t be bad, or unholy Anathemic sorcery….. can it?

                                            You push the thought to the back of your mind when you come up to your pavilion. You will cross that bridge when you come to it, and you have more pressing concerns at the moment.
                                            Inside your pavilion, you turn and face Three-Leaves, who remains standing until Masanori presses in the back of a knee. He drops to a knee with a bitten-off curse and a glare at Masanori, and barks,

                                            “What’s this about, then? I turned over my forces to you, didn’t I?”

                                            You say nothing, dropping into a camp-stool and looking the man in the face for a long moment. Then, as you point across the tent, to a jar of plum wine, you mildly ask,

                                            “Tell me, sir Three-Leaves….. do you know what the term ‘retainer’ means?”

                                            “….huh?”, and Masanori rolls his eyes in exasperation, pouring cups of drink.

                                            “A retainer, my good man, is someone in service to a figure of authority. It means, almost literally, ‘one who serves’.”

                                            “….ok. What’s this got to do with me?”

                                            “ That is our agreement. In return for becoming one of my retainers for 5 years, serving me with distinction and efficacy, I will absolve you for any and all crimes you may have committed, both to me and to my people. Thuggery, thievery, assault and murder, all will be washed away….if you serve.”

                                            His mouth gapes, “5 years!”

                                            “Yes, 5 years. Not enough to negate a life of crime, but a good start. What say you?”

                                            He broods for a long moment, “…. And if I work for you for 5 years, I’ll get what Masanori gets?”

                                            “It depends.”

                                            Angrily, “What does that mean?”

                                            You stand up, knees shaking, using Moonlight Over Water for support. The shaft goes ice-cold again, numbing your fingers, and you take solace from the apparent solidarity.

                                            “Masanori has already both proven his loyalty, and proven his worth. He brought 300 soldiers with him, detailed information regarding the forces, resources and fortifications inside Dun Nashuon, and taken out several key commanders on the enemy side. In essence, he has all-but won this war for me, and that, among other things, is why he is getting a promotion. You, on the other hand… have brought with you a scant few dozen men, beardless boys as you put it, and no information. Do you think that is worth an offer of vassalage?”

                                            Quietly, you accept the cup of wine from Masanori, then continue.

                                            “Regardless, I wish to be known as an honest ruler, one who appreciates and rewards his faithful servants, and if you prove to be worthy of vassalage, then by the gods that is what you will get….. at the end of 5 years of service. Masanori is bound to the same terms as you, so do not feel left out. But… I still question your loyalty.”

                                            “In the interests of appearing magnanimous, I am going to offer you the choice how you prove your loyalty, as opposed to simply ordering you. Hmmm…… you can fight, either Masanori or I, proving both your skill and your ability to follow instructions, you can bring me the head of one of the bandit-leaders in Dun Nashuon, to prove your defection true, or…. You can push the ram in the assault, to prove your dedication. Your choice.”

                                            Three-Leaves shakes his head almost instantly. “If I go back to the fort, they’ll kill me before I say, ‘How do you do.’”….. He glances at Masanori, blanching at his sudden feral grin, then turns his eyes to you. As he takes in your trembling knees, weight leant on your spear, the corners of his lips start to twitch up in a sneer, and as he opens his mouth to reply, you lean close and whisper.

                                            “Before you speak, let me tell you how I received these injuries, and just why the Tribes are here in the assault. Tell me ….. what do you know of the Lunar Anathema?”

                                            His face blanches again, and Masanori frowns, lifting an eyebrow.

                                            “There was a Lunar Anathema plaguing the lands of the Paskamansett Tribe, who make up one of the warbands here. In return for their support, not only here, but in the war with Yue, I ventured out to slay the beast.”

                                            Masanori interjects, “You….fought a Lunar Anathema? And you survived?”

                                            You grin, pulling back your lips to expose the enlongated canines, appreciating how Three-Leaves draws back (you begin to understand why your grandfather does it so much), “My clan-name is Qianke, Masanori. Would you please inform Three-Leaves just what that stands for?”

                                            “…. One of the way of the spear.”, he breathes.

                                            “Exactly. I did survive, albeit broken and bloodied, but more, I was rewarded with the spear I now hold. Its name is Moonlight Over Water, and per my clan-name, I am very skilled with it.”

                                            You abruptly toss you cup of wine in the air, watching both Masanori and Three-Leaves jerk back. Before the cup can fall, or the wine slosh from the rim, you whirl the spear around, intercepting the bottom of the cup and balancing the whole ensemble on the blade. Sliding the shaft smoothly between your fingers, you bring the blade up to your chin and sip from the captured cup.

                                            Perfectly chilled. Wonderful.

                                            You turn back to the two men, “Just to ….disabuse the notion that I may be incompetent at combat.”

                                            Aghast, Three-Leaves mutters, “I’ll…. uh, I’ll be at the front of the ram tomorrow, if it please you, m’Lord.”

                                            “Excellent. For now, you can set up your equipment here, in my pavilion, where I can call you if I need you. Why don’t you head down to the quartermaster, to pick up what you might need?”

                                            He nods hurriedly, eager to be out of the range of your weapon. Walking backwards, he quickly leaves the tent, bowing every other step. A few moments pass after he leaves, and you sink into the stool with a sigh. Masanori stays quiet for another moment, then,

                                            “… That was quite a show.”

                                            “I hope so. I was trying to intimidate him. Technically, I am not supposed to fight in earnest for another month or so, so I don’t know what would have happened if he called my bluff.”

                                            Masanori stays quiet again, then says, “ Do you honestly expect him to last five years?”

                                            You recline back in the stool, stretching out your legs with a wince, “Masanori, I don’t expect him to last a week. But, that lack of character is on him, not I. I gave him the chance for absolution, it is up to him if he wants to keep his side of the bargain. I, to be honest, am not even worrying about it. For all I know, he could catch an arrow in the throat tomorrow.”

                                            “Do you want him to?”, as he gives you a meaningful glance.

                                            It takes you a moment to actually understand what Masanori is getting at. “No. No….. I’m willing to give him the benefit of the doubt.”

                                            “Very well.” He is quiet again, then suddenly interjects, surprising you as you almost fall asleep. “If I may ask, what is the plan for tomorrow?”

                                            “Oh! Uh…. Here, there is a map over here…. Somewhere…”

                                            Rousing yourself from the stool, you limp over to your desk and start shifting aside papers. Discipline reports….. materiel reports…. various minutiae….. there! You pull out a redrawn sketch of the map given to you by your light cavalrymen, and Masanori pulls an oil lamp close.

                                            He looks over the map for a long while, then points at several markings on the map.

                                            “These units here, what are they?”

                                            “ The Jianghu Men-at-Arms.”

                                            He frowns, “All of them?”

                                            “Yes.”

                                            “….Why?”

                                            “They are going to be the distracting force.”

                                            “But they have the ram and the siege ladders, as well as the Militia in support?”

                                            “They are going to be one hell of a distracting force.”

                                            He studies the map for another long moment, then nods, “Alright, in that case, who is the attacking force?”

                                            “The Hill-Tribes, specifically the younger bands.”

                                            “Ah.” He breathes, “I see. They are going to flank, while you make a lot of flash and fire from the gate-side.”

                                            “…. Is that something you wouldn’t do?”

                                            “No, that is a pretty ‘standard’ strategy, actually. Not bad thinking, however. Several commanders have problems understanding ‘fix and flank’, believe me or not. In this case, you are making the distracting force appear to be the attacking force, all the while as your real attacking force swoops in from the …. east?”

                                            “And the north. The forest creeps up right to the flanks of the hill.”

                                            “hmmm…. When are they supposed to make their move?”

                                            “Ideally, when they hear the gate crash down, but we will be blowing orders via bugle as well.”

                                            “Will they heed the commands?”

                                            “The youth-bands… probably not.” You say with a sigh, “That is why I am sticking the Hill-Tribe Chosen over there with them, to provide some discipline, as well as support if the attack doesn’t carry the day.”

                                            He steps back from the table, running his chin, “So…. You have two different forces, both of ample size, gearing to hit the enemy from two different directions. Not bad. A pretty ‘standard’ maneuver, as I said, but not a bad one. It certainly helps that the enemy will be divided and disorganized…… when is the attack?”

                                            “Tomorrow, at midday. I originally planned to assault at dawn, but Three-Leaves little stunt here forced me to push it back.”

                                            Masanori scoffs, “Dawn-attacks are …. Overrated and clichéd, if those terms can apply. Everyone does them, and so everyone is prepared for them.”

                                            “Why does everyone attack at dawn?”

                                            “It gives you the most time to pursue an attack, or bring about a retreat. Don’t get me wrong, a dawn-attack is practical, I just ….. preferred shaking things up a bit, to keep the enemy on their toes.”

                                            “When did you prefer to attack?”, you ask, genuinely interested.

                                            He shrugs, “About mid-morning, when the enemy is preparing and eating breakfast.” At you confused look, he continues, “Think about it. A little before dawn, in preparation for an attack, a commander will rouse a decent portion of their troops. You know this; you have seen it done at this very encampment.”

                                            “These troops will, basically, sit there and wait for either an attack, or the all-clear signal. While they wait, however, they are getting more and more strung out, more tired, more hungry. When breakfast is ready, these troops will stand down to rest, and everyone will be freshened for the day. Right before then is when I attack, before the dawn-watch gets to stand down, but as the rest of camp is still in the process of waking up.”

                                            “And you found it to be more effective?”

                                            “Unbelievably so. It pissed off the enemies I fought to no end, but I didn’t really care about what they thought, of them or their ‘rules’. My methods saved lives, both my soldiers and theirs, and to me, that was worth the ‘dishonor’”. This last word is accompanied with an eyeroll and air-quotes.

                                            “What else did you do?”

                                            “Used a lot of siege engines in field battles, mostly. Not to diminish my prowess as a field commander, but ample use of siege engines turned the tide of Fate in my favor more than once.”

                                            “Siege engines?”

                                            “Big, spindly things, throw rocks, that sort of thing.”

                                            You sit back, thinking if you have ever seen any siege engines period, much less one getting dragged out to battle. As far as you remember, the first siege engine you saw in your entire life was at Teriake Castle, and that was a little thing for throwing shrapnel at the gate approach.

                                            “How did you use them? Can you build some now?”

                                            “Not in the timeframe we have left, Lord Qianke. I’ll need large timbers, carpenters, blacksmiths, not to mention the time I need to train you in their implementation. Some other time, there will be other wars to fight…. It is late, and both of us will need our strength for the assault tomorrow. Three-Leaves should be back soon, anyways.”

                                            And, lo and behold, in comes Three-Leaves, tromping all over the carpet spread over the grass, carrying a bedroll and muttering darkly to himself. Masanori helps you remove your panoply (much faster than the guardsman!), then bows and retreats to his own setup by the foyer.

                                            Seemingly almost as soon as you lay your head down, you are woken up by Masanori shaking you awake.

                                            “What time is it?”

                                            “A little before dawn.”

                                            After taking a moment to process the whole ‘waking-up’ thing, you get up off the cot, not without a great deal of help from Masanori, and head out into the main section of the pavilion. Three-Leaves is snoring loudly, and the air has a not-insignificant chill to it.

                                            Autumn is coming, and you had better get a move-on.

                                            Shrugging on a jacket, you turn to Masanori, “I would like to call an officer’s meeting soon, before sunrise if possible, so we can go over the plan of attack. Wake him,” jerking your head towards the sleeping ex-bandit, “and have him bring breakfast, or hot drinks at least.”

                                            “By your command.”, and he strides off, not without delivering a swift kick to Three-Leaves ribs.

                                            It only takes a few minutes for most of your regimental officers to walk in, with some having risen long before dawn, and you are able to start your officer’s meeting just as the sun starts to rise.
                                            You rise to your feet at the head of the trestle-table, “Good morning everyone. I hope you had a restful night, because we are all going to need it before long.”

                                            You point towards the map, staked out in the center of the table.

                                            “Our general plan of attack is twofold. Firstly, the gate of Dun Nashuon will be assaulted by the Men-at-Arms of Jianghu, supported by the Militia, all under my command. Secondly, while we are assaulting the gate, the Hill-Tribes, under the command of Warchief Samoset of the Paskamansett, will climb the hillside from the east and north, in effort to outflank and destroy the opposition at the gate.”

                                            This gets translated to the Hill-Tribe warleaders, who have but a few questions of their own.

                                            “<When do you want us to attack?>”

                                            “<Preferably, when the gate gets destroyed, but we will sound a blast on our horns to signal you anyways. If you can keep the attack silent until then , so to spring a better trap, that would be nice, but I know that isn’t going to happen.>”

                                            He harrumphs in amusement, fully aware of how hard it is to keep a Tribesman at bay, and you continue.

                                            “The general plan is relatively straightforward: The ram, led by Three-Leaves here (he shifts uncomfortably under the gaze of your retainers), will proceed up the road to the gate and try to bash it down. While that is happening, other units, equipped with ladders, will attempt to scale the palisade next to and near to the gate, in effort to flank the defending forces. All the while, the longbowmen, both of the Men-at-Arms and the Militia, will provide covering fire from behind the safety of the mantlets.”

                                            Looking up from the map and around the room, you ask, “Any questions?”

                                            There are more than a few, most of them just quibbling over details, and you take the time to hammer out any misunderstandings. The sun is well over the horizon by the time you are able to dismiss your officers and equip your wargear. When you leave your pavilion, Masanori close by your heels, your ears and eyes are filled with the sights and sounds of men rushing to and fro, carrying equipment and materiel.

                                            In the staging-ground cleared out by your forces, you catch a glimpse of your soldiers being shepherded into formation, grabbing ahold of the mantlets of split and lashed bamboo, hefting shields and double-checking arms and armor. As you mount Xuansu and ride up, the men slowly fall silent, and you curse when you remember the words of Sergeant Oxblood (who, coincidentally, is looking at you expectantly).

                                            Gods damn it, you hate public speaking!

                                            Glumly, you ride out, to the front of the assembled regiments where you can be clearly seen and heard. Raising your voice, you cry out,

                                            “Men of Jianghu! For too long have you lived in fear of the bandits of the Akibara Forest! Too long have you suffered their predations, their thievery, the murder of your family members and the destruction of your livelihoods! NO LONGER! For no longer will you have to cower in your homes. No longer will you have to dole out grain, the product of your sweat and blood, to people who give you nothing in return. For they are there!”, pointing up at the fortress overhead, “And you, are here, armed and armored, ready to take the fight to them, ready to pay back all the pain and anger they have heaped on you! They cannot escape our righteous retribution! Who is with me!”

                                            The roar from the host filling the valley is stupendous, and you only-slightly-hyperbolically think they could have heard it back at Yagura Castle. Wheeling Xuansu about dramatically (but gently, you don’t want to fall off!), you point your spear imperiously up the slope, at the defenders of Dun Nashuon. Brass bugles sound, resonating with each other and reverberating off the hills, and with the thunder of hundreds of feet, your army starts to march.

                                            Wearily, you ride back up to your Oathsworn bodyguards, and as you approach, Masanori, grinning broadly, calls out, “Would you like some ham for your sandwich, my Lord?”

                                            “You want some cheese with that whine, Masanori? Piss off, I hate speeches.”

                                            Masanori keeps grinning, and Sergeant Oxblood starts laughing, trying to hide it as best he can, and eventually, you join in as well.

                                            “Alright, I admit it, I pretty much just stole that one from a storybook.”

                                            “I mean … it wasn’t bad, just….. very clichéd.” Chimes Masanori, still chuckling. Shaking your head, you fall in with the rest of your retainers, and nudge Xuansu up the hill.

                                            Thanks to the helpful advice of both Sergeant Oxblood and Masanori, the actual approach to the main gate was actually uneventful, relatively speaking. Yes, as soon as your army got within range of the bowmen in Dun Nashuon, they started loosing arrows. But, since they were all firing individually, loosing in ragged lines and at extreme range, their projectiles mainly fall well short. On top of that, the militiamen, working together with the ‘actual’ longbow Men-at-Arms, run ahead of the men pushing the assault equipment, setting up the mantlets on top of the berms bracketing the road. These mantlets, in addition to providing cover from enemy arrows, allow the archers to loose arrows of their own, from relative safety. The mantlets even catch the few arrows that do manage to reach your forces, giving your archers ‘free’ ammunition that they are all-too-glad to send back posthaste.

                                            Mainly, you are ….. bored? Bored, as the ram slowly makes its way up the path. Far from being a crude hacked-down tree, your men cannibalized one of the wagons, pulling off the sidewalls and suspending the trunk from ropes. Overhead, they even have a protective roof, made of split bamboo, that provides cover from both the sun and from any particularly-well-aimed arrows. By far, the most arduous thing about the ram is that it apparently keeps wanting to roll downhill. The poor bastards pushing the thing have to spend half as much energy keeping the thing from rolling backwards as they do keeping it moving uphill. Plus, the thing turns like a beached whale, especially around the switchbacks in the road.

                                            It takes at least two hours of steady pushing to move the ram to the front of the gate, and when you rise over the crest of the road, your heart drops. Evidently, one of the bandit-captains was smarter than the rest, and ordered a great volume of water dumped in front of the gate, churning the thin soil to mud. The assault halts as you have to order some of the militiamen to run back down the hill and drag up some of the extra mantlets, in order to throw them into the mud so the wheels can provide traction. All the while, arrows whistle overhead, loosed from militiaman bows, and the longbow regiments form up in blocks on the road behind the mantlets. As the ram trundles forward, the men sweating and straining, you hear their officers shout, “NOCK…. DRAW……… LOOSE!”

                                            With a whisper like the winter wind, over 200 arrows split the air, travelling too fact to see until the very apex of their arc. There, squinting against the sun almost directly overhead, you see the arrows slow, stop, and reverse, screaming back to earth.

                                            They impact just on the other side of the palisade, and you hear what sounds like nothing other than a quiet sigh. Your mouth dries out in a heartbeat as you realize you just heard several dozen men die, all at once.

                                            Almost immediately after the arrows impact, the screaming starts from those unlucky enough to be killed by the fusillade, and another volley is drawn and released. And another. And another. All the while, the ram slams into the pine-slab gate, cracking the boards with every stroke.

                                            The gate holds, withstanding every impact ….. until it doesn’t, cracking into pieces almost instantly. The men go to pull the ram back, but get stuck on something, the mud, a lathe of bamboo, and behind you, you can hear the brass note of a bugle telling the Hill-Tribes to commence their attack. Your heart rises into your throat, and your stomach correspondingly drops to your knees, when you start to hear the warbling call of the Hill-Tribe mountain-goat warhorns, and realize things are not going according to plan!

                                            You curse, then turn to your Oathsworn retinue. “Come on, we are going to have to push in! Help me move the ram!”

                                            Leaping forwards, you get grabbed by Three-Leaves, eyes-wide. “Boss, the ram is stuck, we can’t move it!”

                                            “No fucking shit! If you aren’t going to help push, get out of the way!”, and with that, you grab ahold of the undercarriage.

                                            Your Oathsworn retainers catch your meaning almost instantly, and they rush in to grab ahold of the ram. Counting off, you lift and push the wagon-carriage in unison, heaving it up on its side and mostly out of the way. Turning to the men bunched up behind you, you call out, “NEXT UNIT THROUGH, MOVE IT FURTHER!”, and turn to charge through the gate.

                                            Evidently, that little bit of effort on your part was too much, and your heart pounds in your chest, pulse racing in your ears. Your head pounds with the beating of the march, and you fall back to the middle-rank of your Oathsworn retainers, charging shields-raised through the wreckage of the gate.

                                            Inside the gate is an abattoir.

                                            Easily several dozen men lie dead on the ground, pierced through by yard-long war arrows, and that many more lay wounded or dying, screaming on the blood-soaked earth. What few men remain standing quickly take to their heels when they see heavy shock infantry charging at them, only to be replaced by a motley crew of bandits and ruffians, trying to prevent your men from gaining any sort of headway into the fortress.

                                            You see the front-rank of your Oathsworn drop their spears, drawing swords and daggers for the close-in melee, and you heft Moonlight Over Water into an overhand grip.

                                            The two groups collide with a resounding crash, louder than the breaking of the gate, and the world descends into mud, blood, sweat and steel.

                                            Your Oathsworn retinue, not in a very tight formation to begin with, spreads out almost as soon as they make contact with the enemy, to better put their individualistic weapons and training to good use. The bandits…. quickly learn that numbers only count for so much, and quality almost always beats quantity. The majority of their weapons, barring a lucky hit in the armpit, mostly just glance off mail and lamellar. The impromptu attack is doing extremely well, at least by your reckoning, and you are immensely surprised when Masanori grabs you by the shoulder, shouting in your face.

                                            “Call for a retreat or regroup!”

                                            “Why?!”

                                            “If we advance too far, we will get surrounded and cut down! Plus, we can help the soldiers climbing the ladders!” Following his pointing finger, you see that the siege-ladders have been leant against the palisade, and troopers armed with halberds and backswords are climbing up and over the rampart, struggling with the bandits on the wallwalk.

                                            Nodding, you call out, as loud as you can, “Oathsworn of Jianghu, to me! To me! Fall back to the gate!”

                                            Somehow, they hear you over the clashing and slashing of blades and screams of dying men, and perform a fighting retreat. You are almost back to the gate, where your flanks are secure-ish, when one of your retainers trips and falls. To your horror, you recognize them as Shin Takeshi, the elderly retainer who has been so helpful, with the Tribes and with this campaign.

                                            As you watch, several bandits, recognizing an opportunity when they see one, break off from their group and charge the man. He either doesn’t see them, or is too much pain to respond, and he doesn’t raise an arm in his defense.

                                            You:
                                            1. Leap to his defense! You can’t just let the man die, after all he has done for you!
                                            2. Stay with the formation! You are in no shape to fight, especially against multiple people!
                                            3. Order a charge again! As already proven, the bandits are no match for the Oathsworn sword!
