    I like the idea of separate branches or trees for separate types of performance, at least broadly. It sounds like 3e is expanding the smaller trees, so I'm not too worried about a lack of options for any given character. That said, I do think some Performance charms should be generally applicable, because that helps bulk things up a bit and conveys what Performance is as an Ability, and there isn't really any reason for, say, Phantom-Conjuring Performance to be limited to one type of performance.

    But I don't really think the comparison to a melee tree just for swords is apt, because it's a different level of subdivision -- there are only three or four broad categories of performance that might warrant making a tree for, and they're as different from one another as Melee and Archery, reflecting the higher degree of compression in Abilities that don't have hefty subsystems attached. The best example within Melee would be something like "improvised weapons vs. crafted weapons", or "weapons designed to inflict nonlethal injuries vs. lethal weapons" or at least the classic piercing/cutting/crushing division.

    JimB has a point, I think, which is that there are real trade-offs associated with the various methods. But while I like some general effects for the reasons I mentioned above, I also think that allowing trees to focus on specific slices of the Ability is very valuable for character. Personally, if I were playing a dancer I'd place much more value on the ability to highlight my character's unique qualities with some dance-focused powers than on the ability to take the entire Performance tree without deviating from concept. You can go too far in that direction, but I don't think "let's have separate branches for singing, dancing and oratory" is going too far.

                Originally posted by Edward Eldritch View Post

                I'm in full agreement there. It wasn't done really well in 2Ex but I was happy there was a system for prayers being answered, even if the result was still completely up to the ST, there was a system that said "this much should be enough to get an answer".
                Honestly, I think I'd rather just have prayer wired into social influence on some level, rather than a "press button, get effect" framework. Trying to get a god to answer your prayer should be relatively similar to trying to get a Guild factor to respond to your request for a meeting, after accounting for the extra complications associated with prayers (they're not a great medium for social influence, they need extra work to rise above the background noise of Twitter randos that major gods are accustomed to, etc.) Depending on what social influence looks like, this may mean that you need to make the god like you first in order to effect a mechanical compulsion, or that you can only put them in a situation where ignoring you is a strain, rather than impossible, or whatever.

                In general I am against a model where a certain number of successes is presumed sufficient to make the god do something, even if it's something as basic as "respond". That's a fine way to handwave it when you don't really care, but it should be implemented on top of a system where contextuality is critical, like the social influence mechanics have been hinted to be. There are only two cases where I'd be comfortable with a guaranteed event happening given enough successes: I think it's okay for "bribe size" (how much the God gains from the prayer, etc) to be linked to roll success, and I think it's fine if the number of successes control how noticeable your prayers are. But gods are people in Exalted, and the mechanics shouldn't present them as skinner boxes that dispense magic.

                    See, my problem with the whole "Gods don't give a fuck" argument, in Exalted at least, is that even the most selfish and corrupt little god has reasons to want praise. While it's true not every God is like our favorite field God; acting out of kindness and general compassion for the people who worship them, but there is still one force that can keep the small gods motivated. (As a note, this is all on the assumption you didn't throw this concept onto the burning trash heap as well. If you did, please disregard this post.)

                    Ambrosia. And quintessence, but mostly ambrosia.

                    If it's vital enough that some gods will strong-arm mortals into praying to them mafia style, what's the difference when a priest with faith prays and leads more prayers because, hell, his prayers are being answered if they pray hard enough! Does a little god need a couple more ambrosia to bribe the censor into looking the other way while he does something? Well, these people really want a good harvest, if I give it to them in a way that makes it more obvious I'm helping, they'll sing my praises. I can envision a largely symbiotic relationship existing in many locations in Creation, where charismatic priests lead their flocks into worshiping one particular small god in return for certain favors. Maybe making sacrifices to them as well, rice, fruit, children, still beating hearts, the normal fare.

                    That's where prayer charms can come in. Even if you take out direct references to Ambrosia, it's clear from some fiction that Gods want to be praised, and get something out of it. There's some conceptual leeway on what can be done with this.
                    - A charm that allows the character to simulate a sacrifice with something lesser, stopping human sacrifice with the burning of certain finely crafted talismans for example.
                    - Creating an intimacy in the spirit towards his worshipers, giving him a compulsion to provide service in return for prayers.
                    - Blessing a sacred site to give a bonus towards praying to a particular God.
                    - Blessing a small God in the name of the UCS, allowing this little God to siphon off some of the prayer juice in his domain dedicated to the UCS (or the Solar who set this charm up, allowing the small God to maintain the Solar's cult and get a benefit from it)

                                    In regards to Performance types and charms: I'm of two minds.

                                    One. It can be argued that the end result is the same with all performance: Emotional manipulation. As far as choice of style goes, that's something for the ST to adjudicate the ultimate effect. No one can see your dance? It doesn't matter how awesome it is or how many motes you threw into it.

                                    Two. Observe Larceny. It covers disguising, opening locks, and picking pockets. All of these I argue are more disparate than acting and dancing. Unless 3e has changed things up completely, they involve quite different charms but do not break up the Ability into separate sub-abilities.

                                    If you really believe that Performance types should split up that strongly, I'd recommend doing what I think should be done with Craft: You get one Ability, but gate the disciplines with Merits. You get a free one with your first dot (and maybe extra free ones at 3, 5, however works best for you) and purchase a 1-dot merit to unlock each additional discipline (assuming it works similar to WoD Merits).

                                        Prayer-specific Performance Charms has always felt like a weirdly narrows and branching idea for a collection of Solar Charms to me.

                                        Like, a basis in which to root some Sidereal Performance Charms maybe, but not Solars.

                                        I can see the fact that prayer is a Performance action to make it be a viable vector for regular Charms, mind, meaning that it can serve as a way to socially influence the god over range.

                                        Which, really, seems like the general purpose of prayer anyway; for the most part, I think prayer is a way for people to ask a god to overlook them, often with a tasty sacrifice or offering to go along with it.

                                        I can see Panther or Perfect Soul using it as a means to tell a god to go fuck themselves, but still through the resolution of Performance Charms that would generally be a forceful means of telling somebody to do that, rather than a specific "tell gods to fuck off" Charm.

                                        Prayer isn't really a special thing, it's just a way of communicating with spiritual beings in a manner that they're sensitive to, in a way that songs do so with a person who has ears.

                                        Essence and ambrosia generation are generated through the circumstances described in the Cult Background (now Merit, I suppose), and I don't think it's really worth tracking just how many prayer actions constitute any given level of the Merit (and indeed, most such daily worship is not even such an action).

                                        There are cases where a prayer provokes a specific and notable kind of response, but even those are still generally a manner of the god already having a predefined relationship with the petitioners, like Ahlat with his Brides. So emulating something like that would still likely require the Solar to have gone to the god and established certain terms of service.

                                        At its most basic, I'd see prayer actions as like several forms of bureaucracy actions; they send an intent out into the setting that the Storyteller can account for as a consideration for how things play out, but they're not abstracting it all so much as to give the response the kind of definition that, say, an attack would.

                                        I think there's also something to be said for actions for the sake of actions; where you play out the action as a thing you want the character to be doing in this scene, and where the dice resolve how well you pull it off, but it doesn't actually have a result so much as it's a contribution to the scene. In such instance, I can see a prayer action as something a Solar character does because of its surrounding cultural or circumstantial relevance, including with the invoked god as a spectator, but without direct result. In cases like this, there are Charms that can help, but they're not the point.

                                        I'm think I'm starting to ramble now, but I feel like I've made some ground in articulating why the idea of Solar prayer Charms has never quite sat well with me.
