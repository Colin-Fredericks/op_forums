                        Originally posted by Baaldam View Post
                        its adult children
                        It consistently appears that adulthood is the point at which you're supposed to start making your own way in the world. We do get references to living in somebody else's home before you can afford your own, but that's of a more distant relative than your parents.

                        children with wildly varying ages
                        It sounds like there are a lot of leftovers in that mix.

                        House staff would be a few talented (skill 3-5) mortal teachers, tutors, nannies & other specialists with the occasional weirdo - god-blood, thaumaturgist, sorcerer, wyld mutant, familiar with speech capacity - mixed in, along with about a dozen to multiple dozens of more general, not specially skilled but serviceably competent (all 2s) mortal servants.
                        With the description of how big those places are, it takes a staff of hundreds just to keep the place clean.

                        I agree that it's kind of odd that the household is consistently the main unit of Dynast society but the single time it was ever described in detail was wrong. I also get the impulse to make the household sizable to fit certain kinds of drama despite every detail pushing against it.

                        My personal model to try and reconcile those goes thus:

                        The core of every household is the woman in charge and her husband. Within it, her word is practically law, his only slightly below it. They may have a child under the age of nine at the time, attended to by a personal staff of nannies and tutors; under exceptional circumstances such as multiple births, adoption of leftover children, they may have more than one.

                        Typically there will be a single younger Dynast in residence, just a few years out of graduation and not yet able to sustain a household or married. The relationship is mutually beneficial; the lodger gets not only a roof over their head, but a superior who can provide professional contacts and additional mentorship, plus the odd lavish gift (items to grow one's wardrobe are common), while the other gets a share of the younger's stipend to inject some liquid cash into the house account and an agent for their endeavours with more mobility, plus a bit of extra tutelage for children of present. Sometimes there are two, but supporting that many new Dynasts is a mark of significant wealth and status, and marks the household of one of the family's top people.

                        There may also likely be one or two residents of equal, occasionally greater, status than the householders, here on an extended visit (or possibly because their own current fortunes preclude residing in their own place). They'll be peers to the main couple, possibly participants in their plans and schemes, and in the event of children being present are vital to instilling in them an early sense of House identity and values.

                        Next are the unExalted Dynasts, usually between five and a dozen. Some unExalted Dynasts can support their own households, maybe even some here will go on to do so if they get good marriages or can make a fortune, but many will reside in the home of an Exalted relative to live in the manner that they grew up accustomed to. They still serve a vital function, having been educated to a level and with sufficient status and loyalties to handle jobs that are a bit low for the Exalted but seen as above or not to be trusted with most patricians.

                        Then there are the patricians, who may have significant numbers. Some are people who run important aspects of the household's business, but most are live in members of the Dynasts' assorted entourages (which can include even the mortal ones), here to provide companionship and maybe not exactly earning money to support their own home. They're in the same position as the royal post of lady-in-waiting or the make equivalent I presume existed but don't know the name of off-hand.

                        And beneath them are the staff, the free servants and slaves, often managed by one of the more high-born levels of peasant who typically reports to a patrician or unExalted intermediary but in some cases is respected and accomplished enough to have direct contact with the lady of the house.

                        On the face of it, the internal hierarchy of the household should seem obvious, but interluding Exalted might find that mortals who have been around for a lot longer can exert a bit more standing, especially if they mask it with insinuations and a catty attitude. Even servants can get in on that, if they know the views of the head couple are a tad negative.

                        Things are a bit different in the headquarters of each Great House, those are usually a few households mixed together under the matriarch. Especially in House Mnemon; she likes to invite other households with children for extended stays.
