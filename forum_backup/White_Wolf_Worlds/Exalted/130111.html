    Sins of the Solar Empires

    05-10-2014, 04:20 PM
    Originally posted by Erinys View Post
    The Exalted didn't just kill the soldiers on the field of battle. They went into the cities and murdered the civilians and children and infants, and smashed the eggs, and hunted down the last survivors who weren't even trying to overthrow the Exalted long after they no longer posed any threat whatsoever. That's what genocide means -- cities running with the blood of millions of infants, unarmed refugees run down and shot in the back as they flee, refugee camps nuked from orbit, surrender and pleas for peace refused.
    This seems like an interesting motif to weave into campaign.
    Would any evidence of these things survive to the Age of Sorrows? I don't mean in underworld, but in Creation. Like mass graveyards, ruined settlements, or even Solar made monuments to the massacres. All of this seems like it would be "cleaned" either by Solar Deliberative, or Uprising Dragon Bloods.

    Did anyone do something like this (would like to hear about it)?

    P.S. Hylden murals under Avernus Cathedral from LoK spring to mind.

                              When it comes to the Primordial Era, we have humans, Dragon Kings, Lintha, Jadeborn, those funky bird people with the super-long necks, and the Eyeless Face (which is some kind of weird hive-mind behemoth thing), and the people of Heartwind Isle, who were this weird type of human-bio-alchemist.                               That said, a massive, 40 story tall black jade monolith covered in the intricate Moonsilver and Orichalcum runes of a long-extinct language, erected by the First Age Exalted to mollify the spirits of one of the races they exterminated might be kind of cool, especially if in the Second Age nobody has any clue what this really big, really creepy thing was supposed to mean.

                              But there probably shouldn't be a lot of these things scattered all over Creation.

                                          You know how Alexander was a fantastic military commander who allowed cities who capitulated to him to live and in fact often treated his defeated enemies with honour and magnaminity.

                                          And then how sometimes he tore his way inside a city, killed all the men of military age, then sold the women and children into slavery.

                                          And he didn't really give a shit who knew about either, because both of these acts were not unsurprising for his time.

                                          That's what Solars were like, I suspect. Sometimes they were fair and honourable in victory, sometimes they wiped entire peoples from the Earth in a screaming fury, and both of these acts were great in their scope and the majesty of their power. And that fits what the Solar is very well. The Solar is a mighty hero in the classical term, a person with the power to change the world and the will to do it, but without any kind of moral necessity to it.

                                          I would imagine that the Solars would build monuments to their victories, no matter the form they took. Probably dedicated them to Sol Invictus, as well, who in turn probably felt suitably flattered. I mean the UCS was quite happy for the Dragon Kings to sacrifice all those humans to him, a holy genocide resulting in a massive temple dedicated to his name is probably a-ok in his book.
