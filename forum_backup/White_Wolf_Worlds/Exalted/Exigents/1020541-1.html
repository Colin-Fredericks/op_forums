    As I said, examples. Since the whole point is that your Exigent can be anything, I doubt they'd say "all city-god-exigents have these exact charms" because maybe yours is particularly powerful or weak, or there's something unusual about the city (or rather, city-god). But examples are still important and useful.
    I'm going to be honest, I'd find it pretty difficult to think up a charm-set for, say, the Exigent of Gem's city-god, with just advice about how to cost charms and build trees. But if there's an example of a bunch of city-god-exigents charms, then I can either use them straight if they fit or modify them if they don't. As I said, the point of having charms in the book isn't just about balance, they're also about inspiration. If the book just says "make your own Exigents! Whatever you want! Here's how to cost their charms," I'll find it very difficult to make something interesting, tbh.

                I think "you're not allowed to have the same charms." Is a straw man that is not at all what Isator was saying, rather that if they have the same charms, it is not due to reasons of being in-universe of a "shared category of Exigent" because Exigents are unique. Rather, if they have the same charm, it's because you or the ST liked that Charm and chose to make it available to a different Exigent, and since Charms are not this in-universe construct anyway, it's not really a big deal at all.

                Constructing two Exigents of animal gods in totally different fashions helps to illustrate how unique they are, but nobody is going to tell you that you can't repurpose a charm from one for another.

                You'll run into potential issues of thematic or mechanical disconnect maybe if you don't tweak it a bit to suit the given Exigent, but that's not really like an Exigent problem, and it's really about if you're satisfied with it or not.

                        I don't mind, I guess, the term Architect as an out of universe, reader abstraction for Chosen of City Gods.                                         What I'm expecting from this book is more of a Template that we can modify to suit the needs of the Exigent we seek to create.

                                        Just like how the spirit charms in previous editions were more or less classified in broad terms and categories, but given unique expression depending on the spirit that was using them, this is how I see Exigent charms being shown. While a Forest Spirit and a River Spirit could both have a version of the Hurry Home charm that allowed them to cross their domain almost instantly (don't have my books handy so I am not sure I am referring to the right charm), their expression of that charm would differ greatly. The River spirit may dissolve into the river it governs and reform anywhere along its length... while the Forest Spirit may enter one tree and exit another at the desired location. Mechanically, the two charms function the same, hence the reason they are both referred to by the same general charm name... but each is expressed in a unique way (and likely called different names by the spirit in question... provided the spirit even bothers to distinguish it as such).

                                        ​I see the Exigent book doing something fairly similar, providing charm templates that accomplish specific effects that can be modified (both thematically and even mechanically) to fit nearly any Exigent one could think of. One volcano Exigent may embody the destructive forces of his patron God, with a focus on combat and other destructive uses of Essence... while a different Exigent might focus on the creative and nurturing power of a different volcano god... and thus focus on craft and even medicinal type charms. By the same token, two different Exigents from two different gods could both be Architects in different way, yet have charms that do similar things... the only difference being more thematic than mechanical.

                                        ​To be brutally honest, this is the only way I can honestly see a book on Exigents being even remotely viable. If the book more or less tells us to create whatever we want however we want to without at least some templates to help guide us... it may end up being another 1st Ed Lunar book... mechanically unworkable and a complete mess. Of course I do admit that my own bias may be influencing my opinion, and given the talent assembled on this project and I hopeful that I will be surprised by the end result, even if it is completely different from my expectations (very likely since this is a truly unique Exalt type).

                                        There are three types of people in the world... those who can count and those who can't.
