                            Sanctaphrax, you are a scholar and a gentleman.

                            I will say this; in my experience, players are really, really good at expanding an already-drawn map. I've had very few players who, after they acquired a decent amount of experience with a given Exalt types Charmtree, couldn't make up a whole bunch of relatively well-balanced Charms that branched off it.

                            Indeed, I can't speak for other GMs... but a few of my favorite moments have been when a player went "Mmmmrrrrr... these Charms aren't quite doing what I want them to do." and I reminded them that they could make their own, and suddenly they remembered that was a thing and were like "FUCK YEAH YOU HAND ME A GODDAMN PENCIL."

                            I have very tentatively been of the opinion for some time that the Exigents are going to live or die based on the strength of whatever templates they provide. If those are of sufficient robustness and utility, people will be able to take them and fold, spindle, and mutilate into whatever they need, which is usually way easier than starting with a white sheet of paper and a concept. If they are not, people are really going to struggle.

                            And y'know, even in the latter case, that might be okay. The Exigent book might be very, very demanding to use. That's fine! These are obscure, one-off Exalts of individual gods, which means they're easily ignorable at-table. It's okay to have a splat that isn't for everyone as long as it doesn't suck.

                                I noticed in the Core, the exigent of the God of Wu-Jian was referred to several times as an Architect.

                                This suggests to me that maybe the Exigent book will include several templates for people to base stuff on, and Architect is the city-god exigent. After all, many of her charms are charms that could apply to most cities. Maybe stuff like the red-light district charm doesn't apply to every one, but it'll still apply to most, as would the charm for getting soak from the stone of the city. So it'd seem easier for the book to include a bunch of city-based charms that most city-exigents could take, which can then be modified or added to for the specific god or goddess in question.

                                Similarly, perhaps charms for war gods, charms for terrestrial nature gods maybe, and so on.

                                I think you are quite right Mercushio, having nothing to base it on would be too difficult, and I would not at all be confident when a player brought me their charms for approval.

                                                            Originally posted by Sanctaphrax View Post
                                                            I think that problem could be avoided by making the Exigents clearly inferior to the existing splats.
                                                            No, that doesn't fix it.

                                                            At the end of the day, if you've got an Incarnae suddenly deciding to create a new type of Exalted, it's going to be hard to avoid the implication that this is their "true" Exalted, and hard to avoid the implication of cheapening what it means to be chosen by an Incarnae. I mean -- why are they doing this now? What's the point?

                                                            If your answer is "they just want more power", ugh. Fine, I'll explain: Part of the purpose of 3e's changes to Exaltation are, I think, to avoid cheapening the Exalted. Exalted are chosen heroes, and Exaltation is a transcendent miracle; it's not a cruise missile. The idea of Luna saying "well, the Lunars are pretty good, but, you know, every little bit helps, so I'm going to squeeze a bit more Exaltation out of the end of the tube and throw some cut-rate New Exalted of Luna out there to help them" inherently cheapens the concept of Exalted and the idea that Luna made an actual meaningful statement by naming the Lunars as her chosen.
