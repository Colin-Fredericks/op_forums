        Originally posted by TalosX View Post

        Maybe I'm misremembering, but I don't recall if God of Exaltation was his title. In 1E he supposedly had some power over who an exaltation went too (can't confirm as I never played 1E). In 2E he had no power what-so-ever over Exaltation. His only job as far as Exalts go, is to prune celestial exalts memories as they pass from host to host. I remember a similar argument in another thread a while back. What it boiled down to is their was a difference between Bestowed Power and Inherited Power. Though it was a while ago, and I can't recall if the Devs said anything about 3Es outlook.

        Perhaps he is the 3E God of Exaltation now. Couldn't tell you one way or the other now.
        Exalted: the Sidereals (1e) and Compass: Yu-Shan (2e) both have Lytek as God of Exaltation, the Right Hand of Power.

        He never truly had any power over where Exaltations went or to whom, or any true influence over the process of Exaltation, though some authors (notably in MoXP: Abyssals) believed he had to bless a candidate before the Exaltation would accept them and successfully join. He always pruned memories, believing that it shaped the resulting Exalt in demeanor, outlook, and eventually endeavor; consequently, he had a paternal attitude towards Celestial Exalts, since he knew them intimately. His cabinet, rather than being a gilded cage for Exaltations, was more of an open-air roost where Exaltations rested and resided. Lytek, as a symbol and a theme, represents the idea that Exaltation is instrument and instrumentality, and he's the face for the idea that Exaltation places one into a long lineage of machinations reaching back centuries.

        Pretty much anyone who didn't grasp that imagery ended up misunderstanding the entire concept of Lytek, which unfortunately includes a lot of people. Take a look at that previous thread, with people reacting poorly to the idea that there's a God of Exaltation. The comparison to a river god is apt - a river god, even a god of all rivers, has no real power over rivers or the concept of such, but they can certainly do things that are hugely abnormal or unique that none others can. The idea of someone having a unique relationship to a character's glowing signifier of heroism, deeper in some ways than the character themselves, can be enormously insulting to the setting unless it's properly handled. On occasion, someone would object to the idea that memories of past lives does a more personable job of placing a character into that lineage of heroism possessed by past bearers of their Exaltation, but you can't well punch a memory in the face.

        It's strange what one remembers; in the past, I'd done a lot of research on Lytek, because I was always supremely fond of him and because I'd hoped to write an Ink Monkeys article on him, to further expand upon the roost concept and discuss the idea of Lytek as a sculptor of divine heroism. I was about 5k in when the Ink Monkeys disbanded. I've no idea what Lytek's relationship to Exaltation will be, especially since the concept of Exaltation has changed so much from 1e and 2e.
