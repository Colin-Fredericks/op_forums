          I imagine it'll make a small difference, but not a massive one. The reason is this:

          Making an Exigent lessens a God. It can even kill weaker gods. This is why there's never been that many, even though most Gods can technically make them. It's something you only make when you're truly desperate. Perhaps you're on the borders of Creation and Fair Folk are about to attack. Perhaps you are God of a city about to be destroyed by a barbarian horde.

          On the other hand, if you live in Great Forks, have loads of worshippers, attend parties with other Gods, and so on, why would you want to lessen yourself to create a champion who you may not be able to control?

          Now, I imagine with so many Gods in Great Forks, there'll probably be 2 or 3 Exigents there, but I don't imagine there will be huge numbers of them.

                          I think "at their disposal" is overstating it. It's been mentioned by the writers that Great Forks is something of a hub for the Exigents, and that is a bit of its setting niche in Third Edition, yes. However, they don't inherently serve the state. It's a place many of them (relative to their number) make pilgrimage to where they're collectively elevated above the Dragon-Blooded (individually that may be the case for them in their home culture if the god that Exalted them is a big deal there, like a Chosen of Ahlat in Harborhead, but the impression I have is that Great Forks given its religiously polyglot nature, elevates the Exigents as a rule as an extension of being Hundred God Heresy palooza). That doesn't mean Great Forks can field them as an army. They're all diverse (to the extreme!) free agents.

                          There's still only one state in the River Province that can field an army of Exalted.

                          But I agree that it does up Great Forks' political position relative to their position in previous editions, or at least better justifies it. It's a definite factor in considering them a major power in the Confederation of Rivers, even if not quite on par with the military and economic centers of Lookshy and Nexus (respectively).

                                              Pretty sure Ten Sheaves was destroyed in the process of Exalting Janest. Granted he was a relatively weak god to begin with. So if there's precedent for destruction then permanent weakening at best isn't unreasonable.

                                              Also, we don't know if empowering the Celestial Exaltations weakened the Incarnae (maybe? But it's irrelevant in the end).                                               Amount of diminishment may also relate to the ultimate power level of the Exalt, but that's just speculation. And probably wouldn't be a good thing if it were a thing because most people would just say "my patron was destroyed and I'm more powerful as a result" and nobody wants that to be a common or optimal thing.

                                              The Exigence seems narratively framed as an intentional hard choice: the power to call up a superior champion at the risk of your existence. I find that to be much more meaningful than if there's no real risk for most gods to do so. Without risk it's hard to explain why there weren't more Exigents in history.
