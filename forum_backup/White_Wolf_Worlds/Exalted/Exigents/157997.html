    [Rough idea for an Exigent] The Chosen of Stories

    06-03-2014, 01:22 PM
    So I had the seed of this idea recently on these forums while discussing the Orphan's Tales, by Catherynne Valente. It's germinated in some odd directions and started off tangential at best. But the idea started there, and I feel like it's important to acknowledge one's sources.

    That said, I had a thought for a charm tree where the vast majority of charms don't have prerequisites in Abilities or Attributes or Virtues, but in something a little less tangible:
    Stories.

    Ideally, the endpoint of this Exigent (who likely springs from Talespinner, but could come from anywhere) is someone containing dozens of stories: tragedies, comedies, adventures, horrors, romances, patently untrue legends...and drew strength from them, channeled them, and did their damnedest to make them--or at least the lessons learned--real in some sense even if they never were.

    Occasionally, I'm tossing out examples of real-world stories below, but obviously these don't apply to Creation.

    This would probably need to go to a player who wants to write these herself, even if IC they're getting learned by the character externally.

    Anyway, this is just the rough shape of an idea. I intend to revisit it at least once 3e drops, and probably more heavily come the release of the Exigent hardback.

    Random thoughts follow, more to come as I figure out how to frame them:

    Keyword: Library -- The Exalt is intended to learn multiple iterations of this charm, and finds that subsequent iterations come more easily. After the first purchase, the cost is reduced. Soul-Sustaining Saga is the most likely candidate, but this may pop up in more places.

    Soul-Sustaining Saga
    Develop a Principle based on a story. As long as you retain this intimacy, you gain an extra willpower per night of rest. More potent intimacies may grant more WP or other benefits. This charm may be repurchased any number of times for a greatly reduced price; only the most potent intimacy provides the WP bonuses. This forms the basis for most future charms. The intensity of the Intimacy may be a secondary prerequisite for later charms.
    Sharing Campfire Songs
    The Exalt tells the story and share the intimacy he possesses towards it at minor levels. At higher levels this Intimacy spreads beyond the immediate audience and spreads through a town or city.

    Life Imitates Art
    By drawing parallels between one of her stories and the current situation, the Exalt subtly influences people to continue to act in a befitting manner.

    Heroic Icon Memento
    The Exalt takes on a visible trapping of a particular character in the story (e.g. the shaving-dish helm of Don Quixote or the Lionskin of Hercules), and gains an appropriate specialty (or other appropriate benefit) as long as it is openly displayed. Does not stack.

    In Gilded Memories

    The Exalt creates stories from her own past for Soul-Sustaining Saga. In doing so, she codifies it and elides any details that contradict the core meaning she creates for it.

    Everyone Loves A Story
    The Exalt can treat story-based initimacies he's shared as positive intimacies toward himself.

                              Sounds interesting!

                              I think you still need to divide the charms up *somehow,* if only to keep things better organized and to allow for prerequisites other than bare Essence.

                              OOOOH. an idea. Story exalted (hereafter called Taleworkers) have traits similar to Colleges, laid out according to the five elements of a story: plot, character, conflict, setting, and theme. Each charm requires a specific rating in one or more element of story in order to learn, and may have improved effect with a higher rating in an element.

                              Plot involves affecting action and change around the Taleworker, along with what little capability the Taleworkers have toward directly achieving their intended goals. A charm which twisted Fate to bring two unknowing allies together would be a Plot/Character charm. If the Elements had associated Virtues, Plot would align with Conviction.

                              Character involves directly affecting or modifying the traits of people and living creatures around the Taleworker. A charm which let the Taleworker assume the seductive charm of a great rake from the latest erotic poetry would be a Character/Theme charm. If the Elements had associated Virtues, Character would align with Compassion.

                              Conflict involves direct and indirect opposition to another's plans, and also represents most of the Taleworker's combat capabilities. A charm which grants another character an Intimacy directly opposing one of the target's would be a Conflict/Character charm. If the Elements had associated Virtues, Conflict would align with Valor.

                              Setting involves directly or indirectly manipulating the world around the Taleworker. A charm which encouraged a fire burning near the Taleworker to spiral out of control, leading to a confrontation amidst a blazing inferno, would be a Setting/Conflict charm. If the Elements had associated Virtues, Setting would align with Temperance.

                              Theme is the most subtle element, and involves more indirect manipulation of fate toward a specific idea or condition. Theme also involves meta-story effects, where telling a story has mystical benefits (or detriments) to the listeners. A Charm which penalized friendly interactions in a situation while granting bonuses to aggressive interactions would be a Theme/Conflict charm. If the Elements had associated Virtues, Theme would align with Willpower.

                              In addition to granting charm prerequisites, the elements would offer their own bonuses dealing with their bailiwick - somehow. haven't worked out all the bugs.

                              In this arrangement, Soul-Sustaining Saga would likely be the opening Theme charm. Sharing Campfire Songs and Life Imitates Art would require low and medium levels of Character, respectively, Heroic Icon Memento would be a Character charm, In Gilded Memories would be Plot/Theme, and Everyone Loves a Story would be Theme/Character. I think.

                                  A very neat idea! Kudos goes to Mockery! ^__^

                                  I should really have turned in an hour ago, but eh! I thought I'd add more thoughts to mull over to the small pile of fine suggestions that have already come your way.

                                  I once babbled on some unorthodox ideas of mine before in here, and think some principles from there may be worth considering, with some other similar notions in a thought experiment from Robert Vance here.

                                  The idea might in this case be to create particular sets of Charms/Evocations/whathaveyou, each geared around a particular story and dependent on maintaining an Intimacy related to it, with higher level effects dependent on a higher level Intimacy (as you'd been thinking). These would be the place to find very particular and distinct effects for those stories -- and may govern the calling of characters, creatures, settings, events, moods, etc. -- and may be where most of the Exalt's power lies. These would presumably be eligible for whatever kinds of xp refund situation Evocations generally see.

                                  Other Charms might be expressed in a more traditional Ability-/Attribute-/other-based Charmset, defining general story-like powers the Taleweaver can come to command or more... unflavored effects that depend on whatever story is being told and/or whatever stories are close to her heart (e.g. Truth in the Telling, a Socialize/Presence Charm that enhances an Imbue social action to persuade others that all of or some element of a story is true, which works for any old story, but gives greater benefits dependent on the Taleweaver's degree of Intimacy for it). As a side-note, the effect of Soul-Sustaining Saga feels very solid to me, but purchases and repurchases may be a bit much. Perhaps it or some of its effects (the ability to develop Evocations for any stories toward which one holds an Intimacy, for instance) may just be an innate quality of a Taleweaver?

                                  Another possibility may come from that "Charms as creatures/entities" idea of mine, in which some of the Charms/Evocations/whathaveyou learned for a story can grant a secondary and related effect/use for the Taleweaver, somewhat like the Dreaming/Waking versions for raksha Charms (e.g. created a Charm for Sherlock Holmes? Perhaps the Taleweaver gets a bonus Specialty or extra roll or whatever related to Investigation in a particular context, if not a full-on second Charm).

                                  There are a couple of other tangential, but still potentially interesting, non-Charm/Evocation angles to consider. One may be thoughts on transference of the Exaltation after death; if you decide such a thing happens, it could be fun and interesting (and edifying) to think about who might generally receive it next. Perhaps one who has come to hold an Intimacy for a story the last Taleweaver cared deeply for -- or even the life story of the last Taleweaver -- might be an especially likely recipient. Perhaps Linguistics Charms could offer some interesting crafting options, allowing a Taleweaver to put beloved stories into books, and subsequently allow other Exalts to develop a bond with a story and call out some Evocations from it. Or even to allow the Taleweaver to revisit the Evocations and other properties of stories for which she may no longer hold an Intimacy (with limits, naturally).

                                  Also, HobbitGuy, I have no idea if it was intentional, but a number of those work really, really well as ways of describing and thinking about raksha Graces and the general uses thereof. ^_^ I hope you don't mind, but I may need to borrow some of that.

This charm also laid the foundation for a titanic number of Lunar charms, including DBT, Prey's Skin Disguise, and the ones that increased your size range. At that point, it was giving the players a charm slot and mandating that it be spent on this.

                                      And that's really not fair, as it's tossing you a charm "choice" and then immediately pretty much forcing you to spend it on this one necessary thing.

                                      It strikes me as poor system design to create something universally necessary, treat it as the gateway to most or all effects, and then make it absolutely mandatory that you have to spend limited, quantifiable amounts of resources on its acquisition to be able to play.

                                              Okay, it's been like two weeks since I was asked this question, and I'm still having a little trouble on it. But for starters, this Exalt--again, not a splat, just a single man or woman--generally acts in one of two or three roles:

                                              On their own, they're generally a subtle influence, seeking out unfamiliar stories and sometimes being around to witness--and occasionally kick off--the formation of new ones. In the latter case, the locals are usually aware of the Exigent's presence--he's rarely subtle--though not what it signifies nor of the change that he inevitably heralds. In a group of other Exalted, he's a capable support person, one of the best PR men you can ask for, and like any Exalt probably capable of surviving a tough scrape better than 99% of Creation. But he's better suited to letting the others take center stage against larger threats--especially those that refuse to be put away with anything less heavy or drastic than the razor-honed edge of a daiklave.

                                              In other words, he's a hero (his powers lend themselves quite well to a Guile Hero), but almost never the hero.

                                              There's some definite Sidereal in his conceptual DNA, combined with lore-heavy adventuring Twilight and a smattering of Mirror Flag. But given that it's a single entity with some unique and defining tricks, I'm not too concerned with the possibility that I could adapt this to another splat.
