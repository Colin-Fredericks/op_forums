        Re: Solar, upthread, I'm not unsympathetic, but if the Great Curse includes unwelcome genre assumptions for your group and game then you... take it out.

        As a whole I have not seen any evidence it presents unwelcome genre assumptions for the community of Exalted as a whole.

        (Although a lot of members of the community were introduced to Exalted via conceptions that ignored it being a game about mighty, cursed heroes in the first place, as is on I think literally the first page of the first book written about the game.)

        Re: if meritocracy is a "shitty principle" (JayTee, maybe, if you're still reading), mainly because it interests me.

        Talent in government certainly isn't a *bad* principle.

        At the same time, personal merit, is, I think a lot less important than the combination of

        - Accountability
        - Interpersonal relationships : People working well together as a group, even if their individual merit is low
        - Replaceability : If your system is dependent on the skill of the meritorious, what happens when your merititious candidate dies unexpectedly or decides to hold the system to ransom, because he holds all the cards?
        - The Rule of Law rather than the whims of the powerful
        - Predictability in government (to allow people to make plans) : Are the most talented always the most predictable in their action? Predictable actions allow others than the ruler to make plans, and create a stable society independent of the government.
        - Continuity of government : Sometimes it's better to be mediocre in terms of raw talent and have lots of experience and contacts to fall back on than just being a great raw talent and not having anything else much going for you
        - The consent of the governed

        Meritocracy isn't necessarily in conflict with all of these, but you can't just dismiss them in favour of merit.

        There are also issues of inherent (though not always unsurmountable) challenge particular to meritocracy like:

        - Destructive competition among the talented: If you give everything to the best, disaffected second bests often end up feeling frozen out of the process and so becoming disruptive. Big problem for your society, especially when they outnumber the best and work together.

        There's also a potential for a process of stakes raising possible among would be elites, who are motivated to try to and increase their merit to gain power, even when it isn't actually ultimately productive in improving performance in government. Especially their merit "on paper".

        Finally, you can also add to that that in a meritocracy, those who gain power through merit are encouraged to kick away the ladder, so their position isn't threatened. Or knobble potential competitors. On an individual level if you're the king because merit, you don't have an incentive for any underling to have more merit than you, because then you're not the king. Whereas if you're just the king just because you probably don't care if some other dude is the Greatest Knight on Life (and if fact, if he is, you just got a useful resource to call on).

        - Defining merit : This has at least threefold issues

        a) in the procedures for defining merit risk rewarding known quantitites which can easily and be conventionally measured as merit than unknown quantities or qualities which are hard to measure, while fuzzier measures of merit risk criticism and morale problems from a lack of transparency and fairness and at the same time
        b) in a system where those with merit get power, people have strong incentives to try and shift the frame of merit to mean "Whatever I personally am good at".
        c) When defining merit, you may wish to choose the reliably talented over the erratically brilliant. This tradeoff is a challenge itself to define.

        - Diversity of experience : The best may only really have experience of being the best; they may not know what its like to lose, to be downtrodden, to be unspectacular, to come from a place and time that doesn't allow you to develop the skills to be the best, amongst other things. That's all got some tradeoff value against talent.

        And there are probably more potential problems and other important principles that may conflict than I've listed.

        Re: whether Grabowski intended the game to be a hopeless setting, I think (perhaps as part of the Grabowski cult) he intended it personally to be stirringly tragic, and generally wasn't big on the secret truths of the setting over personal taste and whatever you feel like doing with it. Really. The following are his over ten year old opinions expressed on a forum (as evidence goes it's what we have ):

        http://exalted.xi.co.nz/wiki/Thus_Spake_Zargrabowski/ConditionOfCreation

        http://exalted.xi.co.nz/wiki/Thus_Spake_Zargrabowski/SanctityOfGameWorld

            Originally posted by Fata-Ku View Post
            Part of the problem, imo, is that if the point of the mechanic is to get Players to roleplay the tragic hubris of their characters, then Limit should be something the Players want to play.
            As it stands, it's built more like a punishment than an opportunity.

            Something that the player controls (but the character doesn't) along the lines of "You can spend one wp to bolster an intimacy for a time, your character is overcome and overwhelmed by it, gaining [bonus dice?] to actions that support it and ignoring everything else" already seems that it would serve that purpose a lot better, and it could emulate existing Limit Breaks rather easily (being overcome with Honor = Foolhardy Contempt; being overcome with Hatred for the Realm = Berserk Anger; Overcome with Love for Living Things = Compassionate Martyrdom or Heart of Tears, etc.)

            (It's clearly not a perfect solution, just an example off the top of my head to get discussion going)
            Thought about this during my classes, and have come to the conclusion that the buildup nature of current Limit does have redeeming qualities, it's more the what than the how that was poorly executed. So I've been pondering a more interesting solution:

            (yes, I realize that I'm essentially talking to myself.)

            Still working off the spend-a-wp-to-gain-benny mechanic that related to virtues: roll [dice representative of intimacy] separately. Successes add directly to the roll as well as the Limit track. If Limit track is filled by this, double the bonus for that action and regain that many (meaning Intimacy successes x 2) willpower points; The Intimacy overcomes the character for [essence] scenes and all actions supporting it enjoy the rolled bonus as extra dice. All actions that don't receive an equivalent penalty.
            (Not sure about the second part though. Might be a bit convoluted, having the same result count as successes in one situation and dice in others... but is essentially IAM otherwise)

            The point is to have something that is representative of the Great Curse, and that players find awesome and rewarding to use, adjusting reward level to reflect the desired frequency of use. (too good of a reward and Solars are all obsessed psychopaths, too little and the Great Curse is basically non-existant. Coming from Mage, I like seeing my players devolve unintentionally into nightmarish caricatures of human nature and see in awed and horrified hindsight what they have truly accomplished and become. So I prefer the former.)
