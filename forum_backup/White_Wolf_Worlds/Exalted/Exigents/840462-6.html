        Not really. Luna is ever-changing, unpredictable, and mercurial. There are various aspects of Luna, one of which is probably about Bacchus-style revelry, but overall they tend more towards transgressive, transcendent, savage or bloodthirsty.

        Something like just generic "party animal" is fun for forum jokes, but doesn't fit the Incarnae very well for canon -- they're not that approachable or easily-relatable; they're meant to be distant and somewhat intimidating figures. Luna should generally evoke that quote from CS Lewis about Aslan about him not being a tame lion -- there's definitely a party side to her, but like with Bacchus, there's a degree of danger there. Luna's revelry would probably involve stepping outside of social norms and letting everything out -- it'd be something that people might do for her festival, but not something casual like you'd get from a fun guy at a bar. It'd be a revelry so deep and primal that it would be like a religious experience. (Which is appropriate what with her being, you know, a god.)

                            IRC there's some writer commentary (John Morke) on the Sidereals having a political hand on the Exigence, as a political process, so from that I'd imagine it's not improbable that their patron goddesses and the Lunars' patron goddess, also have some hand in it. We'll have to see what it actually is in their book though (whether the Sidereals and other gods are involved in the process of managing Exigence, or just involved in managing Exigents).

                            Re: a god of light, I tend to think the gods will be things that are specifically relevant to people's lives. Like you have a god of storms (because they can destroy your harvest, sink your boat and have a resonance of might in the mind of man), while you don't have a god of rainbows because, who cares? (unless you're in some variant area of Creation where they have deeply significant magical power), although the god of rain certainly has a rainbow for his bow and a god of prosperity can wield it as her symbol and so on. So I find it easier to see gods of windows, vision, glass, torches and candles and other such things that have an immediate and concrete use to people, than an overarching god of light, and I can imagine there'd be cool things you could to with that... Or they could even be unified under a single god, but he's the god of those separate things combined in one purview, rather than the god of light as such.

                                                            That's the thing, It's been a long while since I saw people outright stating someone "can't do this" because canon says otherwise, though I might have overlooked some incident.

                                                            We have one common ground, a foundation, on which we have setting discussion, and that's canon.

                                                            When someone questions if something would act/behave in some sort of way or be able/willing to do something, I always assume the person is interested in the canon response. Most people that respond will be the ones who were interested enough to have read about the matter and cared enough to learn and remember it.

                                                            The answer being negative, as in "no, X doesn't work like that because of this and that" is not curbstomping fun and keeping it down. It is just the canon facts, if you want it to be different at your table go ahead. Other helpful answers might even add " and I would be reluctant/would not allow it because of that and this".

                                                            Now if you say "I know the Incarnae don't use the Exigence in canon, but what would change in the setting if I were to change that?", that's a whole different question. You might still get people opining that you shouldn't do it for any sort of reasons but, the premise of the question being well defined, it will generate more appropriate answers.

                                                            What I'm trying to get at is that negative (as in opposite to affirmative, not moody, etc...) answers aren't always fun police at work. Some(most of the?) times they're just the right answers within the assumed context.

                                                            PS It is very difficult to express the right tone through the written medium of forum boards. Many times I gave up responding to something because it just read too confrontational, even though I was trying to be anything but. I think the more accustomed to this people get the thicker their forum post reading skin should become. And then you can always choose to not engage with someone.
