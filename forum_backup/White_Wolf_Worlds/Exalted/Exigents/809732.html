    Exigence as a Long Con?

    01-15-2016, 02:22 PM
    This is kinda bizare, but I was thinking about a god of theives to create an exigent for, and looking over various gods and goddesses associated with thieves, Laverna caught my attention because of the association with con artists (and cheats)

    Which then had me wondering if somehow, a Black market Exigence (or possibly even a legit one) could somehow be used in a Long Con (very long, obviously).

    I'm not quite sure what the payoff would be, or exactly how creating an Exigent would be useful to a god in pulling off a Long Con, but it does strike me as an interesting story line.

    But I thought I'd see what other people thought about the idea. I'm probably bending the concept of Exigence a little too far here, I admit. I'm probably better off pursuing my original Champoor Thief God concept (The Cat who Steals the Stars - goddess of cat burglars and second story men) for the god behind the Exigent, but Exigence as a Long Con seems right for a god(ess) of Con Artists

    I'm a Woman, I'm a GM. Of course I'm Evil.

      From reading the title of this thread, I got started thinking about someone who was being conned into thinking they were (or were going to become) an Exigent.

      More usefully for you, what if the Black Market Exigence was, itself, the prize in said con game, with Laverna managing to con one or more gods into giving her their portions of the Exigence (or access keys or divine certifications or whatever form it takes). Possibly she even convinced her crew they were all going to go in and create an amazing Thief Exigent, but decided to use it all by herself, because she wanted the Exalt to do something very specific for her.

      Hmmm.....

      So, Laverna gets together a group of other larceny related gods to con the God of, say, Cabbages out of his portion of the Exigence. The God of Forgery produces some celestial documents that make it look like there is a serious epidemic of cabbage blight occurring over Creation, while the Goddess of Bribery gets several of the subordinates and a couple of plague gods ready to produce some sick cabbage fields if the guy ever goes to look. Meanwhile, Laverna has been impersonating the Goddess of Botanical Medicine and has been cultivating the Cabbage God's interest at the last few heavenly parties. Once he realizes the seriousness of the "blight situation" she's going to be there to suggest that he give her the Exigence so that she can Exalt someone to cure this "blight." If he seems hesitant, she also has The Cat ready to pull an obvious burglary to get him to give the Exigence to the "Goddess of Botanical Medicine," just to "hold it for awhile," while the Lion Dogs investigate the break in.

      And, after getting it, she uses it to Exalt someone of her choice as the Exigent of Con Artists, instead of doing as the crew agreed and combining their powers and Exalting someone as the King/Queen of Thieves.


      Ok a little rambling, but hopefully that helps?

                  This makes all sorts of really interesting thoughts come to mind. I like this a lot!
                  As for what the goal would be... Well, the goal is always, ultimately, power. Whether it's the power of money to free you from the shackles of society, the power of social domination by removing a political enemy, powerful allies to come help you out... or just literally a manifestation of power. A god gives up an incredibly large portion of their power in order to make an exaltation that'll only last for the duration of the one mortal. And as far as anyone knows, it can't be removed from the mortal under any circumstances. So... What is worth that risk? Well, the easiest answer to me is to treat it like money. To say that they're investing money in with the idea that they'll get way more out of it. So first, who are we conning? Other exalts? Mortals? Gods? The Incarnae? The Guild?

                  If this is the God of Con Artists, then the situation I imagine is that it is their own exigence, whether it was made with good intentions originally doesn't matter, what matters is that now it's been stolen! By someone else! And a god of con artists being stolen from just won't DO! So they hire people in secret, can't let that pride slip... And we find out that this exigence is actually extremely old at some point... Maybe it reincarnates even though most exigences are incapable, and each time it retains more and more power, intending to eventually be reabsorbed... maybe it returns to the con artist with it's grown power so that they can take some back.. Maybe the con artist can take control of the Exigent for exactly 1 day per exigent, and uses that to set up some grand plan that nobody can trace, because everyone thinks that the exigence was stolen.. or maybe they're trying to get gods to alter the exaltation enough that they can use it as a distraction while they accomplish something on their own, like retrieve something from the vaults of heaven (key to malfeas, perhaps, to retrieve power from the primordeals?), or so that they can take the power of other gods when it eventually returns to them...

                  Either way, there's plenty of ideas to roll with, with this. the key to making it a long con, though, is that they invest/lose something, only to get it back plus some because of some sorta duplicity, usually involving them seeming weakened due to the loss of (thing).
