                            Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post

                            At least back in 1st edition, it was understood that some people who had just Exalted, at least in areas where the Immaculate Philosophy was powerful, would turn themselves in at the local temple to get killed, or indeed, just commit suicide before they lost themselves and turned into more of a monster.

                            I remember Grawboski saying that one of the goals was to move Exalted away from the "personal horror" vibe common in many other White Wolf products, and that was one of the reasons why it was assumed that your character hadn't just Exalted a couple of days ago, but rather had been Exalted for several months at least - long enough to come to terms with his existence and not see himself as some kind of monster or regret what he had become.
                            [...]
                            This idea wasn't really brought up much in 2nd edition (indeed I don't know if it ever was), and I have no idea how it will work in 3rd edition.
                            +1

                            The conceit that every Exalt knows inherently what they are and what that means (ergo, any Exalt who still insists they're Anathema is engaging in mentally disturbed levels of self-deception) is mostly from mid-late 2e conversations on the forums, with a little help from the bits in the 2e core that the Exaltation can see to some degree into the future of its candidate and so chooses those who *will* use that power, etc.

                            The conversation where this all came up is also the source of Nephilpal's comment about "I am so fucking metal my name is a power chord."

                            But yeah, the earlier assumption was that you had had the time to survive, equip yourself to some degree, and come to grips with what you were, at least to the point you wouldn't hurl yourself on the pointy end of the nearest jade daiklave with a cry of "We're all over here and our secret base is highly flammable!" by the end of the first session. The folks who didn't were largely not going to be PC's anyway.

                                    Originally posted by Rater202
                                    "Oh no, my act of Badassery has led me to become one of the dreaded Solar Anathema! ...Wait a minute, I'm still me. I don't feel possessed by demon sorcerer... holy Shit my religion is a lie!"

                                    I imagine that'd be a common reaction to Immaculate's that Exalt as Celestials, unless they're really really really deep into to the immaculate faith.
                                    I don't think it does those kinds of narratives any kind of service when this reaction is either inherent to Exalting or condensed into moments or minutes versus being a hard struggle that some (emphasis some, not all) characters have to push through.

                                    But if you were raised a Dynast and you don't undergo this struggle, I'd say something is off. At the very least it should be in the backstory and should be something remembered. Because generally people do not cast off their lifelong religion as false without some kind of experiences and soul-searching to get them there. I don't think the intent is to treat such a thing so easily and callously. Unless of course the character only ever paid lip service and stopped being a true believer a long time ago because he didn't Exalt DB but his jerk brother did, or whatever. At any rate, that's still a moment to remember.

                                    The way you write it, it turns what might be a legitimate crisis of faith (and an interesting narrative tied to it) into a byline or worse, a joke.

                                    Originally posted by Rater202
                                    I imagine if that isn't the reaction, then for Lunars or Zeniths, what with their god attending to them after Exaltation, "Your religion is a lie, Anathema are not demons and they earn the power we gave them" will be pretty high up there on post exaltation infor dump.
                                    I don't think Luna or UCS are going to give any kind of clearly-presented information in those moments. Or at least they shouldn't. The first interaction with the god as a Zenith or Lunar should be something mystical and ecstatic in nature, something that is remembered in a hazy and allegorical manner. Not like going to school and having a lecture that you take notes on.

                                    Your statement also rests on UCS or Luna having the particular human need to explain and comes from a very human mindset. It's important to remember that gods are not human, and even if UCS and Luna look humanoid, they're not human beings and don't approach things in the same manner that human logic would. Why would UCS care about the Immaculate Order and feel a need to info dump on you? Why would Luna bother to tell you your religious upbringing was all a falsehood? As if Exaltation is not explanation enough, or as if your future trials will not set you on the great path they hope for you and empowered you to walk.

                                    If you can, go check out Houses of the Bull God (1e) and note how even the highest-ranking members of the Brides of Ahlat still experience their god through ritual and hazy feelings and presences, not like they're called into his office for a "state of the cult" chat. That's the tone to shoot for, IMO.

                                                    Regarding the existential crisis of Solars specifically, I think the nature of the exaltation itself has something of a self-defense mechanism built in. Solars are chosen from those with the potential and strength to become great heroes and accomplish legendary deeds. While they may be 'good' or 'evil' according to their own values they are not weak, dubious, uncertain, or self-hating. Those people become Abyssals or Infernals instead. While a Solar may be wracked with doubt as to who they are and what it means for some time, killing yourself or turning yourself in to let the Immaculate Order do it simply doesn't fit with the nature of Solar exaltation - such things should be very rare.
