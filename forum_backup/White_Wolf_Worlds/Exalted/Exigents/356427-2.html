                                                    Originally posted by Rater202
                                                    I may or may not have been exaggerating how quickly one would make the realization, but I figure that realizing that you are in fact still you and you are not possessed by a demon sorcerer should be the sort of thing that shakes your faith enough to question if your religion is truthful and walk away from it(well, if they were wrong about something this important...)
                                                    People with an incredibly strong expectation that something will happen can certainly have their beliefs shaken. However, they are quite a bit more likely than you're suggesting to try rationalizing away the disconnect, to find some way it was true in broad strokes, even if the particulars are off. "Perhaps one doesn't fall prey immediately. Why would the teachings not reveal this...? Hm, of course; even if not immediate, it is surely inevitable. Better that they view me as a monster with my face now, to stop me before the demon takes full hold." That strikes me as a pretty legitimate thought a character might have early on.

                                                    I mean, the Immaculates have every incentive to spread horror stories of the Anathema, including those of staggering and subtle persuasive abilities that lead even righteous mortal men to turn their backs on all they believed without ever realizing the cause. In the face of such, the idea that the demon's possession could take a form just as subtle isn't far-fetched. There's also the matter of past-life memory flashes. Whether an Exalt perceives them as things that happened to him personally or to someone else, one who had believed in Immaculate teachings can pretty easily turn them in favor of dreading a demon's possession. "The demon must be stirring, at last. It shows me stories... A life I feel-- I know was mine. The demon is canny; I know these are lies -- they must be! -- but they feel so... true. I can't have much time. Dragons, when I am under its thrall, will I even notice?"

                                                    That I tend to imagine the use of Exalted power to feel very natural, perhaps even pleasant, while the possession of the Anathema's power has been long taught to be starkly against the natural order could well lend credence to the "demon be crazy subtle" conclusion's plausibility in the mind of such an adherent.

                                                    Also, I figure "My deads have led Luna/The Unconquered Sun to empower me and lift me to divine status" to be something to take away from being attended to by those Deities, which of course precludes "I've been body jacked by a demon sorcerer that stole power from the Moon/Sun".
                                                    See "really likely to try rationalizing away the unexpected" above. Such Exalted behold a transcendantally glorious entity visiting them, likely far beyond anything else ever witnessed outside their dreams. That it was a magnificent deception brought on by the possessing demon known to be of vast power isn't an unreasonable conclusion for an Immaculate faithful.

                                                    It really doesn't strike me as at all surprising that these are thoughts some of the newly Chosen might have. For some Exalted, these thoughts may get them killed, though likelier less from suicide or turning themselves over than from distraction -- struggling with intense self-doubt can easily lead one to make mistakes. These thoughts aren't going to be common among most longer-lived Exalted (such as PCs who've been Chosen for at least a year) in part from natural selection. Of those who originally thought that way, Exalts who ultimately came to view things differently have one less factor working against them, and those who lacked such views in the early going aren't terribly likely to take them up later.

                                                        Originally posted by Rater202
                                                        Why would you manifest powers before the demon took control? Why would the deception take the form of Luna or Ignis Divine instead of one of the Immaculate Dragons(who of course, the immaculate faithful would place higher than the other two)?
                                                        According to the Immaculate Order the Anathema were powerful Yozi servants who stole the powers of the Sun and the Moon (the leaders of Heaven - the Immaculate Dragons are the lords of Terrestrial world - the Dragons aren't subservient to Yu-Shan, rather the gods of Yu-Shan labor to ensure the safety and prosperity of Creation) and made it their own.

                                                        When you become Anathema, the soul of those ancient beings, along with the stolen power, fuses with you and will slowly take control. You won't notice anything at first - indeed, often times the Anathema are shining champions and paragons of heroism. They are, often wonderful people, initially. But over time they become corrupted by those ancient souls, as well as become more and more powerful. Eventually the Anathema's soul takes full control, and they turn into monsters and tyrants, who will destroy all their care about, and there is nothing that can be done to stop this. Try as they might to fight this, that is the ultimate fate of the Anathema.

                                                        As such, the Anathema need to be killed quickly, before they grow more powerful, even if they do present themselves as heroes right off the bat.

                                                        Those who believe in the Immaculate Philosophy could easily take any flashes of insight or words of their divine patron to be tricks and deception caused by the soul of the ancient Anathema who is trying to subsume control of their body and spirit.

                                                            Originally posted by Mizu

To people who've read Fullmetal Alchemist, think of it like Wrath, who has memories of his prior life but can't really tell if his current personality comes from that soul or not.
