    Well, in my point of view, why you were Exalted is a much bigger point in a Exigent's story that in a Solar's. An Exigency has a much more defined purpose than that, and I don't think it is too much to require a player to justify why a god went through so much to grant his powers.

    If you want to play a Chosen of Death but can't come up with why Thanatos, Supreme God of Death, cut a out a hefty chunk of his soul to empower you then you are going to be the Chosen of Mr Gravedirt, God of Those Who Died When They Were Burying Another Person. And your power level is Terrestrial, at best.

    That is mainly directed at the people who are saying "No ones gonna play Solars anymore, every spirit and their mother can Exalt Exigents, and they're SOLAR TIER!" Which is not true. To get your champion to the level of a Solar you have to be a mighty god and sacrifice a significant portion of your power and submit to the UCS for approval and pay his price. Most gods can't afford a Solar Tier Exigency, not even if they give their whole existence in return.
    This may not necessarily be true (the part about a god having to sacrifice much to call a "greater" Exigent, for all we know, the god has ZERO say over the power of its Chosen; a top-tier god might have a "weaker" Exigent than a field god, but the point with 3e really is that power tier dick-measuring is unfun, so why stress over this?

    I really dislike the notion that a "weak" god can only make a "weak" Exigent. Ten Sheaves was, by all info available, a pretty minor god, but Janest doesn't appear to be weak by any stretch.

    Really, I think this will largely be an OOC issue. A Solar group where one person is an Exigent, should probably not be markedly weaker than the Solars unless they have depth of power rivaling or equaling them in some field (incidentally, this is apparently going to be the case with DBs, where DBs can rarely do things that are as or more powerful than Solars, but only in certain contexts and not broadly).

    As I have said, context is everything. If someone asks, "Why haven't all the Exigents in history been as strong as Solars, if my character can be" the only real relevant in-setting answer is "Because Exaltation is a divine miracle that cannot be deeply understood with rational thought alone." I feel as if that is a sufficient explanation and anyone demanding more should be reminded that 3e is not going to categorically try to define every little tiny mystery (thank god). And Exigents were super rare throughout history; if they're stronger now, the world must really need them, neh?

    Originally posted by Ghosthead View Post
    I think it's a bit much to require players to explain necessarily and justify why he chooses to grant some Exigent a lot more juice than others.
    I don't think it has to do with the "volume" of the Exigence bestowed at all. It's simply a miracle. If people want an explanation as to "why X god's Exigent is stronger than N god's," well, tough shit, an explanation isn't needed. It's just what happened. In Creation, there's not always a reason that can be riddled out. That's the cool thing about miracles.

The UCS can be nonchalant about his champions. He paid the price long ago, if one somehow goes wrong there are many others, and eventually the investment will be able to be remade when the bad one goes away. He is a multibillionaire investing in start-ups, stocks and currency, throwing his weight around to make shit happen. He can give you a bunch of money, and say make me proud. His money is magical and the only cost he will have is the opportunity cost.

He really cut a piece of (or even sacrificed) himself for you to be what you are now and do this for him. What do you do?" That god is most likely like a desperate gambler who just put his rent money on Horse #9. Or a small business owner who took on a second mortgage to open a new storefront.                                             People keep mistaking the thing with tiers, right down to misquoting it.

                                            They've never said that tiers aren't a thing; they say that it is not a terminology they use internally and that they discourage people who play the game from using, because it is reductive and leads to incorrect conclusions.

                                            Different Exalted are more and less powerful than each other under comparable circumstances. Thinking of things in terms of "Solar tier" and "Terrestrial tier" eliminates consideration of things like the newly Exalted versus the Wyld Hunt, or a lone elder, or the collected and organised distribution of the resources of the Realm, and it makes people confused about how to clot in complete non-comparables such as Alchemical modularity, Lunar shapeshifting, or Liminal resurrection.

                                                        Various probably tl:dr-ish and tangential thoughts:

                                                        With tiers, yes, Isator describes it well. Even when we talked about tiers it was often prefaced with "Assuming two Exalted of exactly the same focus, trying to go about things exactly the same way, opposing one...". Which there is an element of "How often does that really happen?".

                                                        I have to admit I found some of the anti-tier thinking a little annoying back in the day, when there was the whole "But... ah, what if you burned down the building he was in rather than let him fight you? Where are the tiers now?! Huh." stuff.

                                                        Basically because that extreme fundamentally dodged out of the idea that maybe you wanted to have a kung fu duel with the guy, if you're playing a martial artist in a kung fu game. But definitely the idea that conflicts are really not constrained in their approach to really simple scenarios, has a lot of relevance.

                                                        With Exigents and tiers, specifically, especially the idea of tiers is kind of wonkier than normal, because Exigents are the place where people will explore weird mechanics which are of course sometimes much better and much worse than they expect, in different situations, which you'll inevitably have to wing and adjust as you go along. (For the inescapable fighting game analogy, there are some always characters who are sort of generally bad against most, but then have amazing matchups against characters who are formally considered "strong".)

                                                        And there's the human element - if the Exigent player shoots for a Celestial level of characters (as much as tiers are reductive) and then just is more creative in coming up with useful Charms and then using them than a bunch of Solars he's playing with then, that's another thing where you just have to deal. It's like we're going to tell them to stop making their character so cool or anything.

                                                        (Thinking about this thread a little at work today, I was musing about an Exigent of a god of nets and waves, with a powerful fisherman god mythos to draw on like you might expect in fantasy with a seafaring focus. Now what if I was shooting for Celestial level with that character, but the Charm ideas came thick and fast and really did just happen to end up being a bit more effective than that, and through luck and feeling really inspired by the character, even more effective in my hands? I've no clue how that would hash out other than to accept that the character is just like that).

                                                        Re: the ongoing discussion with TGUEIROS, it's not that I think you're wrong TGUEIROS, just it is a maybe some bits of an extreme interpretation to go towards, even if it's not illogical at all. Of course we are all doing Kremlinology a little here. Compared to what you think, it is more that there could well be quite a bit more latitude for individual miraculous results that aren't expected by a given god or UCS, as the fusion of essence of the hero, god and sun becomes more than expected. Or for the UCS to be overwhelmed by his own passion in granting power. Or for the UCS to be very clever and calculating (and you know he also happens to be the deeply respected primus inter pares and commander of these five sort of awesome ladies with peerlessly vast insight into the future, and this other lady who's about the slyest and tricksiest thing there ever was) that you don't know why he's devoted so much power to a particular chosen (in practice, until the player pulls something out, eventually). It has to suit the game though, of course, so of course you'll roll with what suits you.

                                                        One thing I found interesting and sort of on this note, on the What We Know page, was that John Morke noted that the list of reasons Tiresias gave for Siakal seeking an Exaltation were awesome... for a Black Market Exaltation, but illegal as shit for the official Exigency. And at the same time when Tiresias asked further about this, part of the comment was "It has more to do with virtuosity than pragmatism.".

                                                        So the idea that Exaltations of Exigency are necessarily because the god has this exactly calculating scheme to benefit themselves and where the Exigent has this exact role in that... Maybe not that much.

                                                        The gods of Heaven are the divine and numinous, beyond human, breathtaking and baffling and noble, yet incessantly brawling, competitive and greedy gods of pagan myth (or at least as evoked by sword and sorcery). For all that, there is a divine plan and order to Heaven and a dignity to the offices of state. Yes, the gods are calculating, and the Sidereals are cunning, and even the Unconquered Sun is canny to realpolitik, but Heaven ostensibly has a duty to this mortal world, and the Exigency is a gift from the sun to the gods to make a better world (I suspect). It is not the equivalent of guns and money on the public purse to put in the hands of any mercenary to further the goals and schemes of a petty god. Heaven is public spirited, even through it's corruption, even if it's spirits are not public spirited. There is still some belief for the gods, or I'd like to think there is in 3e, in the dignity of a Celestial Order that stands for something.

                                                        Old Ahlat is cunning, but not depthlessly so, and when he moves to seek the Exigence, the Sun can know if it is because he looks on his lands, and wider than that to the world and sees they cry out for a hero, or if he is merely looking for a mighty thug to prop up his power.
