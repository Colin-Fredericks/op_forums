    Exigent Q

    04-10-2014, 03:22 AM
    As I understand them, Exigents will allow us to customize Exalted to our liking. My question is, will there still be certain rules of Exaltation that even they must obey, or is the sky the limit?

    More specifically, one of the "unbreakable" rules of Exaltation is that it is permanent, and the Essence can only be freed from its host by true death. But can I design an Exigent whose method of passing on the Exaltation involves personally finding a suitable replacement, making physical contact and then passing on the Exaltation to him, returning the predecessor to normal Humanity to live out his days? Or is this an unworkable idea? Similarly, Exaltations are as indestructible as anything in the setting can be. Can there be an Exigent Exaltation that is destructible, or even short-lived?

    Thanks for any answers!

              In the old forum, I was speculating about exactly that sort of thing at length. Inherited Exaltations (like, the current host selects his replacement before death), Sexually-Transmitted Exaltations (imagine if getting busy with a Dragon-Blooded gave the partner the chance to Exalt rather than just offspring of their union - or weirder, if the Exaltation could be bounced off to someone else like a game of Sexy Hot Potato), Exaltations that can be won or lost in gambling matches, Exaltations that you can take from a guy by killing him (and maybe you can take more than one, Highlander style), Exaltations that are tied to the possession of an artifact (think Keyblades, which come with built-in weapon summoning charms and really can't be permanently stolen unless someone gives them away), all sorts of crazy stuff.

              The idea is that the original big four Exalted had their transmission methods made tamper proof, but Exigents are made to fit specific needs that may not match those of the soldiers in the Primordial War. Anyway, Holden popped in to confirm that that's all do-able, but that the most common type by far is the one-and-done, where the Exaltation has enough juice to empower one guy, then dissipates when he dies instead of passing on at all. Exaltations that fizzle out before the host actually passes away also seem within reason.

                  Well, okay, the first caveat is: technically, you can do whatever you want. When you want to play something off the beaten path there's always a certain amount of "convince your ST/play group" involved, and that's especially true when you're playing a completely homebrewed Exalt, so in principle even if the books say you can't do it that will have less influence than if they say the same thing about a prefab splat like Solars.

                  But since that's not really what you're asking: my feeling is that they would not allow this, because it sets a bad precedent and inevitably spins off a line of discussion about how it could be abused, and what "free will" means, and so forth. The fact that the Exaltation and Exalted are inseparable during life is a pretty big deal and I think they would recognize that opening the door even a crack has the potential to divert the game to a bad place. The Exaltation is not meant to be thought of as an external thing once it has fused with a person.

                  If it were me, certainly, I would say it's a no-go: you could pass on your Exaltation by choice, or have a short-lived Exaltation, but you die in the process. A sort of compromise would be to say that you die, but you live on as a ghost of your previous Essence rating, and get some free ghost gimmicks equal to a fraction of the XP you had spent on Charms. I have been brainstorming some Exigents whose Exaltations have external, physical components, but I think it's important to keep them subject to this limitation, i.e. they can't be destroyed without killing you and you aren't suddenly unExalted if someone runs off with them.
