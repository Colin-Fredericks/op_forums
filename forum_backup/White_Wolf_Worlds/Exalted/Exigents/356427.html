    Unwanted Exigent Exaltations - OR - No Thanks, God of Buffalo Dung

    01-01-2015, 04:12 AM
    It's nice to be recognized, and always a welcome feeling when you realize someone sees some special potential in you, potential you might not even be aware that you possess. Becoming one of the Chosen, no matter the god, should be considered a great honor... right?

    Well, with the upcoming existence of Exigents, I can safely say that there are some divine empowerments I would definitely be willing to turn down.

    Personally, I would look first to the Discworld, specifically Anoia, Goddess of Things That Get Stuck in Drawers. It isn't that I don't sometimes want to get things out of drawers easier, it's just that I'm not sure I want to spend the next 3000 years traveling Creation and solving storage catastrophes with my own unique drawer-centric Charmset.

    What about you, kind reader? What god/goddess's offer of UNLIMITED POWER* would you probably reply to with a polite "Uh, no thanks, I'm good."

    *warning-power is not actually unlimited, and may actually in many cases be very limited

                                          Originally posted by Jutlander View Post
                                          Isn't it a major thematic point separating Exalted from World of Darkness that as a Solar you know upon drawing the Second Breath that you are, in fact, not a monster even if your childhood religion has told you so?
                                          I don't think so. That removes a lot of potential internal narrative tension. And self-deprograming religious conceits should not be quite that easy, or quite that much like flipping a switch.

                                          Like, I don't think it's explicit when you Exalt. And I think being a Solar probably feels pretty damn good and different from just being a normal person, and that should give the Immaculate religious pause because you certainly probably feel (and can perform feats) above your station, comparable to the legends of the Dragon-Blooded.

                                          But Solar Exaltation doesn't wipe away your personality, your cultural mores, or your pre-existing religious beliefs. If anything the fact that you still feel in control of your body and don't have irresistible urges to torture and dominate might make you think the religion exaggerated. But IMO it should take time for you to consider and say "well, I don't feel like a demon is controlling me, I'm not just doing awful things, I have no desire to hurt people...maybe the Immaculate Order is just wrong about that stuff." But even that is a jump away from "they're making it all up, the lying liars!"

                                          Or you worry that the process of conversion is a slow one, you actively fight against evil thoughts and urges that your new power would make easy, when it's really your own urges and desires telling you that you have power and to exert it now.

                                          This stuff should internally work its way out gradually. And absent with being presented with hard evidence of the Sidereal creation of the Immaculates I think one would be more likely to assume that the Immaculates are misguided rather than deliberately preaching a lie.

                                              Originally posted by Jutlander View Post
                                              Isn't it a major thematic point separating Exalted from World of Darkness that as a Solar you know upon drawing the Second Breath that you are, in fact, not a monster even if your childhood religion has told you so?

                                              This would certainly make it a lot less common for Solars to kill themselves when their parents ask them if they have "tried not being Anathema".
                                              At least back in 1st edition, it was understood that some people who had just Exalted, at least in areas where the Immaculate Philosophy was powerful, would turn themselves in at the local temple to get killed, or indeed, just commit suicide before they lost themselves and turned into more of a monster.

                                              I remember Grawboski saying that one of the goals was to move Exalted away from the "personal horror" vibe common in many other White Wolf products, and that was one of the reasons why it was assumed that your character hadn't just Exalted a couple of days ago, but rather had been Exalted for several months at least - long enough to come to terms with his existence and not see himself as some kind of monster or regret what he had become.

                                              So when you're playing your character you're not really supposed to be having any sort of existential crisis about being a monster (unlike many other White Wolf games), but that's because your character already had it off-screen.

                                              This idea wasn't really brought up much in 2nd edition (indeed I don't know if it ever was), and I have no idea how it will work in 3rd edition.

                                              I always found it kind of interesting though.

                                                          Playing odd divine domains for farce never really interested me, but the idea of an unwanted Exigence does in other ways.

                                                          For example, Rabszolga, God of Slavery, is an active and powerful Celestial god who turns his great power to the twin tasks of keeping slavery strong in the world and finding a way to subjugate himself into being a proper slave - and at the first one, he is wildly successful. Obviously, the Guild has a lot of work with him, so it's not hard to picture him somehow getting a black market exaltation... or, for drama points, his absolute adherence to order and submission causing him to render the Unconquered Sun some great favor ("no, Lord, it was merely my place to be ripped in half by Octavian to shield those mice...") - and in either case, I'm reasonably sure that whomever he chose to bestow the Exigence upon would not be pleased, since a slave so endowed would be using the literal power of oppression, whilst anyone else would be getting power... but also a powerful mentor who believes they should strip all comforts from their lives and live as a tortured ascetic. And that's all before you even get to the logistics of using a Charm like, say, By Discipline Inured, which grants you resistance to social actions, but can only be activated by getting injured, whether by yourself or another.

                                                          Alternately, I really like the idea of some warrior god of a tribe which is badly losing a genocidal struggle with its neighbors getting the Exigence... not for one of his own people, but for one of the enemy, both to ensure some legacy survives and because the customs she has upheld for centuries dictate that grand gifts must be given to the victor. She dies in the process of passing on this Exigence, leaving the "lucky" recipient, most likely the greatest hero of the victorious side, abruptly a monster tainted by strange gods and outcast from their own tribe. Awkward.

                                                          That said, Chinese Mythology has left me with a powerful desire to explore the workings of gods who have "un-sexy" jobs, such as a divine stablemaster... who, you know, tends to Lesser Dragons. So maybe I shall have a few Exigents along those lines, too.
