                            Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
                            Well, that's simply not what stunting does. Stunting doesn't bypass the inherent thematic limitations of a given Charmset. It's "merely" (and I use that word regretfully) a really cool description of an action that, by virtue of its coolness, affords the acting character with a boost to their dice pool.
                            Originally posted by Kazorh View Post
                            Weren't stunts more reality-warping back in 1e? I think that's something I heard somewhere, and cappadocius mentioned he only has 1e.
                            I don't know about 1e, but 2e's stunts could do more than Zelbinnean recalls. While not too much, and certainly less than cappadocius described, I recall them giving some limited narrative license in setting the scene.* I also believe an appropriate stunt with an attack that normally does Bashing damage could bump it up to Lethal, if desired.** It's been ages since I skimmed the Stunt rules, but I get the sense that bending the basic rules to do logical and cool things that one didn't necessarily want endlessly reproducible was supposed to fall under their auspices.

                            I could always just be wildly off-base, of course. ^_^o

                            * e.g. if it's not illogical for there to be some crates or a chandelier in the area, a character's stunt could treat them as though they were present, and present they would be.
                            ** I'm not sure I remember seeing this, to be honest. I have seen a few mentions of Charms that let characters treat their unarmed attacks as Lethal instead of Bashing without the use of a stunt. So it's possible that was... not an air-breathing mermaid problem, exactly, but in that vein.
