    You know, it's funny, I've always been a firm proponent of the idea that being Exalted doesn't mean you should be so above petty things like the desire for money.

    Yet, having read Debt and seen all of the interesting arguments and facts it presents about the context in which money may have or has been developed and sought, and all of the implications this has into social organisation and personal motivations and international relations and war, and it makes me think...

    Why not? Why wouldn't the Exalted have been above it all? Not because they're transcendent and enlightened but because, for their power, how easily their needs can be met.

    I mean, one says "oh, we shouldn't need the Great Curse to bring down the Old Realm, because we can already see that it has all of these basic institutional weaknesses", and I'm still now wondering why. It's not as if the Exalted were just dropped in on a scenario in which all of these systems and weaknesses had already gotten off the ground and so you had people perpetuating them thoughtlessly because it was all they had ever known. Within this setting, human civilization began under the scenario of the Exalted being around, which is a pretty big leg up on anything it had in real life. Maybe the Realm was very dissimilar to our contemporary world, but only perhaps mildly dissimilar to various other societies throughout history, and I'm still left asking why it should ever have been in any way similar to us at all. The conditions in which human civilization originated are not incidental, and yet within this setting they're completely turned on their head.

    I think of some of Grabowski's statements to the effect of "when you have the (Solar) Exalted, you don't really need a society based on harnessing and coordinating the labour of massive numbers of people" and I ask "then why would the concept of empire have ever been a thing?" I'm not doubting the idea that Grabowski's statement is accurate in the setting, but there are other hints and motifs of the First Age that don't seem as though they would have logically emerged from such conceits (which, as I'll get to, I think is kind of by design).

    One could make the argument of "oh, well, you'll never be devoid of people who are just greedy and selfish and violent (or even need to relentlessly innovate)", but... I don't buy it. The anthropological and historical and psychological evidence continues to mount that people who have their needs met and are sufficiently divorced from social conditioning to think that their needs are lesser than the relentless pursuit of more tend to be, as I think the scientific terminology goes, "pretty chill". At the very least, there's a lot that is dependent on a context that stretches back through a loooooong lineage of scarcity and struggle and dependence.

    But the Exalted don't seem to have ever been.

    Another thing that started growing on my mind between reading that book and watching Age of Ultron was the idea of the villains of the movieverse Iron Man. How in each case they're people who are part of the abundantly wealthy and secure military-industrial complex, who already each have more money than God, and yet their plots in each film are focused on grandiose schemes in pursuit of even more money, which they will then spend on... what? I'm not too critical of it within the context of its own narrative (beyond how it might kind of validate Ultron's critique of "want to save the world, but don't want it to change"), but imagine if it was transplanted into a setting that was more concerned with validating the logical background and context of certain motivations.

    If you had the Mandarin in Exalted, why would he want to escalate the War on Terror to create even greater purchase orders for weaponry?

    Imagining the origins of human civilization with the presence of people like the Exalted, from whence would ever have come the motivation to pursue things that, in real life, come from a long long long history of want and limit? If we assume (drawing on all available evidence) that such social developments and personal motivations are not paradigmatic to humans and inevitable.

    From... the Neverborn?




    (This is not to say that, if there had never been a Great Curse, the Exalted would have been incapable of making bad, short-sighted decisions, just... the motivation and the consequences and the response might have been in so many ways different. I mean, different beyond my capacity to imagine, because it's trying to model a human personality almost completely devoid of everything I can reference, but still... mental and physical limitations might still be a thing, and yet I continue to find it hard to imagine that a human race with the advantage of the Exalted from the very beginning would be in so many ways better if there wasn't something tugging at them to make mistakes approximating our conditions of doing the best we can)

    I have approximate knowledge of many things.
