        Shining Gear, god of Combat Automata, had fallen on hard times in the Second Age. Though he held a little hope that someone might rediscover the Bronze Legionnaire factory-cathedrals of Denandsor or the hidden thinking godstrider in the Far South, he knew that there was little possibility of recovery.

        Unlike many Celestial Gods who see their post as a stepping stone to greater power, Shining Gear was an ancient being created by Autochthon specifically to fill his role, and he held a deep, abiding love for his work. No longer able to bear the pain of his loss, he petitioned the Most High for an Exaltation, a champion with which the world might once again be reminded of the wonders he held dear. After much deliberation, he selected Maka, a young mechanist and engineer working on guild projects in the small Northern hub city of Hornhearth. He did not survive her ascension.

        Shining Gear has chosen both well and poorly. Maka immediately started correcting what she saw as decades of injustice against her genius. Using her Charms, she constructs increasingly more deadly fighting machines and directs them to rampage upon the city, destroying anyone who she can recall slighting her. So far, the coordinated efforts of the guard and a sworn brotherhood of Outcaste mercenaries have been able to thwart her, but they are reaching their limits, while the elusive Maka seems to have none. On the other hand, Hornhearth is certainly reminded of the purview of Shining Gear, and Guild-backed fanes fill his now-neglected alms cup with ambrosia and prayers for intervention.

            Originally posted by NuSoardGraphite View Post

            People will try. As the ST, all I have to do is say "no".
            As the GM, your power to change the game is only limited by your imagination and your group's willingness to put up with the changes you make.

            This has no bearing on game design, though. The author of the game can suggest how things should work, but can't effectively forbid anything. You can bet there are people somewhere playing gods, demons, Primordials and behemoths in Exalted, as well as people running around with resurrection and time travel magic, even though these things are either not officially supported or outright banned by the metaphysics of the setting.

            Presenting things as strictly NPC or, even worse, strictly antagonist territory has no place in the design paradigm of Exalted, because making something NPC only implies it cannot be reasoned or sympathized with, and that's something Exalted deliberately avoids. Worse, presenting something as just antagonists implies there is objective right and wrong in the setting, and that's antithetical to the kind of stories Exalted wants to tell.

                                    I think the objection is about designing certain splats as NPC-only in the books. People are free to do whatever at their tables, but if Infernals or sidereals or whatever were designed as NPC-only, everyone is automatically "bowed-out" of games involving playing as those, with no option to "bowing-in" at a game that has them unless massive house-ruling is done to allow it.

                                    In other words, restricting certain types of characters to NPC/antagonist status at your table is fine. But writing those splats that way in the books is counterproductive to the intents of the game line, which is clearly intended to tell a broader range of stories than the particular brand of heroism you want in your games. Still, I'm talking mostly about mortals, Exalt types and other beings more or less or that scale (ghosts, Fair Folk, et cetera). Rules for playing as a "Kaiju Behemoth* circle" or rules for playing as a bunch of mundane animals are likely outside the intended scope of the game as well.


                                    *Especially since Behemoths is a catch-all term for a bunch of unrelated things, it doesn't really make sense to have "Behemoth character" rules

                                        I don't like the "Exaltation Symbiote" idea for a few reasons - for one, the symbiotes in the Marvel universe are capable of finding other hosts without killing the original bearer. In fact, Venom is Venom because the symbiote separated from Peter Parker. Also, the symbiotes had ways of being neutralized temporarily (or even eradicated permanently!) in ways that rendered the host pretty much an ordinary schlub. Additionally, we already have a demon that acts as an amorphous mass that can bond with a host and protect said host from danger.

                                        Also, if something can empower a person who's already Exalted and can depart from said host prematurely, that flies in the face of what little we know about Exaltation. While I'm in favor of shaking up some assumptions set in the game, the notion of an Exaltation that can be taken away, well, let's just say I'm against it.
