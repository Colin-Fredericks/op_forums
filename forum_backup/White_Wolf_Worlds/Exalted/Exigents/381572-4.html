    Originally posted by Zelbinnean View Post
    I question that Charm's existence and design space suitability, period.
    It serves an important purpose for Solar Lore.

    In general, one of the thematics of Lore-focused heroes is the "enlightened master" -- someone who guides others on the path to enlightenment. Generally such characters grant their disciples supernatural abilities, too, since in-genre enlightenment is often expressed that way.

    This overlaps a bit with Sidereals, but Sidereals can handle it with MA, since they have that holistically throughout their charmset. "Enlightened master" Solars have to be able to represent a sort of enlightenment distinct from MA, though, which they can still teach and bestow to others. Power-Awarding Prana is one way to represent this. Some Solars need to be able to represent the Solar Exaltation, thematically, as a form of transcendent enlightenment, and as part of that they need some ability to bestow fragments of that enlightenment to their disciples.

    (Now, whether it's mechanical implementation works is another issue, but I feel it has generally been underpowered rather than overpowered.)

                Originally posted by Lioness View Post
                All I know is that I'm not really keen on an Exalted concept whose only power is to enhance others,
                I don't know what limitations the new Exalts introduced in 3rd edition might have, but being forced into a support role like that seems overly narrow and somewhat contrary to my expectations of what it means to be Exalted.

                I remember back in 1st edition we had a conversation about "support Exalted" and Grabowski chimed in to say that, while you might see buff-type abilities here or there, you wouldn't ever see any type of "buffer build" for the Exalted. Each Exalt was meant to be a hero and champion on his own terms and should have the capability to handle a situation without requiring backup - basically, you wouldn't have an Exalt who just stands there and buffs other people as his combat option. He said that would go against the design of the sort of heroes that the Exalted were intended to emulate.

                I think, while a hypothetical Chosen of Lytek may have the ability to buff other people, he definitely shouldn't be restricted to this. If it's an option in their toolbox, okay, but they should probably be totally capable of tackling situations on their own, rather than simply be relegated to a "buff-bot" for other Exalted.

                    I should also point out Fabian Cortez from Marvel Comics.

                    His only power is to enhance other mutant powers --- so the way he goes toe-to-toe against others is the same way some (like Cyclops and Beast - mutants with rather narrow powers) do. A combination of Martial Arts, Artifacts and Mutations. Not all Exalts have an amazing kit for every situation (and this can be part of their fun - whether you play mechanically weaker Exalts, like Dragon-Blooded, who excel at working together ; or mechanically shallow Exalts, like Infernals, whose kits are either amazing at a niche or dissolve outside their niche).

                    Also, he could enhance others against their will.

                    For example, Bishop is a mutant who can absorb energy and then redirect it out as kinetic blasts. In became a sort of "Mutant Policeman" because whenever some random mutant had some weird blast to shoot at him, he'd simply absorb it and throw something back at them (or if the mutant was simply strong & threw him - Bishop could absorb some of the Kinetic energy, thereby surviving the impact and then surprising the brute). Fabian's first encounter with Bishop was to magnify this absorption -- so Bishop began absorbing any and all ambient energy he came in touch with (but Fabian did nothing to enhance his output). He was incapacitated via burn out.

                    So.

                    Dragon-Blooded are a threat? Let me enhance their Anima Banner -- and maybe the environment around them crumbles (including the floor) as a Fire Aspect buries himself beneath fresh slag (and this wouldn't be out-of-theme --- in 1st Edition, a Twilight used a sort of incense-drug against a Terrestrial Fire-Aspect, who upon flaring burnt out a massive area around herself while in the sewers, causing her to get incapacitated by bricks).

                    A Full Moon uses some charm to leap at me and try guttng me? Maybe I can enhance the charm further and have him leap over and away from me.

                    Or something.

                    Point is, playing a support character never prohibits you from being your own hero --- it's more like using a single Infernal kit, you learn to work around your limitations (and it can in fact be more fun, because your ST knows how to challenge you, since it's not like you have an easy "spend 4 motes, side-step this plot hook challenge".

                    Dunno.

                    My Devil-Tiger kit was as close as I could come and I remember enjoying it, because it excelled at tackling situations in a distinctly personal way as opposed to a Solar style generic "I win" button for any situation given enough xp.

                                Originally posted by RedRiotousRam View Post
                                His only power is to enhance other mutant powers --- so the way he goes toe-to-toe against others is the same way some (like Cyclops and Beast - mutants with rather narrow powers) do. A combination of Martial Arts, Artifacts and Mutations. Not all Exalts have an amazing kit for every situation (and this can be part of their fun - whether you play mechanically weaker Exalts, like Dragon-Blooded, who excel at working together ; or mechanically shallow Exalts, like Infernals, whose kits are either amazing at a niche or dissolve outside their niche).
                                There's a difference between having a powerful theme, having a niche and being restricted to only your niche.

                                Imagine if Dragon Blooded charms could only be used as Teamwork charms, or if Lunars were only capable of shapeshifting into animals and incapable of doing anything else.

                                Many of the Exalted have cool niches, but they aren't restricted to only being able to use their niche. They are, generally speaking, capable of a very broad range of powers outside of their niche, though not quite as powerful as a "generalist" like a Solar Exalt.

                                While a hypothetical Chosen of Lytek could easily have a niche of some sort, he probably should also have a fair amount of powers that can be used outside of it as well. Just as Beast Boy makes a good inspiration as a Lunar, Lunars are capable of doing quite a bit more than he is. Likewise, while someone like Fabian Cortez could be a cool inspiration for a Chosen of Lytek, it would only make sense for Lytek's Exigent to be more broadly capable than he is.

                                Exalted aren't meant to be one-trick ponies. They're supposed to have a fairly broad array of abilities, and while some Exalted might be better than certain things than others, you're probably not going to see any Exalted who are capable of doing only one thing and one thing only.

                                                            Originally posted by Mizu View Post
                                                            I think a lot of people in this topic are forgetting that exigents can basically be anything, they aren't beholden to any rules such as 'they must have a certain amount of breadth in what their powers allow them to do'. If someone wants to make an exigent whose natural charm set revolves entirely around empowering others (and is also incapable of learning supernatural martial arts, sorcery, or using evocations to grant themselves personal power because you are really dedicated to just playing support) then you can totally do that. It will probably get boring pretty damn quick, but you can totally do it.
                                                            If you are Exalted by the goddess of cooking, you won't only be able to cook. Yes, you'll be great at cooking, and all of your charms will involve cooking to one degree or another. But you won't be stuck in the kitchen for the entire game. Instead, all of your charms will invoke the concept of cooking. So when you punch someone in the face, how you do it will be inspired by the theme of cooking. When you haggle with a merchant, again, it will draw on the concept of cooking, kitchens, etc.

                                                            You'll have charms that literally invoke the cooking theme (perhaps you'll have a charm that allows you to literally flambe someone). Other charms may be more of a metaphorical interpretation of cooking (maybe a Stealth charm which makes it so that people who watch you don't see you doing anything unusual, even if you are, and is based around the idea of "a watched pot doesn't boil.")

                                                            If you're the Exigent of the goddess of cooking, you won't be limited to having a charmset full of charms that only allows you to Craft: Water really, really well. That's not really how Exigents are intended to function.

                                                            Of course, Exigents will largely be houseruled and homebrewed, and if you want one trick Exigents who are only capable of doing a single thing, nobody is going to send the canon ninjas to your house. But I don't think Exigents, by design, are the, "Design them to do anything and everything you want," Exalted.
