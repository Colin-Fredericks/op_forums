    I think so long as the Exigent is designed to be reasonable, fit the thematic of the God it came from, and creates an interesting challenge, then it wouldn't really be that big of a deal would it? In this case what would the GM have replaced the Exigent with it didn't exist? Probably an Outcaste Dragonblooded, right? That could get boring if you have nothing but Outcaste DBs as far as you can see, at least the Exigent solution leads you to having to differentiate them- if not in mechanics, then at least in appearance-.

    I doubt many GMs will just willy-nilly throw Exigents whenever the party needs competition, because they take a lot of work to make one, and if you're using the same generic Exigent over and over and just slapping a different template on it, then really it's no different from someone just using a DB in that way.

            Originally posted by SpruceStripedGoose View Post
            In regards to what concerns me more. I do see situation where exigents as PC characters could cover a purview that is too narratively empowering...but really Solars already do that.

            My bigger concern is the other view you suggested as a PC facing the GMs Exigents. Now the Exigent of fighting on cliffs with spears is hyperbole, but I do think there is the potential of a easy and boring route for a GM to throw a war god exigent every time he needs a military threat, a trade god exigent ever time he needs to oppose you in a negotiation, or a exigent of a god of artifice to explain away first age shenanigans.
            First, remember that all Exalted can solve any problem. You aren't going to come across a problem that can't be "solved" by a Solar, or a Lunar or anyone else. If the ST essentially decides that the "problem" for his campaign is defeating the Mask of Winters and freeing Thorns, any Exalted type that the players are playing will be able to succeed. The difference will be in the process of how they succeed. Whether one player is playing a Solar or a Sidereal or an Exigent of Masque of Repose isn't going to change their ability to "solve" the problem, just how they go about doing so.

            The same thing is true of Exalted in the ST's hands. If the ST wants to have a trading organization led by an Exalt as a challenge to the players, he could have an Exigent of the goddess of money. Or he could have the leader be a Solar, Abyssal, Lunar or any other Exalt. Having Exigents just gives the ST one more "tool in the toolbox" to pull out. But the thing worth remembering is that 1) creating a new Exigent is a lot of work, and 2) the Exigent isn't going to be any more powerful than a Solar Exalt. So if the ST just wanted the "strongest Exalt" he can get, then he could have just stuck with the Solar to begin with.

            So I don't think there's much fear of Exigents unbalancing the game, or being used too often by players or STs. The biggest problem with them that I can foresee is players and ST's poorly balancing custom Charms and breaking the game wide open. Of course, I've seen the same thing happen with players making Solar Charms (and even with some of the published authors making Charms that ruined the game). Still though, Exigents are a full splat that encourages that sort of creativity and I suspect that you'll end up seeing a lot of complaints like, "Fuck Exigents! My player made this bullshit Charm, and I didn't have enough system mastery to recognize the flaws, agreed to let him play it and wrecked the game."

                        I will defer the OP to the first rule of Exalted: The storyteller is free to change anything they don't like. Just because it was published in the Core Rulebook doesn't mean that you have to use it. If the Storyteller doesn't like it, they don't have to use it or even allow it. I know some players might not like it if you made such a change but if you feel uncomfortable using Exigents in your game then don't use them. It's just another option. Same with God-Blooded characters, you don't have to use them. I've only ever used God-Blooded characters as NPCs in my own games, none of my players ever expressed any interest in playing such a character. So far I've used Exigents as NPC builds to create more interesting game situations. You don't have to put them on the table as player options. It all depends on what you want for your series.
