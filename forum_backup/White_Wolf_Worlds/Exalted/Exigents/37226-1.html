                        I get the impression that you're usually only given one Exalt even if you're powerful, though there are surely exceptions and some Exigent Exaltations reincarnate like Celestial Exaltations.

                        While I do have one Exigent idea based on a canon god (Zhuzhiao, if you're curious), I think that looking at major canon gods and then thinking about what kind of Exigents they might have is a fairly blunt and limited approach to what we've been given here. Part of what differentiates Exigents from God-Blooded is that they're defined less by their relationship to their patron, so while it can be interesting to look at major gods who could have cool exigents, I think it's generally more productive to think about what kind of exigents might be cool in themselves, and then reverse-engineer the god relationship. It's such a rich palette that it seems a shame to focus on the obvious NPCs.

                        The way they handled Exigents was really smart, because it finessed them past two of the biggest pitfalls with the basic idea: the power level required to create Exigents, and the mystique associated with Exaltation. The Exigent spotlighted in the core book, who looks likely to be the lead sig, was created by a miscellaneous local harvest god. You can have an Exigent based on a genius loci, a rank-and-file disease spirit, a Storm Mother, whatever, and in the vast gulf between peons like that and titanic figures like Lytek and Nara-O you have middle-management gods with limited, esoteric domains like "Eastern settlements reclaimed by nature" and "missives penned in the grip of passion". Moreover, the comments we've heard suggest that the line between the god and the Exalt can be somewhat loose -- Janest was empowered by a farm god, but she can cut through armies like wheat, because that's awesome. It's not so much that a harvest God should confer that power as that it's a cool power that can be slotted comfortably into her thematic. I think that we're likely to see that good exigents do not necessarily come from looking at a domain and then extrapolating powers in a direct, straightforward way.

                        Beyond just looking at gods (named or otherwise), there are quire a few ways to brainstorm Exigents. You can start with a system gimmick you'd like to build a character type around and work backwards from there, look at parts of the setting that seem like a rich concept space, come up with something specifically for the plot or metaplot of the region you're playing in, think about a broad character style that might be fun to play, whatever. There's a ton of potential.



                        A postscript: it makes me cringe a bit every time I hear someone talk about an Exigent of Lytek, because it seems like one of those ideas that's bad and nearly impossible to do well, but has an inexorable appeal that will seduce dozens of people into trying it. I don't want to pick on horvagab specifically here, because I think all of us had the idea independently, and I'm sure that 10 years from now there will have been dozens of different takes on the concept. It seems like kind of a weird thing for Lytek to even do, and if you approach the situation from "what makes the Chosen of Exaltation cool?" or "Why would I want to play the Chosen of Exaltation?" there isn't a lot there relative to most concepts. The appeal seems to largely be that Lytek is a major NPC and there's a gee-whiz so-meta quality to it, and while that can be a valid starting point -- the quality of the finished product is mostly independent of what inspired you to start -- it's hard to imagine a more uphill battle to make something good.

                                                        The first Exigent I thought of is probably also up there on the list of things everyone considers (to the extent that it's been alluded to by Holden at least once): a Chosen of Han-Tha. That could get nasty fast.

                                                        When I read back through the West book recently, it occurred to me that Tya is a pretty clear example of an Exigent, being Chosen by Storm Mothers (possibly as a group effort of several of them, so that none of them has to die). Of course, as soon as I thought that, then I wanted her not to be, so that a younger, more PC-able Tya could be Exigented instead.

                                                        And, of course, speaking of major setting powers, the Great Forks Trio and the Syndics both have uses for powerful servants... and provide a neat contrast. If someone like Shield of a Different Day makes an Exigent, she wants everyone to know it, both due to vainglory and propaganda. But if the Syndics had Chosen, they'd keep them in the shadows, since those people would, by nature, have abilities which could reveal the identities of their patrons. Cool stuff.

                                                        Rounding out the list of obvious concepts, if Exigents do reincarnate while keeping the same God's traits (which I find probable, but uncertain), I imagine that many, many people will be the deeply confused Chosen of Swan Dragon, trying to find the source of their powers, and figure out why this crazy old man keeps on showing up. Which, to be honest, is a pretty great subversion of the "wise old mentor in disguise" arc, since the old man really doesn't know much of anything, and the young hero has to nurture him back to wisdom. And, yes, I maintain this will be popular whether or not it winds up making any sense for Lesser Elemental Dragons to have Exigents. Who doesn't love Swan Dragon?

                                                        But for myself, my favorite Exigent concept I've had so far is a former (mortal, obviously), Immaculate Monk, who was part of a group putting the boots to some uppity God. However, that God was really demanding worship so they would be strong enough to hold off the encroaching Fair Folk/undead/demon cult, and to that end, had managed to gets its hands on an Exigency. Facing defeat, it used the Exigency (dying in the process, like weaker gods do) not on someone it would have chosen, but on one of the the only people present - the monks beating down its spirit servants - and hoped for the best.

                                                        The details there are obviously intentionally vague, for maximum use flexibility, though I do like to think the God is of a river, or a mountain, or or something - namely, something with fairly broad associations, but which does not suggest immediate combat mastery*. This, overall, seems like it hits a lot of good hooks to me, and moves me much more than the "mortal Immaculate who, by sheer random chance, became a Solar" thing which crops up everywhere. It has more of a connection between the Immaculate and Anathema sides of things, in my mind.

                                                        * Typing this out, I think really it should be a God of Birds, or of Scholars, or similar. To justify superhaman observation/learning Charms which in turn allow for developing Enlightened Martial Arts based on the Mortal-version katas and having watched Exalted Monks for years, naturally.
