        Actually, I have feelings on that point.

        If Han-Tha is only a small part of your campaign, then by all means. The important thing about Filial Wisdom is that he serves as the heavy to make displacing a mere mid-power God a serious challenge to a Circle (well, okay, him and the fact that you're constantly being attacked by the assorted other nasty factions of Rathess).

        But if Han-Tha is a major, ongoing foe, giving him multiple different enforcers can only help, and I actually really like the duality of having one Solar who's insane and dangerous, but still very much of two minds on his master's agenda (and virtually everything else - see also, insane), and one handpicked champion who is someone Han-Tha got to select for their compatibility with this plan. Variety is the spice, and all that. Stir in shortsighted Dragon-Blooded mercenaries, messed-up Dragon Kings who have recovered a warped version of their ancient powers without the attendant wisdom, amenable Raksha nobles, etc. to taste.

        And, of course, if your plot is about Sidereals or someone rushing to stop Han-Tha from getting/using one or more Exigencies, it'd be a hilariously nasty surprise to let them arrive in time... only to learn that Han-Tha has a crazy dangerous Exalt already in place for security, and make them fight Filial Wisdom while still racing against time.

        The reason that Exigents are, by far, my favorite new Exalt type based on (the very little) information we currently have is the way they add more weight and options to themes the setting already had. Things that are already there don't necessarily ever have to change, go away, or be overshadowed, they just gain more interesting context.

        "For me, there's no fundamental conflict between really loving something and also seeing it as very profoundly flawed." -- Jay Eddidin

                Originally posted by Lioness View Post
                I'm not sure how that's relevant to what Lytek actually does or how an Exaltation that helps you achieve your own potential is meaningfully different from a Solar Exaltation.
                Just as much as any Exaltation is different from each other.
                Solars are the embodiment of perfection, Lunars with impossibility, Sidereals with Fate/Samsara/Esotericness.

                Lytek's dominion is that of Exaltation itself, which is to say the elevation of mortals to becoming Weapons of the Gods. What constraints and influence he has within that domain is a touchy subject that will never be resolved. As such, Exaltation, and thus Lytek's Exigent, represents the Embodiment of Potentiality. Each persons potential is unique to that individual and intrinsically so. Effectively Devil-Tigers without the Transhuman lens. While the difference between this and Solars is subtle, it is but one interpretation.

                        I know I mentioned Dutiful Nhan in a previous topic, the God of Forgotten Things and Dusty Corners who I wrote up for my game.

                                        IMO they offer different takes on the dinosaur idea.
                                        The creatures Arilak takes care of are implied to exist nowhere else, they shouldn't just be seen as megafauna in a world where they're a fact of life but as semi-mythical creature that shouldn't exist anymore.
