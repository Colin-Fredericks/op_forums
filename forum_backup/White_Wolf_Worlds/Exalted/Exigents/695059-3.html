            Some of the worry about Exigents makes the most sense at the "super-powered D&D" end of the Exalted spectrum. The more a group values the conflicts and structures of the setting (and I'm not saying that's the one right way to play) the less an Exigent can steal the role of an existing splat.

            The Dragon-Blooded are the scions of the world's greatest empire, and no exigent of an elemental god can touch that. Obviously there are games in which that doesn't really matter, though I don't think Ex3 should be written around the premise that some groups won't care. I mean, if the narrative of a Realm nobles or an ambitious Outcaste doesn't do anything for you, sure, don't play a DB. Even without exigents on the table, if the DBs' only value is "fire-punch dudes" then you're happier playing something else. In a mixed group of DBs and exigents, the exigent might have some more impressive elemental charms, but he won't have the same role in Realm politics or society or the military, he won't join the Immaculate Order, and while he may be an ally to a sworn brotherhood, he's still an outsider helping out with a thing that DBs do.

            The Sidereals are the Five-Score Fellowship, and no exigent of any god, no matter how prominently placed in Yu Shan, will ever change that to the Five-Score-and-One Fellowship. Or if you want some big-picture mechanical differences, there's SMA, sorcery, astrology, and resplendent destinies to think about too. And despite some significant flaws in 2e's Heaven, I think we'll have a new version that will be a lot more inviting to interact with as a Sidereal. An exigent of Wayang would have a completely different role than a Sidereal - the Chosen of the Maidens are focused on righting Creation, but if Wayang wants to exalt someone, I'd expect his focus to be on problems in Heaven, where I'm sure that exigent would be as popular as 2e's Sids.
