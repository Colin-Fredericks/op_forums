            hm...Exigence thoughts.

            I think Exigents have a single coherent theme that's not talked about a lot. Exigents are made to solve a problem, but outlive that solution. That's a space I don't think Exalted explores very much right now, in a 'whole splat' way.

            When I think of why I want the Exigent book, oddly, it's rarely for the Exigents. I kind of think of them as an Ex Miscellenia category, things that wouldn't make sense for an entire splat but still need a place in the world-a bloodline empowered to protect a lost land, the chosen of a river spirit to drive out the barbaric saxons, a supersolider project gone wrong, a champion of a gods lost flock, etc. And that's fine. What I'm more intreasted in is advice on how to model abillities and charms. How to homebrew better, I guess. Which does mean that Exigents are essentially homebrew, but homebrew with guiding principles, using developer advice, and so on.

            It'd also be nice, however, if your like me and you want to homebrew all sorts of weird stuff and need advice on how to mechanically model powers in general. Kinda like how I hope the Martial Arts book gives advice on how to design your own style, or the Sorcery book your own iniation, or Arms and your own evocations. Exigents are weird in that there, probably, isn't going to be a canon caste as universal as those (although I hope we get statlines for all the ones referenced in the core).

                                                        Although, considering that the power level of God-Blooded was inconsistently applied, and at the upper level never really matched with depictions of the overall setting, a bit of consolidation might be in order regardless of the existence of Exigents.

                                                        Mind, the question can still have answers.

                                                        ​For example, if Ten Sheaves had never gotten around to having a large number of children, it's not exactly an option for him when an army of Fair Folk is going to arrive tomorrow. The follow-up question of "why didn't he have a large number of children" could be answered by anything from him fearing that the presence of such a group would be highly disruptive to his domain, to simply not being very interested.

                                                        ​Besides, if you're a quite powerful god, I would say that the tone around Exigence (even if you happen to be strong enough for it to not destroy you) might imply that if you ever have to rely upon it, the other expressions of your power have already failed, whether they be your own divine strength, armies from nations dedicated to you, or children who have inherited some of that divine strength.

                                                        ​On top of that, there's the matter of how Exigents can have a decent amount of variation in their strength, regardless of the power of their patron. Assuming that Ten Sheaves does not deviate very much from the template of a harvest god that the core gave us, even in the best case scenario with Second Edition-style God-Blooded, the kinds of combat powers his children would have would not be very impressive against an army of Fair Folk. The Chosen of the Harvest seems to be endowed with something a bit more formidable.
