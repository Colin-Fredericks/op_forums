                            Originally posted by Zelbinnean
                            That's one of the great things about the game re-structuring for power curves that make regional goals challenging and easy to make the entire focus for games, as opposed to the old default being you start out working on a regional goal and graduate to something global.

                            For Exigents, I think that more is better to a degree. There are thousands upon thousand upon thousands of god in Yu-Shan, let alone in Creation. Only a couple hundred of them having Exigents would be a little screwy.

                            Also maximum lifespan is truly unimportant given how 99.9% of Exalts don't die of old age. And the power levels of Exigents vary wildly. So...your assertions are alternatively not correct or not relevant.
                            If there's hundreds of exigents, or worse a few thousand of them, then they are everywhere. Creation is big but not limitless. A properly designed region in 3e considers the influence of the following exalt types (in order of ubiquitousness): 1. dragon-blooded 2. Lunars 3. Sidereals. 4. Solars 5. Fair Folk 6. All others (with likely only one other splat occupying a given space at a significant influence level).

                            If there are 100-200 or so Exigents they fall into the all others group and STs can safely ignore them if they wish to. If there are 500+ Exigents then they leapfrog not only Solars but also Sidereals in terms of design space importance. At that point (and geometrically more so if there are thousands) every region has to be designed with the long-term influence of powerful and unique exalted in mind, which becomes very challenging because the capabilities of exigents are so varied. What the average DB, Lunar, or Sidereal represents can be modeled fairly effectively and there are numerous long-running examples. Each exigent, by contrast is adding a wild card to your setup.

                            And maximum lifespan is not irrelevant, because even if most exalts don't die of old age (though your percentage is way off, as dragon-blooded account for over 90% of all Exalted and roughly 1/3 die of old age, so a good ~30% of exalted do die of old age) lifespan of the 1% who do become powerful elders who drive setting design space matters. Dragon-blooded only live a few hundred years (exceptions exist) and therefore the deeds of say, any given Shogunate-era dragon blood or early Second Age dragon-blood who didn't leave lineage are erased by time. By contrast almost every single elder Lunar and Sidereal has a personal influence on the setting. Exigents will inevitably add another layer onto that, but if there are hundreds of them, it quickly becomes an overpowering layer - especially in the second age when their relative power is much greater.

                            Everything the Devs have presented has suggested a greater role for both the Realm and the Lunars. There have been no suggestions of Exigents as playing a massive collective role in Creation's history. The best way to reconcile this with their capabilities and with any sort of continuity with earlier editions of exalted is to have small numbers of them.

                                                    Originally posted by AnubisXy
                                                    Exigents aren't really a collective splat though. Individual Exigents have probably had an impact on parts of the setting. But each one is a solitary hero.* It's not like Exigents will necessarily be working together in any sort of collective like the Silver Pact or the Realm or the Sidereal Exalted. So it's only natural that they would have had less impact on the setting, as they're not working in concert the way that other Exalted do.
                                                    They don't need to work collectively - its the projection of their individual power (and the power of whatever dragon-blooded, god-blooded, spirit, and mortal allies they acquire) that matters.

                                                    Here, maybe it works better if I model the high exigent scenario.

                                                    Let's say that the Unconquered Sun allocates enough divine fire for 1,000 exigents (nice round number to work with). They start appearing shortly after the Shogunate begins and, gods being the ambitious scum that they are, reach saturation in a century or so.

                                                    Many die, whether in violent acts or due to a short lifespan. Let's say turnover accounts for about half, so that at any given time 500 exigents are at roughly the modest dragon-blooded power level. These aren't a big deal.

                                                    Many are relatively unambitious and settle into long term operations never hitting above essence 4, making them roughly equivalent to the second highest tier of dragon-blooded, let's say 400 more do this.

                                                    That leaves 100 who fully develop their power and live long lives. 100 brand new, each completely unique, elder exalts. And at a stroke I have just doubled the number of non-dragon-blooded elders in Creation. yes some get killed or wander off into the Wyld and stop having influence, but they get replaced by new ones. Each of these exalts is powerful enough, to shape a small society completely on their own. They are probably concentrated in the Threshold in the nominally independent territories between Lunar influence and Realm influence. Anything I put into those regions has to contend with exigent influence of considerable potency. There are now dozens of societies in Creation that have been heavily shaped by exigents for centuries, each one is skewed towards a unique goal.

                                                    Additionally, all the non-elder exigents still count. Every major city that doesn't have powers present to throw them out has at least one in residence, maybe two. There are probably a whole bunch of other effects I'm not even thinking of right now that'll hit me soon enough.

                                                    Could this be cool? Sure, it absolutely could be. does it fundamentally change Creation at every operational scale above the village level - yes, it pretty much does. Is that the goal of 3e? I don't think so, and frankly I hope not. It's a very unstable world, enough so that it vastly devalues the return of the Solars as an event, since with exigents all over the place the usurpation didn't really stabilize anything.
