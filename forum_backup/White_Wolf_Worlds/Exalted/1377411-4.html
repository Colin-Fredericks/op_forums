                Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
                Don't get me wrong, I like humanizing the barbarians as well. But I don't think it's inappropriate to protagonize a splat in the book pitched at playing that splat.
                I mostly agree with you.

                I like reading these discussions about the world-building of Exalted, but I also feel like if we go too deep into trying to draw direct conclusions about its premises and its biased presentation ("The Realm is like this so it's actually evil" for the simplest example), we are kind of missing the whole point that these are games with the function of allowing you to play certain types of characters who live in their own world. I think it's a mistake to try and make one unique "world-building" out of all the books of Exalted setting in order to draw conclusions about the setting for ludic purpose.

                I think the Solars in the Core Exalted 3e book are not the same Solars in the DB:WFHW books because these books are not here to tell the same story. I think it's fine to play a good imperialist DB that helps people get over their tribalistic way against the barbarian Lunar, just as much I think it's fine to play a heroic Lunar that rises oppressed people against the bad imperialist DB, even while these characters are mechanically the same and act the same inside the story played, but this specific description needs to be put in context of its specific corresponding playbook.

                It doesn't mean you can't tell a story of bad imperialism in DB or of bad rebellion in Lunar or make any subversion of any kind, but I think these subversions need to be made in context of one (or more) playbooks to make sense.

                Trying to tie every aspect of the world into a singular story that can be understood from a single point of view will lead to inevitable contradictions because :
                1) The authors are lying to us. Not on purpose, but the nature of a fictional story is that you can always be proven wrong about any topic if the writer wishes to
                2) We all have a different setting in mind because this is a fictional shared story
                3) This world doesn't work like ours because it doesn't have causality (on any aspect, physical, social, economic, etc ...). It looks it has causality, but it doesn't, because consequences are decided by our human minds, and by nothing else

                        Whilst both the Realm and the Solar Deliberative are/have been shown as bad, I've yet to see a game that wasn't about the PCs gaining control of their own, Nice Guy(tm) Empire.

                        The whole premise of Exalted as that you are playing a returning god-king. And aside from the occasional anarcho-syndicaist complaining that a fiery bint handing out shards of infinite power is no basis for a government, I've never seen a real challenge to the base assumption that the Realm is bad because it wants to kill you, but your empire is totally good because you are a Solar.

                        And that said... the British Empire ended Sati and numerous other barbaric customs. It brought technological advancement and (eventually) stamped out slavery. That doesn't change the fact that imperialism is wrong (both in and of itself, and due to the additional evils it perpertrates) but looked at honestly, empires aren't onions: they're always a mix of good and bad. And the Realm is no different.

                        If your choices are: an evil empire of elemental super soldiers will save your life but extract taxes; or an evil empire of fanged, mutant cannibals is going to rape you to death, eat your flesh and sow your skin into your clothing (and not necessarily in that order), then I'd be really hesitant to make statements like "there's no context where imperialism becomes a good thing".

                        Sure the Imperium of Man is an outrageous, cartoony, evil that would make Judge Dress blush. Still the good guys in the 40k universe. And Exalted isn't a GrimDark game.

But the books... well, tbh, I always found 2e's charmset to be subtly deconstructionist, but maybe it was too subtle. And Ex3's lead developers, especially John Morke, were very keen on going in for Solars as the good guys.
                                So I do somewhat agree with you.


                                If your choices are: an evil empire of elemental super soldiers will save your life but extract taxes; or an evil empire of fanged, mutant cannibals is going to rape you to death, eat your flesh and sow your skin into your clothing (and not necessarily in that order), then I'd be really hesitant to make statements like "there's no context where imperialism becomes a good thing".
                                While I do agree with your overall point, there is the issue that often that is not the only two choices, but Empires believe/present it as your only two choices, or most of all force it as the two choices.

                                The most pro-British guy I ever met from our various ex-colonies was a guy from Myanmar. Now, most Burmans have a very negative view of their old colonial overlords, but he was from an isolated hill tribe in the north-west mountains. He told me all about this British missionary who'd come to his village and taught them modern medicine, how to read (they had no alphabet for their language, so he made them one based on Burman which obviously benefited them later in learning Myanmar's largest language), and that Jesus loves you but is not cool with cutting off people's heads to decorate your village.
                                So for this tribe, the British were great... except it wasn't really the British Empire, whose policy towards the hill tribes was to mostly just occasionally ride around waving flags and saying "we're in charge, check out how awesome we are" and then go home, whereas the low landers were fully conquered with the whole "pay tax or get shot" policy. Spreading modern medicine, etc doesn't actually require conquering people. (And I think it's interesting that many of Myanmar's hill tribes are Christian or semi-Christian, whereas Burmans are mostly Buddhist; we British were far more successful at religious conversion in areas that were not under brutal occupation. I wonder why? I almost suspect it might be somewhat similar to why most Welsh people under the Empire were Protestant, and most Irish were not.)*


                                With 40K, the Imperium protects humanity from Orcs, Chaos, etc, but it's honestly not that great. It's riven with corruption, elitism, feudalism, etc, that severely impacts humanity's production levels, not to mention encourages people to rebel, turn to Chaos, change sides and join the Tau, etc, etc. The Imperium of man isn't so much a matter of "we must make great sacrifices to defend humanity" as "it's impossible for twelve AI-hating humans to functionally administer a galactic empire without reliable FTL travel or a unified technological base". It's "Was China's response to Coronavirus good?" And the answer is sort of yes, because they can do whatever it takes to crush it, but it wouldn't have been such a problem if it wasn't a paranoid authoritarian state in the first place.

                                There's also the issue that the Imperium's violent xenophobia is a self-defeating prophecy; aliens hate us so we hate them so they hate us so we hate them. War against the Necrons or Tyranids is necessary, not necessarily the Craftworlds or Tau or Zoats or whatever.

                                I honestly wonder if the whole "Roboute Guilleman is back and he's not so keen on what the Imperium's become" is that the writers gave up on trying to present the Imperium as a parody, and were just like "sigh, we'll make half the galaxy the kind of good Imperium, and half can be the grimdark one for the grognards to play in."


                                *So back to the Realm. I have seen sometimes good plot with the tension between the Immaculate Monks who want to educate the locals, heal them, save them from corrupt gods and ravenous Raksha, and destroy their local culture and beliefs**, and the nobles who want to find ways to tax the locals dry but honestly don't care about the local religion that much and are cool with them keeping their own culture, and may even quite enjoy that culture, ala East India Company men. You can also do tensions like the early colonial slave-owners who wouldn't let their slaves learn about Christianity because then they wouldn't be able to justify enslaving them.

                                **Though you can also do tensions between the monks who are happy to syncretise stuff and the ones who aren't. Obviously Pure Way/Realm Immaculates are the obvious one here, but I think you can quite happily do it with just Immaculates.
                                This may be the kind of thing some in my group enjoy, but not others though?

                                Anyway. John, I do agree with your point that of course few (maybe not 0) real-world governments are 100% bad, and that even includes Empires, especially ones who believe in their own "civilising mission" which the Realm very much does. But then, Empire vs Anarchy or Empire vs Conquest By Worse People are often (not always!*** But often) a false dichotomy.

                                And this can be a difficult thing to present clearly. Exalted's not great at it, but I guess it does somewhat try. I think some people would like it to not try, and I don't think that's the answer, because if nothing else it's dull.

                                ***David Ben-Gurion and Aang San are both examples of nationalist leaders who switched back and forth between opposing and supporting the British and other Empires, not because they liked the British, but in a world of clashing huge Empires yeah, sometimes you have no alternative.
                                That doesn't mean we British didn't have an alternative to being dicks of course (and in both cases we eventually f***ed off, though not completely because we thought it was the moral thing to leave), it's just that we didn't leave the locals with much alternative.
