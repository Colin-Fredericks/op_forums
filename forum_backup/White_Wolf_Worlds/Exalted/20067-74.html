                            For Skillful Reappropriation, I believe you're correct. Imagine you're planting evidence. The second part of the charm lets you say, "Wait, what's this then?!" and snatch a bloody glove or confession letter from your mark, or whatever it was you planted. Everyone sees you doing this. It's obvious you are stealing the mark's secret possession, which was definitely in their control the entire time.

                            For Iron Wolves' Grasp, I think you still need to roll 3 successes on the initiative roll. It's a difficulty 3 gambit. In this case, the cost is based on the charm. Normally, the cost is based on the difficulty, it is not the case that the difficulty is based on the cost. If this weren't the case, why would the charm give you double 9s on an unnecessary Initiative roll?

                                        One Weapon Two Blows & Agile Dragonfly Blade?

                                        Gambits only cost Difficulty + 1, and that's one of the benefits of using them instead of a Decisive attack. Instead of disarming someone, you could have done 3+ health levels of damage, BUT with a Disarm, you only lose 4 initiative. That lets you stay on top, if you have a combat strategy that relies on being on top of the init order. It's also less disastrous if you don't think you can incapacitate them in a single blow. (If you hit someone with a decisive, succeed, and they LIVE... they're probably going to get two turns before your next go, and you'll be close to crashing.)

                                        Extra successes don't matter for Gambits, if I'm reading this correctly.

                                        Your read on Guard Breaking Technique is essentially correct, but note the action type. It's a reflexive distract gambit that you can use while Defending Another. War Lion Stance is a scenelong Defend Other effect. Guard Breaking Technique could allow you to attack twice in a single round, so long as one of those attacks is a Distract gambit for your ward.

                                        Your read on Agile Dragonfly Blade matches my own. For example: Alice has 10 initiative, and Bob has 2, Bob attacks Alice and deals 3 withering damage. Bob gains 4 initiative (3 damage, plus a bonus just for landing the attack). Alice loses 3. Bob is now at 6 and Alice is at 7. Agile Dragonfly Blade would allow him to trigger One Weapon, Two Blows even though he doesn't normally meet OWTB's condition.

                                                        10-24-2015, 01:17 AM
                                                        Originally posted by Citadel97501 View Post
                                                        Sorry to have so many odd things come up today...but how does Worthy Mount Technique, and War Lion Stance interact? This seems kind of funny to have both you and your mount using Reflexive Defend others which should penalize your opponent 2 or 3 dice no matter who he attacks, basically giving you Decisive Soak by mutual defense?

                                                        In the scenario where you and your mount are defending each other, the attacker can just choose to attack the one they don't want to hit, and hit the defender. For instance, if the attacker targets you and you use Worthy Mount Technique to defend with your horse's Parry, the attacker can simply say "okay, I hit the horse", and the attack resolves normally. Your Defend Other on the horse doesn't apply, because the horse wasn't targeted by the attack. You were. The horse merely absorbed the hit.

                                                        If you're in a scenario where you're defending Wilting Orchid the Easily Bruised while mounted, and someone targets Wilting Orchid, you could theoretically Defend him with War Lion Stance, but your horse could not defend you with Worthy Mount Technique, because the attack was not directed at you. It was directed at Wilting Orchid.

                                                        Let's switch things around a bit, though. Now Wilting Orchid is the one on the horse, and he has Worthy Mount Technique. You're on the ground with War Lion Stance, defending Wilting Orchid. An attack targets him, and you defend. I'm not sure if, in the case the attack strikes through you to hit Wilting Orchid, that Worthy Mount Technique is still applicable. The rules do not state whether or not a single original target can benefit from multiple defenders. No matter what the ruling is, there's nothing special about the interaction between Worthy Mount Technique and War Lion Stance.
