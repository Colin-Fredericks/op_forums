                            As for why there’s a dedicated group of Exalted Crafter enthusiasts, I’ve got my take on the reason. It might not hold for everyone. I’m not even entirely sure it holds for me, but...

                            Anyway. One of the things about the old Storyteller system is that it has a bunch of attributes and abilities. And, when you’re new to the game, all of the attributes SEEM equally important, and the same for abilities. There’s nothing in the rules or on the sheet that says Dexterity is twice as important as Strength or that Wits is basically never rolled or anything. It all costs the same.

                            This creates an implied promise that you can create a character who is really in to any of these abilities and said character will be viable. And, just because the game lines usually fell down on these promises, doesn’t mean that they didn’t attract people who wanted to believe them.

                            And then Exalted comes along and turns up the volume on this implied promise. “Look” it says “we have a class that is all about traveling around and doing paperwork and we have a bunch of magic powers that you can only get because you’re so awesome at doing said paperwork! You can totally make that Ninja Accountant you’ve secretly been yearning to play, it’ll be awesome! Or, hey, you can be a legendary Blacksmith that makes stuff like Kusenagi or Excalibur, how does that sound?”

                            And, just because the game didn’t fully execute on this promise, didn’t mean people didn’t want to buy into it.

                            Personally, I’m of the right age where all I really want from an RPG is to be able to play MacGyver. Someone who can use Science and Technology to quickly Craft a solution to any problem that might be at hand. And, while Exalted isn’t necessarily the best fit for that character, I had great fun with a mortal Thaumaturge in a 2e game who quickly mixed up a potion to drop a tree on the spirit we were fighting at the time. And stuff like Craftsman Needs No Tools is perfect for this type of person, even if the rules aren’t really set up for mortal level crafts to matter very much.

                            Point is that, Exalted promised me a long time ago that I could make a cool craftsperson and, despite the various issues over the years, I think 3e has mostly succeeded in that area (not that improvements couldn’t be had). And I’d just be sad if Essence backed off from that. Plus, if you dropped that from the game, why even bother with the Twilight Caste?

                                            Tbh, I don't think it'll affect the setting at all.

                                            The setting, being a fictional creation, is somewhat independent of the rules. It's just that the more it seems at-odds with the rules, the less it has versimilitude. But it's not like you can run a simulation of "with this disparity of power level, what does Creation look like".

                                            However, previously in 2nd ed, people often complained that the Lunar Elders and Deathlords were far too powerful rules-wise for what they seemed to do in-setting, and others to a lesser extent.

                                            3rd ed flattened this a bit (Raksi, Ma-Ha-Suchi, etc, are much less powerful than in 2nd ed, and I know the intention was for Deathlords to be significantly less powerful. Certainly the Silver Prince's short write-up doesn't make him sound very powerful). This gives it a bit more versimillitude, because you don't have to ask "why doesn't FaFL just kill everyone with his Creation-Slaying Oblivion Kick" or whatever.

                                            If Essence flattens it a bit more, then I don't think anyone reading the setting would notice unless they'd already convinced themselves that the rules=fluff in a way that makes perfect sense. But as I said, there's not really a way to test it.

                                            Certainly, I haven't got the impression that they intend to rewrite the setting significantly... though I do think Neall mentioned that they might change the way it's presented slightly to emphasise the disparity of power level less?
                                            (Tbh, I always felt the setting presented the Solars as a bit too powerful, considering the average Solar is way less experienced than the average Dragonblood or Lunar. But I guess 2nd ed combat Solars were truly ridiculous in practice.)
                                            My impression was that this was more about encouraging mixed-play than changing the setting per-se, but I could be wrong.

                                            Now, saying that, if the power level between Dragonblood and Solars/Abyssals/Infernals is compressed too much, then you may ask why 300 Exalts are a threat to 20,000 Exalts.

                                            For PCs, it's not an issue really of course; if all the PCs are Solars they'll get xp at such a ridiculous rate compared to NPCs that they'll still be able to challenge Dragonblood who are much older than them. But I imagine that there'll be a lot more mixed groups with 10 options! (Which, considering how few Solars are in the world, I mean it's like 40 in the Scavenger Lands, 20 in the West, 30 in the North... really not many.) So that doesn't really work.

                                            (My 1600-year old Lunar elder flipped out and screamed for several minutes last session when he discovered his new Solar friend knew Solar sorcery despite only being about 80. F***ing Solars and their ridiculous XP gain.)

                                            I also wondered whether, with Dragonblood, they'll somehow fluff it in to explain the lack of disparity.
                                            What I mean is, in 3rd edition, Solars get 46 motes and 15 charms after a few months to a year since exaltation. That's far, far more than a Dragonblood who's been exalted for that amount of time.
                                            But of course, unless you're doing a high-school game, you don't play Dragonblood who've been exalted for a few months, you play Dragonblood after about 10-20 years since exaltation; and you get 44 motes and 15 charms.

                                            So maybe all PCs will start with Essence 1, 5 motes, 10 charms, or however it'll work with Essence, and their charms will be largely of a similar level (with Solars getting some slightly stronger effects, plus the free excellencies, but of course they're social outcasts rather than being seen as living bodhisvatta-nobles), but it's not actually supposed to represent a similar Solar, Lunar and Dragonblood, but rather a 3-month Solar, 1-year Lunar and 20-years-since-exaltation Dragonblood.

                                            So when you fight Wyld Hunt Shikari or experienced Dragonblood legion officers... they're basically little more than starting characters. Whereas a Lunar NPC of the same age has much better stats than a starting character.
