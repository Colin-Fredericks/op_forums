    Kitekura - the city of magic

    11-29-2020, 07:42 AM
    This is a placeholder until I finally get some time and energy.

    WARNING: FINAL FANTASY TROPES NEARBY

    There was once a populous, out of the way city, somewhere between the North and the East. A place of somewhat harsh winters, but alleveviated by the fertility of the soil from the Eastern Pole of Wood. Its people hunted down animals for furs, chopped down trees for lumber, mined iron for ore, and traded with the outside world for things they could not produce. There was a nearbly shadowland, several demenses, and of course, fields for farming. A wealthy land-owning class who lived mostly in the city and had manors in the surrounding lands, selling food to the city at a tidy profit. Farms sold barley, wheat, and other crops adapted to the freezing cold. The nearby mines sold coal, iron, opals, and more minor precious stones. There was nothing special there that would stand out to any other. But its people were fierce, proud of their home, and loyal to boot. There was certainly a thriving amount of literacy in the upper classes, and a small print shop for paperback novels. The people of the realm would call it a backwater shithole, but its residents didn't care. It was one of the places that had good defenses against the dangers of the world, enough living space for all of them unlike cramped Whitehall, and its rivers, mountains, fields, hunting grounds, mines, all kept most of them fed and clothed.

    One day treasure hunters entered the area, and brought out something from a long-abandoned ruin. An artifact from the shogunate, they said. They brought it into the city, deep into one of the catacombs used to store food for winter supplies. The area's greatest mystic and thaumaturge, a man of the name Dandalus, began to examine and tinker with it, attempting to divine its functions. He had experience in this matter, having handled artifacts 2 and 3 before, and expected to be able to figure out the purpose of this. Alas, he was wrong. It was not from the Shogunate. It was from the First Age. And Dandalu's tinkering simply killed him and thousands more, tearing out the heart of the Kitekura in a titanic explosion that turned night into day and shook the earth.*

    The beating heart of Kitekura was shredded. Buildings toppled. Entire families wiped out. And then... came the transformations. A wave of unreality warped around the blast area, not dissimilar to a wyld zone. Then came the blessed. Men who could throw fire, manipulate air, who had claws from their arms, who's eyes could release deadly light. And then came the sorcerers. First one. Then two. Then ten. Then several dozen. Then a hundred. By the time it struck thousands, there was already a sense that something was going to go very, very wrong.

    The Kitekura of ten years after the explosion is something that would seem to be out of a dream, or maybe a Fair Folk Holding. The air is warm, far warmer, kept that way by continuous application of spells. Vast paved roads where 3 carriages can move in parallel lead to Kitekura. The land has been terraformed, transformed, warped by spells to be less like a wilderness and more like a park. There are now a dozen more settlements in the area than before. Fields are blessed by magic, growing even in winter. The timber export has literally tripled, entire fields of trees grown to full maturity with a single application of Summoning the Harvest. Elementals fly through the air, and out of sight, demons work unseen. Great glasshouses grow plants and medicines of all kind, while bushes out in the air give off the heat of a stove, melting snow and making the air warm even in the deep of winter. Kitekura now has quadrupled in size, with a sewer system, and many other things that occur when you have over a thousand sorcerers with a vast majority of them having a fierce sense of civic duty. There are areas with sorcerous workings gone awry, places where they've worked very well, and everything in between. There are now vast 'train' systems pulled by lumbering, gigantic earth elementals the size of elephants that pull cargo from one mountain to another.

    There are, of course, problems:





    *Think of the chernobyl explosion.

    Kitekura is a rather large city between north and east. Very Canada. Very populous mainly living on wheat, iron, hunted meat, fish, that kind of mostly cold climate stuff. Exports furs, wood, iron, that kinda thing. Strong civil society though not without stratification. No slavery. Cause I can't bear to write about that.

    So one day someone found a N/A artifact and accidentally exploded it. The explosion made a pseudo wyld zone, tore out its heart, and killed many people. and it's wake, people began to change. some just got the giant or claw mutation. Others...got fireballs or lightning. And others get sorcery.

    First one then two then ten then two dozen then hundreds of them. Sending shockwaves through society and the surrounding areas. Theres a bunch do Far hunters and abolitionists who's been hitting up the guild. Others are trying to build a demense. Some have fallen into infernaliam. And all that other stuff.

                  The Novibite section:

                  There were nobles within Kitekura - Not quite as extensive as the Realm, but they were the ones with high office in the cities and the surrounding areas. They were the ones who owned the armies. They were the ones who owned the mines, the mills, the fields, the forests. Who owned the local printing shop, who owned the forge, who ran the local blacksmith guild. Who gave out hunting licenses and the right to fish in the lakes. Rich, powerful, and influential, they ruled the land as kings and politicians, playing games far above the peasants.

                  This has changed.

                  The Novibites hate and love the new sorcerers. Several of their number are already sorcerers. But this is irrelevant. For it is not magical power that's the problem, but class. More specifically, owning property. The Novibites love it when sorcerers squeeze out an extra harvest from the field, or cast a spell to transform lifeless land into land as fertile as any Eastern soil. They love it when they use earth elementals to map out ore veins or dig out impossible shafts, or when they build irrigation systems for dry fields or when they use their fire elementals to bypass the need for fuel. They love it when sorcerers turn the weather good, or use spells to make plants grow that are not suited for the North-Eastern climate.

                  They do not like it when a sorcerer sets up a greenhouse and owns the production of cocoa leaves, or fire-herbs. They do not like it when a sorcerer buys worthless land, and uses sorcery to turn it into fertile fields, and becomes landowner. They don't like it when a sorcerer sets up his own mill using a permanently cast Stalwart Servitor, or when he creaters his own lake of fish.The standard dislike the rich ruling class has of the noveau riche. Of course, they never quite say this out loud. Noble-born sorcerers are first to have any contracts and favors, while the noble-favoring sorcerers are the next. They view the sorcerers as useful, but of course, not truly worthy of rule, what with inferior breeding (ignoring that sorcerers have made sorcerous workings on themselves to turn themselves superhuman) and lack of pedigree. The cliche thing. Of course no matter their feelings they can't really do much. They can hire all the mercenaries, send in their armies, and throw in all the sorcerers aligned with their side, but even a single terrestrial circle sorcerer can wipe out a single battalion with a wave of their hands, or turn entire miles of fields into worthless blood-ridden sludge. And there is a distinct gap in sorcerers. Majority of the sorcerers will not turn against their brethren, and so the nobles find themselves outnumbered. Currently, they are evenly matched with no clear winner. Of course, that is where the PCs come in. The nobles are willing to pay in money, land, artifacts, Manses, and anything else the PCs would want to re-assert their power. They are rather uncaring of collateral damage, willing to burn it all then rather share their power.

                  The Guild

                  Good news. There is far more mines for precious stones and minerals than before. Good news. Timber Exports are at an all time high. Good news. The amount of exports coming out has skyrocketed, as sorcerers have cast sorcerous workings and the like to create truly unique goods in Creation.

                  Bad news. The Slave trade has ground to a goddamned halt.

                  Kitekura has never had a good relationship with slavery. It's first exposure to it was being raided for slaves for the Fair Folk. Ever since, they've been cagey about it. Yes, maybe some peasants get beaten. Maybe a little mistreatment here and there. But the idea of slavery is abhorrent and no Kitekuran will welcome it. A slave-catcher entering the city would be shunned at best. Beaten to death at worst. But for all their dislike of it, Kitekura is isolated by both river and mountain. Even if it had the power projection to send armies, it would be unable to halt the slave trade by dint of its isolation. And of course, disliking something is not the same as being proactive in its destruction. Only a select few sorcerers and citizens go out and kick slavery in the nads.

                  However, that has changed. Sorcerers using Ramparts of Obedient Earth have built highways, bridges, and the like, and put on a high toll or even completely bar any attempt to send slaves through. Mercenaries have been hired to smash slave caravans and slave-raiders. And several weeks ago, the great chain breaker Shining Fist broke a great caravan destined to the local Fair Folk Holding, freeing several thousand slaves, and then went on to murder the fair folk holding as well. Sorcerers use high speed travel spells to murderer caravan guards, and then use their ability to fly to bypass things like 'vast canyons' and 'mountains', using the difficult terrain to their advantage, turning the area for several dozen miles around Kitekura into a death trap for any slavers.

                  This has caused the guild several headaches. Slaves are immensely profitable for one. The local guildmaster Obsidian Heart (who specializes in slaves) has been reprimanded for this and humiliated, especially when several ludicrously profitable contracts with the Fair Folk Holding she negotiated were rendered null and void when the fairies were all murdered by self-directed iron golems. Mercenaries are now refusing contracts with her because no one wants to be torn apart by a blood ape or reduced to mincemeat by Obsidian Butterflies. Now she seeks revenge. Shining Fist is the leader of the movement, its great driving force. Gregarious, charismatic, and a force of personality, killing him will stymie the movement and at least give her some breathing room. Moreover, she wishes to smash the sorcerers of Kitekura for interfering at all, and will welcome anyone who is able to slay them en masse, whether it is a Dragonblooded mercenary or a Celestial. Whether or not this will lead to several dozen demons being sent after her as retaliation is something to be seen.
