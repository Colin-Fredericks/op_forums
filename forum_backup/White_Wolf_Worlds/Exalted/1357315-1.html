            Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
            What's the difference between unlocking flight and blasts of lightning with Mela's Coil's Evocations and a Warstrider's Evocations?
            It depends on the warstrider. If it's one of Karvara's, then nothing.

            But Cathdral of Sublime Annihilation has an actual lightning ballista and implosion bow and those targeting modules built into its chassis. There's a difference in characterisation between that and the other one, even if the necessities of consistency in the system require it to be something unlocked by spending experience points in tandem with roleplaying out actually becoming good with the stuff.

            Mind, there's still a genre background to the idea that harnessing the machine requires a bit of synchronisation with the pilot, although it amounts to somewhere between that and somebody wearing an Iron Man suit needing to develop the proper intuition to fire off those weapons accurately and reflexively.

            There's also the matter of special activation rules (i.e. getting the Evocation for free), which might be less frequent with power armour and run on conceits that are less character oriented. Or perhaps might be seen as driven more by function than theme.

            That said, I find the description given by developers and in Arms of the Chosen to which I was referring to be concerned with more than how they are purchased, more on what they are in total and what concept ties them together.

            Originally posted by vwllss trnt prncss
            It just feels wrong to me that "I use the rocket boots!" is an Evocation. I think if I were redesigning Warstriders (or Power Armor) I'd make them collections of artifacts. There's the core frame, everything needed to move and fight, it's the Warstrider. Then there's all the components that aren't needed but really should be in there like the essence thrusters, shoulder-mounted flame cannons, or the memory-occlusion plating that I have trouble justifying as "sleeping within the artifact" but don't want clogging up the attunement bonus.
            I get that, and while I do think that there can be explanations that reconcile it with things like needing to maximize one's synch rate with the armour's nerve connections or it's power armour that is still being fuelled by your qi so those rocket boots of shoulder cannons have no energy source until your meridians hook up to them or something, it does rest a bit on suspension of disbelief.

            I'm personally motivated to find the spot that reconciles them because while I personally never cared much for power armour in the game that already had magical armour (nor am particularly enamoured with the subject at all), I've seen so many other people express enthusiasm about the concept that I see great value in finding it a niche and making it work. And hey, sometimes it's even nice to have the concept of Evocations but not need to personalize them so much.

            Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey
            There were 13 Staturn V rockets built and launched, not only were each of them modified and fine tuned, in fact every one of their Rocketdyne F-1 engines was unique, even though each rocket had 5 of them. So, no, they weren't all assembly line mass produced things. There could be room for some of that I think. Why can't there be the 7 identical blades of a master swordsmith, forged for each of the seven daughters of the emperor?
            Yeah, see, this is the kind of thing I've tried to point out in the past when people get antsy about the idea of certain Artifacts being fabricated in a factory. So what if it's a factory, that doesn't mean that it's more of a Ford than a Ferrari.

            Or hell, even one of the Ford racing cars rather than the kind they rolled out for the public consumer.

            Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey
            Something that you could tell at a glance is one of the 5-dot wonders that survived from ages past, or one of the tiny handful that was actually produced in the second age. Something that tells you that the armor itself is almost as dangerous and versatile as the Exalt wielding it.
            Well, in terms of how much it's going to wreck your shit I don't think there's ultimately much difference between a five-dot suit of armour constructed with Craft (Artifact) and Craft (First Age Artifice), assuming that neither is strongly specialized for a combat scenario that is distinctly suited or unsuited for the situation at hand.

            Mind, when thinking about a non-First Age armour, sure dot rating is no longer tied to actual armour quality so that can cover everything between a buff jacket and full articulated plate, but it's funny to compare the latter one to First Age Artifice when thinking about how heavy plate armour was precisely engineered within its own milieu.

            But I digress. I picture a major part of the difference as being that the Artifice armour has powers that are not metaphors for something, that are not trying to tell a story. That they are utilitarian in a manner that is not necessarily more threatening to the opponent but might be more convenient to the wearer.

            Also just gives players and Storytellers more latitude in designing Evocation suites.

            I wonder if I could look back over depictions of Dragon Armour in the prior Editions and filter out key features of their capabilities.

            I have approximate knowledge of many things.

                    I respectfully submit that it probably isn't a great idea for a one-person suit of armor to require four separate hearthstones just to operate properly.

                    Mela's Coil requires two hearthstones for its ultimate Evocation, and only because it does something as crazy as turning the wearer into an actual goddamn dragon (which is honestly more dramatic than anything I would expect out of the Most Terrifying Armor of the Air Dragon, although I'm willing to be wrong about this), and even then I'm not fond of the notion. Even a warstrider only requires a single greater hearthstone to operate.

                    I doubt anyone would build an artifact (or set of artifacts meant to be used together as a unit) that requires four unless it's intended to be the personal armor of a Great House matriarch or something. And even the very few Exalted who can actually procure four manses for their personal use might not consider that a worthwhile use of resources.

                    I honestly think it's easier (and more consistent with the rest of the game) to just build power armor as a five-dot Artifact with a varied suite of Evocations, some of which may require a hearthstone to be slotted in. The tropes you describe (aside from perhaps modularity) could easily be represented in a single five-dot armor.

                            I would suggest, if you're designing dragon armor and want to preserve both the idea that the various advanced features are represented by evocations, and the notion that various suits of dragon armor are built from designs, rather than each being absolutely singular works of craftsmanship, that you could make the basic functions of the armor be represented by a few evocations which could be selected in any order, then say that those basic evocations were essentially the same between all copies of the armor (possibly with some minor variations to represent tweaks in the individual designs), and then present follow-up evocations to them and specify that those evocations are more variable, representing the specific way that one user has learned to use the abilities of her particular suit.

                            So, for example, for a set of Armor of the Immaculate Dragons (Air Model), I'd give it eight "basic" evocations, and the benefit of attuning to the armor would be unlocking one of them for free automatically: one charm each for the filtration baffles, sensory augmentation visor, exomuscular fibers, cloaking device, astrological occlusion field, integrated elemental lens, lightning gauntlets, and essence thrust drive. All of those would be unlockable at Essence 1, probably. Then, I'd build evocations that chained off each of those specifically, representing more complex or potent use of the system in question. For the essence thrust drive, for example, I think I wouldn't make the basic evocation able to sustain full flight (that's pretty potent at Essence 1), instead having it allow hovering a range band or two above the ground, while subsequent evocations would allow full flight, provide better mobility, and add various tricks to deal with flight in combat, as per the various flight artifacts in Arms of the Chosen.
