    Originally posted by Colapso View Post
    Just because something was published doesn't mean it is a good idea.
    You don't want to get me started on how many things are a bad idea in exalted. It is entirely possible to write a supplement sized book just going over all the design flaws and the ways it caters only to certain player types. Hell the way Martial Arts was designed and implemented in Exalted is about on par with a virgin writing a fan fiction sex scene.

    Originally posted by Colapso View Post
    Becoming a number in a generic blob is the very opposite of the core concept of Exalted.

    Exalted are never extras, and each conflict in the setting shows well that they cannot be treated in these generic terms - to create 500 DBs, of any power, like that is to engineer your very own, personal Usurpation.
    I find the notion of being the badass anime character trope to be boring and pointless. The wandering hero shtick was played out for me by the 1990s, then again so was playing the villain. Exalted is supposed to be about playing an RPG on a truly epic scale. But what exactly is epic in this case?

    It isn't fighting a hundred guys by yourself, it isn't making a poem that makes the gods themselves weep, it isn't creating a new sorcery spell and it isn't recreating a character clone from an anime you saw one time.

    I ask people what their end game would look like for a given storyline or character. The responses I get are generally things like "I beat up the realm" or "recreate the deliberative". But they rarely if ever get that far.

    In my sort of games, the fall of the Realm is a beginning to middle game sort of storyline. Forget recreating the Deliberative, I am thinking making something new, something different. I don't want to push the world map from its core book scale back to the Dreams of the First Age scale, I want to carve out a map the size of that one fan made one that shows the blessed isle as barely an inch across compared to the rest of Creation.

    But here is the thing, after a certain point, things like Terrestrials and Dragon Kings become mundane NPC mooks. So when I talk about how to deal with breeding an army of Terrestrials it is from the perspective of those sorts of later plans where just being exalted doesn't really account for much.

    Consider for a moment what sort of threats you would have to design to threaten a Creation where every major city is an alchemical Metropolis, each one boasting a standing Terrestrial Legions of 50,0000 Dragon-Blooded and another 200,000 Tiger Warriors. Consider a Creation so huge that the Terrestrials and Alchemicals have an equal say to the regular celestials just due to numbers. Consider a Creation where some of my most insane Manse designs are everyday infrastructure and in some cases the only way to manage a Creation of that size. Because that is the sort of game the Exalted system is supposed to let you play.

    Consider that sort of game setting for a moment, let it simmer in your mind. Now hop on the forums and listen to people talk about if it is toxic to the setting or not to allow some one to breed a couple hundred Terrestrials. Because to me, complaining about that just feels like such a lack of imagination and drive.

    A person can go to any other game setting and play a character that accomplishes nothing of note, but in Exalted your starting character is essentially where most other games force character retirement. By that same concept the end game designs should be similarly out of this world. But instead all I hear about is the same levels of dreck I heard about back in my days playing AD&D. It is disappointing to say the least.

    I will step off my soap box now....

                Originally posted by Bastet View Post
                Why not give us some ideas? This seems to be your preferred level of play rather than the outright victory most groups would consider it as. What challenges do empires like that face?
                Well first off as you expand the size of Creation, you end up expanding the size of the Underworld. So even if you have created a strong society and pushed the Deathlords back into the Underworld and hunted down practically every shadowland, the Underworld and its denizens remain. Since you have expanded the Underworld so greatly, this means the Deathlords and the Neverborn will be more entrenched and powerful than ever before.

                Imagine the sort of scenario you could face if all the deathlords started actually working together in a unified fashion with no pretense of hiding what they are capable of. The last time something even close to that happened resulted in the Great Contagion.

                Eventually you will want to take the fight to the Underworld itself rather than just containing incursions. But this means changing tactics because all the essence works differently there than it does in Creation. This means your Terrestrial troops and even some of the Celestials are limited in power duration. Of course even if you some how manage to defeat the deathlords and put more functional bindings on the Neverborn, the only way to ensure Underworld compliance is to colonize it. This in turn means a whole new breeding program to create strains of Death-Tainted Dragon-Blooded to permanently live in the Underworld.

                The threats from the Yozis are kind of a permanent thing so you have a continuous threat of Akuma and Infernal attacks. Though I could easily see using Malfeas as a training grounds for fun and profit.

                But for truly huge scale stuff, consider the possibilities of Creations made by rogue Primordials deep in the Wyld. We have the example of the one the Unconquered Sun destroyed, but it wasn't entirely wiped out, so it could have been rebuilt. There could now be dozens of such Creations all poised to attack.

                Then there is the side story of that Terrestrial in the late First Age that was given a dream to take almost an entire gens into the deep wyld. If they survived to this day, how many of them could there be? They could easily return to creation not unlike the clans from the MechWarrior lore that returned to the core system to reassert their dominance with thousands of years of weapons tech development. Perhaps to get around the limitations of their Terrestrial natures, they started upgrading themselves like the borg or warhammer 40k tech priests to gain access to something that approximates celestial crafting charms.

                Of course there is always the Well of Udr, which if it truly is a gateway of sorts to alternate realties and other worlds, well then you could just as easily add access to the Gunstar Autochonia setting for cross over events and having to deal with a fighting force of Primordials that is actually stronger than anything previously faced. But this starts getting into DC Comics levels of alternate earths with your one reality slowly consuming and adjusting the rest.

                Then there is the fact that "temporal weapons" were used in the Primordial War, so eventually either by being reengineered or by finding a remaining one, you can get into a nasty form of Time War which can draw on Dr. Who levels of messing around with things. Then cross that with the Well of Udr and things get......complicated.

                But strangely enough when you take a step back from it all, yeah a lot of this is only possible because you have some Celestial Exalts setting things up and planning this out, but in truth it is almost impossible to pull off without the unlimited Terrestrials and potentially the Alchemicals.

                Of course if you convert some of the Infernals into new Primordials, well then things really become interesting. Because then you start running two games back to back, where one game you are playing the Exalt crew that built this grand end game scenario, then the second game is having the players using different characters trying to bring it down. Their knowledge of the setting and situations makes them their own worst enemies.

                                The way the Second Edition core lines up the whole thing is actually pretty nifty, now that I give it more consideration. The idea that newly graduated Dynasts go off to live with other relatives provides the skeleton for how the Great Houses are a coordination of enormous extended families, cross pollinating individual households rather than letting them just build up inside of themselves, while still existing under the direction of ancestors of the next step up (meaning that, while your adult children go off to be under the more direct authority of your siblings or cousins rather than yourself, they still fall under the umbrella of your grandparents and great-grandparents).

                                It also provides a context for why there are diminishing returns for having too many young Dragon Blooded under one roof, and it's about the same as when they're still children; the expense. It just changes from the expense of educating them to the expense of rewarding them for their household services (which is also partly the expense of educating your own children ).

                                It's a good way to emphasize that Dragon Blooded are never pawns, they're simply players who operate in different arenas of the game. After you graduate (and have your first wild vacation in the Threshold), you join another household of your family (or possibly your father's family), and bring the values of being young, vigorous, lacking social establishment and a steady job or family, the values of being somebody who can be sent out to acquire assets or further interests for them while they remain home and oversee their own responsibilities, and in return you get expensive gifts, introductions and commendations, being delegated portions of your host's property. You're not a pawn, you're an agent, and one worth overseeing directly (and expensive enough to be about the limit of what they can manage).

                                Indeed, that kind of cross-pollinated mentor system probably also helps maintain the conservatism of Dynast society, because you get to bring that young, vigorous, possibly even idealistic new demigod under the wings of the establishment, where they can be moulded into upholding the status quo and thus eventually assume their own place as somebody with a job that maintains the Realm and a marriage that ensures the future of the Dynasty. After all, the things that make junior Dynasts so valuable to their seniors are also the exact kinds of things that can make them terribly dangerous; youthful energy that could be directed against you, lack of social standing to undermine or attack, no established assets or responsibilities to target or take away. No, better to manage each one carefully, make them care about those things so that they'll work for you to be given them, and thereafter they're just like you and little threat to the system. It's for their own good anyway; surely that model for Dynast society and life cycle is the superior way in which to live, since it's the one you grew up in yourself.

                                If you had large numbers of young people who were all gathered at once in non-descript masses in which you couldn't really take a direct hand in managing them, well, they might start consulting one another on how to do things, might start getting ideas, and nobody wants that. Something the size of a Sworn Brotherhood is fine, but the very idea of a Dragon Blooded youth movement? I can imagine the Scarlet Empress grew up with horror stories of places in the Shogunate that went all Les Miserables, and was eager to shape her Dynasty to avoid it.

                                Hence scenarios in which having the largest number of Dragon Blooded, especially young adult ones, isn't actually very optimal at all.
