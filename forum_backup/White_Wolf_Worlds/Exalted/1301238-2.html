                    In my experience, some people sit on their experience because they're not sure what to get, but those tend to be the people who are not the sort to buy stuff to get them out of a certain situation.

                    Of course, people do buy charms at the end of sessions that they think will be useful for the next session, but I don't really mind that. For one thing, if it's too situational, it'll help them for one session, and then not again for ages.

                    (There's also a theory of learning that this is kind of how things work, hence all that Task-Based Learning that was fashionable 10 years ago, but it's a bit complex.)

                    Anyway, obviously sitting in a jail cell and learning Larceny 5 and a charm for escaping is ridiculous, and I just wouldn't let people do it. But I don't think making people spend 4 months weight-lifting to buy a dot of Strength, as realistic as it might be, is actually very practical for a game. There's other ways to do it.

                                    I see (and treat) training times as a safety valve for helping players focus on the long-term arc of their character's development instead of whatever they happen to be doing right now. Saying that once you decide you want something it will be X time before you get it helps.

                                    That said, I've never felt the need to be super strict about them. The layer of unreality between calendar time and narrative time makes it really awkward. So in practice I end up holding advancement to in-between story beats rather than specific amounts of downtime. If the players fight someone in Chiaroscuro and then immediately chase him across the desert for two straight uneventful weeks before picking up the fight again in a hidden manse, I wouldn't count that as a story break. But on the flip side, if they were to liberate Thorns from the Mask of Winters and the head of Oct breaks through from Autochthonia in the middle of town while they're still pulling Volcano Cutter out of the smoldering remains of Juggernaut, they'll still get a chance to spend their XP before we resume play. In short I'm not going to incentivize or penalize advancement mechanics with timing choices made for dramatic effect. But as a general principle I expect that where the XP goes when the break does happen will be mostly a formality because the player already indicated where it was probably going to go.

                                        Originally posted by Clophiroth View Post


                                        Same. My games are usually very "daily", that´s it, most sessions are one or two days, with very little downtime because player characters are very busy. So, if I applied training times, we would have the problem of players having 40-50 XP, and not enough time to train to spend their XP!

                                        So, I demand justification. You want to learn sorcery/occult/martial arts? Find a mentor, a secret manual, or whatever. And it doesn´t matter how much experience you have, you can only improve the same trait one dot at a time.
                                        We have found the same thing occurs. Keeping training times but helping players feel it isn't so onerous I've instituted training accelerators as suggested in the book.
                                        Want to speed up learning? Then trainers knock it down one time increment from the core training times. e.g. Months to weeks, weeks, to days, days-halved. Find an exceptional trainer and times are knocked down two increments.

                                                        I'm in two games right now, one of which has waived training times entirely (I run this one) and one has loosened them.
