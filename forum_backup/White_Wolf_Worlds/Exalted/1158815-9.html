                                    All-Conquering Colossus: The story of this one strikes me as being the archetypal warstrider, even with the slight twist of the love story motivating its creation and how that informs its resulting powers. I do actually derive amusement from the idea that the thing was inadvertently presented as a romantic gift in the form of being able to effectively fight off being impulsively attacked by the object of affection; where's that in our Marvel movies, eh? As for the powers themselves, they mostly reflect that story; it's your basic concept of a warstrider, the big armour to fight opponents that are very large, very strong, or both, in various elaborations of strength and durability (with hints of that underlying purpose in the occasions where proven durability enhances strength). While I imagine that later warstriders will have a bit more flash to them, I actually like at least one of them to have that sense of focus, while still proving the concept of expressing their power in Evocations. Hell, assuming that none of the others date to the War, I could even use all of that to add a bit of flavour in my own mind with the notion that part of the simplicity derives from it being one of the earlier examples. All in all, I'd say that this was a good one to start off with.

                                    Cathedral of Sublime Annihilation: It's nice to see the Scorpion Empire make a return, as well as a warstrider focused on ranged combat. If All-Conquering Colossus was a straightforward one for brawling, this one is similar in terms of laying down a barrage of firepower. I find it to work, though, partially because of the addition of various diagnostic tools, and because the contrast between how the Artifact siege weapons function here and how they did when standing alone gives a certain sense of power and purpose beyond it just being a warstrider with an implosion bow welded on. It helps that it has things such as Reality-Stabilizing Cannonade to maintain a certain mystical flavour for it. And the description of Godsigh of Noonday Triumph makes me want to play Asura's Wrath again (or find a new game of a similar style to try out) .

                                    Crusading Spear of the Depths: Was Oadenol always a Sidereal? Eh, doesn't matter. So, warstrider specialized for submarine combat. It doesn't specify so, but I assume that Crusading Spear is buoyant enough to effectively swim and remain suspended if its pilot wishes. While I do like the preceding warstriders, it is nice to see one whose capabilities are a bit more diverse, making good use of its concept. In just this one, you've got improved underwater mobility and senses, a cascade to create a signature attack, various forms of defensive water manipulation.
                                    Emerald Chevalier: Huh, a centaur-esque warstrider. It's a bit odd, but I suppose it's a good idea to showcase something to give a sense of diverse possibilities for them. What's most interesting here is this concept of using a warstrider as a means of taking a particular fighting style and then scaling it up.

                                    Godspeed Vanguard: One of those more stealth-oriented warstriders that they brought up in the outline. This would be another one I can find less to say about, although I find its realisation of overcoming a natural limitation of the warstrider to be effective, and do like the afterimage based Evocation.

                                    Now, for the royals.

                                    Ascendant Nova Phoenix: Hmm, unusually massive and capable of flying. I'll be interested to see how its Evocations realise those feats attributed to it. Looking over it, it definitely does feel powerful, although depending how far into the Evocation cascade a character has taken it, possibly something that requires the chronicle to have taken a very extreme direction to feel as though it's getting its full use (or to keep it from being simply overwhelming). A high mobility transportation Charm feels like a curious choice for the pinnacle, but... I guess anything more would be redundant compared to the preceding arsenal, and this ultimate power gives a sense of omnipresence to it; a notion that at the height of its power, no matter where a threat might arise, this devastating response would be able to arrive either in time to stop the threat or avenge its victims. Between this and the other orichalcum warstriders, while I wouldn't model the powers of the Unconquered Sun off of him, it does give me this image in which he was a typical source of inspiration behind their construction.

                                    Karvara, the Walking Devil Tower: Hmmm... hmmm... oho.

                                    ​Y'all built it up too much; it's okay.

                                    So that's all of the warstriders. I'd say that they've done a fairly good job of elevating the concept, and realising its initial promise. I'll summarise my overall thoughts on Arms of the Chosen at a later time.

                                    I have approximate knowledge of many things.

                                                Karvara, the Walking Devil Tower: The origin story of this one is neatly detailed; if nothing else, it should satisfy people who were dissatisfied by prior Editions never letting Lunars accomplish anything cool without dying. I also like the turn of phrase saying that being unable to exist in Creation meant that if it was somewhere, Creation would be the thing to go. As for its mechanics... it's funny to see that this is the only warstrider that can be attuned only by a certain kind of Exalt. Assuming that they don't have Limit Tracks, even Ascendant Nova Phoenix could be wielded by a Terrestrial, but Karvara cannot because they would be insufficiently corruptible. On that note, this really is a fearsome extension of prior examples of corrupting Artifacts, fitting to its elevated state both as a warstrider and N/A level. Consider its Evocation development, I guess that a necessary narrative path for one to take while associated with this warstrider is to seek out scenarios that provoke Limit, which can probably be expressed in-character in a number of ways. It's also interesting to consider how some of Karvara's effects, such as aiding in social influence or more-or-less erasing people from existence, might create an impulse to use it in situations unusual for a warstrider; more low scale and personal. I could really see that playing into a narrative of the warstrider getting under a pilot's skin, luring them into donning it with greater frequency and at times when the force would seem unnecessary; I have this picture of the uncanny scenario of one's circlemates realising that one is starting to wear it almost all the time, or some petitioner coming for an important meeting with an Exalt and being disturbed to find them conducting business from inside this distinctly terrifying machine.

                                                ​(I later commented on the notion that Karvara might spend a lot of its time getting to know you and discerning your Intimacies, so that when it assumes control one of its highest priorities is to go around systematically eating and erasing people whom you care about, until one day its pilot wakes up under the impression that the warstrider is the only meaningful relationship that they've ever had.)

                                                    Here's my short reactions to each of the weapons, along with a personal grade:

                                                    3-Dot Weapons

                                                    Courange and Galliard:
                                                    I love these artifacts. While its fairly restrictive in that you need two characters to use them, it is a perfect example of intermingling story and mechanics. The capstone - fusing into a single being - is flavorful, but I feel likely to be clunky. 5/5

                                                    The Distaff:
                                                    Not as much of a fan here. The story is also fantastic, and it has a great arc presented through story and mechanics, but the other charms are lacking. This is largely due to interacting extensively with grapple and crafting rules, both of which are weak. 2/5 purely on the strength of the story

                                                    The Fist of Titans:
                                                    Story isn't nearly as interesting as the first two, but it provides a neat mechanical minigame via generating and using charges. Unfortunately, charges are very limited, and can only be generated with Aim actions, so it is difficult to use the other evocations. Despite this, it shows good unity of themes and solid mechanical weight. 3/5

                                                    Frost-Thorn Knuckles:
                                                    Another pretty dull story, but great mechanical execution. Its just really really good at killing things, with a secondary focus on surviving the cold. This is a really niche focus though, and makes them less useful anywhere outside of the North - and likely the far north. 3/5

                                                    Hawk Star's Jess:
                                                    Really boring. It's a sling, which doesn't evoke much awesomeness, and it's just literally David vs Goliath. It introduces a promising Doom mechanic, and then never uses it in interesting ways. I cannot imagine making a character that actually uses this, just because it's so dull. 2/5

                                                    Heartsong:
                                                    Story is okay. Not as dull as the previous few, but not really gripping either. But I love these evocations. It uses the under-used Custom Gambit concept, and then builds on it and ties in some other lesser-used aspects of social interaction as well. It's missing a really great capstone to bring it together though. 4/5

                                                    Heaven and Earth Gauntlets:
                                                    Pretty interesting story, and a plausible reason for a player to have them. Evocations are also pretty good, working with Smash attacks, but also not really great. Capstone needs a few more examples too. 3/5

                                                    Irenio's Bell:
                                                    Interesting story, and a great fluff description. Its defensive, and most of its powers are reactive rather than proactive, but still seem fun to use. I worry that its ability to break non-artifacts and summon second circle demons is too powerful for a simple evocation, but you get them at later Essence levels anyway. 3.5/5

                                                    Rainwalker:
                                                    It's an artifact umbrella shield, with a decent origin story. Combine that with good evocations and ties in to Water's concepts of flow and redirection, and I really like the result. 4/5

                                                    Razor Dancer and Wise Steel:
                                                    Gah. It's a fantastic concept for evocations - basically rooting a new martial art within the weapons - but its wasted on a "new" artifact as an excuse to not write up more evocations. Hugely disappointing in the end, I want to see more of this. 2/5

                                                    Summer Thunder:
                                                    Pretty weak story, but an interesting combination of effects for evocations. Horse archery is cool, but relatively unexplored. I like its growth from a simple skirmishing weapon to a true war-leader's symbol later on. The lack of truly unique effects holds it back somewhat. 3.5/5

                                                    Sun's Brush:
                                                    Another fantastic concept, a weaponized brush. It ties combat and lore together nicely, and has a full suite of cool evocations. Nothing really jumps out at me as character-defining, but its all useful and fun. 4/5

                                                    Tusk of Galaech-Ma:
                                                    Boring story, interesting mechanics. It's about what you'd expect from "spear used to hunt things", but it's solid overall. 3/5

                                                    4-Dot Weapons

                                                    Asphodel:
                                                    A really great concept and story behind the first intelligent artifact in the book. Its evocations are fine, if a bit plain, but they serve the story well. The biggest drawback is the storyteller complexity tied to the bargains and retainers 4/5

                                                    Burning Branch:
                                                    Another cool story, and a great variety of evocations. Its combination of fire and green jade make for some unique abilities, but I don't like its heavy use of Disarm, which is a gambit that makes combats less interesting. 3.5/5

                                                    Flying Silver Dream:
                                                    A cool artifact, and one that may fight better than your character. It adds complexity to combat by having essentially a second character to control, and its capstone is essentially undefeatable except by ST fiat. I love the concept, but I doubt I would want it in my game. 3/5

                                                    Gnomon:
                                                    Another great artifact, it combines an interesting story with an evocation tree that really lives up to the idea that 4-dot artifacts create their own style of fighting. Its time-twisting powers couple well with its larceny support, and the capstone is a unique story addition. 4/5

                                                    Mistweaver:
                                                    Also fantastic. Its story provides a usable seed for an adventure, and it creates a unique opportunity for stealth combat. I like the way it uses the mists to enhance using stealth in combat while also providing ways to counter it so that it doesn't become one sided. A great artifact 4/5

                                                    Nightmare Shard:
                                                    This one's just alright. It's got a cool concept, but its heavy focus on fighting the wyld makes it very niche. Also, its infection angle doesn't seem very useful, as it slowly drives victims mad over the course of days. 3/5

                                                    Sekhem:
                                                    Love the concept and most of the Heat system. I really don't like the random chance to basically lose the artifact until downtime though, and would definitely house rule in an alternate penalty for Cracking. Other than that, it's really solid and a great fire weapon. 3.5/5

                                                    Shipbreaker:
                                                    It breaks ships. That's about it, which is disappointing for a 4-dot artifact. You don't want to invest this much into a weapon that only works against huge enemies, because outside of specific campaigns that's not going to come up very often. 2.5/5

                                                    Sirrush:
                                                    Starts off so cool, and then crashes down. It's an air-aspected flamepiece that doesn't fall into the idea of shooting lightning, and it gets some cool abilities with that combination. But then it starts using Ammunition Checks as a mechanic, which approximately 0 people have ever used. Terrible idea to base half an evocation tree around 2/5

                                                    Strife's Crucible:
                                                    An amazing story, concept and fighting style. This feels more like an NPC artifact than a PC one, but leads to interesting stories either way so I like it. 4/5

                                                    Sunflash:
                                                    An incredible artifact. Its themes remain strong throughout, and it provides great story hooks in the form of Kiragaru. Unfortunately, it doesn't really describe the god, which isn't good given that the gods appearance and personality are part of the evocations. 4/5

                                                    Talion:
                                                    Another really good artifact. It's got a great story potential, and fits a common character archetype. Its evocations and story mesh nicely together, but the evocations feel a little lacking overall. 4/5

                                                    Vainglory:
                                                    A well done social/combat artifact, it ties its themes together very well. I like the Conceit system, although it could do with a couple more evocations hanging off of it to modify the value. While the final tree is a bit lacking, it still feels solid with great themes. 4/5

                                                    Zelator:
                                                    Incredible weapon, I just wish we knew more about the Hexmanse. It's also exactly what you would wish for in an anti-sorcerer weapon, being able to both fight directly and lead armies. Being a weapon used against sorcerers and also buffing sorcery feels a bit weird though. 4.5/5

                                                    5-Dot Weapons

                                                    The Forgotten Blade:
                                                    I'm not a big fan of the concept of this weapon, but I recognize that other people are, and this is a very well done execution of the idea. 4/5

                                                    Gorgon:
                                                    Awesome imagery, it's a great example of a demon weapon done right. I wish it did something with the people turned to stone, but it's an army killer 5-dot. 4.5/5

                                                    Soulfarer:
                                                    Well, this is the artifact for people who want to do combat sorcery. It's a pretty classic soul-drinker weapon combined with sorcery stuff that feels like it may be OP but I'm not sure. I'd have preferred it if it stuck more with just the soul-drinking rather than working sorcery in, but it's a great artifact either way. 4/5

                                                    Stormcaller:
                                                    A really cool storms artifact, similar to a concept I did in a previous game. Intensity works because it provides multiple paths to improvement and ties in to most of the evocations. I do worry that it will lead to some bad blood among players, since it leads to friendly fire. And did we really need 3 "evil bound demon weapons" in the 5-dots? 4.5/5

                                                        So, my last word on the book:

                                                        ​Magic items, especially weapons, are always a hard sell to me. Even on their own, I think they often have difficulty expressing a capability that really makes them feel worthwhile or lively, and in a game system they have a difficult balancing act between effectively conveying those powers and being balanced mechanically. That gets even more complicated when they're part of a setting in which other forms of magic exist; the distinction between what a device can do and a person (or whatever other forms of magic) can always seem fuzzy, and they often feel redundant against one another.

                                                        ​The basic concept of Evocations felt from the beginning like a very good way to navigate this, and the core book felt like it had good potential, but with room for development; not enough examples to paint a full picture, but still a firm foundation.

                                                        ​This book has really brought them home. It's more than them just feeling legitimately powerful, realising the potential of Evocations to give an Artifact powers that are nuanced and expansive, without being all over the place, and either meaningfully different from what their wielders can do on their own or having a sense of alignment with them. It's the way that the Artifacts really feel alive, dripping with personality, history, purpose, and they help bring the setting to life along with them. And there's enough variety here to not only intrigue in their own right, but provoke the imagination for what other Artifacts could be; I've always believed in the credo of them all being unique, but this provides a clearly grasped foundation to actually realise that.

                                                        And to have extended this as far as warstriders is a real achievement.

                                                        ​It's more than just something that made magical weapons feel real and meaningful to me; it's a book that excites me to the same degree that Masters of Jade did, and I can think of no higher praise than that.

                                                        ​To the developers and writers, I say well done, and thank you.
