                                Essentially, when Obama got the Iranians to put their nuclear program on hold, America was dominating Iran, but I'd hardly say this makes Iran part of an American Empire.

                                Sure. But I'm not arguing about this.

                                My point isn't that economic, cultural, religious, etc, domination doesn't exist. You said "but what about cultural and polticial domination?" My point is not that this is irrelevant, but that your examples of political and cultural domination are not Empires, just like they're not, say, theocracies, or nation-states (Japan is a nation-state. South Korea is a republic, not an Empire, despite its dominance in Asian pop culture. Egypt is a republic, not an Empire, despite the dominance of its TV in the Arabic world.

                                    Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
                                    Imperialism is evil. The setting needs to have an empire to provide tension and conflict for stories.
                                    Yes... but I can see where people are coming from.

                                    A purely monolithic empire of evil is quite dull, and simplistic. It should be one where you can tell stories with some sympathy for either side, even if you ultimately say "yes, side A are the bad guys."

                                    Especially when it's a common theatre of play for groups (in a way that, say, loyal Abyssals of a Deathlord probably isn't).

                                    One of my friends made the comparison to the Imperium of Man. We used to play Dark Heresy, and of course in that you are the torture-police of a xenophobic, brutal, oppressive empire of evil. The Imperium is bad, no question. But that doesn't mean every member is evil, or that, say, when our PCs were fighting chaos cultists or genestealers or brain-eating aliens on behalf of the Imperium, we weren't doing something good.

                                    Similarly, even if the game overall says "the Realm is evil", that doesn't mean you can't have examples of Dragonblood saving peasants from Raksha or the Undead or barbarian hordes led by twisted were-beasts who gain power by eating people's hearts.

                                    And that's before you get into people believing the propaganda, and, as was mentioned earlier, good people genuinely believing that they must kill Solars, or they should serve the Dragonblood because they're perfected demigods, etc.


                                    Now, personally, I didn't actually read WFHW and think "the Realm is a snidely-whiplash villain who loves eeeeevil", so saying "the Realm needs to be shown more sympathetically" isn't something I'm worried about. But clearly other people got that impression, and I can understand why they'd argue for a more sympathetically presented Realm, for reasons other than Imperial apologism.

                                            When people say "The Realm is Evil", they use Evil as a shortcut word. The Realm is not actually Evil, it's a huge nation with policies that adversely effect the world while consolidating power for itself. It has good people in it and bad people in it. Some of the places it touches are likely made better by it, but that's also a simplistic shortcut. "Better" for some people but not for others. The inverse is also true.

                                            The Realm's huge population lives in relative peace and prosperity. That should count for something. They spread peace and -well...not prosperity- at the point of a spear to other nations. Would the rest of the world be a better place if the Realm ruled over all of it and didn't have to worry about external groups going to war with it? Yeah, probably not, but for some definitions of better, perhaps.

                                            And just because you're from a place that tends to have a negative impact on the rest of the world, that doesn't mean you can't be a hero.
