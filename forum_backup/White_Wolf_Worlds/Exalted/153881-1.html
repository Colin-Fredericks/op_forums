            The Outcaste are basically the best thing in Exalted (besides Alchemicals, toward whom I carry an uncommon bias). I've played several and never regretted it.

            The Realm and Lookshy carry a fair amount of baggage with them, being soldiers or sorcerer-engineers or aristocrats but generally having a structure to build their live around. (And, to their tremendous advantage, other Dragon-Bloods to back them up.)

            The Outcaste can do absolutely, positively anything they want on a level that even Solars can't manage. I play one who is a paladin. I played another who was a farmer, because that's what he wanted to do. (Speed Farmer irrigates Cecelyne, saves the world.) Yet another who was from a long-standing river trader family (with DBs of their own besides her) that were rivals to the Guild. They are dragon-blooded freed of the soldiery and aristocratic trappings, who have the whole world available to them. And though they can be the greatest allies to your Solars, they are also liable to be the fiercest opponents to a return to days of old because they are chefs and chieftans, warriors and wanderers, matrons and madmen and masons, assassins and artists and accountants and you will never get them to go back to being soldiers.

            *cough*

            So, er, yeah. Independent Dragon-Bloods are everywhere, doing everything, and they are awesome and I hope to Burning Feather that Ex3e gives us them in all their glory.

                                                        Originally posted by Crumplepunch View Post
                                                        Zeniths are priest-king royalty, true... but the Dragon Blooded are still the nobility.
                                                        I love that the Dragon Blooded contain factors that incline them towards aristocratic social organisation and family planning priorities, but I'd also think that there were a lot of complicated and extended social and historical factors that went into them actual crystallizing these imperatives into being a collection of aristocratic families, only truly realised in the body of the Scarlet Dynasty (which is to say, daimyos or no daimyos, even the Shogunate hadn't fully gotten into it).

                                                        I imagine many places and long periods of the First Age in which the Celestial Exalted, and more than a few of the actual Terrestrials themselves, were irritated by how many of those Exalted took their hereditary nature as cause to band together, become insular and style themselves as a kind of aristocracy. I see many Terrestrials who didn't really care to base their own power on how many of their children they could get to Exalt (or at least placed higher priority on marrying into other powerful social groups, securing their own position with the promise of having members of their own people Exalt throughout the generations), who break away from highly traditionalist core family groups and thus have lower Breeding (and introducing bloodlines that persist in such).

                                                        And yet, they're still Exalted, they still have all the powers necessary to function as scholar-soldier-sodalt-bureaucrats of the highest order, and the sprawling Realm and all of its greatest heroes ready to make use of their talents, with ready institutions to scoop them up, bring them together and find work for them.

                                                        If there are as many Dragon Blooded outside of the Realm of the Age of Sorrows as there are within it, then that would seem to suggest you can get decent numbers at large without needing to buy very much into letting them act like an aristocracy, nor treat them as such. Good breeding only matters if you want to make sure that any given dozen Exalts are your children (or grandchildren or so on), and thus nominally have power over them, and that kind of... provincialism only really confers significant power in a time like the Age of Sorrows.

                                                        That's if you keep the numbers as they currently are; before the contagion, they might have been ten times greater, or a hundred. When there are hundreds of thousands of other Terrestrial Exalted are out there in the wider world (and there's an actual sense of connectivity to make there be a wider world), what does it matter if only one of your children inherits the power?

                                                        I see the mid-to-late First Age as a time in which the overwhelming trend of society for the Terrestrial Exalted was more like those animes where you've got mystical people in modern Japan who are all clannish and still acting as though their aristocratic sensibilities are relevant, and you've got the young rebel who wants to go out and be master of their own destiny. Except where the rebels are the majority, the mystical clans are known to society at large, and the greater trend setters give a lot more support to the rebels (even if the insular traditionalists are conferred with a degree of respect and protection, in honour of their services and those of their ancestors). The time when the stronger trend towards banding together in a way that makes family bonds more pronounced and valued is about the time in which they call themselves Dragon Blooded more than Terrestrial Exalted (in other words, when they start agitating for Usurpation).

                                                        So what I'm getting at is... Dragon Blooded are only really nobility when they want to present themselves as such, and that only really comes from the ones who are interested in rule (because it certainly helps to firmly establish your reign when you're being aided by a dozen or so deferential Exalts). The Scarlet Dynasty is the logical conclusion of Terrestrial Exalted who want to rule things, and even they have people who just don't buy into being socialized to want to rule, and go off into the morass of alternate ambitions found amongst the scattered, disorganized Dragon Blooded of the Threshold.

                                                        Originally posted by Crumplepunch
                                                        On the other hand, the Realm has spent a great deal of time and effort educating everyone that it has any influence over that the Dragon-Blooded are the ordained rulers of Creation and anything that they want is theirs by right. That's not a trivial influence over the Dragon-Blooded themselves or the people they would seek to rule.
                                                        One is in a bit of a Catch-22 there; any place where the Scarlet Dynasty has such a powerful conditioning influence is a place where Outcastes will have little recourse to reign over people, and any place that would be more open to Outcastes is a place that has likely been a bit resistant to (or distant from) Realm indoctrination.
