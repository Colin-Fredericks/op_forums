Guess what They still are, Only difference is that the Lunar player just have to wait longer for fun stuff compared to others, that still will pull ahead and get their fun stuff faster.
                                                I imagine that experience costs such as this are predicated on the idea that it being slightly harder to build up excessively powerful combinations alternately creates an incentive to focus one's character building elsewhere, or creates a delay that allows a smaller number of powers that produce power proportionate to somebody else's larger number of Charms that are less powerful or create less powerful combinations, so that they functionally remain on an equal footing even if that emerges from different numbers of powers.

                                                ​Think about it; you're granting the possibility of powerful combinations, so the question becomes one of whether or not the Lunar player is able to build those combinations at a fast enough rate that they actually pull ahead of other players that have spent an equal quantity of experience points (or have otherwise all been playing for the same length of time).

                                                What you're saying with "it doesn't make a difference if it's too powerful, because it's still going to be too powerful when you finally do reach it"...

                                                ​Imagine if Solar Circle Sorcery was available at Essence 1 through Supernal Occult. In a certain sense, it actually is more powerful at the earlier stage then it would be by the time that it is possible to buy it, in the sense of how much the chronicle has developed, how far-reaching characters are in the early stories, and how one player is measured against all of the others. Solar Circle would be a lot easier to get early than many other Essence 5 powers, since it only has two prerequisites. Think about what that would mean at character creation; three Charms and one spell, not even a third of your starting power suite, and now you can summon Ligier from the word go. Sure, Ligier is going to be just as powerful by the time you get to him, but there's such a thing as narrative progression and an escalation curve.

                                                ​That, by the way, is the manner in which I assess experience costs; it's not a measure of value, it's a pacing method.

                                                ​As for Lunars, I feel as though, if they're unable to get favoured costs for Martial Arts and sorcery, that's a necessary offset for how Attribute Charms work. Lunars are probably going to still have about as many Charms as the Dragon Blooded at the very least, but they're divided between 9 Attributes rather than 25 Abilities. No matter what way they slice it, every dot of an Attribute opens the door to about three times as many Charms as a dot of an Ability does for Solars and Terrestrials. Come to think of it, I think my prior query about a given Lunar having access to a large and diverse arsenal might still hold true, just that rather than being based on the Charms being cheaper, it would come from it being a lot easier for Charms to be the only thing that they need to worry about spending XP on. They're going to be able to get discounted costs on the dots and Charms of four out of nine of those Attributes. I think that there may be a few ways in which the numbers there put Lunars at a slight advantage, even if the costs of individual Charms aren't easier.

                                                ​I think that would be necessarily offset by that not including Martial Arts and spells in the range of things that one can get at a slightly faster rate.
