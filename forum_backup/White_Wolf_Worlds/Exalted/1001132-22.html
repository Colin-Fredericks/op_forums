                                                        It seems that most people want to send Masanori off, to avoid fighting right now.
                                                        Sounds good.

                                                        -------------------------

                                                        You turn from the doorway, where you were lost in thought.

                                                        “….. Go back to your men, Masanori. Prevent this attack.” As he starts to rise, you grab him by the shoulder and jerk his face up close to yours, eyes inches apart.

                                                        “Do not betray me, Masanori. I take to those who abuse my trust about as well as you could expect…”

                                                        He smiles slightly, nodding, “yes, yes, of course my Lord. If I were to betray you, you would capture me, do various unpleasantries to my body, and show my broken corpse in public. I know the drill, have no fear.”

                                                        You let go, standing back ,” Just about how many men do you think would cross sides?”

                                                        He shrugs, “ I dunno, probably two hundred or so? Maybe more? I am willing to bet most of those who cross sides come from my own crew. They are the only ones I would trust, anyways, and that is saying a lot.”

                                                        “….Alright. I expect to see you in about a week. Dismissed.” And, with a grandiose bow, Masanori and his men leave, in quite a bit of a hurry.

                                                        As soon as they leave, Aya Cho rushes over , “Are you wounded, Lord Kasai?”

                                                        You shrug, feeling around your cheek, “No, but it was close. Remind me to stop doing stuff like that…..”

                                                        “Was letting him go a ….wise decision, my Lord?”

                                                        “Maybe, maybe not. He kind of put me on the spot…. Which is almost-certainly why he brought an army here. He is smarter than he looks.” You look over to the barkeep, who is still agitatedly waving his crossbow about.

                                                        “Want to put that away, goodman? I’d rather not take a crossbow bolt to the stomach.”

                                                        “Not until someone tells me what is going on!”

                                                        “You insolent son of a peasant dog! Do you know to whom you speak? I should take your head for your words alone, much less thre..”

                                                        “Aya, relax. I did almost just cause a bloodbath in his bar, his suspicion is warranted.” Walking over to the barkeep, “ I am Kasai Qianke, and I am the Lord of Jianghu.”

                                                        “I…..what….oh fuck.” He looks at the crossbow in his hand, as if seeing it for the first time. Hurriedly, he puts it away back under the bar. “My deepest apologies, My Lord.”

                                                        You wave him off. “Not a problem.” Turning back to your retainers, “ Now, I may be an optimist, but I am not that naïve. Just because we let Masanori go doesn’t necessarily mean he will keep his side of the bargain. That said, I want the mustered levy brought inside the palisade, as soon as possible, just in ca…”

                                                        It is at this point that the Liangzhou militia shows up, armed with clubs and staves. They flood the taproom, meaning it takes quite a few more minutes before any meaningful work is done (You have to explain who you are more than seven times!). Finally, the militia starts to filter out, and you hurriedly rush back to the muster camp.

                                                        There, you snag Sgt. Maj. Oxblood and explain the situation. He curses liberally, glaring up at the heaths overlooking the town, and at the assembled levy.

                                                        “How they managed to sneak that close, I don’t know. I had patrols around the perimeter, Lord Qianke, evidently they weren’t paying attention….”, fixing the levy with a glare that would’ve soured milk and made women barren.

                                                        “From what Masanori said, he had his troops dispersed in groves and hills across a few square miles of empty country. I am not surprised they weren’t found, especially if the patrols only kept close to the Liangzhou walls. No harm, no foul, at least this time.”

                                                        Once Sgt. Oxblood relaxes a bit, you continue, “ Once the rest of the levy and militia gets here, I want to make a move on the bandit-held fortress of Dun Nashuon. How feasible is that, and how long do you think we can get there?”

                                                        He rubs his chin, “ Attacking a fortified point? Eh…… the Men-at-Arms levy is ready, and the militia is moving along nicely, although I wouldn’t say they are quite ready yet. It isn’t that they lack skill, more that they lack cohesion, drill. They aren’t used to fighting as a unit yet, and their officers and sergeants lack experience and knowledge of the unit both. But….. so long as you don’t just throw them into a battle-line, they should be fine.”

                                                        You nod, “ Understood. How long will it take to make a move on the fortress?”

                                                        He shrugs, “How far away is it, and what sort of terrain do we have to march through?”

                                                        Shin Takeshi chimes in, “The closest point to the Toge Hills is the Bulrush Creek, which is several days away, about halfway between Liangzhou and Yagura Castle. The lower parts of the flow are open, surrounded by farms and heathland, but the upper reaches are forested until the hills themselves.”

                                                        Sgt. Oxblood ponders that for a moment, “Well….. a good rule of thumb to keep in mind when marching armies is that the ‘base’ marching rate for trained troops is about ten to fifteen miles a day, over good terrain and with weather permitting. If the roads suck, or if it rains or is too hot, or if the troops aren’t trained in long-distance marching, the army slows down. Other than that, you have to keep logistics in mind. ‘An army marches on it’s stomach’, and all that, and you almost-always never want to leave your beans, bandages and boots straggling behind your forces if possible.”

                                                        “I prefer to keep a slow march, so the army doesn’t pull ahead of it’s own baggage train, or get all strung-out, and even then, if it is possible, have the troops carry a supply of materiel on them, like the Shogunate-of-old did. If every soldier has five days of food on them, you can afford to leave the wagons behind a few days. Most commanders don’t like that, they want to go go gogogogogogogo, marching troops to the bone and effectively making them fight tired and hungry. That is……inefficient, to say the least, but being able to strategically outflank the enemy with a forced march could be worth it……”

                                                        Despite the rather dry subject matter, you pay rapt attention, trying to soak up as much knowledge as possible. After all, you will be leading these forces one day, and you will take as much help as you can get! After listening to Sgt. Oxblood ramble on for a few minutes, mainly about logistics (In your old and dog-eared copy of the Thousand Correct Actions, the topic of logistics was referred to as “Heaven and Earth”), you politely interrupt.

                                                        “So….. since most of the roads in Jianghu are made of packed earth, it will take slightly longer for the men to march over them. When we get to the Akibara, the men will get slowed down even more, especially since the wagons will almost-certainly trail behind…”

                                                        He says to you, eyebrow raised, “With that in mind, M’Lord, how long do you think it will take to march to this fortress?”

                                                        “…… Erring on the side of caution, probably about a week to get to the Akibara forest, and another few days to pass through the forest?”

                                                        “Maybe less, M’Lord. I’ve travelled on the road between Liangzhou and Yagura Castle, it isn’t that bad, we could make good time. The forest……..ehhhh.” He shrugs. “We’ll have to see.”

                                                        “Will there be time to take the fortress, before the harvest season starts?”

                                                        He shrugs again, “It all depends on how fast we move. A siege will be ……iffy, but it is probably the safest option, with the least loss of life. An assault could be fast, over and done with in a day or two, but more people would die. We will have to see what the best option is when we get there.”

                                                        “Alright. Before we move out, however, we have to wait until the rest of the levy gets here. Plus, moving materiel beforehand would be preferable. Finally, I don’t quite trust Masanori Mitsuhide yet, and I still fear an attack. Let us move the muster inside the walls of Liangzhou.”

                                                        “Good idea, M’Lord. I’ll see to it.”

                                                        Thankfully, either Masanori was genuine, or he was scared off by your fortifications, and you watch the sunset pass with a sigh of relief. Over the course of the next day, the rest of the levy and militia wanders in, confused at how the rest of the army is standing guard over the palisade of Liangzhou. They settle in, with more than a little direction from Sgt Oxblood bawling orders (audible even from the town center), and when the sergeant is free, you sit with him to plan out the movement of your (you still aren’t used to the idea of such ‘things’ actually ‘belonging’ to you, even after a few weeks in Jianghu.) army. In addition, you dispatch Shin Takeshi, with a retinue of 100 miltiamen, to travel to Yagura Castle and pack up as much food from the vaults as possible, and to link up with the rest of the levy at the mouth of the Bulrush creek.

                                                        The next day, Sgt. Oxblood musters the whole levy in what he refers to as “parade formation”, and the town takes on an almost festival air. Citizens gather on top of the palisade wallwalk to watch the soldiers form up, and farmers at work in the fields stop to watch. Even you have to admit as you ride up mounted on Xuansu, that there is a hell of a lot of people here. When you bring this up to the Sergeant, he chuckles,

                                                        “M’Lord, begging your pardon, but this isn’t that many soldiers. The Lord Iysen has about 10,000 Men-at-Arms, and believe you me, that is a sight to see when they get fully mustered. The ground shakes when they march.”

                                                        You stay silent, instead nudging Xuansu forwards. You ride out in front of the assembled regiments. It feels like every eye is on you (they must be, in all seriousness), and it takes you a moment to find your voice.

                                                        “ Good evening. My name is Kasai Qianke, and I am the Lord Paramount of the Jianghu Marches.” At this, everyone, with a rattle of arms and armor, takes a knee. “ I am sure you are wondering why you have been mustered? The answer is this: The bandits in Jianghu have been ever-bold, marauding across the countryside, stealing crops and burning farms. For too long have you suffered under their rapacity, and it ends now!”

                                                        “The largest, boldest and most troublesome group of bandits has a stronghold in the Toge Hills, an ancient hillfort known as Dun Nashuon. By taking it, we both deny the fortress to the enemy, and send a message to the other bandit-gangs in Jianghu: ‘We will not be cowed any longer!”. We will break their backs here, my soldiers, and make Jianghu safe for your families and children!”

                                                        At this, the assembled soldiers break out cheering, and Sgt. Oxblood blows a brassy blast on a bugle. The signal is taken up by the other buglers in the army, and the sound echoes across the river valley. With some shouted orders, the levy heads out, and you buttonhole the Sergeant as you pass by at the head of the column.

                                                        “How was that, Sergeant?”

                                                        He grins, “Alright, M’Lord. A little short, with a lot of big words, but good enthusiasm.”

                                                        “Ugh. I hate public speaking.”

                                                        “Better get used to it. Most troops expect a speech before, after and even sometimes during a battle. When they don’t get the speech is when they get worked up. The familiar is comforting, you know?”

                                                        You sigh, already ruing what is to come.

                                                        Surprisingly, the clear weather holds, and even the humidity stays at bay (which is usual for Jianghu, you are told), and the levy makes good time. Only a day or so after leaving Liangzhou, however, you start coming across farmers and fieldsmen streaming west back towards Liangzhou, terror and panic writ large in their body language. When you stop a few of them and ask them just what the hell they are running from, the universal reply is:

                                                        “The Tribes!”

                                                        A little perturbed (just what the hell have the Tribes been doing?), you keep the army moving east, and in spite of your predictions, the army isn’t slowed down all that much by the dirt track you call a road, even with the refugees travelling in the opposite direction. Only 5 days after leaving Liangzhou, you crest over a hill and take in the sight of the Bulrush running into the White River. Lifting your eyes from the river to the hill beyond, you can’t help but let your jaw drop in surprise.

                                                        That has to be the largest number of Hill-Tribe warriors you have ever seen!

                                                        The entire eastern side of the Bulrush, from shore to forest, is one massive encampment, with fires and shelters sprouted like weeds. Your militia under Shin Takeshi is already here, as evidenced by the white canvas tents in orderly rows, down by the ford, but the Hill-Tribe warriors look to outnumber the Vanehani several times over.

                                                        Your Oathsworn retainers, rising over the crest behind you, catch a glimpse of what drew you up short, and you hear several curses of various degrees of blasphemy. Aya Cho rides up, and in a stunned tone mutters,

                                                        “When you said the Tribes would help take the fortress, My Lord, I didn’t think they meant this level of help!”

                                                        “I know it.” you answer, still in shock. “I…..I guess they really want Dun Nashuon. There must be 500 warriors here, at least!”

                                                        After hanging up on top of the rise, you direct the army down towards the ford. Once you get closer, you see Shin Takeshi ride towards you, accompanied by several others, whom you guess to be representatives of the Tribes.

                                                        Once they get closer, you entrust Sgt. Oxblood with the setting-up of camp and go to meet with the worthies. In the shade of a larger pavilion-tent, you meet with the representatives of the Paskamansett, the Nemasket and the Onset.

                                                        One by one, the sub-chiefs of the tribes kneel before you (which is a surprise in and of itself, to be honest), and the sub-chief of the Paskamansett intones,

                                                        “Hail, sachem (<chief>). Us warriors join you to take back the ancient home of Dun Nashuon.”

                                                        “Uh…..<Well, thank you for coming, but I must say that I am surprised by just how many people showed up. I thought most of the warriors were with Ousamequin and the Vanehani?>”

                                                        He grins, exposing the boar-tusks “< Most of the warriors fighting with Ousamequin are those with something to prove. While some of the Chosen went with our chiefs, most stayed home, to assist and protect the relocation of our people. They, along with the youngbloods that have been causing problems, are here now.>”

                                                        “< But why do many?>”, asks Shin Takeshi.

                                                        “< Your chief helped us, Vanehani. We are just repaying the favor. As for the Nemasket and the Onset…..>”

                                                        “< In addition to giving us back a piece of our history and culture, Chief of Jianghu, you are seeking to make your land safe, both for your people and for ours. Why would we not help?>

                                                        “< Besides,>” says the Onset sub-chief with a vicious smirk, “< You did promise a spearhead or arrowheads for every bandit-head we bring you. Why go through the effort of hunting them down when they are all here?>”

                                                        You take this in over a couple of moments.

                                                        “<So…. What happened to make my people flee in terror?>”

                                                        The sub-chiefs share a look, then, “<Nothing we did, Chief. All we have done so far was encamp here, the farmers fled on their own.>”

                                                        “<And…. How are you feeding yourselves?>”

                                                        “< We have brought food, from the last of the winter stores. On top of that, the youngbloods are hunting in the woods for game, as well as cooling their ardor by wiping out any of the bandit-scum they come across. The last tally was……. 100 spears, I think.>”

                                                        Walking out of the pavilion, you look up at the Bulrush creek and the Akibara rising behind. Only a few feet into the treeline, the light dims and sight becomes almost impossible.

                                                        Turning back to the assembled sub-chiefs, you ask, “<Are you fully prepared to move out? Harvest-time is in two weeks, and I would rather not be late…..>”

                                                        “< We have been waiting for you, to be honest.>” At your look of surprise, he continues, “< You Vanehani are slow, and we have not forgotten the forest-tracks, nor the gods that walk them. It only took us a week to get here from the mountains, and we have been here for 4 days already.>”

                                                        “<Well, we won’t be ready to move again until tomorrow.> Shin, please inform the men to be ready to move by daybreak.” After he leaves, “< You said you had your youth-bands in the Akibara already? Have they brought back word of what the terrain is like?>”

                                                        “< Indeed, Chief. They say almost all of the wood is severely overgrown, with trees and bamboo and brambles, and only the banks of the creek itself are passable without a great deal of brushcutting. Even those open areas took a great deal of time to pass through, and the youth-bands are faster and more agile than your Vanehani fighters. It is largely flat, however, until you get to the hills at least.>”

                                                        You sigh, looking back at the Akibara again. This forest could make or break the campaign.
