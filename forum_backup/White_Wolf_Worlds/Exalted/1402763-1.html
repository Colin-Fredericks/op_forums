        Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post
        What if the bargain roll manipulates an intimacy? “You don’t want to go tomb raiding for my silver? That’s a shame. You know, I hear that rebellion isn’t doing so well. So many rebellions these days falter due to lack of funding. Running out of food, weapons, bribing officials. Tsk-tsk, poor management all around. Not that any of you care, I’m sure you’re not connected to such a bloody revolution. Oh you’re interested now? *pointedly puts a hand on the chest and drags it back to his side of the table* the tomb, and its prize. Then you get my coin.”

        There we go, now we roll bargain. This isn’t a persuade roll, he’s not telling them go go raid the tomb for their own sake, he wants something out of it. A persuade roll there would be “go raid that tomb, it’s never been opened and I know you ‘can’t resist a mystery’ right?”. He’s getting something out of it, it’s a transaction.

        I object to players just being able to say “no he didn’t convince me, no roll.” just because they don’t want to be convinced.
        Why is the player no-selling it?

        If they just don't want to play Exalted, then rolling against their character's Resolve isn't going to help the game. You just get a reluctant player feeling railroaded through your plot. It's not legitimate to say "no you're not playing properly and I'm rolling 15 dice with ten automatic non-Charm successes anyway! Ha! I convinced you!" You need to discuss it OOC. Honestly, it's something you should have discussed on Session 0.

        If they have a legitimate reason to think that the Bargain action is insufficient despite hitting an Intimacy (e.g. they have Defining support for the revolution, but are pretty sure robbing this tomb is a suicide mission and they're not convinced that a chest of silver maybe is going to help), then the player gets to no-sell the roll. Just like you can't use the Defining Intimacy of Revolution on any random Persuade roll -- the roll has to actually, legitimately call on the Intimacy in question. You can't say "You love your wife, eh? Well there's nothing wives love more than regicide..."

        If the player does think the offer is sufficient and wants funds for the rebellion... why are they rolling? Go rob that tomb!

        Generally speaking, it would be my expectation that if someone is offered a Bargain that aligns with their Intimacies, the player accept the Bargain. If it's a grey area (perhaps because there are Intimacies supporting and opposing the Bargain), then maybe roll for it. But I wouldn't expect that to happen more than a couple of times a Story.

            It’s for exactly the same reason that Persuade exists. If I can just say “My character can’t resist a mystery, he’s telling me the tomb is mysterious, so I’m going for it!” Then that’s it and there’s no need to bust out the system. If it’s dangerous, and I’ve heard that actually it has been broken into before and there’s nothing in it anyway, and I might want to go do something else that’s also kind of pressing right now, then the silver tongued devil whispering in my ear is what convinces me. He uses words to fill my character’s mind with visions of lost lore and treasures, all while soothing my reservations by saying how the people who supposedly broke in before were simple folk who couldn’t possibly have understood what they saw or even gotten past the outer door.

            When it’s a bargain roll the roll is there to convince you to take this deal, you need it, you can only get it from me, and it has to be here and now. You think the rebellion’s war chest is doing fine, but is it? You’re dealing with rebels, they don’t necessarily all think stealing is wrong what if they start skimming the war chest? What if mistakes get made? It happens so often. Building fears in you, anxiety, filling your head with visions of running out of cash two steps before the finish line, and having your revolution crushed and the oppression return with a literal vengeance. This, though, this silver alone will fund your armies through months, maybe a full year, of incursions.

            Do you ACTUALLY need it? Maybe not, but that dude sure made a good case for it. If r beat your resolve anyway. If not you’re free to shrug and say “I think we’ll be fine, thanks.” and walk away

                                                        Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post

                                                        Who decides if Oscar is in a good mood or a bad mood? Who decides if Oscar is going to step up?
                                                        The player decides if Oscar wants to step up. The dice decide if Oscar does step up. If they didn't, the player might as well decide everything.
                                                        Why does the player then not get to decide whether his sword strike hits that death knight? How dare you force upon the player the decision of having missed his attack?

                                                        I contend that the player decides what their character does. The player decides if they're going to rescue the princess or guard the door or go get high.
                                                        The player decides what his character wants to do. If the player gave his character a flaw of drug addiction, it really isn't too much abuse to then ask him to periodically roll for it.

                                                        But Oscar isn't a character in a book. And he's not a real person. He's MY character. I make the decisions for my character.

                                                        In combat, I decide whether I take an Aim action or an Attack action. You don't get to say "Aiming is unrealistic, you have to Attack". I get to choose what my character does.

                                                        If you insult my character, I decide whether I laugh it off or challenge you to a duel. That's the point of me playing.
                                                        But if you take the aim action, and then attack, you have to roll your attack to see if it hits. Why is it so outrageous to you that a character with intimacies that might make him a bit of a choleric twat might need to roll to stay cool and laugh off that insult?
                                                        Exalted gives you all the tools, if you want to be cool as a cucumber then give yourself an intimacy that makes insults roll off you, if you want a hothead, have an intimacy that makes it hard for you to ignore insults.

                                                        I get where you're arguing from and you're not wrong, I just don't get why you insist on skipping this huge middle ground between your position and the straw man opposition of ST dictatorship ruining your fun.

                                                        As for "following the rules" the rules say, in black and white, that the offer must be "worth the difficulty or danger of the task you’re asking him to perform". I am advocating that you follow the rules -- you must offer something which is worth it.

                                                        The question is, who decides what's worth it?

                                                        If the player decides that an offer is worth it, just accept the offer. If the player decides it isn't worth it, then according to the rules, the Bargain fails.

                                                        If the ST decides that Oscar is having a bad day and the opium hit seems worth it to him, then sure that blows the middle ground a mile wide. But I contend that this shouldn't happen -- the player should decide whether Oscar is having a bad day.
                                                        Maybe my argument for middle grounds was too much of a middle ground. I agree that the ST just saying "you're having a bad day, sucks for you" sounds like the ST being a jerk, but let's just pretend I phrased this more elegantly, and we can probably agree that there might be days where Oscar might say yes, and days where he might say no, right?
                                                        I feel like rolling dice is an OK abstraction on deciding what kind of day you're role playing at this instant.

                                                        Oscar and Sally don't exist. John, the player, and Chesire, the ST, exist. Oscar doesn't get a say: John decides what is and isn't important to Oscar, and John decides if Oscar is going to step up or shirk off. Because Oscar isn't real. Oscar doesn't want to descend into the Labyrinth and foil the plots of the thousand year old Deathlords. Oscar doesn't want anything. Oscar exists purely as a vessel for me to tell a story. I invented Oscar. I made him a drug addict. I made him a functioning drug addict. I know exactly how strong his addiction is, because I'm the one who invented him. I know how much he cares about guarding a door, because I'm the one who decided he cares about guarding the door. I should be the one who decides if he cares more about guarding the door or getting high.
                                                        I feel like you absolutely get to decide all this at character creation, and your intimacies should reflect that. But if there is no mechanism of conflict you accept as overriding your player agency, then you might as well not play. Oscars struggle with addiction is not a worthwhile flaw, or interesting character trait, if it is impossible to leverage for conflict.
                                                        And to put on my pretentious RPG nerd hat: if you created Oscar with love and passion, then it should feel as if Oscar does want things, and it should immediately resonate with you what that is.                                                         Oscar is treated as a character in the story with as much agency as any non-trivial character, whether PC or NPC, for story integrity (your mileage might vary by table) - and you can certainly argue with your ST about what a sufficient amount of temptation is to force a roll, and I certainly think an ST who is making you roll to do incredibly dumb shit vs very little reward is abusing the bargaining system, I agree with you there.
                                                        But I feel like you're making this more absolute than it has to be. And if ST and Player can't agree, the player can always spend willpower, which is exactly the resource the game gave us to have characters not do stuff they might reasonably be doing.

                                                        And if your Oscar isn't that hardcore of an addict, or still has intimacies besides getting high that are strong, maybe even a defining sense of duty, that bargain roll will be quite tough to succeed at anyway. Bargain rolls aren't supposed to be auto-win, they're supposed to let you trade something in place of leveraging an intimacy.

                                                        For NPCs, this is a completely different equation. For NPCs, the ST has discretion and the middle ground is truly enormous.

                                                        If Sally is a PC and Oscar is an NPC, then roll away.

                                                        But there's a fundamental difference between NPCs and PCs.

                                                        Are you honestly saying that I shouldn't be allowed to say "My character, Oscar, doesn't think Sally's offer to let her pass for a hit of opium is worth the danger that puts him in"? You're saying I don't get to make that decision for my character? Who does get to make it?
                                                        I'm saying if your character has a Flaw of opium addiction, there might be a worthwhile discussion about whether he really doesn't want that opium. But TBH as you describe the situation, Oscar does care about the door, and it does put him in danger, so the bargain action would be asking for quite a serious task and might be opposed by strong intimacies, so the roll to convince Oscar might be really hard.
                                                        And even if it succeeds, you can spend some of Oscar's willpower for him.
