    So I'm doing this setting on a Giant City Boat, the same one that in the South West basically, and I want to go a little bit into the details of the population on this boat. This is supposed to be a huge town, not yet the size of Champoor, Palanquin and the like.

    At the moment I'm sitting on 3 major cultures living on this boat, with basically a population of 20 000 - 40 000 each. Do you have any insights (may they be historical, from the lore or from playing) about this topic ? (Number of people, what to think of when certain critical numbers are reached, etc ...)

        There's something I've been wondering. Solar Exalts, or any exalt with bureaucracy, can do wonders with organisation. They can purge corruption. They can make infallible plans. They can speed up projects that should take decades into months. They can move with efficiency undreamt of.

        So here's the thing. Let's say a Solar, bureaucracy supernal, with all the charms, currently is in charge of a famine alleviation. The food arrives on time, without loss, and everyone gets a fair share. Now here's the thing. If a solar can make a project move faster than what should be possible due to his own supernal excellence, can a solar make do with fewer resources than normal people? Can a solar, effectively, feed more people in a famine situation than what should be possible considering the numbers of people and the food supply?

            I think what you're describing is probably a bit too small, actually, unless you want nearly constant interaction between basically all the Exalts on the boat. It's not so much the population numbers you've got (which are actually very large - you've got just shy of 100,000 people, which is a metropolis at the tech level that Creation generally exists at). Rather, it's the physical size. If all these people are on one giant boat, they're going to be packed in pretty tight, and there won't be much distance between them. I'd estimate the whole thing would probably be no more than 5 kilometers across (for reference, the medieval city of Paris, one of the largest cities in Europe at the time, packed around 300,000 people into an area only about 10 km wide, and they'd have room to grow outward that a group on a boat just wouldn't.

            So, what's likely to happen is that every single conflict or encounter that makes noise (or, even more obviously, flares anima) will attract the attention of most other interested parties, who will probably want to arrive on the scene, if only to scope out their potential rivals/allies. And Exalted usually have powers that let them move reasonably quickly, meaning even someone who starts at the extreme edge of the ship-city could very well arrive in a matter of minutes, if not sooner. I'd expect most fights to snowball in such a circumstance, as various people arrive and pitch in on whichever side they feel they should be backing. If that's the feeling you want, that works! However, if that feels like it would be more involved than you're looking for...

            I'd suggest, as an alternative, making the setup be a bit more split up, space-wise. Instead of one single giant ship-city, make it several smaller boat-towns, that stay close to one another, but still keep significant distance between them in most circumstances, only "docking" with one another to trade or for major cultural events like festivals and ceremonies. Personally, I think that creates a more interesting situation in any case - if you have, say 10 large community ships, that gives you far more distinct governments and societies that you can engage the players' interests with, and makes for a more complicated social network, with the attendant increase in plot hooks. You can also tie each boat-town to just a few Exalted, making it much easier to manage which ones should be showing up at any given point.

                                                        Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post
                                                        I’m pretty sure I know the answer to this, but Speed the Wheels doesn’t give an entire project super speed, right? Just the paperwork or other more strictly bureaucratic tasks?

                                                        Actually, by the wording of Speed the Wheels, it does affect a project as a whole. It has to be one accomplished by a bureaucracy, is the catch. But that does cover a pretty wide range of things. I think that the restriction I would put on it is that it can't speed up the pace of anything accomplished purely with another skill. So if you were using it on a project to build a navy and were Essence 3 (hence three steps), what would normally take more than a season to accomplish would now take only a week, which would include procuring all the materials and personnel required, setting up any organization required, renting or buying construction facilities, etc. But actually building the ships themselves, which would be Craft, would still take the normal time.
