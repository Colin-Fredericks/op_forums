                Sure, but the techniques still have names. And Exalted is as much anime as it is wuxia, which is to say, inspired by both in addition to a lot of other things. There is nothing mechanically lost in people naming techniques and/or yelling them out, so why ban it? Why not just say "Some of the things that inspired Exalted have characters that do these things.                     Martial Arts Charms are explicitly named technique-widgets in-setting. Sidereal native Charms probably are as well. Solar native Charms are not, they are purely an abstraction of intangible skill, even the ones that don't necessarily fit that mould like Phantom Steed and GSS.

                                Brawl

                                Number of Charms: 49
                                By Ability requirement: 1/0/7/9/32
                                By Essence requirement: 11/11/19/5/3

                                Nonstandard dice tricks: Reroll 10s, reroll 5s and 6s until they disappear, free full Excellency, reroll 1s and 10s until they disappear.

                                Structure-wise, this is an odd one – less than a quarter of the Charms are accessible at E1. If my back-of-the-envelope numbers are right, Brawl has more Ability 5, Essence 3+ Charms than Awareness has altogether. It feels like an obvious Supernal candidate, again in contrast with Awareness.

                                Fists of Iron states that it lets you parry Lethal barehanded. Can you not, normally? I mean, you couldn’t in 2e, but I don’t remember that in the combat section.

                                This is a pretty offense-heavy Ability, but it’s not purely ATTACK ATTACK ATTACK – we get, for instance, a turnlong Onslaught negator, which is a pretty a big deal. Onslaught’s a pretty big thing for Brawl, actually: if your opponent has been piled on, you get more damage, more defense… Edit: And here’s an effect to make your Onslaught penalty stick around, and one to cripple them as long as the penalty sticks around,

                                Good grief. Heaven Thunder Hammer is an no-risk effect – that is, you pay to activate it after you hit with it. If you connect with a Decisive, you send the opponent flying. At 4 HLs of damage, you get to also drop the opponent from a medium height. Hey, remember those falling rules? The ones that make people, uh, explode? HTH is an Essence 1 source of falling damage on tap; if you ever let a Solar Brawler connect with you, be prepared for the ~4L you actually take to be supplemented with another ~7L of unavoidable damage that doesn’t have to be paid until afterwards. (The Charm says that E3, you double the successes for the purpose of determining how far someone is hurled. Does that modify their falling damage as well? It seems like it would have to – the two are linked – but that means a base-initiative, no-Charms-enhancing love-tap could stick on another 7L as a surprise after-effect. It’s also unclear whether the 7+ success effect is only applicable at E3+.)

                                Here’s a grappler preserving his turns of control, even if he gets attacked. I am getting the vibe that, once a Solar Brawler lands a Decisive hit with any amount of Initiative, you’re dead.

                                Thunderclap Rush Attack looks like a great dip – free extra movement, applicable for use with other Abilities than Brawl, which is cool. Aaaand then we get more of that “break ties in favor of the better stunt” thing.

                                Okay, this has got to be a goof. Solar Cross-Counter costs 1i (+ other stuff) and has the Perilous keyword. There’s a note that the Solar can spend 1wp to remove the Perilous keyword, i.e., to let it be used in Crash. But it still costs 1i, and Charms can’t pay Init costs below 0 unless they say they can, even without the Perilous keyword. Literally, this lets you pay 1wp to accomplish nothing.

                                Burning Fist Burial enhances an existing punch. I thought Reflexives didn’t do that? Also, it doesn’t specify whether it can be used with non-Brawl Abilities.

                                Ooh, Blade Rebuking Wrath is a disarming Charm!

                                I do like the “All the Essence 1 stuff first” ordering, but the resulting set does feel a little disjointed. Just came across an E2 grappling Charm, and was like, “Oh, yeah, that was a subtree, wasn’t it?”

                                Wow, speaking of good dips – Dancing With Strife Technique is a free WP for avoiding an attack with 5+ successes, with a not-impossible reset condition. That’s really handy. But why is this Reflexive-no-cost instead of Permanent – because of the reset? Why do we have both Reflexive-no-cost and Permanent as types?

                                Adamantine Fists of Battle – rerolls 10s, but doesn’t reroll the rerolls?

                                So, funny story: I remember one of the first fights I got into with Hatewheel – back when he was still relatively new and we all thought Exalted might be dead-ish except for Ink Monkeys – was over the Charm name “Cancel the Apocalypse.” I argued that “punch a dude real hard” was not the most straightforward effect to associate a name like that. Five years later, we’re all a little older and wiser, a little calmer, maybe, and yep, I am totally sticking by that assessment; it’s a ridiculous name.

                                I like that several of these effects reward having high Strength – Exalted needs more reason to have other Attributes than Dex.

                                Fire-Eating Fist lets you repeatedly juice up by blocking your opponent’s Elemental Bolts or similar. Er, who’s going to continue throwing elemental bolts after you eat the first one?

                                River-Binding Wrath is 2m for +.125 suxx/dice – i.e., about a quarter more successes than normal – on a grappling roll. Yep, grapplers will kill you.

                                I have no idea why Wicked Dissolve Dust has the name it has. Didn’t this used to smash up buildings or something? It’s a “put your captive between you and an incoming attack” Charm now.

                                Eh heh heh, Shockwave Technique lets you go bowling for enemies with a guy you have grappled. That’s fun. Fatal, probably, but fun.

                                The Ferocious Jab + Fivefold Fury Onslaught combo is somewhat reduced. Honestly, a little more concerned about HTH right now, but I mean, these are great tastes that taste great together – punch a guy six times, and if you ever hit, tack on an extra 7L for funsies. (By “great tastes” I mean “this ends most fights from base Initiative with the other guy exploding.”)

                                Striving Aftershock Method is the second half of that “reset to 6i” combo I pondered on the other day. (Rereading, I see this does not grant free attacks, the definition of the Reflexive keyword notwithstanding.) Here’s the pattern:
                                1. Pop Foe-Vaulting Method; make a surprise attack. Pay 3i; hit with a Decisive with whatever you have left. Land the hit.
                                2. Be a Dawn, with anima flaring. Reset to 4i.
                                3. Pop Striving Aftershock Method for 2m. Reset to 6i instead. Reset Foe-Vaulting Method.
                                4. Repeat as many times as desired; total cost to reduce your opponent’s Defense by 2 each turn is 2m. You won’t do much base damage with any particular hit, but you’re a brawler; you have options.
                                Obviously not an infinitely sustainable combo, since you have to hit in step 1. On the other hand, gambits are decisive attacks -

                                - aren’t they? P.199 says yes, they’re “a special sort” of decisive. P.183 just says they’re similar to decisives. Which is it? If it’s the former, you could spam grapples every turn; 1m for Vicious Lunge makes you almost certain to succeed at the attempt. Given that you will kill the enemy if you land the hit, and he’s at -2 Defense for surprise, that’s a pretty terrifying combination. Stack River-Binding Wrath if you want a virtually assured grapple success for 3m.

                                That’s… man. That’s terrifying. That’s 2e-style “if this hit lands, say goodbye to the fight (p.s. this hit will land).”

                                There’s an undodgeable, unblockable attack – first of those we’ve seen. Withering-only, though.

                                Orichalcum Fists of Battle requires having a turn to burn, but dang. Stack this with FFO – every attack gets +Essence dice and rerolls 1s and 10s until they disappear. (This Charm specifies it retains successes from rerolled 10s. Adamantine Fists of Battle didn’t. Is this significant, or just an oversight?)

                                Heaven Fury Smite gives you a free Decisive after crashing the enemy. Which… a grappler can do in one turn, trivially. I just… there is so very much lethal stuff here, I’m a bit overwhelmed.

                                … which brings us to Ascendant Battle Visage. “Her hair may lengthen and change colors” – yeah, I’ve seen enough anime to know etc.

                                Themes: Clashing. Grappling. Doing all of the damage, no matter how little Initiative you initially hit with.

                                Yes: There’s a lot of really powerful synergy here – you can feel how a lot of the Charms work together to give you this utterly unstoppable juggernaut damage-engine. Which sounds like great fun to play, but…
                                No: …I do not see any way to avoid being basically murdered by a grappler, at present. The on-contact damage potential is just so very high.
                                Just Plain Weird: Sledgehammer Fist Punch: “This attack is neither withering nor decisive.” Er, what? (It breaks stuff, not people, but still.)

                                        Bureaucracy

                                        Number of Charms: 26
                                        By Ability requirement: 2/1/3/2/18
                                        By Essence requirement: 11/5/7/2/1
                                        Nonstandard dice tricks:

                                        Hope you like Ability 5, kids! This is another one that feels like a pretty weak choice for Supernal – 3 Charms at Essence 4+?

                                        Okay, hot off the crazy murderdeath that is Brawl, let’s switch gears: Bureaucracy. I’m particularly interested to see what Bureaucracy Charms do in light of the… underwhelming?... leadership system earlier; what does it mean to be a Supernal Bureaucrat, in this setting?

                                        So, here’s the Charms at a glance:
                                        1. Detect quality of a good.
                                        2. Know how much you can sell a thing for.
                                        3. Know someone’s budget and whether he plans to cheat you.
                                        4. People can feel how great a merchant you are.
                                        5. Know what Bureaucracy specialties the people around you have.
                                        6. Scenelong - add Bureaucracy to a Read Intentions roll, sometimes.
                                        7. Unfailingly sense people’s Intimacies towards an organization.
                                        8. Sense when someone tries to turn a member of an organization against it.
                                        9. Accelerate granting bureaucratic requests.
                                        10. Scenelong - add Bureaucracy to Socialize and Investigate in a bureau; all members of the bureau trust you.
                                        11. Scenelong – add half Bureaucracy to social influence or bureaucratic ends.
                                        12. Bargain with double-7s.
                                        13. Sense deals being made around you via Bureaucracy or Larceny.
                                        14. Remove corruption, with unclear results.
                                        15. Induce corruption, slowing bureaucratic process.
                                        16. Add half Bureaucracy to social influence to follow The Rewlz.
                                        17. Sell anything to anyone.
                                        18. Recover willpower for doing Bureaucracy.
                                        19. Sense magic targeting your organization; stop it, if necessary.
                                        20. Profile the person using magic on your organization.
                                        21. Kill a project.
                                        22. Free bureaucracy Excellency, once per season.
                                        23. Finish a project, without actually undertaking it.
                                        24. Your bureaucracy may no longer _____.
                                        25. Compel a person to act in his official capacity.
                                        26. Enhance an organization’s die pools; make it a bulwark against the Wyld.

                                        #8 – Enigmatic Bureau Organization – implies, but does not actually state, that it only works if the Solar is… what, leading the organization? A member in authority over the affected character? As written, it applies to all organizations, which, uh, seems like the Solar’s going to be getting a lot of mental sticky notes. Why is this free Reflexive and not Permanent?

                                        #9, Speed the Wheels, accelerate the bureaucratic delay. Soooo… how long are those, normally? Is “The Fair Folk won’t turn over records for three centuries” a common thing?

                                        #17, Soul-Snaring Pitch, is legitimately a terrifying and useful power – automatically successful against most mortals by Essence 3, and enormously expensive to resist regardless. If it succeeds, and it’s likely to do so, you make the target willing to pay very nearly anything for your chosen object.


                                        I do love the sole Essence 5 entry here – the last one in the list. “My organization supports reality itself” is just very cool.

                                        A lot of these, though, are… kind of thin. There are a bunch of pseudo-Infinite Masteries here, granting limited scenelong Excellency use – that’s #6, 10, 11, and 16; #18 and 22 are similar slow-time resource regenerators (saving you Willpower and motes, respectively). Some of the others are just underwhelming; #1 and #2, Frugal Merchant Method and Insightful Buyer Method, are together about equal to 2e’s Frugal Merchant Method – and the 2e version also granted bonus dice! Did anyone feel like the 2e version was just too potent a package? So many of the others have the problem that small-scale haggling in Exalted is basically meaningless; if you don’t change a price by a full order of magnitude – that is, by a resource dot or more – there’s no actual in-game consequence to the shift. This isn’t D&D, where a 10% reduction in gear price would be an actual big deal – whatever this does for you, it’s doing in the land of flavor text. That’s a tough environment to write Charms for, I’m sure – and the powers here that feel legitimately powerful break out of that “slight edge in bargaining” slot to make broad edits of organizations or human desires. Of my four players, none have taken Bureaucracy Charms, and it’s not hard to see why – when the name of the Ability is bureaucracy, you need a hook to grab people right out of the gate, and Antiques Roadshow: The Charm ain’t it.

                                        Themes of bureaucracy include knowing what people want, and giving it to them; gaining persistent competency (expressed through scenelong die-adders) in bureaucratic situations; and convincing members of a bureaucracy to do what they’re supposed to do (or not, depending).

                                        Yes: Soul-Snaring Pitch offers a lot of power in a small package. I can think of many approaches enabled by having this Charm.
                                        No: Almost all of the Essence 1 effects.
                                        Just Plain Weird: #20, Omen-Spawning Beast, is one of what’s a rare breed so far: what appear to be Solar reality-hacking Charms. Activating it, you find notes detailing who has used magic against you – notes that are, apparently, called into existence by the Charm itself. #23 – Infinitely Efficient Register – is similar. Who has completed the project? What resources went into its development? Apparently, the Charm retroactively does that for you. (Is this magic? It’s described like magic – is it supposed to be Stuff That Just Happened Normally, expressed in Charm form?)

                                        (Is the numbered list useful at all?)
