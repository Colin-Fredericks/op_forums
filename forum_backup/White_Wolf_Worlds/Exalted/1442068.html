              So, someone wielding Death at the Root can potentially suppress or even negate sorcerous workings (via Broken Circle Ruin, Lunars p. 338), which normally can't be done. I'm not sure how the effects work out in some cases.

              An (admittedly weird) example:

              A sorcerer uses a fairly simple Terrestrial (Ambition 2) working to create a species of rabbit-scorpion hybrids. Let's say the sorcerer originally creates a single breeding pair. A year later, a Lunar carrying Death at the Root shows up at the sorcerer's manse and quickly gets tired of dealing with scorpion-tailed bunnies. They're obviously the work of sorcery, so he brings the grimcleaver down and chops that miracle in half.

              Presumably, the original breeding pair dies, splits into one pair each of (very confused) rabbits and scorpions, or otherwise comes to an unfortunate end. What about the rest of the group? Does every last scorpion-bunny perish, or are the rest of them fine? (I'm not sure if a newly created species would be considered part of the working in their entirety, or if they're merely the results of the working.)

              Example 2:

              A sorcerer performs a powerful Solar (Ambition 2) working to make his home city fly. Later, while the city is a mile in the air, that guy with Death at the Root shows up and suppresses the working.

              Does the city just...fall (probably killing most of the population on impact)? Or does it just descend, land awkwardly on the ground and stay there until the magic turns back on? (My assumption is the former, but yikes.)

                                                  Originally posted by shkspr1048 View Post
                                                  Exalted covers a lot of territory, culturally speaking, but ignoring the outliers (barbarian tribes, First age infrastructure, and what-all-else), if you had to pin down a time period for Creation (classical, medieval, renaissance, etc.), generally speaking, what would it be?
                                                  I wouldn't try. I don't think that it's a useful way to describe things, both because Creation is all over the place in terms of its inspirations, and also because real-world time periods don't work that way — there's no smooth worldwide progression of culture or technology to use as a template for a "time period."

                                                      We haven't hashed out the specifics of forbidden gods in 3e yet.
