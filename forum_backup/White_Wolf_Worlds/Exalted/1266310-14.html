    Originally posted by Aquillion View Post

    In a larger moral sense, all sentient beings should be held to the same standard - more or less, with recognition that their state or status might grant extenuating circumstances.     Sure. This is a perspective worth holding, and the only way to remain moral if that moral code is to be isonomic. Yet, this is a complication that will arise eventually. Bridging acknowledgement and morality can be really hard.
    Originally posted by Aquillion View Post

    Originally posted by Aquillion View Post

    Well, of course. Dehumanizing is the most basic tool to make people feel no remorse about destroying people. Why even bring this up? It's not applicable to the current discussion: every single case of de-humanizing humans is fundamentally different from treating with a truly non-human creature in the first place. This is not relativism. This is an axiom of the setting. The two brothers are not human, just like humans are not goats, just like sea sponges are not human.     As such, they are literally beyond our capacity to understand comprehensively (and they will never be portrayed perfectly as such because humans are writing the script).
    Originally posted by Aquillion View Post
    That isn't some sort of vast cosmic mystery that necessarily implies (jazz hands) DEEP COSMIC CONSCIOUSNESS. We can create and destroy life, too. Part of the reason I'm trying to carefully avoid calling them "gods" is because the word is a thought-terminating cliche that leads people to ascribe this vague handwavy higher-consciousness and freedom from moral constraints to an entity without any rationale beyond "well, they use that label, and they glow yellow, so clearly they're so super-smart and super-civilized that morality doesn't apply to them."
    But it is, isn't it? They are portrayed as something beyond humanity.
    On the scale...While we alter the planet far more than most(probably all) earthen life forms ever did, we are nowhere near as powerful, nowhere near as immediately destructive as those two things were.     Compare to your "all nuke total destruction" scenario. We have what, 15 thousand nukes in the world? A rough estimate is 600 gigatons of yield. You can round up by saying its 15k tsar bombas, a total of 750 gigatons. 3,7 x 10^21 joules of energy released. A few millenia of radioactive waste at most.
    Which means that you can wipe out humanity(along with a bunch other creatures)... armed with the total combined efforts of millions of people backed by the work of billions before them, and the actual delivery probably demanding weeks upon weeks of precise strikes, and even then it might not wipe humanity out.     The gods thanos snapped humanity off.

We are somewhat disconnected from our paleolitic cousins through how we can transform the world, and these humans that conquer stars will be disconnected from us for the same reason)

    Originally posted by Aquillion View Post

    I don't think it works that way. Sentience and sapience are a hard line. Once someone has those traits, they have certain rights and certain responsibilities, which are absolutely equal and completely reciprocal whether we're talking about the highest, most transcendent cosmic god or the lowest crippled beggar - if the highest of holies murders a beggar, it is still murder, and they should be called to account to it. The fact that they aren't is purely because of the differential in power, not value - again, the logic of tyrants and bullies.

    My view on this is, in part, because our stories inevitably exist in the context of the real world; you can't show the Demon of Darkness genociding a world without considering the context of genocide in our own world. Because of that context, a story that implies that a god has more worth than a beggar is a detestable story. I'm not interested in any story that would imply that there exist superior sentient beings who have some sort of moral right to freely genocide and murder lesser sentient beings, and seeing stories like that makes me concerned about the mindset of anyone who would write (or even consider) something so horrible.

    (As I said above, while I'll argue this until I'm blue in the face, I have begrudgingly conceded that there's a high priority that RWBY is that sort of detestable story. Still, I'll keep watching in the vague hope-against-hope that the endgame is Salem and team RWBY banding together to bring the Brothers to justice.)

    Obviously everything I've said about the Brothers applies equally to the Primordials; that's part of the reason I get so irritated when the books (or posters here) seem to be engaging in apologetics for their mindset. Obviously they consider themselves to be above the morality of lesser beings, but their current situation shows that, well... if you're going to lean on the logic of bullies and tyrants to put yourself above anyone you consider incapable of harming you, you're likely to live by the sword, die by the sword, so to speak.

    (The state of the Primordials also shows how pathetic that sort of "VAST AND SUPERIOR" argument looks when it's stripped of the power that normally accompanies it. I don't think "the Yozis are superior beings, so we should let them out and allow them to kill everyone else in the world at their whims if they choose to do so" is meant to be seen as a sane or moral course of action. My own opinion is that it's so two-dimensionally stupid and moustache-twirlingly evil that the books wasted far more time on it than it was worth already. But, again, it does seem to have its defenders, given how many people still side with Ozpin.)
    You keep going back to how they are evil because they harm our moral standards.
    I agree.
    I wholeheartedly agree with that. They are monsters. And they will be monsters in this sense for so long as they are without peer.

                                    ...Also I was watching the last seasons of Series of Unfortunate Events, BUT ANYWAYS.

                                    LET'S TALK ABOUT LOST.

                                    Not the Abrams one, the RWBY one. *rewatches to get bearing*

                                    A lot of Volume 6 has been pre-occupied with subjects and themes I've personally been finding dull on the whole-No, I did not want to know or care about Salem's backstory, and honestly reincarnation took out a lot of my interest in Ozpin's backstory, and hey kids, did you know that the sort of conflict you're facing now looks an awful lot like the job you went out of your way to train for? The house was cool, as we finally started redefining the conflicts for Blake and Yang, finally started delivering Qrow's alcoholism as a conflict, got into Weiss's trepidation, and the Apathy as a thing on top of all of that was a cooler metaphor and conflict than the actual Volume theme, but by and large this Volume just hasn't been there for me. But then we got to Lost, and it just highlights so many of RWBY's problems with storytelling philosophy and method, in particular to some of the very bad ideas it has about setup and payoff. I do not want to dive into that subject without going back and talking about the other seasons of RWBY and I've got too much to watch for that-so we'll try and keep the more pertinent points concise while we go through just the episode.


                                    The Oscar Has No Gas-Stakes in a Barren Character
                                    First off, when last we left off, we were left to chase off after Oscar-which is already a bit of a rocky place to start a conflict from. Oscar Pine, from his inception, has not really had much characterization beyond his base trope. Oscar has never been a character the series has dug enough into for us to invest his-we never got a sense of what his life was like, what his ambitions were, what he thought of the world, etc-since his introduction, the series has been more about just getting up and going with him to the plot points without working to get us invested in him. It's not like this season hasn't given us a lot of room to do so-but then again, RWBY is not as good at balancing its' character loads as some of the shows it tries to ape. The most they've done if sell us on the idea that he problems with the psychic invasion of Ozpin and that his arc should involve his surrender, but either Oscar just gives up too quick or not enough is given to his protests to really make me care too much about him. So going into the episode trying to be worried about Oscar just isn't going to happen-he's been too much a boilerplate trope to much care about him, too much of a concept to see him as anything worth losing.

                                    Normally this would be where putting Ozpin at stake would be effective, and in fact has some cool potential despite the fact that Ozpin has been losing a lot of his dynamics the older and more immortal he gets, because we at least get that he is important to the story and that his loss means something...except for two problems, namely a) having sidelined Ozpin for most of the Volume has put him out of sight, out of mind, and so without the character there to cause some tension and fear for his sake just isn't going to happen, and b) the show really wants you to understand that it's concerned about Oscar, seeing as the Big, Bad Ozpin is such a big bad man, which, sorry, what am I supposed to be holding Ozpin accountable to here? At worst, he's going to summon the gods again, and while I'm definitely for finding an answer without those assholes, really I just don't feel the stakes in stopping Salem near as much as the show would like me to. She has to be stopped now. That's it. Fighting her again and again is really no different from just fighting the Grimm over and over, and honestly that feels more thematically resonant with where RWBY wants to be anyways.

                                    As is, I'm just not particularly convinced Ozpin would summon the gods, and so beyond that, he's...what exactly? So without me feeling Oz is actually accountable for the MAJOR PLOT TWISTS OF THE SEASONS, It's harder for me invest in feeling bad for Oscar when I'm busy feeling like everyone else is being stupidly overbearing on Oz.

                                    So even before the episode begins, I'm not particularly invested in what's going to happen. The show knew this, and so had a clever plan!

                                    As You Know, I Must Explain Things You Already Know-The Worst Waste of Telling Over Showing
                                    Hey guys, do you remember that one episode in Volume 3 where we got all we needed on Emerald's and Mercury's background? Have you been enjoying the way that Rooster Teeth has been telling you how they've been feeling and reacting now that their leader, Cinder, is no longer there to provide context on the sort of shit they've been getting up to? Have you felt rewarded for being a careful follower of the story they were telling through body language and meaningful production choices?

                                    Well, Rooster Teeth wants you to know that you were too stupid to pick up on any of that, because they decided to just spell it out for you and the rest of the dumb audience. That's what the beginning of this episode feels like to me.

                                    Like, I'm just sitting here and watching the dialogue between Emerald and Mercury slamming my head into the desk, constantly repeating "YES, I KNOW, YOU SHOWED ME ALREADY. I KNOW THIS ALREADY." The only new thing I learned was that Mercury had his Semblance stolen from him by his father, which...I could have learned any number of other ways, namely entertaining ones where the elucidation of his situation might have an impact on one of our protagonists? Otherwise, I'm sitting here feeling talked down to by the show like I couldn't be expected to know this is exactly how they've been feeling and thinking all along because, you know, I was watching the show.

                                    And then Tyrion walks and I manage to lose even more interest. Team WTCH in general has been a bore. I'm still maintain interest in Cinder, though much like learning about Ozpin's immortality makes me less interested in what his actual story is, Cinder's relationship to Salem and the rest of the losers here makes me care less and less about what she wants, why she wants it, and where the root of her arc is. Hazel had the potential to be interesting but got shot with the most boring explanations and expressions in Volume 5. Watt is...vaguely interesting? Not enough to muster words for.

                                    And then there's Tyrion. From the moment this spiny tailed pissant appeared on screen, I have just wanted him to shut up. I hate him talking, I hate him worshipping, I hate him fighting, I hate him freaking out. His characterization is blunt and over-tried at, and he goes from being the sort of fun over the top you might enjoy in other characters to the sort of over the top that makes you wonder why no one else has killed him out of annoyance. The worst part is that he could have possibly worked, with the traits they gave him, as the minion of someone down the line, like maybe an assassin for Watts the same way Mercury is for Cinder-that would have been more rewarding to watch, but the fucking idiot ball as one of the four dragons for Salem, the big bad? His postion makes his hamminess fall apart. for me. He pisses me off so much it's hard to articulate what drives me nuts about him, beacause I'm too busy being mad that he exists.

                                    So anyways, Tyrion lays out some plot stuff and also exhorts the kids to, instead of being concerned with what they are getting out of their arrangement with Salem, to instead find joy in what they are doing, as a not-subtle way of explaining he would enjoying killing them like we hadn't picked up on that before hand, and like, that might be neat if I thought that was going to possibly move some things on the board of RWBY's story? Except that it's clearly not, because RWBY puts it's tells up like a big flaming dick on the horizon blaring "These Hoes Ain't Loyal" (that, yes, you hear from the horizon) RWBY does some things well, but effective intrigue with it's foreshadowing has never been one of them.

                                    Gah, I'm done with this part. What next?

                                    Oh. Right. Team RNJ.

                                    *sigh*

                                    Team RNJ and The Lack Thereof-The A In Other A-Team Means Ass
                                    Okay, that's a little unfair, so let's back up a second.

                                    Some of you might remember the Volume 1 reviews I was doing when I was basically the only one with the subscription to Rooster Teeth. If you do, you might remember me not being too fond of team JNPR. I warmed up to them eventually, though, and by the time I got around to Volume 3, I actually really sort of dug them. I mean, okay, I never got into Ren and Nora, but I got into Jaune and Pyrrha to some extent, and I was, at the time, cool with everything that shook down for them at the end of Volume 3, and Hey, Look At That! There is a reason why they were here, all along! They're here to be Ruby's team in the meantime! And then we'll get to see them step up and become actual A Team material! Surely we'll discover all sorts of

                                    Look, that didn't happen. In fact, time and tide has made the remainder of Team RNJ kind of clearly lacking for place and purpose in the story. They feel like a relic of the first three Volumes that no one had the good sense to look over and rethink how to use them.

                                    They just haven't contributed much to the story. The comic relief has been, well, lacking-they never got laughs out of me that much to begin with but have literally not gotten an emotional reaction out of me for years. Character arcs and development? I do not have the hours tonight to explain how badly fucked up the story of Ren and Nora were in Volume 4, just suffice it to say that instead of an emotional journey that could have really fleshed out and made those two dynamic culminating in a catharsis of facing down their demons, we got told about their demons the laziest way possible before ending it and, oh, I guess Ren and Nora can be a couple now, like anything's going to come of that for the story. As for Jaune-Well Jaune is about to get his own griping session, but for now, know that "they killed my girlfriend and I have feelings about" loses out more and more over time. Plot action? No cunning plans, no badass rescues that didn't feel like they were there just to get it out of the way, no integral sacrifices or improvisations, nothing. Combat action? Eh, maybe, but I'm the guy who would have ended the Red Trailer on that first gunshot, so I'm not going to stress much out over that.

                                    RNJ just hasn't had anything to do and hasn't even tried to go out of their way to do more, and I just sit here with the problem of thinking that most of the roles they have been filling could have been filled by other people (Particularly Qrow, since the evolution of his relationship with Ruby from "cool uncle" to "flawed adult" has been the main thrust of her interpersonal relationships in the show since Volume 4 anyways), and unfortunately, the open reminder that they could always just stay in Argus (which, unrelated, is actually a lovely city design and I am cribbing it) and their doubts about being involved with the fight against Salem really hammers down how much of a non-contribution they've been making. At a point where I should be like "No, don't give up, I'm rooting for you guys!" I really want them to sideline themselves, and that's a far way to fall going from unexpectedly shouting with glee at a victory for a blonde-haired shit I hated a lot up to the point when he took a stand against a bear.

                                    Speaking of.

                                    She Should Be Standing Here-Jaune and Pyrrha and How Things Have Gone
                                    I need to briefly explain the series of events before I get into this, so episode recap from where we last left off:

                                    So Team RNJ and Jaune's sister Saffron (yay lesbian representation, btw) are strolling through the city, looking around for Oscar and generally feeling depressed. Saffron, at one point, makes the above suggestion that they could just stay in Argus, do some good here and just quit the mission, and Jaune lays out that it's not that easy. Saffron leaves, and Jaune goes to sit down and feel bad, and after spying a cafe, Nora and Red decide to go get drinks and let Jaune have some time to himself. Jaune, feeling antsy as much as he feels depressed, gets up, and walks into the park across the street.

                                    And there he finds it. The thing that broke me. You could not have been more hamfisted or schmaltzy if you had had the heavens open and the angels sing out and the trumpets sound and from the celestial chorus descendeth the Holiest of Holy Girlfriends. There's a temple with John Goodman sitting on a throne that would have been less mind-numbingly jarring and ill-thought out to walk into than this. A sense of loss and respect and clarity and wonder were all lost by the god damn audacity of this park.

                                    Because in the middle of that park, gentle reader, was a fucking huge memorial statue to Pyrrha Nikos. Because of fucking course he does.

                                    Jesus Fucking Christ.

                                    Okay, gotta do more before I strip this apart, I can do this.

                                    SO.

                                    The music plays, and he approaches the statue of Her Exalted Girlfriendness For This Loser(Who Is You), and he is about to have a reaction, then goes to leave when GASP! A woman who could possibly be anyone and certainly not Pyrrha's mother talks to him. They spend some time telling you things you already knew and that Jaune already knew and doesn't really change anything, and then Ren and Nora come back and and there's more telling each other things they already knew and that we already knew, and one actually really heartwarming note from Nora and Ren establishing that Jaune has got to stop thinking of himself the way he does and they love him that really should have been expanded on and delivered on instead, but anyways, the stuff is said, and now it's time to go back to the house.

                                    Okay, caught up? Caught up. Good. Let's get the minor stuff out of the way.

                                    One, once again, 90% of the dialogue doesn't actually change anything, for the character or the viewers. As mentioned, the most effective thing was Nora and Red telling Jaune that they loved him and as such valued him and really gave a real sense that he needs to start understanding how to belong and draw inspiration from the others around him and to similarly inspire and strengthen the people around him instead of being hung up on his dead girlfriend. That aside, everything is shit that has been established and easily inferred from conversation mid-volume 3 onwards. This conversation did nothing for confronting their actual sense of weakness and non-contribution to the affairs of things. By and large, it's like Jaune got reminded to do his laundry. We got no sense of the dynamics that led to Pyrrha being who she was and how we or the characters could learn the same lessons she did and become more like her, nothing genuinely aspirational or inspirational for anyone.

                                    Two, okay, on a nitpick level, am I the only one who remembers how part of the problem that sparked the downfall of Beacon was Pyrrha overkilling a student and having that only be marginally be okay because it turned out the student was actually a really shady robot program on Atlas's part and therefore is probably actually something of a persona non grata? Like, admittedly, I was always curious why a student dying in this pretty hardcore tournament was a thing that Cinder would make a deal out of, but since it was, it's still worth noting that Pyrrha overkilled a fellow student. That's not a thing people are just okay with. You wanna tell me there's a memorial, sure, but not a huge fucking statue, you don't want to start celebrating your town pride over the woman who sparked all this shit happening.

                                    Honestly, the scene could have just worked with Jaune meeting up with Pyrrha's mother and talking, and in fact that, would have been a lot more quiet and intimate and heartfelt, particularly if you picked up on the unease of people around Mrs. Nikos and the relief she would have gotten just from finally being allowed to be openly proud of her daughter with someone who knew her and what happened.

                                    As is, the scene is stupidly overwrought and overblown and it takes me right out of it. It's just so. It's so on the nose. It's so convenient. It's just hamfisted and schmaltzy and even obvious that it just kills everything in it.

                                    By itself, it's just bad. It is fucking lazy storytelling. But there's a line that forces you to think outside of it, and it is a problem that has hung over RWBY since Volume 3, and will hang over it from here on.

                                    "She should be standing here."

                                    I mean. Yeah. She should be.

                                    Here's the thing-Jaune just doesn't have that much dramatic potential. No one in RNJ particularly had it to begin with. Consider, by contrast, Team RWBY-everyone in that story has complicated, dramatic relationships with people who hold power over major cogs in the world's wheelhouse that create a natural narrative arc for claiming power and responsibility in service to the ultimate conflict with Salem-Weiss has the Schnee Dust Company, Blake the White Fang, Yang the Branwen Tribe who even Salem didn't want to lightly fuck with, and Ruby her relationship to Taiyang and Qrow and closer relationship to Ozpin, plus she also gets to skate through with her being the Silver Eyed Special.

                                    Now consider RNJ. Ren had that potential, but his story is almost effective as a sort of failed state of that-someone who, had things gone differently, been in a position much like anyone on Team RWBY. Nora might still have it, but realistically her role is set and matched here, and I don't expect any real change or introductions of interesting dynamics with her. And then you have Jaune, who for all that he feels like a big deal is pretty openly a nobody in the story. THat is how they decided to play him, it was the text of the climax of Volume 5.

                                    On top of that, all of his fury and sorrow just sort of feels like a thing. He's Mad Because His Girlfriend Is Dead, and Because Some People Work Behind the Scenes, They Deserve His Ire. I know it has more logic than that, but it doesn't have any emotional or dramatic weight than that. Every conflict he has is not actually a conflict for him, it's just him being mad about his perfect girlfriend dying. It all rings hollow and cheap coming from him. A lot of his conflict just feels like that.

                                    Because, really, honestly, the better story would have been if he died fighting Cinder instead of Pyrrha.

                                    No, think about it, really think about it. Think about what that would have done to the story. Jaune's arc was always that he felt like a loser who had to stand up to expectations and realistically couldn't. He wanted to be a hero even though he couldn't actually rattle up a proper heroic effort. He had a good mind for approaching things, because his weakness made him look for advantages-but Jaune has always needed to prove to himself that he could Put Up The Fight, not just provide the clever idea. His most natural conclusion is to defy the fates that seem laid out in front of him and a loved one, and put up his best effort, and maybe even still have it be pathetic-but still be just enough to have made a difference. Imagine how gratifying it would have been for the kid who could never do anything got to go out doing just enough.

                                    Meanwhile, Pyrrha was always the girl who was going to die. Part of that is because she was the perfect girlfriend for Jaune (don't glare at me like that, that is one hundred percent of the logic of her characterization). She was always a setup for a punchline involving Jaune and Ruby getting together (Speaking of, oh yeah, that's inevitable kids) and basically just serving as tinder for Jaune's arc. But imagine if Jaune had flipped the script on her?

                                    First off, the blatancy of her story is instantly subverted. Everything that had been sloppily just laid out like it's the most natural thing in the world would have been pulled out from under us, and we'd have gotten a genuine surprise instead. Second, It would have taken her context and out of being nothing more than Jaune's perfect girlfriend, there's now this story world after character concept that's full and rich and ripe. She has a whole range of dynamic relationships to explore while that hole is being filled, and all of it is intriguing because none of us were waiting for it. Her relationship with Ruby, Ren, and Nora would not only be drastically different from Jaune's, but from the sort of stuff we're used to in storytelling. She would have to go and become someone else entirely new, both in her own eyes, the eyes of her peers, and the eyes of the audience.

                                    And you know, the place that starts her off as is just way more interesting too. After all, she's still a champion and legendary figure to a lot of people, who should, by all rights, still have a lot of clout as it goes-except that's now all tainted by the murder of another student and the starter of the Fall of Beacon. Her reputation is compromised, and that's a thing that would haunt the group from then on and demand a fight with a lot of complicated questions to answer in order to fix. That's a situation filled with an inherent push-and-pull, inherent tension, that has room to create a lot of narrative scenarios that would be rewarding to follow-up on. And that's before you get on her personal involvement with a lot of the major reveals since Volume 3, mixed in with the fact that she can wrap up the same sort of angst that Jaune has in the same thing, except that it hurts even more, because his brilliant heroism just highlights how much she failed, particularly since she has more all the more power to make the difference.

                                    I can't watch this episode without being forcibly reminded of how RWBY took the laziest option for that entire story thread, which is a problem that RWBY frequently has-that so many things are done just because it seems like that's how you do, much like the attitude of this entire Volume. And more importantly, it reinforces that, yeah, a sizable portion of our protagonists shouldn't be standing here, because they have contributed nothing.

                                    You'd think it would end there, but NOPE.

                                    OH FOR FUCK'S SAKE-What Narrative Tension?
                                    Okay, so we resolved a major thread of doubt that's been going on for some of our heroes. Nevermind no one cared about it, because the problem was not only obvious but really in line with what they were doing, nevermind that it happened with characters who could disappear from the story at this point and not change anything-we got a Character Moment, and from There We Can Change Things. So back to the main problem. RNJ may have found new heart, but Oscar is still out there in the city, possibly lost, definitely still emotionally conflicted, still feeling diseased from the man who will take over his soul some day, still alienated from the rest of the group as the carrier of this Ozpin, so now it's surely up to RNJ to find him and make amends, so they can all come together and

                                    HE IS LITERALLY JUST WAITING FOR THEM AT HOME, MAKING THEM ALL DINNER.

                                    Did. Did the plot just give up? Did the writer's just give up? What even the fuck.

                                    I trust I don't need to tell you how ending the narrative tension of the last episode's ending by just having that all work out in the end is just plain bad, but I'm going to-THIS WAS A BIG DEAL. Oscar was clearly upset, and he was clearly going to need to have some connections made-and instead, it's just solved. Like, WHat?!

                                    This isn't like the episodes of Black and White, back in Volume 1, where in Weiss changed her mind after a day of walking around, I should note (because I can see some of you thinking that and even possibly remembering how I wrote positively about it back in the day). Going with a quick comparison, the big difference is that we got to see Weiss thinking about it, and even got to see it cleverly portrayed, by both the subtle shifts of her dialogue and her body language. You could see her going at like a mature adolescent, someone who was literally growing up while working out her feelings and thoughts on the subject throughout a long, hard day, taking into account what everyone else was saying and doing in response to Blake. That might be over-reading a scene, but at a bare minimum, there are scenes of her possible growth to over read. Here, he's just-gone and then back and over everything.

                                    And you know, it's not like elements of this couldn't have been kept. HE COULD have been out shopping for dinner stuff as away to clear his mind and sort stuff out-he could have been found by Team RNJ contemplating the clothes, the active part of his brain telling him to sort step up, and make the changes to himself but the darker parts telling him to drop everything and just go back home, and they could have had a brief but mature apology-and-discussion of empathy and understanding, and Jaune could finalize that arc of his and help Oscar also actualize a character point of his own, well and truly fully stepping up into being both a contribution to this team and the Opener of the Way for Ozpin.

                                    But instead, he's just back home, fully actualized, having grown up entirely off screen and just WHAT THE FUCK IS THIS WRITING.

                                    And then they come up with the kernel of an idea for how to get to Atlas and Qrow has some crow shoved down his throat and that part's actually pretty satisfying.

                                    I Can't Even This Episode-In Conclusion
                                    As I've mentioned, some of this is just tip of the iceberg for me. I like RWBY a lot in spite of itself more than because of it. RWBY is a very flawed series-but where it has fun and heart, it shines really strongly. But by contrast, some episodes are just so flat and hollow and paint-by-the-numbers that not only does it break with the logic of the show itself, but it completely makes you forget that the show does have heart. Previously, that episode was Kuroyuri, and that one is still really bad. And asides from that, RWBY managed to avoid breaking it's narrow tendons keeping it together.

                                    But Lost is just astonishingly lazy. It's the sort of episode that just evokes an onslaught of "because of course" after "because of course" It spends time telling you things you already know while not really thinking about things it should have thought of, and all of it so flatly predictable and unfleshed out you'd be better served reading TV Tropes-and to make matters worse, it just highlights how much RWBY gets by on that sort of logic. RWBY doesn't have isolated flaws, little nicks and scratches that can be ignored, but instead has some deep structural issues as a result of the way it fails to consider the consequences of what it's doing.

                                    Back at the end of Volume 1, I mentioned that I had fallen in love with the heart of the show, but that the best way I could think of to sell RWBY was a show for newbies into anime as a means of quickly learning the "language" of the movement. I thought, after the way Volumes 2 and 3 had developed, I was going to eventually turn out wrong-but the most depressing thing, after these three Volumes from there?

                                    Is that I'm forced to consider just how right I was to begin with. And that's not where I wanted RWBY to end up.

                                    Ah well, we'll see where it goes from here.
