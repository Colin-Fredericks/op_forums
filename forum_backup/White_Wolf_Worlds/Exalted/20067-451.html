    Originally posted by The Unsung Hero View Post
    Then they probably shouldn't use the charm designed to kill things permanently forever.
    They are new to exalted and that wasn't clear to them from the charm - I told them about Lethe and Oblivion and they assumed it would be Lethe.

    Originally posted by nalak42 View Post
    Or if you don't want to add a charm tax you can just say that a ghost that has been taken out by the Ghost Eater lacks the power to materialize at all and lacks sufficient power to resist the pull of Oblivion or Lethe (whichever it's more aligned too) and is pulled in post dissolve. God knows that's the excuse I use with the player that always gets uncomfortable with soul obliteration.
    Originally posted by TheCountAlucard View Post
    I seem to recall some stealth errata occurring sometime around MoEP:Abyssals, in which they took a sidebar to explain that Solar and other not-Underworld Exalt types' spirit-killing Charms worked via forcing them through Lethe, where those "edgy" Abyssal Charms worked via sending them to Oblivion.
    I think that at least for this edition, having that explicitly spelled out is pretty unnecessary, since it seems like something no one in the setting would really have any way of knowing; how the hell would a Solar even find out if the nephwrack he ghost-ate reincarnated?
    Better by far to let them speculate on its nature in-character without a definitive answer, like what ominous portents the red comet held in A Song of Ice and Fire.
    I like that idea personally since it makes it an existential issue but it may mean rolling their character back several charms if the character doesn't want to permanently take souls out of the cycle (they would have never learned them because of the risk). It also means that the charm and it's follow ups are XP tax for that player who definitely does want the capstone charm.
    I like Nalak's idea if this becomes an issue for their character concept (since it makes the XP tax problem irrelevant) while still keeping a theme of consequence that causes a character to pause before destroying a ghost.

    ps. [I admit I've never read the books (or will any time soon) so I don't know how the portents felt like in the reference]
