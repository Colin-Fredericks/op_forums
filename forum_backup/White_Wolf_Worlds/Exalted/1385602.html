    Thorns: What To Do?

    05-04-2020, 10:33 AM
    So, I'm running a game over in Thorns. And for once, I actually kinda agree with excising some of the past material here - the whole Wisdom Whispered bit, the Thornguard as a SS analogy... yeah, there's stuff best left on the cutting room floor. But what should Thorns be like? What do you guys think of when it comes to the old City of the Dead?

      its not a city of the dead - that's sort of the point

      its being made into one


      I think the best way to sell thorns is to go full Half Life


      Think City 17. Think black steel bulkheads appearing overnight as they phase in from the underworld, cordoning off entire sections of the city. You don't hear a thing of what happens in there, but the cordons don't go away... and more and more of the city is becoming off limits.

      The thornguard - previously well respected as defenders of the land - now they're... off, somehow (abyssal mind control charms) - present them as Combine troopers. And keep in mind that 9 out of 10 ghosts in the city "live" up in the mask's palace on top of Juggernaut, only coming down to the city either to work or mess with people when they're not working


      Now, i don't know what kind of game you're running, but if its your usual gang of solar players - then have them sneak into the city to meet up with a contact to get the lay of the land. They'll have to sneak past rice and grain fields being worked by zombies, past invisible ghost patrols, and deal with blood-tinted rain that leaves the whole region smelling like an open sewer. Or perhaps just sneak in via a merchant ship going straight to the city's port?

      When they get to the right address in the aspir haven port district, far from a several-block wide steel cordon (where the Mask has demolished most of the old ritzy neighborhood and built weapons factories)... oh no... the contact they were supposed to get the lay of the land from - a frightened neighbour says that the thornguard came in the night and hauled him off.

      Time for a prison break! Of course, little does the circle know that Shackle Maw Penitentiary is but a torture factory for the production of maddened ghosts and battered zombies. Sure, it was once a well respected hospice known as the Hoshosen Hospice - a place well known for attracting dragonblooded healers-in-training that need sick or infirm souls to hone their skills on - now its a place where skeletal arms reach out of dark cell windows, begging for food and water.

      ...depending on how subtly they carry out their escape, they're then told to regroup in the undercity - in a place called the seven-tiered sanctuary, but the sewers and catacombs of the city have merged with the underworld labyrinth, so that's going to be a tad tricky. Once the circle finally get there, they'll be met by a suspicious but 'friendly' leader of the resistance known as Silken Laughter (shifty sort, looks just as likely to sell you out as to help you) who their contact claimed to work for. SL is happy to hear of his minion having been rescued, and from there on...

      well - now the players are in the city and in contact with someone who can fill them in on the lay of the land. How you wanna take down Thorns is really up to yourself - but there are some key things I would argue should always be kept in mind:

      1) if you make enough noise, the mask will drown you in a never-ending tide of zombies. you'll fall asleep fighting before they stop coming - so brute forcing this might not be that viable an option, unless your players are really damn good at fighting

      2) who can you trust? after the circle's first few stunts, perhaps after their prison bust operation depending on how sneaky they are, they'll learn that the mask has put out a handsome reward for information leading to their capture. Who can they trust to help them? Will SL sell them out to give his own operations better cover?

      3) there will be abyssals in play in the city - abyssals who can operate in public, while the circle can't - and abyssals like Typhon is more than happy to use all kinds of charms to lie-detect and force information out of people, or mess with their memories to make people hate you


      So ya - lots of potential for fun in Thorns. My suggestion: Run it as a "la resistance" type of game, with a half-life aesthetic.

          Heheh. I haven't actually played Half Life but I get what you're saying here!

          I'm absolutely intending a resistance angle. The PCs are a very mixed group - three Dragon Blooded, two Lunars and a Solar, all with reason to try and drive back the Mask. I'm starting them outside the city but they'll be back soon.

          My question is really on the nitty gritty of the game in progress once it gets underway. We've seen lots of oppressive regiemes in media: the Combine are a fine example, but my mind turns to things like Dishonoured, Thief, even Assassins Creed 1 to a more limited extent. They're fine examples of a city under lockdown but each one has something of its own flavour to it, and I'm not sure how to make Mask's forces feel like something other than generic jackboot goons who happen to have zombies on a leash. Any ideas? ('Ghost spies' is obvious but kinda cheap. And they already know to expect it. And it's one trick.)

          I guess dial that back a bit. What is Thorns like? What should it be like? Ex3 Thorns is introducing Red Iron Rebuke so I feel we should have some freedom to rework things within the overall outline of its story. It's the city conquered by Mask, in the shadow of Juggernaut... and what does that mean? Yes, tyranny, but how does it manifest and what's it done to the people and culture of the city?

              Originally posted by Crokamancer
              It's the city conquered by Mask, in the shadow of Juggernaut... and what does that mean? Yes, tyranny, but how does it manifest and what's it done to the people and culture of the city?
              I like the bits and pieces such as the Mask of Winters sending diplomatic overtures to other parts of the Scavenger Lands and that opera house he had that spin an idea of him trying to present himself as a legitimate authority within the city. I believe Thorns has also often been presented as a city of formerly high culture that he utilises and might speak to why he would target it.

              It definitely feels like part of the transition should be instituting a culture that is amenable to the dead, but you want that to be distinct from Skullstone…

              Let's see, what are some of the significant things to a city... Well there are the economic factors, for a start. Assuming that the Mask wants it to return to a functional state in the long run, he's going to want to get commerce and industry back up and running, and that might necessarily require winning over the pre-existing crafts and merchant houses to his side, and maybe reorienting their output to something fitting his priorities. If his intention is to completely sweep the board, then he needs to be instituting programs to replace their expertise and infrastructure. So there can be oppression to greater or lesser extent of "make sure everybody knows there's a party line to toe, while also knowing where the points for compromise are and occasionally making the right showy gesture to ensure compliance".

              If he wants to get the city back up and running, some measure of bureaucracy is going to have to be revived, covering everything from record keeping to regular waste management. I've read accounts of things like the Communist takeover in China and rise of the Islamic State that indicate that this is an area where intrusion is often minimal; there's a period of initial tension after which the occupiers gather together everybody who had that job before, informs them of who they'll be reporting to now, and tells them to return to business as usual. Where it goes from there depends on whether or not you think the Mask has personal hangups or ideological priorities that have him start disrupting the bureaucracy with demands for shows of loyalty or being subject to purity reviews that are liable to result in purges.

              I definitely think that Thorns would still be at a stage in which people are not free to gather in an unsanctioned manner, in any venue. I expect an oppressive atmosphere in which people don't hang about just outside of their homes, in that public space in the middle of the housing block just to play and gossip; there's not even an official decree forbidding it, just people don't feel safe to do so, out where they can be seen and have to watch what they say. It's not strictly illegal, but patrolling troops are likely to interject themselves and ask questions that make folks nervous, if they don't outright harass. Similar with things like inns or shopping; people still do them, but in smaller numbers, quickly and efficiently, they don't stop to chat or browse and are looking over their shoulder. The Mask of Winters came as a dread conqueror, until such time as he establishes a position of trust people are going to be squirrelly even without any official policies of repression.

              There are Abyssal Exalted in the city. Even assuming that not all of them are going to take licence with people from the power they now possess over them, can the city be sure that the Mask of Winters will rein in any that engage in abuses? If he's not, then at least some are liable to do that kind of thing even without an official sanction. Same with the other levels of his military structure.

              Particularly with ghosts. I'm thinking that ghosts are going to be rising up to openly walk the streets, feeling liberated where before they were repressed by the veil and Immaculate dogma. Even apart from the generally uncanny quality and spooky atmosphere of them just being around, you'd think there'll be quite a few who'll feel empowered to take what they please from the living. That would be hard enough in better times, but if the city remains in a state of functional blockade then anything material that gets taken from people is likely to be something they'll have to go without indefinitely.

              Yes, people all over are probably hoarding, stashing staple goods and personal valuables in hidden spots of their homes and communities, and still liable to have some gaunt ancestor come along and take a few things as reparations for a few generations of neglect.

                      I get an impression that the default starting place for the reign of the Mask of Winters is that his actual agenda remains ambiguous, and if a Storyteller wants to portray that while keeping options open or otherwise being unconcerned with defining his goals behind the scenes...

                      I can see the oppression within Thorns as still largely consisting of uncertainty that prevents people from forming a stable routine and engaging in community. Business enterprise and city elites remain reserved in an ongoing state of negotiation to determine exactly where they stand, but it's continuously delayed and frustrated by lack of a clear hierarchy to negotiate with. You keep trying to engage in talks with officials of the puppet government, servants of the Mask of Winters, even the occasional Exalt, and it's like... is any of this progress? Am I actually speaking to somebody with authority? Every time we seem to be getting somewhere with one, another comes along carrying the apparent buck? Does this represent factionalisation that we need to blindly figure out what to align with, or are they just as in the dark as we are?

                      This with a touch of seemingly arbitrary violence from figures with some degree of authority (as opposed to the excesses of rank and file occupiers). There are prominent people getting harassed, brutalized, openly murdered or disappeared, but there's no transparency as to why, nobody can figure out if this is a systematic policy or the sign of being under a whimsical sadist.

                      There's less death than people assume there to be, the city just isn't big enough for mass killings to go on without quickly emptying it out. But the isolation and prominence of the worst examples sets imaginations running wild.

                      Although as disappearances go... a child strays too far from a group while out for a bit of air, gets snatched up to be pressed into domestic service in a barracks, they're only actually a few hundred metres from their family. It's just the family has no recourse to search or make inquiries, and under these conditions can rapidly assume the worst.

It's the city conquered by Mask, in the shadow of Juggernaut... and what does that mean? Yes, tyranny, but how does it manifest and what's it done to the people and culture of the city?
                          ok, here are some ideas on how the tyranny shows:

                          1) Every living human in thorns under the mask is a slave - you live at his behest, you have no personal freedom. the best you can do is stay out of sight and unnoticed.

                          2) the city is effectively split in two:

                          The aspir haven district, which comprises the harbor and what used to be city's upscale neighbourhood, where all of the mask's weapon industry and favored servants have been moved. Here people wear normal/nice clothes and at least pretend to smile. Foreign merchants and whatnot are shown around here. Here thornguard patrol - no zombies on leashes or anything - it has to look presentable.

                          The rest of the city has been merged into a giant district known as Legacy - here people are dressed as zombies in the making (in rags) per decree from the mask, because that is ultimately what the mask thinks of them. He has no need of any further living population. Any kind of wealth (including clothing) is systematically being stripped and collected in warehouses at the port for the mask to sell off, so he can buy stuff needed for his weapons industry that he can't mine or produce himself. Here people simply hide and try to scavenge what food they can. You wont find a single cat or dog walking the streets anymore. Hungry eyes peer cautiously from windows, only to quickly hide again if they think they're about to be seen. It's also in this district that there is the most rebellious sentiment/support, but food in very short supply.

                          There is also a third district, that of the shroudvant district - the city used to have several more districts, but the mask simplified everything because he had no need for complex city planning, and this district is gated and patrolled quite heavily. The mask just needed a layout of the city that said "This is where people work for me, this is where people rule for me, and this is where human stock slowly dies for me. As an example of tyranny, then this shouldn't be underestimated: A tyrant who renames your neighborhood into "meat reserve nr 3" is sending a very powerful message to the people who live there. That's the kind of place where people put on makeup and nice clothes before bed in case they're murdered in their sleep, that they might make a beautiful corpse.

                          The shroudvant district is where people rule for him, for that is where the only palace of the autocrat is located, along with other former aristocratic and noble-house residences. The people who live here now are the mortal traitors and bootlickers of the mask, people who've more or less willingly submitted to his rule and are actually helping. There's no love lost among the rest of the population for these people, but they are in turn protected, fed, clothed and paid quite well. Of course, being rich under the mask isn't that much more useful, so most of them blow their money as quickly as possible on lavish parties and as many drugs as and as much alcohol as they can get their grubby mits on. The ghosts who work for the mask greatly enjoy these parties just the same, and the masks's mortal minions can't exactly tell them go away.

                          There's no real economic freedom: the only businesses that exist are the ones that the mask has sanctioned - and if he doesn't need it, its not happening... at least not legitimately. Pretty much nothing but black market activity in the Legacy district.

                          Similarly, with the merchant shipping coming and going from Thorns, there's a large amount of human trafficking going on, as desperate parents pay what little coin they have left to merchants to either take their children away from the city, or if they can't afford that, then at least bring them on as ship-slaves. Anything to get them away from the city. On the plus side, then a lot kind-hearted captains do this for free, so far as possible.
                          - there could be a plot-hook about finding someone's children and ensuring that they got out of the city safely, perhaps either rescuing them if they didn't or just confirming that they got out.
