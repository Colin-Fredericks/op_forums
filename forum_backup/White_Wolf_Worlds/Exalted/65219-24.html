    Regarding Destiny:
    • The Maidens were originally third circle deva of The Lidless Eye That Sees, cut voluntarily from his soul hierarchy
      • Nobody can decide whose idea it was to cut them loose, or why: The Dragon's Shadow? Oramus? His Own?
      • He accomplished this using An End To Darkness. Difficulty: Before Sidereals, Demons, or greater astrology existed.
    • Samsara is the world-body of Sacheverell, a perfect four-dimensional model of Creation that his souls can look upon.
      • Samsara is governed by the uncertainty principle, which is why shit gets awkward when people observe it.
      • Samsara is (meta-)physically located in the glint of Sacheverell's eyes, so he really needs to keep them closed.

    Regarding Justice:
    • Cecelyne was the original drafter, arbiter, and executive of the laws of heaven still enforced in Yu-Shan today.
    • The third circle deva associated with this was excised as part of her surrender, to gain control of those laws.
    • The Hidden Judges of the Secret Flame are that third circle's second circle component souls, still on the job.

    Regarding Cataclysm:
    • Autochthon's conceptual inventions include the idea of destructive self-modification (from haircuts to trans-humanism).
      • This concept is otherwise known as the Void, and Autochthon's cancer is his own, out of control, self-reinvention.
    • Somehow, he convinced She Who Lives In Her Name to learn it from him, resulting in the Three Spheres Cataclysm.
      • It hasn't happened before or since because self-destruction is outside the Titans' nature. And they don't want cancer.
    • He did it with the best intentions: to contain the rampaging shards created by Incarna who were slain in the War.
      • The ideas destroyed were key to attracting those shards to hosts. He drew them off using backup copies.
    • It worked, but inspired the design of the Jade Prison, monstrances, and the way Infernals are exalted by the Yozi.
      • Oops. (Said Autochthon, as usual...)

    Regarding Essence:
    • Motes are the fundamental particle of narrative. More interesting stuff involves more motes. Boring stuff involves fewer.
    • Pratchett's Law is the only absolute of motonic physics: essence (reality) can neither be created nor destroyed.
    • Motes can be thought of as self-executing computational objects, with arbitrarily mutable properties and methods.
      • Some motes implement methods that limit their apparent mutability, this is called being Shaped.
      • Methods of one mote may call methods of other motes, and this process is called Magic.
      • Some motes implement methods which modify other motes, executing them is called Shaping.
      • Motes can contain other motes as property values, nesting to arbitrary depth and complexity.
      • Motes in the Wyld are constantly operating on themselves, creating pseudo-random variations.
      • Oblivion is where motes go to die - it clears a mote's properties and methods, leaving it immutable.
      • Void tech has memory leaks - it bleeds motes into who knows where, and it's annoying to find and fix.

    Regarding Alpacas:
    • There are alpacas that live in Fucking Nowhere. They win. For certain definitions of winning that are really confusing.

        Regarding Primordials:
        • Primordials are thought-entities composed entirely of ideas, which invent or adapt new ideas into themselves.
          • The early Titans constantly shared and adapted each-others' inventions, creating all manner of nuanced ideas.
          • The core differences between the Titans extend primarily from the ideas a given Titan declined to adapt at all.
          • A Titan's absent concepts defined the way it interpreted the concepts it adapted from other Primordials.
          • A Primordial cannot truly grasp an idea without making it part of itself, and seeing it through its own lens.
        • The Primordials are so internally consistent with their own concepts, they posses only a limited form of free will.
        • Primordial component souls spring from the conflicts between a Primordial's concepts, allowing conceptual flexibility.
        • The Primordials once maintained active commitments to the Shaping effects they used to build Creation.
          • These shaping effects shaped both Creation and the committed Primordial him- or her-self, linking them absolutely.
          • This forced the Titans to be more static than they liked, but defined major aspects of each of their identities.
          • The Maidens and other wonders were created to maintain these effects in Creation independent the Titans.
          • The Three Spheres Cataclysm was the end of one of these Shaping effects, not the imposition of one.
          • Some aspects of Creation remain tied to actively committed effects held by the Yozi and their demons.
        • The most essential difference between a Titan and a Yozi is that Yozi have been stripped of the ability to change.
          • This was necessary, in order to create a stable context against which to design the surrender oaths.
          • This also prevents the Yozi from enacting direct changes to the fabric and structure of Creation.
        • The most essential difference between a Titan and a Neverborn is that the Neverborn are stripped of all free will.
          • The Neverborn are absolutely internally consistent, their concepts calcified into true physical laws.
          • The semblance of Neverborn intelligence is a construct of their trapped deva and intra-hierarchical politics.
          • If all the Primordials were slain, the world would obey strict physical rules and magic would cease to function.
          • Truly destroying the Neverborn would unmake their contributions to Creation, causing unknowable Cataclysms.
        • Battle was the least part of the Exalted's work in overthrowing the Titans. Understanding was the most vital aspect.
          • The 'defeat' of each Titan required entirely new strategies, techniques, tools, and modes of thought and action.
          • The Exalted learned to plan surgical victories after early, brute force efforts created the Neverborn.
          • Each Titan had to be defeated in such a way as to implement precisely designed changes to its soul structure.
          • Designing these alterations was an architectural task at least equal in complexity of executing the design.
          • There is a lost Craft - Soul Architecture - which was invented explicitly to complete this undertaking.


        On a related note, I really want to run a Primordial War game some time, structured such that the defeat of each Titan is essentially a huge existential heist movie plot. Unfortunately, Exalted is... ill-suited to that type of gameplay, mechanically. Also, I'd be terrible at it.

                Regarding Martial Arts:
                • Every supernatural martial art is a reflection of an entity that once existed in Creation.
                  • Terrestrial martial arts reflect the nature of now-vanished gods and spirits.
                  • Celestial martial arts reflect the powers of fallen incarna, deva, and great heroes.
                  • Sidereal martial arts reflect concepts torn from the Titans in their downfall.
                • A supernatural martial art might or might not resemble the panoply of the thing it reflects.
                • Martial arts are not so much invented as they are discovered and explored by the enlightened.
                  • The first master of an art seeks the emptiness of the art within himself, and fills it.
                  • In this way, even a forgotten art may someday be rediscovered by a new first master.
                  • Truly creating a new art would required building a great entity, then destroying it.
                • The spiritual void left by the death of a powerful entity begs to be filled with essence.
                  • Essence poured into these voids naturally takes the shape of a martial art's charms.
                  • This is how mortals can learn martial arts, despite having no intrinsic magical powers.
                  • Accessing these powers involves mimicking the lost entity, in form as well as in spirit.
                • The spiritual void left by the death of such entities may be felt even before their demise.
                  • The existence of styles associated with the still living suggests their eventual death.
                  • This begs the question of the origin of the various Hero styles of the Exalted.

                    Exaltations reflect their exalt type when it comes to the moment of exaltation. (as well as the selection process for an Exalt where appropriate).

                    Solar Exaltations are highly specialized and biased towards their previous owners' skillsets. It's possible for a Solar exaltation to change caste, but it is highly unlikely and in fact it's much more likely that the Exaltation will skip over you and find someone more suited to its skillset. When it does Exalt you, it still increases your power, but it prioritizes skillsets you already have and/or have need of at the time of Exaltaion. A swordsman isn't suddenly going to become the world's greatest speaker, and if he did he wouldn't in the middle of a mass melee.

                    Lunar Exaltations don't particularly care about getting the same caste that they previously held and are perfectly fluid in that regard, but do bear the same totem each life. Lunar exaltations try their best to give boosts across the board so that the Lunar is as prepared as prepared as possible for as many scenarios as possible. Since a Lunar's caste is not fixed, they don't need to worry too much about the skills of their exalts, so they don't. Lunar exaltations also heal their host completely at the time of Exaltation, and give them a rush of energy/excitement (see Keychain of Creation) so that they'll live through whatever scenario made them so desperate to be chosen for Exaltation.

                    Dragon-blooded that are fated to exalt (for lack of a better term) do so past a certain age, but only while doing something that reflects their particular aspect's temperament and/or skillsets. Fire aspects may get their exaltation while angry, or passionately screaming, or in a fistfight or whatever. I'd elaborate, but I don't actually know enough about Dragon-blooded to comment further. (I'd get the caste book but 3e's finally close to coming out and all the info there would be obsolete)

                    Also, Exaltations are sentient enough to make judgement calls on whether or not a person is worthy to Exalt.

                            After centuries of the native peoples of the Caul building gigantic mechanical idols and monuments to their protean god-beast masters, the Lunar cabal in charge decided to just add admissions and concessions stands and make a mint off the place. Now, Lunarland, "The Most Primal Place in Creation," rakes in millions each year, even if it takes a full week sometimes to get a ride on Splash Maru.

                            In RY 700, House Ragara sought to build a super-bridge from the Imperial City to Thorns and initiate a series of mega-bridges to more fully prosecute Imperial policy in the Threshold. After the Mask of Winters arose in Thorns, the project was halted, but this left a full two hundred and fifty stretch of unfinished bridge stretching out into the Inland Sea. Now, with himself fully in control of Thorns, the Mask of Winters has been offering opulent sums to the Imperial Ministry to finish the bridge, for "Peaceful Co-Existence," of course.

                            Not to be out-done in overly-contrived and rather ill-thought out plans, the Lunar Cabal of the Caul built a bridge from Feroxi to the Lap. Using Sorcery and Fae allies, they only worked at night for seven years straight, with neither Satrapy able to make any sense of the bridge that seemed to build itself up at an incredible rate. Only the gift baskets with the rather cryptic notes of "HA! We beat you!" sent to each of the Satraps gave any indication of who or what built this gigantic structure.

                            The last in the Bridge Wars, the Fae Courts of the East and West put aside their differences and built a bridge of their own from the Pole of Wood to the Pole of Water. This bridge, existing on a scale far beyond the scope of even Fae imagination has infinite length, and no one yet has traversed it in its entirety.

                                    There IS no such city as Hollow/Nexus...and there never was.

                                    First Age Exalted experimented with and perfected the magic behind the illusory city of Atsiluth Eternal, and used those techniques to create Hollow, a consensual hallucination that has some degree of "reality" to those attuned to it, without ever actually raising a wall or laying a stone to do so. This illusion persists to the current day, and countless generations have been born, lived and died on the riverside banks of a city that wasn't even there.

                                    The illusion is anchored to the massive Jade "dam" in the riverbed; anyone who has ever heard of the great city of Nexus and/or approches it by foot or by the enchanted waters of the river is sucked into the delusion. Beings who approach by air who have somehow managed to never hear of the most famous city in the world have a chance of seeing the truth. Also, beings outside of Fate with a Primordial nature (such as GSPs) may be able to penetrate the illusion fairly easily. The Fair Folk are probably immune, but are used to living in fiction and probably find the whole thing hilarious anyway, so they just play along!

                                    The Emissary is an artificial being, more program than spirit, set up by the creators of the city to maintain its existence and to delete any who threaten its stability.
