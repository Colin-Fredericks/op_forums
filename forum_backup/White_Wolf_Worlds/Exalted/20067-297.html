                    Originally posted by crabbadon View Post
                    I don't think this has been asked:
                    What happens if you try to Defend Other against an attack which already targets you? e.g. when a battlegroup is attacking.
                    We've been essentially treating it as being two attacks, but we're not sure if there's any particular textual backing.
                    Defend Other lets you use your Parry value in place of an ally's. If an attack doesn't clear your Parry value, then it doesn't generally matter who the attacker(s) is/are. For the sake of discussion, we'll say a Dawn is Defending her squishy Twilight friend with her Parry of 6. Whether one thug (Size 0) or a dozen (Size 1) attack the Twilight (or the Dawn, for that matter), nothing's going to happen unless the attack gets six successes.

                    Now let's say the one thug gets seven successes. He can attack the Dawn using those seven successes, or he can use the one threshold success as his attack against the Twilight. Keep in mind that those threshold successes then need to overcome the Twilight's Defense in order to hit. If he'd picked the Dawn as his original target to begin with, then it's no different than any other attack.

                    If the dozen thugs got seven, they'd get to attack both the Dawn and the Twilight through the usual area attack mechanic using their full seven successes against both targets. It's an all or nothing deal here because they really are attacking the Dawn and Twilight simultaneously anyway, it's just that the Dawn is spending an action to effectively raise the Twilight's Parry value to match her own.

                    To put it another way, a battle group is abstracted as a single character, so it gets one attack roll and applies it to all desired targets within range. For close combat that's anyone within close range of the battle group, such as our Dawn and Twilight in the example above. There's no call or need to roll separate attacks against those two whether the Dawn's taken a Defend Other action or not.

                                                        Wisdom of the Celestial Crane, the Crane Style capstone (pg. 446), lets you take an existing Intimacy and strengthen it to Defining intensity. This requires that a) you incapacitate the opponent with the Charm and b) the Intimacy to be strengthened fits Humble Enlightenment Commentary's criteria.

                                                        That being said, since HEC allows you to instill a Minor Tie to a group or a Minor Principle representing a philosophical or ethical belief, your ST might decide that an Intimacy of love toward your particular character (such as the Tie created by TLD) doesn't fit HEC's criteria, and thus isn't compatible with WotCC. You might be able to get around that with creative stunting, assuming that everything else checks out.

                                                        On the bright side, WotCC doesn't care about the Intimacy's initial strength, so you could even go from Minor to Defining in one go under the right circumstances.
