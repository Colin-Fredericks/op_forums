    Nalak: These examples may not be the best ones in your defense since they seem to support the Ex2 mentality of "abyssals are evil nihilists who kill for fun and profit." How would you play an abyssal that wasn't a proponent of Oblivion? Or a pacifist? Is that even a thing you want to be able to do?     Well to be fair murdering for fun and profit crops up in basically every splat and is tricky standpoint to move away from when your group for exalted is also your dnd group.

    Pacifists don't generally work in our games we don't have anything against it just none of our group finds the idea of being completely refusing to fight very fun, so nobody plays it. You'll get characters who prefer to find other options of course, but if it comes down to it the only times the party isn't willing to throw down is when they think the enemy will just destroy them.

    Actually the characters worked pretty well. I mean let's be fair slaughtering bandits and slavers helps people just not in the same way that a doctor does or converting those criminals to guards or something. So the one in it for the hell of it didn't care about Oblivion and would just do things based on his own opinions with information from effort and potential backlash. The one who was out for killing slavers he just considered them a blight on the world that had it coming. It wasn't a case of there is no point to anything more of "these people are scum and don't deserve to live." Essentially the more psychotic avenger type evil being subjective.

    I got nothing on the caster that was just the kind of person her player wanted to play and no one was made uncomfortable by it,so it went through.

    The other two really operated on the idea that the solar was a psychopath and would cause death and destruction regardless of any actions by them. (The solar tended to try and set off minor wyld hunts so he could kill them. If not direct attacks on imperial bases.) Their logic was if they kept this solar alive they could do use him as a smokescreen for pursuing their own activities with the claim of "So long as he thinks we're his allies, and not all that bad, we can manipulate him to do our job with less risks and we get to reinforce the 'Solars are monsters' view in the world too." So they got to use the Malice ,the solar, being a psycho to justify why they would do things that weren't directly feeding their faction goals.

    As for what we do when we play we make use of the death aspect. Usually their death informs the character in some way that was kind of important. It really varied based on what the individual wanted to do with the character. I mean I've had one player who literally played an abyssal because they wanted a justifiable excuse for blood drinking. Frankly I've had at least one abyssal character who worked the mentor angle on the grounds that he knows failure better than others because of the circumstances of his exaltation. (He got himself killed attempting heroics and got to see the impact of his failure on the event.) I'm the ST though, so mentors and informants tend to be the more likely characters to be recurring you know.

            They focused on avoiding resonance where they could. If they don't do anything to trigger resonance then I can't really hit them with resonance. I mean a basic thing for them was the faction goal is kill all things that one of them decided he would prioritize the destruction of slavers meant he was still going along with faction goals. So in a hypothetical scenario a town is under attack by bandits,slavers, monsters, you know things that aren't the forces of oblivion. PC goes in and decides to destroy the attackers, even though doing so saves the lives of the towns folk that isn't a resonance triggering effect since the method chosen was slaughter. I can't very well throw resonance at a player for no reason because then they get peeved and people aren't having fun. As for the Deathlords well the PCs were all new exalts with about a year under their belts they had been given a job to do for their boss, but otherwise the Deathlords felt the PCs were just insignificant tools not like they need micromanaging. As for the oblivion connection well there's not a whole lot to be done in regards to having an indirect connection to a blackhole.

            As I said previously the Abyssals are the dead their characters are the ones that have a great versatility in just that fact and how that based for them. Did your character die failing, die to betrayal, die randomly, die alone, die with others, die a hero? They accepted an extended life in exchange for power and service do they regret this, are they ok with this, do they think it was right, do they give a crap about it, hell are they trying to get an old friend to exalt abyssal too? I'm the storyteller I play characters, so what kind of person I am allowed to play matters and there is a lot of versatility in the kind of character that is justified in their backstory including the phrase "I think it went wrong once I started to be able to look through my chest and see behind me."

                Nalak: yeah if the only thing your party wants to do is have fun and spread some mayhem then they are naturally going to resent you for putting any restraints on them including the Great Curse and/or resonance. That doesn't mean these are bad mechanics in general, just for your party. Honestly I think you'd be better off just ignoring such mechanics in your game in favor of "Grand Theft Yeddim" rules where none of the power players work against you and the Wyld Hunt is comically inept. The majority of canon exalted is not set up in such a way as to support this playstyle of "no consequences" and I really don't think it should be.

                Death and second life are a major theme for abyssals, which is one of the reasons I'd like them to have more ghost-like mechanics such as those proposed. I don't think that the "you only live twice" mechanic is enough to separate them from other splats by itself, though. Most exalts have near-death experiences as part of their backstories; as mentioned previously most Dawns would have died if not for their timely exaltation. The question becomes how do you keep this theme fresh? That's why so much of the abyssal charmset is death-focused: they want you constantly thinking grim thoughts. So I'd say that what you like about abyssals is preserved in their charmset, which I'm not proposing to change except to add more goals besides "kill that guy."
