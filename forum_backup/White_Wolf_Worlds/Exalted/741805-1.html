    Originally posted by Tikor View Post
    I refuse to believe grapple-bonus charms won't work just as well or even better if you are a literal gorilla. For every shape that would make a Charm enhancing action that would seem odd or perhaps be weaker (range bonuses to ranged attacks if you are a rhino?) there's going to be a grapple/gorilla example. This sounds like a hard problem, out of the scope of my homebrew.
    But that leaves you with only a few options:

    1. Gorilla Lunars are literally unbeatable in a grapple, because they're throwing around ridiculously big dice pools for free, plus powerful Celestial-level charms. Remember, Lunars aren't going to be the only people who have "grapple really well" as a theme. Letting the Lunar gorilla grappler get his hands on you is probably a bad idea no matter who you are, but there have to be people at least competitive with them, and I don't see any way to do it if Lunars get massive gorilla strength plus grappling charms on par with everyone else who is good at it.

    2. Lunar grappling charms are noticeably weaker compared to any other Celestial splat that has a grapple build, or their overall grappling suite, relative to everyone else's, has holes in them that you're supposed to use turning into a gorilla to fix. This means that it's impossible to be a really good Lunar grappler without turning into a gorilla; that's at least plausible design, but I don't think it's where things will go.

    3. Lunars have grappling charms that have extra bonuses when you're in human form, or which are somehow incompatible with the bonuses from being a gorilla, or cost more when you're a gorilla or whatever.

    Making Lunar grapple-bonus charms work as well or better when you're an actual gorilla means you end up with Lunars that are either hilariously unbeatable in gorilla form, or noticeably worse grapplers than other grapple-focused Celestials in human form.

    I suspect that the best answer is something akin to 3, but to do it in a subtle way that doesn't immediately leap off the page as "this charm is weaker when you're a gorilla". For instance, Lunars might have a "strength of a gorilla" that raises your strength to a gorilla's even when you're in human form -- this feels thematically appropriate, and the fact that it doesn't actually do anything when you're already a gorilla (or maybe has some alternate minor effect, to keep it from being useless) doesn't leap off the page.

                                    Originally posted by Tikor View Post
                                    Making dice in excess of the character's normal dice from shape-shifting count as dice from Charms seems like a readily available anti-stacking restriction that would put the best Lunar grappler, when stunting, on par with other Celestials.
                                    That's a start, but it alone is not enough in 3e. Remember that in 3e, IAM (and its equivalents) do not exist. For most people, scenelong dice-adders are simply not a thing; getting five extra scenelong dice, even if they're not cap-busting, is basically like getting five extra motes an action, and absent other concerns it means that over the course of a fight you're going to trivially crush almost everyone, since they're going to have to burn motes every action just to keep up with you.

                                    So even if Lunar bonuses from shapeshifting count as dice from charms (which I assume they will), it's not enough to balance them on its own -- there will need to be other considerations across the Lunar charmset to balance out the fact that a Lunar shapeshifted into a bear is throwing around massive dice-pools for free that other people would have to burn motes every action to sustain. The trick is to do this in a way so that:

                                    1. Shapeshifting is still awesome and makes you very very good at everything it should logically make you very very good at, and,
                                    2. Shapeshifting isn't so essential that Lunars completely suck in human form.

                                    (Lunars have grappled with some variation of this problem in all editions, really, but it's a particularly thorny issue in 3e because nobody else really gets powerful scenelong buffs on par with turning into a bear. In some ways this is a good thing, mind, but it's still tricky to balance.)
