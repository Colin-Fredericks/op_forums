    While I am firmly of the opinion that Creation is on the verge of an industrial revolution, it is worth noting that at least one force in Creation has been actively suppressing technological advancements across much of Creation. The Realm doesn't want Threshold states advancing technologically. It's much easier to maintain control of a population if their weapons and armor are centuries behind your own. They cant challenge you financially if they can't produce anything better than you. Keep the populations paying in raw materials rather than processed goods so that there isn't any incentive to advance manufacturing technology.

    They scavenger lands have not excuse though, they should be I a really good place technologically, especially with The Guild being based at their heart. The guild has every reason to drive advances in technology. More goods, better goods, cheaper goods, new goods, faster travel, better security, The Guild needs it all and is the reason Creation needs an Industrial Revolution.

                        Originally posted by Kyeudo View Post
                        The state of technology in Creation is fascinating. It's been over 700 years since the last major catastrophe, a catastrophe that didn't send human understanding of the world back to the Stone Age. It's a world with thousands of people with super-human capacity to invent and innovate - a Terrestrial Exalt with just a Lore and Craft Excellency is capable of feats of thought and craftsmanship so far beyond Einstein and Da Vinci as to make them look like college frat boys. Yet it's a world that hasn't really made it out of the Iron Age. Considering that the Black Plague killed ~60% of Europe's population back in the 1350s and today we've been to the Moon and are working on traveling to Mars, what's holding back Creation? Creation even has advantages that our real world doesn't have, like a longer growing season and more landmass to support large populations.

                        A thought came to me while I was considering the futility of arguments for or against the existence of God: the scientific method doesn't work properly in Creation. Here in our world, we can reliably perform experiments. When we burn hydrogen and oxygen, we always get water. If we drop something, it always falls towards the center of mass. So on and so forth. We can design experiments that allow us to hold the majority of things constant and only adjust our experimental variables.

                        In Creation, however, the world is animistic and it's difficult to control enough variables. You are doing Mendel Gregor's pea plant experiments, just about to work out dominant and recessive genes, and then along comes the god of pea plants, who likes you because of how many pea plants you've been growing, and then "blam" suddenly your short pea plant strain now produces children that are taller than your tall strain. Or you are trying to work out the equations for gravity, but the Loom of Fate chokes on a demon's influence in the next province over and so today feathers are falling slower than they should. The best you can get with a simple experiment is a "most of the time, this seems to be the result". That's fine for most things, but the more complicated the thing you are trying to get to work, the more you are piling uncertainty upon uncertainty until the result is a complete roll of the dice.

                        Perhaps the easiest way to grasp what this means in practical terms is that in Creation, anything you hear could be true. If the lady next door shows you a gold nugget and told you she pulled it out of her oven instead of the cornbread she had been trying to make, that could actually be the case. Baking another batch of cornbread in the exact same oven and getting a loaf of cornbread wouldn't disprove her claim. Her experience could be one of those one-offs, where a god decided to influence things or a sorcerous working went sideways somewhere or the pattern spiders decided her fate needed to change.

                        Figured I'd share this and see what other people thought about it.
                        I love your original post because this is what I try to explain over and over to a lot of players : Science doesn't mean anything in a lot of settings, because science depends on philosophical premises (like reconducing experiments in the same conditions, etc ...) and thus we can't have science in Exalted. I'd even go a bit further than you and say than even without the interference of nearby demons and gods in your neighborhood, the experiments would fail because, well, is the world even made of atoms and of the same physical laws that are universal in Creation ? I don't think so.

                            While 2e heavily emphasized that everything in Creation is made of Essence on a fundamental level, in all editions there is an assumption that barring explicit magic interference, yes, things do work like in real world. There may be magical diseases cooked up by the Deathlords that make you crap out your soul, but cholera is still caused by bacteria in the water and avoidable with proper hygiene - and you could still cure it with antibiotics if you knew how.

                            The problem with promoting the scientific method and worldview in those conditions is that once you encounter that Deathlord-brewed disease that makes you crap out your soul, all your knowledge of bacteria won't help you one bit, but that magical spring blessed by a goddess of healing still might. And if never drinking water that has been touched by fire is what's keeping the Fair Folk away from your tribe, you're pretty screwed if someone craps in your village well. So it's often easier to just place your trust in the supernatural.

                                Well, I'm fine with this interpretation (or this rule, if it's written explicitly), but I really prefer taking from the larger spectrum of "We can't have science" (or rather, some characters can have science because this is sort of their paradigm) where there is no clear objective statement about "The world works like this", and it can then be left to the interpretation and powers of the characters to try to understand what is and what is not. A bit in the same way, I don't like playing in "our world" in a setting where the ST would say "God objectively doesn't exist in my setting. I feel this is sort of information that can take away a form of immersion from the player, even if this has obviously no effect on the character.

                                            Scavenger Lands are littered with remnants of bygone ages that offer untold riches to those bold and smart enough to claim and use them. Why invest in alchemists to improve their technology of extracting cocaine from coca leaves when you can invest in an expedition to capture a dinosaur that pisses liquid cocaine? Why invest in your silver mining technology when a single artifact from a raided Solar tomb can net you a price that dwarfs the lifetime yield of several silver mines?

                                            And let's not forget slaves. I heard a theory recently that the Roman Empire didn't undergo an Industrial Revolution of its own because slave labor effectively obviated the need for automation. Dunno if that's true about Rome specifically, but it does stand to reason that cheap and abundant labor force will at least deprioritize technological advancements of the kind that led us to the Industrial Revolution.

                                            And if you can somehow combine magic and slave labor... now that's the kind of innovation the Guild really likes! Check out the story of Luna's Sorrow in Masters of Jade for a prime example of how the Guild innovates without bringing Creation anywhere closer to an industrial revolution like the one that happened in our world.

                                                Necessity is the Mother of all invention.

                                                That's Creations problem.

                                                An exalt encountering a problem will probably solve it with magic (charms or sorcery). If that won't work he could produce a machine that solves his problem.

                                                But if a machine can solve his problem a magical machine will solve it even better. That machine could be reproduced by another exalt (perhaps a bit modified to better suit that exalts situation) but it won't become a technological breakthrough.

                                                So as far as exalts are concerned, they probably won't have a large technological impact. And the presence of exalted will slow down mortal invention overall. What's the easiest way for a mortal to solve a problem he can't solve on his own? Invent something noone ever thought of before? Or ask/pay/humbly request an exalt to solve his problem for him?

                                                I also has to agree that with an unreliable scientific principle and no fossil fuels something like our own technological revolution probably won't happen.
