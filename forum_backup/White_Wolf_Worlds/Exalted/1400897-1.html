        Originally posted by LadyLens View Post
        More toward the realism end
        I dunno; that's the end that has to deal with things like inbreeding and the fact of that level of isolation and confinement definitely driving people insane.

        If not actual the actual full-blown hallucinations, dissociation and suicidal tendencies, people in such a situation are liable to become extremely irritable with one another, and there doesn't exactly seem to be much to be occupied with besides sitting around and becoming more and more bothered by the behaviour of others.

        Originally posted by LadyLens
        over the course of their long isolation, the people of the vessel have certainly adapted to their environment by at least having a higher tolerance for cold and pressure, and since the population is so small and the margins of survival quite narrow, well...
        I kind of feel as though your focus on things like adaptation to physical factors and the logistics reinforces my point about generation ships being a thing devised by hard scientists that a sociologist or psychologist would bring down in the planning stages. The things you're alluding to at the end there just add to the stresses that people are under.

        Could a society ultimately adapt to such pressures? Possibly, but the proposed starting group is so small that it's hard to believe they wouldn't have wiped themselves out within a generation or two.

        Also the lack of access to sunlight is going to be mentally and physically unhealthy. I'm not sure if that aquaculture will provide them with much vitamin C.

        If nothing else, the scenario in which they're actually discovered by players ought to be the kind of thing that comes with the most extreme kinds of culture shock. Holding out hope for escape for a thousand years... after half that time I'd wonder at the inhabitants even believing that there's anything more to the world besides themselves and the water above.

        By the way, I find the premise that this ocean-going vessel would be crewed by personnel who would not know how to navigate on open water. Even if the ship was fully automated or something, sailing is one of those professions that tends to have a very enduring tradition of planning for the possibility of things going terribly wrong, and that can include being trained in navigational techniques that aren't so dependent on their instruments.

        There's also the matter of how ships tend to log their travel plans before departing port and take consistent and frequently travelled routes for purposes that include the fact that if something goes wrong you're more likely to be happened upon by somebody else that can help. Those kinds of things are why it was ever possible to find sunken vessels decades or centuries after the fact even without survivors popping up to seek help.

        Still, I'm saying this primarily because... well, I think it's interesting, but a close second is to advise not delving too deeply into the can of worms and just softballing the idea.

        Heck, I would suggest just having them happen to land in a deep sea demesne and let the Essence mutate them into a form that could more readily survive in their bubble (although if that also covers for them psychologically then that would just enhance the degree to which I would expect them to come back out strange and alienated)

        I have approximate knowledge of many things.

                I'd imagine that they'd be on the one side incredibly gregarious, and on the other side, extremely solitary, swinging sharply between the two.

                The lack of sunlight is a potential problem, unless they can synthesize vitamin C, as almost every animal in the world can, and can get vitamin D from their diet. Of course, that assumes that those vitamins even exist in Creation, which is not a given. Indeed, there's evidence that the humours are real, in the form of the Soul-Perfecting Elixir and its accompanying Merits.

                Massive culture shock is pretty much a given and if not the point, then certainly a main point in this scenario.

                I'm a small-craft sailor myself, and I do know a bit about celestial navigation, enough to know that if someone hasn't explicitly trained in doing so, navigating the open ocean without at least charts of some sort is extremely difficult, and if, like a Water Aspect, you don't have the benefit of vessel and crew to keep a mostly-steady course, every time you take a rest, your position changes in an unknown way, so you need to re-establish your position every time you wake up. It's not impossible, I don't claim that, but it's extremely difficult, especially since DB can no longer orient themselves on their elemental poles.

                You're right about logs, but most salvaged vessel are recovered from shallow water, and not after civilization-ending catastrophe.

                Oh, and the Sarli came to rest in what we'd call the mesopelagic zone: very little light, but more than none.

                                        Bad diet can still unbalance the humours. The problem then becomes one of finding suitable strengthening foods, perhaps some sea-floor plants that thrive wildly in the bright light of the tanks. Back on the topic of society, I think they'd be strongly reactionary, given the difficulty of simply surviving and the risk of any change killing everyone, or at least dropping the population below the threshold of viability. Resources are very scarce, to the point that most people likely wear only loincloths made from tanned fish skins and cord for the central stalks of kelp-analogue water plants, and everything is used and reused to the last, including their dead going to the aquaculture tanks, where they nourish the plants that purify the greywater and allow fish to thrive. The tanks need constant monitoring and the pumps (by now just a series of bladders and valves) are the main drain upon the Sarli's scant resources.

                                                Originally posted by LadyLens View Post
                                                During the Usurpation, many vehicles were destroyed, or at best wrecked beyond repair, including the submersible passenger-carrying freighter Sarli, lost with all hands somewhere between Wu Jian and the Caul. An enormous vessel, carrying roughly five hundred people, passengers and crew combined, the vessel suffered battle damage that jammed the ballast hatches open, and sent it to the bottom of the ocean. There in the black cold, the vessel settled mostly keel-up on the bottom, and the survivors turned their attentions to the problems of survival. Air and water, provided by air recycling and desalination equipment intended to function indefinitely, were not problems, but food was another matter, for upon the floor of the deep sea, there is no light to nourish crops, and little food filters down so far. By necessity, the people of Sarli have converted their vessel's immense holds into aquaculture tanks, the main draw upon the vessel's power supply being the tanks' sorcerous lamps. The ship draws power from the dragon lines of the sea, its supply inexhaustible but far from limitless; there is enough for some heat, enough that the ship is cool (about 13C/55F) rather that frigid, light for the food tanks, and for the purifiers, but not much beyond, a few lamps in the corridors.

                                                The battle damage is relatively minor; a single major repair project would be enough to restore the vessel's ability to surface. Unfortunately, the key part needed is too sophisticated to be replaced without Craft(First Age Artifice) and Celestial sorcery, and there were no sorceror-engineers with the needed breadth and depth of knowledge aboard when the vessel sank.
