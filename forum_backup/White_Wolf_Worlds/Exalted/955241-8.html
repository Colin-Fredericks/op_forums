                                                            Leases and Literacy: 7 (WINNER! 4-Vote Powerup Unlocked!)
                                                            Breakfast and Intrigue: 3

                                                            Wyld Times at the Immaculate Temple: 3
                                                            Morning in the Market: 1
                                                            Dancing with the Eels: 1

                                                            It feels strange to turn down free food…but right now, you and Dwi are doing business together, and you don’t want to be in her debt any more than you already are. Besides…staying here, staying warm and dry and enjoying the gentle euphoria of the poppy juice…it’s too tempting. You don’t want to give yourself a chance to rethink your decision not to stay and work for Dwi over breakfast.

                                                            “Thank you, Aunt Dwi,” you lie, “But I’ve already eaten this morning. I think I should go, I’ll get in your way if I’m here. Can I leave this River Dragon eyeball and the teeth with you?”

                                                            “Of course, dear.” Dwi nods as you put the goods on her desk. “And after I’ve made our deal with the alchemist…how can I find you? I do want to make sure you get your share.”

                                                            “I’ll come back tomorrow morning,” you promise, making your way to the door. “And Aunt Dwi…do you know any landlords with cheap rooms to rent?”

                                                            “Hm. I know a few...you’re looking to get your own apartment?” You nod. Dwi raises an eyebrow.

                                                            “Living space in Nexus isn’t cheap, child. It’s why so many of my girls choose to live in their rooms here, I give them a discount. Most people split the cost with several room mates…what’s your budget?”

                                                            You’re a little paranoid about telling anyone you have money on you, especially so MUCH of it. Living on the street has taught you to be cagey about that sort of thing…but you decide you can trust Dwi.

                                                            “I, uh, have one Dinar.”

                                                            “I’m impressed! And do you have any friends who could chip in to split the rent with you?”

                                                            You consider. The closest thing is Rat, and he’s just as broke as you were yesterday. “…no.”

                                                            “Hm.” Dwi shrugs. “A Dinar won’t get you very far down here in Nighthammer proper unless you split rent with several other people, there’s too many people who want to live close to the foundries and the dock markets…but if you head north to Nightside, rent is pretty cheap…actually, yes, head to the Blackwall neighborhood and talk to Gleaming Smile. He’s one of my old regulars, and he manages some property there. Mention me and I’m sure he’ll give you a fair bargain. Make sure you get something on at least the second floor.”

                                                            “Floods?”

                                                            “At least a couple times a year.”

                                                            “Thanks, Aunt Dwi.”

                                                            “Please, think nothing of it. But you know, for a Dinar, I could let you stay here for…”
                                                            You shake your head. She stops.
                                                            *****


                                                            Stepping out onto the street, you stuff a wad of rough fabric into your mouth to wet it, then unfold it and tie it once more around your mouth and nose-the masks work better when they’re damp. The Poor Man’s Breath has gotten worse as the morning shift at the foundries has gotten to work, and the fog rolling through the streets is a dingy gray. By nightfall it’ll be black.
                                                            Nightside isn’t a long trip, but you make sure to make it take a little longer-you avoid the parts of town where you know some of the bigger kids hang out, and you try to cut a line somewhere between the parts of the markets wealthy enough that you’ll be out of place and the parts poor enough that you’re likely to be jumped. After about an hour, you’ve reached the Blackwall neighborhood.

                                                            It’s…well, if you were out begging today it’s not where you’d be going. The tenement buildings are uneven here, and the roads are little more than muddy tracks. The alleys are tight even for Nexus-you could walk down them without difficulty, but an adult would need to move slowly, and in single file if he was in a group. Blackwall’s name comes from the coloration of its buildings: they’re all just clay bricks and mud and timber supports and straw…and the plaster of the outer walls is discolored below a height of about eight feet from years of Black Snow and repeated flooding. It’s crowded-you bump into plenty of people on your way in-and everyone is wearing masks. Through the hubbub of conversation in the shanty markets, you can hear the constant sound of coughing. Young men with tattoos and long knives tucked into their sashes lurk in doorways smoking and talking quietly to one another.

                                                            Whether it’s the black fog, the drugs, the floods, the violence, or the mold that lingers after the floods recede…people born in Nightside don’t usually make it past thirty, and old folks who move here don’t usually make it more than a decade before the Black Lung takes them.

                                                            But they do have homes! You can see windows open all up and down the squat tenement blocks here, with faces looking out of them. Some of them have flower pots with flowers. You see a few gleaming balls of red amidst the greenery on a rooftop-someone’s tomatoes are coming in. The sky is full of clotheslines flapping in the breeze, and the clotheslines are full of clothes. You’re not sure how you’d feel about wandering this neighborhood after dark, at least before you got to know people…but in the smog-tinged morning sunlight there are little kids chasing each other around in the street and old ladies selling squash and herbs. Even the fishy smell of the shallow canal one block over seems comforting. This neighborhood seems...nice. You’d definitely want to learn all the rules: what alleys are safe and what aren’t, who is friendly all the time and who is dangerous when drunk, and you wouldn’t want to stay here forever…but you think you could find yourself liking Nightside.

                                                            Gleaming Smile isn’t a hard man to find-asking about on the street quickly gets you directed to his “office.” It’s a run-down room on the first floor of a run-down building. The building is right next to the canal, and the stairs are caked with mold from the floods. A rat as long as your arm stares at you fearlessly from one of the steps, only scampering away as you walk past. When you arrive, you find that Gleaming Smile obviously lives in his office, though he’s drawn a curtain across it to separate his desk and several cabinets of paperwork from his living area. His name fits: his smile is full of false teeth, mostly copper or brass.

                                                            “So. Dwi sent you, eh?” He smiles, and the light from his open window makes his teeth sparkle in the gloom. He must polish them. “Must be pretty handy ‘tween the sheets if that old witch is doin’ you favors, eh, boy? Well, you’re lucky. I do have a room to rent. Third floor, even! My current tenant is late on her rent, and not the first time neither. Haven’t seen her in weeks, wouldn’t surprise me if she got her damn self gutted in an alley someplace... Three quarters of a Dinar gets you a month. You pay up front, you stay the month. If I don’t have next month’s rent by the first day of next month, you’re late, and I charge you one eighth-Dinar as a penalty. If I don’t have my money by the sixth day of the month, I come into your apartment, take all your shit, and sell off your possessions to try and cover the rest of the month. And I kick you out on the street. Got it?”

                                                            “…got it.” You nod.

                                                            “Good. I take payment in Guild Scrip if you got it. You pay in coin, I give you a monthly discount of one-eighth Dinar, it saves me on exchange fees.”

                                                            “Deal. When can I move in?”

                                                            “First you gotta sign a leasing contract agreeing to to all that, plus covering any damages you make to the building. You punch a hole in a wall or something and you pay to fix it. You can’t pay to fix it? Then you WORK to pay to fix it. You owe me on repairs and try to run away or weasel out of it…well, I’ve got a deal with the Iron League, and they track you down. This contract makes it so anyone helping you hide from them is impeding trade. Got me?”

                                                            Gleaming Smile has pulled out a contract and an inkwell from a drawer and placed them on his desk.

                                                            You nod. “I won’t damage anything.”

                                                            “Then sign here.”

                                                            “I, um. I can’t write.”

                                                            “Can you read, boy?”

                                                            You shake your head.

                                                            “Shouldn’t sign something if you can’t read it.” He glowers at you. “It’s not safe.”

                                                            You give him your best disarming smile. “Aunt Dwi told me you drive a hard bargain but you’re an honest man, Gleaming Smile. I trust her taste in men.”

                                                            He gets a chuckle out of that. “She’s your Aunt, is she? Fair point, lad! Just…put an X right here, and dip your thumb in the inkwell…then wipe it on this cloth…good. Now press your thumb by the X on that line…good. It’s just this once, though. Don’t go signing shit you can’t read! Or at least get somebody you trust as can read it for you!”

                                                            “Thank you. I won’t.”

                                                            “Good. Right this way.”

                                                            You’re given a brass key on a braided cord thong and advised to keep it around your neck at all times-replacing a lost key counts as damaging Gleaming Smile’s property, he explains. You go up another flight of stairs and down a hall…to your new apartment! You can’t help being excited. Your heart beats a little faster as Gleaming Smile opens the door.

                                                            It’s…furnished. There’s a wooden table, and a chair, and a cupboard. There’s a closet with hangers. There’s a window-no glass, but bamboo shutters, and a little ledge with a flower pot and a dead plant on it. There’s a chamber pot, and a washbasin, and even a little black iron cooking stove on legs and a wooden box next to it full of pressed charcoal! A bedroll has been rolled out onto the floor and takes up most of the room.

                                                            The whole place stinks-the chamberpot hasn’t been emptied. The hangers in the closet are festooned with adult-sized clothing. The cupboard is open, and appears to be filled with dirty dishes…as is the washbasin. There’s a huge scorch mark on the ceiling above the little iron stove where something cooking below set the ceiling paint alight. There are clothes all over the place, and every surface-the table, the chair, most of the floor and bedroll-is stacked high with crumpled scrolls and empty wine bottles…and books. You’ve never seen so many books!

                                                            “Fucking scholars.” Gleaming Smile growls. “Blew all her money on drinks and books, I wager. Owes me two months' back rent, and how am I supposed to make up the loss? Not like I can sell empty bottles…and I’m betting the only other people who’ll buy her fuckin' scrolls and books are other broke wannabe-intellectuals. I swear, if the whole damn University of Nexus went up in flames nobody’s miss ‘em.”

                                                            “It, um. Seems a bit messy,” you point out.

                                                            “True. I can clean all this shit out before you actually move in…or, if you’re willing to save me the trouble and deal with the mess yourself, I can charge you a five eighths of a Dinar instead of three quarters for your first month.”

                                                            “Charge me half instead of five eighths and you have a deal.”

                                                            “That’s a quarter Dinar for a day’s cleaning? No deal, kid.”

                                                            “Fair enough.” You point out. “But before you make your decision…those wine bottles aren’t all empty.”

                                                            “So?”

                                                            “…the chamberpot was already full.”

                                                            “Oh. OH.” Gleaming Smile looks around. “That’s…okay, yeah. Fine. Your apartment, your mess. Half a Dinar for the first month’s rent, after that it’s back up to three quarters.”
                                                            “Deal.”

                                                            You show him your Dinar, and Gleaming Smile heads back down to the office with you to make you some change. With your discount for paying in silver, you wind up getting your first month’s stay for only three eighths of a Dinar!

                                                            Gleaming Smile gets you a bucket and some soap and advises you to scrub the scorch marks off the ceiling. He also gets you a large woven-wicker basket to put trash in, and requests you dump it down the third floor’s main chute instead of just throwing it all out the window onto the street-piles of trash in public walkways apparently hurt local property values and are an incredible nuisance. He goes on about the subject. At length.

                                                            Finally, he departs, and you’re left in your new home, surrounded by a huge mess. You are about to get to work cleaning the place out…when the clothes hanging in the closet move.
                                                            A slightly chubby woman in her early twenties with bags under her eyes and tangled hair pokes its way out from between two coats. She’s wearing thick white foundation and smudged violet lipstick, and round glass lenses held to her ears by a bent wire frame. She looks sort of like an owl might, if it got hungover and somebody attacked it with the contents of Aunt Dwi's makeup kit.

                                                            “Is he gone?”
                                                            *****

                                                            What now?
