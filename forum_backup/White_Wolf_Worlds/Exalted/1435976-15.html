        Originally posted by Erinys View Post

        When Beastfolk from two different hereditary lines mate, do their children inherit traits from two different animals, and can they pass the mixture on to their children? Or does each child take after just one parent?
        I don't think there's a canon answer in 3E.
        It probably depends on what happens when a beast folk and a human have kids - I can guess at 2 options: a) they have a 50/50 chance of being beast folk or human and b) they end up with some but fewer beast folk features.
        In case a) I'd imagine the child of 2 beast folk has either 1/4 chance each of being human, being a single-beastfolk, or being a double-beastfolk or a 50/50 chance of being either kind, and in case b) I'd imagine 2 beast folk have a child with some but fewer features of each

        Originally posted by Erinys View Post
        I'll also happily take any numbers that were implied by 2E or 1E, though. Were there any?
        My knowledge of 1E and 2E isn't good enough to answer this.

        Originally posted by Erinys View Post
        One of the things I've wondered is what sort of animals are "used" as templates for Beastfolk, at least when they're made intentionally. Is it mostly animals that would provide abilities useful in warfare? (Wolffolk, bearfolk, hyenafolk, eaglefolk; but not mousefolk, guppyfolk, or mousefolk) After reading headcanon suggestions that Beastfolk are probably a downtrodden slave race in baseline-human countries, I thought about what types would be especially valued, but I'm not sure if anyone would go to the trouble of creating Beastfolk for slavery when humans are so easily enslaved.
        Lunar Lineage creation is based on their spirit shape(s), so won't be oriented to any particular usefulness. Existing ones in canon include Apefolk, Wolffolk, and Goatfolk
        Lunar individual beast folk creation, as well as sorcerous and deliberately wild-created beast folk will probably be oriented around creating useful lineages, but they may be useful for things unrelated to fighting - a beast folk who can survive harsh conditions and eat anything is useful for exploring/extracting value from terrain humans would die in, a beast folk with pheromones and dramatic crests is a useful herald, a beast folk with padded feet, chameleon skin and enhanced senses is a useful spy or thief.
        Raksha mutating people might also create beast folk for the aesthetic rather than any particular purpose.

        I think the idea is that when a baseline human nation encounters a smaller beast folk tribe, the humans will often enslave the beast folk, because that's a common thing to do when you encounter people weaker and different than you. I don't think many nations are deliberately making beast folk slaves.
        If they do, it will probably look more like turning people they've already enslaved into beast folk for some purpose, rather than creating new beast folk out of nothing

        Originally posted by Erinys View Post
        It surprises me that Wyld mutations can result in anything as mundane as Beastfolk, but if that is the majority of them then I'd guess they could be any random animal that lives near the edges of Creation.
        I think the Wyld often draws on Creation for shapes rather than creating things wholly alien to it - there's a section in the 3E corebook detailing how Raksha in different directions are shaped by the direction they're in

            On that note, even where a Lunar is passing their hybrid body traits onto children, I think there can be more varied applications than war.
