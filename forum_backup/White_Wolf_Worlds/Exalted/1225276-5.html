        Originally posted by armyofwhispers View Post
        So reality aside, how does this work in Exalted?

        My idea is that it is a visual thing.
        This might be silly, and feel free to reject it if you want to ^^
        ​
        When my mind conjures up an image of 'iron', it is an image of a black metal, while my image for 'steel' is a light grey metallic. Now this most probably isn't -correct- but the point I want to make is that the idea of something is not necessarily the same as the object or thing that it actually "refers" to in reality (or in Creation, in this case!).
        So the fair folk may know or recognize 'iron' as "the black metal, the one that hurts us" (mayhaps they got to know it as such in ages past for one reason or another) and visually distinct from other metals, including steel. And since iron turned to steel no longer falls under the [rakshas'] idea of 'iron', it doesn't/can not hurt them in the same way.*

        Of course this is based on the assumption that iron is visually distinct from steel.
        And also based on my understanding of Creation as completely alien to the fair folk, who struggle to understand how things work within 'reality', and are only able to grasp pieces through larger ideas, symbols and myth. Such things that are recurring, and relevant, enough to the human experience as to leave marks in the collective conciousness and be directly relatable and recognizeable in near any corner of the world.

        But if iron isn't in fact visually distinct from steel, well. It always can be, in your Creation, right? it could be the black or rainbow coloured metal that, when mixed with coal to make the stronger, less colourful and more useful to society; Steel, loses it's well known hue and with it its mythological status.

        *Kind of like how in a bugs bunny cartoon, you won't fall if you do not realise you are standing in the air.


        (Sorry if this idea has been sggested before! I only skimmed the previous pages...)

                        Originally posted by armyofwhispers View Post
                        First, it makes no sense that iron represents earth essence for two reasons: 1. Iron and metals are more the purview of Autochthon than Gaia. There might be a few elementals made of iron or other metals, but I think we can agree that the majority of them are made of earth or stone. However, elementals of metal and iron are common in the halls of Autochthonia.
                        I'm aware this isn't going to be completely compelling to the larger point, but this doesn't seem accurate to the setting.

                        First, the Primordials don't have exclusive purviews... they don't even have purviews in this sense. They are tautological entities. Iron and other metals are part of Gaia because they're part of her world body. Autochthon has a greater percentage of iron and metals because he's more limited in his world body, not because Gaia is less associated with such things. The differences in things like elementals are going to be flavor. Gaia's metal elementals as part of the greater hierarchy of earth elementals, are going to be more attuned to the natural occurring forms of metals, because Gaia is the world as it exists before people do things to it. Autochthon's metal elementals are going to be more industrial as reflection of his nature as the Primordial of tools and inventions.

                        Second, if we assume that the land that makes up Creation works like the crust of our planet, iron is one of the most important elements towards it's functioning, and the only one that's of any use at the technological level of the setting; that is, Creation isn't in a place where aluminum and silicon might be 'more' Earth essence than iron but it doesn't really matter without the ability to make use of them.

                                    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post

                                    I'll take a stab at this (though I feel I'm repeating other people):

                                    It's not iron that's special, it's steel. Steel inspires people with the possibilities of what metallurgy can accomplish as the useless or limited utility forms of iron are transformed into one of the most potent technologies humanity has ever discovered. But the awe and wonder of what steel can do, means the Raksha can interact with it safely unlike other forms of iron.

                                    This is consistent with the setting, because of the dream-narrative nature of fae creatures. While steel isn't to the level of the standard magical materials, it could easily be called the first magical material that humans could access without thaumaturgy, sorcerery or those that naturally work Essence. Like the major magical materials, and the other minor special materials out there, their special standing in human imagination is strong enough that fae creatures aren't harmed directly by it.

                                    It makes sense because the idea of steel being 'special' in some fashion beyond chemical superiority is something we see in real world human mythology. Multiple cultures have myths about the steel produced in meteors, or the Greek concept of "shining iron," so on. While steel was in the phase of becoming commonly understood as a thing to make, but still before mass production, it grew to have a special place in the minds of people. Even today people have illogical assumptions about how special Damascus steel and Japanese folded steel are (I mean, they look cool, but they're about turning mediocre steel into good steel, not good steel into awesome steel). In Creation there's no reason to assume there wasn't a similar psychological process. Steel is special, steel is what elevates metal working to a civilization growing tool.

                                    If that's not good enough... I'm not sure what any of us can really say.
                                    So this is one that I actually find interesting.
                                    I like it, a lot.

                                    But it doesn't really explain why every other base form of metals doesn't do what cold iron does.
                                    Is it the fact that steel stole every bit of faith humanity had in iron?
                                    If so, did bronze not do the same to copper?

                                            True, but I was trying to address the OP's initial and restated objection to the separation of "iron" and "steel." The OP didn't ask why iron alloys at all.

                                            The "why iron at all" question isn't one that we can really answer to the OP's satisfaction. It is literally the way the setting was written because of a modern recontextualization of stray bits of myth and fantasy with no eye towards in-universe logic.
