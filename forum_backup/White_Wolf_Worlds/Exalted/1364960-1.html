        Te first game of ex3 I ran was in a kingdom near Vaneha and it didn’t have much of an over arching plot aside from the characters personal growth. It had about 20 different locations of interest and each of them had their own mostly self contained stories.

        Second campaign took place a satrapy only the South. It was formerly comprised of two peoples, a culture that lived in the city with the aid of sorcery and Artiface, and a wide spread desert dwelling people that lived among the more fertile mountain areas and ranged into the dune sea. Then eventually the city people became infernally corrupted, began summoning demons and waging war on the desert people in an attempt to gather slaves and sacrifices without hurting their own citizens. Enter the Realm, who offered the desert people the deal of becoming a satrapy, marrying some Dynasts into their royalty to create a cadet house, and bending the knee to the Empire. In exchange they would help them take the city and all the treasures in it. The desert people agreed and with the support of Realm legions and Exalted overthrew the city.

        Centuries later the city is suffering. The water screws that once brought limitless fresh water up from underground are all but wrecked, the fountains no longer flow, the water spirits have become sickly, disease years through the impoverished parts of the city and in the background the smell of sulphur is in the air. The Realm is demanding more tribute while sending less aid to fix everything. A duo of Sidereals smell something is off so they manipulate a young group of solars into gathering together into the area, reckoning that unholy things don’t last long in proximity to Solar exalted.

        From there the PCs worked from the shadows, staying hidden from the Dragonblooded while trying to fight a resurgence of demon worshipers in the satrapy, and somehow fix the water and disease problems. Eventually it was discovered that an Infernal exalt was using a sword to open gateways to hell and letting demons in, the twin of one of the PCs blades. On top of that House Cynis got fed up with this whole affair and sent a massive force to sort the problem out. Also the Wyld areas to the south had grumbling s of a Raksha lord wanting to take advantage of the whole situation. In the end the PCs managed to slay all the demons, make peace with the fry, and install themselves as the new leaders of the satrapy, totally unbeknownst to the Realm when the campaign ended.

            Originally posted by Lanic View Post
            The Wizard of Oz After reading about your campaign I started asking myself whether my group actually plays Exalted. Tell us how it ended once you know it.
            So, this is probably quite important...

            I don't actually advocate that every game should lead to massive, world-shaking plot.

            My first 2e game ended with trying to stop Walker in Darkness spamming the Sword of Creation all over the world... but by that time I think one character was 700xp (and you got less xp back then). That's many years of sessions.

            In my 3e game, which was Essence 4-5, they... protected a jungle, took behind-the-thrones control of a few local kingdoms, and one character made her own small magic kingdom.
            That's it. From 200-350xp.


            Not every game needs to be balls-to-the-wall Elemental Dragons vs Incarnae.


            And the first story arc (which I skimmed over, as it's not the plot now, and was a kind of introduction), was just one small kingdom, the main villains were e2-3 Dragonblood and some weak Raksha, and a Guild merchant.

            The second arc was actually supposed to be the PCs fighting House Tepet's invasion of Iscomay. They kind of skipped that and went straight for the 3rd story arc (I don't mean each is a "Story" in the sense of wp and 1/story recovery).
            I didn't want to railroad them though, they seemed motivated to go to the Elemental Pole of Air and stop the Winter, so I just rolled with that. I was pretty excited to get to the Third Story Arc anyway, so I can get to the Fourth Story Arc. Which... actually is a lot less detailed than these ones, it's a bit sandboxy, I thought I could work out the details later, but we had more sessions than I expected, they're much more focused than I expected (normally these players bicker and piss around a lot, but they haven't in this game) so get through stuff faster, and of course they skipped from my planned Arc 1 to Arc 3 (which I'm fine with).
            Though perhaps after all this they'll return to the Kingdom they were in, and have to deal with House Tepet to the South anyway, before going on to the more sandboxy thing.

            But tbh, we've been playing Exalted for ages (13 years?) and I'm kind of bored. So I decided f*** it. Lunar PCs only, unlike the other 3 games full of Solars. Raksha because we've not really used them. Only Japanese-themed Dragonblood (so House Tepet), not Chinese-Roman. We'll put the game way, way in the North-East away from the normal stuff we've used a bunch. Nexus is a forest city because it's in the East. The Realm is destroyed. Pasiap and Hesiesh are dead. An Elemental Dragon who's destroying the entire world. The Realm say they're body-hopping spirits who possess people and stole power from the Unconquered Sun... so let's lean into that. (I'm not going to reveal exactly how accurate that is, but it's not entirely inaccurate in this game.)

            And let's throw in a bunch of Mesopotamian mythology (various Sumerian/Greek/Biblical flood myths and others). And some Indo-European stuff... all the Solars' true names are the names of Indo-European hero-gods who killed Dragons (Tarhunzas, Tesshub, Indra so far, the others will be similar).
            It fits Exalted yet actually isn't really used in the base game.
            Plus a bit of Asian stuff that again, is not really used in the base game but fits, hence the Yama Kings.

            The really interesting thing of course, is what happens if the PCs fail and Mela freezes the entire world...

                    Thanks, though that wasn't intentional per-se.

                    Tbh, I've used Abyssals loads, Infernals and the Ebon Dragon a bit, various random Dragonblood ,even Solars, as the main antagonists. So I thought what should I go for this time... and I decided on:
                    One single Realm House (I chose Tepet as they're the most distinct from the others, in 3rd ed at least).
                    Elementals and Gods, but purely Terrestrial ones.*
                    Raksha
                    (Each of these potentially could be allies or antagonists, depending on the PCs. 100% definitely the other side villains, like Yozis and the Neverborn, are dull to me.)

                    Anyway, an angry goddess who's important to the world, but also threatening to destroy it, seemed appropriate to me. It fits with some of the Mesopotamian mythology that inspired my idea (I mean, you can't just deal with Zeus or Enlil or Yarwe flooding the world by killing them. Except this is Exalted so I guess you can, though it might not be a good idea!).

                    *I don't generally enjoy plot about spirits. But I realised that actually what I don't like is plot about spirits who are far away in Yu-shan, or in sanctums, or dematerialised. Elementals are fine, because they can grab territory, can't dematerialise, etc. Terrestrial gods I got to work better than previously by thinking of them as, essentially, wacky community leaders/heroes rather than "spirits".

                                            Originally posted by Chausse View Post

                                            I'm very interesting in hearing about your experience after your new test, because I have the exact same problem with my spirits and have no idea on how to handle them to make the &quot;poltergeist&quot; scene interesting
                                            I’m on my phone right now so can’t give a very detailed response, but a lot of it will have to do with how you describe things. First of all, you’re the ST, break the rules a bit or make up your own. If you want to have the ghost of a drowned girl be able to flood any room of a house she’s in with water and lock the doors, go for it. If you want a ghost appear just out of the corner of your eye, or have them create an illusion that the grand ballroom of their dilapidated castle has been restored to its full glory, it’s done. Or more subtle creepy things, moving things that the players put down while they’re not looking, have them instil sadness or rage while dematerialized, or have them boil eggs in their shells.

                                            Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                                            Whereas with gods, I found them too mystical, so they didn't really attach to the plot. I don't think making them just like humans is the way to go either... but I have found making them similar to Exalts, in a sense, works well.                                             I’m leaning towards gods who are not interested so much in having direct control and concrete relationships with the mortal world, but still having a big effect on it most of the time. If they want to rule mortals they’ll do it through worship and prophets rather than direct governance. When they show up personally it should be an event, and dealing with them a bit strange because they’re beings who are from two worlds. Which isn’t to say that they’re absent, just that they’re ageless beings who may have different methods and perspectives on things.

                                            I might liken them a bit to people who are like 40 year obsessive experts in their fields. Like I listened to an interview the other day with somebody who’d studied caffeine and caffeinated drinks for the past decade and he was fairly convinced that the industrial revolution was initiated and made possible by caffeine. I don’t really know if I agree with that but I can see a god of tea agreeing. The Queen of all Bees would probably tell you in great detail how much bees are needed to agriculture and all flowering plants, and how without bees no civilization would ever have been possible. Worship the bee, you owe it everything.

                                                            The plot of the current Exalted 3e game I'm running is that the Circle are a group of explorers from Autochthonia. The Tripartite Council of the Metropolis of Wisant, desperate to find some way out of the trap the Nation of Nurad found itself in, built Project Razor on their own initiative, without consulting the National Council, and sent the party through to recontact Creation. The members of the Circle all Exalted as Solars in the first few days after reaching Creation (the Zenith of the group, though, has since returned to Autochthonia full-time, and been replaced as a PC by an Alchemical).

                                                            Since the breakthrough, the Circle has been exploring Creation (I'm doing a "hexcrawl"-style game, where each map area has an adventure hook or two, and needs to be surveyed to get a sense of what resources it contains). They're looking for sources of resources to help their city and Nation survive the oncoming disaster of being engulfed by the Blight. So far, they've discovered a couple of demesnes and manses, and made friends with some locals, some of whom have been inducted into the Nation of Nurad. They're currently travelling back to Autochthonia, where they've been summoned to address the National Tripartite Council of Nurad to provide a report on the Creation project, and probably to justify its continuation.
