        The odd thing about "past lives" is that it's either personal (and we care) or not (and we don't).

        It's useful if you get players to do brief blurbs on their past lives. Treating past lives as variants on my current character, I could highlight consistent themes or ideas for my concept - and anything the changes is clearly superficial. As an ST, you might see:
        • "Oh, this Eclipse always favors Occult - she's a shaman or diplomat for spirits. But her region/culture varies and influences her little since she seems to always abandon them in favor of her work as a messenger and spiritualist."
          .
        • "Oh, this Dawn has a multi-incarnation rivalry against Isidoros. Perhaps he rather stories about fighting demons or giant beasts - instead of leading armies against the Realm. Less Wyld Hunts, more Demon Boars, got it."
          .
        • "Oh, this Zenith has a serious attachment to their Lunar Mate. Introducing the Lunar might be a better hook than kidnapping a random cousin."
        Conversely, I've had an ST request & then ignore our past lives - and flat-out invent (horrible) things to demonize us - and I felt zero attachment. It was like being hired for a job and being expected to care about what an ex-employee did in the position. Not my actions, not my problem - I feel neither guilt for their behavior nor pity for their victims.
        And I should note that I have no problem with having prior incarnations who do horrible shit (and I usually include some of that). It's the fact that the ST wanted to super-glue memories that had zero synergy with the character concept that made it foreign and unrelatable.
        If she wanted a "evil 1st Age eugenicist and bio-engineer", she could have just asked before having us make our characters. But my characters motivation was to close-off a Shadowland in the East. A shaman to a (now dead) tribe (of maybe 30-40 people), how on earth was he to relate to a city, let alone bio-labs, let alone mass eugenics projects? Dude lived in the jungles most of his life hunting and shitting in bushes.

        So it's useful if you're an ST requesting past-lives so that you can see the character concept from other angles & see what your players are into.

        But if their past lives are just an excuse for the STs personal fan-fic, I could see most players shrugging and shoving it into their pocket like any other random key or password they encounter while exploring a dungeon.

                                            Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                                            Before each session I usually try to write out a couple of pages as a short story that people can read before we play. These stories will flesh out the world in some way. For example, they might show what's going on with important NPC's in the setting, or show how parts of the setting where the PCs are not currently located are changing.
                                            So, since I'd done plenty of First Age memories, I tried doing this instead. I was a bit worried about spoilers (I nearly put in a big spoiler by accident by putting a bad guy's real name, but I noticed it just before sending out the email). But I think it's been okay.

                                            I haven't talked to all the players about this, just a few, but they told me they preferred the short stories to the first age dreams. I guess it's more relevant (also, some said that the First Age memories had too much sex in; probably fair!). There was also an interesting difference of opinion about whether it's better to have all the dreams about one particular incarnation (presumably the High First Age incarnation), or lots of different ones. As these characters are Lunars, I had a lot of different incarnations; the point really was to give an overall idea of the various lives they'd had, good and bad, as well as foreshadow some things and very vaguely showcase exalt types that used to exist and no longer do, in case I ever want to do something with that (like Sidereals, Solars, whatever Hearteaters used to be, etc).
                                            But then, I feel if it was all about one previous incarnation, someone particularly important, I'd have wanted to ask them for their previous life's details themselves.


                                            Anyway, so I've started writing short stories from NPCs' point of view to send out between sessions. I think it's good for fleshing out the world as you said; it also helps to set the tone of the game, which isn't always easy in game (especially as my setting is Vikings/Anglo-Saxons vs mutant Samurai, but the plot of the first story arc is L'morte D'Arthur crossed with the White Witch; some of the players said they found that a bit odd because they don't really see Arthuria as Viking-esque).
                                            But I think the main thing is it helps the players remember the setting details; names of cultures and places, names of NPCs who've only been mentioned once or twice in-game, etc.


                                            So this is one I wrote between sessions that had a big fight against two Dragonblood and their soldiers:
                                            Another day of frozen misery. Another day of boredom.
                                            Nandorun Samatsu sighed.
                                            Taizen raised one perfectly-manicured eyebrow. "Well," said the southerner in his polished accent, "It's your turn."
                                            Samatsu sneered. "Okay!" He turned over one of his tiles, revealing the symbol for Wood.
                                            Taizen gave one of his mocking smiles, and responded immediately, revealing Fire.
                                            "Ah! This game is stupid! These tactics would never work in real life!"
                                            Taizen gave another mocking half-smile. "Step on a spider, chop up a snake, Nandorun-kun."
                                            From outside came a cough. "Uh, Taicho-samas?"
                                            Samatsu stood up with relief, and threw open the tent's fabric door. Any excuse to get away from Taizen's infernal smirking. How he wished he'd been allowed to go with Jadis, rather than stuck here with that lazy rat!
                                            "Report, soldier!"
                                            "Nandorun-dono!" The guard lazily snapped a sloppy salute. Such undisciplined louts. "There's a hairy barbarian at the gate. His name is Olaf Keelbreaker?"
                                            Samatsu frowned. The name wasn't familiar.
                                            "He says we destroyed his village, and killed his family? He's here for revenge?"
                                            Taizen gasped. "Nandorun-san!" He grabbed the samurai's shoulder. "That's an Oni!"
                                            At the word oni, the memories all rushed back into Samatsu's head. The memories of that day.
                                            The city burns. Samatsu weeps as he holds his dead wife in his hands. At his door, the demon laughs, its witch-mark burning on its brow. "You are nothing, little samurai," it gloats. "I gutted House Nandorun, but I leave you alive. Live in shame, and tell others what happens to those who stand against the Sixty-Six Kuromao, the Black Devil Kings!"
                                            "An oni!" Samatsu cried.
                                            "Twelve years ago, a unit of World Eaters destroyed the village of an oni named Olaf-san," nodded Taizen. "We thought he'd died, but he lived. Now he's tracked us down."
                                            Samatsu grasped the hilt of his katana, his mouth curving into a cruel smile. "And the fool has come knocking at our front gate." He marched out, shouting orders. "Ashigaru, form up! Archers, to the wall, but do not loose until I give the order! Spearmen, arms and armour and then to the gate! Boy, my armour!"
                                            The boy rushed to clasp Samatsu into his silk-and-scale Dō.
                                            Taizen frowned, his impassive mask slipping. "Do not let the fires of vengeance consume you, Nandorun-san. This could be a trick."
                                            Samatsu grinned as the boy lifted on his kabuto and attached the menpō face-plate. "You bring Jadis's pet monsters, Tae-san. I will defend the front. Oni are arrogant in their own power, and this fool will get what's coming to him."

                                            Ten minutes later, Nandorun Samatsu strode out of the gate onto the snow-covered road, encased in his red armour, the colour of the World Eaters. Behind him crowded spearmen, some in the white-and-blue, others in the red. He felt a coil of fear. What if this was a trick? What if he failed again? But the oni was here. Those who run only die tired.
                                            A motley group stood before him. A hulking brute of a man led them, his face badly scarred, hefting a massive axe with an ivory haft and silver blade. Behind him was a soldier on a huge horse, his eyes in shadow. At his sides were a wild savage encrusted in filth and an older man with bulging, frog-like eyes. Above, a dark bird flew, its beady eyes taking everything in.
                                            "I am Olaf Keelbreaker," shouted the scarred brute, showing a mouth half-full of smashed teeth. "Twelve years ago, the World-Eaters attacked my village unprovoked and killed my family! I am here to take my revenge!"
                                            Samatsu felt his qi surging, red with fiery rage.
                                            "My city was attacked by demons like you!" shouted Samatsu. "And today, I take my revenge!"
                                            Fast as a hummingbird, Samatsu's katana flew into his hand as he leaped forward. With an animal howl, the ugly monster charged at him... then flew up into the sky screaming, pulled by Jadis's walkuren.
                                            For a second, Samatsu felt cheated, but he knew the demon would soon return.
                                            But, horror of horrors, the oni's two companions began to change. One became a massive gorilla, screaming and beating its chest, while the other transformed into a massive python.
                                            Samatsu's terrified troops hesitantly engaged the two beasts, while he went after the man on the horse.
                                            His blade flashed with the speed of a crackling fire, but the horsemen parried with supernatural skill. But the man was off-balance, on the defensive. In the corner of his eye, Samatsu saw a woman shooting a massive silver bow, and he heard Taizen shouting to the men who were taking a beating from the beasts, but he was too consumed with rage to care.
                                            Samatsu was about to cut the massive horse's head off, when the horseman cackled. He spoke in a dark and cruel voice. "Foolish Samurai. Don't you remember? It was I who burnt your city, but I did not kill your family. Instead, I cursed them, binding them to me as my animal slaves! Bwahahaha!"
                                            For a second, Samatsu paused in horror. It seemed like a lie, but what if it was true? Death was bad enough, but unending slavery to a demon? But then he smiled beneath his mempo. Today, Voharun smiled upon him. Now, he would free his family.


                                            This one I wrote as essentially a session synopsis between games, to help players remember the plot as the next game was a month later:

                                            All PCs
                                            Kari poked the fire, watching the cooking fish. She'd never been a good cook, but a shield-maiden didn't have to be.
                                            "This is bad business, bad business," growled her father. Karl Kingsmilk had once been a great warrior, if not as great as he liked to boast. Now in his 50s, he should have been at home with his grandkids. "What's my milk-brother going to do?"
                                            Sigurd the Berzerker looked bored. "Well there's a meeting this afternoon, he'll tell us then."
                                            "I heard that the Lady Amira and Atal the witch have arranged a duel to resolve it," said Black Paladin in his strange, southern accent.
                                            "A holmgang?" Carl's eyes lit up. He'd rescued Kari's mother from her first husband by killing him in holmgang.
                                            "It was Arcturus who banned holmgang," said Kari, "He won't want that. It'll be a judicial duel."
                                            "Who's going to take on Many Spears though?" asked Sigurd.
                                            "The king asked Krove, that beastman, but he refused. I heard he asked Olaf Keelbreaker too, but Olaf hasn't decided," replied Black Paladin. Kari had thought him some form of black-skinned demon when she'd first seen him, but he was an honourable and thoughtful man.
                                            "I should do it, not some foreigner." They looked up to find Jarl Galchmei standing above their fire, his face glowering with anger. "It was my brothers who discovered the crime, and my brothers who Many Spears killed escaping."
                                            Karl looked uneasy. "Look, Galchmei, you yourself told your brothers to leave it. And Many Spears has been one of your best friends for years."
                                            "Blood comes first," snarled Galchmei.
                                            Black Paladin frowned. Like Galchmei, he'd also been close to Many Spears.
                                            "Is a duel a good idea? The goddess watches over Many Spears."
                                            "Fuck the goddess," snarled Galchmei, never one for reverence.
                                            "Hey," said Kari, eager to change the conversation. "What's that?" She pointed out north-west. It was snowing, but a set of black shapes were just visible. Mostly man-sized, but some were much, much larger.
                                            "That must be Jarl Olaf of the Mammoth Clan, said Black Paladin. "He was supposed to get here soon. And he's supposed to have Jarl Ragna Half-Rat of Clan Relic with him."
                                            Karl looked worried. "Ragna is Many Spears' second-cousin. She is going to be awkward."
                                            "No-one's going to get in the way of my vengeance," growled Galchmei. The others looked at each other uneasily.


                                            This one I wrote essentially as another synopsis, for a player who missed the session. It also mentions a few details that foreshadow later things; stuff I want players to know, but without drawing attention to. Chekov's guns:
                                            Arcturus
                                            It'd been a week and a half of trudging along the snowy road and Alfhild was bored. When the messenger to raise the Fyrd she'd been delighted. She'd taken up her father's spear, Goblin-Woe, and taken her place amongst the warriors. One of the Thanes of Clan Relic had rebelled, and it was time to show the treacherous beastmen their place. But being a soldier was less glamorous than she'd expected; no glorious battle, just a lot of marching. Ivan Lionfriend led the column, so she'd seen a tame lion. But all the other famous heroes had gone ahead on horseback, and she was stuck walking here with the annoying boys from her village.
                                            The weather was miserable. It was Rising Wood, she should be in the fields planting, but it was still snowing. The road was exposed to the angry sea to the south. Also, her dad's wooden shield was too big for her. And she'd heard a rumour that the Thane they were going to fight was Many Spears, the great hero, who'd kidnapped the Queen. But that didn't make sense! Why would he kidnap the Queen? Some of the guys had even said the Queen had run off with Many Spears because the king was impotent, but that sounded like bullshit.
                                            She was broken from her reverie by a cawing sound. A bird flew down near the column. But then it somehow shifted, she blinked, and there stood one of the best looking men she'd ever seen. He wore mail of iron links, a thick golden torc and armrings, and on his head was a steel helmet. Things a farmer's daughter like Alfhild could never afford. She knew without a doubt that this must be Arcturus; Mighty-in-War the skalds called him, Bane-of-Elves, Moon-Loved, Witch-Born, Bull-Hearted, Silver-Tongue, King.
                                            "Column halt!" Arcturus's voice boomed out like an avalanche; as the soldiers stopped all down the column, Alfhild realised all 500 had heard him clearly.
                                            "We are betrayed!" he continued, stern-faced. "The Guild of Merchants has brought in mercenaries, the World Eaters, and seized Caer Moroth!"
                                            Alfhild was shocked. Did that mean Arcturus had been out-manoevured?
                                            "Fear not, my loyal subjects!" As his charming voice settled in her ears, Alfhild forgot what she was thinking about before. Under the glorious King all their enemies would bleed!
                                            "The foeman is mighty. But we are mightier still! Their treachery will only make our own victory more glorious!" As he spoke, Alfhild's mind leaped ahead. She'd kill ten foeman, be named a thane, and return home laden with treasure. Then Bjorn Fair-Face would dump that slut Helga and marry her instead. All around her the Fyrd cheered.
                                            She saw Ivan Lionfriend hurry forward, followed by his lion, and speak to Arcturus quietly. Arcturus lowered his voice, but she just caught it as she was close. "I'd just finished naming new Thanes," said the King, "Olaf Keelbreaker, Amira and Jin. But then the shaman Atal told me Tabun had appeared to him as a ghost, and said the castle had fallen."
                                            Ivan looked shocked. "Tabun is dead?"
                                            Atal nodded, grim-faced. "And probably Torsten and Soldva, who I left to guard Caer Moroth." He gestured to the troops, who were clumsily turning around. "But we've got a large force here, and the Huscarls are riding hard to meet us. And I have the blessing of the Moon God."
                                            "What of the Queen?" asked Ivan.
                                            Arcturus shook his head. "We'll have to deal with it later. The capital comes first."


                                            I used to write straightforward session synopses (the PCs did this, then this, then this) but I think this is more interesting. Might be a bit difficult to follow compared to the more straightforward ones though.

                                            And these two are the last synopses, from the same session (but I was stressed with work and wanted to write more, essentially). The point is to show where the PCs currently are and what's going on, especially as the party has split so they may have not been paying attention and missed what the other group is doing:
                                            Atal, Underfoot and Krove
                                            Mighty Pelt was a sad bear. He was on a wooden iceberg, stuck to a big tree with a big white leaf. Humans and Bad Cat kept coming and shouting at him. He tried eating Bad Cat, but it hit him. One human looked nice, but also smelled like dead bear. Sometimes the humans fought for territory. And there was a storm.
                                            Baalu said if Mighty Pelt just waited and didn't fight, the humans would bring him fish. Strange. But he was stuck on a strong branch, and couldn't fight.
                                            The wooden iceberg floated near some land. Mighty Pelt tried to get off the iceberg, but he was still stuck to the tree by a strong branch.
                                            He slept. He woke up. A human was sneaking around. He got up. The branch had come off the tree! He dived into the water, and paddled across to the land. He still had the heavy branch stuck to him, but he was free. He found some woods, and settled down for a nice sleep.
                                            He woke up. In front of him was a human-cave-tree. What? How did that get there? He could see inside Baloo and his son, snug and out of the snow, and one of the other humans, the loud one that smelled like a toad.
                                            He felt something warm and furry resting at his side. A lady-bear? No, it was Bad Cat. Bad Cat had followed him to punch him again! No escape!

                                            Jin and Olaf
                                            Einar Hairy-Arms was in a good mood. His pockets were full of gold, his belly was full, and while the weather was miserable he was in a nice warm dwarf-built keep. He leaned back on his cheer, and raised his full tankard of mead. "To Grim the Rich!"
                                            His friends grinned and raised their own tankards, giving their own toasts. "To Katsunaga the Southman!", "To the girls of Clan Dragon-Bull!", "To the Guild of Merchants!", "To the ales of Caer Moroth!", "To the White Witch!", "To the blood of the foeman on our blades!"
                                            "Hey," said Dag Dogbiter. "There's Akiko. We should invite her over." He waved at the mutant mercenary, while Einar gave a smirk to the others. Dag had been mooning over the mutant since he saw her skewer two guards on one spear.
                                            "Dag!" said Akiko as she swaggered over and sat down, surprisingly graceful in her heavy steel armour. She took Dag's offered mead-horn in her seven-fingered hand. "I see today strange thing, you tell. Is big fat animal, like grey dog, lives in sea. What is?"
                                            Dag thought for a bit. He wasn't the sharpest axe in the woodshed. "Seal?" The others nodded.
                                            "What kind of animals live on your southern beaches then?" asked Magna Axewife.
                                            Akiko paused, pain in her eyes. "Once, crab, fishes, too much birds. Now, nothing. The Kuromao killed all."
                                            Dag looked awkward. He wanted to flirt, not remind Akiko about her dead family. And they hadn't had been happy when Katsunaga desecrated the Moon-Lord's temple, something they'd rather forget. But Dag was saved by a thrall's interruption. "Uh, Karls? There's something going on outside."
                                            Einar put his tankard down and stretched. "Come on then."
                                            The group trooped out into the yard. At the gate, they could see a merchant with a massive black horse arguing with the guards, his eyes in shadow despite the light. He had a huge ugly guard with a mutilated face. The guard stepped forward, pulling the cloth of his huge axe; a massive thing with an ivory haft and a silver blade.
                                            Einar and his mates stepped forward, hefting their axes, but Akiki hissed. “Is Aku-ma, moon demon!” The guy didn't look like a demon, he just looked butt-ugly.
                                            Slice.
                                            Einar blinked. The gate guards both slumped to the floor, decapitated in a single stroke. A bloody head rolled out into the yard. The ugly brute stepped through the gate into the yard, hefting his axe like it weighed nothing, followed by the merchant who'd drawn some weird-looking thin sword. Voharun's balls. Einar did not want to die today.
                                            “Katsunaga!” Einar yelled at the thralls. “Get Captain Katsunaga here now or we're fucked!” They ran.
                                            Einar turned back to face the demon. Shit.

                                                        My last character actually had a quirk with his exaltation such that his mind overrode the body of the recipient of his exaltation, so this was a huge part of his story as he sought to 'get the gang back together' so to speak and gather his old allies (Similarly blessed with their minds passed down their exaltations as well) and reforge some semblance of the First Age in the modern era. This was quite the fun challenge, RP-wise as my character was almost always in full plate and I didn't even know their sex until three sessions in when I rolled on a dice for it. Up till then, I was playing him as a gruff war veteran who even took another PC Solar under his wing as he taught him swordcraft and regaled him with tales of wars long gone. Then I rolled, and my character became a teenage girl from An-Teng (A collaboration of ideas between my ST and myself). I continued as I had, but it led to some hilarity as Valentinian XIII was many, many times not taken seriously because of his youth and slight frame.

                                                        Apart from that, I find past lives or some correlation to them to be an easy framing device for how my characters acquires their artifacts, so I often do have some level of interaction with them. It's just an additional cool piece of flavor for a character, too, and I'm a sucker for stuff like that.
