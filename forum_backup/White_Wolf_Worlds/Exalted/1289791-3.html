            Originally posted by Roadie View Post
            Yes, there are mythical shapechangers that have giveaway traits. Why must there be an obsessive focus on those ones, who are almost always antagonists, not protagonists, over mythical and literary sources where there are shapechanging protagonists that don't? Why make it mandatory for PCs?
            Because Lunars are those shapeshifters. Their whole deal is that they are monstrous heroes and guardian devils. A typical Lunar protagonist is indeed the villain of a typical Dynast's story.

            And because, frankly, the perfect kind of shapeshifter sounds really freaking boring to play. There's a reason a common Mary Sue Power is "shapeshifting without true limits or downsides." It's not a power anyone can reasonably counter, which leads to the problem of making the Silver Pact seem stronger than their nemeses, the Realm, as opposed to equally powerful or slightly weaker. That brings down both of them (it makes the Scarlet Empire, masters of All Creation, seem like a stronger-than-average rainstorm away from collapsing, and it undersells how cunning, strong and heroic the Silver Pact is for having become its collective nightmare).

                    Originally posted by Roadie View Post
                    I find it kind of telling (heh) that the art for Tula uses the cop-out of (presumably) the scar as a Tell. Like, that's been a "man, I don't even know, just do whatever" cliche for Lunars for years and years, and even the developers are doing it?
                    Jeez, here I was thinking that knowing that's No Moon because you've seen that scar before on a different face could be an effectively compelling turnaround.

                    Originally posted by Roadie
                    ...which makes the "recognize as the same individual" mechanic even sillier.
                    I know an answer like this indicates that there's really no winning, because every response will just feed into another complaint, but I just want to do something to kind of close the circle a bit.

                    I feel as though a game such as Exalted is, not unreasonably, asking for a decent amount of dramatic license. I'm not talking about in the stuff like the existence of gods and millenia-old demigods (although it is in some of the implications of their existence, such as not trying to be too deep about the question of what a person who has lived for thousands of years might logically be expected to be like), but in some of the things such as how the history has unfolded, how personalities clash against each other, some of the ways that institutions in the world can work and, most pertinent to this discussion, ways in which the mechanics prioritise making dynamic and engaging stories rather than being an arms race.

                    Hence you get stunting, mechanical rewards just for describing things happening in interesting ways. Hence the description of the basic mechanical difficulties, from how the lowest difficulty sets the baseline of "stuff heroes do all the time" (indicating that this isn't stuff for everyday life), up to difficulty 5, in which people can do kind of impossible stuff like read print by feel.

                    And here we're talking about an action that is difficulty 7.

                    So is it strictly logical that a person might make the leap that the speckles on the tail of that bird look distinctly out of place, or (probably more likely) find recognition in them from what they've seen on a Lunar in the past? Well... honestly probably a little more likely than one would think, people have done stranger things. But the important thing is that it creates a dramatic avenue for a Lunar being sussed out. Not an unearned one, mind, not a convenient one. Even if you have seen the Tell before, open and exposed by the light of the anima banner, you're going down from "basically only gods could notice this" to "some of the most heroic observational types in the world would have to strain to see this (and draw the important connections) even before it being one of their bad days".

                    Originally posted by Roadie
                    If there's really a desperate need for Lunars to have some kind of give-away with their shapechanging, I would rather have them be "bad at it" in in a way that feels natural and requires some investment to be good at it
                    , and actually interacts with other systems in the game like disguises and noticing disguises
                    And a lot of people would probably argue really vehemently against it.

                    Shapeshifting for Lunars is, after all, more than just a feature, it's supposed to be a bit of a selling point. Sprucing it up a bit over mere disguises could be viewed as part of it.

                    Originally posted by Roadie
                    rather than have what's implicitly described as perfect shifting but then drop on a trait that only exists to be a downside in the character creation chapter with entirely standalone mechanics that just kind of waggle fingers vaguely at the GM to reinvent disguise rules.
                    I feel as though this is really not giving fair shakes to what the book describes and affords for seeing through the deception. As in, rather than being focused on the disguise rules, it places most of the heft on the read intentions and profile character rules. If anything, the fact that the best first step to seeing through it is not in the body, but recognising something off in the underlying personality, kind of reinforces that the shifting is paradoxically perfect in spite of its giveaway feature.

                    (Implicit descriptions are great, aren't they? They let people project whatever meaning to the text that allows them to be outraged at it being contrary to what is explicit.)

                    Originally posted by Roadie
                    Yes, there are mythical shapechangers that have giveaway traits. Why must there be an obsessive focus on those ones, who are almost always antagonists, not protagonists, over mythical and literary sources where there are shapechanging protagonists that don't?
                    Leliel already gave a good answer for this, and... that is all I need to say about it.

                    Originally posted by Roadie
                    Why make it mandatory for PCs?
                    Apart from all of the dramatic reasons, so that the selling point of shapeshifting doesn't also turn into something that basically saps tension altogether out of things.
