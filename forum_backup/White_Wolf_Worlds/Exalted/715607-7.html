        Originally posted by WhirlwindMonk View Post
        And that would be great, if that charm actually said it did that. It doesn't. It doesn't automatically convince people to trust you against their better(?) judgement. All it says is that they succeed on a Read Intentions action, which does precisely nothing to mechanically affect the actions of that character. Any other effects you give it, such as forcing the DB to acknowledge that maybe this Anathema really isn't all that bad of a guy, is your own choice and is not an explicit mechanical effect of the charm.
        I'm suggesting that it's difficult for people to think, "I think I successfully read this person's intentions, but I probably only think so," even if nothing mechanically enforces that. That kind of thinking requires careful consideration, overwhelming dedication, and/or a distrust of your own skills (which is difficult for Exalted to manage). Can it be done? Sure. But my point was that it's hard. People don't like to assume that they failed at something when they can believe that they succeeded instead. The mechanics can't perfectly replicate the experience of being a person.

            Originally posted by Irked View Post

            Twisted Words Technique (#22) is… either crazy-powerful or not, I’m not sure. The Charm itself suggests that it acts as an Inspire action, creating behavior – like the monk going to live with the prostitutes. But the sidebar seems to be explicit that it’s only modifying Intimacies, kicking them up or down a level (in the direction opposite what it appears to do). If it works in the former way, it seems trivial to convince an Immaculate to abandon the Wyld Hunt (by getting him to agree that Solars must be hunted). In the latter case, you might weaken his Intimacy, but he’s still probably going to try to kill you. The verbiage in the Charm talks about “persuasion caused by this Charm” – but persuade is a system term, meaning “induce behavior.”

            Rereading the opening parts of the Charm, it does seem like this is behavior-creation – “the target will feel overwhelmingly compelled to act…” So that is persuasion, and the Charm is correct. Does it also function as an Instill action, or is this an either/or? Does this trivially dispatch Immaculates, given that you’re appealing to a very strong Intimacy and they can’t spend Willpower to resist?
            This charm feels like it's incredibly powerful and allows you to be Bugs Bunny, just from my read?

            "Man, them dog people are whack, right?"
            "You're telling me, buddy."
            "I seriously can't think of anything that would be worse than living with dog people."
            "Exactly! I am totally a cat person."
            "I think you should live with some dog people."
            "Man, I'm gonna go live with some dog people!"

            Between the charm and the sidebar, it seems like this leaves the intimacy intact on their sheet ("Man, I can't stand dog people.") while the actions contradict that, and that someone has to apparently manipulate the social influence system to convince them "Hey, you don't like dog people. Why are you living with dog people?" to get the effect to snap. This would be less effective in a blunter society, but in places where you just don't admit the emperor is naked, it could be ridiculously powerful.

            So, yeah. Hello Solar UMI with extra mind-screw. @_@

That kind of thinking requires careful consideration, overwhelming dedication, and/or a distrust of your own skills (which is difficult for Exalted to manage). Can it be done? Sure.                     I don't expect them to perfectly replicate being a person, but in this case, the mechanics are fully capable of handling what you're suggesting. If the charm also made a reflexive Instill action that attempted to give a minor intimacy of "Believes His Motives (That Anathema Over There)", that'd do exactly what you'd describe. The average person would accept it, no question, your average Realm dragonblooded could resist it using a minor or major intimacy of "Anathema are not to be trusted", and a devout Immaculate monk could probably call it unacceptable influence due to their defining intimacy of "All anathema are evil". Or you could model it as a Persuade attempt to convince the people around to believe in you. Heck, you could probably make it work with an Inspire action with a magical twist or two from the base mechanics. There are plenty of ways you could accomplish "Not only does this look true, but you believe that's it's true," and the charm instead interacts with mechanics that have zero explicit mechanical means of accomplishing that.

                                10-29-2015, 03:41 PM
                                Lore

                                Number of Charms: 48
                                By Ability requirement: 1/2/3/3/39(!)
                                By Essence requirement: 7/7/20/9/5
                                Nonstandard dice tricks: Reroll 6s until they disappear, “recurring 10s,”

                                Themes: Give and take supernatural resources, train people, know everything past and future, CRAFT NEW WORLDS BY DINT OF YOUR SUPERIOR WILL, drop nukes.

                                This is our third-largest tree so far, coming in behind Brawl and Craft. Don’t have Lore 5? Probably not the Charm tree for you. On the other hand, this is probably one of the strongest Supernal entries – over a quarter of its Charms are E4-E5. Cry your bitter tears, Awareness!

                                Charms:
                                1. Protect an object or character from Wyld-twisting.
                                2. Characters within short range are protected from the Wyld.
                                3. Get ALL the Lore Specialties, and get bonuses when they apply.
                                4. Teach the unteachable. Row row, fight da powa.
                                5. Spend your XP to train someone else. Roll XP as dice at end of story; recover successes.
                                6. Transfer motes to an ally.
                                7. Transfer Willpower to an ally.
                                8. Hide one lesson inside another.
                                9. Reroll nonsuccesses when using #5.
                                10. Gain bonuses when introducing/challenging facts.
                                11. Reroll 6s when introducing/challenging facts.
                                12. Reveal the function of an object.
                                13. Accept wounds from an ally.
                                14. Make #6 and #7 Reflexive, with bonuses.
                                15. Use #5 to train many people at once.
                                16. Use #5 to teach spells or Charms.
                                17. Receive a vision of how to use an artifact.
                                18. Gain Evocations for free.
                                19. Add non-Charm dice to thinky problems, permanently.
                                20. Push wounds onto a target.
                                21. Drain motes from the target.
                                22. Convert the target’s personal motes to peripheral or vice-versa.
                                23. Steal damage from an attack through your mystic mumblings.
                                24. Regain 1m when you roll a 10 on a Lore roll.
                                25. Reset your anima to dim.
                                26. Burn out your anima, restoring motes to allies.
                                27. Allow #6 and #7 to be used at range.
                                28. Destroy Wyld-twisting effects.
                                29. TEAR NEW REALITY FROM THE LAUGHING TEETH OF MADNESS.
                                30. When using #29, create specialist workers.
                                31. When using #29, gain bonus successes.
                                32. Make artifact weapons using #29.
                                33. Skip a roll when using #29.
                                34. Make a book you thought up using Mind-Scribing Method.
                                35. Grant Charms you don’t know to a character (maybe you!).
                                36. Predict the future, granting bonuses to those who follow your predictions and penalties to those who don’t.
                                37. Steal Willpower and Initiative by posing a riddle.
                                38. Recover extra motes.
                                39. Boost the effect of #24 to 8s, 9s, and 10s for one roll.
                                40. Reduce cost of one use of #29.
                                41. Gain double-8s on one use of #29.
                                42. Build a manse using #29.
                                43. Regenerate motes while using #29.
                                44. Drop a nuke on an enemy nation from your basement. Yes, really.
                                45. Gain 3wp.
                                46. Trade motes and Initiative for Willpower.
                                47. Reduce cost of #29
                                The entry-level Charms here are solid stuff – broad Wyld protections, big expansions of your breadth of knowledge. Really good Charms for many different concepts to pick up.

                                Flowing Mind Prana just sort of hurts. “Train NPCs, but you’ll pay roughly half the cost of the traits they gain” is just not a fun notion, especially after already paying the 8 XP for the Charm. You could, I suppose, have someone else in the party pick up the same Charm and train each other – on alternating stories, I guess – but that’s not less weird, and it potentially leaves the two of you quite a bit ahead of the rest of the party after the first few arcs. This really seems to be designed to feed off the Craft Charm that turns color points into real XP, which is great if you’re a crafter, I suppose, and kind of an annoying barrier otherwise. Tireless Learner Method (#9) offers you the chance to sink another 8 XP into training Charms to up your average return to 75% of the XP invested. (“You can go over the original amount!” it promises, as it inexplicably bills you an extra 1XP per use. Yes, and you can roll >11 successes on 10 dice, but how often does that happen?)

                                I’m still not wild about how the Lore rules are so different from the entire rest of the game’s resolution engine, but I do respect that the Charms here integrate well with them. Some oddities, though; Bottomless Wellspring Approach says it doubles extra successes when introducing/challenging facts – okay, but that doesn’t seem to do anything. Facts are a flat difficulty, with no reward for grades of success.

                                Truth-Rendering Gaze (#12) specifies that it’s a Read Intentions action to figure out what an object is for. Okay, but what difference does that make?

                                Essence Font Technique (#14) makes it hard to read your splat descriptions, or tell where one subheading ends and another begins. (I kid! I kid!) More seriously, it introduces recurring 10s as a system term for “reroll 10s until they stop appearing,” which seems like it would be nice to have in use somewhere other than this one Charm.

                                Selfsame Master Instructor (#15) seems to have an effect unrelated to its name; more to the point, it allows you to train someone else in Charms or spells. Er, can’t you normally just learn Charms or spells? Is there actually a barrier this is overcoming?

                                Hey, how does one figure out how to use an artifact normally? Sacred Relic Understanding (#17) suggest the answer is “a high-difficulty Lore roll, with this Charm” – which would have been my guess for how one does so without a Charm.

                                Hi, there, Wake the Sleeper (#18). I’m Fate-Shifting Solar Arete. With me, a stunt, and a point of Willpower, you should be able to average >21 successes on a Lore roll, which is enough to grab eight Evocations off an Artifact 5 in one pop, at no Experience cost. That’s 80 XP worth of goods per story; for reference, if you favor Lore and Larceny, you could buy the 14 Charms needed for this combo for just 112 XP, and you’d still have all those Lore and Larceny Charms at the end.

                                Force-Draining Whisper (#23) is a great Charm – it’s a solid combat Charm in a very non-combat Ability, which feels like it’ll fit well for a number of characters. It… doesn’t really seem like a Clash effect, though?

                                Flowing Essence Conversion (#25) would have been great in 2e, but I feel certain there’s some better way to reset an anima banner in 3e. Like, say, the very next Charm. (The Charm also doesn’t seem to trust us to know what the Mute keyword does.)

                                Power-Restoring Invocation (#26) loses the Perilous keyword at E5. Since it costs Initiative, though, this still does… precisely nothing. (This section in general is really inconsistent about saying “Ignore this requirement if Lore is your Supernal” – Force-Draining Whisper, for instance, lacks it. Has any other section bothered with that?) Er, couldn’t half the text of this Charm be eliminated by adding “3a” to its cost?

                                HECK YEAH WYLD-SHAPING TECHNIQUE (#29). This has been one of the most absurd Charms in past editions – either it threatened to kill you with every use, or it gave you anything you could ever want. 3e appears to finally chart a middle course that is crazy-useful but not outright better than most alternatives. I really like the push-your-luck mechanic of making and remaking the world until it fits the pattern you want. It does seem to contradict itself, though – it says at one moment that you need the deep Wyld for it to work, and in the next that you can shape gold “at Creation’s rim.” Are we to understand that the middlemarches are the rim? Has that been explained anywhere?

                                One note – WST says to “remove 1 from the roll result.” “Remove” here is clearly “subtract,” but “remove 1” is awfully close to terminology that does something rather different.

                                You are going to get mobbed by raksha if you use this Charm. I don’t understand the sentence relating number of demesnes to successes “spent on upgrades to its area” – the example given seems to contradict the text. Once you have Wyld-Called Weapon (#32), this is still pretty clearly the fast, efficient way to make weapons – again, if you can hold off the raksha.

                                Prophet of Seventeen Cycles (#36) has inconsistent rules and examples. The rules say: if you follow the prediction, you get a bonus, and otherwise you get a penalty. Well and good. The example says: you predict Mnemon will muster forces this summer; if she does, she gets a bonus, if she doesn’t, she gets a penalty. Okay. Then the rules say that if you attack Mnemon this summer, you take penalties. What? Where did that come from?

                                Unstoppable Magnus Approach (#45) lets you convert motes and Initiative into Willpower. It also comes with a free sidebar, chiding you for slowing down the battle by converting Initiative into Willpower. Pick one! (The best fix I’ve seen so far allows you to substitute paying m/I somewhere that you would otherwise pay 1wp. That seems like it avoids the issue, though either way it’s awfully pricey compared to Integrity’s 4m:1wp conversion rate.)

                                My overall impression is that this is a very solid tree, albeit one with some Charms I wouldn't touch with a ten-foot pole. That said, it also feels as though it was written out of sequence with most of the rest of the book - it's missing some standard terminology (spending anima levels), adds some non-standard verbiage (explaining the Mute or Supernal keywords to us), and introduces some terms the rest of the book never picks up on ("recurring 10s").

                                Yes: HECK YEAH WYLD-SHAPING TECHNIQUE.
                                No: Tome-Rearing Gesture (#34). “I can Wyld-Shape a book!” Um… good for you?
                                Just Plain Weird: God-King’s Shrike (#44). “Nuke a region from your basement” feels entirely out of proportion with the scale of the effects we’ve seen so far.
