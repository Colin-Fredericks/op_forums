    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

    I personally like the way that the game establishes difficulty 1 as a thing that is standard fare for somebody heroic, and scales up from there. Both for the manner in which it establishes a baseline for what kinds of things should even call for a roll, and general tone of the action. The way that I read it is that it's more concerned with the dramatic context of any given action.
    Standard fare in the sense that they'll almost certainly succeed, or that it's something challenging for someone heroic (in this area)?

    I'm not really fond of the idea of tying conceptions of difficulties to skill levels because I like to emphasize the point that a character of any Attribute and Ability level is theoretically capable of achieving any difficulty.
    Well, sure, but we need some kind of way to work out how difficult it is. We need to look at what's generally going to happen, not what's average.

    So if the players want to, say, sail a boat through a storm successfully, I might think "hmmm, I reckon that'd be tricky but possible for a professional sailor, and very hard for an amateur, so I'll go for 3".

    Like, an amateur singer could have a really good day, get really lucky, reveal hidden talents, and do an amazing performance.
    But they probably won't.
    It's possible, but challenging. That's why I said things like "challenging", or "easy but not effortless."

    With spending Willpower, an exceptional stunt, and maybe one or two other circumstantial bonuses, a character with 1s in the relevant stats has a chance of rolling five successes. I think that's the one that should be focused on.
    If, when deciding difficulties, I say "well a person with 1s could potentially get 5 successes" and focus on that, I'm going to massively over-estimate the difficulties for things, and my PCs are going to fail most stuff.
    Difficulties should be assigned more averagely. If the fat, short Twilight who never does any exercise does manage to get 5 successes on his Athletics roll by an incredibly lucky roll, it'll be impressive, and that's fine. But a lot of the time, he'll fail.

                            I just sat down again and wondered about what you wrote. And then I applied math to what you said...
                            To get 21 successes is a minimum of 11 dice (5 Attribute + 5 Ability + 1 Specialty), or 10 dice and a success with a willpower. The average on 10 dice is 5 successes, which is a 50/50 shot on making a difficulty 5 roll.
                            And if he wants to really succeed, he's going to stunt for extra dice, use charms to add more dice, use charms to reroll low numbers and charms to get double successes on more high numbers, and basically spend to bunch of essence to make this roll (and then he rolled well to boot).
                            The main thing is though, he took a roll that had about a 50/50 shot at blowing it if you were the best of the best (and way beyond average), and made it as easily as Solar's are supposed to do.

                            And that's something to think about. Attribute 5 and Ability 5 have only a 50% chance at making a difficulty 5 roll without stunts and charms.
