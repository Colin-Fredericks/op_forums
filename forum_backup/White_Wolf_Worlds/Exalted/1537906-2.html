    Not specifically. War and official mass combat rules didn't really come into the game until 2e (not that there's nothing in 1e at all, but mechanical heft is lacking). 1e Core suffered a lot from not really thinking through where the game would go once characters got past "You're a newly Exalted Solar, and you're still working on surviving the dangers of Creation," and the supplements were constantly playing catch up to cover things that the core just hand-waved or at best gave some advice on a one-and-done roll to see what happens. And that's not really just unit-level combat. Exalted 1e started as very freewheeling in how to handle anything outside of combat and sorcery: just pick a dice-pool and huck it was the general approach to most things. That's why the Exalted 1e quickstart basically spawned it's own fan-game that made the whole thing like that, rather that where the game went: trying to give every ability equal heft (still not there yet but a lot closer).

    Books like Savage Seas, Savant and Sorcerers, and Manacle and Coin expanded a lot of things, but if something you wanted more mechanical heft didn't become the focus of a supplement, it probably didn't get anything until 2e at the earliest.

            Specific advice to the OP: Combat in 1E is incredibly lethal. I've definitely played a Solar or two who didn't survive their first combat scene because I misjudged how many times I was going to get attacked. That's not even discussing Terrestrials, who never stop worrying about where they're going to get their next defensive roll.

            From what you've said about your group, I'm going to guess a lot of the PCs attacked early in the first round, using their only Charm of the round on offense. Maybe they dropped an enemy, maybe not, but either way, they had no defensive action to respond when another mortal took a swing at them. Even a mortal can seriously injure or kill an Exalt with a decent damage roll, and a dice pool of 5 will almost always hit a difficulty of 1.

            If you're playing a combat focused character, make sure you get a scene-length Charm that provides reflexive defense rolls in character creation, then activate it as quickly as possible in EVERY combat, mortals or not - good examples are the Solar Charms Flow Like Blood or Fivefold Bulwark Stance. Once you've got one of those up, you're defending against every incoming attack with a dice pool of 10ish, making it very unlikely that a 5 dice attack will sneak through. At that point, a starting Dawn could probably have soloed your 12 militia, though it would likely be a long and tedious slogfest.

            Even non-combat Exalts should invest in an instant reflexive defensive roll Charm - for Solars, those are Dipping Swallow Defense or Shadow Over Water. That lets you attack with your action and defend with repeated use of the defensive Charm as necessary.

            As a side note, crappy Charms are the true reason Terrestrials are always going to fall behind Solars in combat, not mote pool issues - from what I remember, they don't have a Charm like Flow Like Blood or Fivefold Bulwark, so even mortals will always be a threat. So you're going to need to house-rule some new Charms into their trees, if you want them to remain viable next to Celestials.
