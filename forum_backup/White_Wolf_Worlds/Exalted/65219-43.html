                Originally posted by MaxAstro View Post
                I cannot like that enough times. I almost cut the Lunar Bond from my game. Armed with that headcanon, I am extremely glad I choose to keep it.

                The most beautiful thing about that is not only does it not matter that the bond gives all the advantages to the Solars, it actually works that way on purpose. If the Bond gave an advantage to the Lunar, it would be something the Solar could fight against - and anything a Solar can fight against they can beat. But the Bond gives them everything. Their one Lunar is completely at their mercy, completely devoted to them no matter what.

                How do you fight that?

                I am stunned, because that explanation caused me to completely reconsider the context of the Lunar bond and like it 100x better, without changing anything about the bond itself in any way.
                It also makes it nigh-impossible for the Neverborn and Yozis to argue against - if the Lunar's benefits were equally impressive, then the Abyssal could simply tell himself 'she stays with me for the benefits SHE gets' and come to resent her for it. But when the Abyssal (or the Infernal, or whatever) can't come up with any reason the Lunar would stay other than 'she wants to'? When the bond can't be turned to any other purpose? How do you spin that?

                "Oh yeah, she totally hates you too. That's why she trusts everything you say, cradles you in her arms when you cry, defends you against every threat, tells you she loves you and this argument sounded a lot smarter when I started."

                And sooner or later, the Lunar's love for Creation and the Abyssal's desire to destroy it are going to come to a head. What is a Deathknight to do when told to kill the only thing in all the worlds he still cares for?

                You could do something similar for Infernals...

                                        Starmetal, also known as Hydrargyrum, forms naturally from the workings of Fate, dripping from the strings of the Loom. It cannot be collected until it hardens, as it's initial liquid state is like the poison of a pattern spider's bite. It may also be artificially created using a thaumaturgical process that calls upon mercury being mixed with crushed diamonds.

                                        Adamant was the metal once favored by the primordials and is found in its greatest quantities upon the layers of Malfeas. Infernals have no special connection to it but they have easier access to the stuff. It's mundane equivalent is titanium and the thaumaturgical process for manufacturing Adamant calls upon that metal.

                                        Soulsteel forms "naturally" in the Labyrinth, the calcified remains of spirits that wandered too far. It's artificial, manufacturing process calls upon souls being boiled in lead. There are other forms of Soulsteel that don't form naturally at all, "Grey" Soulsteel, which has willing souls forged into iron and "Red" Soulsteel which uses lower souls and copper. When compared to these, normal Soulsteel is called "Black". Rumor has it there is a "White" Soulsteel, supposedly made from platinum and created by being dipped into the River Lethe.

                                                My own version of the Solar bond was that it was an unintentional byproduct that came about to do the inherent, preexisting link between the Unconquered Sun and Luna. The Unconquered Sun originally viewed it as a potential flaw and wanted Autochthon to scrap the Shards and start over from scratch, but Luna and Autochthon convinced him that the potential benefits outweighed the drawbacks.

                                                If I were to put it in to mechanical form, it would be a high risk, high reward style mechanic for both parties involved. The higher the bond rating, the more each character's personalities warped so that the other was considered their highest priority, often taking on personality traits that the other had, something that could probably be done with Intimacies in 3e and with Virtues in 2e.

                                                    The more a particular exalt reflects their Exaltation (for lack of a better term), the more powerful they become. This can be done in a number of ways, though the most common is to exemplify a heroic ideal or the exalt's patron. A Solar gains more power from his exaltation the more skilled he becomes in a particular area (especially if that area includes something the previous incarnation was skilled at) and the more he embodies Sol's virtues, for example. This was originally designed with the intention that the most powerful Exalts would come to embody the heroic ideal their exaltations represented. This backfired immensely when the Neverborn and Yozis got their hands on the Exaltations and began providing their own flavor of essence, but hey, good try.

                                                            Lytek does not have a cabinet. He has a holy vault, where the only light comes from the constellation of exaltations that swirl and dance ethereally through the center of the room. They are only perceptible to essence sight or hearing, but for those who could perceive them it would be as though a shoal of stars were dancing to heavenly music. This vault is high-ceilinged and unadorned, for Lytek believes that in the sublime presence of even one naked exaltation all other adornment is meaningless.

                                                            Ages ago, Sidereals and other Exalted sometimes attempted to divine omens from the intricate patterns of the dancing exaltations. Since the Usurpation, Lytek has placed unsleeping guardian gods at the doors and permits nobody at all to enter, except himself.
