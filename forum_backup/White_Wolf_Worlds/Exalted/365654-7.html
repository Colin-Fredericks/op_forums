                        At the very least, it seems to me that the Scientific Method (and empiricism) can at least be used, to some degree, on dealing with Essence. The question isn't, "Can you use the scientific method in conjunction with Essence," (Yes/No) but rather, "How much can you use the scientific method," and may vary from table to table.

                        But generally speaking, it seems to me that, the sorts of things involving Essence that you can't test tend to be of the more esoteric variety - things that are more or less outside the scope of the average game.

                        Meanwhile, the things you can test, getting some understanding of how much Essence it takes to attune to a daiklave, how many daiklaves you can attune to, how often you can use a charm, what will happen if you build a demense in a certain area, what will happen to the Essence if you break that wall there, whether or not one type of Exalted generally has a larger Essence pool than another Exalt, how many times someone can probably use a given charm, etc, were likely things that were tested to some degree by the Exalted of the First Age. And they would have come to various conclusions, written treatise and such about them.

                        While things like this may be seen as mysteries in 2nd Age (where Exaltation is purely a spiritual matter, a gift from the Dragons, and therefore it would probably be considered fairly heretical if you were to start testing the limits of what your Exaltation could accomplish), they probably weren't as mysterious during the First Age, and the Exalted back then probably came to various understandings that may be forgotten by the Time of Troubles.

                            The way I see it, I'd say there is probably a bit of scientific thinking (though probably little exact science stuff) to the interpretation of various signs of the movement of Essence as part of the work of geomancer, but it relies more on sensibility and intuition and broad principles than hard rules. Even if there are some sort of rules to it, and there probably are, it depends a lot on interpretation and an intuitively sensing and understanding of the "flow of nature". I guess it could be said to be more like understanding the Tao (or maybe understanding the Tao Te Ching as a tool to comprehend nature) than understanding engineering. Not guessing, but comprehending stuff through perceptions that are not really rational.

                            To give an example within my area of expertise, consider traditional acupuncture: there is a scientific aspect to it up to a certain point - results can be reproduced, there are etiological causes to diseases, symptoms are considered in the diagnosis (it is not just intuitively knowing what is wrong with the patient), specific points do specific things that are cataloged, various combinations are known to give particular results, today we know quite a bit about the physiological mechanisms that make it work* and so on.

                            On the other hand, sometimes an appropriate, well directed intention is considered more important than a good needle-manipulation technique or a particularly precise insertion in a point**. And manipulating the needle in a clockwise or counterclockwise motion can have different effects depending on cultural background due to the change in how you perceive that motion


                            * Of course, in the current state of Creation, they probably wouldn't know much of those mechanisms, since it is hard to study neurophysiology with bronze age technology. That said, Exalted's equivalent of the Yin-Yang and 5-Element theories are probably much better models of that reality than they are here in our universe, so that is likely to be much less important.

                            ** The points are really regions, so the treatment can still work when you don't get it dead center on the perfect spot. Of course, excellent technique, precision and intention following an excellent diagnosis is the ideal.

                            Edit: Of course, any decent geomancer must also be a capable architect, but his more intuitive understanding of essence informs his more hard-science construction of a manse. He must use science to know how to support that large crystal using vines or whatever, but the reason that has to be done is not entirely rational.
