    The Element(s) of Poison

    04-22-2020, 08:52 PM
    I was thinking about mid-season episodes of Dr. Stone a day or two ago and, while reading the Wood Aspect anima power again (post 1e, of course), it struck me as odd that Wood is the only element associated with poisonous substances. While plant life certainly gets by on using them as a defense through a lot of different flora, Creation presumably has lots of volcanoes, springs, and other sites of harsh geology that give off toxic gases. Lookshy, as of the pdf expansion preview, even pulls a Delphic Oracle bit with a less toxic example of this concept (one sorcerous ritual relies on what is ethylene gas, to us, making the sorcerer goofy and giggly for a bit, like alcohol consumption). The Mountain Folk Storytelling chapter for 2e even acknowledged the hazards of spelunking with dangerous gases that lurk below the ground. I feel that this is very much underplayed for non-Wood Aspects, while Wood probably dwells a bit too much on amygdalin and plants like the Manchineel.

    It's not like this is a subtle, little-known and obscure danger, either. Volcanoes and geysers and rather loud and noticeable when active; they'll also stink and make bystanders gag on sulfur fumes. Any body of water near a volcano is liable to kill those nearby en masse, too; if there's a god present and of sufficient ambition, they might even advertise this fact, and garner worship by threatening to make the problem creep down further than it usually does, or release all of the "quiet gas" in the lake all at once. By all rights, a toxic anima effect ought to be common among every Aspect but Air.

                  I agree with Isator.

                  It's not always easy to delineate the themes of the Aspects, but it is kind of important. You could of course have them all using similar powers with just different visual themes (and there are a few charms like that, ie attack with your element, but they do tend to at least have small differences: fire bolts do slightly more damage, while water bolts can flow around shields and lightning bolts get bonuses vs metal armour).
                  But I think that would in reality make Dragonblood more boring.


                  Also... in terms of combat specifically, I think Wood sometimes suffers a bit. Earth is about being super-tough, Fire is about doing more damage, Air is about ranged stuff, Water's all flowy and dodgy and Kung-Fu*. They have done a bit with Wood and Stamina this edition (hence why my Wood Aspect archer has Strength 4, Dexterity 4 and Stamina 5... what kind of other archer would have Stamina as their top attribute?), but giving them poison is a big deal, combat-wise.** Without it, I can really see Wood Aspects feeling like they're the poor man of Dragonblood combat characters. And unlike Solars, the intention with Dragonblood is that one Aspect is the non-combat social aspect.

                  *Kung-Fu is such a big part of Exalted that being slightly more about Kung-Fu than all the other Kung-Fu heroes is pretty significant.

                  **Despite saying this, my 300xp Wood Aspect doesn't have any charms that inflict poison. He's got a few workhorse and anti-undead archery and occult charms, and a ton of Resistance charms. I actually don't like using poison much, as I find it yet another thing to keep track of in combat, plus it's often not all that great. But for really difficult fights, when I'm flaring, then anyone who takes anima damage takes a difficulty 9 poison hit, and I took Poisoner's Deft Hand since it has few prereqs, so I can boost that to difficulty 11. Now that's serious poison, even if it's only 2i a round/-1. The kind that makes people throw down big fat Resistance excellencies (if they can).
