        So, the general consensus is “run + fight + call for help”, with some added flair

        Quickly, before the two groups can box you in tight, you slap a mailed fist down on Xuansu’s rump, causing him to rear and snort in surprised anger. He surges forwards, out towards the main pathway and the crowd.

        The assailants, advancing shoulder-to-shoulder between two canvas walls, couldn’t have dodged even if they had time to, and get bowled over by about 1200 pounds of horseflesh. A couple of the men go flying, into the canvas walls and out into the pathway, and one gets trodden underneath Xuansu’s hooves. This close, you can clearly hear bones snap, and the man so stepped on do not get up.

        Before the enemy has a chance to recover from this sudden reversal of fortunes, you push Blue-Pines out towards Xuansu, out to the open where you can work together to “block off” the narrow alleyway and prevent your assailants from surrounding you. One of the men thrown into the tents tries to grab her by the leg as she passes by, in order to drag her down to earth.
        Without missing a step, she drives the spike on the butt-end of her spear through his forearm, breaking the bones with a crack, and as the man screams, she stomps on his head, driving him face-first into the mud (er, part of it is mud, at least). With this, she whirls about, spear at the ready, giving you the breathing room necessary to rush up to Xuansu and pull your spear from where it is lashed to the tack.

        As soon as you step out from between the tents, you go to call for the militia, or anyone available, and are immediately hit with a wall of sound. The thunder of hundreds or thousands of stampeding feet, the crash of stalls as they get turned over and trampled, and screams, from the throat of the man at your feet, crushed underfoot, and a rising crescendo of cries of fright from the rushing crowd. Above all else, however, is the building roar of fire, racing from tent to stall and back again, a backdrop above the spreading morass that is the tournament-grounds. The sight of the flames climbing into the sky shock you into dumbstruck silence, if only for a moment.

        Someone, somewhere, set something on fire, and now not only do you have to fight off the assassins in front of you, but now you must also beat the flames, or risk dying from smoke-inhalation or burning. Already, the smoke is building, swirling around your feet in thick black eddies, and it is rapidly becoming hard to see and breathe.

        Grimly, you turn back to Blue-Pines, who is apparently having great success in fighting off the assailants all by herself, fending off the group via the reach of her spear, and you take this opportunity to silence the assassins that were thrown to the ground, still struggling for purchase in the muck. After a few quick stabs with the blade of your spear, they are brought down, and you step forwards to assist your armswoman with the remainder.

        Evidently, the roughnecks weren’t expecting such aggressive/competent resistance, and disheartened by the rapid loss of half their group, the remainder quickly turns and flee. They disappear into the maze of tents and alleys, and it would be suicidal to follow them, even if there wasn’t the fire to contend with.

        Coughing, you reach into your waist-sash and pull out some scraps of cloth, passing one to Blue-Pines and tying the other over your nose and mouth. It helps but little, and your eyes are starting to blur and water from the flying smoke and embers.

        As soon as Blue-Pines catches a glimpse of the growing flames, by this point rising up to the level of the walls of Teriake Castle, she curses , eyes wide,” ever-shifting hells! What happened?”

        “No idea. Regardless, we have to get out of here, or the fire will kill us just as well as sure as those assassins. “

        After but a few minutes of stumbling through the smoke, you come to realize that the central-pathway through the tournament grounds practically no longer exists. The stampeding crowd knocked over and crushed the market stalls and carts, and wagons and pallets of supplies were left sitting in the middle of the path when the porters fled.

        You might be able to clamber through the twisted wreckage on foot, but you would certainly have to leave Xuansu behind to his fate, and after examining just how much stuff there is to climb over, there is no way you would be able to make it through before the fire reached you, not armored as you are.

        You turn to Blue-Pines, who in spite of only being arms-length away, is barely visible.

        “Come on, we have to go back!”

        She stares at you, “Through the fire? Are you crazy?”

        “We aren’t going to get through here! Do you have a better idea?”

        She thinks for a moment,” How far away is the stadium? There was some clear space around it that should stop the fire from getting to us!”

        “We are going to have to hurry, then! Can you run?”

        “Not in this smoke, I can barely see, much less run!”

        You curse. Already, you think you can feel the heat of the flames reflecting off your armor, and a line of cold sweat is trickling down your spine. After a long moment of thought, an idea enters your head and you grab Blue-Pines hand.

        “Hold on to this, Xuansu will be able to pick a path!”, and you guide her hand to one of the girth-straps of Xuansu’s saddle.

        With each of you holding the saddle in a death-grip, Xuansu finds his way through the wreckage and smoke, keeping as clear from the actual flames as best as he can. To help him out, you and Blue-Pines probe ahead with the butts of your spears, to find obstructions through the smoke.

        Even with your spear thrust ahead of you, you stumble over something left lying in the path, and Xuansu balks away from it. Bending forwards, you start when the body of man comes into view, huddled on the ground. From a cursory inspection, there is no way to tell what killed him, but the corpse is badly burned, and you suddenly realize that the smoke smells not only of burning wood and canvas, but also roasting meat. Sudden nausea rises in your throat, and you retch up your breakfast.

        Standing, throat burning with smoke and bile, you jerk on Xuansu’s saddle, pulling him forwards. After a few yards, you find more bodies, singly at first, then in clumps and groups, and Blue-Pines cries out when she finds out what she was stepping on. All of the bodies are covered in burns, and some were crushed, either by collapsing structures or by the feet of their fleeing compatriots. Men, women, children, no one was spared.

        There is no time to reflect on this, and you get brought back to earth by the sudden crash of a nearby tent, collapsing into an inferno with a roar of flame.

        All ahead of you, tents and stalls are consumed in fire, a roaring dragon of smoke and embers that dances and swirls overhead.

        You start to feel the cold buzz of panic. In all this smoke, you can’t tell where the hell you are, much less the way to the open plaza around the stadium. You are lost in the flames, just waiting to suffocate or worse, get consumed by the conflagration. In a sudden fit of desperation, you plead to any deity that will listen, asking for some way through the hellscape.

        As if by a miracle, a sudden gust of wind rushes by you, cutting through the smoke and clearing the sky for but a moment. Ahead of you, under the light of the afternoon sun, lies revealed the stadium, still rising above the tent-city. Heartened by your sudden good fortune, you all-but drag Xuansu and Blue-Pines towards the possible salvation.

        After a few minutes, you reach the plaza surrounding the stadium. To your chagrin, you are not the only one to have this idea, and the open space is filled to capacity with refugees from the fire, to the point where people have to shy away from the fires but a few feet away, cringing from the heat and flames. The air is filled with the cries of the frightened and the injured, to the point where human noise drowns out the crackle and roar of flames.

        Pulling the bunched cloth away from your mouth,” This is a disaster just waiting to get worse. Where is the militia, the fire-brigade?”

        Blue-Pines shakes her head. “ There … there isn’t much that can be done against this, Boss. All the water in the river wouldn’t stop it now. They are probably waiting behind the town walls for all this to burn out.”

        You snarl, angry at this cowardice. “There are hundreds of people here! They can’t just leave them here to die!”

        She shrugs weakly, exhausted. “You tell me, Lord. There is fuck all I can do.”

        You glare, at the flames and at the people standing around, effectively waiting to die. There has to be something that can be done, some way to salvage something from this catastrophe.

        You:
        1. Try to organize the crowd into doing something. Build a firebreak, fight the flames, something
        2. Push you way through, to the stadium, and try to get inside. The space inside should be a refuge from the smoke, if just for a little while, and which is, despite the obvious flames, the most dangerous aspect of the fire.
        3. Head back into the tent-city. There might be parts that have already burnt down, and you can escape the fire that way.
        4. Try to help the wounded. Even at this distance, it looks like many people got crushed by debris, suffer from burns, or are just sitting listlessly, deep in shock. While there isn’t much you can do, anything is better than just sitting here.
        5. Something else?
        OOC: Just a little snippet, before we get to the end of the chapter.

        Now we get to the meat of the matter. Shit has hit the fan, and it is being sprayed around the room at high velocity.

        I originally meant to make this longer, and end the chapter on this post, but I realized it would be a good 15-20 pages without much in the way of a decision point, with the exception of the very end, which is obviously a pretty major, arguably the major, decision of our early career as an Oathsworn. Not liking that very much, and thinking ahead to my (fucked up) work schedule, I decided I could fit at least one more meaningful decision in. I'll probably have one more after this, dependent, of course, on what you choose as our course of action.

        Our actions here, along with how we have behaved thus far in Teriake, will determine how the common people will view us in the future.

        On combat:

        I am kinda amused at how seriously you lot took the "assassination" (which is, of course, is what it was, albeit a very unprofessional and quickly-planned one).

        I mean, mortal or not, Kasai and Blue Pines are in armor, and comparatively well-armed, and Kasai fought off 5 bandits unarmored in a 5v1 earlier in the Quest, giving a good indication of his skill level (I've got him pegged at Melee 3, with a Specialty in Spears, for those interested). The "real" danger here wasn't from the actual assassins per se, but because we were surrounded, and through the implication that we pissed someone off enough to warrant an assassination in the first place.

        However, if you guys decided to actually stand and fight, things would probably have been much more hairy, but we would have come through in the end, albeit in quite a bit worse shape.

                                            It appears that the general desire is ,“ organize the crowd into doing something”, and “Try to help the wounded”, along with a couple “get into the stadium” and, “head out into the already-burnt part of the tent city”.

                                            On another note: these last two weeks have been rough, with 12-hour overnight shifts, along with two overnight snow plowings. After this chapter, I’ll have to seriously evaluate how much I am actually able to do in the future. Not saying “I bit off more than I could chew” by undertaking this Quest-thread, I merely didn’t anticipate the level of work required. I am, of course, planning on finishing this. Amusingly, I actually have ideas for several other Quests brewing, but ..... let us finish one thing at a time.

                                            During the overnight shifts, I actually did a whole line-diagram –type deal with how several possible chapters could go out, so I have quite a bit of stuff down. It is finding the time to get it on paper (out of my mind and on the computer, in actuality) that I am having problems with.

                                            ------

                                            You curse, well aware that if something doesn’t get done soon, a whole lot of people are going to die painfully, you included. More importantly, it is rapidly becoming apparent that you are going to have to organize the something. As irritating as it is, being the only one willing to step forwards, you realize with a burst of wry humor that this is what being an Oathsworn is all about, and while the situation is a bit …. ah, extreme, you are supposed to serve as an inspiration, leader in both peace and war, through the following of your sworn Oaths. Here, they are directly applicable.

                                            Hell, if anything, this situation is the easiest by far situation to interpret through your Oaths. Either you act, and people (might) be saved, or you don’t, and they die. Through your actions, others might be inspired to act, where before they might stay silent. The right course of action is now clear, and with that in mind, any tension and trepidation fade away, replaced with a calm sense of purpose. Sure, things look bad, but at least you can do something!

                                            Blue Pines starts at your sudden laugh, filled with relief as you are.

                                            “Is something ….. funny, Lord? Because I don’t see it.”

                                            “No, just finally realizing something I should have long ago.”. She looks at you like you just suddenly grew another head, and you wave off the building question. You have work to do.

                                            Nearby, there is the wreck of a wagon or cart laying in the middle of the plaza, with refugees clustered around it, and you clamber up to the top in order to get a better view. Up above the heads of the crowd, amidst the swirling smoke and sparks, you suddenly spot the conical helmet of one of the Teriake Militia!

                                            “Hey! HEY! Miltiaman!”. Of course, over the crowd and crackle of the flames, they probably can’t hear you, and you jump off the wagon and push over to the man. Grabbing him by the shoulder, you wheel him about, only to come face-to-face with a young man, barely out of adolescence, all gangly limbs and bones, with a face filled with terror. Face pale, lips drawn tight, and you can even feel his limbs shaking through his overlarge jacket.

                                            “Hey, do you know what is going on? ….. I mean, do you know what happened?”

                                            The kid stares at you for a second, then mutely shakes his head.

                                            “Are there any other militiamen around?”

                                            “N.. Not that I have seen! I was only hired a week ago, for the tournament, I don’t know anything! I ran here after the fire started. A… are we gonna die?” The poor kid is practically in tears, and you clap him on the shoulder, as much a means to keep his attention as to give comfort.

                                            “Not if I have any say in the matter. Do you have a trumpet, or a horn or anything?”

                                            He brightens a bit, “ yeah, I’ve got a whistle, but what are you gonna do?”

                                            “First, I am going to get the attention of everyone that I can, then I am going to try to organize this mob into something that could save us all. Stick by me, son (son? He is only a few years younger than you, it looks like), I could use some help. Do you have a name?”

                                            “H.. Haru, my Lord”, as he fumbles through his padded coat for his whistle.

                                            “Good, Haru, follow me and we will get through this. When I get on top of that wagon over there, I want you to blow your whistle as long as you can, until you get somebody’s attention. Got it?”

                                            He nods, and even with such a meager task to accomplish, he looks much more focused, still pale and frightened, but determined.

                                            When you get back to the wagon wreckage, Blue Pines is leaning against it, staring out glumly at the huddled crowd and the encroaching flames. So enthralled, she doesn’t notice when you get up next to her, and you have to grab and shake her shoulder in order to drag her attention away from the scene.

                                            “Are you ready to jump into action?”

                                            “What?” She looks incredibly confused.

                                            “Just follow along”, as you climb again to the top of the wagon. Even in the few minutes it has been since you’ve got here, the air is filling with smoke, and even a few feet above the ground, it is increasingly difficult to make out individual people in the crowd below, not to mention hard to breathe. You are going to have to get to work fast.

                                            “Alright, Haru, blow your whistle for all you are worth! Blue Pines, go and get the attention of the closest people who are willing to listen!”

                                            Even with the press of the crowd, it takes a few minutes for the piercing whistle and Blue Pines shakings to rouse the people from their shock, and a few moments longer for them to gather around you in a decently-sized group. All the while, the smoke and flames creep closer.

                                            One large, roughish-looking man, bolder or less-shocked than the rest, calls out ,” What’re you calling us over here for, boss? Things are a little fucked, can’t you tell?”

                                            “That is what I plan on doing something about, my good man. If we don’t, everyone here is going to die, either from burns, or more likely, from breathing in the smoke. Now, I don’t know about you, but that doesn’t seem like my cup of tea.” You grin at your attempt at humor, and it even draws a few chuckles from the crowd, lame as it is.

                                            The man shakes his head, “ So, what’ve you got in mind?”

                                            “Well, fire needs fuel, right? What happens if it doesn’t have fuel?”

                                            “It….. goes out?”, he replies, confused at such an obvious question.

                                            “Right! Now, with the fire here, it is fueling itself on the tents, shacks and other stuff on the ground. If we push the stuff that isn’t on fire yet back, into the flames, there won’t be any fuel left for it to burn. Plus, that will limit the space it has to advance. How does that sound?”

                                            “It….. sounds a hell of a lot better than just sitting here and waiting to die, honest.”, to the murmured affirmation of the crowd. “How do we go about that?”

                                            “Take hold of some poles or staves, and knock down tents and such, or push the wreckage that is in the streets into the flames, to open a path.” You meet the man’s eyes and hold his gaze. “Can I leave you in charge of that?”

                                            He holds your gaze, nodding assent. “Sure”.

                                            Your shoulders sag in relief, something you are barely able to keep the crowd from seeing. “Good, good. Rouse up some sturdy boys and get to it. Don’t forget to send some runners to the other parts of the plaza, so they can get to work on their side.” With that, the man walks away, grabbing several other people and dragging them with him. Some of them grab poles and chunks of timber, while others race away around the plaza.

                                            You turn to Blue Pines, “We need to get away from the sparks and smoke, and in order to do that, we need to get inside the stadium. Between the walls and the plaza, we should be safe from the fires for some time, enough time for it to burn itself out, hopefully. I need you to take charge of that.”

                                            She nods, “Any ideas?”

                                            You shrug, “To be honest, so long as we can get the wounded inside in one piece, I don’t really care. Same thing: grab some people and make it happen. Once you get inside, start moving people inside, but make sure there isn’t a stampede.”

                                            You clamber down from the wagon, only to get buttonholed by Haru, “ Is there anything you want me to do, Lord?”

                                            “Not just yet. Stay with me; I am going to do the same thing all around the plaza, and I need your whistle to get people’s attention.”

                                            And so you make your way through the crowd, stopping every time there is a crowd to repeat your plan and give orders. In many cases, runners have already beaten you to the punch, and the people are already up and animated, needing only a slight push to get working, and by the time you complete a circuit around the stadium, the “aura” of the crowd has changed drastically, being given purpose and hope, even with the actual situation becoming worse. The smoke has thickened (probably as a result of more fuel being added to the flames), blocking out the sun and dropping the world into an artificial dusk. You can only see by the light of the very flames you are trying to extinguish, an irony that would be highly amusing in any other situation.

                                            Slowly, you notice the crowd start to move in a single direction, and you make your way over to where you left Blue Pines to her work. There, you see her, along with several others, shepherding the crowd into the open gates of the stadium through a series of queues, cunningly built of piled lumber and other wreckage. In addition, the most heavily-wounded are being carried directly into the stadium, on stretchers made of tent-canvas.

                                            Now that things are actually proceeding in an organized fashion, you feel yourself relaxing, even if only slightly. Delegating tasks out to others took much of the work off your shoulders, letting you keep an eye on the big picture, while ensuring the necessary things got taken care of at the same time.

                                            At this point, you no longer really need Haru with you, and you send him inside the stadium, to supervise the people milling around inside on the field. As you do so, you hear the people around you whispering to their fellows, and several of the bolder ones come forwards, grasping your arms and thanking you for saving them. This actually makes you rather uncomfortable, unused as you are to praise from the public eye, and you brush it off awkwardly, explaining that your Oaths required it of you, that it was nothing, etc. You try to make your way to Blue Pines, only to get held back by more ingratiatingly-thankful townsfolk, and by the time you get up to the gate, you are a little irritated by the attention.

                                            Blue Pines looks even more exhausted, if possible, than she did an hour ago.

                                            “How goes the relocation effort?”

                                            She shakes her head in annoyance. “It’s like herding cats, except cats are probably smarter.”

                                            Chuckling, “Good work, anyways. How are you organizing them?”

                                            Gesturing to the barriers, “well, the guys you have knocking down stuff brought this back, and the people who ca…”

                                            “LORD, come quick!” Haru comes sprinting up, helmet bouncing around loosely on his head.

                                            “What? What is it?” you reply, hand going to your sword hilt.

                                            “There are dead people inside!”

                                            You shoot a quick look at Blue Pines, then stride off behind Haru, who is running up the stairs and benches of the stands, up to the box where Lord Iysen was sitting during the competition. Your stomach drops, and you hurry up into the box.

                                            Inside the wooden-walled box is an abbatoir. The chairs inside are turned over and smashed, several crossbow bolts are buried into the walls, and strewn over the floor in pools of black, congealed blood are several bodies. One of them, you notice in sudden disquiet, is the armored figure of Lord Iysen’s bodyguard (Nori?).

                                            You quickly step over to him, turning him over as gently and respectfully as you can, so you can better see what killed him. Almost instantly, as he rolls over, his throat yawns open in a second mouth, and you hear Haru retching outside the box. The cut to the neck is but one wound on his body. A crossbow bolt in the thigh, another buried under his arm, a cut to the elbow that left the couter dangling by a half-severed strap. In spite of these wounds, he obviously survived long enough to take out those who attacked him, and when you pick up his sword, you can see where the blade struck bone

                                            Despite all this (and not meaning any disrespect to Nori), you don’t see what you came to look for: any sign of Lord Iysen, or her children.

                                            Unnerved, you go to check the bodies of the possible assassins. They are, to a man, comparatively unremarkable. Clothing of middling cuts and colors, no jewelery, insignia or identifiers that could point out who they were or who they worked for. Even their weapons are unremarkable; munitions-quality crossbows and short backswords, something any well-off peasant could buy.

                                            “L… Lord, what are we gonna do about this?”

                                            You turn your head, to look at the young man over your shoulder, “We? We are going to do nothing about this. There is nothing that can be done, not while this fire is still burning, but I have my suspicions about that.”

                                            “What do you mean?”

                                            You shake your head, “Nothing. You never saw any of this.”

                                            “I… I don’t understand.”

                                            Spreading your arms, you indicate the bloody scene before you. “This. You never came up here, you never saw this. There are no bodies; no indication there was an assassination attempt on Lord Iysen.”

                                            “Oh….. I…. I understand.”

                                            “Nobody knows there are bodies up here?”

                                            “Asides from the people who heard when I told you, no.”

                                            With that taken care of, the two of you return to the stadium floor, which is now filled with people. As soon as they come in, they start prying up boards to build impromptu lean-tos, covering them with scavenged canvas and other materials, including what looks like silk. Others meander out into the preparation chambers and stables, coming back with bales of hay and reports of cisterns and containers. Once hearing these, you organize groups of sturdy men to go and fill these containers and bring them out for people to use. Once there is an ample supply of water available, many of the people who are comparatively unwounded go around to those worse off, offering water that, while tepid, must seem like Heaven to smoke-ravaged throats and eyes.

                                            While all of this is going on, you take a moment to sit down and think about what has happened. So far, you have what looks like an assassination attempt (or a successful one, you have to admit, uncomfortable with the implications), a fire that is raging out of control, and a still-panicked populace that, if the news of the first gets out, or the second gets worse, could boil over into a riotous mob. Pondering on all of this brings you into a morose funk, and lost in your thoughts, you don’t notice that you have people standing before you until one of them loudly clears their throat.
                                            Lifting your head, you see several men grouped together, many with strips of cloth bound about their upper arms in a crude uniform of sorts. Leading them is the man from earlier, the one who headed up the debris-moving crew, and he looks right-pissed. Several of the men are all-but dragging others, in various stages of disarray and bloodiness.

                                            “What is going on, gentlemen?”, you ask, confused.

                                            “Right, Lord, we were working on dropping tents and moving wreckage, and we came across several guys who were, well, not behaving too well, to put it politely.”

                                            “What do you mean?” standing up.

                                            He snarls, “Some people apparently didn’t think the line was moving quite fast enough for them, and they decided to push ahead in line. A fight broke out, and several people got stabbed or coshed. Some of the people who got hurt died.”

                                            Your lips pull back from your teeth, almost of their own volition, and the group pulls back almost as a man. Struggling, you control your sudden anger, and settle on a glare that causes the restrained men to flinch away.

                                            “Really? You didn’t think there has already been enough death and pain today, you felt the need to add to it?”

                                            The headman continues hotly, “That’s not all, we were pulling down a tent, in order to slow the fire, when some guy in armor comes running out, yelling at us to stop. We ….. well, we ignored him, after telling him what we were doing, and he drew on us! He cut up Tachi!”

                                            “Where is this man?”

                                            “Right here, I followed this mummer’s dance to see this farce for myself.”, an Oathsworn says as he elbows his way through the group.

                                            “Enough”, you shout, attracting the attention of seemingly half the stadium. Many of the closest people move to surround the situation, muttering darkly when they hear about what happened, and Blue Pines rushes over, spear at the ready. You stop her with a gesture.

                                            “Why, exactly, did you bring these men to me? What do you want me to do with them?”

                                            “… To dispense justice, Lord. We don’t have the authority.”

                                            Massaging your temples, you consider just what has happened here.

                                            1. Let all of them go. You don’t have time for this, and you can understand how tempers can flare in this situation

                                                        1) He is in armor, or, really, parts of armor. Lamellar breastplate, splinted greaves and bracers, the only thing he doesn't have on is a helmet. While his full panoply is heavier than Kasai's mail, in his current state the two are about equal.

                                                        2) Oathsworn can kill each other either on the field of battle, or in an official (aka, announced, with challenges recieved, and with a number of witnesses and seconds observing) duel. Any other time is murder. So long as you get caught, that is. Nobility (and Oathsworn) assassinate each other all the time, relatively speaking. In many cases, a quiet assassination is preferred to a duel, feud or war.

                                                        3) If Kasai just up and killed anyone, he would be imprisoned until a Magistrate (almost always a fellow Oathsworn) could hear the case. At that point, Kasai and the injured party (if he killed someone, the deceased family members or dependents) would be allowed to present their side of the story. After hearing both sides, and any other witness recounts, the Magistrate would pass sentence based on precedent, the laws of each domain, and the circumstances of the crime. In the case of murder, there would almost always be money changing hands (kind of like weregeld, but not codified in law), with actual punishment being doled out based on social status.

                                                        Kasai is technically a noble, so if he killed a commoner, he would basically get away punishment-free, asides from the death-fee (and any social/reputation damage). If he killed a noble, then things could get dicey. Depending on the social status of the deceased noble, he could be imprisoned for an amount of time (with accompanying hard labor), stripped of rank, made a slave to the deceased' house, or executed (privately)

                                                        If Kasai was a commoner, he would just get imprisoned, or likely executed (publicly)

                                                        Of course, official duels between nobles are outside and above the law, so they don't count as murder.

                                                        For an "official" duel to be official, there has to be a recognized injured party, where their honor, dignity or somesuch has been offended, challenges have to be officially exchanged in the presence of witnesses, and the combatants have to have seconds and 5 witnesses apiece. Most duels are to first blood, third blood in the case of particularly grievous slights, or death when nothing else will suffice. Duels to the death are rare, and frowned upon, but not legally prohibited, as one's honor is considered "above the law" in the eyes of most nobles. Finally, both parties must be "equal" in equipment; challenging someone to a duel in plate and longspear, when all they have is mail and sword is considered highly dishonorable and contemptable.

                                                        As long as those conditions are met, the two parties can pretty much go to town.

                                                        Duels between nobles and commoners are rare, as it is considered exceptionally poor form to challenge someone not of your social class to a duel of honor. On the other hand, commoners are supposed to be deferential in most respects to nobility, and nobility are "allowed" to strike, beat or kill commoners that fail to show proper deference. It is considered rare to actually do so (most nobles will respond to improper deference or outright impropriety with a roll of the eyes and having the rude peasant removed from their presence, and most commoners aren't going to go out of their way to be assholes to people technically allowed to kill you), as it is considered low-class to be so ruled by your emotions, but the "right-of-class" is still present.

                                                        For instance, this is why people weren't giving Kasai much shit about being a Hill-Tribesman, at least not to his face. If he was sufficiently insulted, he could strike them or even kill them. If he actually went through with killing the asshole monk that spat on him, he would receive no official punishment, at least from the secular authority. The abbot would probably ask him to do some sort of penance, however.

                                                        For other crimes, there are fees and punishments, but generally nobles (and Kasai) are going to receive lesser versions, unless they piss off someone above them enough.

                                                        4) No, Oathsworn are not officially allowed to take the law into their hands. One has to be a Lord (or a Lord's duly-appointed representative, such as a Magistrate) in order to deliberate on the law, and pass judgement and punishment on cases.

                                                        For example Kasai couldn't pass judgement on the peasants on his father's estate, but his father could. He was Oathsworn and the Lord of the estate (although a poor and rural one). One can hold land and be Oathsworn, and not be the Lord (in which case they have to bring the case to their Lord), or one could hold land and be Lord, but not an Oathsworn.

                                                        Generally, depending on the domain, around 1/2 of the warriors that "fight as" Oathsworn (aka, heavy infantry + cavalry) aren't actually Oathsworn, just nobility. Being an Oathsworn at least nominally requires the swearing of Oaths (hence the name) that many nobles find onerous or impossible to uphold (not that many Oathsworn are all that great about upholding them).

                                                        But, as above, the circumstances of a case influence the judgement, and if Kasai were to kill people in order to prevent a mob or riot, then his punishment would likely be highly lessened.
