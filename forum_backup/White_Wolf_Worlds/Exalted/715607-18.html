                    Okay: “Chapter Seven: Martial Arts and Sorcery.”

                    We get an opening fiction with Shen, and gosh is it verbose. The first half-page or so boils down to “Shen sells drugs as a cover for his actual work”; the latter half is him actually doing things (summoning, infecting, and promptly banishing a demon, in this case). The art of the same on the following page isn’t really a standout in either direction – it does its job, and that’s that.

                    We’re told that martial arts “are not compatible with” Charms of other Abilities – but what does this mean, precisely? I get that I can’t activate both Brawl and Martial Arts Charms on the same instant, but what about long-duration Charms? What about permanent ones? I assume using a martial art for offense and something else for defense (or vice-versa) is kosher, but all of this could stand a little more explicit delineation.

                    Styles may have “lost” Charms known only to ancient masters, or newly uncovered through rare and remarkable circumstances. That’s kind of a nice touch.

                    Form Charms are still a thing, but switching Forms only requires you to pay the difference between them. That’s a nice touch.

                    Okay, so Solars, Dragon-Blooded, and everybody else all use the same Charms now. But, if you’re a Solar-tier Exalt, you get the “Mastery” keyword for bonus effect; if you’re a Dragon-Blooded, you get the “Terrestrial” keyword for reduced effect (unless you’re an Immaculate monk!). That’s a nice solution to the old problem of “There are, like, two guys who practice Snake Style, tops,” and it also lets a Solar train in a Dragon-Blooded dojo and then do the whole “Now I’ve surpassed my master” thing. (One presumes Sidereals get access to Mastery, as well?)

                    The whole “buy a whole Ability from scratch” thing rankles a little, with Martial Arts. They are, of course, combat stuff you can buy with Solar XP – but at 19 XP for the Ability, plus, what, 80+ XP for the actual tree? That’s a minimum of 25 sessions to buy one full tree – by that time, you’ll be Essence 3, and you haven’t bought any sorcery/Evocations/any other stats. The latter has felt most critical in my games so far; no one’s been interested in much in the way of XP dumps, but raising stats has gotten a lot of attention.

                    Snake Style

                    Essence to Master: 3
                    Armor: Light or none.
                    Weapons: Unarmed, seven-section staves, hook swords.
                    Shtick: Have a higher Initiative than your opponent, and then cripple your opponent with penalties and poison. Backstop your offense with bonuses to soak and limited damage-negation.

                    Charms:
                    1. +1 Evasion; can be activated after attack if you have higher Initiative.
                    2. Bonus damage against slower enemies. Extra bonus damage if they’re crashed.
                    3. Form: attacks on you take -1 die, or -3 if your Initiative is higher. Bonus soak.
                    4. Ignore armor soak or some hardness.
                    5. Perform gambits to blind or paralyze your enemy.
                    6. Upgrade #3 to give extra soak and Withering damage (and Decisive damage, with Mastery).
                    7. Drag an enemy from Medium to Close range.
                    8. Reflexively Clash with an enemy.
                    9. Use your Initiative to cancel damage from a Decisive attack.
                    10. Poison your enemy with Aggravated damage-dealing venom.
                    Serpentine Evasion (#1) is a less good Drifting Leaf Elusion, even with the Mastery discount. And the two don’t stack, which is uninspiring.

                    Snake Form (#3), on the other hand, is kind of a big deal. -3 dice to all attack rolls is a major shift in the probabilities. Even better, these rules adopt something introduced at the very tail (har) of the Monkeys era: a way to reflexively shift into a Form, if you don’t have a turn to spend activating a Simple Charm. Snake Form has a nice, easy shift condition that’s almost certainly going to come up if you push for it – nice! If you’re in light armor, it wouldn’t be unpleasant to sit in Snake even if you don’t use the rest of the style – pretty wasteful of XP, sure, but…

                    Ignoring armor on a Withering Strike, as Armor-Penetrating Fang Strike (#4) does, is nice – but shew, also expensive. I don’t have a good sense of how powerful this is for the cost.

                    Crippling Pressure Point Strike (#5), by contrast, is cheap and potent. You could paralyze both of your opponent’s arms with a couple of hits – but then, you could also deal him 8L instead… Shouldn’t this have the Crippling keyword, like Joint-Wounding Attack?

                    … Wait, hang on. Joint-Wounding Attack definitely has the Crippling keyword. You know who doesn’t have the Crippling keyword? The Keyword list.

                    Uncoiling Serpent Prana (#7) is just cool. This is the first “Get over here!” power I’ve seen, and it’s a great counter to kiting builds without dumping scads of XP in Athletics. Take that, horse archers.

                    Snake Strikes the Heel (#8) implies gambits are Decisive attacks, because your Decisive clash has to be a gambit, contrary to earlier Charms that implied they weren’t. So which is it? The Terrestrial note implies this Charm does not normally consume your attack for the turn; does that need to be explicitly stated?

                    Heck yeah, Tiger Style illustration. That is a picture of a dude whose expression fully commits to the fact that he is about to be murdered by a magical kung fu lady (who is also sort of a tiger). Looking forward to that one.

                    This is a tight, satisfying tree – as was foretold! It’s got most of your bases covered: Clashes, nasty offense, range, soak, late-step Defense buffs, Decisive attack negation, and so on. There’s no Onslaught negation, but that’s the only major hole that stands out to me. In general, the powers here are less good than dedicated Solar Charms – Countless Coils Evasion (#9), say, is similar to but less good than Heavenly Guardian Defense. On the other hand, they’re all in one short tree…

                    This is a tree that really, really wants you to be faster than your enemy; if you can’t regularly sustain that, you’re probably in for a bad time. A lot of its powers are more effective if the enemy is Crashed, though, so if you can get that upper hand, you’re going to be piling on a lot of hurt.

                                                Tiger Style

                                                Essence to Master: 3
                                                Armor: Light or none.
                                                Weapons: Unarmed, tiger claws

                                                Shtick: Close with your enemy. Punish him for trying to run away. Leap at him from the shadows, knock him to the ground, and savage him in a grapple.

                                                Charms:
                                                1. Steal 1i from an enemy when you successfully Rush him. Extra Rush successes grant bonus dice to your next attack on that enemy, as long as it’s within the next round.
                                                2. On a Withering attack, some of your extra attack successes become post-soak damage. This is increased if your target tried to run, or if you’re a Solar.
                                                3. Form. Some extra attack successes count double on a Withering roll. Fight prone without penalties. Bonus dice to Rush or counter Disengages; gain Initiative when the enemy Disengages.
                                                4. Gain soak and Hardness; drop this Charm to subtract Hardness from an incoming Decisive.
                                                5. Bonus rounds of control on a grapple.
                                                6. Deal extra damage, and reset to higher Initiative, when you savage a grappled enemy.
                                                7. Knock an enemy prone with any attack; steal Initiative if he tries to stand up. If Mastery, your attack on a prone enemy is undodgeable and does even more damage.
                                                8. On a successful Decisive, trade damage for preventing your enemy from moving.
                                                9. For the scene, enemies at short or medium range must Disengage to move away from you and take -1 Defense vs. surprise attacks.
                                                10. Limited double-9s, double-8s, or double-7s on an attack roll.
                                                11. Upgrade #3. Some extra attack successes transfer to damage on Decisives. Rush reflexively. You can’t Disengage.
                                                Can I just say how nice it is that these Charms are about 2/3 crunch, with a sentence or two of fluff at the start? It’s a very clear divide to when the Charm starts talking about actual mechanical things. I also like that so many of these powers reward you for raising your other physical Attributes (in this case, Strength).

                                                The Mastery clause of Striking Fury Claws (#2) is kind of awkwardly worded – it looks like the word “dice” might have been in this sentence once upon a time, to contrast with “successes.”

                                                Again, the form Charm (#3) is a broad, useful power that almost no close-combat build is going to be sorry to have around. Like Snake, Tiger has a solution to the problem of mobile enemies, and the solution is to run up to them and murder them to death, laughing at them when they try to run away. Not a lot of finesse, there, but hard to argue with the results! Looks like the reflexive activation is part of the standard form-Charm package now – good!

                                                Celestial Tiger Hide (#4) is not a great defense – given that you aren’t in much armor, we’re talking about something like -2-5i, costing a Willpower, usable once per scene, and removing the soak bonus afterwards. I’d much rather have Snake’s defense vs. Decisives – but Snake seems like a much more defensive tree in general.

                                                Okay, wow. Stalking Cat Movement Meditation (#9) is huge battlefield control – that’s a big shift in how well people can move you, even two range bands away. I was not expecting that in a scenelong power.

                                                Leap from Cloaking Shadows (#10) is one of the very few powers that grant double-Xs (even limited double-Xs) on an attack roll. It’s expensive, but if you can get the surprise attack, you’re going to have a pretty painful Decisive to throw around. Snake felt like it stood mostly alone; Tiger feels like it really wants some Athletics or Stealth to really bring on the pain. The Batman stealth combo would go really well with this Charm.

                                                Angry Predator Frenzy (#11) is very strong overall – rushing reflexively for the rest of the scene is great! – but it’s the Mastery effect that really sells me on this: your anima turns into a tiger and eats the other guy’s anima. That’s amazing.

                                                On balance, this is a very offense-heavy package. It has amazing mobility, good accuracy, and a lot of ways to boost your Decisives – but it’s not going to care a lot about Withering attacks, and you’re just about on your own if you get hit. It feels like it would play very differently from the last style, which is nice. This feels like a very Night style – Dodge (or Melee), Stealth, and Athletics are all going to play well with it.


                                                Single Point Shining Into the Void

                                                Essence to Master: 3
                                                Armor: Light, medium, or none.
                                                Weapons: Slashing swords, reaper daiklaves.

                                                Shtick: Let your sword speak (and attack) for you. If you are faster than your enemy, cut him. If he exposes a momentary weakness, which you could exploit at great personal risk, cut him. If he has the temerity to attack you, cut the attack. If he hurls impossible twisting magic at you, cut that, too. And if he lets you build up long enough, launch an attack that cannot be countered and will end with you taking his head.

                                                Charms:
                                                1. When you parry an attack, give the Onslaught penalty to your attacker instead of taking it yourself.
                                                2. Add extra damage to a Decisive – more, if it’s a big Decisive – but risk losing more if your attack fails.
                                                3. Form. Your sword Joins Battle and makes its own attacks each turn.
                                                4. Gain extra damage on a Decisive if your Initiative is higher than your enemy’s, but lose extra Initiative if the attack fails.
                                                5. Scenelong extra damage to Withering attacks; you can end the Charm for a big hit with a Decisive (or two big hits, if your sword helps).
                                                6. Strike an enemy out to medium range, adding accuracy and damage the further out he is. Slice all opponents between you and him.
                                                7. Clash reflexively, albeit expensively. Deal extra damage on this Decisive.
                                                8. Clash with shaping magic. Cut a path through the Wyld with your irresistible sword spirit.
                                                9. Make a gambit with no need for an attack roll. If it succeeds, make an undodgeable, unblockable Decisive attack.
                                                Shining Point is definitely the weirdest of the three styles so far, once we hit the form. (Does delaying your sword’s action cost it Initiative? One presumes so.) There’s some extra bookkeeping here, but the power of a free attack each turn is pretty huge. Unlike other options so far, you’re either going to get Shining Point up at the start of the fight, or you’re not going to activate it without a Simple action – there’s no chance of a mid-fight buff. This is not a great form for Terrestrials – 1wp/turn for a second attack is pretty rough – but a Solar is going to get free attacks every turn with basically no downside.

                                                This illustration of a Shining Point stylist is pretty great. I can’t quite follow what’s happening here, though somebody seems to be getting chopped up, but the stylist himself looks rad. I would play that guy.

                                                There’s a real all-or-nothing theme so far – attacks that are murderous if they land (add the difference between your Initiative and the other guy’s to damage? Ouch.) but will mess you up pretty badly if they don’t. Keeping a good defense handy against a SP stylist looks like a good call.

                                                Void-Slicing Wind (#6) again calls for you to know who is in a line between you and your enemy. The game needs battle maps, book! Commit to that fact!

                                                … Whoa. Six-Demon Scabbard Binding is probably the best response to no-defense twisting magic so far. It’s certainly a step up from Integrity’s “Give up a bunch of Willpower, hope for the best.”

                                                Blinding Nova Flare is absolutely terrifying. If this is not the first un/un attack so far, it’s pretty darn close – and this is an attack with no cost. There doesn’t seem to be any clear response to this power that isn’t a perfect defense – you can’t avoid the gambit, and as long as that goes off successfully, you’re going to eat the second attack. That almost feels out of step with the game as presented to this point – if your attacker gets to this point, you’re dead; just accept it.

                                                Some balance concerns aside, great style. Risky, but ready to murder the enemy if it succeeds. Your only defense seems to be some (quite expensive) Clashes, though the “return their Onslaught” effect is fun. Both the descriptions and the actual powers capture the high speed iaijutsu anime feel really well – this is not a style with a lot of options, but the options it has are amazing. I’m a little surprised there’s no Join Battle enhancer, given the focus on this being a fast style.

                                                My one critique is that this is also a wordy style - it lacks the short, clear statements of Snake and Tiger. Some of these Charms take a half-page or more to describe their effects.
