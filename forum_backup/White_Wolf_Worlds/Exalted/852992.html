    Brainstorming: Sidereals and the Great Curse

    03-25-2016, 11:47 AM
    Tangenting off from the Acquintances' thread;

    The Sidereal Great Curse has traditionally been expressed as 'They suffer from myopia when in large groups' - their mechanically impactful Curse has been Paradox in the form of 'abuse Astrology and get fucked'.

    Those aren't... to me, tremendously interesting or evocative.

    So- brainstorming!
    Sidereals, like other Celestial Exalts, have a Limit Track and undergo Limit Breaks.
    Unlike the Solar Limit Breaks, which tend to be extreme expressions of emotion undermining their positions (and more generally, express the epic myth concept of heroic flaws), Sidereal Limit Breaks should be used to express the dark side of their themes.
    The themes of the Sidereals are secret agents of Destiny- they draw from the spy genre, esoteric wisdom, and secret masters of the world- in both the Pei Mei sense and the Illuminati sense.

    * While in Limit Break, Sidereals suffer from visions of alternate realities.
    * ..., Sidereals gain a vision of a future that they must ensure (or avert) from coming to pass.
    * ..., Sidereals seek to prove their freedom from the system, sabogaging some destined thingy.

    ... Eh, these aren't gelling for me either.

    Bah.

    Any ideas?

      I love the name Paradox. It's great and evocative and has the core of what it means to fuck up as a Sidereal.

      I Hate the mechanic. It's awful and doesn't actually have anything to do with paradoxes at all.

      I posted this in another thread a while ago:

      Originally posted by Fata-Ku View Post
      In Awakening, a horror game where reality is literally out to punish you and erase you from existence, Paradox still isn't as bad (read: is more fun) than it is in a game about high-octane kung-fu bureaucrats. Come the fuck on!

      I like the vision that it's bureaucratic red tape and that you can lower it by staying late at work, but it's still missing an actual engagement mechanic. Doing generic extra paperwork isn't engaging, nor is trying to weasel into the smallest possible paradox pool (it might be intellectually rewarding the first couple of times, but once you've solved that puzzle it's just rote).

      By "engagement mechanic", I mean something that actually generates story and player action. Examples include:

      - Your Fate Request means that the pattern spiders can't overlook a certain aspect of creation, causing reality glitches that you must fix.
      - Your Fate Request directly competes with an other one, creating a destiny loophole that the PS are too busy to solve. Solve it yourself or get the other party to cancel their request.
      - The Pattern Spiders are tired of your bullshit and have decided to give you the silent treatment. You are now a glitch. Every Scene that you start in Creation, roll a d10. On a 1, you cease to interact with Creation, as though you were an illusion, until you pay a cost equivalent to what gods pay to Materialize. On a 7, you are mistaken for a local celebrity by literally everyone. On a 10, you gain [essence] points of storyteller-decided mutations for the remainder of the session. These points stack, but are purely cosmetic and don't actually exist, they just look like they do.

      - The Pattern Spiders are so tired of your bullshit that they're taking it out on you. Gain the Enemy flaw for the remainder of the story at a level deemed appropriate by teh ST. This may be a scorned lover, an ally who feels betrayed or a Deathlord that decided that you are their next plaything.

      - Seriously, stop pissing off the pattern spiders, they gave your address to some fairfolk/demons/ghosts that were looking for a good time, and more will come until you do X.

      - I warned you about the pattern spiders, bro. I warned you dawg: Instead of giving you the silent treatment, they're giving you an angry rant. Sometimes stairs lead to the opposite of their destination, doors open to the wrong room, even your own pockets are sometimes those of someone else. Whenever you roll for an action, roll a d10. If the resulting face is higher than your amount of successes on that action, the environment changes such that the opposite happens. For unrolled actions, treat this number as 5. This doesn't apply to combat. They're mad, but they're not that mad. Yet.

          Paradox of course comes from OWoD Mage and was the "punishment" levied by the unconscious human gestalt's consensual reality on mages who performed vulgar magic - i.e., blatant magical effects that could not be explained away as coincidence or some super-exertion of mundane forces. Reality was stretched like a rubber band for the magic effect to occur, then snaps back into your eye as the cosmos say "wait, no, humanity as a whole rejects the notion that what just happened, can happen at all, so you get a haymaker for that little display."

          It's a cool word that, like many things way back in 1e, got shoehorned into the Exalted gameline to play up ties to OWoD (and thereby sell product). It doesn't remotely fit with what the Limit mechanics want to do (express tragic hubris flaws), and consensual reality in Exalted either doesn't exist, or is somehow cool with turning into a gazellecarp mid-battle but not with subtly altering someone's fate in the background???

          I'm not sure how to fix it, and I'm not really sure each splat really NEEDS a separate Limit Break result, even if the Solar breaks don't seem to "fit" Sids very well.

              So, still been brainstorming on this.

              In spy stories, the 'sudden complicating factors' element that Limit Breaks serve seems to generally be something that happens to or around the protagonist, rather than something that they directly do. Of course, it frequently turns out that the protagonist *was* responsible for the sudden complicating factors- typically by exercising discretion in an untoward fashion.

              So, theory: Pull inspiration from the 2e Abyssals 'venting' resonance and 1e Sidereals 'What happens when Fate breaks' tables.

              Every Sidereal has some special destiny that they are responsible for- akin to Solar Limit Breaks. Sidereals never unknowingly violate this destiny, but may chose to do so for whatever reason (love, mercy, rage, etc). They also accrue Paradox from acting against their intimacies, as well as for some of their bigger Charms.

              If your Paradox gets too high, it bursts into weird fate disjoints around you. These can manifest externally (people drowning on dry land, politicians making speeches without being present, otherworldly incursions) or internally (typically as some sort of madness, as the Sidereal sees the branched paths and goes down the wrong one).

              You can reduce Paradox by spending time quietly in Creation, with your Acquintances. By laying low and not using charms, but also interacting in a low-key fashion, you give the Tapestry time to heal. You can also reduce Paradox by reinforcing the integrity of Creation or supporting your special destiny.

              Thoughts?

                          How's this look?
                          THE GREAT CURSE

                          Sidereals walk in the spaces between what was, what is, and what shall be- and it is not an easy walk. More than anyone else, the Chosen of the Maidens know how fragile the veil of reality can be, and how quickly it can be torn asunder. When the weave of Fate becomes tangled, strangeness begins to haunt the world and madness plagues her people. Where the weave is thin, the enemies of Heaven marshall their forces, awaiting a fatal tear through which they can march against Creation.
                          And there is always the temptation- to punish the arrogant with eternal voyages, or trap someone in a home they would do anything to leave; to enjoy the attentions of a certain paramour, or see a rival's love turn sour; to see your people victorious in battle, or a blood feud settled on your terms; to learn forbidden secrets, or conceal darkest shames; to extend the life of a loved one, or cut the thread of an enemy short. To take Fate into one's own hands. Sidereals are invested with a great deal of discretion when it comes to such matters- as the Chosen of the Maidens of Destiny, it is assumed that they act in the interests of Heaven. However, as Rakan Thulio illustrated so long ago, such trust can be misplaced.
                          Unlike Solars, Sidereals do not accrue Limit. Rather, they cause Paradoxes- contradictions between crossed weaves on the Loom of Fate. Sidereals gain Paradox in the following ways;
                          CAUSING PARADOX
                          • Once per scene, when a character acts in a way that opposes a Defining Intimacy, roll a single die, gaining one point of Paradox for each success.
                          • Once per scene, when defying the known will of Heaven, roll a single die, gaining one point of Paradox for each success.
                          • Overuse of certain charms or powerful Astrological Designs can create Paradox.
                          • Each Sidereal guards and shepherds a special destiny, known as their Kismet (or sometimes, their Doom). One's Kismet can change over time, although they should remain thematically similar. Once per scene, when a Sidereal fails to uphold their Kismet, roll three dice, gaining a point of Paradox per success.
                          • Once per story, a Sidereal can renounce their Kismet. This costs 1xp; roll 5 dice against a TN of 4, and gain a point of Paradox per success. Until the next story, however, you do not accrue Paradox from acting against Kismet.
                          Examples of Kismet:
                          • Ensure the prosperity of the Guild.
                          • Bring together two children of rival houses, both alike in dignity.
                          • Mentor a stripling into being a hero- or a villain.
                          • Ensure that the lost heir of Lookshy remains lost, until their time has come to be discovered.
                          • Bring the end of Gem.
                          • (For more possible ideas for Kismet, consider the Major and Mythic Fates in the Storytelling Chapter of Exalted: The Sidereals, from first edition)
                          If a Sidereal reaches 10 Paradox, a Backlash occurs. The web of destiny has grown too taut; lines fray and snap, thrashing unexpectedly and causing wild changes within Creation. Broadly speaking, these Backlashes can take several forms.
                          BACKLASH

                          Causality Violation
                          1. Relatively minor violations of causality occur around you. Water flows backwards, flags flap in the wrong direction, milk curdles, candles burn in the color of your Maiden, flowers bloom or wilt out of season.
                          2. Severe but limited violations of causality occur around you. A contact you needed drowns on dry land. An acquaintance knowingly betrays you into the hands of your enemies.
                          3. Moderate violations of causality occur in a wide area around you. A drunken riot burns down a neighborhood, as the wine barrels refuse to empty.
                          4. Severe violations of causality occur in a very wide area around you. A prince's kingdom falls into civil war after his assassination, even though he is still alive.
                          5. Very severe violations of causality occur, potentially impacting the region forever. A volcano erupts from a mossy hill; a cyclone flattens a city from clear skies; a spell for clear weather suddenly creates unending drought.
                          Incursion
                          1. A ghost, hobgoblin or unbound first circle demon enters Creation. Although they will not directly seek you out, their presence is likely to complicate your life.
                          2. As above, but the entity leaves behind a rift, allowing communication and possession, but not transportation forms between Creation and some other realm.
                          3. A larger incursion of up to half a dozen such spirits. Alternatively, a single more powerful entity may come through, such as a Fair Folk noble, a powerful nephwreck or a second circle demon.
                          4. Breakthrough; a region of Creation is lost to the Bordermarches, breaks into a Shadowland, or becomes a path to the Demon Realm. Malign influence is certain to seep in from such a thing.
                          5. Invasion; a region of Creation is sundered and claimed by the enemy- it falls to the Middlemarches of the Wyld and a court of Fair Folk nobility, or becomes a Shadowland centered on a powerful and malicious ghostly force, or a Second Circle Demon is waiting to march upon Creation with dozens of lessers at their call.
                          Madness
                          1. For the next day, you are plauged by haunting apparitions out of the corners of your eyes, the shadows of pasts that never were and futures that will not be. You cannot spend Willpower during this time.
                          2. For the next day, you are haunted by the ghosts of your regrets. Spectral accusers shout your failings at you; your hands may drip with blood, or your reflection twist and mock you. You cannot spend or gain Willpower during this time. Alternatively, you may suffer from the first level for a week.
                          3. For the next day, you become prone to fits of inappropriate emotion- breaking suddenly into tears over dinner, laughing hysterically during a funeral, or falling asleep in a fight. Alternatively, instead of inappropriate emotions, fate may simply conspire against you, resulting in disastrous failure of what should be the easiest of tasks- most often when trying to warn someone of the consequences of their actions. Once per day, the Storyteller may opt to force one of your rolls to be a botch. They must declare this before you roll. Alternatively, you may suffer the first level for a month, or the second level for a week.
                          4. For the next day, you adopt one of your Resplendent Destiny personas. You are unable to act against your persona's intimacies, and believe the persona to be your true self. During this time, you are also unable to adopt a Resplendent Destiny; your fate is too fragmented to weave another atop it. Alternatively, you may suffer the first level for a season, the second for a month, or the third for a week.
                          5. You suffer one of the previous levels for (respectively) a week, a month, a season or a year.
                          Doom

                          You gain a geis; a taboo of obligation or prohibition. Upon violation of this geis, your Doom falls, inflicting the specified amount of damage upon you. Until that time, it lurks, waiting and hungry. You and your Storyteller should work together to create an appropriate geis for you to labor under. This damage can also be safely dealt with; whenever you could remove a point of Paradox, you may instead remove a die of damage from your Doom. Once the damage is dealt, or if you reduce the damage to nothing, the geis is ended.

                          Damage from Doom cannot be reduced by charms or armor. Fate will not be denied.
                          1. You will suffer 3 dice of bashing damage.
                          2. You will suffer either 6 dice of bashing damage, or 3 dice of lethal damage.
                          3. You will suffer either 10 dice of bashing damage, or 6 dice of lethal damage.
                          4. You will suffer 10 dice of lethal damage.
                          5. You will suffer 15 dice of lethal damage.
                          When a Backlash occurs, roll the Sidereal's accumulated Paradox (maximum of 10). Each success is a Backlash Point that is spent by the Storyteller on whatever effects they feel best fit the cause of the Paradox.
                          For obvious reasons, Sidereals prefer not to allow a Backlash to occur.
                          Fortunately, Sidereals can reduce their accumulated Paradox more safely over time.
                          RESOLVING PARADOX
                          • Once a season, one point of Paradox fades away.
                          • Once a year, by spending a week in Creation gently tending fate without the use of essence, you allow the weave time to heal naturally; roll your Acquaintances and subtract one Paradox for each success. Many Sidereals save their vacation time for such trips.
                          • Finally, a Sidereal may intentionally generate a Backlash when their Paradox is still manageable. Reduce your Paradox by your Essence, then roll the lost Paradox as a Backlash. You may only intentionally create a paradox once a season.
                          • Certain charms allow a Sidereal to reduce their Paradox, although typically at some other cost.

                                                  I gotta agree with Unsung Hero. Just suffering damage is one of the effects of Pattern Bite that was the least interesting, same for catatonia. It's not really fun to just be taken out of the game.

                                                  Something that creates plot hooks or gets players to act in interesting ways usually works better.

                                                  Some suggestions for madnesses:
                                                  - The characters intimacies are reversed for a [day/week?], and the player must act accordingly.

                                                  - The sidereal gains a fixation they must adhere to, like speaking only in rhymes or needing to mention the subject of their kismet in every sentence (and rolling a die of paradox if they fail to do so).

                                                  - Once per scene for one session, the Sidereal gets either nightmarish or ecstatic, but very vivid and potent, hallucinations relating to their kismet, which the Sidereal is convinced are prophetic (but may not be).

                                                  - For a day/week the Sidereal dissociates from their identity, becoming convinced that they are someone else entirely, often using Resplendent Destinies to this effect. Attempting to reconcile them with their "true" self causes distress and tangles their fate further, either draining one WP or adding a die of Paradox. Repeated attempts may trigger the Sidereal to withdraw further into their new personality as the paradox reactivates. Once the effect passes, they remember both selves as being simultaneously true.
