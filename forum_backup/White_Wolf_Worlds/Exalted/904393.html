    How many Solar tombs are there?

    05-31-2016, 04:31 PM
    I am not looking for an exact number or even an official estimate. This topic is more about... well, maybe I should explain it differently.

    We know there exist Solar tombs, wherein previous incarnations are buried along with their panoplies of weapons and armor and stuff. We know they're usually trapped and guarded to hell. It feels like it's been stated both ways that 1) the Dragon-Blooded created them to house the slain Solars after the Usurpation to sedate their hungry ghosts, and 2) the Solars made them to supplement their vanity.

    So when Solar Joe created a Tomb for himself and his future incarnations and then died, Solar Jim (his next incarnation) goes into the Tomb and gets his stuff. What happens when Solar Jim dies? Does he get put in Joe's Tomb? Or does Joe get his OWN Tomb? Where does Solar James go (Joe's 3rd incarnation) to get his cool stuff? Where does James get buried?

    I guess my question is, are there roughly 300 or so Tombs out there, likely built after the Usurpation, or are there probably hundreds or even thousands from many of the Solars that have ever been? I doubt there's one every few hundred square miles of Creation or anything, but I'm curious how people view this issue. Again, I'm not expecting an canon answer, just a discussion of possibility/likelihood.

          I remember musing in the backstory to my Dawn that the first incarnation to hold that Exaltation mantle foresaw this problem, and didn't build herself a crypt. She built a mausoleum. The rules were simple. Her successors could, if they get through the front door (which still requires evading a few simple traps and fighting a guardian automaton. You should see the path that trespassers are dumped into) could take any weapon or armor they wanted from the mausoleum. Swords, spears, bows, smashfists, plate, silken armor, it was an entire armory.

          They were of course limited to how many they could take by how well they got through the front door (ST fiat, to keep your players from going Battle Hydra, usually one weapon and one piece of armor) but they were still able to pick something.

          Each artifact within the mausoleum was also enchanted so that when the current holder died, all of their gear from the mausoleum was pulled back to its resting spot within, waiting for the next Exaltation holder.

          This proved very useful, as that particular Dawn's next incarnation quickly became the Circle's Kenny, never lasting more than a decade or so before dying to something brutal. It got to a point where this exchange happened.

          Zenith: Okay, Dawn-

          Dawn: My name is White Tiger.

          Zenith: Yeah, and the one before that was Weeping Lotus, and the one before that was, um...

          Twilight: That was Bull, wasn't it? Bull, Auroch, something bovine.

          Zenith: Right, Something Bull. Point is, your predecessors didn't last long. Make it five years and I might learn your name.

                      Setting answer "One always exists where the story demands one, and never anywhere else."

                      More helpful answer:

                      You are correct there are two primary sources of Solar tombs. However, in Ex3, the length, nature, and culture of the First Age are undefined. I think it's safe to say that most of the Solars slain in the Usurpation got a tomb of one kind or another. The Dragon-Bloods built them for a good reason, and they had the time to be thorough. So, we have nearly 300 tombs, there.

                      Beyond that, I really think you have to consider how common you want them in your Creation and work from there. There are simply too many variables to decide otherwise. If the First Age was ten thousand years long, that's many Solar incarnations, each with a chance at their own tomb. If it was only a thousand years long, then that's far fewer tombs. Additionally, some tombs may be completely lost (destroyed by a cataclysm, lost to the Wyld, etc). And, of course, culturally it's possible not every Solar warranted a great tomb (not every Pharaoh was powerful enough to get a pyramid).

                      So, after you control for how many Solars had tombs, and how many of those were lost, you're basically back to playing out a Fermi problem with First Age variables, tossing out a number that feels right, or just not numbering them, and putting them where necessary.
