    Exalted Written Campaign

    10-15-2020, 01:12 PM
    I’ve been thinking about writing an entire Exalted campaign, although maybe a short one, like something that takes 10+ sessions to get through. I’m not quite sure on the best way to do it. I’ll start with some hurdles that I don’t think I need help with.

    I don’t think I need help with the Exalted problem of players just having too much power and wrecking any campaign you might have. Sure they have a lot of power, but you can make campaigns for high level DnD characters that can teleport across the universe, decimate armies with a single spell, divine impossible to know information, and just straight up wish for things to happen and then they do. It still works you just need to take that into account. Sure the players could just right angle and leave the whole thing, but that’s true of any kind of published adventure in any game. I’ll find a way to deal with it.

    Where I need some help is how do I present different varied difficult tasks, and looking for suggestions on what people might want to see it be about.

    For example I know with the way characters can vastly differ in optimization that many of the scenes are going to have to have basically a difficulty slider. How would it be best to present that? I’d like to keep one setting as a kind of default to make reading it flow easier, but what setting is the default, hard or easy? I don’t want people thinking their bad at the game for having to turn on easy mode, but I also don’t want STs to feel like they’re being unfair turning on hard mode.

    Another thing is how wide should that gulf be? Should I even attempt to write for a group of hyper-optimized characters? Or should I just ditch that and write for more what the system expects? Maybe I should include a sample circle or guidelines for what the campaign expects?

    Also taking suggestions for what it’s about. Adventure on the high seas squaring off against an undead captain/fleet. Deep investigators solving crimes in the big city? Tomb raiders exploring a series of ancient ruins? Battling cults and the forces of Hell in the frozen northern wastes?

      Maybe write with the default expectation of an Exalt having 7-10 dots and 2-3 Charms? That's a fair estimation of a secondary specialist or a low-op build, to which you could add modifications for "what if this is someone's Supernal?" and "what if no-one really invested in this?"

      I feel like the more important part will be in how you structure the challenges, rather than the difficulty of any one scene. If the circle is going to have to fight off a legion of hungry ghosts, descend a thousand-mile deep cave, and persuade a god of death to leave their village alone, it won't matter so much if one or two challenges get trivialized--something else will wind up being an obstacle.

                                  I honestly think you're asking for something impossible. Anything beyond "here are some principal characters, their goals, flaws, and abilities, Enter Sandbox" is doomed to failure.

                                  Think of a game with really good quests, like New Vegas or Witcher 3, and think about all the work put in to making different options available to you. In New Vegas you could conceivably have a skill rank 100 in Stealth, Lockpicking, Science (Hacking), Repair, or Medicine, and a Perk on top of that, which means if that option exists than you find it and succeed at your strong suit. The problem with Exalted is that you can reasonably expect a player be the best person in the world at their strong suit. Combat aside, I've never felt challenged in my specialty in Exalted. You're just too damn good unless its a head to head with other Exalted designed to oppose you. In Witcher 3 you don't really have any skills besides Use Sword On Monster and its twin sister Use Sword On Man with their two cousins Highlight Plot Point and Jedi Mind Trick, so in that game all quests are really decided by characterization and choice, deciding who lives and dies, and dealing with the consequences of that decision. That kind of quest lives and dies in the ST's ability to roleplay NPCs and the player's level of engagement. Its probably the easiest thing to fuck up.

                                  So if you want to design a ten session adventure, you basically have to give me a broad strokes only coloring book picture and let me paint in everything else. Solar Exalted all have the same power, and that's access to the Supernal Ability called Breaking The Game, and there's just no way to account for that. You'd have better luck giving us a handful of scenarios and colorful NPCs and letting the ST change things to fit the table. DnD adventures work because player abilities are tightly controlled and even then, players still screw everything up. Just google a reddit thread about Waterdeep Dragon Heist to see what I mean.

                                  Please be warned: this is not champagne, this is most likely a duck.

                                      Respectfully I disagree. I don't think any adventure can claim to be immune to players breaking it in one way or another, in any system. I've done Dragonheist, Dungeon of the Mad Mage, Icewind Dale and a few more and they've all gone absolutely butter smooth for me. Dragonheist isn't even that well written, there's no heist!

                                      Either way, there's strategies to help control the narrative flow. Having events take place in a sequence, events that take place when the players aren't there, controlling information that the players have, etc. For example, let me lay one out


                                      Session 1
                                      The players are in a satrapy and at a big social function they learn, one way or another, that there may be a rash of missing children cases showing up in the city. An investigation reveals it's not just children, although the adults going missing are mostly people that society ignores. Furthermore some information leads them to believe the missing people are being taken to a location a day's travel out of town. Something more is off though, the last people they got that info from were definitely more badass than your typical mortal kidnappers.

                                      Session 2
                                      The location however is again suspiciously difficult to find, clearly being hidden by magic, and the surrounding area has animals or animalistic spirits far more dangerous than one would expect of that area. Either somebody did that intentionally, or something else has them riled up. The location they find though is just a temporary stopover, to another place where they are "processed" The final person here is also supernaturally gifted, maybe an Exalt of some kind, and attempts to convince the players that what he's doing is not wrong.

                                      Session 3
                                      This place is nearby, again maybe a day's travel. This time though, the place isn't just in an area with riled up unnaturally dangerous shit, it's clearly wrong in some very serious ways. Just the trek to get there leads through a tangle of venomous barbed plants, crawling with legendarily massive predatory insects. Inside it's even even stranger, and the first evidence of what's truly happening here appears as creatures from the Wyld are among the defenders. They rescue the most recent captures, but find that the rest have been long gone to their lord to be dream eaten or worse.

                                      Session 4
                                      The group is large, dozens of people at least, and leaving them to return home on their own through the Wyld tainted forest is a death sentence, they must be escorted back or die. The trip however, reveals just how deep this corruption goes, encountering many dangers that are made all the more difficult because of the people the heroes are trying to protect. The last of these dangers is a Raksha hunting party. That party comes with the information that this is no mere storm of chaos blowing in from Creation's edge, but a crusade. An ancient fey with a grudge against this area of Creation has come to claim it and fill it with chaos. He's already retaken the old capital and is using it as his headquarters.

                                      Session 5
                                      Either through the player's lore knowledge or possibly allies in the city they find that the location of this old capital have been expunged from the records, even Exalted scholars who've tried to search for them have been unable to find even the records of where to start looking. Likely unbeknownst to the players this expunging of info was carried out by Sidereal Exalted, masters of the craft. There are very few people who even know where to look for the information, and they hold their secrets tightly.

                                      Also the surrounding countryside is being ravaged by the Fey. All over for a hundred miles it seems people are being taken and killed, and monsters roam freely.

                                      Session 6
                                      The people, or maybe even person, who knows where the info might be is an exalted socialite who's holding a grand party, it might be the end of the world after all so let's get it on. The information simply doesn't exist anywhere except inside her head, and they have to get there and convince her to give it up. Of course this isn't as easy as just walking in the front door and asking her, for various reasons.

                                      Session 7
                                      The players are successful, they find that information, and indeed, do find the information. The city was sent from its location in the valley, deep below a mountain by some incredible magic (Neighborhood Relocation) and it's from there that this fairy king commands attacks on the land. He's the only one that can stop them, or at least his death will kill his crusade's momentum. Also he has likely taken this ancient city and warped it into a truly truly epic fortress. Not only equipped with ancient defense mechanisms, but the structures have been warped by chaos and filled with terrors. Still the heroes succeed and kill him. Unfortunately he has one last spiteful stab of fury, captain Ahab style.

                                      Session 8
                                      As the Wyld Storm begins to break, its final stages are the most dangerous, and the city is besieged by nightmares. The land heaves, buildings walk, and through it all the heroes make a last desperate stand to mitigate the untold damage and prevent the city form being torn apart. After which the storm subsides, and if they're anathema, they now have to deal with revealing that to a devoutly immaculate population if at any point in this adventure they displayed their power.


                                      It's only 8 sessions because I want to leave room for players to roleplay more, or do their own side things if they feel they have the time. Also I came up with it in like 30 mintues.



                                      That sounds like it could be a pretty good outline, and I'm struggling to think of a Supernal that turns that into a complete joke, especially if you really vary up the stuff and keep the pressure high. There's no Essence 5 Lore or Investigation charm that just lets you instantly know that this missing persons case is actually connected to a massive fairy crusade, and also where the King is, so you can go Dawn him in the face in one session.

                                      I can definitely see that this chain of events might go off the rails if the whole party is just like "Fuck missing people, mortals are worthless, let's get out of this place" but you really need to expect at least a certain amount of player buy-in.
