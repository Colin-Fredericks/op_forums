    Originally posted by ZX_XZ View Post

    Well, its certainly not constant running around. You are allowed to have several weeks sabbatical per year depending on your Salary rating. A large portion of the drudgery can be passed off on to Secretarial gods. It's a common mistake I see among STs who aren't that into Sidereals is following the meme of "Constant work, no sleep, Creation is constantly falling apart at the seams". A huge amount of Sidereal work is showing up at the right place at the right time, saying something and leaving because they know what will happen if they change something small. Playing as a Sidereal should be fun, you should be able to relish in the fact that you are a mighty being who knows all, see's all and can plan all, shaping Creation for the better (and to your personal goals) from the shadows.
    IMO one of the best ways to play Sidereals is a bit like playing Shadowrun. You get a mission of a goal that must be accomplished, and then are dispatched into Creation to fulfill said goal without accidentally breaking the masquerade. Along the way you'll have problems because of personal goals conflicting with the mission, jobs from your Convention or Faction that are counterproductive to said mission and missions that are counterproductive to yourselves. And its you and your circle's jobs to navigate this tangled web to make the world a better place. Then you have sessions set in Yu-Shan which will be more conflict with the system and other Sidereals, where your goals will clash with others as you try to lobby for your Convention at a planning committee, expose the corruption of your superior, or deal with the dangerous crime rings that lurk in the slums of the Heavenly city.
    Also a note, if you have friends that you cannot bear have forget you, there are several charms that let them remember you in several trees. Of course most of them have side effects or limited numbers of uses but its always an option. You are a Sidereal, fate bends at your whim; the last thing you are is helpless in the face of your goals
    Now I'm starting to like them. Vacations, yay! Passing off crap jobs to your underlings! Lobbying and politicking! Charms that let people remember you!

    I really dig your description. I hope Sids keep being this awesome in this edition (actually I'm fairly trustful of the devs, so I see no reason why they shouldn't. The War in Heaven narrative should even enhance the whole thing, IMVHO).

        I'd note, even considering sabbaticals, if you've got a Sidereal who is ultimately spending the majority of their time in Yu-Shan, the difference between Arcane Fate and being mundanely forgotten is mostly a matter of a bit of mystical veneer that reinforces the character of Sidereal Exalted.

        ​That comic in Manual: Sidereals? Where a bunch of washer women struggle to remember details about some hero who saved them a few years back? That's the kind of thing that would happen no matter what they were (assuming that nobody made a practice of repeating a description of them once a week or accurately painted them or something). Much the same for that shopkeeper who struggles to remember May Blossom; I would say that it's not uncommon for people who have casual sex encounters to have a hard time remembering the details a year or so later (something that I say with no stigma or judgment whatsoever).

        ​Or with parents; assuming that the Sidereal is somebody who, having been given access to the whole of the world still wishes to periodically visit their parents (while likely drawing from cultural inputs that don't really have things like telecommunications to condition the idea that maintaining a lifelong relationship with them when you don't live within walking distance is the norm), there are means and a bit of magic to allow that, and even then it's only a concern for a very small part of the Exalt's life. In most cases though, I would say that it's not too much different from, say, being an early European colonist to the Americas (or from one end of America to the other, during western expansion) of modest means, in which the departure means that neither shall see or communicate with one another for the rest of their lives. The difference of actually forgetting that you existed might be a bit distressing, but if you were never going to see them again anyway, the practical difference to your life is kind of minimal.

        ​The main thing about Arcane Fate is not what it does to the individual (as viewed through a lens of modern circumstances and relationships), but what it's done to widespread awareness of the Sidereal Exalted. If a Dynast saves a village, odds are that the villagers will forget everything about them except that they were a Dynast in a few years, and some generations after even that detail (and that any saving was done) might be lost, and if you introduce yourself as a Dynast or Dragon Blooded many people are likely to get what you are. Sidereals just get an additional effect that means that they happen to not get even that (and by the institutions through which the Sidereal Exalted exert control over the world, that's kind of how they like it anyway).

                        Hmmm, I don't think I quite agree. I mean, yes, if you're working far away, it may not make much practical difference if your parents don't remember you, but I think it makes a big emotional difference. It'd be pretty distressing.

                        I think there's a lot of difference of course, depending on when the Sidereal was found. The Sidereals prefer to find new Sidereals as babies, and spirit them away to Yu-Shan. So for most Sidereals, it won't really be such a big deal: they'll grow up in Yu-Shan anway, raised with other young pre-Sidereals, by some nanny-gods (which also has the effect of reinforcing group loyalty).

                        But for others, they'll grow up in their village, with their family, perhaps even get engaged or married... and then suddenly, one day, they exalt, and their own family and friends start to forget them. That's very distressing.
                        And Sidereals growing up with their families is supposed to be more common now in the Time of Tumult (though I don't think that quite makes sense, since it's only lasted a few years, so all the Sidereals who haven't been found are presumably babies anyway).


                        And I think it has a significant impact on the Sidereals, as it creates a distance between them and the world: their close friends are, by necessity, other Sidereals and gods. Yes, with effort, they can cultivate small relationships with normal humans, but it's very difficult for them to create anything deep*. So this has the effect of institutionalising them (plus, of course, the fact that they live in Yu-Shan anyway, that only other Sidereals really understand them, that it's a hassle to go to Creation for an evening anyway, that Yu-Shan is generally nicer than Creation, etc, etc), distancing them from the lives of ordinary people. Which makes it easier to see people as just their job, ensuring fate applies, making hard decisions.
                        Which seems appropriate for Sidereals, genre-wise.

                        *I suspect some Sidereals create an Arcane Destiny of a pretty normal person, and use this to make relationships with ordinary humans, but those don't last years.

                                            Just to provide some elaboration:

                                            Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                                            The Sidereals prefer to find new Sidereals as babies, and spirit them away to Yu-Shan. So for most Sidereals, it won't really be such a big deal: they'll grow up in Yu-Shan anway, raised with other young pre-Sidereals, by some nanny-gods (which also has the effect of reinforcing group loyalty).
                                            ​That was in Second Edition. In First Edition, the Sidereals only kept a distant watch, trying to shape the circumstances around the child to ensure that they would be prepared for the moment when their Essence would awaken, and intervening only in cases where the prospective Sidereal was abandoned or threatened with murder, not uncommon reactions to them owing to their uncanny fated nature. Even then, Sidereals only relocated the children to other, better suited families, since they lacked the time to raise children themselves (and it was probably not considered conductive to their development).
