            Originally posted by Cobra View Post
            If I use Tiger Warrior Training Technique and pay the xp cost, and then at some point there is a nasty fight that eliminates them, do I get the xp back like I would if my sorcerous working was destroyed? Does this also apply to the various pet charms and other charms with XP investment?
            There aren't any explicit endorsements in the Core book about the "sanctity of merits", so, rules-as-written, no, you wouldn't get the XP back. However, I think a general principle of "if something you paid XP for is lost, you get the XP back" is a good one to follow, so if I was running the game, I'd refund the xp spent on such warriors. Note that in addition to the refund on sorcerous workings, Solar Survival does specify that xp spent on training familiars and such are refunded if the pet is lost - see the the "A Familiar Investment" box, p. 412.

            Originally posted by Cobra
            If I use Power Awarding Prana to get a charm that is one essence higher than my current rating, do I also get the benefits of additional effects listed on those charms for higher essence ratings? I'll be Essence 2 in a few sessions and Tiger Warrior Training Technique has one of these effects that starts at Essence 3.
            I'm honestly a bit torn on this one - on the one hand, the general principle is that effects that just have Essence as a prerequisite are available early if you get access to them before that (taking War as your Supernal ability, for example), whereas effects that use Essence to set a power level (a hypothetical charm that let you train up to (Essence) Size of group at once, for instance) uses your current essence. So, going by that, Power-Awarding Prana should give access to the Essence 3+ option for Tiger-Warrior Training. On the other hand, granting access to charms before you meet the Essence prereq is already a fairly potent benefit. And if you limit it to treating the charm as only a single Essence level higher, you then have to keep a note on the charm that that's what happened permanently, which is annoying bookeeping, as far as I'm concerned.

            I think, after considering these issues, that I'd rule that while Power-Awarding Prana can give you access to a charm's base effects one Essence level early, you're still assumed to be your regular Essence level for most other purposes, including "free" upgrades. However, if there was a repurchase option available, I'd allow Power-Awarding Prana to give you that one Essence level early too, since that doesn't require tracking how the charm was learned.

            Originally posted by Cobra
            I've seen people online list Fate-Shifting Solar Arete as a way to buff Sorcerous Workings (this is a common thread in top google search results). The charm says it can't be used on extended rolls, and a Sorcerous Working is explicitly an extended roll. Am I missing something, or are these online folks mistaken?
            The rules seem pretty clear to me - Fate-Shifting Solar Arete doesn't work on extended rolls, workings are extended rolls, so no, it shouldn't work. That said, I could see allowing FSSA to work once on any given extended action, representing a lucky break or other fortunate coincidence. But allowing it on more than one roll in the extended action would probably be unbalanced, especially for extended actions with longer intervals like workings - it's really too easy for such actions to end up benefiting from FSSA on every roll, otherwise, since FSSA lets you use it once a day.

            Originally posted by Cobra
            I can't find rules for how long it takes to train or develop units or groups more generally, nor how long it might take to train up Drill specifically. 1) Is TWTT just an expedited training process to increase Drill more quickly in this regard? 2) Does it also magically enable the new profiles listed in the book for Average/Elite soldiers that wouldn't otherwise get those new profiles without additional investment of resources or training outside of Drill, or is that just a function of how non-expedited drilling would work?
            Such rules don't exist, as far as I know, so no surprise you can't find them. :-)

            I don't think TWTT is supposed to be giving warriors traits that it's impossible to get otherwise (it doesn't make sense, for one thing, to assume that all average or elite soldiers in Creation are the result of training charms), but I do think it's greatly accelerating the process, and combining the process of training soldiers in both basic fighting skills and working together effectively. If you want some suggestions for how to handle training without charms, I'd suggest the following:

            A completely untrained individual usually starts with only 2 dice in their attack and combat movement pools, and 1 in defense. A collection of such people will be Poor Drill. Improving an attack or Combat Movement pool requires one week of regular (though not constant) training for the first die, two weeks for the second, three for the third, and so on. A skilled commander (someone with more War or Lore than the number of dice they're trying to add), or constant training (at least 10 hours a day, and the trainees won't have the energy for anything else) reduces the time by 30%; having both reduces it by 50%. Once you've given a group of people at least one die in attack and combat movement pools, you can improve their Drill from Poor to Average with a month of regular training (someone involved absolutely has to have War 3, or War 1 and a relevant specialty, though they don't have to be formally in charge of training). Again, an experienced trainer (War at least equal to the Size of the group being trained) or constant training can reduce the time required, by 30% for one, or 50% for both. Training from Average drill to Elite requires at least two seasons of training (again, reduced for constant training and skilled trainer), but even once they put in the training time, they can't upgrade to Elite until they've seen at least one actual battle - at the end of such a battle, make a rout check (using all the modifiers accrued by the battle). If it succeeds, the unit gains Elite drill (there's no negative consequences on a failure, besides the failure to achieve the drill).

            If a unit is made up of at least 50% soldiers who came from other units with better than Poor Drill, they can train faster (halve the times above), and if they were from an Elite Drill unit, training to Elite level doesn't require fighting a battle, just the training time.


            Originally posted by Cobra
            If I use Legendary Scholar's Curriculum to train groups, can the training times overlap with TWTT or other training charms? TWTT can last a month and LSC could potentially be activated multiple times on the same group within that time.
            I'd say yes, in general - the Solar training charms generally don't seem to require more time spent training than regular training does. However, I'd impose a limit of no more than two simultaneous training charms on a group at once, because that would basically use up all their waking hours.

            Originally posted by Cobra
            Do all non-solars train at the same rate for the purposes of training times? Is this also true for non-exalted? Non-magical beings? (I'm asking for the purposes of what time it would take for these groups to train others as well as be trained).
            The training charms in other sourcebooks (Dragon-Blooded and the Lunars manuscript) imply that by default, everybody including mortals uses the training times provided in the core book. Remember, though, that those training times do assume regular training - you're either doing stuff that counts as training, or probably spending about 4 hours a day training. That sort of commitment to learning requires some dedication that a lot of people don't necessarily have. Doing so probably counts as an inconvenient task, requiring at least a Minor intimacy to convince them to do it, while constant, heavy, all-day training is at least a serious task, needing a Major intimacy to talk them into it.

            Originally posted by Cobra
            When a battle group deals an amount of damage to someone's initiative that is greater than their current initiative value, does any damage that would reduce their initiative total below zero get applied to their health track as part of that attack, or do they continue to head into negative initiative and then have all future attacks applied to their health track?
            The current devs have clarified that, by a strict read of the rules, it's the latter - the attack that takes you from positive to negative init just pushes you negative, and its only subsequent attacks that directly hit your HL track. I get the feeling they might prefer the other option, but personally, I think the RAW is actually the better approach - otherwise, being at low init around a battle group is just a bit too lethal for PCs. If I reset to base init next to a battle group, for instance, it does not take an incredibly good roll on their part (at least with things like command actions boosting their dice pools) to knock me down into crashed and then just eat all my health levels all at once. By having the first hit just take you into negatives, and only if you start there having it do HL damage, it means there's a bit of a buffer period where you can get out of range, recover, or do something to cope.

            Originally posted by Cobra
            1) Putting this incredible investment into a mundane item to make it very slightly better seems to put a lot of focus on making an outstanding mortal very special, but the benefits are very marginal, so am I missing something that would make this more meaningful/powerful?
            Well, for one thing, it doesn't have to be a mortal who benefits from it - in fact, a mortal probably won't benefit from it at all unless you roll well enough to activate a permanent evocation, since mortals don't have mote pools and thus can't pay the cost of an instant evocation. But not being able to significantly enhance mortals doesn't make this useless, since many Exalted won't have artifact weapons, so giving them an evocation for their mundane weapon or armor is still a nice bonus.

                    The ST decided that since we couldn't find clear RAW, battle groups should be more dangerous because there is a perception within the group that they are generally weak, but if there's clear RAW then we might be able to have the discussion again. Do you know where I can find that?
                    It's how the devs interpret the Core book. Dev rulings can generally be found in one of the 2 ask-a-dev thread, but it can be a pain because there are so many pages. Battlegroups are very swingy - without support they lose badly, but when properly supported by an Exalt they can kill PCs in two turns flat. If you allow them to deal damage on the same turn they crash opponents then they can do it in 1 turn.

                    For the answers I gave before :

                    For Power-Awarding Prana, it's my reading that nothing in the Charm's description indicates that you are 1 Essence higher when using the Charm, you're only 1 Essence higher for the purpose of learning it. So you can activate a repurchase with Power Awarding Prana, but you can't activate an Essence-dependent effect. If that weren't the case, it would lead to degenerate gameplay where you are encouraged to not learn Charms that have improved effects, because they would actually be stronger when you use them through Power-Awarding Prana than when you learn them normally. I really don't think that's the intent of the Charm.

                    For simultaneous training, the "general rule" I referred to is the description of Legendary Scholar's Curriculum, which mentions that even when using the same exp on 2 characters, you're still doing 2 separate trainings in terms of training times. Also it's my opinion that you can't give a lessons in Craft (knitting) to an Exalt and also drill an army in combat tactics literally at the same time (at least not without a great stunt or a clever use of Hidden Wisdom Bestowal). Of course you can advance both trainings in the same day, just not literally at the same time.

                    For training times representing characters who train on the side and being improvable by dedicating time or having a teacher, that's in the Training Times section in the Core.

                    My plan with LSC is to ask my ST to see whether he'll grant a base might of 1 to the group if I do what I described; I should have mentioned that. I guess what the question is more about is why the charm is so hard to get and to use for the fairly minimal impact it has. I feel like I'm missing important/relevant insights into what it does or can do that makes it Awesome.
                    Legend-Spirit Convocation is a Charm made for a Kickstarter backer who wanted to use Wake the Sleeper on non-artifacts. It's where it is simply because it's meant to be an upgrade of Wake the Sleeper. It's not that strong because lore-wise the very idea of giving Evocations to a mundane weapon is an incredible almost lore-breaking feat, so making those enhanced weapons anywhere as good as real Artifacts would simply have gone too far. At least that's my take on it.

                                                        Originally posted by Uknown DarkLord View Post
                                                        How much would someone with Occult 4 know about the Neverborn?

                                                        Personally, I'd rule "very little", or at least "very little about what the Neverborn are". I interpret Occult as practical knowledge of spirits and magic, rather than academic. So someone with lots of Occult could probably tell you that in the Underworld, as you go deeper, it becomes twisted into a terrifying labyrinth populated by maddened ghosts, and that eventually, as you go towards the bottom, you come across vast buildings/landscapes that seem to be the source of the maddening whispers. But they wouldn't be able to say that those are the tomb-bodies of the dead-but-not-gone Primordials. That would be the province of Lore, with an appropriate background.
