    The fall of the First Age is definitely supposed to be a tragedy, but as one gets more hints and flashes of it one might start to suspect that it's more a tragedy in the detached literary sense than in the human toll sense.

    The First Age could be a time in which grand armies, vast beyond measure, equipped with the arsenals of heaven and led by the greatest of heroes clashed against one another in warfare where people could win amazing glories. That's lost now, but it was primarily a thing that people could live and tell amazing stories about; it wasn't necessarily to the good of people.

    I'd say that's what Holden means when he makes a point about how Creation has never had lasting prosperity or central unification, or what John means when he says that there are social problems that have plagued humanity throughout the Ages of their dominance.

    It is of course going too far to say that the First Age became a time of wall to wall atrocity and mind control, but it hurts the thesis of the game to say that, prior to the blooming of the Great Curse, it was a time in which the issues of the human condition were solved.

    To the extent that people had peace, I'd say it should be the peace of Rome, of Britain, of the Mongols; the people in the interior live without fear, but there can still be a lot of violence at the edges. There are societies that view themselves as peaceful because they don't perceive a threat of war that could actually destroy their civilisation, or that impacts upon their everyday life; historically, many such societies have felt very comfortable with bringing war to other people.

    So the Old Realm is liable to have had that.

    This should be primarily subtextual.

    That said, I think that Solars should lack the essential motifs that would be widely seen as marks of villainy (like the usual interpretation of their compelling presence as insidious mind control); that's not to say that Solars are always or even necessarily often "good" people, but at least not necessarily people who would readily come across as... I don't know, something a bit mustache twirling.

    It should generally be plausible that somebody would look on a Solar as a venerable hero, even if it's largely because the Solar is impressive (or, if everybody is looking at them with dread, at least with a mind to that everyone coming from a questionable hierarchy).

    I have approximate knowledge of many things.

        I like to think of the Solar exalted primarily as Chosen by the Sun. TUC is a god, a personification of different concepts attributed to him. Concepts like perfection, greatness, heroism, goodness or light-conquers-dark. TUC isn't human, and I also like to think that he has a very poor understanding of humans, considering his domains. Where humans are nuanced he is the extreme in every case, and his nature forces him not to accept anything that is less than the extreme.

        When TUC chooses his champions, or when he sets the principles for Solar exaltation, he looks for people who he likes. I.e. humans who somehow embodies the same concepts that he does, that strive for perfection, acts heroic or does good, in the same way that he understands these concepts.

        This means that at first most Solars will seem like heroes and champions of all that is good and holy. But just as concepts like perfection and good vs. evil breaks apart in a complex context without a objective scale of measure, so will the Solars stray from what is expected of them. In the early days of the First Age the Solars still had guidance, the Sun still tried to influence their morals and actions. But as TUC can't stand things that conflicts with his concepts, and because of a growing addiction to the Games of Divinity, he turned away and suddenly the Solars had no one who told them what to do. They had no boundaries. And as the Solars grew thousands of years old and gained power beyond the gods, they lost touch with reality and the rest of creations inhabitants. The way the elder Solars acted then became the norm for younger Solars, and that is what I like to think caused the Solars collective madness and decent in the First age, not primarily the Great Curse.

        The Solars of the second age are still chosen by the same standards as those in the first age, they are somehow great and/or good and all in the moment of exaltation. But then they change, as all humans do (which is a strange concept to TUC) and they may become something entirely opposed to what TUC had in mind.

        So, to tie this to the topic, in my head the Solars can be exalted because the are good in a way TUC recognizes. But they could also be exalted just because they strive for perfection, greatness or a heroic legacy. Goodness (the way TUC understands and defines it) is not a requirement for exaltation, but it is one of the things looked at.

                    Yes, the solars are heroes, but not in the modern sense, rather, they are the heroes in the way the Ancient Greeks used them.
                    To the greeks, a hero was someone who was strong, powerful or influential, and who was willing to use his power.

                    The best example of this is probably Achilles. Really goodlooking and an absolute monster on the battlefield, men want to be him and women want to be with him, son of a nymph, almost completely invincible, all-in-all.
