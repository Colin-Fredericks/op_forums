                                      Originally posted by Daredevil View Post
                                      I feel like I am getting the same vibe while reading the earlier material. How do you think they intended that to work in practice?
                                      Well in 1e Heavenly Guardian Defense actually cost a willpower to use. The intent might have been to use PDs only against very dangerous attacks, but without a full understanding of just how easy it was to make an attack incredibly dangerous and maybe how cheap they really were. Dipping Swallow was 2m still, so like, for 1 extra mote instead of ignoring all penalties to your DV you just blocked the attack perfectly.

                                      Originally posted by Chausse View Post
                                      I see these PD being talked about every now and then. I'm not interested in going too deep into 2ed to understand the discussion so what does it do approximately ? You just activate the Charm and are immune to every attack for the round ?
                                      A perfect defense comes in 3 main forms, perfect parry, perfect dodge and perfect soak. The first two effectively set your Defence Value to infinite against one attack, parrying it or dodging it no matter the successes they rolled, the last one negated all damage from the attack but didn't negate other effects like an on-hit knockdown or something. It only works for one instant, so one attack, but they were cheap, like 3m cheap. It was literally cheaper to perfectly defend than it was to raise your defense by 2 with the excellency.

                                      The only down side was that they had to have what was called a "Flaw of Invulnerability". These were essentially meaningless though, in most cases. The Flaw of Invulnerability for Conviction stated that you couldn't use it if the actions were contrary to the characters primary motivation, which is like their single greatest defining intimacy.

                                      It was also really really damn easy to make attacks that were super deadly. There were even perfect attacks that always hit unless blocked by a perfect defense. Which lead to basically everything revolving around perfects. You could beat a perfect by just attacking 5 times in a single tick with charms, they'd have to perfect every attack, but there were also flurry-breaker charms that let you block the attack and then get away instantly to avoid being flurried more.

                                          Originally posted by Chausse View Post
                                          I see these PD being talked about every now and then. I'm not interested in going too deep into 2ed to understand the discussion so what does it do approximately ? You just activate the Charm and are immune to every attack for the round ?
                                          In 2nd ed, Solar Dodge had (Dodge 4, 1 prereqcharm) Seven Shadow Evasion: 3m, dodge an attack without any roll. In 3rd ed, it still exists, but it's 1/fight and costs wp.
                                          So, every time you were attacked, you did a stunt (regenerating 2-4m, occasionally 6) and spent 3m, and... didn't get hit.
                                          There was also a 4m version in Melee.
                                          Notice how if you were good at stunting, you were impossible to hit but didn't actually go down motes.
                                          Thus, the only way to kill Solars was unexpected attacks or making multiple attacks at once.

                                          Other splats mostly had equivalents that were marginally pricier.

                                          It's also important to note that an unarmoured exalt hit with a grand goremaul would probably take about 16 dice of lethal (not Withering, lethal), and the wielder could do that every round.

                                          It was a combat system that was really dangerous unless you had a perfect defence, but really easy if you did. So not very fun.

                                          Admittedly, it tended to be fine when social Dragonblood duelled with their swords, or armoured Dragonblood fought with daiklaives (as long as they didn't use multi-attack charms since they were so slow) but any combat-built Celestial was likely to make a fight boring. At least, that's my experience.

                                          STing Bronze Age Exalted
