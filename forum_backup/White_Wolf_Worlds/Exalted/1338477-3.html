            This has a very simple solution. The core book is explicit about initiative and withering attacks being an abstraction of gaining the upper hand in a fight. If a character tries to make a decisive attack against a different opponent than the one he's been fighting, he immediately resets to base initiative. Gaining advantage against Kingfisher Swift doesn't confer advantage against Harmonious Jade.

                                    Originally posted by Hakkonen View Post
                                    You've got to put Swift out of the fight before you engage Jade; otherwise, Swift has a clear shot at your back.
                                    That kind of does already happen, sort of. If you crash swift and then turn your attention to decisive attack Jade, Swift has a fantastic chance to wreck your shit.

                                    Like let’s say you’re battling Swift, force her down to -3 initiative, which gives you a big initiative break and shoots you up to 22i or something. Then you say “ah good now that I have all this initiative I can smash Jade.” and then you turn around, leap to Jade and decisive, maybe killing her or forcing her to accept a crippling injury. Then you reset to 3i and it moves to Swifts turn.

                                    Well now you’ve turned your back on the person you’ve been battling this whole time, so she smashes into your back and deals 4 withering damage to you, which puts you at -1i. She gets 1i for hitting, 4i for her damage, and 5i for Initiative Break. Which puts her at 7i. Then because Initiative Shift, she gets to roll her Join Battle pool and add that to her initiative, so now she’s at like 11i, then her turn immediately resets for that round, so she attacks you again, withering you for another 5 withering damage. Now you’re at -6i with 2 onslaught penalties and she’s at +17i. Then the round ends and she gets to go again because she’s got the most initiative and she either decisives you with her decent amount of initiative, or takes the opportunity to hammer you further into the dirt in preparation for finishing you off.

                                    There are some times when that might be worth it, like if Jade is about to unleash some devastating attack that will kill you, but you definitely get punished for switching targets between gathering initiative and launching decisive attacks.

                                                        One thing I've been trying to test for a little while but have been having issues with due to how my.plauers have resolved encounters or discussed what they want out of my game is splitting combats.

                                                        For instance, say your Circle of 5 Ecalts engages 2 Exalts; a Dusk, a Daybreak, the Daybreak's undead Rhino Centaur bodyguard, and a group of Zombies.

                                                        Rather then a massive pile of Initiatives, I'd prefer to split up to where, for instance, the Dawn and the Night engage the Dusk, the Zenith fights the Rhino Bodyguard, and the Twilight and Eclipse fight the Daybreak and the Zombies.

                                                        In this idea, each of these would in effect be a miniature fight in itself; you can't 'cross over' fights; the Dawn can't smack the much weaker Daybreak to farm Initiative to kill the Dusk, and similarly the Dusk can't do that to the Eclipse; they have to stay focused on their own opponents or they risk opening themselves up for an attack.

                                                        However, in addition to this would be special Disengage Gambits that use the same rules as Disengaging but rather then leaving the range, they instead 'Tag-Out' without another ally, swapping places with them in their fight, possibly getting a bonus to their next attack for doing so as a 'surprise' action.

                                                        So for instance, the Dusk might call the Zombies to distract the Dawn and get a sneak attack on the Eclipse, or the Twilight might leave the zombies to the Eclipse to go join the Zenith in dealing with the monster.

                                                        As each battle ended you slowly fold the combatants into other fights, or they stay out of the others if they're too exhausted or injured.

                                                        Hopefully I get to try this relatively soon and get some notes on it.
