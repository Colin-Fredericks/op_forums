                                    I always figured the medical treatment worked because, in the clearly-magic setting of Exalted, that IS how diseases are created, and dealt with. If someone gets infected with consumption, it's because they were dominated and abused by those more powerful than them, which for some incomprehensible-to-humans (particularly us-humans, who are more-or-less used to a physical explanation for stuff) reason is kind of like an accidental spell that sets off this reaction in the victim. He responds to ginseng etc not because of an antibiotic effect, but because, for the same reason, that is anathema (if you'll pardon the term) to the workings of the "spell". Cholera is created by criminals bathing in open water, because of their impurity or lack of justice, which causes the essence to warp in such a way to literally harm the god of the body of water, who spreads it on because, well, it's a god of water. Purification rituals don't affect little bacteria in the water, they affect the god, cleansing it of the impurity created by the criminal/goat, which means no more spreading of the disease.

                                    It's Exalted. Trying to tie it to real-world explanations kind of misses the whole point. When I watch fantasy movies, I don't bitch about how the magic violates the conservation of energy, or why the lightening bolt doesn't instantly earth itself after being cast: the wizard throws the lightening bolt with magic. Magic != science.

                                    Our world works, in broad, because physics. Exalted works, in broad, because gods and magic.

                                    But back to the main point. Could you create virusmen/bacteriamen if you combo the right Knacks? Sure. It'd require those entities to exist, but hey, it's your game, and if it makes it more fun than more power to you. Course, it requires like 3 or 4 Knacks, to get the right kind of virus or bacteria for the kind of people you want to make, and frankly has finite use in my eyes (although, given mortal susceptibility to disease, trying to address that angle does seem in line with the Silver Paths goals), but given that it explicitly states that Lunars were given the gift of change and flexibility, changing the assumptions that they only can turn into animals like wolves or squirrels seems right in the theme. Plus, nobody would see a normal, if sickly, looking individual moving in and, just by going about their daily normal lives, spreading disease, possibly productively, coming. And who is better at hiding their agenda and methods than the Lunar? (Apart from the Sid, mayhap).

                                        Okay, I'm a little sad that I have to point this out, but, uh...well. One of the things I tell my prospective Exalted players to suck 'em into the game is that Exalted is a kitchen-sink setting. I generally challenge them to think of something they'd like in a game, then briefly describe the part of Exalted's vast setting that is that thing. So.

                                        Theoretical Prospective Player: Hey, Guru? I've been looking at Lunars, they're like humans with a totem animal that gives them powers, right? Can I play a guy who's like, a virusman? Like a living disease given physical form, or a guy with virus-like traits?

                                        Me: ...actually, yeah. See, way back in the ancient dawn of time there was a mighty civilization of sorcerer-priests who worshiped and studied the life energy in all things, known to other cultures as the Genesis Lords. Their magic was all about reshaping and repurposing life: living ships, geckos that live on your ceiling and luminesce when you clap, magically altered diseases that they'd inject into you to fight off other diseases...theirs was a small but mighty empire, and so the Chosen of the Sun grew jealous of their power and waged war upon them. Most of them died, and many fled on a great living ship into the madness beyond the world...but a few elite warrior-priests rendered their consciousness down into viral forms to wait out the destruction. Every now and then someone in Creation will come down with a case of the sniffles, and her personality will slowly begin to change as she dreams of an ancient city with spires of green filled with iridescent birds. Over time, she forgets who she is as the long-dead Genesis Lord takes over, gradually remembering more and more of himself and his ancient lore. They are subtle, these Genesis Lords, and many of them work to build a world where Creation will once again be safe for their kind, who they hope to revive from a few drops of blood or a drop of sweat or a fingerprint. They burn out the bodies they wear quickly, and when they die they wake again in someone else somewhere in Creation with a nasty stomach flu, and must again wait for their memories to slowly return.

                                        ...I guess I could let you play one of them. You'd definitely have some control over diseases and some knowledge of the physical and mystic building blocks of life.

                                        Theoretical Prospective Player: ...wait. You mean I can play a Time Lord?

                                        Me: Well, I did say Exalted has everything.

                                        So I'm making God-Kicking Boot, an Exalted webcomic, now. Updates on Sundays. Full-color, mediocre but slowly improving art. It's a thing.

                                                I'm just going to go back and reiterate that when you get all excessively wanky about how disease works, you probably wind up with something that doesn't function the least bit like disease in real life, and that both removes a lot of the resonance, and a lot of the actual relevant implications of disease in play.

                                                I mean, when cholera is called the besieging army's bane in First Edition, that's kind of an implication that if you have your army of tens of thousands of people squat down in the same place for months on end, their local water supply is going to get mixed up with their toilets, and that's going to leave them ravaged by disease.

                                                So your biggest problem with portraying cholera as a thing that happens when criminals bathe in water is that, well... you're probably going to have an army that isn't ravaged by disease, and that's kind of a big deal for every army ever.

                                                Part of the reason that disease is so resonant and terrifying is because it spreads in a manner that, when you have the perspective on it that we do, we would expect of highly transmittable microscopic organisms. Indeed, part of the reason that people attach disease to easily parsed social narratives, often predicated on scapegoating, is because of just how terrifying disease is for the fact that nobody can really figure out how it's happening.

                                                And capturing that is important.

                                                    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                                    I'm just going to go back and reiterate that when you get all excessively wanky about how disease works, you probably wind up with something that doesn't function the least bit like disease in real life, and that both removes a lot of the resonance, and a lot of the actual relevant implications of disease in play.

                                                    I mean, when cholera is called the besieging army's bane in First Edition, that's kind of an implication that if you have your army of tens of thousands of people squat down in the same place for months on end, their local water supply is going to get mixed up with their toilets, and that's going to leave them ravaged by disease.
                                                    I see your point, and I don't want to sound like I'm being dismissive, but first, that's stuff from First Edition, which as far as I'm aware isn't necessarily relevant or correct in 2nd or 3E. So I'm hesitant to involve stuff from 1st Ed. And second, since when did people turn into wolves and/or summon hundreds of obsidian butterflies from their hand to mince their enemies in real life?

                                                    But onto your main point. I'd say there that the problem isn't a conceptualisation of disease functioning or anything then, it's just being 'excessively wanky' as you put it. And that can spoil any aspect of the game. You can start arguing about how the Lunar changing thing doesn't conform with the conservation of mass/energy, or how changing like that should kill the person before they can finish, or how the Sid ability to 'have never been here but was' interacts with the general difficulty of teleportation in Creation. They're contrivances to facilitate gameplay and fun, and the books treatment of disease is inherently ambiguous anyway. So maybe the main problem isn't that it does/doesn't make sense, but that we have conflicting information. So, the real question we should be asking, rather than "is this allowed/do bacteria and viruses exist?", is "which rulebook am I saying has precedence over which other rulebook in the case of conflicts?" Some treat disease as being mystical, which is fine. Others treat it as more biological, which is also fine. But Exalted, being the game that it is, will more or less inevitably deal with disease at some point in some way, so I guess the ST needs to make the best decision for his group and the story he wants to tell.
