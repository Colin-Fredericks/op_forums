                    Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                    This is how medieval society worked.

                    And I wish was how my country worked today, but sadly it is not. I mean, I can understand why many Irish, Welsh and Scottish people feel they must resort to Nationalism, but I can't agree with it in the long run. (English Nationalists have no excuse.)


                    If there's only one ideology in power that 99% of the populace go with, it's fine. (Sucked for the Jews though.)
                    Or people are just non-ideological and accept that they must bow to their government or be killed, and whether that government is legitimate is something best not thought too much about.


                    But basically I think is an argument about semantics because you're talking about different things.

                    I think Grod and Alucard are talking about the academic poli-sci definition of Nationalism and Nation-States (which is a distinct ideology), while I think John and Sunder are talking about the non-academic definition where Nation is synonymous with Country or State. Like when you say the Nation of the USA or the Nation of the United Kingdom (neither of which are Nations in the academic and ideological sense, but are clearly things people call Nations, hence the United Nations).


                    Saying this, while nationalism might only exist in a proto-form in the Time of Tumult, I can actually see quite an interesting (if morally complex) game about building a (academic-definition) nation. And I can see some parts of that being part of a Bureaucracy system (such as building a... well, bureaucracy. Maybe creating firm borders. Maybe less so spreading a unified group-identity, promoting a particular view of history, etc. Those could be done via bureaucracy I guess... hmmm, I suppose in practice they are).

                    (I mean... obviously some would argue that Nation-Building by its very idea is attacking the basis of Nationalism, but Exalted is quite happy to deconstruct ideologies!)

                    In fact, one of the PCs in the essence 5 game is doing this. (And it's almost as bad as you'd expect. I mean, there hasn't been any actual genocide, but it's caused a lot of civil and external war, an inquisition, purges of priests and nobles, etc, etc.)



                    Anyway...
                    Certainly I can see why people argue for a system about running a State. Though I can also see Alucard's worry about it, certainly previous editions had their issues. And I personally enjoy the personalisation of power within a monarchy, in terms of running games. (Ie, you bring your State together by going and socialising with regional nobles, assassinating your enemies, etc. Rather than rolling dice to represent administrative skill. Although of course if you don't what's the point of the ability and charms?)
                    Perhaps something just for downtime.
                    I don't disagree that people are using different definitions of words, just that the conversation started based on what was clearly not the pure "academic" use of the word, and that attempts at clarity in the discussion led to something of a derail (Wikipedia suggests the Byzantine Empire, Fifteenth Century England or the Dutch Republic as the first "nations", if not the medieval feudal states, ethnonations or the city-states -- this doesn't seem incongruous with the Realm proper as a "nation").

                    Still, state-building (if that's more academically acceptable?) is a fertile ground for RPG stories, and I'd like to see it supported. If you could link me to an academic definition of "nation" (I freely admit that I don't know what a nation is in the academic sense), I'd love to educate myself (as introducing academic nations to Exalted seems like an epic social quest -- from my inference, it could be the basis of a fantastic campaign, so I'd love to see rules which facilitate this).

                    But again, the point was a social system for managing a relatively large group of people organized under a single structure (be that a government, business, church or what-have-you).

                                                Sudden reboot of my computer kind of ate my reply to this. But I actually kind of disagree on this. CRM has actually more issues than you note.

                                                The first is it actually more or less has the same issue that Sail does in 3e. It is basically in the end, a bunch of rolls at fixed difficulties or contested rolls that determine success or failure at your endeavours. This means that anyone with the right Excellency breaks the system in half. So while something simpler would be nice, you have to create "buffers" so it doesn't create kind of just this run-away problem rendering it half useless as a result.

                                                The second is that it kind of reifies issues wiht "seeing like a state". Actions in CRM really only about obtaining Captial or affecting Assets. Success or failure is determined more or less as to whether you do the thing, which when positively successful, gets you what you want. I think an issue there is that really any failure points are more about whether or not you get to do what you are trying to do. Success or failure is simply whether the leader is competent enough. This includes things like say, improving Competence, which is not about actually rooting out corrupt people or finding good ones, but just improving an abstract stat. It turns societies into systemic logic-engines and makes it difficult to assess people. Note, also, that a lot of the project system we have in 3e, while abstract, does a lot of these with the main conflict point being plot-generating to solve things, not working on asbratced Assets. Basically I think that CRM leans too hard on Civ-style mechanics, as the people in an organization aren't really people, just traits. A bit why 3e doesn't have these systems is in part becuase to my understanding, the devs for the corebook do subscribe to a bit of James C. Scott here.

                                                Kind of related to that too, note is that success doesn't necssarily mean good. But the way CRM is structured, there's no way to do that. Like, remember that a lot of fammines were done by very successfully implemented land reform built on shoddy ideas. Or that some things like highly currated forests for industrial scale lumber harvesting had great short term but long term catostrophic effects. Or even just odd down-stream stuff like how corruption often comes up due to individual action and sorta spreads if not taken care of, not becuase a trait in the organization is bad. These are in CRM, not replicatable. They arguably do what looks ot be a success, but result in actual problems/failure. CRM only measures whetherw hat you do is doable, not whether it's useful/generates story.

                                                Thirdly, both CRM and MoH depend on the ST doing too much effing work. A big thing both kind of assume is that you as an ST have an ecosystem of just-as-complex-as-the-PC's stuff going on around for them to interact with, otherwise you hae captial or other sacks of good sitting around for htem to hit like a piñata. Any system needs to have it so that the ST doesn't have to have these effing things prepared or at least not have to have them banging around in the background like NPC Civs in order for it to be less tedious. This is ranodm personal experience having used both before.

                                                Again, this is all a bit why I think what we need more is 1) Bureacracy just needs like, example show to use it like we have for Larceny and such. It need not be an entire infrastructure system, whcih seems to be what folks have conflated it as being since ever.

                                                And 2) Probably very very abstracted traits to help us get an idea of what organizaitons have, want, and can do. And what problems might arise. I think here, just giving clearer ideas of the Merits, sizes, and experitse of groups migtht help, but in this regard I think just giving more guideliens on the features of projects from the corebook go just as far as going and trying to make yet another system someone wiht the Excellency will trivialize anyhow, and which somehow tries to capture a successful bar shop to the probably once-in-history Veneician Arsenal (which probably in Exalted is an example of what you can do with the Artifact system without it actually being an Artifact IMHO).
