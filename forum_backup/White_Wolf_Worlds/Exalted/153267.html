    Sidereals As “Prepared Casters”

    05-29-2014, 08:17 PM
    This concept came up in a discussion about how the celestial splats can be balanced against each while still maintaining the concept of Solars being the best at what they do in a very straight forward sense of achievement. We were trying to come up with roundabout ways in which the other splats can work around or, even negate the power a solar gains from just “being better” than everyone else.

    I’m not super familiar with the Sidereal charm set so this concept might already be represented in some way, but I am pretty sure there aren't charms that function exactly this way. What if by committing motes a Sidereal could craft fortuitous destinies and hold them in reserve until they were in need of them. For example the Sidereal could envision a destiny where they dodge a mighty blow and make a stinging counter attack, then tuck that destiny away. Later in a fight against a solar where the momentum of the fight is very much against them, their motes are running low, and they have a few bad wounds, they could pull out the destiny they made in the image of their peak performance and hope to turn the fight around. These would be charms that were most effective when prepared ahead of time, perhaps costing a surcharge of motes or even paradox when used on the fly.

    Another thought revolving around manipulating fragments of destiny was the thought of a Sidereal stealing the good fate surrounding an enemy’s actions and giving them to an ally to use. For example the Sidereal senses that Solar is about to make a devastating motion in a meeting of lords, so he steals the good fortune surrounding this action, and gives it to his ally. The Solar finds his argument suddenly fall flat, and the person across the table from him suddenly puts forward a stunning rebuke…in a way that strangely reminds him of something he would say.

      Interesting thought experiment. I think you'd need to be careful that a Sidereal caught without prep (or without someone else's success to piggyback) isn't made too helpless. I like to think of Sidereal powers as flexible plot devices that, with a little bit of effort, the Sidereal can make fit a whole range of situations. You maneuver in real time to line up your "shots", like figuring out how to tell your target the opposite of what you want him to think before dropping Avoiding the Truth Technique. To me that's what feeds their "secret agent" vibe and more generally makes their idiosyncratic powers actually fun to play with in the game.

      In comparison you seem to be suggesting that they instead get the broadly-applicable power to fabricate special-case powers in advance. In my mind that turns them into gadgeteers, something more like Q than 007. That is, something more like a D&D Wizard (obviously intentional, from your title). Being caught with your pants down because you didn't prep the right power doesn't suit Heaven's elite troubleshooters very well.

              The biggest issue with sidereals is that their schtick is intrinsically linked to being ST NPCs.

              Sidereals essentially mesh Burn Notice, Person of Interest and Kung Fu the Legend Continues all into one character type. All of which hinge on the concept that they function with ST level knowledge of the setting as to what is currently happening and what will happen. It might be simpler just to make Sidereals an explicitly ST only territory, rather than a player choice.

              Sidereals are by definition supposed to operate Xanatos Gambits as a default. But if you try to make that into a mechanic you wind up with things like Shards of Obsidian style final charm which is essentially choosing to use a given fate based outcome.

              On the other hand if you make it just a question of backgrounds which can be used as tools, the whole thing hinges upon the abilities of the player to connect the dots. This leads to the scenario where people do white room simulations and "prove" that the splat is under powered because there is no direct mechanics to prove supremacy..... or you get players like me who look at all the toys and start grinning. I mean how much damage can my character do by just saying he was an architect during his mortal life and has an interest in geomancy? But then that is considered broken because once again no direct, simple rock-paper-scissors mechanic to counter such a play style.

                      Personally I favor "oh yeah, this is totally what I prepared when you weren't looking" powers over fire-and-forget wizardry, for three reasons:
                      • First, fire-and-forget wizardry is based on an annoying dance of having to read the ST's mind and the ST having to work to ensure some but not all of your preparations are actually useful. Break the plan too often and the Sidereal feels cheated, don't break it and the campaign's boring.
                      • Second, it puts too much onus on the intellect of the player over the intellect of the character. It's good to challenge the player's intellect, but their Sidereal is almost certainly supposed to be better at this than the player is, and the player is equally good at planning regardless of if they're playing a Sidereal or a Solar.
                      • Third, and maybe most importantly, retroactive preparation puts the Sidereals on the winning side of their narrative. Almost any time a plan is prepared for on-camera in fiction, it means it's going to fail. Any time a plan is prepared for on-camera in a game, there's going to be something that happens partway through that makes the entire plan blow up. Fictional plans work when we the audience don't see exactly how they were set up, or were shown some of the steps but didn't have the actual plan pointed out to us, and similarly plans in a game have to be made without the ST's awareness or you can practically guarantee they're going to fail. Giving Sidereals the ability to have had a plan all along makes them Nate Ford revealing the victory twist at the end, demanding that they plan out everything in advance and in view of the ST makes them Hannibal from the A-Team creating the plan that doesn't work...which only works because the planners are actually very good at improvisation, which is the opposite of what you want to convey with powers that demand extensive real-life planning.
                      Fire-and-forget wizardry primarily leaves D&D wizards on top because D&D wizards are hilariously overpowered for the setting and game system that they're in, and simple preparations legitimately leave them at zero risk of anything except another spellcasting-capable creature being an actual threat to them. It doesn't work for Sidereals because they're not on top of the power heap.

                          Originally posted by TheCountAlucard View Post
                          Not only do I disagree with this, I reject it.

                          The name of the game is "Exalted." Not "Solar Exalted," not "Dragon-Blooded Exalted." If something is - by its nature - unplayable? It shouldn't be a type of Exalted.
                          You are entirely free to reject it, but it doesn't change the fact that bad game design doesn't magically go away if all one does is change the window dressing, but still keep the same flawed core intact.

                          The problem with prepared casters is that after a certain point they are dependent on foreknowledge and will actively avoid any situation where they can't have it. This automatically limits role playing and character mechanics. Playing a prepared caster without said foreknowledge is basically agreeing to be in a combat focused game with no combat charms in your load out, let alone critical things like perfect defenses.

                          On the flip side of the coin, if you are given said foreknowledge, then how do you balance that against the other classes? If I know there is going to be a battle at such a place in the future, I don't show up to it, I place a bomb under it and detonate it days later when everyone else is focused on their declarations of blood & honor. But that means you have invalidated the functionality of the other character builds. The ST can attempt to counter this with story based contrivance, but after a certain point it gets rather ridiculous how often this becomes a restriction.

                          It's like having an ST who traps every single door, chest, floor tile and toilet paper roll. It is almost impossible to stop because the foreknowledge character doesn't show up to deal with things directly. Thus all those shiny mega-damage combos don't do any good.

                          What usually happens next is that the game design attempts to add the appearance of being a prepared caster with fluff, but uses mechanics that only work within specific scenarios such as combat. But that in truth isn't controlling fate, that is just another way to stunt or use a standard charm with a face lift.

                                              Actually that's kinda like the resplendency powers they already have.
                                              For instance there is a resplendency that makes you ignore fatigue penalties for a task and instead treat them as bonus dice. That's a very good tool in a situation where you want to challenge someone in arm-wrestling. Combined with sidereal shell-games it's very easy to defeat stronger opponents when you're prepared.

                                              these power can really make a huge difference and they need prior planing since they take hours, if not days, to create a resplendent destiny. And since you're limited to (essence) destinies, you can't just activate all of them.You have to choose which powers you will be using. So it totally fits what you are advocating.

                                              They are even more interesting because they cost nothing in terms of "normal" resources (motes, wp, health levels). That gives even more advantages if choose them wisely. I think the only thing needed is having powers covering a bit more diverse options. Some are quite redundant and the themes they cover are limited. Also some powers are totally "generic" like accelerate willpower recovery, travel faster. Specific powers like "instantly createa talisman" or summon arrows that bring outside-of-fate creature inside are much cooler and would fit more situations where you have a plan or somehow know in advance about what's about to come.

                                              But there are a few issues for them to be practical :
                                              - when you create a destiny, duration and endurance are in competition but the destiny ends when either the duration is finished or you use the last endurance dot.
                                              - The role of the destiny can get stuck between the identity and the usefulness the the resplendencies. If you do meaningful things with the identity and need to keep it (if you get a position of authority through that identity) you're screwed. Especially since you can have only one destiny for each college.
                                              - you can't "refill" the destiny's endurance dots or increase its duration either
                                              - the main ressource used, endurance, is fixed for each destiny. You can't use a destiny in a specific college to fuel resplendencies in another college

                                              A few changes are required to make the system less painful. Either :
                                              - Remove the "duration" trait for destinies. They last as long as you haven't used the last endurance dot

                                              - Make both uses independent : destinies only have a duration trait and will last until the end. Endurance dots are gathered in another way and do not impact the destinies, and they possibly are a "global" ressource not tied to a particular college.

                                              - Allow cross-colleges use of endurance dots, but you still require an active destiny in the college to use its powers. That way you can have one or two "trash" destinies that exist only to fuel your resplendencies, and you keep your important identities safe from disappearing due to endurance depletion.

                                              - Allow for a method to replenish endurance or move it from a destiny to the other.

                                              TL; DR :

                                              Resplendencies have the ability to cover what you need, just tweak how you pay endurance and change a few "boring" powers to make them fill gaps in the sidereals' charmset and it will be perfect.

                                              The fool can only learn from a sage, but only the sage can learn from a fool.
