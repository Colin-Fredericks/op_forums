                                        This is quite a long answer, and it's a bit of a tangent from the main thing of the thread, so I'm going to put the answers in spoilers.

                                        Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

                                        ​I'm not sure what this means. Is it saying that the Solar Charms themselves might not be much more powerful than Terrestrial Charms at the same level, or that a given Solar won't feel as powerful once they reach Essence 5 because they're assumed to have already taken the Charms a while ago, or what?
                                        Okay, I'll try and explain it.
                                        At character gen, Solar Sherlock and DB Magistrate Dee both want to be really good at investigation. But not go crazy.
                                        So Solar Sherlock gets 10 investigation charms (and a handful of non-investigation charms so he can survive combat and stuff). This includes charms like Judge's Ear, Flawless Interrogation Technique, Ten Magistrate Eyes (which is Essence 3), and Evidence-Restoring Prana (also Essence 3).
                                        DB Magistrate Dee buys 5 investigation charms (and an excellency). They're all Essence 1. He can't buy more than 5 because he'd need essence 2.
                                        Solar Sherlock is a far, far better detective than DB magistrate Dee. He's got more investigation charms, and they're much higher essence (and they're Solar charms).

                                        100xp later, Solar Sherlock now has 15 investigation charms (which is virtually all of them), including (the only) essence 5 charm (he's also bought a bunch of other charms, obviously, since those 5 investigation charms only cost 40xp, but he needs to buy other stuff so as to not just have investigation charms).
                                        DB Magistrate Dee buys another 7 investigation charms, including essence 3 charms. He doesn't have enough XP for essence 4 yet though.

                                        So Solar Sherlock is still a better detective, as he a)has slightly more investigation charms, b)has essence 4 and 5 charms rather than only essence 3 charms, and c)they're Solar charms.
                                        But the difference between 10 essence 1-3 charms/5 essence 1 charms is much bigger than the difference between 15 essence 1-5 charms/12 essence 1-3 charms.

                                        250xp later, Solar Sherlock has 15 investigation charms (which is virtually all of them), including the essence 5 charm. And DB Magistrate Dee has... 15 investigation charms*, including any essence 5 charms in the tree.

                                        Solar Sherlock is still a better detective, because his Investigation charms are better, but now the only difference is DB charms vs Solar charms, rather than number of charms and the essence of charms available.

                                        *I'm assuming the DB investigation tree isn't much bigger than the Solar tree. But obviously I'm just using investigation as an example.

                                        Of course, you could argue that Solar Sherlock was never much more powerful than Dee, because Dee started with lots more charms in other areas. But Solar Sherlock was a much better detective, which is what both characters aimed to be good at. (Also, see my answer below)



                                        ​I still kind of maintain that, in effect, Supernal is less about being powerful than it is about having the option to expand vertically, rather than hitting ceilings all over and needing to expand horizontally.
                                        Sort of. I mean, theoretically yes. And I do find a fair few characters actually don't start with more than essence 2 charms in their supernal anyway, maybe 3, because they want to cover some other bases. Depends a bit on what the supernal is (since some charm sets are way bigger than others).

                                        But on the other hand, I find in practice it does make you more powerful, because the charms you're using are more powerful. People use supernal to get the particular powerful charms they want to get. For example, if you take all the essence 1 craft charms to increase your craft xp, you'll get a bit more craft xp. But if you take the essence 2 capstone of that tree, suddenly you get silly amounts of craft xp.
                                        (I recently started a new game, I've played 1 session so far, and in the first session I got 84 silver xp and 30 gold xp. In one session. Without that 1 essence 2 charm I'd have got maybe 25 silver and 14 gold or something.)

                                        Also, in my experience, Solars get power from specialisation. You don't want to be completely specialised, but the more specialised ones seem to be able to do far more than the less specialised ones. I could certainly see that in the game I ran.

                                        In addition, having that option actually serves as a means of raising the ceiling for all of the others by being something that you have few constraints in spending your Essence-raising XP in.
                                        Sorry, didn't quite follow that. All the other what?

                                        ​I'd also suspect that in many circumstances, the Supernal of an Essence 5 character is liable to still be apparent until a fair amount of time has passed, and would continue to be relevant through a combination of probably having a greater number and variety of Charms than many other Abilities that had to develop more slowly, and the character's playstyle having been conditioned into focusing on that Ability.
                                        Sort of.
                                        In the essence game I play (which I used to run), there's a Dawn who's either Resistance or War supernal. I can't remember. He's got 20 war charms and, I don't know, 12 resistance charms? But I think his supernal is actually resistance (since War caps out at 3 so he thought it was a waste to take it as supernal).

                                        The preachy Zenith I assume is Performance Supernal, because that's what we get him to do, preach.

                                        The spymaster... I'm not sure actually. Probably socialise? I know she's got 4 Socialise charms. I assume she has more, but I only remember her using Wise-Eyed Courtier and one of the ones that increases your Guile for cheap. It might be Larceny for all I know.

                                        And my own heroic, Godspeed Vanguard-riding Cult of the Illuminated Night Caste is... Athletics Supernal? I'd have to check... Yeah, Athletics Supernal. He's got 7 Athletics charms. Same as his number of Melee charms. And 12 Craft charms. But those aren't Supernal for Nights.

                                        As to the dragon-riding sword-wielding solar-sorceress... I actually have no idea. I'm going to guess it's Occult, but she only has 3 or 4 occult charms. I just assume she picked it because she's a sorceress. She might have chosen Presence or Ride.

                                        So, basically, I don't find it makes a huge difference at essence 5. People buy up their Supernal early (because they can) and once they've got to essence 3, they've probably got everything they want from it, so go and buy other stuff to fill in gaps. I wouldn't mind getting more Athletics, but I've got enough to do what I want and there was loads of other stuff to buy (melee, presence, craft, evocations, etc).

                                        Not to mention that, over a whole campaign, people sometimes get a bit bored with their Supernal. We used to have a supernal-crafter Twilight, but I think he got bored with crafting. He didn't make that many artefacts (though he did make big non-magical projects, like castles and arming battalions). I think he had about 13 craft charms, and after that he mostly bought Medicine, Larceny and Melee because they were fun and useful.


                                        I do have an essence 4 Zenith monk in another game. He has 4 charms from his supernal (Integrity). And 7 investigation charms, and 19 occult charms. I've got what I need from my Supernal... and you can't choose Occult or Investigation for a Zenith's supernal.


                                        Whereas, from what I've heard, in the essence 2 game my friends play (they might have just reached 3), supernals matter a lot more. The melee-supernal Samurai is amazing at fighting with his sword, and that's where a lot of his power lies. The stealth-supernal ninja is really sneaky. The athletics-supernal messenger is incredibly fast, though as they've gone up in essence she hasn't got much faster, but she has got much better at punching people. The medicine-supernal Sorcerer has a lot of spells, but he does also have a lot of medicine, which as far as I can tell is why the party keeps an evil cackling sorcerer around.

                                        One last thing I should say though; I think some of this depends on what your Supernal actually is. Some charm trees are much bigger than others, so Supernal's going to be important for longer. And it also depends on the character: sometimes people can't pick the ability they want most of because it's not supernal for their caste. Or the supernal just isn't very important, because they're more of an all-rounder. Whereas if your character is something like "the greatest swordsman in Creation", supernal's going to be very important for the whole campaign, and you're right, even at essence 5 it's going to be pretty obvious you're Melee-supernal.

                                        STing Bronze Age Exalted
