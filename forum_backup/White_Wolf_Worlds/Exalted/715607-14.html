            Resistance

            Number of Charms: 26
            By Ability requirement: 2/3/6/5/10
            By Essence requirement: 10/8/7/1
            Nonstandard dice tricks: Enemy takes -1i for each 1 rolled on attack roll. Enemy rerolls successful damage dice.

            Themes: Have all the health levels. Recover health levels, or be rewarded for having them. Resist poison and disease. Absorb damage without harm; possibly gain rewards for doing so. Use armor more easily. Bring down the red haze and kill all that moves.

            A surprisingly small number of Ability 5 Charms, here! It really feels like you can invest in Resistance at a number of different levels – which is nice, for the only universally-applicable defensive Ability. Note that, unlike 2e, Resistance Charms have to be declared before the attack roll (unless they say otherwise) – being able to mount a cheap soak-based defense is likely to be a big deal, given that it may never go off.

            Charms:
            1. 4 Hardness, -2 damage to all attacks for one tick.
            2. Post-hit soak booster. Upgrades to remove damage successes.
            3. Soak unsoakable withering damage; against a Decisive attack, create extra health levels.
            4. Extra health levels.
            5. Heal fast. (Recommended with lots of #4.)
            6. Gain [~.25*your health levels]i.
            7. Put on armor in a handful of turns. Er, how long does it take normally?
            8. Once your armor is on, its Mobility penalty is 0.
            9. Scenelong bonus dice vs. poison.
            10. When struck by a Withering attack, pay 1i to recover (damage dice/2)m.
            11. Become immune to minor scenery damage, from brambles(?) to bonfires. Gain soak or hardness. Can’t be used in armor.
            12. Halve (or more) post-soak damage of a Withering attack.
            13. Apply soak to a Decisive attack(!). Perfect soak vs. uncountable damage.
            14. 1/fight, heal when you recover from Crash.
            15. Store your armor in Elsewhere.
            16. Bonus successes vs. illness.
            17. 1/fight, when struck by a Decisive, pay 2i to gain 1wp.
            18. Go into a berserk rage. Gain +1 die to combat pools; reduce wound penalties by 1.
            19. Heal your -0 health levels gradually, but in combat time.
            20. Reduce the penalty and duration of a crippling attack. (What’s a crippling attack?)
            21. 1/scene, force attacker to reroll successful damage dice.
            22. Summon magical glowing sun-armor. (Still not a technique!)
            23. Gain immunity to any poison or disease you’ve encountered before, and resistance to the rest.
            24. When struck by a Decisive attack, gain (half the non-successful damage dice)i.
            25. While under #18, go into a more-berserk (berserker?) rage. Gain +3 dice to combat pools; ignore wound penalties; recover 1m/turn.
            26. Gain 20 Hardness, bonus soak, and post-soak damage negation, but only as long as you make big attacks every turn.
            The ticklong duration of Durability of Oak Meditation (#1) is a big deal – that makes it a mote-efficient reply to some of the big magical flurries. This feels like a good dip for anyone expecting to run into a bunch of Iron Whirlwinds; 3m for one chance at -2 damage doesn’t feel great, but 3m for six chances feels quite a bit better.

            The upgrade to Spirit Strengthens the Skin (#2) is worded confusingly. Is it correct that this effect has a base cost of 1wp, 2i, and then costs +2m for each damage success bought off? The actual wording says the cost is one thing, and then immediately states the cost is something else. Also, is there any limit to the upgraded use, or can I buy off as much damage as I have motes to spend?

            Iron Skin Concentration (#3) has a pleasantly anime feel, where you take the enemy’s big hit and are barely scratched. Not sure I love how completely different the Withering/Decisive effects are, but that’s a minor complaint. This Charm protects against unsoakable damage; the only Charm granting such is in Black Lotus, so far, as well as…

            … ahaha, the description of cauterizing a wound mentions it’s unsoakable damage. Given that it’s actual damage, I bet so.

            Ox-Body Technique (#4) scales with Stamina and doesn’t have trap options now. I approve!

            At Essence 5, Body-Mending Meditation (#5) + Fate-Shifting Solar Arete can let you recover a -0 bashing level every thirty seconds. I’m just sayin’. ([3600 seconds in an hour] / [19 successes * Essence 5], for the record.) Note that Ox-Body alone won’t raise your healing speed; absent BMM, you can get stuck in your -2s for a looooong time.

            Are Armored Scout’s Invigoration (#8) and Whirlwind Armor-Donning Prana (#7) meant to stack? As written, it doesn’t appear that they do – ASI doesn’t kick in until the armor is worn. (Why is armor such a pain to put on? I mean, multiple Simple actions with a Charm? Most of the fights I’ve seen so far are over in that much time.)

            Adamant Skin Technique (#13) is a great one-turn panic button – soaking Decisive attacks is huge. Here’s the weird thing, though: if you use AST, make sure the attack hits. Like, don’t pump the Excellency, do whatever you can to lower your Defense. Let it be 0, if you can – it’s not like that will (generally) add extra damage. Because, look: you’ve got to activate this Charm before the attack gets rolled, right? So if they hit, you’ve spent 8m to consume almost all of their Initiative, taking little or no damage. If they miss, you just spent 8m to drain them of the 2i they lose for missing. It’s unfortunate that AST requires Diamond Body Technique (#11), since there are a lot of builds (i.e., anything that wears armor) for which the latter Charm is useless. (It’s also kind of weird that 10 damage bounces from AST, 100 damage kills you through it, but infinity damage… again bounces off. What?)

            Illness-Resisting Meditation (#16) is weirdly less useful than Poison-Resisting Meditation (#9). They’re both cheaper Excellencies, but mid-combat poisoning feels seems common – mid-combat disease doesn’t, and if you’re not getting sick in combat, who cares how expensive the Excellency is?

            There’s a persistent theme here of Stamina mattering for Resistance – Charms are faster or cheaper or more efficacious with high Stamina. I like this.

            Immunity to Everything Technique (#23) suggests some kind of funny behavior patterns – poisoning yourself now to gain resistance later. (It also makes the old 2e-ism of “same stats as Arrow Frog Venom, but not Arrow Frog Venom,” a lot more dangerous.)


            A couple of rough spots aside, Resistance looks solid. I’m not sure I’d want everything in it, but there’s a solid core of powers to take here. (Might not be a great choice for Supernal, though – one Charm over Essence 3.)

            Yes: Aegis of Invulnerable Might (#20) does a lot to create shifted combat priorities – it makes what would normally be bad moves into interesting decisions. Kudos!
            No: Whirlwind Armor-Donning Prana. Just take Glorious Solar Plate, if you don’t want to wear armor. Or, well, you could, if it wasn’t a prereq.
            Just Plain Weird: Battle-Fury Focus (#18) and Bloodthirsty Sword-Dancer Spirit (#25) are both powerful Charms – they’re about the only source of persistent penalty-negation and combat bonus dice. They also eat up two full actions, in addition to their other costs. Again, isn’t the fight likely going to be largely over by then?

                        Ride

                        Number of Charms: 36
                        By Ability requirement: 1/2/7/6/20
                        By Essence requirement: 8/14/14/0/0
                        Nonstandard dice tricks: Enemy loses 1 damage per 1 or 2 rolled. Gain 1m for Ride Charms for each 10 the enemy rolls.

                        Themes: Have the highest mobility of anyone on the battlefield, especially when running away. Never lose your mount, to circumstance or damage or unhorsing. Your mount is a combatant in his own right.

                        A lot of Ride Charms here, given that none of them are over Essence 3.

                        Charms:
                        1. Gain a number of minor mount-enhancing Abilities – prevent fatigue, call it to you, etc.
                        2. Mount resists fatigue, gains a movement success, and can use Graceful Crane Stance/Monkey Leap Technique.
                        3. Reflexively Disengage while you are mounted.
                        4. Your mount moves at greater speeds while outside combat. (How fast does it normally move, outside combat?)
                        5. Mount can rise from prone for 1m.
                        6. Your mount gains its own move and attack actions, as well as its own Initiative track.
                        7. Your mount can reflexively Defend Other on you.
                        8. Spend your health levels on behalf of your mount, at a reduced rate.
                        9. Tack and bard a mount in 1-4 rounds.
                        10. Automatically succeed at a Disengage as long as only one enemy is at close range.
                        11. Convert mount’s speed bonus to successes.
                        12. Double mount’s speed for the scene.
                        13. #11 can be used to enhance a Join Battle roll.
                        14. Your mount ignores wound penalties.
                        15. Recurring 1s on a mounted movement roll; leave a trail of bonfire flames in your wake.
                        16. Recover instantly from an unhorse Gambit.
                        17. Unhorse someone and steal his mount.
                        18. Transfer half your Initiative to your mount, or vice-versa.
                        19. Over a scene of treatment, absorb and reduce your mount’s wounds.
                        20. While using #6 and #7, your mount may Decisively counterattack for attacks on you.
                        21. Your mount (barely) avoids Crash or death, as long as it had at least 2i or 2 HLs to begin with.
                        22. An attack on your mount loses 1 damage per 1 or 2 rolled.
                        23. Summon an Essence-horse. But it’s an expression of skill, and no one would try to label this a… yeah, you get the joke.
                        24. Ride off into the sunset. Recover motes and Willpower, and remove a point of Limit.
                        25. If someone approaches you after a Disengage, retreat two range bands.
                        26. For each 10 the enemy rolls, gain 1m for use on Ride Charms next turn.
                        27. Substitute Ride for Awareness in Join Battle.
                        28. Move three range bands a turn at extreme range from all enemies, or two bands/turn at closer ranges.
                        29. Your mount runs on water or air for one turn.
                        30. Rush an enemy in the air.
                        31. Summon your mount’s tack, barding, and weapons from Elsewhere.
                        32. Mount armor has no Mobility penalty.
                        33. Activate “attack-evasive Dodge Charms” on behalf of your mount.
                        34. Your mount gains 1i/turn, plus bonuses when you outrace an enemy.
                        35. While using #6 and #7, your mount may (reflexively?) Decisively Clash with attacks aimed at you.
                        36. Your mount gains bonus soak for the scene.
                        Elusive Mount Technique (#3) – i.e., “Disengage reflexively” – is a pretty big deal for some archetypes. If you’re an archer, being on a horse is a big boost with just a couple of Charms.

                        Am I reading it right that Seasoned Beast-Rider’s Approach (#6) makes your mount a potential weak spot? If it has an Initiative track, you can aim Withering attacks at it – and to this point in the tree, it’s likely to have a lower Defense than the Solar is, which means enemies can use it to juice up to attack the Solar.

                        Worthy Mount Technique (#7) is fueled by the mount’s Initiative. What happens if the mount doesn’t have an Initiative track?

                        Does Untouchable Horseman’s Attitude (#10) replace the cost to Disengage, or supplement it? The latter, I assume.

                        A number of these effects appear to sidestep the normal Charm-die penalties – boosting Speed, as per Supernal Lash Discipline (#12), seems to be uncapped. So how does this interact with Speed Fury Focus (#13), which adds (Speed) successes to Join Battle?

                        Immortal Rider’s Advantage (#18) specifies that it only lets you transfer half your Initiative, rounded up. But, um, the Charm is Reflexive – so transfer half, and then transfer half again, and then… Also, I think the cost for this Charm should be “-“ – typically, quantities transferred are not counted as costs. (Cf. Essence-Lending Method.)

                        Ariana, what exactly is holding you onto the back of that horse?

                        Mounted archer is nasty here. Move while aiming; Disengage farther than normal; reflexively recover from being dismounted. So, say you’re a close-range warrior on foot – do you have any way to combat this, other than going indoors and never coming out?

                        Fierce Charger’s Pulse (#26) adds a Ride-Charms-only mote pool. Been a while since we’ve gotten another of those, but I can’t say I’ve missed them.

                        A heavily-invested Solar rider is going to be mean: mobile, generating extra Initiative off the mount, making an extra attack, and so on, and – critically – defended by the Solar’s own Dodge Charms. This is an archetype that appears to heavily favor the ranged attacker, though; Melee-based combatants aren’t going to be able to defend their mounts, won’t care about the Disengage bonuses, and may find the mount is more liability than help once enemies begin swinging at it. The primary benefit of being on a mount in such circumstances appears to be countering other mounted combatants, and given that the Disengage benefits outpace the Rush advantages, that’s unlikely to be enough.

                        Does anything clarify whether the mount bonuses apply to sapient mounts? I haven’t seen anything saying no, which suggests that you and your Tyrant Lizard Lunar companion are going to be a pretty brutal combination.

                        Yes: Untouchable Solar Speed (#33) is absolutely necessary to make an Initiative-equipped mount a safe investment rather than a liability.
                        No: Harmonious Tacking Technique (#9). Even with this Charm, it could take you four rounds to get a mount ready to go. Completely outclassed by Whirlwind Horse-Armoring Prana (#28), which does the job instantly.
                        Just Plain Weird: Hero Rides Away (#24) is exactly as weird as it was… what, five years ago?... when it meant that John Wayne gets to dispel limit for riding off at the end of the movie, but Kwai Chang Caine doesn’t.

                                                Sail

                                                Number of Charms: 39
                                                By Ability requirement: 0/0/5/4/30
                                                By Essence requirement: 13/15/11/0/0
                                                Nonstandard dice tricks: Treat enemy 1s as 10s. Reroll nonsuccesses. Free full Excellency.

                                                Themes: You’re on a boat! Get bonuses to your speed, your maneuvers, and your ship’s durability – they can’t stop you, because you’re on a boat. Crush other people’s ships beneath you – this ain’t Sea World, this is real as it gets. Gain various personal “salty old sailor” bonuses – you’re on a boat, and don’t you ever forget.

                                                …

                                                … Sorry.

                                                This will be a nice juxtaposition. In 2e, it was often argued that Sail and Ride were, effectively, one Ability; I’m curious to see how 3e distinguishes them.

                                                At a glance, this may be one of the most lopsided trees so far. Over 75% of the Charms are at Ability 5; none have Ability requirements less than 3. The Essence requirements are uneven in the other direction – nothing over a requirement of 3. So to get much mileage out of this, you want to be all in on Sail – but not all-all in, because it might not be the best choice for Supernal. It doesn’t come through in the description so far, but this is also one of the most compact trees I’ve seen; very few Charms run more than a paragraph or so of text.

                                                Charms:
                                                1. Permanent benefits: recurring 6s on Sail; add Sail/2 to Resolve vs. supernatural fear; recover instantly from a fall; find your way to anywhere you’ve been before.
                                                2. Permanently +2 Stamina for resisting deprivation (food, oxygen, etc.).
                                                3. Permanent bonus: if you wake up on a ship, you can ignore the WP cost to resist one social influence that day.
                                                4. +2 dice to avoid hazards, or +2 successes if you’ve avoided these specific hazards before.
                                                5. Anyone on your ship without permission takes -1 die. You can pull 5m from the ship.
                                                6. Ship’s Speed +1.
                                                7. Creatures of darkness in your crew don’t count as such for the purpose of social magic, your anima, or your area-of-effect attacks.
                                                8. Double-9s or recurring 1s on a Sail roll, after the roll is made (!).
                                                9. Double ship’s Maneuverability and add 1 to its Speed for one maneuver.
                                                10. +Speed to a Conceal maneuver.
                                                11. +Essence dice and +Essence non-Charm successes to a Broadside maneuver.
                                                12. +1 level of damage when ramming the enemy; cancel the enemy’s Broadside maneuver.
                                                13. In a Positioning maneuver, treat enemy 1s as your 10s. Cancel the enemy’s Ram maneuver.
                                                14. Permanently gain X dice to your ship-defending actions, where -X is your ship’s hull penalty.
                                                15. Predict the weather for the next day or so.
                                                16. Ship’s Speed +1 for one day. Incompatible with #17.
                                                17. Double ship’s Speed bonus for sails, but reduce any rower bonus to +1. No bonus for being dragged by sea monsters. Incompatible with #16.
                                                18. Negate all damage to the ship for one turn.
                                                19. Spend 3HL to heal one level of damage to your ship.
                                                20. Negate your ship’s hull penalty.
                                                21. Reroll all nonsuccesses on a Sail action.
                                                22. 1/scene, free full Sail Excellency.
                                                23. Reduce wind-based penalties (are those a thing, prior to now?) by 3.
                                                24. -2 Momentum to the cost of all maneuvers for the rest of the scene.
                                                25. On a successful ram, also shock and board.
                                                26. +3 non-Charm dice to Join Battle on a boat.
                                                27. Double Momentum gained via a Positioning maneuver.
                                                28. Sense danger (Essence) minutes early, apparently whether on a boat or not. Bonus successes to Awareness on a boat.
                                                29. Render the ship and crew immune to the Wyld.
                                                30. Gain extra health levels equal to the ship’s current hull.
                                                31. Commit motes to keep your ship from falling apart at 0 health.
                                                32. Expend your anima to make SWEET BURNING SUN-SAILS that terrify creatures of darkness.
                                                33. +(Essence) auto-successes to fighting a storm for the next hour.
                                                34. Give bonus dice to your fleet, when maneuvering.
                                                35. Permanently enhance your sailors’ stats.
                                                36. On incapacitating another ship with a maneuver, you keep all your existing Momentum (and gain more!) instead of losing it.
                                                37. On incapacitating another ship with a maneuver, gain motes and Willpower.
                                                38. Add your ship’s Speed to your personal movement, and its Maneuverability to your Defense. Speak an ancient ocean language. Become immune to drowning.
                                                Salty Dog Method (#1) is a permanent Charm with more broad utility than many of the Simple effects in other sets. This does not prevent “she immediately falls up” from being a ridiculous turn of phrase.

                                                This is three Permanent effects in a row. Sail would be a great dip, if it didn’t have a five-dot Ability prereq for some of these. SDM is probably still worth the dip.

                                                The base effect of Perfect Reckoning Technique (#4) doesn’t even break even mote-wise until you’ve used it twice in the same scene. The earlier naval combat examples seem to suggest you’ll be rolling enough to make that worthwhile – five times for a storm, ten times for Bluehaven. Not sure I love that, though – the multiplication of rolls feels too much like make-work so an Exalt can’t blow through the problem with one Excellency use.

                                                Is the effect of Ship-Claiming Stance (#5) permanent? The duration listed is “Instant,” which isn’t much help. Can I claim any number of ships?

                                                Ship-Sleeking Technique (#6) adds 2mph to your ship’s speed, with a Charm purchase and committed motes. Woo? Edit: It looks like I was unimpressed too early; Tide Cutting Essence Infusion (#16), at Essence 2, is a more expensive and shorter duration version of the exact same effect.

                                                Okay, Orichalcum Letters of Marque (#7) is actually really cool, if kind of character-specific. Hi, Moray Darktide!

                                                That is… a picture of a really enormous ram at the front of a ship. I rather like this picture – it’s got a swashbuckly “Full speed ahead!” sort of feel.

                                                Immortal Mariner’s Advantage (#8) is by far the best double-9s or reroll 1s effect so far, because you can wait to activate it.

                                                Deck-Sweeping Fusillade (#11) says to “[treat] the result as if it was (Essence) successes higher than it really is.” Don’t do this, game; either tell me to add [Ess] successes, or to add [Ess] non-Charm successes, but keep with the established notation!

                                                Hull-Preserving Technique (#18), i.e., “a perfect defense for your ship,” is pretty cool. I can see some fun uses here, where your vessel just refuses to go down while the enemy hurls sorcery at it – this is something a good Solar captain might go “Heck yeah” at. Hull-Taming Transfusion (#19), which will kill you long before it fixes your ship, is not.

                                                So, uh, how does Ship-Leavening Method (#20) interact with Ship-Imperiled Vigor (#14)? Do you have no penalty while SLM is up, or are you just ignoring it?

                                                Burning Anima Sails (#32) again has an implicit cost of 3a that is communicated only via text.

                                                Storm-Weathering Essence Infusion (#33) is just a strange Charm. It’s another limited Infinite Ability Mastery – this one applying only to storms. But we were told that fighting off a storm all night might require five rolls, and this Charm only lasts for an hour – so you’re going to pay another 6m, 1wp for each of those rolls, right?

                                                Yes: Salty Dog Method is clear and powerful. Burning Anima Sails is going to be a great in-play visual, with several plausible uses – repairing sails, countering attacks on your mast, and roaring into battle against Skullstone or the Lintha.
                                                No: Ship-Breaker Method (#12) and Superior Positioning Technique (#13). Look, game, commit to your design principles. If combat is transparent, then let it be transparent; don’t tell me in exactly two Charms to write down what I’m doing secretly and reveal it post-roll. Just make the maneuvers chosen secret in general, if this is design space you want.
                                                Just Plain Weird: What the heck is up with Ocean-Conquering Avater (#22)? “Duration: one turn?” Applies “to her next Sail roll?” That’s not the templating used for any of the other free-full-Excellency boosters. Shouldn’t this be like Supremacy of War Meditation or Supernal Control Method, which are Reflexive, Instant? Or Awakening Eye, which is… er, Supplemental, Instant? Or Eclectic Verbiage of Law or Life-Sculpting Hands Technique, which are… Permanent…? Or… uh…

                                                … Since we are in Sail, WHO HIRED THESE CHARMS?
