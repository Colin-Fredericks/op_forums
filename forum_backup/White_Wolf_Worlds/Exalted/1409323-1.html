            On Dreams, I think I found it fine at the time, but mostly since I like Neph and think what he wrote was at least interesting. As a book though I think it kind of did a lot of harm to the line. It felt like the Star Wars prequels in a way to me in that it reconceptualized a lot of the setting after it into relation to it, and that wasn't in itself a good thing.

            There were also just parts badly written and those often depended on the specific part. For instnace, in Lands of Creation each chapter was written by different authors and it showed. The South, West, and East were written with this "resent just examples, let players fill in." The North and Blessed Isle were written as "There are X kingdoms, here's who rules them, that's it." This stood out ot me then a lot. I also think that despite the emphasis of diversity, the First Age felt pretty homogenous. It didn't use its 3500 years great I think, but hte nature of the product probably wouldn't have let it. And even some of that didn't work well when you did have good Direction stuff, like having to find origins for various mutants in Creation here (dune people and Djala probably didn't need origin sotries as slave races). Also whoever thought needing basically Lintha True Brujah living on an island was a great idea needs questioning.

            It also I think solidified a few conceptual things of power, the relation of the Exalted, and so on in ways that were both outgrowths of 2e up to that point, and continued onward. We got Essence 6+ Charms that were basically sorcery spells because "Solar Circle Sorcery only went to Essence 5" and so we saw a depreciation of sorcery's importance over artifice that started in Wonders of the Lost Age. The blood purity stuff started in MoEP: Dragon-Blooded continued with the canonization of everyone being legendary breeding and miscongenity with mortals being a degradation, and the introduction of Purity keyword stuff which in hindsight, not a great idea. The population of DBs being over a million also does a lot with reinforcing DBs as failures/fuckups/loosers/degenerate/barely Exalted that 2e was running the ball with too. Sidereals not getting any support initially was also kind of one of those "Wait what?" things. Hell, there was even the start of what amounts to a Wiki editor's war in Guide to Meru where the author wrote how Exalts went crazy when their Incarna died...which then led to something later being written to counter that.

            Something else kind of more subtle to me was that it was a highlight of 2e going a bit conservative on its gender politics. Save for Neph's stuff in the West, literally every single Lunar-Solar pair in the First Age was a man and a woman married or at least implied to be so. This is besides things like generally just bad NPCs (Desus and Lilith were pretty cringy, the pushing of Black Shattered Ice as the Lover was unneeded, being cute on Chejob being exactly one year from dying in modern time, or Admiral Arkhadi being a rapist.)

            Mechanciswise, got not much to say. I think Essence 6+ as a paradim from 1e and into 2e was kind of busted an dthis kind of highlighted that in spades. SOme cool Charms, especially for DBs, though. And the later errata gave us Throne Shadow for Sidereals.

            There's a few good ideas scattered about, note. Some locations had some potential to be lifted, I liked the implicaiton of Gaia having more souls in there, and such. But as a whole, I think it as noted is very much a product of its edition. It culminated a lot before it, established a lot that would build on it later, and in general condified 2e as very much its own thing different from 1e and which 3e was rebooting from for me. I was lukewarm on it when it came out even though I was still pretty much in get everything for Exalted mode. I think I was excited more by people working on it though than the product itself and that I mostly remember positive bits to the parts Neph wrote to me indicates that it was more something I wanted for him writing it than as a product I wanted in itself.

            And stuff.

                                            Originally posted by Penelope View Post

                                            Okay. I was very new to tabletop gaming in general when I read it, so I didn’t pick up on that. What makes it so badly lacking?
                                            Basically the supplement doesn't use the space it has very well and the setting presented feels like the result of a bunch of compromises that didn't really make anyone happy.

                                            When it first came out a lot of Celestial players jumped at the chance to play characters who enjoyed similar levels of privilege and magical artifice to Dynasts or to play absurdly high essence PCs but instead found themselves in a late First Age setting where the greatest enemy was the growing ennui of a world that's increasingly becoming unable to challenge them and themselves.

                                            If you wanted to play a Dragon-Blooded then the books barely talk about them. You're going to have to make up your Gens and its customs, figure out your own answers for what it's like to grow up as a First Age Dragon-Blooded when there's over a million of them because the books give you nothing to work with. It's particularly egregious when you realise we as readers know things about Desus' private life that he would suicide us in order to prevent from getting out and tarnishing his reputation, but we don't know the name of the Dragon-Blooded family that's sworn to him or where he rules.
