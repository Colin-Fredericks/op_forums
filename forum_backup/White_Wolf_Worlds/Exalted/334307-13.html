                                                        thank you for the comment Guru, it means a lot .

                                                        hmmm lets see here, well i think that the silhouette technique might work for you depending on how dark you make them.

                                                        Otherwise one trick that the comic artist that inspired me to start making my webcomic, Fred Perry, often use for background characters is to pretty much only draw their rough shapes (a couple of "internal lines" for things like clothes and armor but no real detail lines), and then fill it with a desaturated dark color (not super dark like black but just a bit darker than usual, blue and purple works well for this, however depending on the background sometimes you can use a slight variation on the background color instead).

                                                        Another trick that might work for you is to do similar to the previous trick and draw the rough shapes, but with a bit more detail and instead of using a desaturated color, you use your "flats" colors without actually rendering them, making them look almost a bit faceless (this might turn out a bit strange though if the background character is to close but it works if they are a bit further away).

                                                        My suggestion is to try with the silhouettes technique at first, just to get a feel on how they work, if they feel ok to you then i suggest that you use them for background characters that are placed "in front" of the characters you want to have in focus since unless they are 100% black opacity they should be a lot less "eye catching" than the "focus" characters.
                                                        They can also be used for background characters that are farther away.

                                                        the same trick can be used for the second technique i mentioned, you can also try to draw a "blob" when it's a tightly packed crowd and then add a few heads to the "blob" trimming it a bit to make it feel a bit more "natural".

                                                        the final technique I suggest using for when characters stand a bit closer, though not for characters that are standing in front of your "focus" characters since that might draw attention away from what you want people to focus on, this technique can also be used on more important characters that are standing farther away though you may want to give those character a bit of rendering.
                                                        Also if it is a character that is very bright like lets say that you at some point draw a scene where someone's being chased by a fire aspect in full anima and you want the fire aspect to be visible but the focus to be on the one that he/she is chasing, i suggest that you use desaturate the bright colors so it become's a bit less eye catching.

                                                        I hope that these tricks will be of some use to you since i know how annoying and tiresome background crowds are to draw and i hope i made sense, if I didn't let me know and I will try to make better sense .

                                                        oh and one more hint is to actually copy paste a few slightly more detailed background characters characters, (maybe mirror them so they are looking in another direction or something similar) and then "merge" them with the silhouette crowd.
