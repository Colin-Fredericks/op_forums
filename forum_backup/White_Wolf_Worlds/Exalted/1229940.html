      Exalted 3e crafting system replacements are something of a dime a dozen. It depends on what you don't like about what is. Some want to chop down the Solar charm tree to like 10 charms, some want to eliminate what they see as 'grind' (integrating a crafter's story as a crafter into the narrative of the game so there's more than just "I feel like making a legendary daiklave even though I haven't made so much as a butter knife on screen"), etc. Have you determined what you don't like about the 3e system?

      V: and yeah, this. Craft in core is designed to reward the guy who looks at every situation and thinks, "what can I make to make things better?". If the preference is "I never craft unless I'm making something that is Big and Important and the Focus Of An Entire Story" it won't work as well for you, so that's a thing to tackle. (I conceptually at least love the idea of it so, I never had a big issue with it)

      Anything that creates an emotional intimacy in someone, gets you a material benefit, or serves one of your intimacies, will get you craft XP. Without limit. Once you realize this, it goes from "I have to make ten thousand stupid things to get anywhere" to "holy crap I am swimming in craft XP by making the world a better or worse place as befits my concept".

          The crafting system's craft xp (silver, gold, white), and the project slots are the two chunkiest parts of the sub-system.

          The craft xp is designed to reward most someone who is always crafting (Always Be Crafting is the base line i tell anyone wanting supernal Craft). Have them think like an engineer or MacGuyver and they will make plenty of stuff and generate plenty of silver xp, and some gold to boot. Also keep in mind repair work counts too

          If you want a person crafting just to churn out Artifacts, then reconfigure how you see the Craft Xp. This is also the material you need to make the Artifact just as much as the creative output. So have the group go adventuring to gather half the needed xp in the form of the needed exotic component, or just buy one or two things via some bureaucracy maneuvers.

          Slots are not as bad, they are how well you can multitask multiple jobs. The bigger the slot, the more of your life it takes up. Making more than one Artifact level project at once seems extreme, but the rules are there for it just in case.

          Its a system built to look terrible in a white room scenario, but great for the MacGuyver crafter who punches rocks into statues and always leaves a town running smoother than they arrived. If you can reconfigure the big stuff into a slow progress bar in the background of your game when speedy and meaty chunks when in downtime. I think you'd be surprised with how well it runs.

              I currently use the system mostly as written with the definition of basic projects here save for a few key differences.
              - Craft Xp is renamed Inspiration because that's less confusing and gives me an excuse to reward a bunch of it to a player who'd been struggling to get involved.
              - The existence of Project Slots is ignored until Superior and Legendary.

              Not a house rule as such, but my introduction/cheat sheet to the craft system only covers Basic and Major because Superior steps up the complexity a lot by introducing Goal Number and Terminus. My new player whose actually using the system is a logistics officer/field cook so actually wants to be MacGuyver or in her words "beleaguered single mother to an entire army" my system priorities would be very different if someone envisioned their character as a hermit living alone on a mountain who made awesome swords for the people who managed to find them.

                      I'll add my take to the list - as others have said, they're a dime a dozen. To distinguish the purpose of my rewrite from others with different goals:
                      This version of the craft system is designed to be small, simple and easy to use. It does not attempt to regulate your play, or insert itself into any part of the game other than actually making things. It does not aim in incentivize certain types of player behavior, only to adjucate what characters are capable of.
