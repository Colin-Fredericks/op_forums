                      I liked the bit set in Wu Jian, since the setting location interests me not just because it's full of competing martial arts 'gangs', but also for the hive-like aspects of the city allowing you to use changing height as an added method of travel, or fighting.

                      But for my favorite piece of fiction so far, I'd have to say my vote also goes to Strawmaiden Janest... It's a very compelling in character examination of the 2nd Breath and just how meaningful it is for Exigents in particular, since in this case the God isn't powerful enough to survive the process, and even with his reasoning that the Wyld horde would kill him anyway, it makes for a noble sacrifice. And that is the kind of thing that one would expect to deeply affect a character, were you to see it in a prospective PC's background. And the threads of potential plot that all this forms in my mind as the story itself leaves us on a cliffhanger, is better than knowing any further concrete knowledge about the character.

                      (Though I still wouldn't mind learning a bit more about Janest in the upcoming Exigents book)

                                                  I love fiction so this is a good topic for me. That said, other than the core, I haven't backed and so haven't read, any of the other Ex3 books so far. Just waiting on them being published and then going to buy. So I can't assess any of the quality of fic beyond the core.

                                                  On the core, I liked the intro fiction, for various reasons I've enumerated elsewhere (and can be found by a forum search), but mainly because it seems like it was the only fiction that really had a good understanding of how to quickly draw sympathetic and archetypal characters with enough nuance and originality to suggest more depth of personality and create interest, and because it was the only fiction that actually managed to very much at all evoke the world of Exalted. Most of the other fiction was very much "This could be happening in any fantasy world".

                                                  The other bright spots I can remember which were *about* something interesting despite failing at execution (the above points of world and character evocation) were Shen's chap fic and Perfect Soul's moment of Exaltation chap fic. Conceptually they were good.

                                                  Volfer's fiction I did not enjoy because it seemed to me to be trying to get me to buy the idea that crass, petty, immature and impulsive reactiveness as something that I would empathise with in its hero, and on an "awesome audacious exploit" that I found particularly lame. I guess it's like Alucard rather smartly mentioned, but for me in a less good way that suffers in comparison - it evokes audacious "sword and sorcery" heroes (Conan, Fahfrd etc) but Volfer suffers a little through comparison by seeming to just not be very cool and perhaps its author not seeming to have a good understanding of what makes a hero or maybe what humans in general cool (in the sense of radiating charisma, control, honor, genuine passion, charm, etc).

                                                  Janest's fiction I found did not move me, as I fundamentally saw it as cynical and didactic - basically trying to upsell us on the idea of Exaltation and Exigency as a positive force in the world of Exalted and in the gameline of Exalted respectively, using pretty predictable tools and conventional writing. It seems like something put together for a purpose that seems too obvious and directed, rather than the product an author who can at least smartly disguise that purpose as a genuine, authentic and spontaneous exploration and reflection of mood, tone, character, etc with no ulterior purpose. (Even if this is age old, and simply feels more of a problem to me these days, I guess I am increasingly harsh on this as a problem of writing in our world. Lack of respect for readers as forming their opinions and beliefs, and a decline of writing that plausibly simply exists for the purpose of exploring the authors own feelings and ideas and genuinely conveying them, without much thought of trying to manipulate the audience, either to sell them something commercially or change their beliefs. If you're trying to do either of those manipulations, well, at least have enough respect for me to bother to hide it!). And again it's got that kind of "This could be any fantasy setting" quality.

                                                  (Apologies of the above is too negative; no one take it too personally or seriously as a criticism of your personal taste).

                                                      (Apologies of the above is too negative; no one take it too personally or seriously as a criticism of your personal taste).
                                                      I think there's a disconnect (between you and I certainly) as to what the chapter fiction is supposed to achieve.

                                                      For me, it's about book-ending content. Breaking up the prose of the rulebook to refresh the mind. Foreshadowing the content to come in the next chapter, or recapping what has just been said -- presenting the dry textbook as a story.

                                                      It's not about being interesting literature in its own right (though it certainly can be).

                                                      I don't care for the WFHW fiction, but it walks us through the story of Dragon-Blooded as the rules present the world more clinically. What the rules tell us, the chapter fiction shows. That's what the chapter fiction (in my mind) is supposed to do, and WFHW does it well (perhaps too well because I find all the characters unsympathetic, insipid, boring, pathetic and evil, and reading the fiction makes me never want to play Dragon-Blooded -- but the chapter fiction does faithfully show us the world the body text describes, and it flows well).

                                                      The point of the Janest story (in my mind, as a simple fan) is to bookend the Core. It's to summize what has been said (you play heroes of the gods against impossible odds), compliment the intro fiction (which with its style and tonal differences, I think it does superbly), and leave the reader wanting more from the gameline (the next chapter, so to speak).

                                                      I agree with all of your literary criticisms (and if the story was less expositional and more nuanced I'd rate it more highly), but I think it's somewhat unfair to level them at a two page bookend. It's like complaining the Mona Lisa isn't as grand as the Sistine Chapel -- you're not wrong but that's not a realistic expectation.

                                                      This is fiction put together for a specific purpose. That's literally what chapter fiction is and is supposed to be. It's not meant to be a genuine reflection of the author's soul -- writing for the sake of writting. It's meant to be an evocative summary of the book.
