    I think 2E's descriptions of The Games of Divinity and the tactical games popular in the Realm (like Gateway) give us the impression that some tacticians are not unfamiliar with top-down tactics.

    I absolutely agree that your standard barbarian state will doing things in terms of scouts and messengers, but the Realm's imperalistic conquests, concern with geomancy, top down strategy games and access to flight (agata's if nothing else) just makes it implausible to me that they've never heard of maps.

    Mapping out the heliograph system if nothing else. That's not going to be done on foot by local guides. And once you realise how helpful maps are at home, you're going to be making them abroad.

    Heck, the Battle of Futile Blood where the Tepet legions were devastated because they didn't know the terrain would make the Bull the Realm's Napoleon.

                        Originally posted by Synapse View Post
                        Even those won't necessarily be tightly bound to material precision. the way that dragon lines cross and spread is what's more important to measure and thus geography without any sensible resonance would be a sidenote.
                        "Points of Light" logic is solid for these mapping paradigms.
                        Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                        Which could always be represented with maps that look more like mandelas.
                        Responding to both since the point is essentially the same.

                        It is certainly possible to represent a lot of the relevant information in an abstract way. Subway maps are like this all the time. But as the case with NYC subway maps demonstrates, there is information important to people that is lost when you do this. New Yorkers didn't like the fact that they couldn't easily determine which stations were near each other or notable landmarks at a glance in the proposed abstract map.

                        When creating a geomantic survey, especially if you plan on doing anything to alter that geomancy, you will need to consider doing things like raise or level hills, plant rows of trees, change the course of roads and rivers, etc. Having a mandala map makes those kinds of jobs vastly more difficult. If you are redirecting a river knowing local elevation is very important if you don't want to accidentally flood something important or dig a trench vastly longer than you need. To say nothing of predicting how the new arrangement is going to affect other aspects of local geomancy. e.g. a stream diverted around a small hill might not cross the wood line on the other side of the node you cared about but may now intersect half a dozen other minor lines.

                                                        Okay, let's talk about other things than maps. Here's some points to discuss.

                                                        1) How are troops raised? How are they paid? How are they trained? Socially, economically, culturally.

                                                        2) How is communication done in a battle, or when trying to co-ordinate several allied armies?

                                                        These are things which have come up a lot in our games.

                                                        For example, once the PCs were defending a city from a barbarian horde. They trained up paramilitaries in a matter of weeks using charms, and built massive walls. But then the enemy cast Heart of Darkness in the city, covering a surprise attack. There was no way to send messages (normally the commander used drums or flags, but sound is muffled), and the troops had no orders about what to do. So they mostly sat around confused until the Dawn personally went and fetched them.
                                                        And several PCs have had big problems raising troops, because most people are farmers or nomadic herders.
