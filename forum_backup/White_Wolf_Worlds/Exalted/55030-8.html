                A bit of a late reply here... I've been busy.

                Originally posted by Kyeudo View Post

                I'm not asking that being an Abyssal gets the Divine Stamp of Being Okay. I'm asking that the books stop ascribing a moral superiority to the state of being a Solar. I'm asking that the books stop considering being an Abyssal to be an offense against all Creation and a state that no one could ever want to be in unless they were a murderous sociopath.
                But... it literally is an offense against all Creation. Spesifically, attempted murder. If you're saying you want that to change, then I think you're throwing the baby out with the bathwater. In my view that's totally core to the entire point of Abyssals. Never mind playable splat, if they aren't inherently inimical to Creation there's no reason for them to exist at all.

                I don't think you (should) need to be a murderous sociopath to want to be an Abyssal, however. You get enormous supernatural power, and you'll live for as long as the world does. Instead of a farm-hand or a soldier or a village doctor, you get to be a world-striding legendary warrior, and drink with ancient sorcerer-kings in obsidian palaces. It has its perks, is what I'm saying. That said, to choose an Abyssal Exaltation over a Solar Exaltation, all else being equal? Yeah, you'd have to be pretty morbid, or pretty insane. But there's that useless hypothetical again, which no-one in the setting ever gets, so it doesn't matter. As a player you're choosing who you want to play, not what you want to be.

                Originally posted by Kyeudo View Post

                You are missing the point.

                The first and great commandment about a playable splat is thus: If someone chooses to play this splat, they should be able to have a fun play experience as that splat. The Abyssals broke this commandment in 2e.
                Well, I played three Abyssals over the course of 2E, none of whom ever stopped being Abyssals, and I had three distinct fun play experiences... I do see your point, and I agree that the 2E conception of Abyssals got a bad, overly restrictive implementation, but don't say that it wasn't possible to have fun with 2E Abyssals, because people did. And I do maintain it was an implementation issue. I want something very close to 2E Abyssals in 3E, but better written.

                Originally posted by Kyeudo View Post
                No other rule of game design or story trumps this one. If the Abyssal story is supposed to default to "Sociopath or Redemptions" they should never have been made playable in the first place.
                «Supposed to default to»? To the extent that they have an iconic story, like Solars can be said to have «conquer the world, and try to avoid the mistakes of the past», it is «kill the world or do everything you can not to.» Which you could call «sociopath or redemption», if you were feeling uncharitable, although the world-destroyer doesn't need to be a sociopath and the renegade doesn't need to ever turn into a Solar. But I've never seen a Solar game «default» to world-conquering. And yes, at the theoretical limit, Abyssals have fewer stories than Solars within their thematic bailiwick. But that doesn't make them narrow in any meaningful sense. While you should be able to do many concepts in multiple different ways, there are some concepts for Solars that won't be workable as Abyssals, just like there are some that won't be workable as Sidereals or Dragon-blooded. Abyssals have their own stories, they're not just a different mode to play the Solar stories in. And it's not a bad thing that not all the Exalted types have equal scope for diversity, it helps give structure to the setting. Abyssals cover a more narrow set of character types and stories than Solars or Dragon-blooded, but no more narrow than Sidereals, and in my view, actually broader than Alchemicals.

                Originally posted by Kyeudo View Post
                The Abyssals, as a playable splat, need to stop punishing players for not falling into the default molds. They need to assume that players will stay Abyssals for the length of their stories and that those stories will be diverse.
                This is true.

                Originally posted by Kyeudo View Post
                The only way this works is if we ditch the idea of "redemption = Solar".
                This is not. Why does redemption have to figure into the story at all, any more than every Vampire story has to be about Golconda? Heck, originally, redemption was presented as a rumor that might not even be possible. That ship has obviously sailed, but I have never understood why redemption is always front and center whenever there's an Abyssal debate. Be an Abyssal, deal with being an Abyssal, focus on what you do. It's probably going to be a story about death, and being cursed, and the relationship between means and ends. But it doesn't have to be a story about the destruction of the world, any more than a Solar story has to be about conquering or saving the world.

                Originally posted by Kyeudo View Post
                Sure, we should address the ramifications of the choice and what if means for the Abyssal who made it - I will fight you with knives before I see the choice go away. They should be constantly tempted to solve problems with death and violence (more so that Solars are). It should be a melodramatic experience and questions of morality should probably crop up a lot. However, it should never be a necessary thing to stop being an Abyssal or else they are not a truly playable splat.
                Why is it necessary? It isn't necessary! The story is about the character, not about the Exaltation.

                Originally posted by Kyeudo View Post
                When I say "traditional hero", I mean the classic "I'm here to save the day and make everything right" hero. If I want to play the Abyssal Lone Ranger or Abyssal Batman, I should be able to do it. I might have to pay a little more and do a little worse than if I used a Solar to make the Lone Ranger or Batman, but screw Solars - I wanted to play an Abyssal and ride a ghostly steed across the pale deserts of the Underworld.
                I'm not sure if you chose your examples very deliberately, but as it happens, both of them make for great heroic Abyssals! The Lone Ranger is the mysterious wandering stranger who shows up, helps the townsfolk, stops the villains -and rides away without allowing himself to make any ties. (In this case, to protect the villagers from Resonance.) Abyssal Lone Ranger is probably a bit less square-jawed,a bit more Man With No Name, and most likely not as careful to always aim for the bad guys' guns instead of their guts, but basically the concept is completely unchanged. And he still rides a pale horse. ;-)

                Batman fights injustice using fear and theatricality as weapons, preying on criminals' cowardice and superstition. The city he protects is a dark and gloomy place. His methods are sometimes vicious, but he restrains himself from crossing certain lines, for fear of going too far. And periodically he has to ask himself if his presence is contributing to Gotham's problems, causing escalation with his war on crime and acting as a lightning-rod to all kinds of psychotic villains. (The critical point here, with respect to the Resonance-analogy, is that there is an element of truth to this, but Gotham is still better off than it would have been without Batman -at least so long as he never fails. This is what allows both the Dark Fate and Abyssal Batman's heroism to be validated simultaneously.)

                Now by contrast, Superman and Robin Hood would be pretty difficult to do as Abyssals. Both of them are idols and protectors in a way that Abyssals really can't be -not without Resonance afflicting the things they are fighting for, and the means they have at their disposal tarnishing the ends they are working towards.

                                    Originally posted by Kyeudo View Post

                                    Personally, I like to think of Resonance as a by-product of Abyssal Exaltataion and something inherent to their being. It's best described as an intuitional reaction of Creation's Essence, actively decying and succumbing to entropy around the Abyssals. It's not that you're actively trying to destroy the World, it's not even that it's some kind of a ridiculous "precausion methode" implemented by the Deathlords to ensure the mission of their chosen, it's simply the effect of two different World clashing together. Like I said- anti-biotics fighting sickness. That's why it's called Resonance- a word used to describe disharmony caused by two forces meeting each other.

                                    Inherently, it has nothing to do with morality of your choice or actions- you simply belong to another World now. Reality sucks and doesn't care and the rules of Creation are what they are- you can be the purest of the pure in heart, but you are now something different that interacts in disasterous manner with the Realm of the Living.

                                    Incidentally, that's why I'm pondering the idea of helpful Resonance. Perhaps Resonance you can learn to limit or control, in similar way that Vampires learn to live with their condition. In my vision, mechanics that could allow you to target it. Or maybe it just works on it's own to help you. I think that when you get down to it, Abyssals have to treat the World as a limitted resource, the same way they treat food, for example. Would you rather have your loved ones eat, or people on the other side of the World, whom you know nothing about? Of course you'll choose your loved ones. So does it really matter that some people die, if you can keep people you care about alive?
