So, it had to have always been there, or maybe Sids couldn't do that in the FA? Or can the Maidens retroactively insert a charm anywhere into a charm tree when they create them?

    Can an Ability have more than one Prayer Strip charm? Can the Maidens create a Charm over Ess 5 if they want? Would all such charms have to have prayer strips?

            Special thanks to Limited Reagent for reporting actual play experience.

            One reason I am not such a fan of the 1e Sidereals book is that while lots of people raved about its writing and the "surrealism" (another You Keep Using That Word situation) of Sidereal powers, from what I hear not many people played Sidereals. One core purpose of a splatbook is to make you want to play the splat, and Exalted: the Sidereals didn't do that. (The paragraph in the Astrology section about how the system is supposed to be complicated, tedious and not-fun because, y'know, Sidereals operate in a bureaucracy is one of my nominees for Worst Gaming Advice Ever.)

            Reports from people who cut through the blither about how Sidereals are soooo weird and difficult and too cool for you make them sound a lot more fun to play. I hope that 3e takes a more straightforward approach to explaining how they operate and how to play one.

                    Originally posted by SuddenThunder View Post
                    Can't believe I'm the first to mention this but but ... really? Are we saying there are Exalted (or other character types) out there who are somehow handicapped by having time to plan? Everyone does better by being prepared. That's more or less the definition of being prepared.
                    This has probably been covered elsewhere, but I'm not sure that's actually where the "Sidereals and Planning" connection comes up. It's not that they don't benefit, but that they benefit more than other characters might. I think the idea comes from how some of their Charms are potent, but in a somewhat limited and often strange capacity. They're not the things you will necessarily want to reach for in every situation. Part of the "planning" idea (along with part of the "manipulators" idea) comes in as a result of thinking that Sidereals may want to steer circumstances toward those limited circumstances to get the most out of those Charms. If they're going for particular chokepoints, it can be beneficial to plot out a basic course in advance.

                    Note that this comes from observation of one who has not played Sidereals (nor even much Exalted, alas) and who has no idea how well it achieves this in play. Just full-disclosure on how much weight to give that.

                                        Pretty much what Coik said, yeah.

                                        Sometimes I think the reason people go so heavily on the "Sidereals work best when they plan ahead" meme is so that they have an excuse to say "and so it's okay that they're sub-par when surprised, blindsided, ambushed or otherwise in situations PCs tend to find themselves in." (Seriously, when was the last campaign anyone had when the GM didn't spring surprises on them?) As a Sidereal player I don't actually find that okay, and to be honest, I wouldn't mind if "planning ahead" got abstracted into something I can pull out on the fly OOCly, instead of forcing me to try to read the GM's mind at all times. (I'm also pretty sure that GMs in general would appreciate Sidereal players not converting every other session into a Shadowrun-level planning session. This has happened more than once to my Sidereal group.) I also wouldn't mind if it stopped being an excuse not to bring their toolkits up to Celestial standards.

                                            Cheating itself as a theme reminds me of the 2e Ebon Dragon, and what a turnoff that character was. "I want to win. Even, or especially, if it means making everything suck, and making every conflict uncool", and the players and fans that character appealed to.

                                            As much as they are about being subject to different rules, Sidereals would be more satisfying if they were sold on being capable rather than succeeding on making others worse by lying to them and hiding information and hypocritically breaking rules they appear to agree to. The characters have to appeal to people beyond certain kinds of dorky, misanthropic, smirking teens who want to play a justified yet amoral antihero. Or leaving this aside, how much can the agents of order really be about cheating and breaking the rules of the system, without this feeling hypocritical and self defeating?
