                The Underworld is first and foremost a place of Memory. The memories of the dead form the land out of the Labrynthian memory materials. Oblivion is a byproduct of the memory of the Neverborn, their many hates and desires to destroy, eliminate, and ultimately die. In this way, Oblivion has a will. Soulsteel is the memory ores of the Labyrinth tempered by the memories of the dead in such a fused way, it becomes truly physical.

                In this way, cities are patchwork things, with era after era combining and overlapping the shape of the land and the architecture. Grave goods similarly come from many era, and so items made from them, like armour, appear just as patchwork. Deathlords and their Abyssals have greater pride than using grave goods for anything but bartering goods and as such are the most concise stylistically in the underworld.

                Due to the history of the Underworld beginning at death of the first Primordial, and being a place of Memory, it is rife with the imprints of many First Age works. These works are harnessed by many in the Underworld, but many are unfortunately not truly physical and as such cannot leave the Underworld, beyond the Bonestrider, an echo of a true Warstrider made of bone and soulsteel. There are currently investigations to truly makes these ancient wonders manifest. The current usage of them in Creation is strictly in Shadowlands, which is why Shadowland development is crucial to the Deathlords strategy and advantage Dea

                Monstrances of Celestial portion are less like cages, and more like coffins or sarcophagi formed of orichalcum and soulsteel. They're very flat in design, until the Abyssal binds himself to it, changing in design to suit the Abyssals personality. The Abyssal can rest in their Monstrance to recover, and the Monstrance is where the Abyssal Shard will return should the Abyssal be slain, the Monstrance reverting to a blank form.

                Every Abyssal shard is marked by a dead soul of the Neverborn, for the Yozi taught them how to corrupt the shards in similar ways to their own. The Deathlords contain the power of a more powerful dead soul of the Neverborn, likely Third Circle.

                All Deathlords and Abyssals wear specially crafted masks for ceremony. Every Deathlord either always wears a mask at all times, or adopts a form that bears one for ceremonies. Abyssals are given a mask when they officially give up their old name, for a new one. These masks are usually made of Soulsteel from Plasmid creatures. These masks meant to always be worn, but most just wear it only for ceremony. This practise extends in the ghost populace, with Ghosts that do not desire Lethe, but do not want their past lives, fetter themselves to new identity with the use of a mask, usually made of grave goods.

                                My Ligier used to be a One-Winged Angel. Ruvelia had the other wing, and they would clasp hands to fly together. They also had a tandem combat style, with Ruvelia swinging the sword and Ligier lifting the shield.

Later, while a freshly-reconstituted Ligier was leading the Demon Exodus across Cecelyne's wastes for the first time, the Lunar partner of that Solar tracked him down and challenged Ligier to a duel to the death. This was in utter defiance of the surrender conditions the rest of the Exalted had just finished negotiating, but Ligier agreed anyway. The Lunar died as well, but Ligier's wing was shredded. He could probably regrow it, but chooses not to in respect for his last truly worthy foe, the only one he's ever met who fully understood the intensity of Ligier's own mourning.

                                The Unconquered Sun's lack of wings was also symbolic, of excellence surpassing tools, and his four arms represent the breadth of his perfection, while the depth thereof is self-evident. With four arms, he could do everything Ligier and Ruvelia combined did. and more - lift both blade (valor) and shield (conviction), offer a hand to an ally (compassion), and still have one in reserve (temperance).

                                    The slain Primordials are truly and utterly dead. But their component souls aren't. In the Primordial War, when the Primordials fell, their souls seemed to fall with them, and nobody had to deal with them again, so nobody really worried about ensuring that every soul had been eliminated.

                                    That's where the Neverborn come from. They are the memories of the dead Primordials, given shape by their component souls, whose primary function was always to define them. Yet, without the unifying, structuring influence of that singular Self, the souls of the Neverborn lose their power over the Wyld and the world. Each of seven souls places different thoughts in the mind of its Neverborn, and with no Will to guide and restrain these thoughts, they become a jumbled mess. It is for this reason that those who come into contact with the "minds" of the Neverborn experience that connection as Whispers.

                                    The first and greatest power of a Primordial's souls is to define their Primordial. It is not something they choose to do, but rather an inevitable product of their very existence. It is by their shaping power that the paradox of undeath, and the Underworld itself, were created. None of them accepts that. They look to the ghosts who have come to inhabit their world, and falsely interpret that since those ghosts remain due to Fetters, so too must the Neverborn be Fettered to all of Creation. And thus, they spur the Deathlords on in their futile campaign of destruction. Some of the Deathlords understand this, and keep up a pretense of service to the Neverborn only in order to maintain their own power.

                                    One Deathlord has intended from the beginning to use their power to defend Creation by conquering all twelve others. Once this is accomplished, they will turn their attention to the Neverborn, using the vast resources of the Underworld to hunt down and destroy the souls that give them false form, and finally seal the defeat of their ages-old Primordial nemeses. Until then, that Deathlord must never speak their ultimate intent to anyone, for the Neverborn hear their every word and see their every action.
