        Hmmmmmmmm..............

        It's my experience that low-xp games' players are really keen on getting those charms, but after 300xp, they often leave large chunks of XP on their sheets because they're not sure what to do with it (except the sorcerers, of course).

        So, in the FFG Star Wars (which in many ways is similar to a simplified Exalted, with range bands and ability/attribute and favoured xp costs) each class has 4 talents (kind of like charms) for 5xp, 4 for 10xp, 4 for 15xp, 4 for 20xp. The more expensive ones are either stacks or better, and require cheaper ones.

        Essentially, I think it'd be fine for xp costs for charms to scale the way abilities do. An essence 1 party could get a charm each a session, while an essence 5 could get one every 5 sessions.

        I doubt they'll go for that though.

        Hmmm... I hope, in the attempt to make things simpler, they do make it simpler than 3e's XP system (with Solar xp based on various rp-stuff, which is a bit complicated).

                                Shaping Rituals seem like something that would need to be massively simplified for this project even if the basics of sorcery itself need minimal attention. Currently they take about 1 page of text each, which is a lot of room being eaten up for some relatively minor character customization (esp. since you can learn multiple).

                                I also don't think, again more for space concerns than anything else, you can just rely on a massive spell list (even in more simplified language, most spells would eat up ~1/4 page each). Some sort of "make your own spell" system seems like a far more efficient use of space. The VtR supplement Blood Sorcery has a complete "build you own spells" guide in 14 pages, including incorporating the thematic differences of the different kinds of blood sorcery in the game, and 6 pages of examples with around 5 per page there. So in about the same amount of space, Blood Sorcery covers the same material for its setting, but includes rules for making up spells which Exalted doesn't in the same space (and keep in mind same space as just Ex3 core, not additional stuff from the other books that fall under more spells or more shaping rituals).

                                        I'm not sure merging them is a great idea.

                                        First, they're not the same. Attack spells don't always use Intelligence (Perception is fairly common) as their base is a direct issue with that premise.

                                        More importantly though, it makes it much harder on meta-sorcery Charms because adding successes to your shaping rolls is now also adding successes to your attack rolls (if applicable). At the moment, if you want to toss a ton of motes at shaping spells fast, and ensuring an attack spell hits hard, it's very expensive because it's two rolls you have to boost separately.

                                        If there's going to be any sort of streamlining here, it might not be bad to steal a page from D&D. Get rid of the roll to hit and have that be a static value, with reduced effect if it's not high enough to hit the target appropriate to the spell (esp. since so many of the attack spells are at least descriptively area effects even if they only target one thing mechanically).
