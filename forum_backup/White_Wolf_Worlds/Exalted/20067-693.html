            Originally posted by Epitome View Post
            Yeah I took too many shortcuts with that example, let me start over :
            [snip example]
            To me, that seems overcomplicated. Requiring a separate Intimacy to be created for every bit of factual information you want someone to believe means the whole process just takes forever and involves way too many rolls. It also implies that PCs would also need similarly bloated lists of Intimacies to cover all the stuff they know.

            I do think most facts are backed up by some kind of Intimacy for most people, but I'd rather lump them together much more. In the ongoing example of the farmer we're trying to convince, the information that "my wife died of disease" might be supported by his Intimacy towards the local healer, who told him what happened. Or, if nothing else, I think most people have an implicit positive Intimacy towards their own decisions and conclusions, so if the farmer determined his wife died of sickness on his own, that's what you've got to overcome.

            I think that there's really two broad parts to convincing someone of a fact. First, you need to make them believe that you believe what you're telling them. That's basically a passive process - if they have reason to disbelieve you, they roll Read Intentions or Profile Character, and try to overcome your Guile. If they fail, they believe that you believe it. If they have no reason to be suspicious (if the information doesn't contradict anything they already know, and they don't distrust you, for example), they may never roll anything, and you don't even need to use Guile. The next step, making them believe the fact, is the active part. This is where leveraging an Intimacy might come up. What's necessary here is that the target has a stronger positive Intimacy towards you (or the source of your information) than the Intimacy that supports what they already know.

            So, to return to the "convince the farmer to attack the witch" example, here's how I'd handle it.

            We know the farmer has a Defining Intimacy of love towards his wife, and believes she died of illness. Step one is to determine what Intimacy supports that belief. Bringing the illness up in conversation is probably enough for a Read Intentions action to uncover this, while a Profile Character action will take longer, but won't necessarily require specifically reopening emotional wounds (which could backfire). We'll assume that he has a Minor Intimacy of trust towards the village healer who treated his wife, and told him that her death was caused by disease.

            Step two is then to either find or build an Intimacy stronger than the one that supports the belief. We could start trying to Instill such an Intimacy towards ourselves, but it will take some work to build that up to the Major level, which is what we'll need. Instead, some more conversation or observation reveals that he has a Major Intimacy of respect towards the village headwoman. That's a better hook. We do take the time to build a Minor Intimacy of trust towards ourselves, though, to support the next step...

            ... Which is to now tell the farmer that you heard the village headwoman talking about how the witch has been cursing people with death that comes in the form of sickness. The farmer might, at this point, use Read Intentions on you, but we'll assume he fails to overcome your Guile, and thus accepts that you're telling the truth. Because of the Minor Intimacy towards you, he's willing to accept the fact that you heard the village headwoman say it, and because of his Major Intimacy towards her, he's willing to accept the fact that cases of "sickness" are really curses sent by the witch, since his Intimacy towards the headwoman is stronger than his Intimacy towards the healer who told him it was just natural sickness.

            Now, the final step is to make a Persuade roll to try to get the farmer to attack the witch, based on his Defining Intimacy of love for his wife, who he now believes was killed by the witch.

                                        The -5 penalty you refer to is mentioned in Instill attempts specifically (i.e. for creating the belief "The World was created by a pink Elephant riding a unicycle"). A strict reading of the rule does not clearly state that you can shove lies into a Persuade attempt in return for taking a penalty.

                                        Following Kelly's reading of the rules, what I would do in your 2nd scenario is have the farmer immediately try to see if you're lying when you tell him the Witch killed his Wife, and give you -5 to your Guile on the farmer's roll for a truly unbelievable claim. Now if you're the greatest liar who ever lived and have a resting Guile of 5 you will still succeed against the mortal's roll with an Excellency, but anyone else will need to be lucky to pull it off. And don't even try to pull the same thing against an Exalted who can Excellency their roll.

                                        If the Farmer buys the lie, his Defining Intimacy is now a legal target for your Persuade action, if not your attempt automatically fails.

                                            Originally posted by Chausse View Post
                                            1st scenario : Tell the farmer the last wish of his dead wife was to kill the witch.

                                            2nd scenario : Tell the farmer his wife was killed by the witch and now he should avenge her.
                                            I would allow neither of these, at least not without some other setup. In both cases, you're trying to use a lie to leverage the Intimacy without first convincing the farmer to believe that lie. That step is critical. If you don't have that, the Intimacy doesn't apply, and the Persuade roll runs into the "unacceptable orders" clause.

                                            Note that the penalty for implausible beliefs only applies to Instill actions, not Persuade. In this example, where it would come up would be if you were trying to convince the farmer that he should trust you (that is, building a positive Intimacy towards you) because you're really his wife's long-lost sister, who she just never happened to mention before. That's a deeply implausible lie, and deserves the -5 to your roll. But if you manage to overcome that, the farmer will have a positive Intimacy towards you (barring him spending WP to resist, at least).

                                            Originally posted by Chausse
                                            3rd scenario : Tell the farmer to kill the witch because the headwoman demands it (major intimacy is enough).
                                            I wouldn't allow this, because I think "kill the witch" is a life-changing task, and thus requires a Defining Intimacy to believe. But if the farmer's intimacy towards the headwoman was Defining, you'd still need to establish a reason for him to believe you when you convey the headwoman's fictional "orders". That will probably require an Intimacy towards you, with the strength depending on exactly how implausible the idea of the headwoman ordering the farmer to kill someone is. If that's the sort of thing she does all the time, Minor will be enough. But if it's completely and utterly out-of-character for her, you'll probably have to develop a Defining intimacy towards yourself first, and even then, degrade the Intimacy towards the headwoman down to Major, at least for long enough to convince him.

                                                            Modeling all lies as Instill actions is not satisfying to me, because not all lies are intended to make a long-lasting change in your interlocutor's beliefs. In the game I run, the Night Caste in the Circle often uses lies less as a way of manipulating people into specific actions -- which the system clearly models as Instill action into Persuade action -- but as a way of keeping people at arm's length. Which, to me, seems like a Read Intentions roll: you succeed on the roll, you Read her Intention and know that she's deflecting again.

                                                            Also, Intimacies represent things that are important to a character, so what if the lie is intended to persuade someone that something is unimportant -- "pay no attention to the man behind the curtain"? It'd be weird to have an Intimacy of "That random nameless merchant we met is just a humble traveler and not an agent of the All-Seeing Eye." To me, that's clearly a Read Intentions vs Guile situation -- the PCs roll Read Intentions to Read whether the merchant's Intention is to sell them tchotchkes or to spy on them.

                                                            And Intimacies are supposed to come in the form of Principles -- statements of, like, philosophical belief -- and Ties. If you have to model every factual belief a character has as an Intimacy, you get massively bloated Intimacy lists.

                                                            To weigh in on the example being discussed, of the Witch and the Farmer's Wife: I'd model that first as an Inspire action -- using the lies about the Witch to stoke a Passion of anger -- then as either a Persuade action playing off that virtual Major Intimacy (if persuading the Farmer to attack the Witch is a Serious task), or Instill a Minor Principle of "I must take my revenge against the Witch", play on his love of his wife to raise it to Major, play on his Passion to raise it to Defining, then use that for the Persuade (if it's a Life-Changing task, or you want to narrow the range of Intimacies the Farmer could use in a Decision Point or something).

                                                            (Plus, it's just weird to me if guile doesn't rely on the Guile stat at least part of the time.)

                                                            (For the record, the specific lie that prompted the question seemed like a pretty clear Read Intentions vs Guile rather than Manipulation + Ability vs Resolve, so I ruled that the character with Judge's Ear Technique rolls against Guile with the [Essence] auto-successes, but doesn't get the other-guy's-1s-are-my-10s effect because she wasn't rolling, which I felt like was a reasonable ruling because automatic lie detection with a pile of autosuccesses is already plenty powerful. It'd be nice to have an official answer, though.)
