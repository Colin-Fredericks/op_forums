It seems like a fitting story that he'd grant the petition that he knows is truly going on in the god's heart in order to teach the wayward Solar a lesson about hubris.

                            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

                            What are you basing that on?
                            That it's hubris to try and cheat death?


                            The comparison to workings makes me think about how that's able to be a much more straightforward action in comparison because the consequences of creating Exalted are greater than that of any working.

                            In a sense of theme and character, mind, not necessarily saying that they're all the ultimate power.
                            That was partially my point.

                            If you want to create magical servants, you can already do it via workings. One of my friends made a whole bunch of western-style dragons; they fly, they breathe fire, etc. He also enchanted most of his (well, her, the character is female) populace to turn them into Mernii, a race of 6'6" pallid magical elf-like people with better stats, some of whom have jet black skin with fiery veins and fire-magic.
                            Another friend's sorcerer made a dualistic guardian spirit who has two bodies with two forms each; at night a small orbiting moon and also a four-armed warrior woman, and in the day a small orbiting sun and also a snarky tall blue male warrior. He's now working on a set of dragon's teeth that he can throw in a lake to produce a swarm of aggressive gribbly monsters who'll die after a few hours, and a sneaky chameolonic monster spirit that can stalk his enemies in the jungle before jumping out and ripping them apart with its claws.

                            If you want to create a powerful servant, or a race of servants, you can already do it in a way that is less awkward for the setting.
                            And if you want to just empower humanity, you're probably better off making huge numbers of people more resistant to disease and aging (as one of my players did near the end of my 2nd ed game with a Craft: Genesis parasite that empowers its host).

                            To create self-replicating power that passes from mortal to mortal (either by inheritance, magic, or sticking their souls into golems) achieves little more than that* for you, but is perhaps more disruptive to the setting, thematically.

                            *Unless the point is to create, in the long-term, way more powerful champions. Which I think is an issue in of itself, in terms of deprotagonising the players (already an issue with, say, 2nd circle demons, and I imagine 3rd circles though I've never actually seen any players summon a 3rd circle).

                            It ought not to be something as straightforward and mechanical as a working, because the setting ascribes higher stakes to it.
                            Definitely.

                            I think some things should be given a hard no if they amount to story and gameplay cul de sacs.
                            Indeed. That's why I think I'd be much more okay with this near the end of the game than halfway through.

                            (Unfortunately, this has led to issues for my successor. I was much more lenient with the players' schemes, aggressive nation-building and workings near the end of my game, as I was fairly happy to end the game with "you win, well done." But they wanted to keep playing, and the guy who took over the game found some of this problematic.)

                                                            Originally posted by Stephen Lea Sheppard View Post
                                                            My operating theory for some time has been that Exalts really shouldn't be able to create more Exalts, but, they should be able to create something new that is to Exalts as Exalts are to gods or gods are to Primordials or Primordials are to... the... infinite roiling Wyld? So:

                                                            Because if the answer to the question "What comes next in the cycle after Exalted?" is "More Exalted, possibly forever," that's boring.

                                                            (And yes, I know there's arguably another version of this that goes Wyld -> Primordials -> elementals -> Whatever the elementals could have gotten around to doing, if they had, which they don't appear to have done.)
                                                            Yep. I've maintained, whenever this conversation comes up, that you're the champion of a god; to make a champion of a champion is weirdly hubristic in the way that Exalted characters might plausibly attempt in-game, but many folks never seem to realize why it doesn't make a ton of sense out of game. Your power stems from the Unconquered Sun, or from Luna, or the Maidens, or whatever; it isn't wholly yours. Exaltation is a gift, and making a new gift out of the old one doesn't really work.

                                                            Still, at your table, do what you think is fun. If you want to kickstart the next turn of the cycle, go bananas.

                                                            As for the Law of Diminishment, while I doubt we'll ever mention that by name in a book, I think it's an attempt to curb the Infinite Exalts issue of "Why can't my Twilight make Orichalcum Castes? Or their own <Twilight Name Here> Caste?", which is bizarrely circular and recursive as an endgame goal, and carries a lot of assumptions with it, like "And they'll all do what I say!", because that has historically worked well in Exalted's canon. It's also a Tolkien reference in a game deliberately devoid of them, which makes me think it's not a deliberate Tolkien reference: You can make something greater than yourself, but you forever diminish yourself in doing so. Sauron's creation of the Rings forever changes and diminishes him. With the Ring, and those it Chooses, comes great power, but without the Ring Sauron's but a shadow.
