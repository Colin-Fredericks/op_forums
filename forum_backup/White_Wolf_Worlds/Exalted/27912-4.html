    So my Resonance conclusions.

    Essentially that in most games I've seen with Renegade Abyssals have the character(s) in question through a shitstorm of consequences from walking away from a Deathlord while dealing with the stigma of being Anathema and a Creature of Darkness.

    Resonance has a tendency to feel redundant and impersonal while that is going on. So my thoughts are that it should lurk in the background and cause the lion’s share of its problems for Abyssals who’ve found safety and acceptance building primarily during the uneventful days until it makes them eventful.

        This makes sense, although it's worth noting that the various personality/behavior mechanics have usually been massively overdetermined if they exist at all. Like, once you determine eligibility for Limit or Clarity or &c, the mechanics put you on a strict path with not a lot of room for judgment calls of any sort. At the other end of the scale you have 1E Sidereals, who didn't have any kind of behavior/personality mechanic at all, just some narrative guidelines for their flavor of the Great Curse.

        What you're describing sounds... actually, it sounds a lot like breaking an Eclipse oath, come to think of it! The Eclipse oaths split the difference there; they have a mechanical underpinning and mechanical guidelines, but they also put a lot of power in the hands of the GM via narrative control for when breaking the oath is gonna rear up and nail you. It isn't a perfect analogy, Resonance isn't so much about "punish you for going renegade" as it is "even if you go renegade, you're still destroying the world, whatcha gonna do about that, my fine friend?", but it sounds like what you're saying is that if Resonance is going to be like that, it needs more granular controls than what it has now, where it is kind of massively impersonal and that isn't fit for purpose.

        I should try and track down my old Promethean books actually, because this is a really great idea for Resonance and it also sounds a bit like what the Prometheans had going (where if they stopped and tried to be normal for too long the universe started rejecting them) and I'm curious if there's mechanical goodness to be gleaned from there as well.

                Originally posted by Murcushio
                a bit like what the Prometheans had going (where if they stopped and tried to be normal for too long the universe started rejecting them)
                Prometheans had two things of that type, which you're conflating a little bit here. Disquiet was the effect they had on people and communities, in which extended proximity gradually gave a sense that there was something off about the Created which would culminate in strong impressions and occasional glimpses of their true nature and appearance, combined with a kind of infection from the concentrated intensity of their Pyros, which would create harmful associations with the character (often a desire to drive them away, but for one group an obsessive need to possess them); Wasteland was an effect the Promethean had on the environment, in which staying in one place for two long would cause one's Pyros to start corrupting and warping the landscape to reflect the elemental association of the character (so Frankensteins would cause increased electrical discharges culminating in extreme lightning storms).
                Originally posted by Murcushio
                I'm curious if there's mechanical goodness to be gleaned from there as well.
                It's got a fairly straightforward "roll resistance against Power Stat" thing (Prometheans have a number of advantages and disadvantages assocaited with their Power Stat along with a way to decrease it, so they've got a balancing act there).

                                    Wasn't sure where else to post this...

                                    The Eye and Seven Despairs in my games took a curious evolution from comedy to nightmare fuel.

                                    The first time I used him started out as a parody of the MoEP: Abyssals version with the Prioress of Bloody Sands persona obviously him in a crude disguise.

                                    Somehow this worked really well for him, because despite having read the book my PCs didn’t treat Star of Dirt and Doubt with the least bit of suspicion because after the ridiculousness of the Prioress they expected nothing from him.

                                    Now the players were Renegade Abyssals who’d teamed up with Chorus at Midnight and helped her get rid of Scar of Uproar who weirdly showed up again despite having been confirmed killed (the Midnight Caste actually ate him it's hard to get more dead than this). So they go exploring Cold House and discover a room with a woman who has recently undergone reconstructive surgery to look like Chorus at Midnight at the behest of her master. They also found Chorus’ Monstance which was crafted in her likeness begging the question of if she even looked like herself or if he’d remade her into the likeness of his old circlemate.

                                    They immediately stopped treating Eye and Seven Despairs like a joke...

                                    Future portrayals of Eye and Seven Despairs have really built on that moment of horror while discarding the silly canonical aspects and play him up as the mad scientist Deathlord.

                                    In the current portrayal he’s known to obsess over cadavers where he spends weeks or months analysing a dead body to learn as much as he can of the circumstances of death and make deductions on who they were in life. Invariably cloning them to observe them at different stages of development and when the subject fascinated him greatly allowing those clones to go out into the world and replace the original because they bring something to the world that was “lacking”.

                                    As a patron he’s disinterested virtually everything, so his Abyssals are allowed to run the show while he occupies the lower levels of Cold House like a monster living in the basement who occasionally emerges to bestow a gift that will aid in one of his Deathknights goals showing that sometimes he does pay attention.
