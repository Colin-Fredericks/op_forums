    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

    Hmmm...

    {looks out the window}

    Nope, factually incorrect.
    Okay, here we go...

    1) "it is possible for groups that attain sovereign power through militant means to then sincerely incorporate the concept of rule of law"

    Who's law?

    If I come to your house, take your things, commit violence upon your family and then say "if you pay me protection money then I won't come again" then I can sincerely commit myself to my word. I want your protection money. I am therefore best served by keeping my word. And if you pay me, then I will keep my word.

    But does that mean I'm committed to the rule of law? What about the rules that you were following before I came to your house? The law that said "don't go into other people's houses, take their things and do violence upon them"? Because I've taken a steaming dookie on that law. As it happens, I've committed myself not to any universal concept of "rule of law", but to the rule of the laws that I have made myself,

    That's what the Normans did when they conquered England. No concern about Saxon law (which was actively, and very violently stamped out). But a strong dedication to Norman law. Hell, the Normans pretty much invented our modern English legal system.

    That's what the Europeans did when they conquered America. No concern for the local tribal laws or the native people. But a deep regard for their own law, Where it suited them. Little matters like not murdering your neighbor and payign fealty to your king still got casually tossed aside when it became inconvenient. And minor notes like not invading sovereign nations get disregarded to this day,

    That's what Russia is doing, as I type this, in Ukraine.

    And that's what happened in Exalted. The law of the Primordials was casually tossed aside and replaced (in theory) by the law of the Unconquered Sun. Except where the First Age Solars decided to imprison agents of heaven inside their Artifacts or to break the fabric of time itself or to steal worship away from their patron. I get that the First Age lore is going to change with E3, but Yu-Shen and the Deliberative have always been portrayed as committed to the laws that benefit them while ignoring the laws that inconvenience them. Just like every other nation on Earth.

    2. "One could actually read whatever "Mandate" the Exalted were given over Creation as being a direct example of such procedures; an assurance that actions and relations between Heaven and Earth are based on cooperation and law, rather than the fear of force."

    I'm not saying you are wrong about this. But I still disagree.

    The Mandate was the Unconquered Sun saying "take Creation, you earned it, go nuts. But leave Yu-Shen to us". It's Exalted's equivalent of the Munich Agreement - a bribe to lay down arms. The tone is 1at was different to 2nd, but when you look at the Gaes on the Mountain Folk and the departure of Autobot, you see a culture of fear in the First Age. Sidereals finally breaking into Lytek's office and wrestling him to the ground doesn't paint a picture of cooperation between Heaven and Earth. The resulting stagnation in the second age as heaven is left powerless without Iggy D or the Deliberative to guide them, also speaks of a tyrannical rule (not dissimilar to how the Realm is falling apart without Her Redness).

    The Mandate is an agreement signed over the bodies of the Primordials by their murderers. The Louisiana Purchase might have been a fair and legal document where France gave the USA dominion over its lands, but these were still lands stolen from the Native American Indians. But hey, who cares about the Indians? Because it's not like they are strong enough to contest the legality. The Mandate of Heaven is contingent on the Primordials (and the Lintha and the Dragon Kings) simply being too weak to contest that Creation shouldn't belong to the hairless monkeys who killed the architects of reality.

    3. "Likewise, not all cases where social systems descend into disorder and violence are a proof that only the threat of force was holding them together."

    Not proof, no. But these things do trend together. Remove an oppressive government from Somalia and oppressive terrorists fill the void. Have hurricane Katrina blow away the cops and watch the rioting begin. Have yourself an Arab Spring and suddenly the Ba'arth Party doesn't look quiet so unbeatable.

    Suffer for an indeterminate amount of time under the Primordials.... bur overthrow them once you are strong enough to over-power them.

    The Exalted didn't win because Truth and Justice were on their side (though, even DC's Superman isn't as close to Truth and Justice as Uncle Conqy is). The Exalted won because they had a bigger stick than the Primordials. They didn't win by calmly laying out how the rule of law will benefit all creatures great and small - from the Behemoth to the Field Mouse - they won through genocide and ganking fetich souls and then enforced a legal system that glorified themselves, protected their human stock and let the rest of Creations sentient (and sapient) beings go hang. And here's the crucial part without the protection that Primordal law had endowed them with.

    This is no different from the Mafia taking away the rights that your government grants you, but instead enforcing mafia law upon you. Or invading a nation because you don't like their system of government and you think your own system is superior (like Nazi Germany did in WW2 or the USA did in Iraq).

    4. "The circumstances are often more complicated than that,"

    Are they? Now, I'm not saying that the Nazi invasion of Europe was the same as the First Gulf War or the Titanomancy. There are always circumstances and qualifiers, moral frameworks and mitigating circumstances. Murder is wrong, but if one murder will prevent ten murders then where is the greater evil?

    I'm not saying that the use of force is always wrong. And I'm not saying that it is ever right. I'm not making a moral judgement.I'm making a factual observation.

    The Exalted killed the Primordials and usurped their rule. Whether this is a just and right thing to do or not, is... not irrelevant as such but it is... not pertinent. They did it because they could. They used force. Fact. Successfully. Fact (outside of Gunstar). You can be Mafleas and attach the moral judgement that this is bad. You can be M-r-l and attach the moral judgement that this was good. But looking at the bear naked facts, the Exalted won through strength of arms. They were more powerful than the Primordials, so they got to make the law.

    The Roman's were more powerful than the nations they conquered, so they go to make the law. The Normans were more powerful than the Saxons, so they got to make the law. The American Secessionists were more powerful than the English oppressors, so they got to make the law. The Union was more powerful than the Confederacy so they got to outlaw slavery. Nazi Germany was more powerful than Poland so they got to gas the Jews.

    The circumstances that surround these events are all very different. The moral issues behind these events are also different. But what is not different, and where I disagree with you is where you think that the circumstances make a different. They do not. The strong make the law. End of story.

    4. that Yu-Shan did not back up the Shogunate probably had more to it than "Terrestrial Exalted weren't strong enough to make the gods do what they wanted".

    Yu-Shen (when it was ruled by the Primordials) did not back up humanity. Until the Solars were strong enough to bring about a revolution. The Mountain People did not want to provide the Deliberative with weapons. Until they were given no other choice. The Western Arbiter of Storms was not desperate to be horribly mutilated... but he was because the Solars were stronger than him.

    There are a myriad of complex reasons why Yu-Shan didn't back Dragon-Blooded rule of Creation. And these are touched upon through-out the Exalted game line. These reasons include Dragon-Blooded and Sidereal violence against the gods. And, were circumstances different, the Dragon-Blooded probably could have gained legitimate backing from heaven without resorting to force (I see a virtuous Terrestrial Circle making an impassioned case before the Unconquered Sun, explaining their case in detail and throwing the Dragon-Blooded host on his Mercy instead of inventing Iris-Bulb Discourse possibly swaying things). I am not denying the existence of compassion or saying that violence is the only way of getting things done. Because it's not. Violence is often a terrible way to achieve your goals (see the Arab Spring example I gave earlier).

    BUT..,

    The Solars casually violated heaven and they got away with it because of their vast powers. If the Dragon-Blooded had the power to best heaven (or casually mind-rape gods with Solar-Tier social charms) then things would be different.

    The Dragon-Blooded's lack of personal power was not the only reason that Yu-Shan failed to back the Shogunate. (And slavery was not the only reason for the American Civil War.) But if the Dragon-Blooded had been more powerful then they would have been able to force cooperation (whether through coercion or by killing all the gods and giving Yu-Shan to a faction more biddable - which is exactly what the Solars did to earn the Mandate of Heaven in the first instance.)

    5. "{looks out the window}

    Nope, factually incorrect"

    Really? You follow Native American law and America doesn't invade sovereign nations? You don't have presidents like Nixon and Clinton who flaunt the laws of your own nation? You don't have criminals who break the rule of law because they can? You are the citizen of a nation that enforces the law without using physical violence to put people into cages or to kill them with lethal injections?

    Or do you perhaps see another country when you look out of the window?

    Whenever a policeman uses a taser, or pepperspray, or a gun, or self-defense training to take a purp into custody they are using physical force. Whenever a criminal comes in peacefully to avoid getting tazed or wrestled to the ground then law enforcement is using the threat of force. Every guard on the prison wall is, at least, using coercion to keep the criminals in the pen. And the criminals stay imprisoned because they are not strong enough to break out.

    I am not attaching a moral judgement to law enforcement. I don't think anyone would want to live in a world where murderers and rapists do as they please. But what keeps the scum off the streets is force. You can dress it up in a pretty blue uniform and have men in wigs write down what kinds of force are acceptable on rolls of dusty parchment but the fact is that violence and threat of violence is what gets the job done.

    The law is self-serving. The law of my nation tells me I have to pay tax, and what plants I can eat, and whom I must pay to take a car on the road and what digital media I'm allowed to copy. These are not universal tenants of justice handed down on stone tablets by a creator as "The Rule of Law", but the edicts of men trying to run a nation.Attach whatever moral rational you want to that. I, for one, would rather a democratic government apply the threat of force to me than a gang of pirates or a cooperation.

    But Exalted doesn't even have that. They discarded The Rule of Law that really was passed on down by the Creator, and what they replaced it with was not a democratic republic but a military theocracy. If Al Queida took control of the USA tomorrow and enforced Sharia Law would that be the tyrannical actions of might makes right or the "sincere incorporation of the concept of rule of law"? I leave that one to the reader's own judgements.

    I may be morally incorrect, but you sir, are the one who is "factually incorrect". The facts are that might makes the law. Whether this is good or bad remains subjective but I don't think you'll find many Lintha who think overthrowing the Primordials was right or many Solar's who think that of the Usurpation.

                                                            Originally posted by Weimann View Post
                                                            I'm not sure I'd call Terrestrials Exalted of the People, either, because that's more Alchemicals to me.
                                                            Alchemicals are the Exalts of the people in the sense of being "made for" and "serving" the people, Solars in terms of being "from" the people and remaining clearly human, Dragonblooded in the sense of being "like" the power of the masses.

                                                            Terrestrials are about rough, unrefined, lowly, plentiful power that comes together and becomes much more than its parts are as individuals, by working together as a people. Elements coming together in harmony is a great symbol for this idea of "low" but plentiful power that works together to be something greater. The power of the masses.

                                                            (They're also positioned as aristocrats, I think, just to show that because your power is like the power of the masses is, realistically, no guarantee that you'll be particularly selfless towards the masses!)

                                                            Dragonblooded as soldier Exalts is really more of a spin off of them expressing mass power, more than it is a limitation or focus to them. (Soldiers are part of an mass army, usually for a state or a culture or a people, while warriors aren't necessarily.)

                                                            I think this kind of power seems actually really hard to actually express through Charms though, which are to a degree personalised and individualistic expressions of flair and heroism, which might be why a lot of Terrestrial Charms don't really seek to embody it there.
