            Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
            My second thought is that Exalted would only benefit from loosening Attributes (and Abilities) for a very specific style of play that isn't the primary focus on the game. It works great in the Storypath system, but the Storypath system is a much lighter system that doesn't require nearly as much mechanical consistency in such matters. Exalted is a very complex game, and keeping it running requires a heavier hand from the rules. That's why Exalted relies on Charms for such things (and 3e has so far avoided Merits doing it). Charm design gives the developers the controls necessary to keep everything working smoothly.
            When the system was shoddy in 2e there were things called "Band-aid" charms made by the community. These were things made in order for the game to continue smoothy despite mechanical hiccups and involved using charm tech to fix problems with the core system of the game. One of these things were free excellencies.

            Eventually someone asked "...Wait? Why are we making these band-aid fixes instead of altering the core or base of the game itself?

            This is what led us to having free excellencies and IAM not being a thing in 3e

            If a base system has problems then charm design is only going to mask those problems. They're not going to go away regardless of good design or them pretending them not to be there. This was something learned in 2.Xe paranoia combat.

            Secondly it doesn't matter if you're using Storypath or not. I know Ex3 and Storypath fairly well. I've done thought experiments and tested such things multiple times. Nothing will break. Just making the fix now means a lot less work down the road.

                                                            Originally posted by Fenrir666 View Post
                                                            Anyone should be allowed to play a towering brute who is slow but terrifying without being the fucking idiot who was stupid enough to take Strength 5/Dex 3 because turns out being slow kills your concept.
                                                            I don't know, I think that's the rub. I mean, Accuracy more or less represents how well one can hit a moving target. If somebody's character concept is being slow but deadly when they do manage to hit, does it not kind of make sense that it's hard for them to hit a reasonably fast moving target? It would seem that referring to one's character concept as being slow would mean acceptance? Otherwise, what does one want out of the concept that they're describing; to be slow, but still manage to hit most of the time?

                                                            Now, if it was a matter of Strength being an insufficient boon to damage rolls, that's probably a thing more for figuring out some way to build up Strength rather than tear down Dexterity. If it's a matter of "Strength is fine, but Defence is so binary that my slow character never actually gets a chance to hit somebody at all", well... maybe there are ways to represent a system in which not managing to hit can viably build up towards getting in a major blow, but I think that's a foundational thing, something that would require the base system to be different from the ground up (and might constitute its own opportunity cost), not something with a quick fix.

                                                            If it's a case of wanting to roll Strength in Accuracy rolls, then I feel as though it kind of becomes a case of playing a fast character, just that their fast because of Strength rather than Dexterity. And if Strength is still calculated in damage rolls, being able to pull double duty seems as though it undermines Dexterity a bit.

                                                            Personally, I wouldn't really feel the loss of the "slow but heavy hitting" character concept, because it has no reflection in reality; as far as I know, there is not one single fighting style that does not emphasize the importance of being as fast as possible*.

                                                            And I know that Exalted is hanging on the edge of verismilitudinous and stylized genre fiction, and that in the latter the image of being strong but heavy is viable, so there's a basis for others to be attached to it and motivation to represent it in a game such as this. I still question the means of doing that, barring being a major system revision.

                                                            That being said, I will cop to one thing; when the prior developers were first talking about their shiny new combat system and how great it was going to be, I was thinking that it was going to account for ways in which momentum (as we were calling it back then) might be built up that weren't reliant on being able to hit your opponent. Particularly considering a lot of the complaints around combat in Second Edition. And I do think that the combat system that we got did develop a number of good things, and was an improvement on the predecessor, but... it still labours under the issue of how insufficient Accuracy against a sufficiently high Defence makes participation a bit of a non-starter.

                                                            Which really goes back to issues that others have expressed with getting some viable fighting out of mortals and animals. Same going in the other direction; that there's limited recourse to being able to take part if your Defence is low enough that you will never not be hit.

                                                            Hmm, yeah, I do sometimes think that Initiative was not really being utilised to its full potential; that the foundational concept of "you're building up a battlefield advantage before you actually get the chance to make attacks on health levels" might have been a basis for implementing means of building up that advantage other than "be consistently accurate enough to overcome an opponent's defence".

                                                            * Well, strictly speaking, it's about being to move with as little tell as possible, which the human eye tends to interpret as speed, but the general principle is the same.
