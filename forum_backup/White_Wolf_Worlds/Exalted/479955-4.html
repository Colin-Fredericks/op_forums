        Originally posted by Colapso View Post
        I have to disagree. This is not a case of genre conventions. I'm all for picking and choosing genre conventions! That's IMPORTANT!

        Certainly if the Culture's genre conventions are in effect, they are more likely to win than if Exalted's are.

        But 'Solars are able to take down battlecruisers with arrows and kill the unkillable' is not a genre convention. It is their capabilities. You can't have Solars unable to do that and still have Solars at all; you are not actually doing the crossover, then, you are just having the Culture find some dudes who happen to glow gold and are an inferior ripoff of the Solar Exalted. Solars are terrifying opponents, and that is not part of the genre conventions, but flat-out what they are and how they do things.
        But Solars being able to potentially do anything does not and never has been actually the case, and even if it was, the vast majority of Solars do face limitations of everything they can do. While theoretically yes a Solar could shoot an arrow which breaks the laws of physics so hard that it kills a GSV, that's actually never going to be the case in a game, where the amount of dice you can roll and successes you can get is limited to how good you happen to be at a thing.

        Genre incompatability is a thing. Exalts are magic, and magic isn't real in Culture-land, if it was, they'd have mastered it. Compare the two and you have to decide who is right, are Exalts awesome heroes who can indeed overcome any boundary and because this new boundary has presented itself, they will, or are the laws of physics rather more inviolate than that, sorry, each cell in your body was just given it's own private square foot of land within the heart of a nearby star. This is not hyperbole, btw. This is what the Culture can actually do, with their tech. Not that they would do anything so flashy and glorified, especially if it was in the act of killing someone (if you are going to kill someone at least have the good grace to do it quickly and efficiently and without trying to look impressive).

                The concept that the Culture's genre conventions won't allow a Solar to kill a GSV is ridiculous. In the Culture universe GSV's have been destroyed in conflicts with other powers. Thus fundamentally, a Solar is capable of destroying one as well.

                Now, this of course requires the hypothetical Solar to be a crazy powerful elder Exalt, but I believe we've seen enough charms to make a combo that could in fact do the task. The Solar could even GET it destroying all backup copies of the onboard Mind killing it permanently.

                What this is failing to point out is that while the Solar was activating the combo, said Mind was scanning him, gaining a fundamental understanding of Essence channeling, successfully calculating the Solar's Essence pool and the cost of the combo, as then transmitting all said data back to the other Minds of the Culture. And if the Minds decide to go for a round 2, it will be with an entire fleet calculated to be large enough to mote tap him.

                The fact that a Solar could kill a GSV has no real baring outside of that single tactical battle, and in no way undermines the Culture or its genre.

                Ultimately, what would determine the result of a long term conflict would be determining the actual reason of said conflict, as well as the size of the force on the Solar side of the conflict. The Culture has a overwhelming numerical advantage in numbers compared to basically every form of Exalted we've seen. But at the same time, the Culture has an overwhelming desire to not actually harm the Exalted Host if at all possible.

                            I was thinking about considerations for what makes for a "wealthy" peasant, or even a well-payed one (since one of the points of post-Black Death Europe was an across the board increase in wages and lowering of prices), and it got me thinking that depending on what one's definition of "post scarcity" is, the First Age as it has always generally been hinted or portrayed as being would qualify. If it means "there is an abundance of raw materials and such an efficient means of processing and transporting them, as well as a sufficient variety of ancillary services that lots of people can have jobs and a general social ideology that people deserve to have the means to determine their own lifestyle and generally have a decent quality of life", then I would say that at its highest points the Realm would have qualified. I don't think it would have been generally difficult for a network of interdependent societies to which the Exalted institutionally contribute to have been such that no matter what your lifestyle or occupation, you could have had a spacious living area filled with a variety of manufactured goods and an abundance of food, and could spend nights or days visiting and contributing to centres of culture or entertainment, and even if there weren't really jobs for you it wasn't difficult to go out and reap food from the land and live in communities where all of those kinds of things are kind of shared communally. I'm not saying it's trivial, just that I think Exalted would be really good at harnessing the limited resources of the Earth (and manipulating the essential substance and the gods who oversee it) to create such abundance that, combined with the reduced need to harness massive amounts of human labour in tedious and dangerous tasks, people didn't really need to live in poverty as we would recognise it. I'd see the major logistical concerns of their society as maintaining all of the cultural expressions and sacrifices and geomancy necessary to power the magics that would make such abundance and processing and transport possible.

                                            You could probably model physical exhaustion through the initiative system, especially if you expanded the combat time. So normally one action takes a few seconds, but now one action represents a minute or even an hour, and you simply assume that your and your opponent have been engaged in a furious, equal struggle, and these actions represent unique moments where you can get a major advantage over them.

                                            In such a situation it might be reasonable to require rolling stamina + resistance, and as you fail it drains your initiative, or otherwise inflicts penalties to the point where you're too tired to fight back (I did something like this back in First Edition to model an epic battle which was supposed to take place from sunrise to sunset).
