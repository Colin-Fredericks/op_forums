            The Ebon Dragon lies. But most of all he lies about his nature.

            When the soul knives descended to change the Primordials and make them into things that could be imprisoned, the Dragon's Shadow volunteered its souls to be the least changed... and from the inky blackness emerged the Ebon Dragon all know. But he is not truly the Yozi. Rather, he is the Third Circle Soul to the Ultimate Darkness, a Yozi born of the unknown that lurks outside of the firelight. Of primal terror and potential. Diminished from his imprisonment, the Ultimate Darkness found the secret to escape his Malfean prison long ago... he must die and be reborn as an entity that did not sign the surrender oaths.

            But herein lies the catch. The Ebon Dragon is the fetich soul to the Ultimate Darkness, and unlike his progenitor, he fears the loss of identity and recreation into something new that fetich death would bring. While it would bring escape, what escaped would no longer be the Ebon Dragon, and he finds no difference between that and Oblivion.

            Thus the Ebon Dragon's plan is to let himself be summoned into Creation, there to find the secret that let Granalkin, 3rd Circle Soul of Mardukth, escape the ties that bound him to his progenitor and join Luna in rebellion, surviving even the Primordial's death and rebirth into a Neverborn.

            But were that done, the Ultimate Darkness' plan to escape Malfeas would never be complete, without a fetich to sacrifice.

            Thus does the Dragon betray itself.

                    Solar Exalted have 5 major stages to their lives, largely corresponding to how deep under the influence of the Great Curse they are. Solars have only lasted long enough to observe this pattern once, but since there were 300 of them doing so, it paints an accurate picture. It varies, but it roughly coincides with:

                    1- The Solar is a normal human granted the powers of the gods. This power hasn't really sunk in yet, they're still focused on smaller concerns than a Solar probably should.
                    2- The Solar has gained massive influence, and his goals are similarly lofty. He's starting to limit break on occasion, but for the most part he's still relatively sane and heroic.
                    3- The Solar's sanity starts to slip away. The Solar finds it easy to justify evil actions. It's okay for a little collateral damage - after all, the first age solars reasoned, the mortals who die in pain and horror because of a Solar are still serving the Solars, which means they'll get a luxurious life in their next incarnation. And that giant monster WAS a pretty big threat...
                    4- The Solar's excuses have become less plausible and less justified. This is when the Solar loses regard for the well-being of others, and begin to lose respect for anyone else's power or will.
                    5- The Solar has become an utter monster that does not even attempt to justify their actions anymore, acting on their every whim without any regard for anything else or anyone else. This was the evil so horrid that Sol turned his face away, the monsters so perverse that Kejak never doubted their removal was necessary, the acts so monstrous that even their chosen Lunar partners were glad to watch them die.

                                            The various Shards of Creation all exist at the same time--Canon, Modern, Heaven's Reach, Gunstar Autochthonia, and Burn Legend. They are basically impossible to travel to, however, being extremely divorced from each other by the Wyld to the point where no amount of traveling allows one to reach another. However, those who stray too close to the Wyld end up seeing visions of the other Shards. Every 50 years or so some sorcerer in the Realm returns from a trip ranting about these visions she saw of a Creation where steel buildings reached the very skies while strange metallic boxes on wheels clogged the streets...in the meanwhile, a Voidfighter Dragon-Blooded dreams of a time where the Exalted host slew the Primordials and erected a grand empire whose glories were infinite. And what of the young girl in a Meruvian university, dreaming during a lecture about a time where golden heroes walk amongst the gods and elementals...and filled with the unshakable feeling that this is no dream?

                                            If one allows travel to the other Shards, then things get very interesting--imagine a story where Exalts from canon Creation manage to get into the Gunstar. The Gunstar Realm would be excited to hear from their peers who actually managed to defeat the Primordials, and have their hopes bolstered by the glories of the First Age they will have if they can return to the Spiral and win...but at the same time, learning about the Usurpation, the Great Curse, the Neverborn would leave them mighty paranoid. I can imagine the canon-Creation Exalt talking about the Usurpation and all eyes immediately falling on the Gunstar Sidereals, filled with intense worry.

                                            One alteration I might make is that Heaven's Reach is actually canon Creation after the Solars have managed to fix everything and erect a Second Golden Age, at which point they decide that Creation isn't enough, and aim for the stars themselves.
