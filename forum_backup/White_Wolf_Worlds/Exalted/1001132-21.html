                                            Ok. It appears most of you want to deal with Masanori the bandit-king first. However, many of you brought up the idea of talking with him first, as opposed to marching in and taking names.

                                            Interesting

                                            -------------------------------

                                            After sleeping on it, you decide that focusing your efforts on this bandit-lord Masanori would be the most worthwhile use of time and energy. It would solidify your position while giving you an opportunity to view your troops in combat, and make Jianghu safer in the long run.

                                            After all, you can’t really attract new settlers, or expand your holdings, if they can’t have at least a semblance of security. But….. how to deal with the bandit-king?

                                            From what it sounds like (and it isn’t much), Masanori already has ‘several hundred” bandits following him, as well as a comparatively-well-fortified position. You only have several hundred troops (professional troops, that is) yourself, so you won’t have the benefit of numbers, which is something you understand, even as unskilled in warmaking as you are. Add in the benefits of a fortified position, and he could very well have effectively more soldiers than you do…… and the longer you wait, the more bandits and neer-do-wells will likely be attracted to his banner.

                                            No, you don’t have time to wait, not for prospective recruits to be trained, much less show up. You have to deal with this problem now, before it has the opportunity to grow any more. But the question still remains of how to approach the issue….

                                            A direct assault on Dun Nashuon is largely out of the picture. Even you know that marching a bunch of men through difficult terrain, much less the home terrain of the opposing force, is a bad idea. Hit-and-run attacks on your army will cut it to pieces long before you reach the hillfort, and you need numbers in order to run an effective siege. Coupled with that: logistics. There are no roads to Dun Nashuon, and your troops would have to carry everything with them: food, materiel, etc. That would slow down the marching even more, and make them more vulnerable to skirmishers.

                                            Of course, you could call up your new militia, and have them cut a road through the Akibara while your Men-at-Arms provide security….. but that would take a long time, time that you probably don’t really have. Summer is halfway over, leaving you with only about 4 weeks for meaningful military maneuvers, and it will take at least a week to gather the army as it is. As much as you want this bandit-problem dealt with, it probably isn’t worth having crop failures and angry peasants. You just got here, you don’t want to mess up that badly just yet.

                                            However….. you shouldn’t really leave such a strong defensive position ‘out in the open’, so to speak. At the very least, you shouldn’t just ‘let” the place be occupied by bandits. You are going to have to take the point eventually, preferably before it gets built up any more, but how?

                                            You could…. call in the Tribes? While you don’t have any actual authority over them, they are messing with your people, actions which were clearly proscribed in the peace-treaties. In return for you …..’overlooking’ those actions, they could help you out in return. While the forests of Akibara would be a death-trap to your own troops, it is the territory the Tribes excel in fighting in. They would have no problem engaging, much less wiping the floor with, the bandits. Plus, the fact that the bandits are occupying an ancient Hill-Tribe ringfort would be reason enough for the Tribes to call for blood. Tribal ringforts are/were almost-always ‘multi-purpose” structures. Defensive strongpoints, yes, but also the seat of tribal chief-kings, manufacturing centers, religious sites….. and burial grounds. You would be willing to bet a substantial supply of silver that the bandits in Dun Nashuon are walking about but a few feet over the head of untold numbers of buried barrows. And the desecration of burial grounds is a big “no-no” in Hill-Tribe culture (and probably in many cultures, come to think of it), especially by the Vanehani. During the establishment of the Hundred Kingdoms, the Vanehani often went out of their way to destroy Tribal burial grounds and sacred spaces. Of course, the Tribes would probably want Dun Nashuon back in their hands, if that were the case……

                                            You sigh, rubbing at your temples with the heels of your palms. It is still before 9-Bells, but you already have the beginnings of a massive headache brewing. While some of that may be the results of your concussion, you are certain most of the headache stems from stress. You haven’t been sleeping or eating well (having half your teeth knocked out makes eating painful, and you haven’t had much of an appetite anyways) since you got to Jianghu, and you are fairly certain you have lost weight. On top of that…… your hair is falling out. It does that anyways, of course, molting in spring and autumn to make way for warm or cold-weather coats, but not in summer. Nevertheless, you keep pulling mats of hair from your head, twisted tangles of tan and grey hairs that itch until they are removed.

                                            Pulling out another chunk, you examine it sullenly before flicking it out the window. At this rate, you are going to be bald by years end.

                                            No, the ‘best’ choice is to take out Masanori directly. However, according to both your retainers and the reports of the peasants, Masanori never actually directly interacts with the people he robs, instead acting through go-betweens and subordinates. Hell, nobody even really knows what the man looks like! In order to assassinate someone, you generally have to have an idea of who the target is…..

                                            On the other hand, his actions do not sound very “bandit-y”, for a lack of a better term. Most bandits are out for loot and excitement, and when they lean on villages and such for supplies, they tend to be “all or nothing” affairs, coupled with bloodshed and mayhem. Masanori’s crew, on the other hand, has been comparatively well-behaved, only taking what the farmers can spare and treating them with relative grace and gentility, with what boorish behavior that has occurred being punished (brutally, apparently) in short order. Maybe the man has a sense of honor?
                                            At the very least, it is readily apparent, even to you, that the bandit-king views the farms along the edges of the Akibara forest as ‘his’. Asides from regularly extorting food from the farmsteads, he also protects them from other bandits. If anything can be said about his actions, it is that he is doing a pretty good job of it: bandit attacks, other than Masanori’s crew of course, along the reaches of the Akibara have declined precipitously. The farmsteads report that they haven’t seen bandits other than Masanori’s in months.

                                            Generally, most bandits take poorly to organized leadership, even the ones that stem from army deserters. If Masanori is able to beat not one, but multiple bandit gangs into compliance, what does that tell you about his organizational abilities, or his force of charisma? Bandits….. tend to not “play well” with others, especially other bands that intrude on their turf. If this guy was able to get several bands to work together towards a common goal……

                                            …. He sounds like someone you would like as a vassal, no lie. Leadership ability, apparent motivation towards gaining authority, the only thing you don’t quite recognize is his warmaking skills…. And you suppose you are going to see them soon enough. If he could be recruited, and is actually loyal, you could get not only an effective ally, but someone who knows the bandit-situation in Jianghu inside and out. Regardless, he is dangerous, and should be removed from the situation in one way or the other.

                                            …. You are gonna have to meet the guy to make any sort of decision along those lines. Of course, he actually has to be made aware of a meeting, as well as actually want to meet with you in the first place…..

                                            You rest your head on the desk for a moment, trying to collect your thoughts, then arduously begin writing various letters. This takes up most of the morning, and it is well after noontime before you call Blue-Pines and several of your guardsmen into your office.

                                            Turing to Blue-Pines, you hold out the first of many letters.

                                            “Blue-Pines, you have a very important job, with many aspects. Firstly, I want you to travel down to Teriake Castle-Town and place an order for bulk armaments. Secondly, along the way, I want you to pass along the word about my recruitment rally going on, here in Jianghu. Try to get some people to come up here. Then finally, I want you to scoop up the architect Satoshi Azuchi, from the architects guild. He is apparently supposed to come up here and build roads and suchlike. “

                                            “For expenses and the ordering of armaments, I am giving you two Dirham in silver. Try to focus on cheaper stuff like spears and shields, but if you can order some helmets or arming jackets too, that would be nice. I would love some crossbows, but they tend to be expensive. Other than that, buy what you think is best.”

                                            She nods and takes the letter, “What is this mark, right here?”

                                            “That is my seal, as Lord Jianghu. That will get you through any post-stations without much hassle. Take the cart and some horses, along with two of the guardsmen, you will make good time.”, and she nods again, stepping back.

                                            You turn to the rest of the guardsmen, “The rest of you are to ride across Jianghu, spreading the word about mustering. I want the army, both the Men-at-Arms and the new militia, to muster at the market-village of Liangzhou as soon as possible. Along the way, drop off these letters at the farmsteads along the margins of the Akibara forests, with instructions to give them to the bandits when they appear. Any and all farms being extorted are freed from the next harvest-rent, until this bandit situation is resolved.”

                                            Turning to the final guardsman, “And you are to travel directly to the estate of Shin Takeshi. I am asking him totake this letter north, to the settling Hill-Tribes, and present it to their elder. The letter has other instructions for him, as well.”

                                            Sitting back, “all right, let’s get to work. We all have plenty of work to do…”

                                            And so it begins. After a few days of waiting for the letters to be delivered, you head out, travelling west downriver towards Lianghzhou. Due to your stopping at the manor of every retainer you pass, it takes a little over a week to make it to the outskirts of town. You are pleased to note that a decent proportion of your Men-at-Arms and militiamen are already encamped on the outskirts of town, drilling under the watchful eye of Sergeant-Major Oxblood. Even from your admittedly-unfamiliar perspective, the troops seem to be “getting in step” rather quickly, even the militia, although they are far from ready for battle.

                                            In camp, you meet back up with several of your Oathsworn retainers, the ones you talked to on your way here. Gathered around a trestle-table set up in your encampment, you key them into the plan.

                                            Taking a deep breath, “I am planning on meeting with Masanori here, in Liangzhou.”

                                            The table explodes, and after a minute of chatter, Aya Cho interrupts with a wince, “My Lord, is …. Is that wise?”

                                            You shrug, “I would rather meet with him, try to work out his motives, as opposed to charging into the Akibara with my dick swinging. I’ve done enough of that for one life.”
