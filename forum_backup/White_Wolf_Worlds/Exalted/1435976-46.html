        I do tend to think that a "traditional" prayer ritual makes the most sense as Performance, although I'd certainly allow a player to use Presence if the prayer doesn't seem very performance-like (e.g. not done with, or in front of, a bunch of other worshipers) -- Smoke Ascends to Heaven works with any social ability.

        But I also agree that other Abilities make sense for gods with different tastes. If you're praying to Five-Metal Tang, God of Swords, you could roll Charisma+Performance while doing a sword dance and dedicating your recent victories in his honor, or you could roll Charisma+Craft while forging a blade and inscribing the legend of Tang's former wielder into the metal. (I could imagine a sufficiently cool series of stunts leading to a Charisma+Melee prayer roll during a duel, but it'd have to be a pretty serious fight.)

        I kinda wish there were more prayer Charms for other Exalted types. I thought the special prayer rituals in Sidereals 1e were pretty cool.

                                I would also note that while those descriptions do make Intimacy development largely a matter of player discretion, there's also the sidebar on page 172 talking about how a Storyteller might make rulings on Intimacies if a player is just plain not acting the character in a manner consistent with them.

                                That does actually raise some questions about the applicability of enforced Intimacies that can't be degraded. Like, a Defining Tie of hunger for a person is the kind of thing that a person is supposed to act like they're passionately motivated by at all times. Yet, if it can't be voluntarily weakened and the Storyteller probably ought not to deliberately weaken it, is there any consequence for just not playing it? I suppose that must be expressed primarily in terms of the mechanical implication for social influence, and that otherwise one might play it as "the character is trying really hard to keep it in check".

                                Still, that probably gets into how Intimacies and the social system in general are resting largely on a contract with the Storyteller.
