                Originally posted by Lion Hamster View Post

                There's nothing wrong with mixing sorcery with other archetypes but there's a reason it's always a diplomat sorcerer or a warrior sorcerer or a craftsman sorcerer not a sorcerer diplomat or a sorcerer warrior or a sorcerer craftsman

                I'm not 100% sure I'd agree with that statement, though I can see from your posts where you'd come up with that assertion. I think it's more a mindset thing than a game problem, personally, as if it were a universal game issue you wouldn't have so many counterpoints to your assertion.

                That said, I've always seen Sorcery in Exalted as an... envelope, of sorts. It sits over and covers everything else you are, by virtue of what it is. You're now a Sorceror. You deal with strange powers, weird creatures, and terrifying magic. If Sorcery is any significant part of your concept, the world treats you like a Not Normal Person. Now, your assertion is largely that the mechanics don't back this up, as I understand it. That the "optimal" path for a Sorceror is to use Sorcery as a supplement. I'd argue that Sorcery is a path that requires creativity, a willingness to do things in nonstandard ways, and a change in mindset to really come off as truly the path of the Sorceror.

                Will you be more mechanically effective than the guy who buys three spells and spends the rest of his XP on something else? Maybe, maybe not, depends on how you use them.

                                                    Originally posted by Lion Hamster View Post

                                                    There's nothing wrong with mixing sorcery with other archetypes but there's a reason it's always a diplomat sorcerer or a warrior sorcerer or a craftsman sorcerer not a sorcerer diplomat or a sorcerer warrior or a sorcerer craftsman
                                                    In my experience, this isn't true.
                                                    For most sorcerers in the early-to-mid game, and some sorcerers in the late game, sorcery is their main thing. It's just so dramatic and obvious.

                                                    For example, in one game I play we have a Sorcerer Queen. When you think of her, you absolutely think "sorcery". She has a bunch of summoned demon advisors (Alveua, Florivet, Makarios, and that spite-dragon woman I think?), invulnerable bronze skin, rides a dragon she made with sorcery, and her most iconic combat power is Death Ray. She rules a magical kingdom of people she heavily modified with sorcery. Secondarily, she can parry blows well, ride really fast, stab people reasonably well (but not, for an Essence 5 Solar, particularly well), roll decent but not amazing social attacks, etc.

                                                    In the game my friends play, the Twilight is a sorcerer/doctor. He does have a lot of medicine charms, but his primary thing is sorcery, and they live in a giant floating castle with its own Sun that he made.

                                                    In the game I previously played, we had two sorcerers. The twilight sorcerer was a sorcerer... noble? Architect? I'm not sure because sorcery was the more obvious thing, to me at least.
                                                    My own sorcerer was a sorcerer/shapeshifter. I'd say those two things were roughly even.

                                                    Now, in the late game, I did find we suddenly had a rash of people with sorcery as a minor focus. But that's because in the late game, people have spent a ton of XP on their main thing are looking for extra bits. Of course, they're minor.
                                                    For example, the Appearance-charm Lunar > the Appearance-charm/kung-fu Lunar > the Appearance-charm/kung-fu/territorial Lunar > the Appearance-charm/kung-fu/territorial/sorcerer Lunar.
                                                    And the Crafter > Crafter/Doctor > Crafter/Doctor/Thief > Crafter/Doctor/Thief/Swordsman > Crafter/Doctor/Thief/Swordsman/Sorcerer.

                                                    Essentially, every focus is something you can't focus 100% on, or you'd have a very boring character (and generally, mid-game, you'd run out of charms to get anyway, unless you're a Crafter). I mean, if your swordsman has 20 melee charms and nothing else... man, that's dull.


                                                    Now, I do kind of agree with you that sorcery may not actually involve a lot of charm slots. My Sorcerer/Shapeshifter had a lot more knacks than spells (maybe 14 spells and 21 knacks? Something like that). And it would be nice to have more spells (and we'll get a few more on Wednesday!).

                                                    But sorcery has greater thematic weight than other stuff, because it's so dramatic and flashy.
                                                    Similarly, I think my friend's Twilight character Lao has more Medicine charms than XP put into spells/workings, but since sorcery is more dramatic than medicine charms, it forms a larger part of the character (I think, I haven't played the game. But everyone tells me he's a sorcerer, he does all these wacky workings, and also he can heal people which is why they keep him around despite all the dodgy evil magic he does).
