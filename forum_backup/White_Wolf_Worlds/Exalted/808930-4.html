    Something Grabowski once wrote about that stands out to me was the idea that part of why the First Age didn't industrialize is because it wasn't based on the need to organize large numbers of people to accomplish important tasks. Something I take from this is how so many pre-modern societies engaged in various forms of mandatory labour for the people living within them as a necessary means of constructing large scale monuments or infrastructure, the likes of which tends to make the name of a lot of those civilizations. It gets me thinking of the alternatives, which generally comes to me in a form of "a Solar, Lunar, a few Dragon Blooded, and a relatively small elite work force trained and coordinated by the Solar, equipped with superior tools and supplemented by a bit of sorcerous aid such as bound demons, can manage the work of building a continent spanning roadway by themselves".

    From there, you're creating a social and economic premise sufficiently different from the real life circumstances that promoted industrialisation (a lot of which had more to do with adaptations for imperialism and land enclosure than the invention of the steam engine, which I assess would have amounted to an irrelevant curiosity for the wealthy and intellectual in any other circumstances) that it isn't difficult to find ways for people to operate without the need for large amounts of automated machinery to change the work dynamic.

    I still picture things like factory-cathedrals existing, but more as the kinds of facilities you need to produce the custom parts for a spacecraft (which I'm pretty sure was what they were generally supposed to be anyway).

        I've been thinking a lot about Lore and its power to change Creation. There have been a few mentions of how crappy Tome-Rearing Gesture is, and that made me think about books a little more and why they're so important. It's been ages since I last read it, but Neal Stephenson 's Diamond Age explores the power of "The Young Lady's Illustrated Primer," a sort of book that teaches everything from basic literacy, self-esteem, and critical thinking to more advanced lessons and values, and which some desire as a powerful instrument of revolution. And of course we have real books that some people say have changed their lives and that have overthrown cultures and leaders. Further, there's a lot of debate over what children should learn, how they should learn it, and who should teach it, and not just from the standpoint of how to optimally get knowledge into brains. Finally, a lot of the change discussed in this thread is only helpful if people accept it (whether that means leaders allowing change or common folk permitting it in their lives) and learn how to benefit from it - technology and infrastructure aren't worth much sitting around unused. Or worse, the Solar might create something other than what she intended - her system of roads might further enrich and empower the Guild, for example, spreading their abuses to new corners of Creation, while others don't take advantage of the opportunities faster, safer travel can create.

        So education, particularly through books, strikes me as one of the greatest powers of the Solar. She can come up with a glorious, ennobling philosophy. She can collect lost lore and blaze new paths. From there she has to spread these beliefs, with people and books as her primary means more often than not. This is where I find Tome-Rearing Gesture to be interesting - in some place near the edge of the world or troubled by a pocket of Wyld, she can pull out her great work and let her beliefs take root. Of course she could have had books printed up and bound before she set out, but hey, you rarely know where you'll end up or what you'll find. Anyway, whatever improvements the Solar envisions, she needs to send people out into the world armed with her ideals and her words so that people can learn to take advantage of what she has made and also to build on her achievements.

                    I wouldn't say something as powerful as the Primer ideally suits Creation anyway - a book so powerful that once it's out there in the world you just roll credits and assume humanity is forever changed is a bit much even for Solars. But a work that similarly aimed to motivate, enlighten, and teach makes sense. It wouldn't have every technique for building or farming or fighting disease known to man, but it would give people what they need to survive, learn faster, and adapt to their environment. It couldn't have the perfect code of laws for all places, but it could at least point people toward a more just and fruitful life.

                    Something that grand could be pretty massive, which is where Tome-Rearing Gesture is handy. You want a sturdily bound book that can weather the touch of many hands over the course of generations, not a pile of pages. There are other ways to create books that can last, of course.
