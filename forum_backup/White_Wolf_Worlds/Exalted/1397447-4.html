            I still think a big part of it is that most of the Solars probably didn't expect that they sould be betrayed. If they suspected an attack maybe they could have prepared. But most didn't because they didn't think to look for one.

            To use a weak metaphor, since Charms are just abstractions, many Charms require activation to use. And to uncover a conspiracy you gotta look for it.

            Now i'm willing to believe that maybe one Solar found out about the plan... And cue someone (maybe Thulio) appearing behind them and going "Sorry, you know too much" and them bleg, dead.

            The whole "But why didn't the Solars rolfstomp them back?" Is based on a lot of faulty assumptions. It assumes that a Solar staring down hundreds of Dragonbloods with even more mortal soldiers backing them up will laugh and slaughter them all. It assumes that every Solar knows Adamant Sorcery, or has a 5 dot Artifact weapon they've mastered the Evocations of. It assumes that they expected an attack and had a warstrider or something ready to launch at a moment's notice just in case something happens.

            It assumes that Solars are paranoid PCs who have the mindset of Doomsday preppers who stock an obscene amount of guns and baked beans rather than Solars having differing personalities and skillsets that are the result of their lives instead of being the result of Players building them with an idea of what's to come in mind.

            That's something to keep in mind. Characters in a game don't usually have access to the rulebook. And ultimately this talk is like trying to debate tactical outcomes from historical battles.

            "Why didn't Whats-His-Face attack Those-Guys with a cavalry charge from their north flank and completely turn the tide?"

            One could debate hypotheticals for a while about whether Whats-His-Face knew the flank was unprotected, what the conditions of the battle were like, if a cavalry charge was feasable or not, but ultimately trying to argue whether it could have gone another way boils down to "And yet it happened this way"

            Even when 2e spent most every book it could essentially mocking Dragonbloods for not being 'real' Exalts, especially compared to the great and grand Solars, the Usurpation happened. This is kinda something assumed for all Editions. Regardless of if it would happen or not with a bunch of bad assumptions, it still happened.

            Disclaimer: In favor of fun and enjoyment, but may speak up to warn you that you're gonna step on a metaphorical land mine

                Hmm... Yeah, to the extent the Usurpation could be said to be improbable via the mechanics for Second Age Solars that we have I would prefer the gap to bridged by.... "Turns out late First Age Solar hubris is one hell of a dice penalty! In exactly this circumstance!" over the usurpers simply lucking out and achieving something unlikely.

                Part of that is that the Path of Bronze is supposed to be the relatively safe* but lesser option. If it's actually the "Kind of corner case probability-wise" option that's not really much safer or more secure than the Path of Gold, then it doesn't really do what we're looking for.

                Some of the point of this aspect of the setting is that the Sidereals viewed it as more expedient and easier to usurp the Solars than try to persuade them and return them to virtue - and this is arguably a moral failing, or just and justified pragmatism, depending on where you're standing and how you roll play it out. So if the probabilities look like they're aligning the opposite way to any degree or align as equally probable, that's probably something to treat as "limit to the resolution of the mechanics", rather than an objectively true thing in the setting where the Sidereals made a weird choice.

                *I mean, they're Solars, so relatively safe is still tough odds, but clearly very different to Path of Gold.

                    Originally posted by Tikor View Post
                    I recall that optimized Solar was Essence 1? Without First Age Artifice and an army of dematerialized demons, and lacking Solar Sorcery and...
                    I think it was an Essence 1 Solar with a combat supernal, so higher essence mainly gives it more motes. So, send another 5 dragon blooded to get rid of the extra motes, plus 5 to deal with whatever essence 2+ non-supernal-ability charms they have. 25 dragon blooded is enough that you could send that many to attack every solar and still not be using the majority of dragon blooded. Most solars are not heavily optimized dragon blooded killers.
                    Ignoring the things already mentioned about bringing warstriders and armies to a party - most First Age Artifice isn't better in a fight than regular artifacts (ie. clockwork birds, boats that don't sail on land, siege weapons, gunzosha armor [good in a fight for mortals, but not better than regular armor for Exalts). The attackers are exalts - many of them will be able to see and attack dematerialized demons.
                    The optimized Solar who can kill 10+ dragonblooded alone makes heavy use of launching devastating strikes at the beginning of combat - Sorcery, especially Solar Sorcery, is often too slow. The only currently written solar circle sorcery spell useable in a fight is Death Ray, which is a fairly effect way to kill a handful of dragon blooded in a turn, and then have to survive a round without being crashed. Solars with Death Ray fall into the category of "throw 20+dragonblooded at them and expect heavy casualties", but are far from unbeatable. During the main fight at Calibration you could hit more than a handful, but would also hit a handful of solars. Also, killing a DB in one hit with Death Ray is possible, but not guaranteed - rolling 13 dice, with 6 successes from the spell and willpower is an average of 12.5 successes. An optimized DB has a defense of 6+1(melee weapon)+3(excellency)+1(willpower)+1(stunt) = 12, so you may or may not hit. Damage is initiative (6 might be normal, 9.5 optimized no excellency, 14.5 optimized excellency, 16.8 for awakening eye and a willpower)+extra successes (1-ish)+current temporary willpower (10, -1 for casting the spell, -1 for spending one on the attack) = somewhere from 14 to 30, so 6 to 12 levels of damage. Ox-bodies and resistance charms will cover that. The second and third round are better, especially since the DB can't keep spending 6m, 1wp on defense indefinitely, but also give more time for someone to kill or crash you.
