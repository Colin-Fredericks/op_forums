        The Shadiversity YouTube channel is really good! Shad is a lovable nerd, clearly very knowledgeable, and while he goes on about "historical accuracy" a bit more than I personally care for, I think he manages to walk the line of pointing out unrealistic fantasy tropes without adding an implied "...and that's terrible!" at the end. I got into his channel from the video about wearing swords on your back, and he recently made a video about how magical materials might influence weapon design, which I think might be interesting for this forum.

        Now, regarding the famous bikini witches, I've stated my opinions on these kinds of things previously. I think most of them are consistent, although I'm sure they've changed more than I realise. The core of my stance is that presentation and representation of women is a structural phenomenon, which includes all the instances of such representation, the contexts they're presented in, and the impacts they might have on society.

        To put it simply: I personally think it's okay to like bikini barbarians. I think, in fact, that Exalted provides a context that supports them. Exalted has silken armour that looks like normal silk clothes but is strong as steel, and Resistance Charms that greatly reduce the need for armour at all. Anyone playing such characters at their table, meaning a place with very limited social impact, can only be held accountable by those exposed to it, that is, essentially those participating in the same game.

        It's important to note that Exalted also provides a context that supports heavily armoured female knights. Magical materials and some other Resistance Charms provide great benefit to wearing a big hulking cage of metal around you, to greatly reduced downsides. The same as above goes for those.

        Because both of these aesthetics (in a generalised sense, of course most cases are somewhere in between) are supported, I think the game should present both of them visually. Perfect Soul is as supported by the game as The Lady of Darkness in Bloodstained Robes is, and that should show. However, since the state of fantasy art is as it is, it is probably more important for the game publications to emphasise the armoured side of the spectrum, if nothing else then because there are more ways to be dressed than to be naked.

        Likewise, I think the game should support different states of dress and undress of male characters. Jace and Volfer, again, are both quite supported aesthetics.

        Of course, there's much more to this discussion. Right now, we've only talked about states of armouredness. Presentation and representation of characters go much deeper than that, and I personally think Exalted does pretty well with providing a point of variety in the fantasy genre in terms of societal gender norms and empowerment. Really, if I could rant a bit, Exalted art seems like some of the least in need of pinup aesthetics when one of the historically most attractive attributes has been power. The blowup that happened around Sublime Danger (That's what, 4 years ago now? Time flies.) focused so much on her clothes and body shape, and here I was wondering if no one noticed how freaking hot it was that she was levitating twenty swords. Likewise, the Scarlet Empress is one of my favourite characters in the game, and very little of that is due to what she looks like.

        A way for the game to go is a further contextualisation of nudity and sexuality. If we can show a half-naked person on top of a heap of slaughtered enemies, why can we not show them atop a heap of conquered lovers? If they're going to be naked in battle, then they can be naked otherwise as well, and it wouldn't even have to be justified with magic that time.

                            I think I would agree that the stress is that a lot of it is really about balance. You can take characters like this in small doses as part of the array of characters being presented.

                            And actually I would argue rather strongly that in small doses having characters like this present is fun, and more than that it is also enhancing to the game's feel that it is referencing the genres where character designs like this are present, and for this latter reason it is almost essential that to some degree these characters are present. For an example that exists in game already, Volfer (who Sorcerous Overlord references) is basically VanfromVisionOfEscaflowneXJechtfromFFX in character design, and that reference helps sell that Exalted is an anime-action game built on those sort of sources, and it is similar for not heavily clothed female characters who also callback reference like this in their character design.

                            But at the same time you want to avoid the feeling that every female character present is like the roster of Dead or Alive or some ridiculous harem anime, because that will be alienating and not inclusive and not fun.

                            On female character depiction in Exalted, and in pop culture generally, these days it seems a bit of a shame that it seems at times bit polarized between the ideas that all female characters must be deliberately desexualized to strike a political and social blow against The Male Gaze (voluminous, shape concealing, "modest" clothes and armour, not particularly attractive silhouettes, etc), with claims that various social ills are directly attributed to the failure to do this, and on the other hand rather entitled fellows who get very irate at any suggestion that anything happen other than same few physically idealized and scantily clad sexualized waifus are presented again and again.

                            While in reality the majority are probably exhausted with all this and see no problem with the occasional quite sexualized female character as adding a bit of fun... but would mostly prefer generally to see a nice mid range of female characters who are generally reasonably athletic and fit and obviously would be sexually attractive, without having enormous unrealistically enhanced secondary sex characteristics, wear normal, close fitting clothing without boob windows, thigh highs, etc. And that these characters have strong values and personalities and goals without unnecessary hyping them up as The Strongest, etc. Anyway, that's just my opinion.

                                    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                    Nice to see some new ground being broken.

                                    Somebody brought up helms earlier, and it's having me wonder if a majority of battle helms throughout history have not in fact left the face uncovered to greater or lesser extent.

                                    The fully enclosed helm seems a bit of a historical outlier to my recollection, and I'm curious about why that might be. Metalworking, tactics, or just the style at the time? Still, there are options that reconcile head protection with a visible face. Give Karal Linwei a damn kabuto, at least.

                                    Then give her a mask covering the rest of her face anyway, because those looked pretty interesting.
                                    My guess is that it was to make sure that the wearer could have as much visibility as possible combined with protection and ease of breathing.
                                    For example the roman helmet had a pretty damn good combination of everything. Extra armor on the forehead to protect from overhead swings, cheek protectors by the ears to defend against side strikes, whilst at the same time you will have nothing blocking your nose, ears, eyes or mouth giving you full access to both breathing and your senses so you can hear the orders shouted by the centurions and also see anything coming at you.
                                    This also left the helmet relatively cheap to construct as well and made it easy to repair as at least the cheek protectors where separate bits attached to the helmet meaning that if it was damaged you could easily replace that part.

                                    As for fully enclosed helmets, well I'd say it was probably just due to how expensive they became to make as you need to custom make them for every knight.
                                    Not to mention that you need to send the entire thing in for repair if there's damage dealt to it and it hindered breathing and blocked hearing, sight and muffled your voice.
                                    It did however provide some pretty darn solid defense.
                                    A good example of enclosed helmet would be the frog-mouth helmet.
                                    This was primarily a tournament helmet as it was riveted onto your chest armor, but this made you almost invulnerable to any sort of damage to the head at the cost of vision and hearing.
                                    You could literally take a tourney lance to the throat and land on your head from horseback with only a few bruises.

                                    The more standard bascinet was a simple but solid design that could be modified with visors of different shapes and sizes giving you even more protection.
                                    The benefit of having visor protection was that you could just flip up the visor if you needed to survey your surroundings or take a unobstructed breather.
                                    The visor also had the benefit of being detachable so if it or the helmet was damaged you could just replace the damaged part.
                                    The Bascinet was also a single shape and thus far more solid than the great helmet that was five different parts riveted together.

                                    Another thing that can be worth to think about is the fact that the head is not the most difficult part to hit, the actual face is far harder to hit since the opponent needs to do very little to avoid attacks aimed at the face and it's a relatively small area to hit.

                                    War masks served a similar purpose to visors, they did however tend to be designed or decorated in ways to either intentionally or just by a fluke, intimidate and demoralize the enemy.
                                    Facing an enemy whose face you cannot see can also be quite intimidating in the same way as someone showing no emotion in the face of danger can also be quite intimidating.
                                    It can also be demoralizing to face the "same" enemy again and again.

                                    But yeah, to summarize.
                                    When you design a helmet you usually want a good combination between protection, hearing, breathing and vision whilst also keeping it affordable.

                                    Hopefully I made some sense and didn't just ramble on.
