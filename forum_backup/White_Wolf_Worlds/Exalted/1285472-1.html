    The deathlords really aren't trying to wipe out Creation all that hard. Like yeah, they kill people, but generally that's to some other end. Like ghost armies, or soulsteel.

    I mean, up until Thorns nobody in creation really knew the deathlords were a thing. So clearly, they're not that active.

    The fair folk don't give a shit about destroying creation these days. Balor was cool, but he failed, the story is over. Nobody's gonna copy him. That'd be boring.

    Oh, also the Lunars have like, mortal populations they protect as well. And if a community is in trouble it's god might petition the sun for exigence. So it's not just the dragon blooded protecting everyone. If a community is seriously under threat, people have a habit of starting to glow.

    And you shouldn't discount what baseline mortals can manage. There are divinely sponsored mortal militia like the Brides of Ahlat, or the gunsosha, and I wouldn't assume these two were the only examples on the map. You got the lintha, who mostly want to nick your stuff but hey, can't steal from people if they're dead.

    And beastmen. Beastmen are not just a lunar thing. Yeah, they get charms to furry people up, but the way they used to do it? That's just a wyld thing now. Anyone can do that.

        My first real 2e Solar was designed from character creation to go around unlocking people's hidden power and teaching them Terrestrial Martial Arts, she had herself been an orphan raised by a crime syndicate who got the first real sense of control and purpose in her life from her martial arts.

        I honestly don't miss them.

        Terrestrial Martial Art charms without combos or excellencies were mostly just bad. The charms simply weren't designed to be used solo in this fashion and most of them had effects that were contingent upon hitting the target in the first place, so there were obvious favourites and the rest were expensive speedbumps.

        There's also a lot of confirmation bias on the effectiveness of some styles because they revolved around enhancing tactics that were already way too good in 2nd edition to begin with but are remembered fondly because they were people's first experience of using them or because you could grudgingly respect these strategies from people who don't really have any other cards to play under the circumstances.

        Though I've come to realise there's a bit of a "mortals don't matter" sentiment in there, in that people who attempted more mundane approaches tended to get written off as extras even when they were doing something suicidal brave like fighting a Solar 5 vs 1.

                Originally posted by Nabirius View Post
                Oz, can mortals generally attune to artifacts, or does it have to be a specialized artifact? There was a powerful scepter in 2e that allowed mortals to wield it and create powerful laws, but I don't know how much of a feature this is in 3e.
                I was thinking of stuff like Yellow Jade or the Path of Technomantic Transcendance, or the Staff of the Perfect, yeah.

                In 3e there's Gunzosha Armour (as yet unstatted, it'll probably be in Heirs to the Shogunate) and Vainglory (a chakram in Arms you can attune for WP). And there's a few miscallaneous artefacts that just don't need to be attuned at all.

                Thinking about it, the main way a mortal is going to get magical powers to challenge exalts and gods, is via sorcerous workings. Terrestrial workings can give someone mutations, while Celestial workings can give someone greater powers, like a hypnotic gaze or blood that turns into scorpions when shed.


                Originally posted by Nabirius
                I like the idea of a wise old master who the players can learn from, despite being able to overpower. Its a staple of wuxia fiction, and even heroic myth.
                I think, in 3rd edition, you can do this with either mortal martial artists or sorcerers.

                An old mortal Mantis master can teach you Mantis style, right the way to the top. You'll be better than him of course, and could probably beat him up, but you learn the charms from him.

                Similarly with a mortal sorcerer, though only for Terrestrial Circle unfortunately. If you want to learn the ultimate secret techniques you'll need something more.

                As a side note, how do people feel about the loss of enlightened mortals?
                Mixed.
                On the one hand, it often seemed like you had a Solar (or whatever) going around powering up mortals in very boring ways so they could wield artefacts or whatever.
                There didn't really seem to be any actual enlightenment. Just a Solar grabbing a guy and buffing him permanently.

                On the other hand, enlightened martial artists were the default low-level antagonists for my friend's game, and it was very Wuxia. An enlightened Immaculate Monk using the first 5 charms of Five Dragon and a straight sword was the perfect Immaculate goon. Very genre-appropriate.

                Obviously, now you can have a mortal monk who actually practices Earth Dragon to help them become close to Pasiap. Or a local dojo that teaches Tiger Style (it was ridiculous how mortals in 2nd ed couldn't use Tiger Style when people in real life can).
                But the rules are pretty damn boring, in such a way that a soldier in armour with a spear is better; which is entirely realistic, but doesn't encourage the ST to run things in a genre-appropriate way.

                Essentially, the only way to have martial artists who stand out from soldiers is by making them Dragon-blooded, or maybe God-Blooded or something (which isn't necessarily going to make them better at martial arts). And if your game is all about kung-fu societies and stuff, it's going to get old fast. There aren't that many Exalts in Creation, after all.

                On the other other hand... as Lioness said, it was no good them having Martial Arts when they still couldn't actually hit anything. Some styles had charms to help with that; most didn't.

                        Originally posted by Nabirius

                        Well... how?

                        Originally posted by Nabirius
                        they tend to make mortal survival more in peril than they would be were these groups absent.
                        Some of my points before were that mortals are not in enough peril to widely knock down their population. Enough to slow down worldwide population growth, but not knock the overall down.

                        In places where population density is low, it can be hard to effectively find people, and even when you do the yields are low. In places where density is high, they're probably the best protected by their labours providing fortifications, numbers providing armies, and attracting or clustering around supernatural protectors.

                        Some of my other points have been about pointing to ways in which mortal life being in continuous peril is a thing that can go on for ages without killing absolutely everybody, for the sake of getting one to consider what that kind of world must be like.

                        Originally posted by Nabirius
                        (Please also note that in my original post this was a small aside that we have all apparently decided was the real point)
                        Saying that one doesn't see how mortals survive is a rather weighted question, and when it's linked to the topic of them getting supernatural powers, it would kind of shape the priorities if taken as a given.

                        I think that it's a net positive to devising the powers that they can get if the survival of humanity is not dependent on it.

                        Originally posted by Nabirius
                        Moreover, there are zero factions in the world, now or ever, that had human extinction as a major goal and yet we may still manage to kill ourselves off.
                        But a lot of those are things that aren't really in the game setting.

                        Originally posted by Nabirius
                        Consider the possibility of a deathlord with Supernal Occult and sorcery, with Total Annihilation he could launch several nukes a day (with a bunch of restrictions, fair), and then raise them from the dead as his army for the next invasion. Point being that it doesn't require a ton of people want to destroy creation to cause serious damage to human life and make the setting nothing like its current status quo.
                        I don't think Deathlords will have Supernal or Chtonic Abilities (not that it necessarily matters if none of them are below Essence 5 anyway), and they might not be able to use Solar Sorcery.

                        A few of them might still have highly formidable powers, but going forwards, Deathlords ought to have certain restrictions arising from their ghostly natures. For the sake of consistency in the setting, an incentive to have the Abyssals, and making them feel more like ancient ghosts overall.

                        Hmm, I initially thought to make the "humans with magic" side a separate post, but... it wouldn't be so long.

                        Originally posted by Nabirius
                        For instance, someone pointed out that the animals with essence abilities are because they are familiars, this is not universally true (or at least there are a large number of inexplicably animal-like magical creatures like Barrow Hounds, Gravehounds, aurochs, etc.
                        Are aurochs magical? Regardless, the other things... calling these entities being animal-like inexplicable begs the question of what one would presume a supernatural entity to be like, and why.

                        On the other hand, when talking about something like the cockatrice... regardless of the in-setting specifics of how they exist, I can see the question of "why don't such spontaneous blessings or curses, from the Wyld or whatever other source, come upon humans more".

                        I can think of three avenues for that.

                        One is that such a thing is generally how thaumaturges happen. Thaumaturges might be meant to be uncommon on the whole, but even assuming that the odds were something fairly remote like 1 in 10,000, across the whole world that still amounts to... fifty thousand people.

                        Hmm, that's a bit low for me...shorten the odds a bit.

                        The circumstances that create the likes of cockatrices aren't exactly common, especially when people are vigilant against them.

                        Wait, I said three... damned rambling.

                        Human behaviour might be less susceptible to things like spontaneous Wyld emergences, and the background of the setting has often made humans specifically, even deliberately, non-magical, which might also preclude a lot of weird stuff happening.

                        Originally posted by Nabirius
                        why it is impossible for Solars to do the same to humans and awaken their innate magic
                        Leaving aside innate magic, I'd say that Solars can bestow power in a manner consistent with the Solar way of doing things. Look at how they can train soldiers to have Might when fighting spirits or giant monsters. Barring sorcery, a Solar's training or blessings aren't going to make another Eternal Revolutionary, because that doesn't fit the image of their power.

                        Originally posted by Nabirius
                        if they don't have such magic, then why do animals?
                        A cockatrice doesn't happen because chickens are innately magical, they happen because the Wyld seeps in to weird happenstance.

                        A similar logic probably goes for any instance where a more magical creature has been transformed; that Death Essence isn't awakening their latent potential, they're just a thematically suitable vessel for it.

                        Originally posted by Nabirius
                        Moreover, why are ghosts more magical than humans? Some like the Susurrus makes sense since they are amalgamations of human souls and thus more 'powerful' in some indefinite manner. But others are merely dead people, yet now endowed with powers unavailable to them in life. Why are humans incapable of accessing this potential before death when so many other beings can?
                        I can think of two ways to go with this.

                        One is that, in life, that energy goes into fuelling your living self; sustaining your body, your intellect, your passions. Being separated from mortal flesh frees a lot of that energy to be bent to other purposes, but at the cost of losing Life; warmth, sensation, newness, strong emotion.

                        The other is that ghost powers rise from channeling the Underworld's Essence, which generally can't be done by the living. Maybe sometimes, but those origins might not be pleasant.

                        Originally posted by Nabirius
                        I like the idea that with enough learning, training, or sheer determination a mortal can be a player in the universe without exalting
                        It needs to compromise a bit with the existing setting, but it's perfectly doable. It's often becoming a sorcerer, but can be other things.

                        Artifact backstory in Arms and What Fire Has Wrought often mention conflicts against people that weren't Exalted, but still utilising some form of power.

                        Originally posted by Nabirius
                        I like the idea of a wise old master who the players can learn from, despite being able to overpower.
                        I think that's something that a character can be without having any powers at all.
