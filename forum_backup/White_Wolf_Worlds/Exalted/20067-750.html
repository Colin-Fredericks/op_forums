        The thing about a competent bureaucracy is it doesn't look like much is happening. It's not dramatic. The government just... does what it's supposed to do. Money gets spent efficiently. The government knows what is going on (this one is incredibly important, but actually quite difficult). Essentially, there are no screw-ups. There's no accidental starvation because the new laws about farming turned out to be a terrible idea.

                        I like the idea, but ask the big question : Who are y... wait that's not the one. Oh yeah found it : How do you make it cool ? You mass purged spies and corruption and inefficiencies ? That sounds awesome from the perspective of the result, but it sounds boring from the perspective of storytelling. How did you do it ?                         My advice about this is : If afterwards, you can't portray your action in your head or to someone else as a really cool scene from a movie, then you need to depict it better. Just make one big memorable event that symbolises the effect of the roll and that's usually fine.

                                                        I think that in basically everything other than an extended action or some of the rolls in attacking, what eight successes does depends entirely on what the difficult of your declared action was.

                                                        I suppose the most basic answer is "it definitely means you did not fail", possibly barring being up against some effects that subtract successes.

                                                        Is there any time when you just throw dice against no particular problem and then ask for the results to mean something?

                                                        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                                                        What's the roll? Because an Int+Bureaucracy roll to know what to do
                                                        I've also wondered for a while whether "know what to do" is particularly useful as an action to roll dice towards. It strikes me as being far too burdensome on the Storyteller, and with the kinds of dice that Exalted readily provides access to takes away a lot of decision making or player agency or dramatic tension.

                                                        Like, maybe in a circumstance where players have been presented with one or more options by circumstances and another character and they want hints from the Storyteller about underlying factors or possible consequences, but I think even that shouldn't be "know what to do" if that's in the sense of being told which option is the "correct" one.

                                                        Originally posted by Accelerator
                                                        How would it look like, from the ground level?


                                                        The ground level not being choked with refuse could be a start as visual indicators go, I think.

                                                        I feel as though a story taking place against a backdrop of well-managed bureaucracy (which should be produced by something other than periodically rolling dice to perform routine administration) is the kind of thing that comes up primarily as a response to other actions; if official information about any sector of the society is needed, it's present and accessible, if funds are needed they're available. It's that and a story lacking challenges based on people operating outside of regulations, which depending on what the preceding campaign has looked like can amount to feeling like you can leave the city to run itself for five minutes while you handle something else.

                                                        Originally posted by JohnDoe244

You don't need to be good at anything to just not pursue cases of civil rights abuses or not bother to appoint ambassadors when there's hardly anybody willing or capable of forcing you.

                                                        Originally posted by JohnDoe244

                                                        The only people I can think of who support Trump when they "by rights should oppose him" are those whose loudly stated moral convictions have always been evidently disregarded against their actions and underlying motives (displaying a deep rooted hypocrisy), and people hamstrung by the two-party system.
