        Liminal Exalted preview
        The Liminal Exalted, or Chernozem as they are more commonly
        known to those who have encountered or heard of
        them, are one of the new varieties of playable characters that
        will appear in Exalted Third Edition. The Chernozem present
        a contrast to the more familiar Exalted, whose struggles
        raise up and cast down nations. While Liminals are as mighty
        as other Chosen, their stories are inward-focused, emphasizing
        the world’s impact on the character rather than the other way
        around. As such, they’re perfect additions to ‘mixed Circles.’

        "Death Begets Life"
        A sorcerer cries out to Heaven atop a storm-lashed manse.
        His fortune is gone, expended in its construction that he might
        harness and focus the power of the demesne below. His wife’s
        cold body rests on an open slab atop the mystical ziggurat,
        flanked by fires kindled with rare herbs from the depths of the
        Wyld. A hearthstone gleams on her chest, focusing the power
        of land and flame and storm and spell. This desperate ritual has
        only one goal: to tear the sorcerer’s beloved back from the cold
        embrace of the Underworld, to convince Heaven to reunite
        soul and flesh, to mend the hole in a man’s wounded heart.
        The sorcerer’s demand is beyond the power of Heaven. The
        ritual is doomed to failure. But somewhere in the black and
        storm-tossed reaches of the Underworld, something ancient
        and attentive hears his anguished cries. She cannot—and
        would not—give him what he wants, but has needs of her own.
        She reaches through the window of his agony and makes the
        ritual her own.
        The cadaver’s eyes open and see. Fresh blood suffuses pale,
        stiff limbs. A still heart lurches painfully into motion. New life
        kindles in empty flesh. The body rises, drawing in a shuddering
        breath. Alive.
        The sorcerer cries out his beloved’s name. The figure before
        him does not answer. It is not his wife.


        "Born of Darkness"
        Imagine awakening in unfamiliar surroundings. There’s pain,
        though that recedes quickly, and disorientation, which lingers
        much longer. Your body feels strange and unfamiliar, tingling
        and flushing with sensation as it also awakens, bit by bit.
        You don’t know who you are. As you realize this, memories
        rise up to present themselves. But they’re not your memories—
        you know that immediately. There’s no sense of ownership or
        identification. It’s like remembering a character you read about
        in a book, or heard about in a story. Perhaps it’s only one set of
        memories. Perhaps it’s several, jumbles of lives and events haphazardly
        stitched together. Maybe these secondhand memories
        are enough to tell you where you are.
        Maybe they’re enough to tell you who’s standing over you,
        half-mad with emotion. You know this figure isn’t really your
        creator—there’s a lingering touch on your mind, receding now,
        dark and immense: the receding hand of your real maker.
        You’re not the sum of the memories in your head. You’re not
        the sum of the parts of your body. Who are you?
        No immediate answer presents itself. This is the dilemma that
        greets each of the Chernozem in the first moments of their life.


        "Children of the Black Earth"
        The Chernozem are life brought forth from death. They’re
        not resurrected individuals, although they’re most commonly
        created when someone tries to restore the dead to life. Rather,
        they’re new people, given life by a strange form of Exaltation,
        made of borrowed flesh and full of someone else’s memories.
        A Liminal might be made from a single corpse subjected to
        some form of attempted resurrection ritual, or might be formed
        of parts of disparate bodies combined for some arcane purpose.
        The only unifying factors are an attempt to bring forth life from
        dead flesh; intense, all-consuming fixation in the mind of the
        would-be resurrectionist; and the interference of an ancient,
        mighty force dwelling deep within the Underworld.
        The Chernozem are, for the most part, rootless beings.
        They’re alive, but not in the way everything else is. Life was
        designed to beget life; for life to spring from death runs against
        the grain of Creation, and most individuals find the Chernozem
        deeply disturbing. A Liminal’s first discovery is that he is not
        the sum of his memories; his second is usually that he is not like
        other people, and that other people know this, and don’t like it.
        As a result, most Liminals stay on the move. Some simply
        roam from place to place, vagabonds without purpose. Others
        become heroes for hire, performing great deeds and then
        moving on before unease can overcome gratitude. Others still
        attempt to make a place for themselves in the world, no matter
        how difficult it may be. All come to understand that they are
        in some way incomplete, and wonder if there is some way to
        become more than what they are.


        "Hands of the Dark Mother"
        The mysterious dark mother of the Chernozem granted them
        Exaltation for a reason. The purpose of the Liminals is to act as
        wardens at the boundary of life and death, generally by hunting
        the restless dead when they intrude upon the lands of the living.
        Many Liminals work as warrior-exorcists, ghost-hunters, and destroyers
        of necromantic abominations. They’re most often seen
        in shadowlands, plying their trade and then moving on.
        Yet the Liminals aren’t shackled to this purpose—merely
        inclined to it, and sometimes goaded toward it by their ancient
        matron. Others hire themselves out as caravan guards,
        or join mercenary bands, or become the fearsome champions
        of mighty princes; or attempt to build low-key lives in the great
        cities of Creation, where they might vanish into the crowd; or
        retreat from the world of mortals entirely, becoming strange
        and dreadful hermits lurking in forgotten jungle ruins, or squatting
        in high mountaintop caves, trying to ignore the howling of
        ghosts in the night.


        "Aspects of Life and Death"
        Celestial Exalted. Instead they are divided into Aspects, somewhat
        like the Dragon-Blooded. But where Dragon-Blooded are
        marked by the primacy of elemental Essence running through
        their veins, Liminal Aspects emphasize elements of life and
        death. A Liminal’s Aspect is defined by the drives of his creator—
        different brands of vibrant emotion or obsession produce
        markedly different Exalts.
        Aspects of Blood are born of creators driven by “possessive”
        passion—lust, greed, and ambition. Blood represents the heat
        of passion and the wonder of emotion. Blood is a carrier as well
        as a fulcrum; it can be a river of plagues or a font of virginal
        purity with which to attract and bind the foulest of horrors and
        bid them whip. Blood Aspects are sanguine, passionate, outgoing
        creatures.
        Aspects of Breath are born of creators driven by despair,
        or even nihilism. Breath represents the gift of life, the cycle of
        lethe, the power of possession, and the flow of Essence. Breath
        is an unceasing tide, drawing in from the world and flowing
        out to meet it; properly harnessed, it calls the storm and sets
        it free. Aspects of Breath are reflective beings, often shaping
        themselves to meet the expectations of others.
        Aspects of Flesh are born of creators driven by “savage” passion—
        vengeance, rage, madness. Flesh represents lust, hunger
        and thirst, madness and rage. Flesh is the core of identity and
        the vector of metamorphosis. It is the currency of those caught
        between life and death. Flesh Aspects are choleric, active, and
        often aggressive.
        Aspects of Marrow are born of creators driven by curiosity,
        obsession, and hunger for knowledge. Marrow represents
        secrets buried deep within the flesh, the power of dark and hidden
        places, the strong sheathe of bone around the innermost
        workings of the heart. Marrow Aspects are reserved and analytical
        individuals.
        Aspects of Soil are born of creators driven by sorrow, regret,
        yearning. Soil represents the seminal urge, the lure of life
        and jealousy for the living, the power of decay and the hungry
        whispers that wait on the threshold between life and death. It
        is the final tomb awaiting the dead, and the cradle in which
        seeds sprout new life. Soil Aspects are introspective and given
        to consideration of signs and omens.


        "The Power of the Chernozem"
        The Charms of the Liminals derive from the associations of
        their Aspects and the might of their unnatural bodies. Blood
        Aspect magic, for example, may allow a Liminal to easily track
        a man for hundreds of miles with only a sample of bodily fluids
        to work from, but it also allows confident navigation of the
        dark waterways and torrential courses of the Underworld, as
        well as influence over the desires and addictions of others. Soil
        Charms might bind the dead back into their graves, while just
        as readily helping the Chernozem bring forth new creations
        into the world.
        Additionally, the body of a Liminal is incredibly resilient; severed
        or destroyed limbs may be easily replaced with new ones,
        and the Liminal’s body soon adapts the new addition so that it
        looks like a natural part of her form. Moreover, the Chernozem
        may draw skills and memories from stolen body parts: a Liminal
        might gain grace from a dancer’s legs, skill from a swordsman’s
        arm, or guile from a sly man’s tongue. With appropriate
        Charms, even more impressive and macabre feats of body-theft
        may be possible.
        Finally, the Chernozem even have some capacity to return
        from the grave. As a Liminal’s very life-force is different from
        that of mortals (or other Exalts), she doesn’t pass into death’s
        embrace in the same manner as natural things do. So long as
        her brain and most of her body is left intact, a slain Liminal will
        eventually re-ignite the spark of her Essence and rise again,
        restored to life. This remains possible even if she is wholly dismembered
        and beheaded, so long as the brain survives and is
        later reunited with the parts of her body (or at least a body
        mostly consisting of parts that were formerly hers). Burning
        and drowning may also interfere with a Liminal’s resurrection.
        Many Chernozem remain unaware of this power until they use
        it for the first time.

        STing Bronze Age Exalted

                            Originally posted by Aranfan View Post
                            When you roll Init on gambits, do you double 10s?

                            I'd say you do double 10s on a gambit roll. Double-10s don't normally apply on decisive damage rolls because they don't want decisive damage to have too much chance to explode - it's better for gameplay if decisive attacks have less chance of rolling a bunch of 10s and suddenly just destroying a PC. Whereas gambit rolls are just against a difficulty, and don't generally have much extra effect for beating that difficulty by a bunch, and thus allowing double-10s makes less difference.
