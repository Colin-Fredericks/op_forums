    Does anything majorly bad happen if you just ignore training times

    03-31-2019, 03:28 AM
    Apologies if this has been debated to death already, but as the title says.

    I'll do anything for an easy life and on our third game into the campaign, training times at first glance just seem like unnecessary additional book-keeping that none of us can be bothered with. Your thoughts please, will our hubris lead to our untimely demise or do you ignore them too and allow characters to insta-level?

    Edit to add: we'd be spending XP between sessions obviously, not in-game. We're not complete monsters.

      The biggest issue that comes to my mind is just that players will occasionally end up with things you hadn't planned for them to have. Now obviously it won't be as problematic as them just being able to buy new powers in the middle of a fight, but it might still trip you up from time to time.

      On a more narrative aspect training has a few ways to be used that I find somewhat beneficial. Like on the low end being able to describe how a character trains can work nicely for character traits, then there's the tradition of enemies or what have you just interrupting training. Then there's the perk of making that part of downtime easier to plan since characters are off studying or working out or whatever they need to do while waiting for reinforcements to arrive so the circle can focus on bashing in Tain Without Reflections skull and not getting swarmed by however many undead a magnitude 5 group is.

      There's also my personal standing that it kind of takes something away from the favored/ unfavored divide if you remove training time since the xp gap isn't that big. (10 unfavored charm 8 favored, current ability x2 for unfavored, favored is [current x2]-1) So it feels like that becomes more meaningless if characters master it all with equal rapidity. I don't know just feels like there's something to be said that with a solar starting with a dot in sail it takes about 3.5 months for one who isn't favoring become one of the best sailors humanly possible (Going to 5 dots.) vs the 2 weeks it takes the one who favored it to get the same results.

          We've tried to use them multiple times and always ended up dropping them. They're an enormous hassle.

          They also severely dissuade people from buying attributes, because the training times are so long.

          One game I play is so action packed/has such a split party that most sessions are 1-2 days. If we used training times, some PCs would have scores of XP unspent.

          We do demand some justification though. You can't just one day learn Snake 5. You need a teacher or scroll, and then buy 1 dot a session while you practice in your free time and use it in fights. Etc.

Your thoughts please, will our hubris lead to our untimely demise or do you ignore them too and allow characters to insta-level?

                  Nothing will break, but my experience is that having to spend XP in bursts of downtime rather than drip-feeding it as soon as it becomes available is better for pacing and makes for a more satisfying power curve. It also forces players to make some interesting choices in what to train, rather than dogmatically sticking to a pre-determined advancement path they wrote up at the campaign's start.

                  (Also, I just inherently enjoy fiddling with it.)

                  Evocations for the demonic tattoos gained from the Pact with Mara sorcerous initiation || Pyre-Kindler (Soulsteel and Red Jade Grimscythe, Artifact 3) || Tenebrous Descent (Stormcaller's Black Jade Reaver Daiklave cousin, Artifact 5)

                                                          Nothing breaks, though as people have said it's good to have some sort of alternative around that's less bookkeeping but still useful for pacing things out a bit.

                                                          I always felt the WoD had it better here: You can't raise a trait with XP more than once a Story, and it needs in-game justification. This does plenty to avoid "I learned Crane Style overnight," but it scales to the pace of the game more readily than training times based on in-game time rather than out-of-game time units.

                                                          If you want to highlight favored vs. non-favored, have favored limited by Session, and non-favored by Story.

                                                          This is means dealing with any Charms the modify training times easier to adapt rather than figuring out what to do with them if you flat our remove any mechanical training limits besides having XP.
