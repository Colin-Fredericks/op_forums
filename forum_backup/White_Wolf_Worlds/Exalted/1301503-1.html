                The way I run NPC Willpower for an individual character is to ask myself what kind of week they've been having.

                Willpower is spent by people attempting difficult tasks and navigating socially fraught situations. So just like PCs, their Willpower will be full or nearly full if they're well-rested and haven't had to do anything difficult or stressful lately, whereas their pool might be down to a third if they're run off their feet with the situations they're dealing with. A handy default is two-thirds of their pool - they haven't had time to relax lately, but things aren't out of control. Stressed people are easier to pressure/finesse.

                        I generally find the best thing is to give NPCs willpower pools that aren't enormous (say, 5; maybe they had more but they spent it), spend it to protect very important intimacies and use charms, but not to use it for minor stuff or extra successes.

                        I will say, about NPCs not having to worry about running out... if you're in a fight with Exalts, you're going to want to use all your willpower if it's useful, because otherwise you're going to die.
                        If you're just a merchant and they're trying to scam you... well, you are going to get a willpower back tonight, so you can maybe spend 1 point.

                        And I do track willpower for important NPCs, so for example if a bad guy spends 4wp fighting PCs and then flees (or they flee), and they fight again the next day, he's still going to be 3wp down.

                            Originally posted by Mizu View Post
                            Honestly, the big problem with this is that a ST who is wanting to be able to abuse the system by having NPCs spam willpower for things they really shouldn't by the core rules suggestion is unlikely to agree to being shackled by house rules limiting their ability to do that.                             From my perspective, the issue is less shackling intentionally abusive storytellers than it is helping storytellers balance npc willpower expenditure. Treating it as a resource one has to balance ideally helps make judgments about when it is appropriate to use and when it’d be overdoing it and hobbeling player characters.

                            No rule can actually address the problem of a malicious player/storyteller. The only solution is not to play with them. But sometimes restrictions are helpful rails for well intentioned players/storytellers.

                            I get the response of “just use your judgment if the character would resist or not” but that ignores the fact the way that willpower is balanced mechanically isn’t just a question of IF the character would resist, but also having to conserve wp as a resource - and that doesn’t play out with npcs despite the rules treating them the same as PCs in this regard.

                                            Since I generally am the storyteller I have a set method for npc's that I have used since i started running Werewolf and adapted to all other games.
                                            Important npc main villain use standard creation rules.
                                            NPC average or random use 2 d10 1-05 1 06-20 2 21-45 3 46-55 4 56-65 5 66-75 7 76-85 8 86-95 9 96-100 10
                                            This gives me a quick, yet fairly even distribution of willpower for any given situation involving small numbers in the event of a large scale say a dynast party with a 100 guests I would probably roll a few times no more than 5 and establish an average.

                                                My NPCs are mostly - but not entirely - quick stats with numbers chosen to be a relative challenge level. If they're chumps, then the Circle can out-roll them without major Essence. If they're on par, then they roll about the same pools of dice. If they're a big bad boss, then they're over and above, with other mechanics to support them.

                                                If Willpower is going to be important to a social Scene, I treat it as being discoverable similar to an Intimacy, but in broad, non-numeric terms. They might find someone to be iron-willed, negotiable, a bit exhausted, beaten down, or simply spineless.

                                                I want most of my Scenes to be successful if the players interact with it and come up with a solution that matches the story, so perceiving an NPC to be leaning on his Willpower tends to be a clue what's important to them. In the Scenes that aren't successful, the lack of character success is itself a bit of story, so that overlaps a bit in how it works.
