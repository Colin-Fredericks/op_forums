    Non-Artifact Artifacts and Evocations

    02-19-2019, 06:53 AM
    Maybe more of a thought experiment/topic for discussion than anything else but what are people's thoughts on what makes an Artifact or allows for Evocations?
    I know it sounds like a stupid question, but really... does it need to be made of magical materials? Does it need to have been an Artifact when crafted or could it become one over time? Are Evocations dependent on the magical nature of an Artifact to be able to be expressed or could a sufficiently storied or legendary item have the ability to have some anyway?

    Let's say you have a regular sword, not made of orichalcum or moonsilver or jade, but of steel, it's not the most amazing sword ever, but it is well crafted. It's a families heirloom sword, now wielded proudly by a brave Solar swordsman, it carries his family legacy. It was granted to his great grandfather when he was ennobled after the great battle of the Golden Plains, it was in his grandfathers hands when he cut down the arch-traitor Koteshin, it still has the notch in the blade where it caught on the war-collar of the horrifying demon-beast H'Kul when his father lost his life and now it is in the hands of a man beginning to carve a legend for himself that has the potential to shake the very pillars of Creation.
    This swordsman is not going to give this blade up for any other, no matter how shiny or magical or better it's stats might be because this blade is storied and connected to him in a real, fundamental way that no weapon of the First Age could compete with.
    But it's just a sword, an ordinary sword.

    It just feels to me that there is more joy to be had in allowing items like this to develop evocations that in a fancy jade axe he could grab from a random Dynast he fights, even if it is a storied axe with a legend of it's own, it isn't his legend.
    And what if we say after using his faithful blade for many years his essence suffused strikes have bled some of his very Exalted nature into the wood and steel and it becomes almost unbreakable, or even gains the stats of a daiklaive? Is that okay? Is it in keeping with the feel of Exalted or is it nonsense? Can mundane materials ever be more than just mundane?

                  Something that could work, especially since your hypothetical is of a Solar swordsman, is that the heirloom blade gets augmented by Glorious Solar Saber, which instead of summoning a blade, temporarily awakens the true potential of the history within your family's sword. Glorious Solar Saber allows for Evocations to be built from it, and drawing from the history of the blade and your family would certainly be a consistent theme for those Evocations.

                  In the hands of anybody but the Solar, it's just a really old blade, but the Solar is able to realize its true potential and draw it forth; at least to me, that seems like it would hit the major thematic notes that you seem to be aiming for.

                                          Ah, the Lore charm. I can see why they went with that, but I'm not sure that unleashing the legendary power of the (non-daiklaive) sword of kings should require you to be one of the greatest polymaths and scholars in Creation.


                                          Anyway.

                                          So I rarely take Exalted artefact weapons. They just feel too... ludicrous. I can't take Final Fantasy weapons seriously, except in a magitech-and-anime game.

                                          But I guess you can say, like "My daiklaive is the size of a bastard sword" and generally use it two-handed, one-handed occasionally (let's be honest, most PCs with a Daiklaive don't have a weapon in the other hand anyway).
                                          I have taken a single Short Daiklaive before and used it as a longsword.


                                          Now, the materials.
                                          In some ways, I do like the idea that a smith forges a weapon of incredibly sharpness and strength from certain materials.
                                          Meteoric steel.
                                          Pure silver.
                                          The souls of the dead bound into the metal, giving it the strength of the dead.
                                          Jade, for Wuxia.
                                          Metal forged in a volcano.

                                          But for some of those, the associates mythologically are rather off. Silver is associated with purity; killing beasts and demons. Not madness and dreams. Meteoric steel is associated with incredible strength, rather than spirits and bizarre fate-bending. Forging a weapon in a volcano should give it fire-powers, not sunlight or holy powers.

                                          Anyway.
                                          I think that... I don't know, they're a bit overused? Like, if a quarter of artefact weapons' thing is they're "made from Jade", that's kind of dull. What's more interesting is the other stuff; made from the tooth of a behemoth, woven from the skin of a dragon, etc, etc.
                                          Not to mention the fact so many are just found in a tomb or looted from a Dragonblood's corpse. (Obviously the writers have tried to improve that by putting in the whole evocation thing.)


                                          In the game I used to, players had a lot of artefact weapons.
                                          The fire-sorceress had a giant golden sword that she got from... somewhere? Before the game. Red jade would have fit her better. Or a demon sword, actually. (If Arms had been out, I think she'd have taken Soulfarer.) Also a couple of daiklaives looted from DBs.
                                          The Lunar had a silver blade and a soulsteel blade looted from enemies.
                                          The Zenith had a big golden staff.
                                          The Dawn had a golden blade.
                                          There was also a starmetal spear (looted from an enemy) and a couple more jade daiklaives sitting in people's huts, looted from DBs.
                                          Later, when I played, I made 2 blue jade short daiklaives from the blue jade fighting sticks I took from a Thunderbird I killed.

                                          (Oh, and only the Dawn had artefact armour. No-one else did. Which made the combats fast-paced, I guess.)

                                          Honestly, the only one I really liked was the Full Moon's staff, which was made from his own tusk and the soul of Chejop Kejak, who'd tried possessing him once.
                                          The rest were pretty dull. Including the ones I made.

                                          And yes, I'm sure someone's going to say "Well, that's because they didn't have a cool story." And that's a little true. But after the first one, more golden blades are, let's be honest, kind of boring. "Oh, it's another blade made of solid alchemy-gold. Whup-tee-do."

                                          I should say that Arms is mixed here; there are steel weapons that just have magical material inlay or whatever. And there's stuff like a giant tusk of a monster, or a stone prison with a demon eye in.
                                          But others are purely because of their legend, and not really what they're made of, and at that point, isn't Fodor right? It's just the legend afterall, not the material.

                                          STing Bronze Age Exalted

                                                  In my games, Artifacts are only slightly larger and heavier than their mundane counterparts (if a mortal sword is 3 feet long and weighs 3 lbs, a Daiklave would be 3 1/2 feet and weigh 6 lbs), meaning they can be used by mortals with a penalty, or as a weapon one Size category higher without penalty.

                                                  ive never been a fan of the fuckhuge-weapons of Vanilla Exalted. Artifacts deal the damage they do because they are *magical*, not because they are large enough to use as an ironing-board.

                                                  I also allow mortals to make their own Artifacts, so long as they spend a suitable amount of XP and do suitably-kickass things with the thing beforehand. A mortal killing something large and gribbley *means something*, and if they do choose part of their legend enters their weapon as a result.

                                                  For example, in one of my Quests the mortal fought and killed an Anathemic Lunar in a corrupted shrine to Luna. He resanctifed the shrine by sacrificing his broken spear, and as a gift Luna sent dreams to his boss suggesting she commission a replacement weapon for him. No Magical Materials involved, just skilled mortal artifice, feathersteel and silver, but it was ended up being a fairly powerful Direlance (which he would have discovered if the Quest actually finished :/)
