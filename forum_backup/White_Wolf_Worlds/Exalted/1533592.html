    Historical examples of the Great Curse effecting Sidereals and Lunar exalted?

    04-05-2024, 04:31 AM
    Can anyone point me to Historical examples of the Great Curse effecting Sidereals and Lunar exalted? Particularly between the Ursurpation and current era?

    Note: - By historical I mean actually had an affect on history of creation, not I decided to eat the pawn dealer who cheated/betrayed me, but a Sidereal's pride damaging the realm or Lunar loosing a country because they were going wild due to the GC.

    Background : I am writing my own alternative setting and trying to justifiy keeping the Great Curse included to myself.

    One of the things that struck me is that despite Sidereals & Lunars having some fairly old exalts, I don't think I have read any real examples of them suffering (and causing suffering) from the Great Curse. Particularly after the Ursurpation and especially especially in a historical context.

    Basically what I looking for is the GC doing something the Ursurpation because (unless I am missing something), it looks the GC just disappeared when the Shogunate started or were the Solars the only ones doing bad things due to the GC?

    This is jarring because I refuse to believe characters like Cheop and other elder exalted would not have developed Limit up the wazoo though the millenia.

          Originally posted by TryingToBeSlim View Post

          This is jarring because I refuse to believe characters like Cheop and other elder exalted would not have developed Limit up the wazoo though the millenia.
          I'll point out that out of all of them, Sidereals probably have the easiest time relieving their Limit by doing Destiny affirming stuff.

          I think the thing about the Great Curse is that it has to be read into the overall pattern of a character's behaviour rather than consistently being something with big, obvious incidents that can be attributed to it, because Limit Breaks ultimately don't last very long and many of them can be subtle in their expression.

          So for a Lunar, it's going to be like... there's not some big stand out case where one underwent a Limit Break and then massacred thousands of people or something. It's more like looking at the kind of relationship that Smiling Zamisha has with the Eskari tribes and thinking about how intermittent but consistent cases of things like Laughing Devil Spirit (Lunar acts as trickster-mentor to reveal harsh truths) or or Stalking Barghest Omen (Lunar obsessively fixates on and pursues somebody) become part of the larger pattern.

          I think of it as like being in a dysfunctional relationship, the kind of thing where consistent microaggressions and spikes in the extremes of at least one partner's personality recur without going so far as to create massive deal breakers that they become kind of the background noise, allowing the relationship to persist in a way that means somebody can miss that they're really in such a thing.

          You talk about how the oldest Exalted in the setting don't seem to have easily attributed cases of Limit Breaks to their name, but... none of them exactly seem to be doing well, do they? There's a consistency to them being somewhat alienated, having some hang ups that they're fixated on, and they arguably haven't often built up things to an extent one might assume of people that have had such long lives. We don't get the fine details of how all of this has come about, given the necessary and preferable limits of how much of a biography we get for them, but I think the devil can be inferred therein.

              I don't have any concrete examples. I don't know your alternate setting, or your players, but in my experience it is fine to get rid of the Great Curse entirely. I've run campaigns that way with no difficulty at all. Players are generally more than capable to roleplay how they want to play without it, and there isn't much in the setting that can't be handled or justified in other ways.

              My preferred approach today is that the Great Curse was inflicted on the individual Exalts alive at the end of the Divine Revolution, not the concept of Exaltation or the shards of Exaltation. So those survivors of the war suffered from it, but as they died off during the First Age, or during the Usurpation, the GC died with them. It lets you use it as desired to explain some of the events of the 1st Age, without having it be necessary in game play.

                              Originally posted by TryingToBeSlim View Post

                              My issue with the above is basically the First Age. It's a bit difficult for me to imagine the First Age becoming so bad if there were only consistent micro-agressions etc. If the First Age did become so horrible that the Ursurpation was justified...then it would have taken a lot more then what you described (In my opinion at least).
                              While there have been times in the past where writers opted to go with the idea that the end of the First Age was a time of exaggerated horrors, I think there's consistently been an issue with the credibility of that against making the Great Curse playable.

                              I'm inclined to think of that period in terms of the playable Virtue Flaws and extrapolating forwards from them. Some of that is in determining what the through line of the Flaws and their triggers might be, and thinking about what that would produce cumulatively.

                              From that, I get two ideas. One is that the First Age had become big and complicated enough, with the Solar Exalted assuming so much authority in it, that they become exposed to a constant feedback loop of Limit Triggers that means that their Virtue Flaws become pretty constant (particularly in the Third Edition milieu in which people don't have a single personal Virtue Flaw, so the extreme spikes of their behaviour become fairly inconsistent). The other is that they condition themselves into a point where their Limit Triggers (and general things that create friction in their Intimacies) become something that they have a strong aversion to; the consequence of that is too many Solars trying desperately to ensure that they can maintain absolute control over everything.

                              (And then the nature of the Curse means that when this attempt at control inevitably fails, it feeds back into some of the worst forms of Virtue Flaw).

                              The combination of these things plus the larger political context and the amount of power available at that time (between the number that have risen to high Essence and the amount of wonders that have been accumulated), and you create a gigantic powder keg. One that then has to interface with the fact that the people dealing with it also have their own Curses to affect their judgement.

                              All of that aside, the causes of the Solar Purge can't be used as the baseline for what the effects of the Great Curse are after that, because the other Exalted have Curses that function in different ways, and are explicitly supposed to be less extreme. Even if my idea of what the end of the First Age was like is invalid, it would not be relevant to a question of what the Great Curse has looked like in the Second Age.

                              Originally posted by TryingToBeSlim View Post
                              Thing is I really no longer understand the purpose of the Great Curse anymore. At best it seems like an excuse for Solars to have been horrible in the first age and so justify other splats rebelling etc...That's it. After that it basically disappears from history.
                              The primary purpose of the Great Curse is for the people playing the game, to create a system of "this is why sometimes you act like a Greek classical hero in the grips of some self-destructive irrationality". The end of the First Age is really extrapolating from that, rather than the other way around; an idea of "if the world was ruled by three hundred of Achilles who would periodically mope about in his tent, some other power players would eventually get really sick of that".

If you have a Principle of "no mercy for the Realm", you can accumulate Limit from being merciful to the Realm, even if that's you attempting to be better (or at least pragmatic). With Raksi as a Lunar, she could also have the factor of "not doing something terrible just because I want to is actually a chain that grates on me".

A Virtue Flaw as a result of Limit Break from one too many acts of mercy towards enemies could as easily be a session of irrational and excessive mercy towards everybody as it could be becoming totally and proactively merciless.

                                      Originally posted by TryingToBeSlim View Post
                                      That seems like a good idea. I might go with that. It at least justifies the Fall of the First Age and all the stuff that lead to it while giving a clear slate for later.
                                      If you do go that way, it opens up some doors to interesting possibilities. I've done a campaign using that and only that, and it worked well, and there is no need to go further. But if you want to, I think there are lots of directions you can take that story and still end up in something that is familiar to the modern setting in RY 768. My current campaign goes one step further and asks what does that change mean to the First Age? Here's the one I'm exploring now:

                                      Naturally, not all the elder Exalts who fought in the Divine Revolution survived to the end of the first age. Some died in internal conflicts between the Exalts, others from external threats like behemoths and Fair Folk, etc. Those who died passed on their Exaltations to new hosts who didn't have the GC. So as the First Age nears its end, you have these cursed but very powerful Elders, and then one or more generations of younger, less powerful, and less corrupted (on average; people are still people and some are worse than a GC) Exalts. But no one explicitly knows about the GC, though they have to be thinking something like "man, the Revolution did something to those guys..."

                                      So in a minor side plot of my game, using memories, dreams and flashbacks, I'm exploring the idea that the Sidereals had their big conference to decide the future. The Bronze faction wanted to wipe the other Celestials off the board, while the Gold wanted to "fix" things. In this continuity, the Gold won and they worked with the young Solars and Lunars and their DB allies who saw the dangers posed by the elders, and they staged their own revolution to overthrow the crazy Elders. It started with a big conflict, and turned into a years-long struggle. Many Gold faction Sids, and young Lunars/Solars died but they took out most of the worst elders. And during that time they started to notice that fresh Exaltations weren't happening, but things were too hot to spend a lot of time investigating.

                                      As the conflict wore down, the Bronze faction made their move, using their own DB allies to finish off the war-weary and numerically depleted other Celestials. That was the final betrayal, the actual Usurpation as it would be remembered (by some) a millennia later, but it really happened at the end of the overthrow and not the start of it, when the Bronze turned on their "allies" who had helped them take down the supposed threat. They got almost all of the Solars (young and old alike), and a sizable chunk of the Lunars. Those few Solars who were left and the remaining Lunars were now alerted to the true threat, so the Bronze took their secret project, the Jade Prison which had been capturing all the slain Celestial Exaltations, and they let out the Exaltations of the other Sidereals who were caught in it, and then sealed it away.

                                      The future goes mostly along familiar lines from there: Arcane Fate, Shogunate, Silver Pact (but smaller, less than half the Lunars compared to canon), Scarlet Empress, etc. When the prison is opened it brings back the lost Solars and Lunars.

                                      The main impacts on the modern game are related to the Lunars. You can have newly Exalted Lunars whose last incarnation was in the 1st Age. I haven't decided if I want to do this yet myself, but if you wanted to explore Abyssal or Infernal Lunars you could, since some of them were in the prison. It also solves one of my minor personal pet peeves about the 3E lore, where it feels like the new eternal-war Silver Pact has had way too little impact on the politics and history of the setting IMO, and justifies it because there were a lot fewer of them in this alternate continuity. The possibilities for memories of prior selves can range from "My last self was one of the monsters" to "my last self stayed out of it" to "my last self was one of the young rebels overthrowing the evil elders", and all three of those were betrayed by the Bronze Sids/Shogunate DBs.

                                      We're about a year into the campaign, but this hasn't been a focus in any way. My Solar PC is just having the first memories/dreams that are beginning to reveal a little of this, not enough to get the full picture but enough to start asking some awkward questions. Why does she remember fighting beside the Sids against her elder Circlemate?
