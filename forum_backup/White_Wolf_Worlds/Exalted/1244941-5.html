                                Is he? I don't think he's that old. I thought he was like 300.
                                But anyway, history shows that dictatorship (including monarchy) is a pretty common way of ruling a country. We never had the Scarlet Dynasty in the real world, yet for most of history, most people were ruled by either central dictators, or local chiefs.
                                In the Middle Ages, they looked back to Rome as being the greatest Empire ever, yet very few medieval Europeans created Republics like the Roman Republic.
                                Having the example of a giant centralised state under one monarch is a good encouragement for others to do the same, yet I don't think it's the primary reason they exist. Central and South Americans had never heard of China, or Rome, or Alexander the Great, yet the Mexica, Inca, etc, still had a central ruler (who of course, was not identical to European monarchs, but then, they weren't identical to each other anyway).

                                So, while I do agree that the Scarlet Dynasty is bad (and I feel it's not of little importance to differentiate that from Dragonblood, I think it's very important, just as you can't judge the Gold Faction by the Bronze Faction), I don't think it's their fault that Wavecrest is ruled by the Feathered One, or Skullstone is ruled by the Silver Prince, or Gem is ruled by the Despot, or as Alucard said, Paragon is ruled by the Perfect.

                                                Okay, the question of how representative or participatory various governments would be in their own right issssummm complicated. In character, one can probably at least have grievances based on the fact that the Realm promotes and enables activities that direct the resources of the land and its people for the benefit of the Realm, generally at the expense of the locals. Something like what was going on in the backstory of Perfect Soul; her family felt it was necessary to take extreme measures to remove dissidents, and then Mnemon came in to dig up their heritage and take a direct hand in crushing anybody that would protest that anyway, and what was the point of it all.

                                                They probably wouldn't express it in the terms that I have because a lot of them are educated differently, but it can still form a basis for opposition.

                                                That being said, following what I think was that example in Anatolia, it would be interesting if there was a satrapy in which the Realm instituted a form of government that was... probably not democratic, but at least in the form of a multi-part council, and there was dissatisfaction with it for some reason such as it being highly fractious by design, or just a means of giving direct authority to oligarchs in concert*, and the people kind of want something more direct and singular, if not an outright populist strongman.

                                                * Something that I've often found to render the conflation of pre-modern monarchs with modern dictators to be simplistic at best is that from what I can see, historically, monarchs have often been at odds with and prone to restraining the excesses of the landed upper classes, in a manner that at least incidentally benefited regular people, and in some cases even outright functioned as an authority to which commoners could appeal that would be over the head of local authorities.
