                    I'd say there's a place for something like that if we ever get a more elaborate description of what it means to feed the Fae Nature, although I'd still say it wouldn't exactly be a reassuring thing. A raksha might opt to feed only very lightly for the sake of not creating waves, but I think it would be consistent for that to have a negative effect on their mental state, and possibly create dangers of them becoming erratic and impulsive if more satisfying fare ever presented itself.

                    A person in a situation to perform that moral calculus might deem that it's preferable for them to be satiated and relatively stable, at the cost of a somewhat small number of lives, than one that is constantly hungry and on edge and might be provoked into a terrible rampage.

                    Still, the idea of being potentially benign is probably not enough to discount being creatures of darkness by itself. Like, some of the less harmful varieties of demon and probably a lot of ghosts fit that criteria and it's not enough to categorically exclude them, while there's still an option for individual good eggs to be excluded anyway.

                    Actually, it's occurring to me... wasn't there some sense in Second Edition that shaped raksha weren't Creatures of Darkness primarily because in the era that the Unconquered Sun defended Creation there were hardly any of them and they were largely beneath his notice? I think by the standards of those books, there was also an answer of him not really being in a position to judge them as a threat and add them to the list, whether they warranted being there or not.

                    I'm just thinking about that while I'm trying to assess questions of how to reconcile what the power of the Sun is and is not intrinsically anathema to when he's still supposed to have been once the direct guardian of the world. Mind, I suppose that could be resolved by him not exactly needing to inflict aggravated damage on everything he needs to fight off*, or just being able to invoke aggravated damage against everything he attacks by virtue of his sheer overwhelming might.

                    Ultimately, it might be a bit of a moot point. You don't really need to have the light of the sun channelled through the strength of his Chosen by the thing to harm them in such fashion when an abundant mundane metal will typically do so.

                    * Probably the biggest reason you don't need to inflict aggravated damage on the fae at all is if they don't exactly have much access to healing magic that would give it any distinction from lethal damage in the first place.

Many Raksha probably resulted from her tides anyway - unshaped stranded when the middlemarches turned to bordermarches fleeing through Sundraprisha?

                                Why aren't siaka creatures of darkness, or tyrant lizards, or giant parasitic wasps, or chakra orchids, the Guild, or those neighboring conquerers who will kill your family and take the survivors as slaves? There are tons of monsters that will kill, or destroy the souls of people while leaving the body alive. The Unconquered Sun doesn't seem to approve of these things, and he exalts those who will defeat such monsters, but they're not creatures of darkness/

                                CoD label is reserved for his personal enemies, the fallen primordials and their servants. Why aren't the raksha creatures of darkness, because he didn't fight a divine civil war against them with the other gods and their chosen and reorder the cosmos the lock them away.

                                Another angle might be that creatures of darkness are foes who can be defeated (theoretically). Demons and their masters can be locked back in their prison, the neverborn and their arrayed servants can be cast into oblivion and the cosmic order rightened. Raksha emerge from infinity, calling them out as specific enemies of the Sun would be like designating raindrops terrorists. Sure, you could do it, but it doesn't really accomplish anything. Slay an infinity of raksha with your creature of darkness slaying charms and infinity +1 will show up eventually. You don't defeat the rain by targeting individual drops, you build levees and umbrellas and shelters and since it is Exalted you punch the occasional hurricane in the mouth. The Chosen have the tools needed to fight the raksha, and they're not charms that hurt creatures of darkness more.

                                                It is stated in no uncertain terms that all undead count as creatures of darkness by default, and certain exceptions might be leveraged for particularly benevolent ones. The text for that found in Exalted: Essence has been quoted earlier in the thread, and the version in What Fire Has Wrought is hardly much different.

                                                I don't personally like making it much of a thing that it all ultimately extends back to the big Primordial originator, because I feel that takes too much focus away from the qualities of the beings themselves.

                                                Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post

                                                Do Abyssals even need to drink people anymore to get Essence in Creation in 3e? Because that used to be an ironclad thing to emphasize how they have to spread shadowlands every which way to have an advantage.
                                                The draft manuscript references nothing exceptional about how Abyssals recover Essence one way or another.

                                                Abyssals outside of the Underworld or Shadowlands suffer a -2 penalty to all actions unless they adorn themselves in deathly trappings or while residing in a deathly themed place.

                                                They can build Limit by interacting with the living as though one of them (distinct from being perceived as one of the living for infiltration purposes). How onerous this is can vary, since Abyssals build Limit somewhat slowly and under more specific circumstances than other Celestial Exalted, but also expend it more gradually and can have the effects of their Curse be more unpleasant.

                                                I think that, all in all, Third Edition leans into the idea that being a combination of the living and the dead gives Abyssal Exalted somewhat more latitude than other denizens of the Underworld, and this is suitable to the desires of the Neverborn because being able to walk somewhat more freely in Creation allows them to evangelize the chivalry of the dead.

                                                A thing to keep in mind about Third Edition Abyssal Exalted is that while they are sworn to the death of Creation, the Neverborn don't want Creation to die quickly. Abyssals are not to kill people at will, they have to break down people's desire to live utterly before doing so. Abyssals are to preference the deaths of grandiose figures and great champions of life before moving on to the masses. Abyssals are to break down the boundaries between the living and the dead by encouraging intermingling of the two.

                                                Notably, the enforcement mechanism for the chivalry of death is more in the carrot than the stick. Abyssals aren't punished for failure to act on opportunities to carry it out (barring the Limit they gain if they should deliberately saves or spares lives in a manner inconsistent with mercy for the chivalry of death; curing a dying person so they can go on hurts, curing them because their torment is not yet at an end is A-OK), they're rewarded with Willpower and relief from their limit when they carry it out. Even then, given the scope of how they build Limit in the first place, a big part of the role of the Deathlords is to actually indoctrinate Abyssals in an ideology of the chivalry of death for its own sake. They've got to persuade their deathknights that all of this slow murder of the world is worthwhile and compelling, because the dark Lawgiver who goes their own way has a lot of latitude to just kind of not do that and live their best life as they can within the boundaries of the kinds of powers you can wield as that kind of Exalted.

                                                ... Actually, I suppose the strongest external incentive to carry out the chivalry of death actually would come to an Abyssal who was actively trying to avoid living up to the image by trying to be among the living, since they'll accumulate Limit at the highest rate. It puts them in a state of tension whereby their attempt to remain in the sunlit lands can only be safely maintained by constantly doing things that unsettle or distress the people around you.

                                                In that way, the incentive to create shadowlands isn't so much because it's where they can respire Essence as it is that it can potentially be a more benign way to keep their Curse from harming themselves or others.

                                                            I had a response and then realized a smart person already said it:
                                                            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                                            I think the main reason for the shift is that the original developers wanted to make it more about something that would literally be harmed by the cleansing light of the sun, rather than being on a list of enemies.
                                                            I think that's consistent with a reversion past 2e, almost to 1e.

                                                            The first formal definition of Creature of Darkness that I can find is 2e core which is when Demons and Unshaped were added. In 1e Core the first reference I can find the "creatures of darkness", lower case, is in Melee and a family of Charms that have enhanced effects against "creatures of the night, the walking dead, and Abyssal Exalted".

                                                            I think 2e's definition was when we went to "the sun's shitlist", where before the idea was more a metaphysical state of dark that make you vulnerable to the Sun's light. Mountain Folk's Cephalids and Buried Gods seem more naturally "Creatures of Darkness" than most species of demon. See also Five Days Darkness. Similarly early 1e's metaphysical opinion of Fair Folk seemed to be more "Order resists Chaos" or "Order brings shape to Chaos", not "Order scourges Chaos".

                                                            All things considered I think my preferred definition of Creatures of Darkness would be "Anything from the Underworld, plus individuals and species of gods, demons, and stranger things with particularly flee-from-sunlight resonance. The Sun doesn't have any animosity to these CoDs as a class, though he does have specific animosities toward many of them.
