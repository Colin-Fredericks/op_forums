                        I don't want to take this thread on a whole different direction, so as a final point that remains tangentially connected to the resource needs of a rebellious group I'll just say that I think that historically, religious oppression has not quite carried that same capacity for a civilian population that can shelter the armed rebels, because I think that it more frequently takes a form where like, everyone from the heterodox group is being persecuted simultaneously. I think 19th to 20th century nationalism often operated against and took advantage of an authority that tried to promote a party line of attempting to accommodate and integrate the group that the rebels are hiding among.

                        That said, the other side to it is when there are less centralised administrations with pervasive governing apparatus and smaller armies rebellion from a position of secrecy is less necessary. You just kind of raise an army in a region where the government is weak. Your roving band is a bit less of a mobile cell in a decentralised network than it is a group of bandits with a certain political bent.

                        Mind, historically such groups have sometimes managed to form the seed around which a wider rebellious army crystallizes, like in say the start of Han China. Of course, in cases like that your resource needs increase dramatically.

                        But to bring it back to the subject of portraying that roving bandit group, I feel as though the big thing is that the matter of needing to address shortages of basic necessities should be something that comes up periodically, and with a measure of escalation. Like, every now and then a Story includes a scene where the players need to engage with some merchant who can hook them up with what they need, and those scenes have variations in the characters being interacted with each time or its the same contact but things keep getting harder for them and costs keep going up. One might introduce a chapter or even a whole Story in which something bigger than just haggling with the merchant is needed to secure something important or necessary, out to safeguard a supply chain that is under threat. And it's up to the players to look at these circumstances and challenges and realise that they ought to proactively engage in a more long term solution.

                        Those are things related to the Resources system, but not exactly the Merit, which I think is more about representing somebody who both had more of a reliable stream of income and circumstances that dictate something to continuously spend it on.

                        That would be why I wouldn't think the guy in the jungle sitting on a trove of previous metal really qualifies as having a Resources Merit. Not before they get to a point where they're engaging with people that will exchange that stuff for something the character finds useful or desirable. These things aren't objective measures of quantity and value, they're utilities for gameplay and narrative.

                            Resources has often been a bit tricky to try to fit in with other statted Merits, for us.

                            On the one hand, it's got dots. On the other hand, if you can sell your doorknobs to buy a nation, why keep track of it?

                            For this edition, we've been making Resources (and also things like Command, Allies, etc) things that you can build up with a leadership task. Something like: each dot requires (dot^2) successes, with a threshold of 5, and we can make a stunt and roll for it once per session while we're waiting on other people to show up. Or waiting on take-out to arrive.

                            If you don't have the dots, you can still pay for things (or order people around) but always with some sort of hitch or inconvenience. The market for doorknobs is a bit niche and needs just the right introductions.

                            I'm fully expecting Resources to break down as a game system when we hit Essence 3 or 4. At that point, we'll disregard it or simply describe the army of bureaucrats whisking funding requests directly to the treasury.
