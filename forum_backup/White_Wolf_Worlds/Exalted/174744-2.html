    Originally posted by Coik View Post

    I would disagree; what doesn't benefit the game is having a splat that is implicitly "Martial Arts and/or Sorcery or GTFO". That's why we see things like Murder Moving Fingers Dance, which has absolutely no damn business as a Martial Arts Charm, being written up as a Martial Arts Charm. There needs to be some kinda out for it, even if it's just "Yeah, there's a new Charm but it's really been there all along. What are you complaining about? You're Sidereals, nothing is supposed to make any goddamn sense. Your homework for next game session is to watch Twin Peaks."

    I think another reason that Sidereal Charms didn't fair too well in 2e is that it seemed to adopt a more...frantic pace. "The Reclamation is happening right now. At the same time, the Underworld is invading. Concurrently, Transformers have appeared in the Southwest. In a similar fourth dimensional time plane, a new Balorian Crusade is on the verge of breaking out. And the Realm collapsed 23 minutes ago. What are you doing hurry move move move move move."

    Who has time to cool their heels for five days while the Wood Spirits make you friendship bracelets?

    ...come to think, who has time to wait five days for any investigation? Just doing mundane investigation is far more efficient; the only real use I ever found for Embracing Life Method, love the concept, don't get me wrong, was for metagamy bullshit like "Why do the Solars of my acquaintance seem to become massive dickbags seemingly at random?" and "What's with these jackasses with the Yozi marks on their foreheads?"
    These are all good points, there's gotta be a place of finding a balance between ease of play and making Sidereal players work a little outside the box. From what I understand 1e might have done it a little better than 2e, but I think there's probably a lot of room for EX3 to be much better than both.

                        Okay, before we continue, can we please get the "Sidereal Charms are sooooo weeeiiiiirrrddd" thing to die in a fire? I made an entire thread about this before, but with only a few exceptions, 2e Sidereal Charms were no more odd than, say, Clover Can't Be Found (A Lunar Appearance Charm that lets you disappear in a crowd), or Know the Soul's Price (the Solar Investigation Charm that lets you buy peoples' souls). The insistence by some Sidereal fans that Sidereals are the duct-tape-and-screwdriver Exalted continues to baffle me, especially when they manage to forget about the occasional weirdness from other Charm sets (hey, let me take this Dragon-Blooded Performance Charm to be invisible!).

                        Look, the idea of a deliberately limited Charm set with an unusual, slightly-overpowered construction is, if not good, then at least interesting in theory. In practice, the hard in-canon limit on Sidereal Charms has caused way more harm than good, essentially preventing high-level Sidereal games that don't involve everyone being master Martial Artists or Sorcerers, and ensuring Sidereal players didn't get any new Charms until Glories of the Most Motherfucking High. And even then, they had to use the Astrological Charm and Martial Arts workarounds to get that much new stuff in.

                        We've done the experiment. We've used the limited Charm set approach. It failed. So can we just skip it this time around? Please? So I can play a Sidereal without having to battle my ST over getting new Charms?

                                        Originally posted by MrInsecure View Post

                                        Well, it depends on the context. If you want to talk about the merits of the ideas that motivated Sidereal design, you do need to look at the case where it's worked best. Saying that "we've tried this and it didn't work" because it didn't work in 2e is sort of like saying "I've tried wearing clothing to protect my feet and it was proved a failure" because you bought secondhand shoes two sizes too small for you, then wore them without laces and made some cuts along the sides to make room for your foot. It's all well and good to talk about 2e Sidereals, in other words, but it's important you not conflate the flaws of 2e SIdereals with flaws of the philosophy that motivated the Sidereal charmset originally, and that you recognize that the way people talk about Sidereals is largely inherited from First Edition -- both because Sidereals were unplayable for the majority of 2e and thus most people who played them did it in 1e, and also because those ideas do echo forward to a limited extent in their 2e charmset, purely by accident.

                                        In 1e, the idea of an Essence 5+ game was largely conjectural -- "high power" was Ess 4-5. 3e seems to be returning to this model. In 1e, Sidereals had way more Ess 4-5 charms in their hardback than Solars did in core, because the game was meant to carry you all the way to high power, and that was what "high power" was. Elder essence was an undiscovered country that largely existed for the aesthetic effect it had on the actual game, so it was never really meant to have serious support in a system compatible with the regular game. 2e changed this without recalibrating the Sidereal charmset to match, but I think it's probably better to just re-establish Essence 1-5 as the "real" game, with 4-5 being the "ridiculously high-powered endgame" level, and in that context, Sids are pretty well supplied.

                                                            Ah! I see what you mean. My impression is that 3e is planning things out much more heavily from the start, and is attempting to include the entire breadth of each splat's charms in its main hardback in order to ensure that there's not a need to go back later and do the second half of everybody's charmsets. And, of course, any mid-stream changes to the line do need to consider the degree to which they'll break major blocks of material that precede them.

                                                            But yeah, it is certainly possible that the line could shift in a way that leaves Sidereals in the lurch. I think that if this happens it's indicative of a broader design failing with the game line, but certainly there will be some broader design failings exposed in the first eight years of a popular RPG's life; that's inevitable. In that case I think it is fine for the writers to errata Sidereals as needed, or to retroactively add more charms to their set. The main feature of the closed charmset is that the players can't make new charms, because it's meant to be a carefully tuned machine, and it'd be like allowing the players to invent new moves for a WoW class, or give new specials to a fighting game character. And yet, it's fine for the developers to do that, so long as they do it carefully and with the original thesis of the class/character in mind. It's not closed in the sense that it's "in the can" and no changes can ever happen to it ever at a meta-game level, in other words, just in that it's not arbitrarily extensible, and homebrew is not endorsed for it in the same way it is for other charmsets.
