    Originally posted by Aleks View Post
    An argument was made that since the crafting of an artifact could take years, characters would logically have access to their full mote and Willpower resources for every roll, to which a counter was made that if this was the case, why give Craft Charms costs at all?
    I believe this is the intent of the Craft rules - crafting projects aren't their own independent scenes, but rather take place over the course of several scenes. This does mean, yes, that crafters will usually have their full mote pools and fairly topped-up Willpower pools available. While there are definitely some Craft charms where it seems pretty much pointless to have a cost (Copper Spider Conception, for instance), I think the mote costs for most are still at least possibly relevant, for a couple of reasons. First, the possibility of being attacked or otherwise challenged in the middle of a scene of crafting is always there. If your crafter has just activated a bunch of Craft charms, and then the ninjas burst in, they may be in trouble.

    Second, and more proactively, remember that once you complete the initial work period for a project, it takes much less time, possibly even no time, to complete the subsequent rolls. You just have to have the craft XP handy. So if you're going into a big fight, the crafter could have most of the work done on a game-changing artifact, and just need to complete some or all of the rolls, which could potentially be done in combat time. For example, imagine the circle is fighting Ligier. The Twilight has been building an artifact shield that will block and reflect Ligier's deadly radiance, but the ST has declared that one required component was tempering it with a direct blast of Ligier's light. This being accomplished, the Twilight could pour on her motes and such to try to finish all the rolls in record time, finishing it just in time to toss it to the Dawn, who then blasts Ligier in the face with his own radiation. In that sort of scene, the cost of the Craft charms involved is very relevant.

        Originally posted by Uknown DarkLord View Post

        I'd peg that more as a charisma+craft fire roll. You're using your force of personality to show how it is done.
        Note that I'm talking about "swordwork" in the sense of using a sword, to fight, not making a sword. So it would probably be Melee in your suggestion, rather than Craft. :-)

        That said, I would argue that teaching should pretty much always be using Lore as the Ability. Lore has been defined as the teaching Ability at least since 2e - note how the Lore Charms in both 2e and 3e include effects to teach people stuff from all Abilities, not just academic topics. High Lore is "being a good teacher". Also, if you make it use the Ability in question to teach it, you end up cutting off stories where someone is a great master of something, but bad at passing on that knowledge, which is a pretty common plot element, I'd say.

        As for using Appearance, bear in mind that I'm answering from a 3e perspective here, where Appearance is a bit broader than just "how pretty (or ugly, with Hideous merit) you are". Pretty much any social roll can be rolled with Appearance as part of the pool, not just Charisma and Manipulation, for example. To me, Appearance is better described as the Attribute of "non-verbal communication". If you're primarily making your argument/demonstrating/inspiring emotion with how you look and move, rather than what you say, you're using Appearance. A warlord inspiring her troops by screaming inarticulately and charging the enemies' line, hoping they'll follow her, is rolling (Appearance + Presence) for instance.

                                            Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                                            If a second-circle demon is killed without using God-Eating Technique, do they immediately come back to life in Malfeas, or does it take some time?
                                            There's nothing I know of in 3e to answer this. However, in 2e, the Roll of Glorious Divinity Volume 2: Ghosts and Demons (box "An Immortal's Death", p. 15), it says that a Second Circle demon comes back the same way gods do, then says that "Octavian will reform in Malfeas after a year and a day...". I assume the "year and a day" part is a general thing for Second Circles, not specific to Octavian. However, if you want to interpret it differently, you could also fall back on the line about them returning "like puissant gods", and use the rules from Roll of Glorious Divinity Volume 1, which says (on p. 18, "Death") that a slain god reforms within its sanctum within "weeks or months".

                                            Personally, I'd use the "year and a day" guideline. If it's too much shorter than that, I think there's a real danger of PCs seeing a Second Circle bound to some other sorcerer and saying "let's kill them so we can summon them ourselves!".
