                    Originally posted by Ulthwithian View Post
                    I guess. The issue there is with permanent. Most of my players would essentially equate this with death, with the same end result. Again, I don't have an issue punishing players when they screw up. I, frankly, don't think it's very fair to ask my players to accept permanent debilitations to their characters because I screwed up.
                    Permanent is...not necessarily completely accurate here. In the rule at the end it reminds you that Exalted is a world of miracles, and gods, Exalted, artifacts, ect can be used to fix these things after the fact. Just don't make it totally trivial.

                    The rule is on page 201 of the corebook if you haven't seen it yet. Having a buffer of 5 emergency health levels, and really you could easily houserule that last bracket to be 5-8 or more even, really helps. It puts so many layers of defense in for the players for dying. If an enemy is going to one shot them they need to build up the crazy amount of initiative needed to one-hit a player, keep it long enough to launch a decisive, and then don't mess up the damage roll, and have done it to a character that's already used his crippling injury for the story.

                    If your player has 13 health levels the average decisive damage you need to one-shot him is 33i. If you're using the rule and saying only 5 damage can be negated it's 45i. There's basically no way to build that up in a single turn, so on the way up there some PC is going to knock down the enemy that's trying for it.



                    Also, try running more action scenes, not just combats, in a shorter period of time, but making them much easier. If your players are laying siege to a demon fortress with Octavian in them, instead of just letting them walk in there and roll Join Battle against Octavian, his Agata, his Infernal summoner and a pair of Blood Apes or something. Have a battle against outrider scouts that are trying to get away to warn the fortress, then one where they're outside at the gate facing a battlegroup of cultists and their leader, then maybe a chase scene or something where they leap through an ever changing and shifting fortress-maze that's trying to crush them, until finally they get to the throne room.

                    That helps in a lot of ways. Firstly it can help you stealthily fine-tune things that you may have misjudged. So if after the battlegroup of cultists they're looking hurt and a bit out of motes, maybe tone down the maze, and drop the apes from the final fight. Secondly, it can help for the overall flow and grandiosity of Exalted antagonists and situations. The kinds of things that Solars fight are massive, entire nations, mile-high behemoths, stuff like that, so it makes it actually feel big. Thirdly, it helps make encounters challenging without making them deadly. If your big action section is focused on draining your PC's motes, willpower, and maybe dealing a point or two of health level damage, they're really going to feel the squeeze. They'll be rationing motes, trying to use tactics instead of just powering through everything, and maybe even consider retreating if they reach the last leg with nothing left.

                    Lastly it just opens up the enemy variety a lot. There's still a bunch of enemies that are so weak you shouldn't really use them, all starting a fight with them is going to do is let your PCs regen motes, but the things they battle don't need to actually be able to win, they just need to force your players to spend 10m every round fighting them
