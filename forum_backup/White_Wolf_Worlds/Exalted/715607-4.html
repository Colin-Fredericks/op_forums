                Hang on, folks, this is the long one.

                Craft

                Number of Charms: 60(!)
                By Ability requirement: 1/2/9/5/43
                By Essence requirement: 18/15/17/5/5
                Nonstandard dice tricks: Gain sxp or gxp for each 10 rolled, “roll a free full Excellency,” reroll 10s and 6s until they disappear, bonus non-Charm dice and successes, buy an extended die cap for your Excellency, roll an extra die for every 3 successes recursively, gain an extra 3 dice if the last entry turns up 3+ successes, make your Excellency cheap for a day.

                Well. Here we go. Craft is structures a bit peculiarly; first off, it’s by far the largest tree so far, easily twice as large as several of the others. Second, even in the context to this point, it’s strikingly top-heavy; almost 3/4 of them require a Craft Ability at 5, and nearly half require at least Essence 3. Craft is also unique in that it doesn’t follow the “All the Essence 1s, then the E2s, E3s, etc.” pattern that every other Ability has so far. Instead, it’s grouped in a series of (intersecting) sub-trees, with each sub-tree arranged in the normal order. This makes some logical sense, given the sheer size of the overall tree.

                In any event. The Craft Charms, in the order they appear:

                Charms that help with Efficiency, i.e., “saving on crafting points”:
                1. (2*Essence) free Major slots.
                2. Reduce cost of making a Major slot.
                3. Convert dots from one Craft Ability to a related one.
                4. Spend gxp to raise a new Craft Ability.
                5. Convert 4sxp <-> 2gxp <-> 1wxp
                6. Gain (Major Slots) gp at the end of every story.
                7. Gain one Superior slot.
                8. Reduce cost of making a Superior slot.
                9. Earn sxp while making a Superior project.
                10. Finish a Superior project at lower cost.
                11. Build Superior projects in Major slots.
                12. Once a season, whip out a free 2-dot Artifact.
                13. Chain the gxp from one Superior project into the next, making it easier and more rewarding.
                14. Gain two Legendary slots.
                15. Convert all wxp into real experience points.
                16. Spend wxp 5:1 in place of real experience points for Lore Charms and similar effects.
                17. On finishing a Legendary project, gain bonus successes to your next Superior/Legendary.
                18. While building 10 Artifacts of the same level simultaneously, immediately finish one of them at no cost.
                19. On death, reveal that the you that died was really a magic-robot-clone, and the real you has been elsewhere the whole time.
                Notation switches here from “[color] experience points, which is the notation used in Ch. 5, to simply “[color] points.” I like this better.

                Exegesis of the Distilled Form (Efficiency, #15) has a pile of costs to reduce its efficacy, and caps at 20 XP in any event… but it still fundamentally bugs me. Spend-xp-to-get-xp is not a great thing to have, whatever the caveats; either Craft is overpriced and this balances it out, in which case make Craft cheaper so this isn’t the must-have for parity, or Craft is priced appropriately and this makes it undercosted, in which case this should go away. Neither option strikes me as particularly good.

                I am strongly tempted to simply remove project slots as a concept, which immediately pares off about ten Charms.

                Dual-Magnus Prana (Efficiency, #19, i.e. “the Doombot Charm”) is just as vexing as it ever was. I’ve heard this described as the “Lupin in the turkey” problem: it doesn’t matter how good your Solar Investigator was, or how thoroughly he searched the scene, if Lupin can retroactively declare himself to have been hiding in the turkey the whole time. In the same way, doesn’t much matter how thorough your ambush plans, or how intense your magical scrutiny, if it turns out the Solar was a robot, retroactively. Surprise! This is before we come to setting-integrity questions about the ability to have fully-functional Solar duplicate clones running around. (Why just one? Why not have you and your doombot active simultaneously?)

                Momentum (i.e., “get more points):
                1. When you roll for a project with no Excellency, gain sxp for each 10.
                2. +1sp per objective on a Basic project
                3. +1gp when meeting all objectives on a project
                4. Finish a Basic or Major project in moments, with your bare hands.
                5. Roll dice every story, gain sxp or gxp for roll.
                6. Gain sxp and gxp for each Craft Ability at 5, once a day.
                7. Accelerate Superior or Legendary projects to weeks/months.
                8. Gain 3 wxp/story.
                9. Invent artifact tools out of the air for a temporary purpose.
                Heck yeah, Craftsman Needs No Tools (#4). Stay awesome.

                Peerless Paragon of Craft (#5) – why “roll a free full Excellency?” Why not just roll the pool? It’s the same number of dice.

                One of the more interesting suggestions I’ve heard – thanks, Aquillion! – was to use Words-As-Workshop Method (#9) as a way of playing a Girl Genius-style character – always able to pull some useful gadget out of nowhere. That sounds fun! It seems implicit, but is not actually stated, that the wonders you create in this way shouldn’t be, say, an occult lens that boosts your energy attacks – that could stand to be explicit, either way.

                Repairing and Reforging (i.e., repairing and reforging):
                1. Break stuff.
                2. Make a mundane object permanently durable.
                3. Repair a nearly destroyed thing.
                4. Create a major slot for repairs only.
                5. Extra craft points for repairs.
                6. Make an object temporarily nigh-invulnerable.
                7. An object – and anyone nearby – can resist the Wyld for extended periods.
                8. You and allies gain (Essence) dice to rolls using smarty-pants Abilities.
                9. Add Evocations to a weapon.
                10. Make an object that lasts forever – an ever-burning candle, say.
                11. Bonus successes to repair an object.
                12. Switch up which Evocations in a weapon are live.
                13. Add Evocations to a weapon again.
                14. Free Evocations.
                I really like Durability-Enhancing Technique (#2); while it goes against my usual “clear task you couldn’t otherwise perform” standard for good Charms, it gives good reason for a Solar to make mundane things in a way mortals can’t, which is cool. Chaos-Resistance Preparation (#7) is similarly interesting, giving the crafter a handy way to march his army into the teeth of madness. So is The Art of Permanence (#10), which lets you make pseudo-minor-artifacts by imbuing mundane things with permanence. This is definitely my favorite Craft sub-tree so far.

                Crack-Mending Technique (#3) is very cool – I can see a Solar investigator sifting through the ashes, piecing a burned letter together again. It’d be nice if the actual crafting side of it had more than some vague “This may reduce difficulties, I dunno,” language.

                Design Beyond Limit (#9) and Hero-Forging God Weapon (#13) are hard to evaluate, given that they talk about Evocations in a way that no longer applies (I’m told – haven’t gotten there yet). Soul-Forge Tempering (#14) is a little vexing, in that “pay XP for a Charm, so you can spend more XP on a weapon, so you can save XP on Evocations” way. Do other Solar XP sinks – sorcery, martial arts, etc. – get this kind of discounting from a properly-invested character? I could see Sidereals having something similar for teaching.

                Power (i.e., POWAH, UNLIMITED POWAH):
                1. Reroll 10s until they disappear (and 6s, with repurchase).
                2. Free full Craft Excellency once a week.
                3. Basic and Major projects get double 9s, 8s, or 7s, with repurchases.
                4. Act as if you had three more Craft Charms than you did; you can switch up which ones.
                5. Bonus non-Charm successes/dice on non-Basic projects. (Why both?)
                6. Spend craft points for bonus non-Charm successes.
                7. Convert non-successes to 10s, for every three of a kind success you roll.
                8. Infinite Craft Mastery. Like, literally, exactly that, except daylong.
                9. Spend sxp to raise the limit of your Excellency.
                10. Roll once for a Superior or Legendary project; this doesn’t count against the maximum allowed rolls.
                11. Roll an extra die for every three successes on a Craft roll.
                12. Get an extra roll before the end of Superior/Legendary projects.
                13. If #11 produces 3+ more successes, add 3 more dice.
                Flawless Handiwork Method (#1) again fails to specify “keeping successes” as you reroll 10s.

                Bright-Forging Prana (#4), whatever that means, is just irritating – buy one Charm now so you can get any three free Charms you want, swapping ‘em out once a story.

                Unbroken Image Focus (#6) requires mentally separating “# successes” and “# dice that came up a success,” which sounds like trivial, but obnoxious, mental overhead.

                The bean-counting game is strong in this tree. First Movement of the Demiurge (#7) is probably the worst offender here, though Divine Inspiration Technique (#11) and Horizon-Unveiling Insight (#12) are about as bad. I can’t even really picture what it would look like to roll this tree with all the bells and whistles running – and even then, in all likelihood it’s going to be one of six or more rolls that you need to make, not the one that actually ends the project. I can’t help but feel like you’d get the same result as a lot of this just by saying, I don’t know, “You get +1 success for every 4 successes you rolled; you may repurchase this Charm (Essence) times.”

                I made a list of all the Charms that revolve around slots or silver points, and there are about 18-19 of them. I suspect if you cut those, and brought the tree down to a more manageable 41 – still 41 Charms! – the result would feel less absurdly bloated. (You would probably want to eliminate sxp entirely in this case, but I'm not viewing that as a negative.) Note that you start with 15 Charms, and by Essence 5 you could have bought up to 37 more; if you spent all of that on Craft, you still wouldn't have the full tree presented here.

                Yes: Most of the repair and reforging tree. Craftsman Needs No Tools, i.e. “Craftsman Actually Gets to Do Things On Camera.”
                No: The 10+ Charms that do nothing but grant slots, or make slots cheaper, or allow you to use different slots, or slots slots slots slots slots. My how-many-in-this-tree numbers don’t even consider that most of those can be repurchased multiple times.
                Just Plain Weird: So does the game want you to make basic projects, or not? Are these an important part of preserving the narrative of a crafter, or are they busywork to bypass once you’re a Solar? The actual crafting system says the former; the plethora of “ALL THE SXP, FOREVER” Charms say very much the latter, once they’ve mugged you for a few of your Charms.

                                                Dodge

                                                Number of Charms: 26
                                                By Ability requirement: 0/1/4/5/16
                                                By Essence requirement: 8/10/7/0/1
                                                Nonstandard dice tricks: +1 Evasion for every 1 or 2 your enemy rolls, enemy loses 1i per 1 rolled, enemy loses one success per 1 rolled, gain 1i per 1 or 2 enemy rolls.

                                                That’s exactly one Charm over Essence 3, for those of you keeping score at home, and something like 2/3 of them at Ability 5.

                                                The Charms:
                                                1. Spend Initiative to raise Dodge, breaking the cap.
                                                2. Disengage with double-9s.
                                                3. Keep the Initiative for Disengaging (or even earn it back, if you close again).
                                                4. Have Evasion 2+ when ambushed.
                                                5. After dodging an effect, move one range band.
                                                6. When you dodge, the enemy loses 1i.
                                                7. Your Evasion wins ties, rather than losing them.
                                                8. Remove any penalties to Evasion.
                                                9. Disengage while at short range, rather than close.
                                                10. On a successful dodge, redirect the attack to anyone at close range.
                                                11. Gain the 1i lost by the opponent from #6.
                                                12. Perfectly dodge any attack.
                                                13. Opponent loses 1i for each 1 rolled on the attack.
                                                14. +1 non-Charm bonus to Evasion for rest of scene.
                                                15. Ignore all Evasion penalties against enemies with lower Initiative. Cut cost of #1 in half. Gain 1i each round you are at close range and not struck.
                                                16. Convert enemy 1s into -1 successes.
                                                17. #7 and #16 don’t count as Charms for the purpose of #21.
                                                18. Cancel damage at 4m/die. Or maybe 4m/success. Who knows?
                                                19. Retreat twice after disengaging instead of once.
                                                20. On a successful dodge, gain 1i per 1 or 2 enemy rolls.
                                                21. On a successful dodge without Charms, crash the enemy and steal his Initiative.
                                                22. Learn an enemy Charm and become immune to its effects.
                                                23. Create extra -0 health levels; if hit by an attack that only damages these levels, you count as dodging it.
                                                24. Gain 1i for every -0 health level you lose.
                                                25. Stand still for a round, and heal health levels.
                                                26. After using SSE, gain 1i/round until you are struck or move too far away.

                                                Reed in the Wind (#1) seems very unclearly worded. The text says “Reed in the Wind raises the cap on how much the Solar can enhance her Evasion by her Essence.” So: is this saying that’s a permanent improvement in the Solar’s cap, just for knowing the Charm? Is it saying that as long as I invest at least 2i in a bonus through Reed in the Wind, I can buy extra bonuses from my Excellency? Is it saying that I can buy an extra (Essence) points of Evasion, but only via Reed in the Wind? What?

                                                Leaping Dodge Method (#5) is not explicitly a Disengage – does it benefit from Shadow Dancer Method (#3)?

                                                Can I use Shadow Over Water (#8) to negate, say, a Solar Brawler’s ability to add my current Onslaught penalty to his Defense? If SOW “removes any penalties to the Exalt’s Evasion,” does that mean I can be not suffering from Onslaught while he uses Wind and Stones Defense? (Actually, is Wind and Stones defense just useless, unless your enemy has been stuck with persistent Onslaught penalties? Normally, those go away on your turn, right?)

                                                Is 0i a suitable number to spend to count as “using” Reed in the Wind, for the purposes of resetting Seven Shadow Evasion (#12)? (P.S.: Hi, SSE!) Presumably not, but the rules don’t indicate as much, and in general, “no cost” is a suitable cost to pay for a Charm – look at all those 0-cost Reflexives.

                                                Vaporous Division (#18) contradicts itself – its cost says it’s 4m/die, while its text says it’s 4m/success.

                                                Hundred Shadow Ways (#22) has a sidebar explicitly clarifying its usage. Which is good, I guess, because the sidebar says things that the Charm text doesn’t even vaguely imply – such as that an attack enhanced with only the Charm you’re “studying” automatically fails. From the Charm text, you’d think such an attack only resolved as it normally would without the enhancing Charm.

                                                Themes of Dodge: Charging up from not getting hit, daring your enemies to take swings, disengaging, and time travel(?).

                                                Yes: Most of these effects are pretty solid, honestly – I like almost all of them. Seven Shadow Evasion – our first perfect defense! – probably takes the cake, though.
                                                No: Dice triiiiiiiicks.
                                                Just Plain Weird: Reflex Sidestep Technique (#4) is just… well, plain weird. It explicitly undoes damage, in its fluff – which doesn’t match its mechanical effect at all (“better dodging when ambushed”). It also has a weird side effect – it gives you 2 Evasion, +1 for every 1 or 2 the enemy rolls; you can only use Dodge Charms if this gets you back to full base Evasion. That means the worse you are at dodging, and so the lower your Evasion is, the more likely you are to be able to use Seven Shadows Evasion (say). Harm Dismissing Meditation (#25) is even weirder – it actually does undo damage, which is described as “stepping outside the moment” you were hurt at some point after the fact.

                                                            Integrity

                                                            Number of Charms: 31
                                                            By Ability requirement: 2/1/5/3/20
                                                            By Essence requirement: 6/13/10/2
                                                            Nonstandard dice tricks: Gain 1m for every 1 or 2 rolled on social influence or curses, (Essence) of your enemy’s 1s are treated as -1 success or (Essence) successes are rerolled.

                                                            Roughly 2/3 of the Charms are at Ability 5; again, there are only 2 in the E4-E5 range. (Weirdly, the E1 range is also pretty thin – this set lives heavily in the E2-E3 range. Integrity is also the only tree (so far) to use the Bridge keyword, which allows a number of these Charms to be taken without actually buying into Integrity. Glancing through, the theory seems to be that martial artists, in particular, should be able to meditate without entering Integrity. Not sure I follow the motivation, there, but all right.

                                                            The Charms:
                                                            1. Ignore penalties from injuries and other harm to your Resolve and Guile.
                                                            2. Gain +2 Resolve against issues you’ve resisted in the past.
                                                            3. Resist the natural twisting power of the Charm.
                                                            4. Magic can alter you, but it cannot immediately render you unable to be who you are. (“Turn into a duck” becomes “Become duck-ish, but continue to daiklave things just fine; also, you can break this curse.”)
                                                            5. Become immune to any curse you’ve broken via #4.
                                                            6. Spend 5m for 1wp when resisting social influence or magic.
                                                            7. Gain 1m for every 1 or 2 rolled on social influence or curses.
                                                            8. +1 Resolve when defending with Ties/Principles. Effect stacks with multiple Solars.
                                                            9. Meditate for an hour; gain +2 successes on a concentration-based roll.
                                                            10. Treat a use of #9 as a full night’s sleep, also restoring 20m and resetting 1/day Charms.
                                                            11. Reject influence even after being persuaded.
                                                            12. Make one of your Principles (nearly) inviolable.
                                                            13. Sense when someone nearby is considering acting against a Principle you like.
                                                            14. Pray with your followers for an hour; for the duration, they can resist the Wyld, disease, and hardship.
                                                            15. Use your Willpower to resist an attack with no clear means of defense.
                                                            16. Your followers gain Intimacies and bonus dice when they see you succeed socially.
                                                            17. Your sermons feed your followers.
                                                            18. Outside combat, turn motes into Willpower.
                                                            19. 1/day, raise your Resolve at 1m/point instead of 2m/point.
                                                            20. After being forced to act against an Intimacy, shatter the effect controlling you.
                                                            21. When your Intimacies are challenged, ignore the cost of three Charms to resist harm or influence.
                                                            22. While using #9, you cannot be influenced, but you also can’t react to others.
                                                            23. Steal successes from your enemy’s social influence.
                                                            24. Use a Defining Principle to shake off major contradictory magical effects.
                                                            25. Everyone around you realizes you have a particular Defining Principle.
                                                            26. Learn a bunch of non-Eclipse-tagged Spirit Charms, as long as they fit your Principles and Charms.
                                                            27. When using #9, heal and avoid the need for food.
                                                            28. While using #9, become immune to harm.

                                                            Stubborn Boar Defense (#2) makes a reference to “the Solar’s Lunar mate.” But the earlier Lunar section spoke of the destruction of the Lunar-Solar bond – one wonders how these will interact in future publications.

                                                            Integrity Protecting Prana (#3) is permanent, but with a cost. You have to activate it. What? Also, what? Like… what possible reason is there for this not to be Reflexive, at this point? It’s also scaled waaaaay back, functioning only against twisting environmental effects and not directed action.

                                                            I kind of like Destiny Manifesting Method (#4), but it’s there to fight off effects for which we have no examples. I want to see the use before evaluating its benefit.

                                                            I was very excited by Undying Solar Resolve (#7) until I noticed it’s another “pool of motes that can only be used on one thing.” I do not need five of these.

                                                            Mind-Cleansing Prana (#9) is… odd. The die bonus isn’t great, and neither is the hourlong duration – though it can reward you with a WP at the end. (The cost seems almost meaningless, honestly – if you have an hour to kill, you could recover 5m, right?) On the other hand, this becomes the basis for a lot of the upper parts of the Integrity tree; they really, really want you to play Integrity Guy as this sort of… meditative, invulnerable messiah figure. There’s a pretty clear archetype in here, actually, which is kind of cool.

                                                            Energy Restoration Prana (#10) resets your 1/day Charms when you use it. It, uh… it’s a 1/day Charm, with no cost. I think the intent is clear, buuuuuuut…

                                                            Righteous Lion Defense (#12) really shows the change in mindset from 2e – rather than a perfectly inviolable defense, it’s a Principle against which the enemy must succeed twice if they want to convince you to act against it.

                                                            I’m a little uncomfortable with Phoenix Renewal Defense (#15)’s implication that there are attacks with no clear defensive vector.

                                                            Spirit-Tempering Practice (#18) blows my mind a little – it’s a flat 4m->1wp conversion engine, with no restrictions other than “not in combat” and “once a scene.” This is the biggest remover of the “No, willpower is really important you guys” philosophy that – I had thought – Ex3 was holding to pretty tightly; several of the, “Under these exact circumstances, you may regain a Willpower once” effects look pretty pale if you can just flat convert motes to Willpower multiple times a session.

                                                            Righteous Soul Judgment (#21) says it can be used “to complete a feat of strength” – does that mean you auto-succeed, or what? Do you still need to meet the Strength prereq?

                                                            Eminent Paragon Approach is an E3 Charm to let everyone know you have a Principle. Other methods of achieving this end: announce “I feel really strongly about X! Also, I have 8 XP available!”

                                                            Themes: caring for your followers, being a wise meditative figure, being completely unflappable. Fits very well with what is seems like Integrity should do.

                                                            Yes: Most of the twist-resisting Charms. Most of the messianic effects.
                                                            No: Divine Mantle (#26) completely bypasses the otherwise very firm limits on Spirit Charmshare. There are, uh, some pretty mean spirit powers out there.
                                                            Just Plain Weird: Spirit-Tempering Practice. “Willpower’s really hard to get, unless you’re this guy, and then eh.”
