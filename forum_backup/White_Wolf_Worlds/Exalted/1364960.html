    What's the plot of your game?

    02-03-2020, 03:49 AM
    Are you breaking chains in the South? Uniting the tribes in the North? Manipulating nobles on the Isle? What's going on?

    My group is sailing the Western Ocean finding four ancient geomantic "anchors" that are binding a First Age Lunar Leviathan to the bottom of the sea, and they've heard nothing but contradictory stories of his motivations and why he was locked down there. They only have one anchor left before the big showdown- what does Teeth of the Deep want, and why was he imprisoned instead of killed?

    How about you?

          So when I think about the game I would run, I always return to the same germ of an idea: The lovable waif who wasn't.

          Its a coming of age story where our circle comes into contact (hopefully as unobtrusively as I can manage as an ST) with said waif who idolizes them, wants to emulate their greatness. She/he/they become a constant companion, a side-kick of sorts, and the players see themselves meaningfully reflected in their gradual maturity.

          Of course multitudes of dark forces gather to either influence or kidnap said waif, but why?

          In my original telling (that I'm not saying has to be your telling) it's because the waif is a young primordial (and the first Creation born of their ilk) who represents the zeitgeist of the current age, and the players have a real opportunity to shape, but not stop, their apotheosis.

          I recall someone kindly made up a series of pseudo-charms to represent the growing power of the littlest primordial, I should go look for those.

                  The game I'm running is based around Gem. The first sessions had plot, though they were mainly about establishing the city, familiarizing everyone with the districts and the society. The overall plot, though, revolves around one of the Sahlak daughters having been manipulated into falling head-over-heels in love with Makarios (the fact that he got statted for 3.0 was an unexpected bonus). Through various complicated manipulations involving a Fair Folk siege and a trio of powerful demon-bloods assisting her, she bought Makarios's hand in marriage for 100,000 marked souls and brought him into Creation, along with a city sculpted from dreams to supplant Gem. The Despot was driven from the city and is scheming to get his throne back and the characters are currently working to collect allies around the region to fight the Despotina and her consort. They also know that they need to figure out how to break a Malfean matrimonial contract so that Makarios will be expelled from Creation and thrown back into Hell. We're 67 games in now, so obviously there's been a bunch of other stuff that's happened, piles of various NPCs, and stories that have only somewhat interacted with the main plot. But long run, it's building up to the PCs finding a way to annul the marriage and probably to storm Gem with whatever forces they can muster and retake it.

                  If and when they do that, they get to figure out what to do with the city. Run it and deal with the various hassles involved, leave it to some friendly king and go off in search of their past lives, or abandon it entirely and go off in search of other adventure. The PCs will probably have a lot of leeway at that point.

                          The PCs actually just discovered the plot (well, some of it) after... 13 sessions? Something like that. They're only Essence 2.

                          The first story arc was the collapse of a Lunar-led kingdom due mostly to the corruption of the Changing Moon King and his court. But the collapse was incited and pushed by the Guild, a Raksha, and a group of mercenaries who wore blue-and-white, or red (I guess you could say vermillion), led by Dragonblood samurai and Yamabushi.

                          In the second arc, while the Lunar PCs eventually made peace with the Dragonblood and got them to leave, the land was wracked by earthquakes and an eternal winter. If not ended, humanity may starve to death.
                          When the PCs spied on the Dragonblood and talked to gods, they heard it rumoured that Mela's brothers Jormungandr the Earth Dragon, and Sutr the Fire Dragon, may be dead, and Mela might be angry, hence the winter. It seemed that the Sacred Land to the South, the Dragon Empire ruled by the Red Queen, has been destroyed by an army of demons; some call them the Nephilim (which translates as either "giants" or "the fallen ones"). Others call them the Yama Kings, the 666 Kuromao, the Black Devil Kings, the Prometheans.

                          So the PCs went to investigate, by travelling to the Elemental Pole of Air. The No Moon believed the story about dragons dying was rubbish, and in fact Jormungandr had cast down Mela and was now in her palace attempting to destroy the world.

                          When the Lunars reached Mela's castle (thanks to following a group of Dragonblood-piloted airships with wind-controlling Sail charms), they found demons fighting Dragonblood and their mortal ashigaru. They saved some Dragonblood and forced them to surrender and come with them. In Mela's ridiculously massive throne room, the Changing Moon met a group of strange people; they looked like normal humans from the North, but some carried strange weapons. They revealed their identity; an ancient, prehistoric form of Exalted. Long ago, they were not only Dragonblood and Lunars, but many other forms; Exigents, Getimians, Umbrals, Hearteaters, [something utterly unmemorable], and the Exalted of the Sun: the Solar Exalted. It is these "Solar Exalted" who have escaped Hell and returned to Creation. They haven't yet revealed their powers, but they seem to command demons, and several have implied that they're able to swap or steal bodies.

                          The leader of the Solars, an old man named Tešup, although he is better known by the name Yurgen Kaneko, Bull of the North, told the PCs that the Dragons long ago betrayed the Solars and cast them into Hell (hence presumably the term Nephilim, "the Fallen Ones"). Now, he claims, the Dragons plan an "Impergium", an apocalypse that will destroy all of humanity. Thus, the Solars must kill the Dragons to save humanity.

                          However, the Dragonblood captives claim that Mela is only unleashing Fimbulwinter to stop the Yama Kings. If the Demons are defeated, Mela will be safe and Fimbulwinter will end.

                          The Changing Moon convinced the Solars to allow them to try negotiating with Mela first. Now, they walk across the massive icy cavern floor towards her massive throne, whose base is defended by an army.


                          So, next session, maybe the PCs will make peace, maybe they'll fight prehistoric body-stealing sun-witches who spent 1500 years in Hell, or maybe they'll fight a bunch of Dragonblood and the Elemental Dragon of Air.

                          Or maybe Mela will just freeze everyone to death. We'll see! I'm certainly pretty interested to see which way it goes!

                                          Our game takes place in Vaneha. The holy mountain of Ama-Ni-Traya was taken over by a strange enemy leading an army of undead. One of the characters was a monk in the most important monastery on the mountain who exalted during his ritual seclusion in the sanctuary at the very top of the mountain only to discover that his fellow monks are slain.

                                          In order to find out what happen to them, together with the guardian spirit of the mountain, Taiyotora (apologies to all who actually speak Japanese), they tried to find Chinigo, the Wise Spirit of Izumi and discovered that he was sequestered and imprisoned in Yasal crystal by the clan Budzoke. With the help of other PCs, they managed to free Chinigo. They learned that under the Ama-Ni-Traya was located the tomb of their past incarnations and a powerful artifact called The Dam of Black River. Back in the First Age, they divided the spell allowing to control the Dam into three parts which they taught three Wise Spirits of Vaneha. The enemy was a powerful Abyssal, Face of the Void, who once used to be their circlemate.

                                          PCs managed to enter the tomb, a place of magitech wonder to discover chambers miles high containing millions of crystals. As they learned later, the Dam was built to trap the souls of dying and enable the rulers to "manually" control the reincarnation process in their kingdom. They retrieved crystals with the names of their own first-age predecessors, but had to leave the tomb, when the army of Face of the Void attacked and took over the manse. He also was looking for Three Wise Spirits to learn the spell controlling the Dam.

                                          Then they found the second Wise Spirit, Yorumi, and learned the second part of the spell convincing the spirit to reveal it, since he was worried of their pride which made them build the Dam in the first place. Meanwhile, they were uncovering a conspiracy in which some Vanehian aristocrats (with family connections to one of the PCs), factors of the Guild, an ambitious Realm officer were trying to provoke the war between Vaneha with the Realm which would be quickly ended with a treatise which will grant the Realm a strategically located part of Vaneha where Yellow River and Maruto river cross, and at the same time replace sword-prince Gensuji with one of the conspiracy members. The PCs were also fighting with a drug flooding Vaneha whose victims could apparently be controlled after death by conspiracy members. They learned that Face of Void is somehow involved in that plan.

                                          The protagonists decided to thwart the plan by turning one of the conspiring clans to their side. They reached it by bringing Furo Budzoke, nominally the head of the clan, back to the human form. Considered long dead, he was wandering for two hundred years in the shape of a crane because of the curse laid upon him. The clan Budzoke was each year purely ritually searching for him (as in. it was an actual rite they performed). Officially they had no leader, which was a source of a great disgrace for their clan. PCs wanted to perform a ritual which would bring him back to human form.

                                          However, one night before they finished preparations to the ritual, one of the crucial conspiracy members, the uncle of one of the PCs, Kehigo, contacted them and revealed that Face of Void knows where the third Wise Spirit is, but Kehigo can bring them to that very place before their enemy will reach it, using first-age portals which also played a prominent role before in the campaign (by the way, there is a Wyld zone in the space between those portals which they also visited). He offered them limited cooperation: they will not impede the plot to start the war and let them replace the ruling prince, but in turn they will be given an opportunity to stop the Abyssal from learning the third part of the spell (and, most likely, killing the third Wise Spirit of Vaneha).

                                          Facing the decision whether to stop the looming war with the Realm or to stop the Abyssal from reaching the Wise Spirit, they chose to stop the war. Currently two of three Wise Spirit are slain, Face of Void controls the Dam, Protagonists know two of three parts of the spell to control the Dam and so does their enemy. With this cliffhanger, we had to put the campaign on two-three months hiatus.
