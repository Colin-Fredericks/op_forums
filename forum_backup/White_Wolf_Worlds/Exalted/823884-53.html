    Originally posted by Crumplepunch View Post


    E5 weapons generally have few E1 evocations. The Forgotten Blade has only Faceless Warrior Method and Elegant Fugue Strike, one of which is a discount on Solar Charms, Gorgon has only Stone-Sinew Offensive and Stormbringer has only Fulminating Edge Flash.

    Still, you may be right, it needs something for a more active participation. Maybe an attack Charm that only functions when you have points of Ignition but doesn't actually expend it.

    That definitely is the trend in the published material, I just think it's a wrong headed one (based on my Exalted play experiences). Running strictly RAW (because the actual answer to this problem is to just start a campaign at E3), it takes a borderline unfeasible amount of play to reach E5. Part of this might just be the fact that I'm now old enough that me and all my players have full time jobs and lives to run which can be hard to schedule around, but I've heard similar things from other people on the forums. Still, this effectively means that large chunks of the game live at essences players will never reach running strictly RAW. This isn't really a problem with solar charms, what with having a fast track mechanic in Supernals, but it really shows as a problem for Evocations. This isn't so much a problem with your write up of Volcano Cutter because it's already a lean evocation set with no speed bumps and is basically small enough that you can just sprinkle it into a combat build like murder-seasoning. But for evocation trees that stretch longer, it is better to give characters access to meaningful decisions with the evocation set sooner rather than later.

    Basically, if you do feel like adding one more charm in, I think putting in a way to expend ignition at E1 for an active effect might be cool. It would be a good way to introduce a player to the delicate balance of ignition buffs and ignition expense at the start of play.

    Exalted is an aesthetic.

        A question regarding Evocation philosophy: which works better as the capstone to an Evocation tree?

        (1) Evocation that upgrades two or three prerequisites and substitutes the wielder's Essence for the item's power mechanic, indicating greater personal mastery of its power (and also refined control, as wielder can choose to use less than full Essence rating).

        (2) Evocation that unleashes the item's might in a mostly uncontrolled wide scale display of power.

                        Payos (moonsilver and starmetal ring)

                        Payos is a plain looking ring of moonsilver beads connected by a wire of starmetal. Five of the beads contain a sliver of jade corresponding to each of the five jade types.

                        Payos was created in the early years of the Shogunate by a young lunar smith named Bear’s-Paw. Upon taking her second breath she experienced a potent week-long vision. In the vision she witnessed people she felt love for being betrayed and slaughtered. Bear’s-Paw did not know if it was a vision from an ancestor, or a memory from a past life; what she did know was an embodied rage that came from the witnessing.

                        The young lunar’s awakening did not go unnoticed for long, when the Daimyo’s men came to lay claim to her tribe’s lands she transformed into a great bear and laid waste to their forces. The medicine people of her tribe acknowledged her right to rule and begged for her to guide them. Bear’s-Paw accepted, but knew her people must move lest be killed by those that wanted their land. While her fury was unabated, she realized that in killing the Daimyo’s men she doomed her people to never be at peace.

                        Bear’s-Paw and her people travelled, keeping just one step ahead when words of her deeds reached others blessed by Luna. One such visitor came in the shape of a coyote bearing gifts of moonsilver, for he had heard rumors of the bear Lunar who led her people across many lands. The Lunar who would not reveal his name impressed upon Bear’s-Paw the importance of subterfuge in aiding her quest to revenge those whom she felt wronged her. During their travel together his tutelage included taking Bear’s-Paw out to double-back and kill the Daimyo’s Dragon Blooded trackers and scouts.

                        Her anger seeing new purpose, Bear’s-Paw took jade chips off fallen weapons and armours and fused them inside beads of moonsilver. The coyote lunar gave her a final gift before leaving. A coin of starmetal which Bear’s-Paw forged into wire for the beads, allowing her to finish and name her artifact, “Payos”. Payos was intended to be the undoing of the Daimyos, a weapon to be used to gain trust in plain sight – like the snake who waits under the leaves or by the rocks for prey. Unfortunately for Bear’s-Paw, the death of the Dragon Blooded scouts brought the Daimyo’s attention sharply to the threat she and the coyote Lunar represented as anathema.

                        For months she and her people avoided the larger host of the Daimyo until they met at a lake’s edge with nowhere to move forward to, and a sworn brotherhood of Dragon Blooded leading the elite Gunzosha at their backs. Not having yet used the ring to its intended purpose, Bear’s-Paw enacted its evocations and strode towards the enemy host surrounded by a whirling torrent of flame.

                        It is unclear what exactly was said as she strode forward towards the army. Tales from the tribe’s descendants say she bought their freedom by agreeing to be taken peacefully, the flames that surrounded her a testament to her acceptance of Hesiesh and a need to repent. Bear’s-Paw was never seen again by her people and the whereabouts of the ring Payos is a mystery.

                                Yosamu

                                - Inflicting an automatic level of bashing damage per turn unless the wielder makes a decisive attack is incredibly harsh, especially considering how much of an investment Element-Protecting Prana needs. I'd replace it with something like "Every turn the wielder doesn't make a decisive attack, she and everyone within short range of the weapon is exposed to the following environmental hazard: damage 1B/round, difficulty (1+turns since the blade has gained Warmth). If any character takes damage from this hazard, the blade gains 1 Warmth."
                                - The Resonant effect on Tendrils of Frost feels very weak and more like a trap than a legitimate strategy.
                                - Shivering Frostbite Strike is a much, much better version of Shining Ice Mirror's Winter Night Cut. I definitely wouldn't allow an effect that flat-out denies a character the ability to contest movement actions without a Willpower cost, and I especially wouldn't allow it at Essence 1.
                                - Eternal Icy Hibernation feels like it's more interesting than actually good. Now, the problem with this is that the trigger doesn't actually support the interesting use of the weapon much - you can only use it on a character you've incapacitated, you can't actually try to save a circlemate's life by flash-freezing them until you can get them to the Twilight, or disguise people as ice statues, or anything like that. Additionally, the cost is incredibly prohibitive and the Warmth benefit doesn't really offset this (if you can trigger this, you've already leeched at least 7 points of Warmth off of your target, that +2-3 is unlikely to matter too much).
                                - I'd probably ditch all of the freebie clauses, Arms doesn't really do them and you shouldn't either.
                                - If you go with my proposed change, Devouring the Earth could just be a Permanent that says "if the difficulty of the environmental hazard reaches 3, increase the damage to 3B/round".
                                - Swallowing the Sun takes a very cavalier attitude to the fact that the actual sun in Exalted is the war chariot of the Most High, and not some distant ball of fire floating in space. Ditch the idea that it can affect the sun (what would you use it for besides showing off, anyway?) and it will be fine.
                                - See, Ninefold Winter Apocalypse is actually a half-decent place to plug in the sun-killing imagery because it actually makes some sense there. Again though, if you go with my advice about the environmental hazard, you should probably just say "the difficulty and damage of the hazard is set to 3 plus one for every ten points of Warmth the blade has, the effect is extended to long range, and it persists even if it has gained Warmth in the last turn". Although my first instinct is that this sounds like a very impractical capstone to use, requiring as much or nearly as much setup as Volcano Cutter does.

                                    The Hand of God

                                    - Characters with a halfway decent Resolve score (and/or an excellency) are pretty likely to die before they become fearful. You may want to do something about that. ("Automatically instills an intimacy of fear if the attack deals damage equal to, or greater than the target's unmodified Resolve" - there, fixed.)
                                    - Cracks in the Mind sounds hideously powerful to me, but maybe it would turn out to be a non-issue in actual play - after all, you need to have dealt sufficient decisive damage to your target in the scene for this to apply without setup, and even if it does, you can get a 2 mote discount per attack at best (but probably 1m unless you did some groundwork beforehand).
                                    - The Resonant effect of Promise of Doom doesn't make a lot of sense - the original target "suffers the same penalty", by which I suppose you mean the penalty that currently affects the attack's witness. The penalty that only applies if the original target is incapacitated and probably shouldn't worry about any new intimacies anytime soon.
                                    - Blazing Juggernaut Stance should be explicitly incompatible with other effects that increase the character's initiative upon resetting to base.
                                    - Know Not Defeat is disgustingly overpowered and should not exist at all. Maybe in the form of "against a fearful target, double up to (Intimacy's strength) 9s on the attack roll; if the Clash succeeds, increase their defense penalty from failing a Clash to (Intimacy's strength)". On the upside, this effect doesn't need to cost 6 motes.
                                    - My immediate instinct is that Know Not Challengers is slightly undercosted; it would probably still see play at a 4m or even 5m cost.
                                    - Endless Nightmare Strike, by itself, feels okay(-ish). The effect is powerful but it's powerful in a way that, to me, doesn't feel like it would impact combat balance too much, because if you already dealt enough damage to stick an Intimacy on your opponent and then successfully fired off a Gambit with a difficulty that's realistically gonna be somewhere between 5 and 8, they're pretty much only still alive because you decided to fuck around with your fun capstone instead of painting the walls with the contents of their bowels. On the other hand, I'm not entirely sure I'm comfortable with the current form of Cracks in the Mind if there is a way for the wielder to magically intensify the Intimacy to Defining. But that's not a problem with Endless Nightmare Strike.

                                                    Sargasso
                                                    (Green Jade Reaver Daiklave,
                                                    Artifact •••)

                                                    Carved from a single block of green jade, Sargasso is a reaver daiklave approximately four feet from point to pommel. The blade is short—just under three feet, wide even for a sword of its style, and inlaid on both sides with carvings of winding water dragons and fronds of seaweed. Its cutting edge is a sweeping curve that doubles back to form the beginning of a crescent before ending at a notch to rest the first of the nine rings linked through holes along its spine. The hilt is a foot-and-a-half long, unusually thick, with the grip and pommel blatantly phallic, the cross guard sculpted as an open crab claw, and the rain guards as a widely spread yoni into which a hearthstone may be set on either side.

                                                    The Lintha Family tells stories of its use in the rebellion of the gods against their makers, when their distant ancestors fought the Exalted in the name of the Sea That Marched Against the Flame. Expertly carved from green jade over the course of a decade, it was enchanted by the sorcerer-prince Lo Ng Siu to evoke the vast forests of sargassum that surround and protect Bluehaven to this day. Many invading ships were boarded or sunk thanks to its power, but eventually when the old Lintha Empire fell to the forces of the Deliberative it passed into enemy hands. Over the intervening millennia it has been used by dragonblooded ship’s captains and admirals against the Lintha and other pirates, smugglers, and would-be rebellious fleets, eventually becoming an heirloom of House Peleps in recent centuries. Rumor has it that Peleps Nalin Guk’s death at the blades of unidentified freebooters has returned Sargasso to Lintha hands. Whether it will reappear, and to what purpose, remains to be seen.

                                                    Type: Medium (+3 ACC, +12 DMG, +1 DEF, OVW 4)

The tags from which she may choose are: Balanced, Cutting, Disarming, Flexible, Grappling, Piercing, Poisonable, Shield, Thrown (Medium). These additional tags are the keys to the sword’s Evocations; each requires certain tags to activate.

                                                    Special Activation Rules: This Evocation requires the wielder to have attuned the ring adding the Shield tag beforehand.

                                                    The Thing in the Weeds
