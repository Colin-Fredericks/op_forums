        Not hugely into detail, mostly just inferences. Like... the Infernals splatbook had Named Poison, which targeted whomever is named a specific thing. The trick to it was knowing which name to choose - if the target's food taster had the exact same name, say, then it works best to tack on one of the intended target's titles. As long as they don't lose that title before consuming the poison, it'll slip by the taste-tester and poison the target as intended.

        You can basically make up whatever effects you want. Kimbery is "Poison" in general. Metagaos is Devouring, which can mean anything fron cancer to the devouring of distance, individuality, or Time. Szorney plants might mess with Identity. Hegra plays merry havoc with emotions.

                                                    This is one of those (to me) theories instead of practice. In general while there were methods, they were often resuling in what is the same thing,a nd as the game went along "elngihtened mortal" was a generic systme term that represented all of those different things, including assumed capabilities, access to magic, and so on. 3e in general is going with things being more botique. "Enlgihtened mortal" as a generic thing a Charm granted someone or which someone who worked hard enough got to be is not something 3e assumes is the case. This is to encourage more actual diversity isntead of what was often in the text beyond Scroll of the Monk honeslty depicted as just kind of a generic power-up. Especially since that led to things like casually making sorcerers, mote-using thaumaturges, or supernaturla martial artists as a resoruce rather htan a useful thing.

                                                    Note that there are characters who have all the things noted in your question. We have characters like Mist hte Eternal Revolutionary turned into a unique Wyld thing; Ku Nenuva with a daiklave made from her own tears and the Evocations to go with it; Ashana Ikatu who is the sifu of a dojo who thorugh the magical snake powers of its founder and inheriting the position can do Snake and Ebon Shadow styles; and Eldran Tabrar, the Witch-King of Lock who's got an artifact called the Sky Stone that only attaches to mortals and which is driving him to complete it, but in the meantime he gained a pile of supernaturla power from it to enforce his will.

                                                    A general train of thought is if somehting is generlaly able to do magic, they're not mortal anymore more than being enlightened mortal. And that rather than being a generic trait, supernaturla mortals end to be much more bespoke. This is especially in the case since 3e isn't trying to really support mortal play and the infrastructure needed to have such play be robust along with Exalted stuff and which Exalts could pick-up also.
