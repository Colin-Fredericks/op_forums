        Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
        The return of the Solars is the core conflict of the setting and underpins its themes, and the Second Edition approach smacked far too much of them only getting to be relevant for a few years before being pushed aside to make things revolve around the new hotness.

        You talk elsewhere about how being successors to the Yozis should make the cap on their power greater and their narratives grander. Do you not think that smacks of a certain ego in the concept, a thing that pushes the other groups aside rather than relating organically?.
        Incidentally, that's a good reason Solars are the corebook splat. It's almost like the fluff version of preventing power creep. Solars are the mightiest chosen, so any book that comes after isn't allowed to power creep them. They're also the focus of the central upturn in the setting, Solars, Abyssals and Infernals return to the world and rock the boat everywhere, and very explicitly are given ambiguity about their role in that. Not that there can't exist existential threats to Creation, but there shouldn't be a splat that gets released that makes the world feel like they're now the ones that really matter.

        So far we've got Dragonblooded, who made the Realm larger, more interesting and more terrifying than ever, but as much as some fans of DBs might want it to, it didn't make the Dragonblooded so great and powerful that the only thing in the world that matters is stopping them. The Realm is not laying in wait for the civil war to inevitably resolve itself and then steamroll the whole rest of Creation.

        We also have Lunars, who've grown and expanded, even getting an empire of their own way far away from the centre of the world. They still have powerful elders in charge of entire schools of Lunars and ruling nations, but their grand plan to take over everything isn't presented as something that's totally going to work. It might, but they're not like closing in a noose that makes battling the Realm seem like a pointless venture unless you want Lunar overlords.

        They're all just powerful but ultimately limited players in a world where centuries long norms are being upheaved. That climate magnifies their powers to affect change on the world, so magical powers that were already considerable lend themselves to the possibility of even grander stories. A ton of work has been put in this edition to make the world massive enough that a story that doesn't even involve a whole direction of Creation can still feel huge and epic without making it seem like you're hamstringing your players. It would suck to have all of that undone.

            Originally posted by Blaque View Post
            A bit of a problem with the Infernal-Solar arms race to some extent is that it in the end, made the entire rest of Exalted irreleviant or appear so. This is because the long term assumption is that 1 Solar = 1 Primordial. Which means that 1 Solar can defeat 1 Primordial. Which means that there being 300ish Solars either means 1) There are a fuckload more Primordials dead or Yozi or the number of Solars is pretty superfluous or 2) It had to be something time-gated (which Ex2 did with Essence 6+ admitedlY) that prevents this from being more than an "Angels on pinheads" situation...and so pretty much useless for most games of Exalted ever played since very few actually went to Essence 6+ and so a waste of time, concept space and word count..

            Like, for me, I never liked the Infernals = Yozis thing as it played on the "Solars eventually become oomnipotent" trope that made other Exalts feel superfluous and even at times the gameline's outright hostility to some of them. In trying to maintain Solars and derivitives as their peers, having a part of them be potentially 50 more Yozis meant that you do a lot to warp the setting's power scales in ways it was never built for or assumed. Exalted, early on, was a Dying Earth setting about the kind of romance of empires, heroism, and folks living up to legend of armies of the gods fighting great singular beings in titanomachy. When you can become one of those things it took armies of the other PC types to take down, the setting just kind of gets warped weirdly. Becoming a Yozi-level thing is interesting. It is also just in the end, probably something I think not explored in Exalted in a way that's healthy for setting integrity.

            When you have 300 or so Exalts who have various versions of "Become Dr. Manhattan" some of the stuff that sold folks on the setting relating to entirely different genres of fiction get lost along the way. No one gives a shit you made Zorayas' Zojad, Romances Han-style collapse of the Realm, or games of political wrangling over the corpse of Alexandar when oh hey this guy became the Ebon Dragon, gotta stop him or the whole world ends!

            When the arms race of "How can a singular Solar fight a singular Infernal wiht a real Yozi-sized soul pantheon..." means "The Solar can singularly ifght a titan", you just end up with a setting that is more about fighting big numbers and not really interacting with the setting. Exalted really was never I think originally designed to be this DC New Gods duking it out or TTGL fighting Anti-Spirals. But hwen you are doing stories on that level of scale, stuff like I dunno, the Wars of the Diadochi, Romance of the 3 Kingdoms, or I dunno, Mahasamatman taking on Heaven by founding the EIghtfold Path or something, feel like they lose a lot of their relevance.

            I'd rather see just how Infernals, as characters you actually see play, wield power in the world they are expected to play in. You are Chosen and you have power. That power is mighty and alien. But you are still the person wielding that power for your goals and it is still a representation of your character's skill and will on the world. I'd rather see focus on that than some naval-gazing thing on how being "super duper powerful means I can pretend to be something lockedin Hell for eternity to sfufer and meant to be a device to spawn the NPCs I enslave with sorcery."

            See the thing is, while I do enjoy the "Solars eventually become so powerful as to be effectively omnipotent" I don't actually see it as an impediment to the other splats because...well, it's like you said: They're ALSO miraculously empowered Chosen of the highest powers? To me a Solar being outnumbered and beaten down by Dragonblooded isn't a statement of Solar weakness, it's a statement of Dragonblooded strength. Heck I even remember fluff in Lords of Creation's Transcendant Gaian Harmony (I remember someone being told to "think bigger" when they asked if the end state was that of a Third Circle Demon or Behemoth) suggesting Dragonbloods have their own level of evolution beyond conventional wisdom they might reach if sufficiently long lived (there are hearthstones that confer immortality after all) and with enough effort. And the admittedly extraordinary and otherworldly but still DB-managed Sea of Mind has been present since 1e. I've heard a lot of people say similar things about 1e but having read into it after 2e I can't help but feel the editions don't truly contradict each other, just expand. One thing I enjoyed was how the Exalted apparently existed in Oramus' Beyond-arsenal as impossible heroes, and that Oramus was alarmed on the day they stopped being in there!

            Which brings me to my perspective: Coming into 2e even if the actual gameplay seldom takes place at that level, to me Exalted always HAS been about DC New Gods duking it out with TTGL wrestling the Anti-Spirals...BUT ALSO interacting with the setting. I don't see those things being mutually exclusive at all, because Creation as a setting always read to me as being intended to raise questions about consequences moreso than insurmountable limits. Heck, I could use those examples as reference points: Just as being a champion warrior of a race of supradimensional self-aware ideas endowed with the universe bomb-containing Astro Force didn't stop Orion from forming friendships with the humans he rescued, I could see a Dawn capable of hurling a daiklave at the horizon so hard it cleaved a path into the firmament befriending a merchant who took care of his wounds when he was injured. Simon certainly doesn't look down on Nia or the rest of the Dai-Gurren brigade even though he's the one who gets the most "oh shit!" moments in the story, just like how a cocky Lunar warlord might feel paternal loyalty to their horde.

            I don't really think, in my experience, that it's the setting's powerlevel that dictates the gameplay. I think it's the players that do, and the powerlevels exist to give a scope of grandeur for the enormity of their characters' actions; background music to the opera, if you will. It strikes me as right that the Solars and their peers be first among equals, but just as correct that their subordinates hold the potential to challenge insurmountable odds just as the Solars themselves brought down the Primordials at low Essence.
