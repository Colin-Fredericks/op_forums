    To get back to the topic at the top of this page, about functional resurrection, this is something I recently had issues with:

    One of the PCs (at another player's suggestion) wanted to use his long downtime to create a being that was essentially functionally a resurrection of a dead PC (the other player's). Using a mighty sorcerous working, he made a human body that looked like the dead PC, and filled it with memories gleaned from Creation's akashic record (he could have easily used some other more canonical method). The player of the dead PC said that surely the new human would earn Exaltation, most likely Zenith again, since he shared all the same qualities as the fallen hero, having his personality, skills and memories.

    And that, ladies and gentlemen, is how you get Liminals. The iron law of "no resurrection" cannot be tricked!*
    So that player is now playing a Liminal sorcerer** with the memories and appearance of the dead PC.

    *Except when it does. I probably could have been convinced to let him play this guy as a Solar, just not with the same exaltation (or soul, of course, which may or may not make a difference depending on whether you follow the Hindu or Buddhist conception of a soul [I realise Hindu/Buddhist is a gross simplification]).
    But then of course, he'd be near the top of the list of the Bureau of Endings for removal before his very presence disrupts Fate.

    **Because since he's a sorcerer, and spent several hundred XP on getting the same abilities as his old character, I haven't had to write any Liminal charms.

    (Before I convinced him to play a Liminal, I also brought up the point that guarenteeing Exaltation is impossible. Maybe he doesn't have the same heroic opportunity to shine. Maybe he makes the wrong choices with his free will. Maybe there are no exaltations available. Maybe his previous character changed to become less heroic and worthy of exaltation, after Exalting, since the new character was based on the old character at his death, not just before his exaltation.)

    To get back to the suggestion on the previous page.
    Originally posted by Exthalion
    There is a point where its hard to say why exactly you haven't brought back the dead. A biomotonic replicant made from the body and both ghosts of the deceased with dream crystal infusion of all their memories with the same celestial exaltation starts to be really hard to tell apart from the genuine article.
    How do you get the ghosts to fuse back together after death? Did you have enough dream crystals to fit in all their memories at the moment of death? Most of all, how do you ensure it gets the same exaltation?
    In short, Erinys' point about the souls is very valid.

    I actually myself play the biomotonic replicant of a Solar. The replicant believed himself to be the Solar, replacing the Solar in the party, while the Solar was comatose. When the Solar awoke, I lost his Solar powers which he'd been transferring to me through an artefact inside my skin. I had all of his memories (though the recent ones were vague and patchy) and so believed myself to be him, and didn't know how I'd lost my Solar powers. Then my Raksha girlfriend was kidnapped by evil magitech ghost-powered monsters (never really got to the bottom of how they worked), so we went on a quest to save them. It turned out she'd been kidnapped... by her evil Solar boyfriend who'd betrayed the party and was working for an insane magitech AI woman we'd fought before. So I punched him and exalted as a Lunar.
    Now, I insist that I'm not him: I have, after all, a different soul. One of the other PCs insists I am him: I have the same body, the same memories, souls are irrelevant. This has led to my Lunar having severe body-image and obsessive issues which led him to develop permanent mutations so he looked less like the Solar. And much discussion about what truly differentiates humans: their souls, their memories, or what? It's just somewhat stymied in that he's the only mental primary PC and the others are not very interested in philosophy.

                                A: John Mørke

                                                            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                                            They're sure that with other parts of the combat mechanics, character stats and decent Storytelling, it will be possible to differentiate martial artists from one another and other Abilities.
                                                            Indeed. Of the combat mechanics, Gambits were called out in particular. We still lack a clear idea of how those work. There's a remark* that Shadow of the Colossus style fights was one of the things in mind for them, but not much else. It really does seem best to wait and see the shape of the final design before getting worried.

                                                            * This one (spoiled for space) from here:
                                                            Originally posted by Eric Minton's 'Ask the Devs' Summary
                                                            danelsan:
                                                            Something I was wondering about giant opponents. And I mean REALLY gigantic – not Tyrant Lizards but Juggernaut, Oliphem, Mount Mostah and so on. In many videogames and movies, human-sized heroes deal with such beings by sort of systematically disabling them, avoiding or otherwise defending against attacks and progressively destroying parts of the thing, sometimes disabling some of the creature’s attacks in the processes (though also often enabling others as the thing gets more damaged and enraged), often climbing up the enormous foe during various moments, and finally ending things with a killing strike.

                                                            I like the feeling those sort of battles get, particularly in videogames due to the interactivity. It really makes for a different feeling than fighting normal-sized opponents and, even if you are superior all things considered (because you are a phenomenal player of that game, or in Exalted because you are a very experienced Dawn Caste), it still makes the battle feel tense, dangerous and impressive. You know, that idea that my Solar is nowhere as much of a destructive force as that walking mountain, and likely can take nowhere near as much punishment, but in the end is still the superior warrior that can defeat it by impossible skill.

                                                            Anyway, what I mean to ask is, can the EX3 combat engine allow me to do that sort of battles against colossal opponents?

                                                            Holden:
                                                            That kind of fight is one of the big things we had in mind when we came up with Gambits.

                                                            Vance:
                                                            One of the antagonists is written up to be this kind of “boss fight.”
