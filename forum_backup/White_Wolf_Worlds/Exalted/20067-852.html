    One thing that wasn't noted that I thought should be pointed out - Lunars, by default, no longer need to worry about this at all (an individual Lunar could still be driven mad in the Wyld, but it isn't a general problem). Casteless Lunars don't have any problems with mutations any more (they're actually a very viable character type now!), and "chimeras" are also a valid character type, not a monster that Lunars risk turning into. A chimera is just a Lunar whose spirit shape incorporates two animals instead of just one. Ma-Ha-Suchi is, in fact, such a chimera, his spirit shape encompassing both wolf and goat.

                            How do you handle Bargain rolls made against PCs?

                            The way Bargain rolls are written, the roll can only be made if something of sufficient value is offered. This makes sense for ST characters - a Matriarch may be forced to exert her will to resist an offer of the marriage of a talented commander into her House, for instance. However, when it comes to PCs, it seems like any player offered something they want would simply accept it without requiring the ST character to roll, while a player who didn't want the thing offered would simply say that it is insufficient and therefore prevent thr ST from making the roll in the first place.

                            This assumes a certain level of metagaming, but it seems hard to resolve otherwise without a logn discussion about separating your character from yourself, and a subsequent discussion on how the ST could claim to know characters better than their players do.

                                        You could force your PCs into a no win combat situation. Fight 10 Abyssals and a Deathlord. It'd be fair under the rules of the game, but if you did it your players are unlikely to want to play with you again.

                                        Persuade relies on Intimacies set by the players. The Red Rule gives players further agency. Don't try to railroad them into doing something they don't want to do.

                                        If a PC has a Defining Tie of Love for his wife and says "my character would do anything for his wife -- even kill a man", then the wife asking him to kill a man is legitimate. That doesn't mean that any random stranger can say "hey, kill that guy for me and I'll give you ten bucks". (And if the PC takes a far more healthy Major Tie towards the wife, deciding that his character won't kill folks for love... that's legit too. Let the players define what their characters would or would not do.)

                                        "You have to marry someone because I overcame your Resolve in a Bargain roll."

                                        Nah. Let them say "that's not sufficient".

                                        Their character is the one thing the player can control. Let them control them.

                                        If it's absolutely neccessary to the story, then do it as Pursaude. Hit the Intimacies that are important to the general of the besieged city or the bride to be as to why they should accept the trade -- that way you are playing to the character and giving the PC a fair defense instead of a fiat yes/no.

                                                Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
                                                Bargain rolls are kinda "meh" anyway. If one party offers something the other person wants, just make the trade. It shouldn't exist as a social roll, IMHO.

                                                Dramatic tension is found in exploring the dichotomy between want and need, and the prospect of a person's reasoned faculties for the latter being overcome by the gratification of the former. Or indeed cases where the person is not sure what they want, and can have it clarified for better or worse by immediate opportunity and compelling argument.

                                                A character has a Flaw of a cocaine addiction and somebody offers them a hit of the best quality in exchange for not providing guard duty for an underground meeting of political agitators tonight. The character certainly wants what is offered, but knows that they shouldn't. They can want it in a way that can rationalize that particular cost away. A dice roll would be premised on the notion that it is compelling for the resolution of that dilemma to be in the hands of weighted odds.

                                                I would try to sell a player on the value of navigating these subjects with a bit of uncertainty. To offer them things that the characters would plausibly want for prices that are, at least at the surface level, fair, even if there are likely to be consequences beyond the immediate trade, and then let it come down to the salesmanship of the one making the offer being set against the character's capacity to retain a firm hold of their own decision making.
