                            Originally posted by JohnDoe244 View Post
                            You've got to contextualize this.

                            1E was a time when you basically weren't allowed to take 5 dots in your character traits in White Wolf games. Exalted had a little more slack, but carried that baggage.
                            I mean, I remembered people getting a bit hot and bothered that the author of the Shadow Lords Revised tribebook had given the sample characters very optimal Attribute + Ability totals in a number of areas, but that book still went to press. Exalted was also a mechanical derivative of the Aeonverse games, and the most recent offerings of it at the time of Exalted 1e not only permitted stats of 5, they encouraged it, because it was supposed to be a part of a character's concept. Aberrant and Adventure even had a two-phase system where transformation points made it super easy to get those maxed-out stats; in the case of Aberrant, it was also super-easy to max out all your human-scale Attributes and get a bunch of Abilities a5 5 dots, with some specialties added on. Even Trinity got in on it with the unpublished manuscript of Asia Ascendant, which gave us the Superior - up to 6 in a category of Attributes and their related Abilities!

                            So while stat-shaming for optimal characters may have been the state for the WoD, Exalted had always been a bit of a different animal in this arena.

                                    The Realm is literally the centre of the world, and has been the capital of world-spanning empires for thousands of years. Yes, when the Primordials made humans the ones on the Blessed Isle looked kind of Chinese (in 3rd ed), but since then there's been so much movement that all kinds of ethnic groups (and mixes thereof) are common and not generally considered important (aside from standards of beauty).
                                    Of course, it could be that due to the presence of the Imperial Mountain people are more likely to have white hair or something, but I think it's not a big thing.
                                    Still, it's a fun thing to play up if you want Creation's humans to look more exotic.

                                    This is not to say that there isn't xenophobic bigotry in the Realm, but it's unlikely to be very colour-based (which is somewhat historically unusual) at this point. It's accent (a good Realm accent or a stupid barbarian accent), clothes (the veils of a Delzahn man? They look like letterboxes!), place of birth (perhaps, like the Romans, they believe character is influenced by your environment, and so those born and raised close to the Pole of Earth are deemed more rational than the hot-tempered southerners or decadent easterners), and most of all, religious practices.
                                    If you're an Arjuf native who looks kind of An-Tengian, you might have people wondering if you are a foreigner. But if you have a Realm name, wear Realm clothes, eat Realm food, etc, you're fine.
                                    If, on the other hand, you have an An-Tengian name, speak with a bit of an An-Tengian accent, and don't go to shrines much, people might accuse you of being a secret worshipper of the Golden Lord.

                                                I think, even in the First Age, there probably wasn't so much movement as to completely destroy ethnic markers; it's a big world, and while Exalts could fly around on magic sky chariots, your average farmer was using a donkey or her own two feet.
                                                Places on major trade routes would likely be way more mixed than the distant periphery, as places like the Neck or deep desert would have less visitors.


                                                However, thinking about the point you raise; people moving thousands of years ago is not actually going to lead to modern diversity per-se, because they'll have interbred with the natives. If an area of easterners had a steady stream of southern labourers and merchants in the First Age, it's unlikely you'll find a large community of black-skinned people there; rather, the locals will sometimes have darker skin or curly hair. Like how Saudi Arabia is opposite Ethiopia and South Sudan, and so Saudis vary between having brown skin or being as pale as Levantines, but hardly any actually look completely sub-Saharan.
                                                It's only places like Nexus or Arjuf or the Imperial City which have had many travellers in the Second Age where you get a community of people very obviously descended from those across the world.

                                                        Wizard, re modern style diversity yeah you would be more likely to have people of homogenised appearance between and within regions, though depending a bit on whether assortative partnering and reproduction maintains structure (castes etc), and the underling "genetic" architecture may also be more "friendly" to this maintainance of structure for Creation humans than RW humans.

                                                        But I would tend to think that, y'know even fairly low levels like 1% migration per year over 100 years is 100% replacement (0.1% a thousand years) and Creation is going to have tons of mass movements technologies available, and huge patchwork political entities that rise and falls more or less want mobility within their borders for the interests of the rulers, and people will want to move to change their lives (or be forced to by wars, disasters etc).
