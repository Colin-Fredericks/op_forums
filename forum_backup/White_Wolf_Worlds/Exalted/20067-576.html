    Certainly, social attacks pretty much always fall under the social attributes. But I don't think that "write an entertaining novel" is a social attack, necessarily.                                 I'd say let them choose. Write it their way. "You need to write in your own voice" as one my tutors said.


                                If they're trying to do a story which evokes strong feelings, Charisma+Linguistics.

                                If they're trying to do a story that raises interesting points about society and makes you think (but doesn't necessarily try to persuade you of a particular answer), with a well-thought out plot, or write something really original and creative, Wits+Linguistics. Or perhaps Intelligence+Linguistics.

                                If they're trying to constantly surprise the reader or distract them with red herrings before the big reveal where the reader realises they'd seen all the clues but missed them, maybe Manipulation.

                                I could even see an argument for Perception+Linguistics. Terry Pratchett told me that he based most of his characters on people he knew (he said Granny Weatherwax was based on a crotchety old lady in his village, who had a particular stubborn habit that struck him, but I can't remember what it was); to write good characters could therefore require you to understand other people (and that's Perception+Socialise). If you've ever read David Eddings or Robert Jordan, you may have noticed they base their characters on a relatively small number of people, and so the same characters turn up again with different names; I assume because these are the only people they feel they understand very well.


                                Since it's just "write an entertaining novel," and isn't trying to really change people's behaviour, I don't think it matters that much what they roll, as long as it's Linguistics.



                                To be quite honest with you, I teach students to write basic argumentative essays (which are not the same as novels admittedly, but there are similarities) and I'd say it's 10% critical thinking, 10% creativity, and 80% just being good at writing (ie linguistics) which is very much its own skill. Writing skill and speaking skill are actually, psychologically, something a lot of people process quite separately (frustratingly, for a teacher).
                                Certainly how convincing and entertaining the essay is, is not very related to how personable and charismatic students are. I've had plenty of quiet, shy students who write very well-structured, organised, well-phrased essays, and charismatic and engaging students who churn out rubbish.
                                Though I would say that boring people write unoriginal stuff, and wacky students with personality, the kind who come out with unusual ideas, generally write more interesting essays.

                                (Of course, there's plenty of heavily derivative, unoriginal books which are very entertaining. And I've read plenty of science-fiction with really interesting concepts that just isn't that entertaining.)

                                                    Originally posted by Fredo View Post
                                                    1. Is researching a cure for a disease covered by medicine or lore?
                                                    1a. Is manufacturing that cure covered by medicine or craft?
                                                    I would say it's Medicine to research the cure, and that would produce a couple of doses/applications/etc. You could continue to create a few doses at a time with Medicine (at a lower difficulty than creating the cure in the first place), but making a bunch at once with the recipe would be Craft.

                                                    Originally posted by Fredo
                                                    2. When someone is using Stormwind Rider, how obvious is it that there is someone inside of the dust devil?
                                                    2a. Any spell or charm work well with Stormwind Rider to totally hide its passengers?
                                                    I generally picture Stormwind Rider as being fairly transparent. Note that while it provides cover against attacks, it doesn't say anything about cover for purposes of stealth, and doesn't penalize people's Perception-based rolls on the inside. That all suggests to me that it doesn't block sight meaningfully. As to hiding the riders, I don't think there's currently any spells or charms to do so, but I could coming up with one to do so. I figure "hide a small group of people" is reasonably Terrestrial Circle, while for charms, something in Stealth seems viable.

                                                    Originally posted by Fredo
                                                    3. "Ride is used to ride on a horse, ox, or more exotic mount, as well as to maneuver in vehicles such as chariots or carts that are led by horses or beasts of burden."Can Ride charms be used while riding a dust devil?
                                                    I wouldn't allow Ride charms to be used with Stormwind Rider (or Cirrus Skiff), since both explicitly say you use Occult, not Ride, as the ability to control them. However, I could see coming up with some custom Occult charms that duplicated some of the lower-Essence Ride charms, but for boosting such spells instead of a mount.

                                                    Originally posted by Fredo
                                                    4. Ingame, how much time does a story (ie time period for Harmonious Academic Methodology) take on average? How many play sessions does that take usually?
                                                    I think this varies too much for an average to be useful. A story can last hours, days, weeks, or possibly months. However, some guidelines I've found useful:
                                                    1) No matter how much in-game time it takes, a story should be about 3-5 sessions of play. That lets people still get a decent amount of use out of those "once per story" charms.
                                                    2) If there's significant downtime (more than a week or so), strongly consider calling the end of the story there, and starting a new one. Long downtime, even if you skim over it in play quite fast ("Two weeks pass. You all train/craft/do sorcerous workings. Then, after two weeks...") still interrupts the flow in people's minds, creating a sense that the story has gone on longer.
                                                    3) if you're taking pacing cues from fiction, try to use movies or TV more than books. Most books have a naturally slower pace than movies/TV, and a TV episode or movie is a closer match, time-wise, to the typical RPG session. Try to think about how many distinct events/scenes are packed into a movie, and aim for something like that.
