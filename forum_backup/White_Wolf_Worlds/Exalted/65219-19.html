                                    There is a subspecies of lotus that is native to the ponds and lakes in the foothills of Mt. Meru. Different varieties have a range of colors, but each blossom always has exactly five petals, arranged in a perfect pentagon, and so it is called the Five-Petal Lotus.

                                    The Five-Petal Lotus is often seen as a symbol of balance, harmony, and respect for the order of things. Philosophers draw a connection between the five petals and the five seasons, five elements, five Fates, five Directions, and so forth. Since the beginning of the First Age, the icon of the Five-Petal Lotus has been on countless flags and banners, usually to imply that the group bearing the sign has the support of Heaven.

                                    The root of the Five-Petal Lotus is not extraordinarily healthy compared to other lotuses. Despite that, people still tend to ascribe mystical properties to it. Stories of Immaculate monks dining on nothing but Five-Petal Lotus roots are only hearsay, but it is still something that common people tend to think that monks do, along with meditating under waterfalls and practicing katas on mountaintops while the sun is rising.

                                    Cynis Taeris is credited with the breeding of the first Imperial Blossom, an unique variety of the flower with five petals of different colors - one purple, one blue, one red, one green, and one white - to represent the five elements. A section of the Royal Gardens in the Imperial City consists of nothing but Imperial Blossoms in cascade arrangements.

                                                        The Raksha or Fair Ones as we know them are commonly believed to be born of the Wyld. This is a half truth.

                                                        They are born of the clash of Wyld and Creation. Wyld being an infinite cauldron of narrative, and Creation being an anchor of shape and solidity. The Fair Ones take on the narrative aspects of the Wyld as living stories, but their stories emulate the many things and elements of Creation and shape. Mere facsimiles of Creation-born, they can manipulate their narrative more readily than Creation-born.

                                                        Before their kind would be born and die by the might of the Sun whose perfection burned the Wyld away at the edge of Creation. Those who fled from this fate went deep into the Wyld, consumed by shapeless narratives, or becoming something greater.

                                                        Since the fascinating tale of the Gods usurping their creators, the Sun has not been as vigilant, and the Fair Ones grew along the edge of Creation, spreading far and deep.
