    Originally posted by Elfive View Post
    I don't think the number was ever supposed to be literally 10,000. That's more like a figure of speech for "a lot". Like how "Myriad" doesn't literally mean 10,000 any more.
    Fair enough - the 10,000 could be the "propoganda" number in DB culture in the Realm.

    Anyhow - rereading this thread and my own posts - I think for this game what I'll do is say that DB's reproduction is low and mysterious. I'll keep the Breeding background for power purposes, but chuck out the fertility rules altogether.

    Obviously the DB's population would be designed to grow for the war with the Titanomachy. Much is made of the DB blood over time running "less pure" in the fluff - and perhaps the 2e canon reproduction rates are what it would have been in the first age. Now, in the fallen Second Age - two DB's are very lucky to have a child once per ten years, and even when sexually active can go decades, and even centuries in between children. Such children do Exalt most of the time, but not all. A DB and a mortal have children as normal but it is uncommon, even rare for them to have a child that Exalts. Patricians very rarely exalt, and outside of these lineages, Exalting as a DB is pretty much like winning the lottery - odds are 1 in 10,000,000 births.

    This way my PCs can think strategically about this. Marrying mortal means lots of patrician children. Marrying an Exalt can involve decades of pain - waiting for the DB woman to get pregnant. (Anyone read up on IRL women & couples trying to have children for a long time? It can make people go crazy.) A plot point could be made of trying to figure out forgotten DB eugenics to increase their fertility and involves story telling and problem solving by the players. Maybe they have to make a pilgrimage to the elemental poles and try and contact the sleeping Elemental Dragons. Maybe they have to find a way into Autochthon and wake him in order to repair damaged DB exaltations. Something like that - which I think would be more enjoyable than munckining manses, elementals and harems.

    Also - another factor left out of DB populations is the % that become celibate monks in the Immaculate order. I don't think we're ever given a hard number, but losing a certain % that never reproduce will crimp long term population numbers.

        The closest we ever got to direct numbers on Terrestrial Exalted was back in the First Edition Storyteller Guide, in the Q&A section answered directly by Grabowski (who basically was the original designer for most of Exalted).

        Q: How many Exalted are there?

        A: As many as you want there to be. As the game was originally posited, there were more than one hundred and less than two hundred Solar Exalted. There are between three and four hundred Lunars, a hundred or so each of Sidereal and Abyssal Exalted. In contrast to these tiny numbers, there are more than ten thousand Terrestrial Exalted in Creation - in the Realm, there is roughly one Terrestrial Exalt for every five thousand citizens, and this doesn't count Outcastes, Exalted inhabitants of Lookshy or the rural bloodlines of the Northern Threshold nobility.
        Also, I think people who take the 2nd edition rules for Exalting and applying those to the game as a whole are making a mistake. Those rules were intended to be guidelines to be used for PC's (their children), not to be a broad rule about Dragon Blooded in the entire setting.

                                    Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                                    Also, I think people who take the 2nd edition rules for Exalting and applying those to the game as a whole are making a mistake. Those rules were intended to be guidelines to be used for PC's (their children), not to be a broad rule about Dragon Blooded in the entire setting.
                                    This. Great point. As a ST I labored under the assumption for years that the rules for PCs applied to everyone.

                                    That being said, I once ran an enlightened mortal/godblood campaign (that eventually turned Solar) where the PCs were members of House Iselsi though they were distant kin to each other. They stumbled onto a plot by one faction of the House who had immediately after the House's disgrace developed a eugenics program to attempt to mass produce DBs for the House.

                                    After two or three generations, and applying all the modifiers to increase likelihood for exaltation, they had an exalt success rate of 70%. The 30% who didn't exalt were Terrestrial Half-Castes (before that was officially retconned and for good reason).

                                    The half-castes were trained to increase their essence to 3. IIRC, when Terrestrial half-castes should reach essence 4, they immediately got to reroll for exaltation. They could only fail on a roll of a 1 which couldn't be mitigated by spending a willpower point.

                                    The handful that failed to exalt were given positions of prominence over the mortal peasants of which there were many at the main location. By RY 768 they had around 1,000 DBs who were raised from birth to believe that eventually they'd take control of the Realm and thus the future of Creation.

                                    The plan was to infiltrate and eventually take over the Immaculate Order which they successfully pulled off. Having control of the IO allowed them to wield incredible social power. They used that power to make things easier for Iselsi.

                                    Many of the DBs who had not trained in the IO martial arts infiltrated House Tepet after its devastating loss to the Bull thrusting the House back into a position of power. The elders of the House realized too late that these "lost eggs" were actually all in cahoots and had their own agenda. That agenda allowed House Iselsi to operate a little more openly.

                                    Some may wonder how a disgraced House was able to pull it off. The DBs in that faction weren't alone. A few members of House Iselsi had become Celestial Exalted since the House fell. With a handful of Solars, a few older Lunars and a gold faction Sidereal to muddy the waters of Iselsi's destiny, they were able to greatly enhance Iselsi's power and influence throughout the Realm and Creation. Eventually their disgraced status was revoked by the Realm's Deliberative, and the House was restored to good standing. Although they never managed to position themselves to take over the Realm, they did manage to save the House.

                                    TL;DR: 2E DB breeding and terrestrial half-caste rules allowed for game/setting breaking changes to be made in a relatively short amount of time.
