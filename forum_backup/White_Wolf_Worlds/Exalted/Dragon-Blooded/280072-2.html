            Originally posted by Godjaw View Post
            I see a lot of reasons why people like Dynasts, which is fine, but nothing specific to Dragonblooded.

            I like Dragonblooded because of their thematics of their power. They are Princes of the Earth. Their power either comes from or is inspired by the 5 Terrestrial Dragons and the Pillars of the Elements that keep Creation anchored in reality. Without them, Creation would likely dissipate. They are figuratively the Exalted of Creation.

            Their power is not given down from on high, it is passed by blood and lineage. Strong traditions run with blood. Blood and lineage are very mortal conceits. This grounds them in mortality and the people. This also sets them above people. The power is passed by lineage, which ties you to something strong. Not just anyone can be them, whereas a Solar is chosen from on high, but anyone could be chosen. They are figuratively the Exalted of the People, those Above and Below others.

            Be they Dynasts or Scavenger Lords they are many, they are powerful, they are deeply tied to Creation in a way no other splat is.
            Well, except Sidereals. Amusingly, I always thought about Sidereals as the "Exalts if Creation", if anything. Their power tends to be about twisting Fate and making the environment not work properly, or about invoking terrestrial spirits and elementals, or just straight up fooling people. Despite being based in heaven, few powersets are as non-selfcontained, which links them to creation maybe not in theme but in practice.

            Dex Davican wrote: I can say without exaggeration or dishonesty that I am the most creative man ever to have lived

                            There are two angles to why DBs fail to prove teamwork beats ego in 2e.

                            The first is that setting-wise the Dynasts (not Lookshy) are exceedingly powered and motivated by egoism. Starting with the Empress, who's the epitome of a solo DB from the moment she got to the Imperial Manse. She made it on her own, she confided in no one, even her fall was so "single" her own children aren't sure what happened. Next is Mnemon, different generation, same story. From the murders in the Heptagram to her solo bid for the throne. Her greatest power? An artifact for her own use, granting her a unique power by DB measures. Sworn Brotherhoods? Never mentioned. Even the lesser, and more protagonist-y, DBs are basically lone wolves. Blackrose, Kes, the Slug...all their triumphs are solo accomplishments, while the pains of their failure are hidden/unconfided.

                            Then there's the system-angle. While results differ wildly depending on optimization/circumstances/etc/etc, it's hard to make a case that Solars aren't operating on a level the Realm is simply not equipped to address at all. Compared to the world-altering powers of Solar Sorcery and charms, the Realm, scraping day-by-day on meager tributary taxes and mounting feeble defenses against the threats overwhelming Creation, comes out so pathetic it's not even worth destorying before the God-Kings deal with more pressing matters and establish greater power bases in the Wyld and the Threshold.

                                            Originally posted by emeraldstreak View Post


                                            There are two angles to why DBs fail to prove teamwork beats ego in 2e.

                                            The first is that setting-wise the Dynasts (not Lookshy) are exceedingly powered and motivated by egoism. Starting with the Empress, who's the epitome of a solo DB from the moment she got to the Imperial Manse. She made it on her own, she confided in no one, even her fall was so "single" her own children aren't sure what happened. Next is Mnemon, different generation, same story. From the murders in the Heptagram to her solo bid for the throne. Her greatest power? An artifact for her own use, granting her a unique power by DB measures. Sworn Brotherhoods? Never mentioned. Even the lesser, and more protagonist-y, DBs are basically lone wolves. Blackrose, Kes, the Slug...all their triumphs are solo accomplishments, while the pains of their failure are hidden/unconfided.
                                            Thinking about it I don't really see teamwork as in competition with the Dynasts ego driven society actually...

                                            I know like when we think of teamwork, the first idea that springs to mind is, like the Power Rangers, or the Elite Four from Kill La Kill, and those style of heroes.

                                            And yeah, OK Dynasts are being pushed away from the sentai thing (Outcastes vary and have more cultural freedom to meet it in a straightforward manner). but think of it this way:

                                            If the Dragonblooded had flexible skills signature to their personality that had effects that varied depending on how they mixed with allies' signature skills, then how much more interesting would plots to get allies on side for one conspiracy or another be? The intrigue genre there is the closest to the Dynasts' default genre - making their magic work together places all the more importance on bonds, betrayal, politics.

                                            The Roseblack or Mnemon should totally be cultivating elite lieutenants who work well with together and with her. These people shouldn't necessarily be indispensable or dear to their hearts (Mnemon is rather a cold, hard woman and the Roseblack isn't exactly warm), but there's definitely room for them to have allies and to be careful to pick those who synergise with them, while that's a tool for their own ego and agenda.

                                            The fact that the RB and those like her aren't written like this, I think is more a failure of writing than how it should be. Partly its because the Caste / Aspect Book format we have encourages characters to talk about themselves as individuals in a certain way - their history growing up, what they think about certain kinds of spirits, etc. - that in many ways fits more for Solars. Honestly, I think for Dragonbloods the Aspect Books were geared to an extent towards showcasing people who were exceptional for their society.

                                            Yeah, Sworn Brotherhoods shouldn't be super permanent in a way that impedes Dynasts having their own agendas, interests and careers, but whenever a Dragonblood is put into a situation, he should be thinking about who he can recruit and work with, get on side, even if they don't care for the power of friendship, and aware that he's at a disadvantage if he's not, because Dragonbloods I think should be inherently that kind of social (but not necessarily pro-social) Exalted, whose powers run together like the elements. Some of these alliances are heartfelt (they're all Stark-Baratheon stuff) and others are purely for political advantage, and everything between that.

                                            On another level, I think that ratchet of power for cooperating (even in a purely self interested way) also gives a certain amount of heroism to Dragonbloods like the Slug who are pariahs or those who turn their back on that society - they'd actually be giving up something more meaningful and doing something harder than if Dragonblooded were built to thrive alone.

                                                Originally posted by Omicron View Post
                                                Infamously, in 2e, the absence of applicability-trumping perfect defenses in the native Dragon-Blooded Charmset means that it is possible for a Solar (and not even all that hard, really) to build a combo that can instantly slaughter every Dragon-Blood within a given radius without possibility of defense on his first action.

                                                You're not wrong, nor was DrLoveMonkey above when he mentioned the archers scenario.

                                                I count myself lucky because I've been able to play Exalted in vastly different groups on both the system and the setting axis. It's enlightening to play the game, with say, people who never read the books. People who don't open a splat and say: oh, look, the Perfect is a mortal with a N/A artifact, the Empress is kindnapped by the Ebon Dragon, and we know what all the evil Deathlords are up to as well!

                                                Meanwhile their polar opposites are the people who are not only knowledgeable of all things Exalted (ok, let's go discuss our supersecret plan at the behemoth's cave where Jupiter stole all secrets, so even Jupiter doesn't know what we're up to) but are also very opinionated exactly on questions like how the Realm represents the evils of imperialism.

                                                To complicate matters more, there are the attribute+ability pools to represent, ie informing the non-book players of important facts/ideas their characters should know or how much Occult should the character of the player with the Jupiter plan have to be in-character to propose it.


                                                When it comes to mechanics, there was the circle content to have 1 dot total of hearthstones/artifacts 60 sessions into a campaign, a fair number of Exalted defeated in tavern brawls, the stressed Solar who was losing a battle in a cellar due to darkness but couldn't remember that Solars are light itself; and then there was the circle who had all the perfects and breakers and magic flurries on the very first session of 2E ever and that wasn't even discussed because it was simply the obvious way of the system.


                                                So, when it comes to the fluff, Zelbinnean nailed it that 2E went way overboard with the dark characters, and the "exception" characters to the point we could not say what the original intend of a splat was, Dragonblooded included. When it comes to mechanics the low bar for DBs is, well, a normal mortal+anima for a DB character with zero investment in combat played by a player clueless of the system (with standard WhiteWolf writeups being not much better).

                                                The upper bar greatly depends on optimization but also the relative place of the Dragonbloods in the pecking order of the crunch. That place was fluid depending on incoming splats, erratas, but also on certain key STs' decisions. Very roughly here are the eras of DB power:

                                                - Early 2E. Right out of the Core itself Dragonblooded had two standings in the system. How come? Well, the issue was stunts. STs who awarded from nearly no to what is now the official guideline didn't allow enough mote regeneration to recoup perfect use. However, there were STs who awarded (and even advocated) stunts on each and every defense, making mote-tapping a Solar spamming Seven Shadow Evasion a baffling epic quest.

                                                - Late 2E. While there were a number of developments useful for the Dragonblooded, the advent of tick and action-long perfects, followed by the Dawn Solution and Overdrive left the DBs in a terrible mook-rank position. That was the point when Dragonblooded had to rely on effects out of their native charmset simply to patch glaring holes in their capabilities. Meanwhile, a Solar starting with a tick-long perfect, Joy in Adversity, and a scene-long surprise negator was borderline invincible in normal circumstances right out of the box; while high XP Solars were Sharp Light of Judgement beacons who could ostensibly engage entire Dragonblooded armies solo, and even more jarring, disengage to safety under the most hostile of circumstances.

                                                - 2.5. This is the version most friendly to Dragonblooded, but probably still below what their relative crunch should be to properly represent their setting place (mind you, Celestial combat superiority is only one way they eclipse the Realm).


                                                OK, but what does that mean in actual examples of the high bar of Dragonblooded power and how ST decisions' impact the system? I'll give you two examples of actual DB antagonists used in high-powered campaigns.

                                                The first one is a (fairly) Early 2E character inspired by the Maiden of Mirthless Smile viewpoint story in the 1E Core, the Hill-Crushing Hero, a fearsome Dragonblooded of unmatched stamina and strength. While the original concept was an Outcaste mercenary, this one is an even more deadly Elder Immaculate Grandmaster. The creation date is probably shortly after Scroll of Heroes. The character relies on various adders and multipliers to compensate for the Celestial perfection via straight out-statting them. Mind you, he isn't a match for Late 2E Solars which underlines how the game changed in the 2.0 itself.

                                                (warning: Merits rage trigger)

                                                Earth Grandmaster


                                                The next character is one who spearheaded the Usurpation itself, now returning to haunt the Lawgivers more powerful than ever. She was created as a story arc final antagonist (under the last 2.0 errata) for 1,000 XP Solar circle. Yup, facing an entire circle, each Solar at 1k XP with backgrounds to boot. However, this campaign had a major houserule: no mote or Willpower regeneration when one has used a perfect defense, for the duration of said perfect defense only. It was implemented to combat infinite perfect spamming, however, as everything else in the Second Edition it could only improve the flow of the game so far before causing problems of its own. In essence, this houserule shifted the metagame to finding ways to remove unblockable or undodgeable qualities of attacks and negating them with high DVs to gain stunt and charm mote/WP recovery. Of course, there were a number of other RAW clarifications and houserules.

                                                Unfortunately for the example, this character is also an Akuma, but nonetheless showcases the final 2.0 Dragonblooded power. A pure Dragon-Blooded would have 1 dot lower basic stats and would have to use Blade of the Battle Maiden instead of Yozi Inevitability. As I have only a quick combat reference sheet left, let me go over her powers:

                                                - Mantle of Elemental Fusion, fused with a Lesser Earth Dragon of the Quicksilver Queen stature. The main benefits derived are Essence Plethoras, Destiny Sponsorship inflicted 15 external penalty on attacks against Dodge DV, Invincible Symbol of Authority:Earth that denies/reduces earth and metal based attacks, including magic materials based on metals.
                                                - Transcendant Gaian Harmony:Air and Wood, inflicting 8 to 16 external penalties on certain attacks per the animas (She's Earth Aspect)
                                                - lesser penalty applicators like Consuming Might of the Fire Dragon and Flowing Water Defense (note that her stats presume a fight going on for some time so she has accumulated enough Water Style free motes)
                                                - Unchain the Dragon's Heart: probably won't come into play, but if it does it's a nasty surprise
                                                - Ordained Talons of Draconic Fury on the Eye of the Fire Dragon. That's the Rate 13 weapon line and the artifact itself does aggravated damage and charmbreaks Solars
                                                - a number of renamed hearthstones which (I guess) represent Jewel of the Flying Heart, White Jade Tree, and Gemstone of Shadows
                                                - Crystal Chameleon Speed: for Surprise/Stealth. Note that Flowing Kata allows moving between Forms as needed
                                                - Joy in Adversity. Yeah. The big mote regenerator available to CMA-mooks to even out the field against Solars a bit

                                                (warning: Cobra rage trigger)

                                                - Dragon-Blooded natively lack in many areas that can be addressed by Celestial Martial Arts; at the time Cobra Style offered 1) perfects 2) reflexive attacks, while the major houserule made the root charm of the style better
                                                - since that's just a reference sheet, not a full build, there are many charms not shown, ie the Style Forms under the Flowing Kata. Something interesting not shown is the TMA Smoke Adept Gesture for acting on tick 0. Anyway, here it is.
