        Fire Aspects were definitely one of the most popular (possibly the most popular) aspect I saw played (with Air second, then Water, then Air, with Earth definitely at the bottom, probably because they had no real combat ability).
        Not just because of their good spread (I mean, I think people just thought shooting fire and fighting while on fire is awesome), but that's part of it. I mean, if you thought "I'll play a social/combat character", Fire Aspects have the most obviously useful set of abilities. Air was generally spies and sorcerers; Water for spies, pirates or martial artists; Wood either wilderness archers or Cynis decadents; Earth hardly ever appeared.
        It didn't help that Melee and Dodge happened to more powerful than, say, Resistance or Archery. I mean, you should have been able to go "I'll play a guy who focuses on being really tough in combat, so I'll play an Earth" but in practice you weren't that good.

        I still wouldn't want the out-of-aspect charge even if it covers more, because it's still maths that players will continually forget.
        I'm certainly not going to bother using it for the NPCs when I run a 3rd ed combat against the Wyld Hunt in this week's session.

                                                Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post

                                                But part of the fun can come from restrictions! Especially considering that DBs are the polar opposite to Solars in that regard. Sidereals are also like this, to a lesser extent. They've got really restrictive charmsets and even their fluff has a bunch of obstacles you've got to get around. They're weighted with responsibility, oftentimes from many places, and they need to balance that against eachother and their own personal wants and values.

                                                For Sidereals, their charms are powerful, but not broad in scope and can't be added onto with your own custom charms. For DBs they're really pulled into their aspect, to the point where it affects their personalities. You can absolutely be a wood aspect melee fighter, but you're going to struggle just a bit more than a fire aspect to do it. It's not much, just a little extra push of essence every time you use it to enhance your sword, but it's there. You have to come to terms with the fact that you're struggling against the blood of Sextes Jylis, and that it's a slightly uphill battle.

                                                It's represented mechanically because sometimes fluff and mechanics are tied together. Solars are the most powerful so they get the most powerful charms, Sidereals are strange fate ninjas so their charms do odd, powerful and sneaky things.
                                                why exactly does it need to be this way? What's the value in having a player who wants to be a Wood Aspect swordsman suck a mechanical penalty every time he wants to use Melee charms? I agree with SpruceStripedGoose. Better to have specific elemental weird flair things than take a mechanical dump on a concept which is what the out of aspect surcharge does.

                                                Sometimes mechanics and fluff are tied together, sure. Not always successfully. Sids' closed Charmset accomplishes this much better than the surcharge or something like formlock. The closed Charmset encourages creative Charm use. Formlock and the surcharge encourage one true builds or limited options in a way that don't actually benefit creativity. What value do they actually create for the DB play experience? I can't think of one, unlike what the closed Charmset does for Sids.
