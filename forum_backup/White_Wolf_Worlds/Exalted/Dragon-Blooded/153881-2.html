    Actually, I think a more succinct summary of my prior point is: the hereditary nature of the Terrestrial Exaltation does lend itself to aristocratic drama narratives, especially when it intersects with those Dragon Blooded who have wanted to hold authoritative power (which just so happens to be the defining force of the Second Age), but it is not the central narrative created by such heredity.

    I'd say the most basic narrative of a hereditary Exaltation is divided between "Interesting family" (on those occasions of people with good enough breeding that their nuclear and possibly extended family contains many Exalted) and "Remarkable Ancestor (whose legacy informs what you're expected to live up to)", in those cases where you Exalt kind of inexplicably.

    The former particularly... I'd say there were Terrestrial families in the First Age who were no more nobility than, say, the Skywalker, Summers or Parr families are.

                        Originally posted by Omicron View Post
                        ...what?

                        That's completely off-base.

                        The Wyld Hunt deals with the Anathema and existential threats such as the Fair Folk; they don't have anything to do with the Outcaste. Sure, Outcastes/Lost Eggs can get in trouble with the Realm, but dealing with them will never be a job for the Wyld Hunt - only for Dynasts or the Legions. And since there are more Outcastes than there are Dynasts, conducting a policy of systematically rooting out Lost Eggs that cause trouble would be a very poor idea indeed.
                        I don't know if it's explicitly stated anywhere how many DBs exist outside of the influence of the Realm and Lookshy. Regardless, both of those states have a major interest in collecting as many DBs as they can, of the highest purity blood they can find, and can suffer a major setback if any Dragon-Blood decides to set up a cult to himself elsewhere (which goes against the Immaculate Philosophy, something most members of the Wyld Hunt won't take kindly to).

                        Yes, any DB that can stay far enough away from the Immaculate Order or the Realm's forces can live well... provided he has the right kind of friends or a large enough bankroll (or lives far enough away) that people won't come by to try to recruit/capture/kill him.

                        Furthermore, Outcastes thrive outside the Realm. The majority of nations or organization with influence have Outcastes in their employ, from Halta to the Haslanti League to the Lintha, with others working for the Guild or just becoming rulers in their own right. The Outcastes have incredible assets, and life is much, much easier from the get-go for them than it is for a mortal - those who want to strike out on their own might have a tough time doing so as would anyone, but the world at large prizes and desires their talents.
                        Right, and foremost among them are the two powers- the Realm and Lookshy- which are founded on the strength of their Dragon-Blooded rulers. Although if you have a bit more of a power base, you can probably expect a more... friendly approach.

                            I don't know that the Realm/Lookshy is going to come down hard on a DB that sets up a power base independent of them, especially if it isn't in direct opposition to their goals. First of all the Realm doesn't really have the time or resources to go chasing after every lost egg (there are tons of them), and second, by setting themselves up as rulers, the Outcaste are directly reinforcing the whole culture of "Divine Right of Dragons" without the Realm even having to do anything at all.

                            I also don't think that, should they have a problem with a particular Dragon-Blood, that capturing or killing them is the way to go. They're much more likely to use the carrot rather than the stick for the sole reason that coercing your prospective elemental demigods into joining your war machine tends to be a terrible idea. If you've got skills, the Realm wants them, surely. But so does everyone else. And if you want to rule a nation you had best be strong or rich or have powerful friends, but... that's always true when you want to rule a nation.

                            Edit: Basically I think said friendly approach is the default mode, and if the Realm is trying to kill or kidnap an Outcaste for some reason other than conquering their lands, things have gone very wrong.

                            Double edit: And it's not like they can't afford to play the long game. Sure, an Outcaste may be causing a lot of trouble now, but give them twenty years to start that biological clock ticking and set them wondering about legacy, and they'll see the merit in joining up with the greatest military force in history, right? Dinars flow like water and mortals (especially ones in the satrapies, who aren't even good Realm citizens) are never in short supply, but a powerful Dragon-Blooded officer is worth his weight in jade.

                                    Moreover, while the Immaculate Philosophy is practiced in Lookshy, it is a muted, reformed variant that (at least until the 3e spoilers) lacks any official analogue of a Wyld Hunt. Lookshy culture stresses that religious beliefs should be personal and private, and dissent from the Philosophy is implied to be commonplace. That's not to say that a Lookshyan with a cult would be overlooked, but it's not like the Realm.
