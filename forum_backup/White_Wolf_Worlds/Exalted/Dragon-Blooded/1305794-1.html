        I think generally most of us can agree that it would be bad for the setting if nearly every Dragonblood has sorcery (just as if it would be bad if nearly all of them practiced Immaculate Martial Arts), but if you want all your PCs to have sorcery, sure. Not a big deal.
        Personally, I'd find that boring (because I think the PCs would be a bit similar), but if I was running the game and all the PCs wanted to be sorcerers... sure. Not a big deal. I'd probably encourage them to have different signature spells, aspects, favoured abilities, etc, so they weren't too samey. And initiations, but then, as Elfive said...
        Originally posted by Elfive View Post
        An all sorcerer dragon blooded game is actually pretty easy to justify while preserving the "sorcery is rare" setting conceit.

        "We met at the heptagram"
        That's the easiest way, so then most of them are going to have the Heptagram initiation (though not all, I'm sure if someone wanted a different one they could justify it).

        I will say though...
        Originally posted by The Fool of Creation
        First, a beginning DB sorcerer gains a lot of additional functionality (the ability to use shaping rituals, the ability to use sorcerous workings, access to a control spell, and access to proprietary merits) for the low price of
        5 charms (though probably you'll take a free excellency, which will be one of those)
        and 3 Ability dots. Second, while beginning DBs have less powerful Charms than beginning Solars, their beginning spells are just as powerful, so Sorcery ends up helping to balance the scales, especially since their extra dot of Essence improves the spells.
        So, while there's the point about actually needing 5 occult charms to get 1 spell and access to workings, it's still pretty strong. As you said, their spells are just as powerful as a starting Solar's (their Occult excellency is a bit worse, but their Essence is better).
        So I'd agree that Sorcery is a very strong choice for a Dragonblood.

        On the other hand:
        Immaculate Martial Arts are a very strong choice for a Dragonblood. They're martial arts that let you wear armour, and for a Dragonblood are kind of Solar Tier. (It's difficult to compare, but with Aura they're probably close to a style with Mastery.)
        And there are no prerequisites beyond a combat ability, which obviously you probably want anyway.
        They don't even cost more XP than DB charms (unlike spells), and you'll be able to scale more as you go up in XP (whereas spells cap out at Essence 1, though there is a good Essence 3 charm DB sorcerers can get).
        And you can combine multiple Immaculate Styles, whereas spells don't tend to combo very well (a little; Wood Dragon Claw works with Brawl, etc. But there's not much in the way of combat charms you can use to boost Death of Obsidian Butterflies).

        Evocations are a very strong choice for a Dragonblood. Your extra merits means you can easily start with an artefact weapon (or armour). You're Resonant with Jade, which means you can get Mastery effects on Jade weapons, same as a Solar. You have to pay slightly more XP than a Solar would, but you do get to buy Essence 1+2 evocations from your starting charms, you can get 2-5 evocations from a tree at character creation (when the extra XP doesn't matter).
        Also, Evocations combo really well with other stuff. For example, you can combo a magic bow that shoots lightning bolts with Archery charms, or a trident for fighting underwater with Melee charms.
        Also, a Lunar who walks around with a giant silver axe is kind of giving themselves away as a monstrous demon-beast. A Dragonblood who walks around with a huge jade spear is giving themselves away as a Demigod who you should probably pay homage to.

        Social charms are a very strong choice for a Dragonblood. A)They have a good set of social charms, though mostly focused on inspiring emotions. B)You're an aristocrat in an intrigue-laden world-spanning power. You've probably started with money and resources. It's quite likely you're in a lot more social situations than the average group of Solars.

        Craft charms are a strong choice for Dragonblood. Okay, in a mixed game you can't compete with the speed of Solar charms. But unlike Solars (and Lunars), you can probably much more easily get your hands on magic materials, which I always find is a big problem for Celestial crafters.

        Linguistics charms are a good choice for Dragonblood. Wind-Carried Words is game-changing.

        Etc, etc, etc.



        Anyway, my point is, yes, sorcery is a strong choice. But so are many other options, and a starting DB with, say, 5 spells, has spent more like half their charms on being a sorcerer. Another character could have bought the Martial Arts excellency and the first four charms of Wood Dragon and Air Dragon.
        Or the first four evocations of a magic sword's tree and 4 melee charms.

        So, if you have a group of four players who are really interested in having powerful characters (as opposed to four who are more interested in fluff and don't care too much about power), then I don't think you'll get four sorcerers. Immaculate Martial Arts and evocations are too powerful a choice, and someone having plenty of social charms is always useful.
        (And obviously social charms aren't really comparable to spells; you can't really make a social-sorcerer whose spells are better at socialising than DB social charms.)

        Thus, I think it's likely, even for players who are quite interested in having powerful characters, that they'll be a sorcerer or not a sorcerer depending on their character concept.
        (Which is... how it ought to work. So it's good design really. I think if Sorcery didn't require four other Occult charms, it would be way too popular, but that requirement means it requires a serious investment. I suspect the Lunars' requirement for four other Mental attribute charms was inspired by it; while it's not very onerous, it does ensure that being a sorcerer is a big part of the character; your Full Moon combat monster with 14 combat charms can't go "oh, I could do with the permanent soak bonus from having Invulnerable Skin of Bronze as a signature spell, I'll just pick that up with my last charm purchase, even though it's not at all a significant part of my character." Especially as there aren't so many Essence 1 mental attribute charms available that you can easily pick up four Perception charms that are purely about combat against non-spirits. An Essence 1 Lunar with four mental charms quite likely has something occulty. Of the three Lunars in the game I run with mental charms, 2 have spirit-fighting charms and the other has the Wits charm so he can stick a magical fire gem in his flesh so he can have burning fists.)

        Originally posted by Fool of Creation
        as there is nothing cooler than being a spell-slinger equipped with powered combat armor while dual-wielding flame pistols.
        This is absolutely a fine concept.
        But if you want to take a really strong choice (which is of course, not always the best way to build a character, but it was your starting point so we'll go with that), you're probably better off taking some Evocations for your flame-pistols and power-armour, and some Archery charms (like the one for reflexively reloading... although that's a common evocation), instead of four Occult charms and a few Spells.

        I think the sorcerer with power armour and flame-pistols is more interesting and cool, but the more powerful choice in terms of combat is Evocations.

                                                            Originally posted by Maseiken View Post
                                                            Yeah like, to be the best sorcerer you can be you should really have Dodge and Brawl at 5 which is not hard at chargen.
                                                            That said, “what I spent all these years bowing a scraping and studying, finally mastered the diagrams, summoned and bound this Blood Ape into my service as a bodyguard, and now I’m supposed to Sweat? Outside? So I can avoid getting hurt?

                                                            That’s what the demon is for, chief.
                                                            I will say, while I see people's points about the irrelevance of optimisation, that's not the only thing.

                                                            Yes, you might want Brawl and Dodge 5. But your sorcerer only needs Occult 5. And maybe Lore 5. And then you want this, and that, and this, and that...

                                                            I have a Essence 5 Lunar with over 460xp. Maybe 480? I can't remember off-hand.

                                                            My highest social pool is 5 dice (Charisma 3 and Presence 2). I have two Charisma charms (both Essence 1), to get the excellency. My Resolve is 3, and I can use charms to increase it to... 4 (without a stunt).

                                                            Why, for a 460xp character, are my social stats so bad?
                                                            Because I've invested heavily in combat, medicine, and tracking. I have like 14 perception charms, 5 poison charms, 10 martial arts charms, 3 dex combat charms, a strength charm, 7 medicine charms, etc. It adds up.

                                                            Everything is trade-offs. You can probably at character gen focus on about two areas heavily. So in the OP's example, we've got a sorcerer (that's 5 charms right there just for one spell), Archer (probably 3 or 4 charms). Then you want some defence charms, say another 2 or 3.
                                                            So that's a combat-sorcerer... and not much else.
                                                            You've probably spent 6 merit points on artefacts (3 for armour, 3 for devil-casters), so you don't have a ton of social merits.

                                                            And what about your attributes? You want a maxed-out Intelligence score so you're a powerful sorcerer (and don't get beaten up when you try summoning demons). So let's say mental primary. Let's say Perception 2, Int 5, Wits 4, so you've got a good Join Battle for combat and a good dice pool to banish demons if you fail your binding roll.

                                                            Then you have Physical Secondary. Strength 2 (don't need it for flame weapons), Dex 5, Stamina 2. You're good at shooting, but your soak is actually quite poor.

                                                            This leaves something like Cha 3, Manipulation 2, App 2.

                                                            In social intrigue, in research, in craft, in athletics and survival... this character is not very good.


                                                            Which is really how it should be. However you build your character, there's other things you're not much good at.


                                                            So, actually the chances are, many sorcerers with a whole bunch of demons actually aren't that good at combat, and so probably are tempted to stay inside and send their demons out to do the work. They've put their points into mental attributes and abilities like Occult, Lore and so on, they probably want some social stats because it's very important as a Dynast, and so they may end up not that good at physical stuff.

                                                            (Actually, in the DB games I played all the sorcerers either were not much good at combat, or got screwed because they were good at combat and sorcery but were constantly socially out-manouevred.)
