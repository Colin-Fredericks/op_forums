                Okay, the general culture of the Dragon Blooded of Lookshy. I think I’ll mostly comment on any interesting distinctions from the Scarlet Dynasty, and their implications.

                I take the line on the military focus to mean that while Lookshy’s Exalted has the same belief in themselves as the top of the Perfected Hierarchy, but in practical terms they express that primarily in military missions for the sake of the city and River Province, rather than having quite the variation that the Dynasty does (even if there are a few gentes that shift in those directions, and would like it if society as a whole would do the same).

                Given how the city is apparently named for the standing order that the Seventh Legion is operating under, I wonder what the in-character basis for the name Lookshy might be. I also wonder exactly what their standards for the emergence of a new Shogun would be; are they waiting on somebody with a proven line of descent from the last Shogun, or a hero figure who proves themselves worthy of the rank, or has it ultimately just become a point of rhetoric?

                Yes, I could summarise the biggest difference between the cultures as being that Dynasts are raised to see themselves as rulers of the world, while… they really need a simple word of their own to be called by… Sevens are raised with military discipline to take up officer duties. It’s interesting that they don’t have a focus on individual glory, considering that House Tepet’s emphasis on same is part of their Shogunate legacy.

                Their education also seems a bit less intense, possibly because Lookshy doesn’t quite have the money to maintain the more elaborate infrastructure. It’s not said, but if they’re married at 18, I assume they graduate from their academies by then as well.

                Yeah, all of this stuff about the emphasis they place upon recruitment and encouraging adoption into the gens seems to validate that point on the difference between many Dragon Blooded, and well-bred ones. Considering the status of the Dragon Blooded, the General Staff wouldn’t be the first head of state to have direct authority over a form of adoption, but the actual trappings of military administration gives it an amusing image.

                Sooo, even when the gentes have their own sidelines, all of their members will have engaged in at least some military service. I imagine that gives the Exalted as a whole a stronger sense of national identity and loyalty than the Scarlet Dynasty does. Personally, I’d want to express things like training and practice drills as forms of regular festivity that Lookshy has; not necessarily that they’re engaged in frivolously, just as the format in which such activity is engaged. As I recall, the weekly archery practice that was mandatory in periods of Medieval Europe was the kind of thing that they treated as a kind of regular picnic.

                Hmm, I wonder if Field Forces might be a bit smaller and more specialized than the legion-like bodies of prior Editions…. Nnnot necessarily, not if they have those methods of ranking. They might still be broken up to distribute on multiple fronts.

                My read on Valkhawsen is that its method represents a single, unified form of sorcerous initiation, as opposed to the more freeform approach of the Heptagram. It’s interesting that the martial focus of Lookshy would make them more comfortable with sorcery as a whole, even if they’re still wary of the special branch for summoning spirits.

                Hmm, is chumyo another open position of the Seventh Legion?

                Ahh, so the academies may be more numerous and less intensive, but they’re all nationalised as a means of maintaining an orthodoxy. It also seems like a good call for them to place such emphasis on the well-being of the troops; keep it from being something that might turn in on itself.

                Ah, I was wondering what the Shogunate Bureaucracy was, as I have no recollection of that whatsoever. Whether it’s new or not, I am amused by the image of the local equivalent of a ceremonial Imperial Court being the last remnant of the civil service. Mind, if they’re addressed directly in the Lookshy Directive… did the book already say where that order originated?
                Hmm, with the description of their First Age Armoury having more emphasis on being gradually diminishing, the changed nature of Artifacts in general, and some of the descriptions of the Realm, I wonder if Lookshy is now less strong then the Realm as a whole (possibly apart from the potential to bring all of its Exalted to bear), and while pivotal in the capacity of the River Province to resist, would be in significant trouble if they had to deal with the Realm alone.

                Money is also a very strong contrast with the Realm, both in the sense that it’s not a natural entitlement of the Exalted and another method to keep them cohesive through state service. If a soldier’s pay is expected to be reliable, I can see the Seventh Legion being very strenuous about keeping its revenue streams constantly flowing (although possibly not to the point that they deliberately sabotage peace to continuously secure contracts).

                The significance of the Immaculate Faith of Lookshy not being a state religion feels more apparent with the preview given of The Realm, if they’re focused entirely on spiritual ministrations rather than having roles such as acting as a kind of morality police. The other stuff about not proselytizing and lacking a cohesive hierarchy, that would be different but could still fit in an officially recognised system, but lacking that kind of social remit really sets them apart. It also lends itself to the plot hook about there being a faction that does want the Seventh Legion to impose those values as far as it can reach.

                Ah, and their belief that being Exalted does not, on its own, constitute elevated spiritual authority would make sense as the basis for their only licensing somebody with Immaculate training to summon demons and elementals.

                Ha, I love the idea that when Realm Wyld Hunts come within Lookshy’s sphere of influence, the Seventh Legion’s response is to upgrade their troops. I’ve suddenly got this image of more than a few leaders of the Hunt wanting to take deliberate detours through the Scavenger Lands for that very reason, even if they’re going somewhere like the Caul.

                Oh, wait, apparently collaborations of that type against somebody very dangerous (probably like Sha’a Oka and other Lunars of the Caul) would be a matter of course. Fair enough.

                Does anybody want to tell me if the Code of the Righteous Warrior is new? It at least sounds like something that would resonate with House Tepet’s warrior codes. In any case, it’s another interesting implication on the manner in which the Faith judges the Dragon Blooded. I can also see it as a particular sticking point between monks of Lookshy and the Realm (as well as something for a few of the latter to start preaching at home).

                Hmm, Lookshy may not be without its issues, but it might overall be a nicer place for its Exalted to grow up, without the extreme upbringing the violence and the mind games. I mean, sure, they have mind games of their own, just without needing to end in murder.

                Yeah, if Lookshy wants to generally avoid direct confrontation of the Realm, I’m definitely feeling that they’re not as strong against them as they used to be. With what I was saying before about the difference in Immaculates, this comment about them being restricted to a certain quarter puts me in mind of the popular Lookshyan image being somebody who goes around publically castigating folks for everyday activities. I’m seeing something like a bawdy comedy in which a monk goes up to some popular Lookshy shop and starts ranting and raving about its sign (possibly getting into some pratfalls as they try to tear it down). Heh, I liked the parts about House Ledaal holding a grudge against the Mask of Winters, so I’m moved by the idea of them having claimed the diplomatic rights to Lookshy in pursuit of that (although I can also see some of them having reservations about the possibility of Ledaal pulling them into their own crusade, domestic reservations about Thorns aside).

                Hmm, has the timing of the Battle of Mishaka been changed? Didn’t it used to be something that was intimately tied to the downfall of House Iselsi?

                Anyway, I’m glad that there’s at least one place in which a strong relationship with the Guild is described.

                Aaaaand nothing new about the city’s gods. They’re fine.

                So yeah, Lookshy is solid. Still not quite as vivid as the Scarlet Dynasty to me, but it does its job. One thing that I do have a particular liking for is the sense that they’re not quite the power in opposition to the Realm that they used to be, and whether there are other new details or not, I feel that makes their relationship a bit more interesting.

                                                    And now to finish off the chapter with the rest of the outcastes.

                                                    I’ll admit, I’ve already gotten a vague sense of what some of these entail from surrounding discussion. It got me thinking about some of the premises of the Exalted when we were coming into this Edition. Between introducing the likes of Exigents, and the notion of as yet unrevealed types of Exalted, I recall people displaying a very distinct sense that the setting was going to start bursting at the seams with an eclectic variety of Exalted (some of whom did not respond positively to the idea). With time, I personally find that the ultimate realisation of the concepts was a lot more subdued (which I think was all to the good), and yet some of the principles seems to have found a home in the Dragon Blooded. I will say, in hindsight, it does seem like the Terrestrial Exalted, with their numbers and diversity and being spread far and wide across the setting, had a largely untapped potential for hosting a lot of weirdness deviating from the orthodoxy of the Realm and even Lookshy. Jenna Moran certainly recognised such; I’ll be interested to see the specifics of the ideas that they came up with.

                                                    For a start, though, I do think it’s good to emphasize the notion that a lot of outcastes stand alone, and a bit of discussion of what that entails. I like the idea that many of them are driven by the example of the Scarlet Empress, even if it’s possible that many far flung Exalted know the barest details about her, as well as the potential that the Dynasty represents for sworn kin. Mind, given the description of outcastes from the perspective of the Realm, I imagine that such an endeavour is most successful when one meets Dynasts engaged in business around the Threshold, rather than journeying to the Blessed Isle expecting a warm reception.

                                                    So, a sidebar giving a brief description of some extra outcaste groups:

                                                    I recall the Cult of the Violet Fang being mentioned earlier, but can’t recall many of their details. I like the description of their home base, since structures carved directly from gemstones have always said “Fair Folk” to me, and I like the idea of them having something like the treasure questing that the Raksha of Editions past engaged in with unshaped, particularly if the opponents are their own immortal ancestors.

                                                    The Grass Spiders have been in the game for a while, although I’m still not entirely sure what they’re about; not what they do, that’s clear enough, but why they do it. Maybe there’s a hint in the idea of them going about assassination artistically? Perhaps their proximity to Lookshy and the Realm makes secrecy and criminal services a matter of preserving independence? They never quite had a lot going for them.

                                                    A secret cadre of Terrestrials that have resided in Yu-Shan since time immemorial, on the other hand, seems like an intriguing premise (and a firm nail through the heart of Terrestrials being largely forbidden from the divine city). I particularly like the plot hook of them occasionally being cast out of Heaven, not just for the potential culture shock, but for the seed of friends and allies with dark backstories and hidden agendas.

                                                    I also like the idea of a nomadic society of Dragon-Blooded, particularly with the isolationist bent that they have. In contrast there’s the more recent desertion of Saloy Hin, and I like how their story has been changed from being on the hunt for a superweapon to leading a patchwork army around a more complicated strategic plan. I particularly enjoy the novelty of them, rather than being a mercenary force, or even trying to conquer their locality, are gathering and distributing munitions through the Realm’s most lucrative satrapies as part of a long-term goal to return home for the big prize.

                                                    And a bit of a more compelling mystery than the Grass Spiders in the form of Yatani’s Children and their odd claims of an extraterrestrial (so to speak) origin.

                                                    Now, what about some of the big ones…

                                                    I like the imagery of the Wanasaan, with their distinct appearance and how their schtick is reminiscent of a few good wandering exorcist/paranormal investigator stories. With all the talk of sorcery recently, I appreciate another vivid example of a form of initiation, and I appreciate a firm example of Terrestrials involved in some freaky magic not just because of the variety it lends them throughout Creation, but because it gives a sense of context for the kinds of powers that Dynastic Houses like Ledaal and Ragara might develop. I might have liked them to be a bit bigger, but I enjoy the manner in which their small size drives action.

                                                    The Sisterhood of Pearls combines a couple of good things from the periphery of the Realm; Immaculate heresy, and Dynasts (or close enough) being far enough from home to break away and start their own game, sans blackjack and hookers. Plus the idea that their heterodoxy emphasizes pacifism and egalitarianism, of all things. Heh, the subversion of many common perceptions of the Exalted alone… I appreciate the motivations behind the growing division in their community, both for either side having a reasonable basis for what it wants to do and the manner in which its criticisms for the other are rooted in their ideology, and how they really don’t want to be torn apart. It’s a compelling dilemma surrounding a complicated situation without an easy answer.

                                                    Hmm, the Temple of the Reverent Whisper… you know, for a moment there, I thought that what it was saying was that they were a group of Terrestrials dedicated to providing sex work for gods, which seemed both narrow, and a bit of an odd throwback to late-First/early-Second Edition ideas about the Dragon Blooded being the ultimate courtesans. But the novelty of their service instead taking the form of them offering specialised forms of worship to divinities with particular tastes, taking advantage of their greater hardiness and capability? On its own, that’s genius, but I find it to also be another strong example of the kinds of stories that are unique to the Dragon Blooded. It’s a kind of… this is the sort of story that you could only do with Exalted that function as a group like this, and it’s in a kind of servile role, but it’s still something that emphasizes their power and dignity as Exalted, which also helps to think of distinct ways that they might have served in the Old Realm (and might relate anew to the Solars) that makes them more than just lackeys or middle-management.

                                                    And finishing off with the Seven Storms Brotherhood, which seems like a nice straightforward gang of bandits…. with trained attack hyenas. Neat. They manage to stand out with some very vivid personalities at their head. If nothing else, I would appreciate the insight into the strongest possibility yet for realising the villains of Ninja Scroll in the game. It’s curious that I never would have considered Dragon Blooded in that role before, but that’s what this half of the chapter is about, eh?

                                                    Kudos on the outcastes here.
