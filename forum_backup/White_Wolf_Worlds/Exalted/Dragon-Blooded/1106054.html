                          The charms are pretty cool. I quite like the intent of them being, well, signature tricks at the heart of each Dragon-Blooded ability charmset with few prerequisites that you can learn as suits your elemental preference. Between the unrevealed details of Elemental Aura and favored costs, you'll probably have enough encouragement to pick the one that matches your element or the aspect favoring that ability, but the flexibility is good.

                          I'm not sure about each signature charm pick locking you out of four potential others. Personally not bothered by it at all and I appreciate how it can distinguish Dragon-Blooded characters, but it might be easy for some perspectives to take umbrage at it as restrictive design.

                          Most of them having no prerequisites (except the Bureaucracy one because I would assume it shares the "main" element of its ability set) reflects well on their accessibility, and all the charms shown are definitely impressive or stylish in their context.

                                      ...okay, Wyld Hunts are gonna be A LOT scarier in this edition.

                                      Distraction of the Babbling Brook is really solid for socialites...ESPECIALLY in conjunction with the Eclipse Oath. Good way to turn the powers of the Anathema against them, or provide still-useful backup in a mixed party.

                                      Sleeping Dragon's Lair...eh, I'm less excited about it, but it's cool for ninja-type characters. It'd pair well with Ebon Shadow Style, for instance...and after the first Dragonblooded ambush by a Wyld Hunt using this charm, PCs are gonna be scared of dirt, which I kind of love.

                                      Tempest Ruling Commander is my new favorite charm. I've been wanting "War Weather" as described in earlier editions for ages. Enough said.

                                      Terrifying Forest Devil Mask...let's you play Zuko pretending to be the Blue Spirit! And lets you play Dragonblooded Batman! And lets you play the White Plains Rider from the quest right here on the forums! It's an excellent charm and I love it.

                                      Wildfire Taming Technique...I didn't love at first. But then I realized I love it. Imagine being HUNTED by a five-mile wide inferno. And the Wyld Hunt following it can walk through it completely unharmed. And if you flee to civilization where it doesn't work...well, the Dragonbloods kind of RUN civilization. This charm feels like the result of a thousand years of war between the Dragonblooded and the Lunar Anathema, and it makes them feel like Princes of the Earth if they're using it as PCs but it's also a great "bad guy" power for when you want to portray a villain not caring about the destruction he causes to try and get to his nemesis. Lots of story potential here!

                                                          I guess I have a question (constructive criticism!) about the concept, just around custom Charms.

                                                          With the Signature Charms as I understand them from the preview, I guess an implication would be that, if they are an open set... then if you go out and write a bunch of Signature Charms, then that means implicitly that you should't go and write a bunch of E5 Charms that do mechanically similar things. As the Essence 3 Signature Charm gets larger, then the Essence 5 Charm selection gets smaller. If so, I'm not sure how good an idea it feels to introduce that kind of extra complexity into custom Charms.

                                                          This kind of thing wouldn't play if there was a sense in which the Signature Charms were distinct from general higher essence Charms.

                                                          Alternatively, and this is probably more accurate to "each Dragon-Blooded Ability has five powerful Signature Charms, one for each element", they are finite and all written by developers and you can't Custom them. But then from that angle, that's kind of restricting homebrew to define characters. It's also restricting the ability of the developers as writers to come up with as many Signature Charms as feels right rather than necessarily five per Ability.

                                                          Essentially, I guess, summed up, like, why this rather than; "At Essence 3 you can buy one Essence 5 Charm in each Ability"? That would seem like it has fewer restrictions.
