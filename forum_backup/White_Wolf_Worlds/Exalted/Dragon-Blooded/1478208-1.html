                                                Just to throw my own 2¢ into this old well, I think it depends on what counts as "loyalty."

                                                Because as many others have said, it's sorta nonsense to define yourself as a political entity by schisms that are ancient history and that you don't have any actionable means to engage with. But I don't actually think that's the main lens to look at this through.

                                                The thought first occurred to me with Lookshy and their concept of the True Heir to the Shogunate (TM), actually, but applies equally well here. A story about "an ancient leader of a golden age whose death precipitated the current age of garbage but who will come back and lead us to a new golden age some day if we maintain devotion through generations of hardship" isn't really a political policy - it's a religion. Not the normative model of a religion in Creation, where most divinities are very active and reinforce their own worship, I'll grant, but an archetypal one for humanity, which I've gotta assume people in Creation are similar enough to real humans to come up with. Especially in a setting where the things that could facilitate such great figures returning - reincarnation (both of the human soul and Exaltation), ancient ghosts, tremendous lifespans and places/magics that can stretch those further, hereditary supernatural power, etc - are just as concretely real as all those divinities.

                                                Now, religions undergo tons of permutation over time and are often founded in the first place on propaganda, so they're not gonna have, like, an accurate view of the First Age, or the Shogunate, or whatnot. And the point that people's politics will focus on their present daily concerns remains salient. To the extent that anybody will know or care about ancient schisms, it'll be to the forms and extents that Christians a thousand+ years removed from ancient Rome know or care about ancient Rome - pretty jerk move to kill Jesus; the feeding Christians to lions thing makes a good allegory to whatever sense of persecution and hardship you're perceiving in the present world; maybe a few nebulously-historical mental images of their fashion and cultural mores invoked by storytellers; but that's sorta it. They're long gone, only the nerds with too much free time care what they thought about anything else. The main utility of any bits and pieces of oral history that remain prevalent about those ancient days will be as a shared cultural lens for interpreting and responding to current events the group is dealing with.

                                                Also important: religions syncretize as much as they permute internally, and 1500+ years is an entire immense history for this religion in its own right, so if they're close enough to Immaculacy to have cultural exchange with them, you could easily expect them to nudge their mental model of their savior so it doesn't overlap with those terrible Anathema. Being founded on loyalty to Solars could mean seeing Solars as your ancient heroes reborn today, but that's far from a guarantee. Hell, they could've had a schism about that 600 years ago, and one group gets driven out and goes to preach someplace and then the original group falls apart for some reason and now the main adherents of the descended-from-loyalists religion aren't even descended from the loyalists anymore.

                                                It's a long time; stuff happens.
