            So, regarding Fantasy Eugenics and the 'heroic motivation' thing leftover from 2e:

            1) The notion that 'all men are created equal' is patently false in Creation, the simple fact that there's a whole sub-race of magical supersoldiers is just the tip of that particular iceberg.

            2) The Breeding background pretty well established that the Terrestrial Exaltation is based purely on your genetics. The original Terrestrials were 100% Exalted and it's only interbreeding that stopped it.

            3) If one were to assume that Terrestrial Exaltation does require the same greatness of personality that Celestials did, it would make sense that those traits were baked into the Terrestrial Exaltation when it was created. You don't want to make a race of easily renewable supersoldiers that have a 50:50 chance or worse of being a lazy insubordinate coward. If you didn't want to run with the assumption that ALL Dragon Blooded are stuck with a personality template it's easy enough to say that the forced personality traits are similarly genetic and NOT 100% tied to the genetic component that triggers the Exaltation.

            Personally I've always run it as a genetic crapshoot with no actual spiritual or mental prerequisites. The tendency for DBs to become notable figures of some stripe is accounted for by a combination of how they are raised, slightly better baseline stats than mortals and (especially for Lost Eggs) the fact that if you've suddenly got superpowers there's a tendency to want to use them for something even if it's just cheating at dice, pushing people around and getting laid. Barring some other option that is truly awe-inspiring, that's probably how I'll keep handling it in 3e.

                                    Originally posted by Mechalith View Post
                                    IC, if you're making a race of superpowered cannon fodder whose whole point is large numbers and easy replenishment, making their personality a criterion for whether or not they are useful would be a pretty stupid design flaw.
                                    One nice thing about 3e revising the setting set forth in 2e is that assumptions stemming from 2e do not have to be kept. Maybe in 2e the Dragon blooded were designed to be cannon fodder, but in 3e the aspects that differentiate them from the Celestial Exalted can simply be facts; just as Sol could not create Exalted with powers not stemming from his nature of excellence, so could the elemental dragons not stoke the flame of exaltation in the same way as the Incarnae. Their chosen were not created to be disposable cannon fodder, but had the noteworthy advantage of creating a lineage of power, even if lesser power than those of the greatest of Gods, which made their chosen a veritable army of heroes.

                                    In this model, the terrestrial exaltation is not a spark at a great moment, but kindling laying buried in the soul awaiting the potential for greatness that is required for exaltation, at which point it ignites in power into a raging inferno - the second breath, and a Dragon blooded is born.

                                    Edit: Hell, we don't even have to keep the same presumptions of 2e. Solar Exaltation is no longer autonomous power packets seeking out people meeting criteria, but the Unconquered Sun personally picking people. Who is to say this doesn't also hold true for lunar, sidereal, and even terrestrial exaltation?

                                    Edit 2: Additionally, sure, a Dragon blooded can fall into hedonism, just like a solar can, but I think it's crucial to their narrative that they have just as much potential and leaning towards greatness as solars, to stymie efforts to declare them "lesser exalted" or "unworthy". An individual may be unworthy at a given time in their life due to events unfolding, just like Demetheus drinking himself senseless after splattering Kidale, but that didn't make Demetheus retroactively unworthy, nor does it do so to a child of the Dragons.

                                        Originally posted by Lioness View Post
                                        I've never had a satisfactory explanation of how the game benefited from this.
                                        1. It means a perfectly normal mortal with no known history of Dragon-Blood can be Chosen as one, which facilitates useful PC and NPC concepts.
                                        2. It lends a sense of loss, and gives the Dragon-Blooded an end to fear and something to fight for. The slow loss of power as an Age comes to an end is a common theme of inspirational material in Exalted.
                                        3. It maintains the symmetry that every type of Exalt has fallen, to one degree or another.
                                        4. You need gradations of purity to facilitate the marriage brokerage aspect of Dynast culture. You can't have gradations of purity without having something defined as truly pure, and other things as impure.
                                        5. The other obvious alternative, that 100% of DB children Exalt, screws up population math something fierce. The setting is unsustainable if there are 1,000,000 Dynasts.

                                        As for the OP, I have never personally felt conflicted about the issue. Some heroes are made, and some are born. The Dragon-Blooded are born. Sometimes that power is used, sometimes it's abused, and sometimes it's just plain wasted. As a vehicle to deconstruct power, Exalted invites us to look at the consequences of the various ways it can manifest. Celestial Exalted, Solars and Lunars in particular, are often those folk heroes who have humble beginnings and become much greater. We see they often fall astray. Then we have the Dragon-Blooded, often reared from birth to wield power and govern, and they too often fail.

                                        I know that view is not popular with people who harbor more romantic notions of the Chosen of the Dragon, but to me, every type of Exalt is equally culpable of being The Problem when they claim to be The Solution.
