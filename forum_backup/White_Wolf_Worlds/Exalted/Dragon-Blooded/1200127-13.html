                                        Okay, going to see if I can’t hash out Occult before turning in.

                                        Hidden Secrets Whisper is the Excellency, and can use 10s on the dice roll to reroll non-1s. But the cascade goes very quickly into an interesting (and non-Air) effect with Soul-Fire Cremation Technique. It’s basically a slightly weaker version of one of the Zenith anima powers, allowing one to perform a funerary rite to rapidly make a corpse combust, and ensure that its lower soul won’t turn into a hungry ghost, although it doesn’t work on the bodies of Exalted. It also provides some relief to the deceased’s higher ghost, either encouraging them to accept reincarnation, or creating a magically empowered positive Minor Tie for the Exalt who used the Charm. Proper respect for the remains of the dead is something that matters a fair bit to me, so I would appreciate a Charm such as this on those grounds alone.

                                        Then there’s Seed and Salt Warding, a Charm that can be either Earth or Wood, whose effect can be pretty readily guessed after the prior one and with the name. Sure enough, it’s a line to produce a slightly more powerful ward of germinated grain or salt lines, meaning that ghosts who could normally cross such lines by spending Willpower can only do so if their Resolve is higher than what the Exalt produced from an Occult roll, as well as being able to repel things like hungry ghosts and zombies outright. I’d say that it’s good to get Charms like these, some basic ways of dealing with some of the nuisances of the spiritual world, out there first, before proceeding into the main path of the cascade.

                                        Spirit-Detecting Mirror Technique is back as the Terrestrials’ unique take on seeing the dematerialized via reflective surfaces, followed by Spirit-Grounding Shout to enhance normal attacks to strike them. There seems to be a slight error, as the text of the latter refers to the former as a prerequisite, while the actual prerequisites section lists it as having none.

                                        Secret Wind Revelation is an interesting take on responding to hidden spirits as though they’re in stealth, providing an early warning for when the dematerialized come within a certain range, and treating the being that manages to win the opposed roll to establish concealment, meaning that even magic to observe the dematerialized will have a hard time finding them.

                                        Dragon’s Sacred Talon is another Earth or Wood Charm, this time calibrated to dealing with certain kinds of extraterrestrial threat; Earth allows one to strike Wyld creatures with the stabilising power, while Wood’s cleansing improves attacks on the undead. I’m reminded of old comments about the issue with Dragon Blooded having Charms with the Holy keyword, and how it had them aping the Solar capacity to inflict aggravated damage rather than having their own. Here I see a nice response to that; basing such power in the Dragon Blooded connection to the substance of the world, and empowering them specifically against beings from outside of it. It’s a small touch, but I like what it adds to the mystical side of them.

                                        Fivefold Resonance Sense looks like it has been expanded quite a bit from prior Editions. It has a basic capacity to sense the presence and nature of the local geographic layout in terms of each element, and provides a slight non-Charm bonus to sense and follow not only jade, but elementals, Terrestrial demesnes and manses, and similar forms of magic.

                                        Spirit-Chaining Strike now takes the form of a gambit, but is otherwise still a matter of rendering a spirit visible and vulnerable to physical harm, albeit very temporarily.

                                        Occult’s Signature Charm is Hundred Devils Whirlwind, a nice little Charm to roll Occult (with double 9s) to create a magical whirlwind that applies the results of the roll against the Resolve of any spirits that come into range; if it wins, it either blows them away or sucks them close (keeping them held tight, both with preventing use of Hurry Home and acting as difficult terrain to actions to escape) as chosen by the Exalt. I must say, so far Air has gotten some rather nice instances of creating manifestations of itself to be applied to the purposes of a Dragon Blood.

                                        Sage of Iron Meditation allows one to enter a state of meditation within the Wyld that can be maintained for several days, allowing them to create a small zone of stability around themselves. It particularly has a nice interactions with the actual creatures out there, granting one a significant Hardness against their attacks, sapping their Initiative if they get close, and inflicting a bit of aggravated damage on those who crash. Considering how the meditation probably requires one to stay still and the range of the stability is fairly short, I feel as though this one primarily functions to bait the Fair Folk and similar creatures, going out into the Wyld carrying a sign looking for trouble and luring them in to break against the stable zone and hardened Exalt, probably best with some friends who’ll help cut down the entities that are offended by stability, while themselves protected from the deleterious Wyld.

                                        Smoke Ascends to Heaven acts as a means of empowering burnt offerings to spirits, and actually acts as a form of prayer Charm that people have often asked for. It specifically takes the form of either giving powerful double 7s to a persuade action made to the spirit in person, or ensuring that they’ll hear the entreaty from afar. It also treats supporting Intimacies as though they’re one step higher (meaning that somebody can undertake life-threatening tasks just on the strength of a Major Intimacy). It can only be used once per story, but is reset if major goals are accomplished through interaction with spirits (which are probably often facilitated by the Charm in the first place). So what do I think of it as a prayer Charm? It’s all right. I’m seeing it primarily as a power to communicate with spirits in a manner improved by their natural capacity to receive prayers and offerings, rather than something that empowers prayer in its own right, if that makes sense. I also think it’s helped by the limited use. I’m not sure how such a thing could be handled by Solars.

                                        Moving on to Crashing Wave-Dragons Warding, for a Charm that lets one convert running water into a kind of spiritual barrier. Ah, right, I heard somebody allude to this going all Lord of the Rings Imladris river, rather than my initial expectation that it would be a simple hard barrier. So yeah, deleterious creatures that enter the body of water are hit by an environmental hazard in the form of dragon-shaped waves, beating on them, making it harder to cross the water, and enhances withering attacks on them. It also includes a nice note of how beings with higher Essence than the Charm’s user aren’t just spending Willpower to be able to ignore the effect, but how doing so is accompanied by a display that makes them look good; it’s a nice encouragement to make the effect more dramatically interesting, rather than my own first impulse for it to just stop, or the being to no longer be impeded.

                                        And with Wood, there’s Eternal Death-Banishing Blossom. When the Exalt is at bonfire anima, a big ethereal tree grows out of it that provides improved Defense and soak (even more so against undead or necromantic stuff), and on the following turn the trees flowers bloom, aaaaand… expel a cloud of ethereal petals that, when they make contact with undead entities that fail to make a rather difficult Resistance roll, inflict a rather significant load of aggravated damage. Even the Abyssal Exalted and Deathlords can take some nasty damage. So yeah, this is another one that includes some rather striking visuals, and constitutes a uniquely Dragon Blooded take on inflicting aggravated damage.

                                        Speaking of, next is Blazing Purification Chant is a method for dealing with entities that have possessed somebody; putting a prayer strip on the victim’s head that bursts into flame and burns the possessor for aggravated damage. It can force an entity out even short of killing them, although if they survive and hang on, then they get a relief period of a day. It’s also another example of a Charm that the Exalted can use cooperatively, although unlike Dragon Vortex, everybody needs to know the Charm, and the participants only provide extra dice to the damage roll.

                                        Spirit-Shredding Attack is back in modified form, enhancing a decisive attack against a crashed spirit by letting threshold hit successes improve the damage roll, and while it doesn’t permanently destroy a spirit killed by it, it does ensure that they’ll come back sans a dot of Essence, possibly with fewer Charms. I’ll be interested in if the Terrestrials do get a Charm to permanently destroy them (at least outside of martial arts); if not, the modification that this represents in who can and cannot permanently destroy a spirit carries interesting connotations for the setting’s history, and the social dynamics between various beings.

                                        Seal of Heavenly Binding is straightforward as a Charm to create a binding circle around a spirit (that they have a potential to break through), while Chaos-Banishing Revelation Gesture (Earth) allows one to free others from Psyche and Derangement effects imposed by powers of the Wyld.

                                        Purifying Dragon Suspiration is the Essence 4 Charm here, an Air/Water one to harness Creation’s purifying Essence to free somebody from the power of curses (such as the spell Corrupted Words) or baleful transformations, as well as weakening magically created Derangements, although it’s still not capable of ending the effects of sorcerous workings. It can take a lot of time and energy to accomplish, but the Exalt is given a fairly lengthy amount of time in which to accomplish it. This here is the one return that I’ve seen of a power that does simply place some enemies beyond its reach; however, it reserves that for the very top of the scale, your Solar Circle Sorcery and your cosmic entities, so I’d say that, considering everything else that they have to deal with, that’s a fair cop on the part of Terrestrials.

                                        Last is the ominously named Anathema-Sealing Tomb provided by Earth and Essence 5. Despite the name, this is not actually targeted at other Exalted, but spirits; when they’re incapacitated, one has the option of sealing them within significant local instance of Earth, such as caverns, noteworthy stone monuments, or beneath mountains, preventing them from reforming for centuries or at all (but still short of properly killing them) if they’re weak enough (although all such spirits can be freed if their prisons are broken). So while Dragon Blooded don’t quite have power to permanently kill spirits, the more powerful ones have something that is close enough for most purposes, while still retaining plot hooks of pissed off spirits being able to wait around practically forever, as well as referencing cool mythological or folkloric imagery of beings sealed in sacred places. It even includes a hanging question of what might happen if it was used on the likes of Deathlords and Yozis.

                                        As a special bonus, there are also some sorcery Charms off in their own space. Obviously there’s the basic Charm to initiate into sorcery, the only one that these Exalted can learn, but there are also a few to supplement them. Five Winds Raiment acts as something like a milder version of the Twilight anima, shielding user a bit while they’re engaged in shaping of a spell, and Dragon-Sorcerer Puissance provides some additional sorcerous motes when one is shaping a spell with a distinct elemental character (with even more if they’re in a matching Aura while doing so). They’re not too much, and seem like some decent rounding Charms; one to prevent people smashing up an Exalt busy casting, one to lend at least a little something to the idea of casting elemental spells.

                                        So Occult came out well, some very vivid effects in there and a nice sense of how the Terrestrial Exalted relate to entities that are from outside of the Terrestrial plane.

                                        I have approximate knowledge of many things.

                                                            Now, to see what they can do with Performance. Like with the Solars, it’s divided into paths of application.

                                                            The Universal Charms start with the Excellency, Audience-Enthusing Display, letting one add successes and eliminating the penalty for targeting several people with social influence. That’s followed by Hidden Petal Aria, which allows one to hide a short, but potentially influential, message within a performance, that is conveyed to a select individual and presents a difficulty of the user’s Manipulation or Performance to magical attempts to pick up on it.

                                                            Invisible Street Performer Technique is back as a curious method of going unnoticed, allowing one to shift the attention of onlookers away from themselves and onto their performance, to the extent that they can engage in other, non-violent actions clandestinely by flurrying them with the performance. Dance of Flashing Swords covers the violent side, allowing one to incorporate their actions into a performance and convince onlookers that it’s all just part of the show.

                                                            Talented Improvisation feels like an interesting take on Wood’s capacity to connect to others, in the form of being able to intuit the whole of a song from a brief snippet, as well as growing a misstep into an elaborate dance, which are mechanically represented by being able to reroll some failed dice on a roll, as well as lowering the Charm’s cost if they make a stunt of their fumble.
                                                            Using Soul-Stirring Performance, an Exalt can make inspire rolls with non-Charm bonus dice, with the result requiring a Decision Point to not be moved by. Then there’s Heart-Strengthening Defense, allowing one to make a performance that will strengthen the Resolve of those who witness it, especially against Psyche effects, being able to flurry that performance with other actions since the boost evidently only lasts while the performance does. Similar is Harmonious Life-Affirming Song, providing a performance that both improves resistance to poison and disease, and increasing the rate at which they can recover from injury; I like to imagine how this can synergize with Medicine, using Charms to downgrade somebody’s wounds to bashing damage, and then putting on a show for them to speed them along to health.

                                                            Then there’s a Water Charm, Irresistible Whirlpool Diversion, allowing one to forcefully draw people’s attention to their performance. It’s less absolute than a similar Solar effect, taking the form of exerting a penalty on Awareness actions to look at anything besides the performing Dragon Blood. Like several others, it lets one flurry the distracting performance with other actions.
                                                            Next is the Signature Charm Thundering Dragon Proclamation, magnifying one’s voice to a boom that can be clearly heard from a great distance. It only functions for inspire or threaten actions, but ignores penalties based on distance and environment, doubles 9s on voice-based Performance and military command, and while it’s scene-long, the user can selectively return their voice to normal volume, which is necessary to engage in other forms of social influence at all.

                                                            Tears-From-Stone Eloquence allows one to make inspire rolls that help align people to traditional values, although it’s the player of the one who is hit by the inspire action who decides what those values are and how they’ll act in favour to them. I like that touch, both for making it something more nuanced than just imposing values on people, and for the image of Earth Aspects who might place a greater priority on people believing in and living up to something, rather than what the Exalt specifically would have of them.

                                                            Immolating Passion Alleluia (interesting spelling) instead makes an inspire actions that can roll some non-Charm dice, creating very intense emotions that prompt characters to engage in actions equivalent to what could come from a Major Intimacy.

                                                            Mesmerizing Siren Call looks much more elaborate than the preceding ones, being a Psyche Charm and another with variable effects depending on rolled successes. The character gets to make a Performance roll, with rerolled failed dice, hypnotizing onlookers whose Resolve is overcome, and spending threshold successes on conjuring particular illusions, even being able to impose different illusions on different people in the same group. The base effect shows people phantasmal sensory stimuli that they’ll know are unreal, one success creates a single illusory being of about human size that can’t imitate a real person, two successes overcome that limitation for a character known to the Charm’s user, and three lets one weave an image the size of a house, including objects and structures, which can also imitate specific people. Because the illusions are dependant on maintaining the performance, it can also be flurried with other actions, and the user can will the illusions to act however they want; while they can’t interact physically, they can engage in social influence (although unable to benefit from any of the Exalt’s other magic). The targets get the option to spend Willpower to break the illusion if they see something that shows it up as unreal, or if the illusion might lead to violating a Major or Defining Intimacy. It can only be used once per story, but is reset by accomplishing major goals through deception. So yeah, chalk another one up to the evils of Water.

                                                            Last is Life-Spirit Symphony, allowing one to cause local vegetation to align with the Dragon Blood’s performance, providing the benefit of allowing oneself and one’s allies to ignore difficult terrain as a result of such flora as it parts to let them through, as well as providing a benefit to resisting threats from plants such as poison or environmental hazards. On top of that, it can give the option to direct the plants at enemies in the form of a gambit to entangle them, which can remain to impede their movement even if they’re hacked through.

                                                            Last of the universal Charms is Blossom Hides Thorns, allowing one to hide an unexpected attack or ambush within a performance, either for the Exalt or an ally.

                                                            Aaaand oh Jesus Performance is long. There’s another five pages of this? I’m going to try my best to bomb through.

                                                            Okay, oratory. Air Charm Lightning Declamation Style lets one reroll certain failures on oratory, Earth Charm Legend-Hewn Wisdom is used to inspire emotion and instil Intimacy based on the moral of a spoken story (with an upgrade to let them give Willpower to those who don’t resist the influence, that can be spent towards staying the course), and Storm-Gathering Fervor grants non-Charm dice when trying to influence somebody with a shared Intimacy.

                                                            Epoch Saga Memory gives one a perfect recall of stories that can benefit their recitation or the mental and social use of an Ability that might benefit from knowing a story, plus strengthening Resolve with its moral or Guile with its distraction. Puissant Precursor’s Monologue lets one make a persuade roll with double 7s for actions going along with the user’s Defining Principles, making it harder to resist for those who share the Intimacy.

                                                            For music and singing Charms, there’s Sanxian-Charming Fingers to give double 9s for playing an instrument, while Voice-Uplifting Aspect lets one reroll singing failures for every rolled 10. Blossoming Instrument Evocation lets one draw instruments out of the local flora. Vibrating Strings Defense is still a defensive Charm harnessing music, but now it’s a clash using a gambit that, if it wins, knocks the attacker down and makes them highly vulnerable to Performance influence if they crash. Then there’s Three-String Sword Prana to let one wield their music as an attack, with double 10s on the damage roll. Last is Battle Anthem of Ten Thousand to provide fighting bonuses to people that hear the Exalt’s music.

                                                            Okay, almost done. Dancing Charms. Swaying Boughs Arabesque lets one dance to lower the Resolve of targets, which can increase the Appearance benefit. Petal-Strewn Pavane lets one add Performance to combat movement rolls and flurry movement with Performance actions, with Wood Aura providing a non-Charm success and removing difficult terrain penalties. Falling Leaves Sway lets one use Appearance to provide non-Charm dice to dance-based inspire actions, which grants an auto success to an influence based on the inspired emotion. Last of all is Waltz of Honeyed Wine, to convert Appearance based non-Charm dice into non-Charm successes and makes it harder for people with Resolve lower than Appearance to resist influence.

                                                            Huh, no sexual Charms? Well, maybe later.

                                                            Performance looks good, although I’ll probably want to give it a bigger look later on.
