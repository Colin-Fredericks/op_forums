                    What about a non-trusting bargain sort of thing? Genre films and books have plenty of plots where characters that have no reason to trust each other are forced to work together to achieve a goal.

                    Have him propose they help him find the Manse, because clearly they would want what's in it, too. He still thinks they are dangerous, and they know he's a Dragon-Blood, and would gladly call a Wyld Hunt on them.

                    Over the next several sessions, it's up to the players to determine how to handle this. Maybe with careful social influence and trust in a clutch situation they can gain his true loyalty. if they are too harsh on their partner-prisoner, he may sabotage their plans at a crucial moment. Or maybe he sacrifices the Manse in an attempt to bury them at the end.

                    I suggest this primarily because it gives the players the opportunity for extended roleplay and real decision making. Do they accept or reject his offer? Do they try to befriend him, or bully him? He can always escape later, creating a long-term antagonist.

                                                            Originally posted by ajf115 View Post

                                                            Well, he's not all that dogmatic with regards to the whole Solars/Anathema thing, although what he is is a sorcerer/historian who is devoted to the First Age. He's not dogmatic, but he has seen evidence of some of the atrocities which the ancient Solars committed, so he's not enormously well-disposed towards them.

                                                            He's of House Iselsi, and hopes to find something in the West that could help restore his House's position somewhat in the coming storm. He's onto something too, as he managed to uncover a repository of documents from the First Age, which were previously kept in a magically-sealed box passed around as a curiosity in the West. Most important for him was a number of letters between an unknown Solar and Lunar referencing a First Age Manse which doesn't match up to any that the Dynasts already know of. He's been working on divining the Manse's position based on the dragon lines in the general area, and has been doing reasonably well, although he hasn't got it exactly. He was on his way back to the Isle to show what he'd found in person before he was *ahem* intercepted.
                                                            Someone like that sounds like he'd go for something along the lines of... be polite to them, act like he's on their side (or has been convinced by them), cut deals with them if he can do so safely and help his own interests or family in the process, then (when it's safe to do so) turn around and stab them in the back for the good at the world.

                                                            Obviously his ability to pull this off depends on his social skills and the skills of anyone in the group, but he has the advantage that he can be mostly honest (eg. he genuinely believes they're not demons and are capable of good, he genuinely wants to work with them, etc etc etc - depending on how he words it and what his intimacies are, it could be tricky for them to realize that he has an underlying belief that they're dangerous for the world and ultimately need to be stopped, since he could also genuinely think they're decent people and be willing to work with them on a short-term basis.)

                                                            He would want to play up the idea that having a DB supporter to negotiate on their behalf could be useful, etc.
