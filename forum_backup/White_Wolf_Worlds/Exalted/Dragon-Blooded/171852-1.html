                                                    Here's the thing on dragons in 2e. That was more or less something becoming canon and defining something which had been previously left open. And actually contradicted some of what had been there before.

                                                    In Games of Divinity, the Five Elemental Dragons were gods. And they made Dragon-Blooded the way they did not becuase of some connection to Gaia, but because they were simply weaker than the Incarna and Terrestrial Exaltation manifested the way it did as a consequence of their nature. Other beings in 3e will do this it looks like, such as the case with Exigents and other new Exalted.

                                                    The 5ED being Gaia's souls caused actual weirdness as well. As noted already, it made Gaia a huge part of the Dragon-Blooded Exaltation more than was probably intended. It more or less caused a weird retcon as to what dragons were in 2e. Until indications of Gnosis havining more, or the Twenty Hidden Souls of Gaia in Dreams, we also had this bit of the general habit of fans and authors to jump to the idea that Talked About = All that there is, making Gaia violate rules given on other Primordials (five souls innstead of at least twelve), and wasn't consistet anyhow (five souls being dragons instaed of the wide vareity of other Primordial souls, no indication of Second or First Circle Deva). It also made Gaia the important part of the Dragon-Blooded lore, rather than the indication I got out of 1e that the "turning of the seasons" and motherfucking dragons were supposed to be their mainn source of metaphysical weight.

                                                    It more or less does to the Five Elemental Dragons like the Yozis did to their Third Circle Demons: Made them a footnote in the "important" entity. It made them in effect "mere" deva working at her whim, rather than gods with their own motivations and lore to draw on. It closed doors instead of opened them.

                                                    It's also blatent retcon in the end. It wasn't the case before for that being their nature beyond fanon. And the nature of lesser and greater dragons as a result is even moreso as it outright contradicts what was before. It is just as much to decouple dragons from the elementals and Gaia again, sure. But that "This is how it is now" isn't a justification, and "It makes the setting more concise and consistent on what beings are" isn't viewed as a plus by the writers.

                                                        Originally posted by marin View Post
                                                        As such, they're not going to Exalt as the Incarnae do. They're going to do it in a way that resonates with their nature, a way that speaks of heritage and the elements.

                                                        I mean, that justification was off the top of my head. The 5EDs' method of Exaltation doesn't have to have anything to do with Gaia's nature, 'cause it's about, you know, the 5EDs.
                                                        Agreed. For the same reason, any other entity creating Exalted -- including a god granted access to the Divine Exigency -- may find the method of Exaltation little resembles that of the Incarnae, or even any other known entity, but that method will resonate with the entity's nature.

                                                        Originally posted by Elfive View Post
                                                        Also I thoroughly subscribe to the "understanding how stuff works just makes it cooler" school of thought. A mystery is only interesting if it has an answer to find.
                                                        A mystery the game introduces can have an answer whether the game nails down what it is or not. The game providing an answer to a mystery gives one answer (and, well, also no mystery), the game providing no answer frees many tables to reach many answers. A complaint many have of 2e is that it answered too many of these, made the game feel too solved, and essentially allowed everything to be compartmentalized as you've been doing. I know I can find fun with this, too, but EX3 looks like it's hewing more toward not overexplaining everything, and I'm looking forward to that. For instance, I don't think EX3 will reveal -- whether overtly or in clues -- that the Scarlet Empress is in Malfeas. It's more likely to say nothing at all of what became of her.

                                                        What I expect to happen is that the game will say the Five Elemental Dragons are Gaea's children, and then not say much else of their origins. It will instead spend its wordcount on things about them more relevant to their place in the world and how their natures reflect their Chosen. The game will then go onto other topics.

                                                        If a table is very attached to the idea that the Dragons are Gaea's souls, their table can go nuts with that; the relatively sparse description the Dragons are likely to receive will mean that interpretation causes very little (if anything) to break. Honestly, it was canon for 2e, so lots of tables are just going to default to it. Tables who want to do something else should feel as free as possible to do so. These groups will already be fighting against the inertia of a previous edition in which the Dragons' nature was set; there's really nothing to gain in adding to that.
