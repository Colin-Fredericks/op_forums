    Originally posted by While My Guitar View Post
    The fact that you can mix-and-match to make your own and the fact that it's abilities basically replace the ones of your regular charm set.
    Eh, point buy is a fun mechanical system and customization can help bridge mechanics and character, but such things aren't the exclusive province of Infernals. Alchemical modules are a similar type of type, as is the Lunar's DBT. The fact that it's capabilities replace your normal is a downside of the charm, and not one exclusive to Infernal Shintai (not one necessarily possessed by all shintai but that may change in Ex3), a Lunar who has taken the form of, say, a swarm of bugs probably won't have access to their normal charms.

    More to the point, those are just mechanical quirks of the charms used to give them unique quirks, any capabilities granted by said charms could wrtten and rebalanced removing the transformation components and just functioning like regular charms. Further it is unlikely that shintai will feature capabilities and themes completely absent in the charmset outside them, infact the nature of the shintai seems to be modifiable based on what charms you know. It seems that they won't be the charms that grant you new abilities, but rather charms that make said abilities more powerful or more efficient. Any improvements granted by Shinta, theoretically, could be granted by charms, it's just thought that shintai would be a cool/interest way to do so, there is no mechanical reason they MUST exist.

        As for Terrestrials vs Infernals, personally, I feel like Tiresias is actually overthinking it a bit. I think it really does come down to Dragon Blooded not having transformation Charms for much the same reason Solars don't (and to the extent that they have Charms to modify their body, it's as their particular motif for conveying the function of the Charm).

        In Second Edition, I imagine that Infernals could transform because it was a neat, resonant thing for them to be able to do, interacted somewhat with the fact that their Charms didn't key directly off of other stats, and helped make them distinct. I really imagine that Third Edition Infernals will still have shintais primarily because they were popular in Second Edition, and provide a convenient inroad to making them even more distinct (especially since they'll have Ability Charms now and they need to preempt the whole "Green Solars" thing) and also because people really like the whole transhumanism and gradual transformation thing Second Edition Infernals had and this is a good way to give that some meaning and substance while also cutting away some of the flotsam of the Second Edition version and overall making it less obtrusive.

        Really, saying Dragon Blooded should have shintais because Infernals do seems to be kind of missing the point of Infernals having shintais in the first place.

        Personally, I'm rather up on the idea of the Dragon Blooded actually having the least implementation of elaborate distinguishing tricks than other Exalted, if only because it feels more natural to them being defined as the lower scale of Exaltation (and no this is not a way of saying "being the weakest Exalted means they shouldn't have anything cool", the actual adornments of their setting place notwithstanding).

                            Really, before we even think about whether or not Dragon-Blooded should get shintai, we need to talk about why Infernals do. In Ex2, Infernals get shintai because it's part of their transformation into the Yozis, but that's not relevant in Ex3, so it has to be something else.

                            From a mechanical perspective, it's probably because transforming into a cosmic titan requires a level of flexibility and specificity that can't be written into charms, but that just explains why shintai are their own separate subsystem, not why they exist.

                            My best guess is that the Yozis are so far beyond humans, even Exalted humans, that their nature cannot be emulated through charms, or that Infernal Exaltation is so broken that Infernals cannot effectively emulate their patrons the way Solars and Abyssals can, and needs the shintai to make up the difference.

                            The answer probably lies somewhere in Sidereal Astrology. It's the only non-Charm, Exalted-specific subsystem we have to work with right now. Well, that and Lunar shapeshifting, but that's Lunar shapeshifting.

                                Originally posted by Tiresias View Post
                                The answer probably lies somewhere in Sidereal Astrology. It's the only non-Charm, Exalted-specific subsystem we have to work with right now. Well, that and Lunar shapeshifting, but that's Lunar shapeshifting.

                                These sub-systems seem like they go to the splats that they do because they reflect, say things about, and flow from the themes of those splats. Lunars get shapeshifting because they're primal beast-demigods and Sidereals get Sidereal Astrology because it's a subtle tool for subtle planners. Guessing here based on dev talk, Green Sun Princes get shintai because transforming into an otherworldly shape conveys the right tone of throwing around immense displays of casual power.

                                Though, on second thought, I can understand wanting to leave out Lunar shapeshifting: if it's supposed to flow from Lunar themes, and nobody outside the dev team feels like they have a perfectly solid grasp of what those themes are, explaining why it fits them becomes trickier. Even if we know the implementation of Sidereal Astrology will be different from what we've seen, we at least have a pretty good handle on their themes.

                                            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                            Dukantha is like that because he's been invested with Infernal power; he's about as much an indication of the inherent capacity of Terrestrials for evolution or transcendence as Thrice-Damned Gorol is.
                                            Yeah, you could take that angle and that's a totally legalistically fine way to argue based on my statement.

                                            I just remember reading Blood and Salt, when I read that they introduced the signature akuma as a Dragonblooded, felt like it tapped into that Dragonblooded transcendence theme. That theme of them stepping up and going beyond their divinely ordained potential as the least of the Exalted.

                                            I absolutely would not argue that the Dragonblooded have a theme of having inherent potential for transcendence inside them - the point of the numina, and Infernalism, and even Immaculate Grand Masters is of Exalted going to sources of power that are beyond their innate capability.

                                            But I think there's quite a few examples of Dragonblooded seeking the path of transcendence because a) shaking up the neat little hierarchy of Exalted power is fun and b) they actually have somewhere to go in terms of power through transcendence (whereas we never could that answer K'tula with a satisfactory answer other than "I just wanna be a big Kimbery").

                                            It's right it doesn't come through too much though, and I think it will less in 3e because there just isn't so much of a neat little, pat, settled hierarchy of tiers of every sort of Exalt there can be, and power seems like it'll get back to presentations in a way that plays more to Dragonblooded strengths (not cosmic gonzo stuff where everyone who is worth talking about is making the First Age again right now, where DBs don't fit so much).
