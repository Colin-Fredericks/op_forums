            I’m planning to do these by splitting every ability tree in two: in part 1, we’re going to talk about the Charms that are available straight out of chargen, whereas in part 2, we’re going to discuss the rest.


            Archery


            Unobstructed Hunter’s Aim: at first, I did not like this Charm. I thought the excellency had a cut-rate version of Sight Without Eyes tacked on, which felt… inappropriate.

            Sight Without Eyes, to me, is one of those Charms that make an incredibly strong thematic statement about who Solars are and how their power manifests: it’s a 1 mote Charm with a duration of One tick that eliminates any and all visual penalties on the attack roll. Contrast that with their other penalty negator, which costs 3m, has a duration of Instant, and adds some extra effects (which are undeniably powerful) – it is E2 Archery 5, as opposed to SWE’s E1 Archery 3. To conquer other adverse conditions does not come as naturally to Solars as negating visual penalties does: SWE reflects the nature of Solar Essence as something that illuminates and cuts through obfuscation with contemptuous ease (used in conjunction with Trance of Unhesitating Speed, it might add up to 15 extra dice split across five attacks for a single mote!). Giving a weaker version of this to Dragon-Blooded as a built-in part of their excellency felt like the writer was missing the point, drawing the conclusion that SWE merely reflected the genre convention that archers are keen-eyed folks, and therefore it should be a natural, basic effect that flows from the logic of the Archery ability instead of something that reflects on the unique nature of Solars themselves.

            However, in the end, I find that I’m okay with the rider. It’s very specific (only eliminating a point of penalty when shooting at living or undead targets) and reflects the more abstract and esoteric side of the element of wood, its interpretation as life force. It also helps that this is the only Charm in Archery for negating visual penalties – if somebody wants to see through darkness and obstruction, they need to invest in Awareness, which is actually where ability logic should point us towards.


            Sky-Calming Draw: what a strange Charm. Used with the ability’s namesake weapon, it doesn’t actually do all that much: spend 4 motes and a turn aiming to negate penalties from wind and weather – this is slightly worse value than the excellency, given that even the most horrible weather conditions tend to max out at a -3 penalty, which means you’re adding 3d for 4m. A fairly situational effect for those times when every die counts. However, where this Charm really shines is allowing firewand users to shoot out to Medium range. On a withering attack, it compares favorably to Righteous Devil Style’s Blossom of Inevitable Demise: you get to extend your range out to Medium for 3m, and then ignore all weather penalties for a surcharge of 1m. I would not have expected an Air Aspected Charm to be all-around better with a firewand than it is with a bow, but here we are.


            Death From Nowhere: unless you’re playing a secondary school game, this is going to be 3m for +2 damage straight out of chargen, which is… not amazing (but also not quite as terrible as 3m for a situational +1 damage, which you get with it at Essence 1). However, assuming you started out with a decent Perception, it will steadily raise in value as you gain Essence, eventually ending up with 3m for +4-5 damage, which is pretty good value, even taking its limitations into account. Another useful feature is that it’s Air/Water aspected, serving as a cheap way to set up an appropriate Aura state for powerful high-Essence tricks like Horizon-Spanning Arc and Arrow Rain Tempest.


            Harvest of the Hunter: honestly, I’ve never seen anyone use ammunition checks, so this Charm is pretty much useless to me. Luckily, none of the powerful capstone effects have this as a prerequisite, albeit a lot of pretty good early Charms do. Such as…


            Arrow Thorn Technique: only at higher Essence does this Charm get really powerful, but when it does, the effects are spectacular. +4-5 dice of pretty much guaranteed decisive damage on an ambush? Dealing 8-10 minimum damage on a withering attack, no matter how much of a soak monster you’re facing? For a measly 3 motes? Yes please.


            Boughs of Burning Autumn: this compares favorably to Cloud of Ebon Devils, allowing a reflexive reload with no restrictions on the next action and no risk of failure for a 1i surcharge. It also adds some flexibility, allowing for the reloading of any Slow weapon, not just firewands, and an opportunity to build either Fire (setting up Blazing Phoenix Pinion or a powerful Dodge suite) or Wood (for the majority of high-Essence Archery tricks) Aura.


            Life-Swelling Sap Strike: spend 3m 1WP to force a -1 Evasion penalty on your opponent and continuously sap his Initiative at a 1/round rate (on average, equivalent to snake venom) until he or one of his allies is willing to spend an action and 4 initiative on a Gambit to cut it off. Good enough, but even better if you have a brawler buddy nearby, who can unleash a reflexive decisive to take advantage of the Evasion penalty and the extra initiative granted by the Distract. May apply to regular decisives in Wood Aura. The ability to insta-kill undead is probably never going to come up, though (this is a decisive-only effect, so the only way for this to Crash an opponent is via the torturously slow 1/round initiative sap, which takes 3 rounds to put someone into the dirt even from base – better hope they don’t manage to land a successful withering in that time [j/k they totally will lol]). (I have no idea why incredibly fiddly and situational overengineered extra effects are so popular on Dragon-Blooded Charms – the fan version was full of them, too.)


            Arbor Sentinel Technique: 4m 1WP for a scenelong +2 non-Charm Defense bonus wherever there is ground? And you can’t run out of arrows? This is really good. The fact that you’re effectively rooted in place, less so – but guess what, the charmset has solutions that help you mitigate this problem (Unmoving Center Enlightenment jumps to mind – or you could just be an Earth Aspect, flare your anima, and knock enemies away from you without having to do anything, albeit you’ll need ways to reduce your anima if you want to use high-level capstone effects). (Not to mention that you can hand over your movement actions to a buddy who has Become the Wave without feeling the slightest bit of remorse.) The only way for this Charm to be even better would be having a way to generate difficult terrain, but a custom Archery Charm or Evocations can easily cover that.


            Drawing Lightning Style: 4m for +3 decisive damage is a very good deal – it’s better than White Reaper’s damage adder, for comparison -, but where this Charm really shines is combining the repurchase with Arrow Thorn Technique, potentially conjuring up 6+ dice of damage for 7 motes. That’s a painful hit even from base initiative, and best of all, even though you expel your Aura in the process of making the attack, it springs right back up at the end of your turn (since you’re only using a mix of Balanced and Air aspected Charms to enhance your shot).


            Spring Follows Winter: this is a really powerful effect. For 5m 2i, you get to increase your chances of hitting a given Defense by 10-25%, which is roughly the equivalent of +3-4 non-Charm dice (depending on the exact Difficulty). Of course, the downside is that you have to pay twice for everything that enhances your attack, so this is less about making your ultimate doomcombo unavoidable, and more about making sure that a shot for which you’ve built up Initiative throughout the entire combat will actually land. (Albeit I guess it can help you make the most out of Arrow Thorn Technique, but paying a 3m surcharge for roughly +0.6 level of damage is not necessarily going to be worth it.)
