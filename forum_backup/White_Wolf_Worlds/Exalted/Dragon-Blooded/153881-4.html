                                Originally posted by MrInsecure View Post
                                Well, in terms of non-Realm DBs, they basically split into two groups.
                                Nooooooooo. oo. Nonononono. A thousand times no.

                                First, your description is, I think, slightly too Realm-centric -- the Realm is absolutely the most powerful nation in the world, but it's like, say, America, or Rome; powers exist outside of it, even ones so far away that it's almost a distant story (if a very big story.) There are groups of Dragon-Blooded even in those distant realms. The Realm defines all outcastes according to a doctrine that says that it is the sole representative of all DBs everywhere, but even with its vast power it isn't remotely close to being able to actually enforce that doctrine creationwide. What you describe is definitely the case on the Blessed Isle itself, and to some extent or another it's felt throughout the Threshold, as well as several other places where the Realm can project direct and immediate power; but that doesn't cover all of Creation by a long shot, and there are still Dragon-Blooded everywhere else, too.

                                I think the books have also sometimes oversold the ability of the Realm to attract Lost Eggs, and the extent to which the Realm is capable of actually defining the experience of being an Outcaste outside its borders -- yes, some DBs will take them up on it; if you Exalt as a Lost Egg in an area controlled by the Realm, you'll feel a lot of pressure. But it's a mistake to assume that the Realm simply wants to attract as many DBs as possible. A small number of people with no family trickling in is good; a huge migration of established DB families with their own resources and connections is bad, because it threatens the Realm's current order. Which isn't to say that they overtly discourage anyone -- official Realm policy is that it stands for all DBs everywhere -- but when you really look at it, the coin and the razor is a pretty ugly (in some cases, almost insulting) choice to offer a Prince of the Earth, isn't it? This is not an accident. They want the DBs who are in a position to be forced into that choice (in other words, the ones who can be used).

                                (It is important to realize, basically, that most DBs outside the Realm and certain parts of the Threshold aren't going to think of themselves as "Lost Eggs" or even "outcastes" at all; they'll just think of themselves as Dragon-Blooded.)

                                But if you're slightly overestimating the impact of the Realm, you're vastly overestimating Lookshy. Lookshy is perhaps the largest concentration of DBs outside the Realm, but I seriously doubt it was originally intended to hold that honor by a particularly overwhelming margin. If the entire Blessed Isle were to drop into a bottomless pit tomorrow, Lookshy would be just one of countless squabbling groups of DBs trying to step into the void.

                                I think that part of the problem was that Lookshy was written up as an example of how DB societies exist outside the auspices of the Realm; and then proceeded to be used as the go-to "other DBs" and "alternate axis of power" example forever, until Creation shrunk to the point where you had the Realm and Lookshy sticking up out of a cartoonishly tiny world lobbing Soul-Breaker Orbs at each other, and nothing else of note -- like this, but with a not-round world.

                                Another part of the problem is that the outcaste book dedicated a bunch of text to Lookshy, and then a bunch of text (as the other major example of a DB organization outside the Realm) to... the Forest Witches. Who are totally cool and all, but were so weird that nobody really knew what to do with them.

                                Anyway, as a general rule, the key things to remember about DBs are, almost uniquely among Creation's Exalted, that they have had time to produce generations worth of established, existing legitimacy almost anywhere, and are able to pass their power down through blood to maintain direction and political power across those generations. That means that it is very easy to see a DB family, or some consortiums of DB families, rising to prominence all over the world, especially in areas where they face less direct opposition from rival clans or other Exalted. I don't think that every DB is guaranteed to produce DB heirs, but still -- all it takes is one DB with a bit of luck to establish a small, local dynasty, so I expect it's happened all over the place. These local dynasties are a lot more transient than the Realm (all it would take is a bit of bad luck for a generation to wipe your typical one out, say, and many could be extinguished in short order by a dedicated, skilled, and lucky assassin), but they're still capable of significantly shaping many parts of the setting.

                                (I think another part of the reason why these have tended to be downplayed is because the Realm itself likely downplays them. This vision of "you are either a member of the Realm or you're some random nobody who Exalted to mortal parents" very much suits the Realm's leading families; but I doubt it actually reflects reality, especially in places that are comparatively far outside of the Realm's reach.)
                                Last edited by Aquillion; 06-08-2014, 08:07 AM.
