            Originally posted by semicasual View Post
            I suppose I'm sore about it mostly because the last two DBs (Air and Wood Aspects) I tried to make were both heavily invested in Sorcery because there was no other way I could enable them to manipulate wind and plants, respectively. Playing with the elements wasn't all they did - the Air Aspect was a bookish academic, a Dynast scholar who had basically been pushed into a Wyld Hunt against his better judgement, while the Wood Aspect was an Outcaste one-woman militia, trying to gain recognition by putting her self-taught fighting skills to work hunting monsters. Both of them had skillsets that made sense for their backgrounds. The problem was that during the character conception phase, both of them had a seed of "Elemental Superhero" that didn't really get to sprout. The Air Aspect couldn't create wind blasts when he was startled (which happened a lot - Valor 1), and the Wood Aspect couldn't wrap enemies in vines unless they spent a lot of BP on extraciricular stuff. It felt like an arbitrary restriction that made the characters mechanically weaker and less focused than they could have been.
            So do the explanations on what the design paradigm actually is help any?

            I have approximate knowledge of many things.

                                                    Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
                                                    Mote surcharges were indeed a bad idea.
                                                    Agreed. I'm not sure different mote costs for in-/out-of-Aspect Charms is a good idea at all, but this was not the way to go about it. Framing matters, and the presentation on it seemed doomed from the word "go." If they were set on going for it, it would have probably worked out better just to make every Charm 1 mote* higher and say that every in-Aspect Charm gets a mote discount. The numbers functionally work out the same, but this presentation would put more emphasis on, "These are my greatest strengths," whereas the present set-up has more emphasis on, "Everything else is my huge weakness."

                                                    * I'm none too familiar with Dragon-Blooded mechanics. However many motes their surcharge is, anyway.
