    Hehe, all of this talk on various models of rulership had me thinking about a scenario in which something like democracy could exist.

    It hinges around the existence of Havesh.

    Nasty, grindy, mercenary Havesh.

    Havesh who, at the end of his story in the Caste book, is being forced to flee into the wilderness.

    Can you imagine Havesh ruling things? I can't. That's not even a question of him being technically capable of eventually developing the skills to do so; I just can't imagine him being bothered. Can't imagine him tolerating the hassle of it. Havesh doesn't want to run things, he just wants a life of ease and privilege. Preferably somebody else's life of ease and privilege, but that can't last.

    He also has a certain sympathy for the underprivileged social stratum from which he originates. He outright despises the upper class of his homeland, and there's probably no love lost between him and the old Dragon Blooded hegemony. And as I recall, Havesh doesn't really like the idea of associating with other Solars, for a few reasons.

    So what is Havesh to do to get himself back from hiding in the wilderness to living the good life?

    I had this notion in which he spends the next several decades striking from the shadows to redistribute assets in his society a bit. Possibly something he came up with by himself, more likely something encouraged by some rebel or rabble rouser. He doesn't care for Varang's ancien regime, so he uses the subtle methods of the Night Caste to carefully dismantle it, and create the grounds for a new system. A more pluralistic system. A system in which those who were once lowest have some increased prospects.

    He doesn't do this out of rightenousness; he wants other people who will manage the tedium of running things, of keeping up the systems that ensure that there are nice things for him to buy as he retires to his gilded palace. People that will not infrequently have need for his services, and pay top dirham for them. Something that reconciles the better and worse parts of his nature. He resides within society, and is a major aspect in keeping it going, but he's not in charge, and is unconcerned with a lot of the specifics of what's going on.

    He's mercenary, but also somewhat pragmatic. It's probably hard to find something valuable enough to get him to actually betray his new Varang, not so much due to a sense of honour or national loyalty (although he might have a smidgeon of the latter) as a sense that whatever he would be paid to do amounts to shitting where he eats.

    I have approximate knowledge of many things.
    Write up as I play Xenoblade Chronicles.

            Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
            Being good isn't the only qualification for being good at running a country. Administrative skill, charisma, the ability to resist bad influences, these are all important.
            I think there's an important distinction to be made here between two different concepts, and using "good" for both of them is clouding the issue. You can be competent at running an organization/nation/empire/whatever - your orders are clear and easily understood, they make sense and use your resources to best advantage, you convince your followers to obey them happily, and so forth - without your goals in running things being morally correct. All the Exalted have the ability to achieve their goals with supernatural effectiveness. What none of them have is the supernatural ability to choose the right goals. Exalted has never put that into the hands of the characters, leaving it up to players and the ST to choose what's morally right.

            Now, I think it's true that, in the real world, there is a correlation between regimes that are morally worse and those run less effectively. If nothing else, empathy is a very useful tool for getting the best performance out of people, and morally corrupt regimes usually take actions that mean people involved have to suppress their own empathy to function. But this correlation is not 1-to-1 by any means, and some very evil nations or organizations have nonetheless accomplished a great deal in real history. And, Exalted is deliberately a game about exploring the nature of power and what people do with it, so I think ignoring the connection between morality and competence in the fiction is justified, to make for a more interesting set of choices for the characters.

            I can see someone in the setting, by the way, arguing that Dragonblooded are at least more likely to be moral rulers, since their powers are much more group-centric than other Exalts, and thus they're more likely to develop that sense of empathy that can prevent atrocities. I'm not making the argument myself, mind (I think the history of the setting pretty much suggests it doesn't hold water), but I can certainly see it being made.

                        Well I think that whether the people are happy is a pretty important point (probably one of the most important points).
                        But also the Huns... well, notice they're no longer here? They conquered for a while, and then lost to the locals because they'd expended too much strength fighting. Most tribal steppe invasions actually only last a few generations at most.
                        As you said, as long as its leader lives, and it's mostly undefeated. But eventually, you get defeated and the leader dies.

                        Constant invasion and plundering actually isn't what most Empires do. First they invade and plunder. But then they need a recurring tax base. This is why China was conquered multiple times by invading barbarians... who then Sinified and became just a new class of aristocrats ruling over peasants, because that's really how you sustain your power. Actually, conquering cultures tend to either settle down and create a base (which they may well use for more raids, but they will have that base as well), or they keep conquering, split and fight each other, and then get beaten themselves.

                        Anyway, the issue with this Dawn is that he's got himself into a war with a Solar Sorceress, with the Realm ready to invade soon. If he thought more diplomatically, rather than "the best way to defend places is to annex them", "the best way to ensure the safety of my river's water source is to invade and conquer the mountains" and "the best way to feed my people is to conquer food-rich areas", then he could have easily taken a diplomatic option, allied with the Solar Sorceress who'd have sold him food from her magic farms, and they could conquer the Realm's nearby territories easily, making it very difficult for the Realm to project power here without local supplies.
                        Whereas, under the previous ruler (a ghost with like War 1, and 4 mortal sub-kings), they got on well with all the local powers, and the Solar Sorceress even gave them dragons to use against enemy tribes, allowing them to defeat them easily.
