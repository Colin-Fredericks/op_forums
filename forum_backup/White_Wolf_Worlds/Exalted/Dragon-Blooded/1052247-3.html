    Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
    Prasad/Ysyr really reminds me of Paragon and Gem back in 1st edition and 2nd edition. In fact, the distance between Ysyr and Parasad's capital are about the same as the distance between Gem and Paragon. Back during 2nd edition it was generally agreed that Gem and Paragon were too far apart to realistically engage in a trade war with one another, let alone an actual military conflict. Admittedly the harsh desert environment was an important factor, but just the sheer distance alone between the nations played a big role in making people wonder how these two states would end up at odds with one another.

    While it is absolutely possible to imagine Paragon (or Prasad) gathering up an army to march on Gem/Ysyr (or vice versa) such an event should probably be presented as a monumental and exceptional undertaking, even in the Exalted setting, comparable to Alexander's march across the world (there's a reason we're still talking about his accomplishment 1700 years later). It feel's a little odd to have it just tossed out there as a matter of course, similar to how the Gem/Paragon conflict felt odd for being tossed out there.
    Prasad and Ysyr are imperial powers. Though they are of course far smaller and weaker than the Realm itself, they are, to my understanding, significantly richer, more populous, and more militarily powerful than any published Threshold state under Realm dominion. In matters of scale and realpolitik, they are more akin to the Realm than they are to city-states like Gem and Paragon.

    This goes against how the Threshold has long been depicted, but that's a large part of the narrative role of the Dreaming Sea — to provide an arena for imperial play at sufficient remove from the Blessed Isle that it takes place outside the shadow of the Realm, while still fitting into Creation proper.

                                        Looking back at the map, it occurs to me that it's very conceivable that Ysyr doesn't want to fight for control of territory that extends inland, but along coastlines.

                                        ​I mean, my initial thought was that Prasad might have territory that extends east from Kamthahar until it reaches either the shore or a lot of subordinate coastal kingdoms, and that Ysyr's mainland territory might actually share little border with them, but it's a border that is heavily contested because Ysyr would want to expand northwest towards the trade routes to the Scavenger Lands, but then I realised "look at how much water is there, going in that same direction", and also remembered how much of the Portuguese empire consisted of coastal settlements so that their ships around the horn of Africa would have places to dock on their way to the Indian ocean, as well as the maritime empires of the Mediterranean.

                                        ​I still imagine that Ysyr owns some of the mainland, but could actually picture it as having expanded into that primarily for the sake of being able to maintain a second front of pressure in any wars against Prasad. I think that's supposed to be why the Carthaginians expanded into the Iberian peninsula during the First Punic War.

                                        ​Indeed, looking at the placement of what are supposed to be the big empires of the southeast, I think it would be very fun to imagine them as having significantly different and incompatible territorial goals.

                                        ​Prasad is largely a land-based empire; it expanded directly to the sea, just enough west to have a lock on any traffic passing through the mountains, and is able to concentrate a lot of power in the narrow northern tip of the sea while overall not being very maritime. It has a larger population, maintains a lot of power over surrounding client states, is able to collect a massive bulk of goods through its meagre ports and firm control of the overland routes to the northern markets. It's able to police the top of the sea to ensure that a lot of trade has to pass through it, and has enough of a cultural tie to the Realm to ensure a decent reception for its traders on the other side of Ma-Ha-Suchi's domain. Their primary goal is to maintain their current monopolies, and gradually increase their control over the grasslands between the Dreaming Sea and the mountains.

                                        ​Volivat doesn't have the landmass that its competitors do, but it has a large network of colonies along the edge of the Dreaming Sea, superior naval power, and its control of the northern side of the Dreaming Sea is largely uncontested. This confers the advantages of having access to a lot of trade goods that the southern empires don't have native access to, as well as allowing it to connect to trade routes that spread north, most notably ones that are able to most effectively bypass Ma-Ha-Suchi's domain. They don't have much need to expand, but know that many of their southern settlements are seen as encroaching on enemy territory, and have a desire to repulse Ysyr's ambitions to establish footholds on the north.

                                        ​Ysyr is the most powerful force on the big island it resides on (which is where a lot of the subservient city-states it is connected to by roads are found, and which it does not have complete control over), and is the furthest away from the lucrative northern trading. It wants to expend its power north largely through establishing dominance over the coastlines of the entire Dreaming Sea, with land-based expansion largely for the sake of having a stronger strategic base for contesting its foes and establishing a bread basket to relieve the burden on its heavily mountainous island territory. Being by far the closest to the Wyld is a unique challenge and advantage for them.

                                        Originally posted by AnubisXy
                                        When I read about Ysyr and Prasad being in conflict, I got the idea that perhaps they both had diplomats in a third party kingdom somewhere in the middle and occasionally interacted with one another that way. I just didn't think that they could be engaged in any kind of direct and sustained confrontation considering the sheer distance between them
                                        ​I imagine that one day we may get a Different Skies book focusing on the region, which may elaborate some of the details of its territorial distributions, but in the meantime* I look at it in terms of being given details that are firm enough to give direction and scant enough to leave a lot to fill in, and thinking that the exact details can be filled in by the desires and sensibilities of the individual.

                                        Do Ysyr and Prasad share a direct border? Do they engage largely through subservient intermediaries? Are they mostly struggling over coastal possessions? Does Ysyr want to take over (territory from) Prasad, or is it the other way around? Are they, in fact, in competition over expansion to seize control of the northern coastlines of the Dreaming Sea, with Volivat as their main native target which pursues a strategy of alternatively fighting them both off, allying with one against the other, and keeping them engaged with one another so that they're off balance?

                                        ​Adjust to taste.

                                        ​* I mean, even if we ever get a book like that, there would be nothing preventing me or anybody else from reassigning their possessions or ambitions or conflicts if something different suited any given game, or simply for the variety.
