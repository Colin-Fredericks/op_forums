            Originally posted by Piff View Post
            I think that passage is poorly considered and an example of the 'Dreaming Sea as a Lake' perception.

            I mean, let's be clear - as per the scale of the new map, we're dealing with a distance that's as great as that between BEIJING and EGYPT.

            I like the new world and scale, because it enriches the 3e setting, but I think the fiction has thus far been unfortunately... inconsistent, on being faithful to the idea of such a vast world.
            Many also ignore how far shorter distances actually are with pre-modern technology in terms of personal travel, poltical relationships and cultural penetration[1]. Even if we shrink the map scale so Ysyr and Prasad are as Spain and Anatolia, we can still fit dozens or hundreds of distinct societies between them in our ancient-to-early modern Mediterranean to whom these distant powers are typically a far lesser consideration than their local rivals, even as they vie for influence with hard and soft power. Recall that to the Ancient Greeks, the Ten Thousand's march from Babylon to Greece was an epic journey through an essentially alien world, and that's around 2000km. Of course, it's a fantasy setting and there are are some individuals who can break these rules wide open, but they're extremely rare, even among exalts[2]. Enough that these things can be significant to the epic narratives of Exalted PCs, but not enough to transform societal dynamics.

            Orthogonally, while I agree that Immaculate ideas have significant influence (if rarely primacy) well beyond the borders of The Realm, I think most people vastly overestimate the importance of the Anathema to Immaculate belief. Understandably so, since mosts PCs are such "Anathema", but the beliefs about the conception of society as the Perfected Hierarchy, the proper relationship between mortals and spirits, the cycle of reincarnation, and the occult practice of ritual, are all surely far more central to both the belief and praxis of Immaculates and Immaculate-influenced believers across creation[3].

            [1] This reddit AskHistorians post has a nice description of how "Chinese" culture failed to penetrate the interior until surprisingly late. Unmentioned, but equally pertinent, would be Taiwan -- a mere 200km off the coast, but not brought into the Chinese sphere until the 17th century.
            [2] There's almost nothing in the Solar charmset that achieves this, maybe a Ride charm or two. Otherwise we're talking about Sorcerors who take Stormwind Rider, and maybe some artefacts.
            [3] I'd liken the place anathema, for most immaculate believers, to that of the apocalypse in the Abrahamic faiths. For some people and some sects it's of central importance, but it just doesn't matter on an everyday basis to most Catholics or conventional Sunnis.

                                                        Prasad/Ysyr really reminds me of Paragon and Gem back in 1st edition and 2nd edition. In fact, the distance between Ysyr and Parasad's capital are about the same as the distance between Gem and Paragon. Back during 2nd edition it was generally agreed that Gem and Paragon were too far apart to realistically engage in a trade war with one another, let alone an actual military conflict. Admittedly the harsh desert environment was an important factor, but just the sheer distance alone between the nations played a big role in making people wonder how these two states would end up at odds with one another.

                                                        While it is absolutely possible to imagine Paragon (or Prasad) gathering up an army to march on Gem/Ysyr (or vice versa) such an event should probably be presented as a monumental and exceptional undertaking, even in the Exalted setting, comparable to Alexander's march across the world (there's a reason we're still talking about his accomplishment 1700 years later). It feel's a little odd to have it just tossed out there as a matter of course, similar to how the Gem/Paragon conflict felt odd for being tossed out there.
