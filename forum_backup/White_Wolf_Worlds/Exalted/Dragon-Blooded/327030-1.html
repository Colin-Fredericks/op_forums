                                                Uhhm.... Because they're Awesome?

                                                Considering you mention pre-errata, I get the feeling that yor're coming from a 2ed-based perspective. Maybe that has something to do with it?
                                                I had always only played first edition, until I decided to try out second for a new campaign last year. I.hate.2ed. for many (many (many)) reasons, but that is just digression. (I guess my point is; "don't listen to anything 2ed" says, and "1ed was really good")

                                                DBs are definately my favorite exalted type. This comes from several points two of which I will write about in short, but hopefully engulfing, sumaries here:

                                                They have the most interesting culture (or: Crouching Vampire, Hidden Thrones)
                                                As opposed to Solars, which are usually by default wanderers or outcasts, the Dragon-Blooded actually have a rich cultural background and heritage. Their society is probably the one with the largest page count owing to it being described in quite some detail in both the core book (1ed) and their own book (Exalted: The DragonbBlooded(1st ed.)), and hence the one with the most depth to it in the setting. The fact that it takes up so large a space in the [1ed] Rule Book, says something about how important and influential it actually is on the setting. The large presence of The Realm, and the way a Dragon-Blooded character is necessarily tied to, or a part of, creates a type of story not found in any of the other types. A DB game is not a super hero game, but an exploration of the rather complex themes embodied in the Scarlet Dynasty.
                                                Short Version: Dragon-Blooded is more akin to playing Vampire: The Masquerade; the point and the focus is not on being a powerful entity, the focus is more on the political side of things. The point is about being a part of a society of those powerful entities and trying to both advance (if the character is into that sort of thing) and survive in it (all this while it is being confronted by threats, both from the outside and internally).

                                                Interesting Characters (or: how I learned to stop worrying and love the Scarlet Empress)
                                                The nature of Dragon-Blooded society is ripe with interesting conflicts, difficult questions of morality, contradictions and double standards (themes such as Imperialism, control vs. freedom, truth vs. good, love vs. responsibility, a whole bunch of questions about family relations, etc. etc. not to mention all the political intrigue that comes with just belonging to a house.) The many layers of conflict and interesting social issues that one needs to take into account, when creating a character from the Scarlet Dynasty, basically makes you (that's "makes" as in "forces") end up with a complex character. The book basically gives you a complex background for your character to relate to, and by defining hirs relation to that complex background the character will unavoidably be shaped by it.
                                                Short Version: The Scarlet Dynasty is a complex multilayered society. The Scarlet Dynasty is a big part of your character = you character is by default a a complex, (probably interesting) character.

                                                I could go on, but I have taken far too long to write this post as it is (which probably isn't as beutifully written as I would've liked anyway because of how late it is).
                                                Well, Hope I may have conveyed something of interest at least. Now Gooo*yawn*od night!

                                                        To be a Dragonblood to me, is to be a masterful showperson in addition to being an Exalt. To be part of the Ten Thousand is to move amongst your peers and influence them with both force and threats therein. That's part of their appeal to me, that courtly intrigue is not only a feature, it's largely expected, as your ancestry, your ancestry's baggage and all that that entails figures both into your ambitions, but also your obligations.                                                             1. Their naming conventions. I find their family and given names difficult to pronounce.
