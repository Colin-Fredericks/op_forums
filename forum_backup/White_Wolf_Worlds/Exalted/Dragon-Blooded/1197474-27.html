    The only potential problem I see with that would be you would then need to apply EFFECT YOU WANT on every roll in order to hit cap, which depending on how many charms you need to hit cap could kinda wash out their uses, because they'd need to be able to be used on every attack without much to do, otherwise you aren't able to hit cap often.

    Then you would need to have more charms that add 1 die than needed to hit cap, so that you could pick and choose.

    While this is a viable design, and I could see how that could be interesting for some people, having to sift through all of my powers just to get all the dice I want to throw at this sounds like it might slow down play to me.

    Another alternative would be having a couple different excellency type charms that don't give you full cap, but can only be used in certain situations... wait I just described Infernal excellencies. That is not a gameplay feature I would want farmed out to every character type, not that I don't think it works, just doesn't work for everything.

    TL;DR That idea has merits, but it's own set of issues. At the end of the day I like what we got.

    edit: for completionist sake, and to ensure I don't come off as strawmanning, if the alternative was "EFFECT YOU WANT and NOW YOU HAVE UNLOCKED ONE MORE DIE ON YOUR EXCELLENCY BUT YOU DON'T HAVE TO USE THIS CHARM TO USE IT" that is less tedious, but it puts a second progression roadblock to getting to your dicecap, the actual cap itself, and buying up the charms.

                                Others can elaborate further, but when I got into playtesting the current slate of Charms, splitting the effects of an Excellency up into multiple Charms weakened the Dragon-Blooded and made them split an already painfully thin mote pool. It didn’t add anything and the issue of before wasn’t an issue in this Charmset.

                                Originally posted by kongurous View Post
                                Also, still not really enthused by the addition of a keyword that doesn't actually do anything except add noise to the charm. Is it really that valuable of an addition to declare a charm is an excellency?
                                Yes. Several Solar Charms address interacting with Excellencies or Excellency-type magics; future Charms may do the same. This means you don’t have to specify dice caps every time you talk about a Charm; it’s just to the Excellency rating.

                                Cheap and efficient diceadders are sort of a hallmark of the Exalted. Whether that curious aspect stays as a piece of design I don’t know, but it’s an interesting curio of design and one that speaks to an Exalt’s primacy in the power tiers of the setting.

                                Neall Raemonn Price
