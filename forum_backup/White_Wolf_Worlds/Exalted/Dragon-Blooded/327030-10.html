            One of the things I like -- and that sets it apart from just about every other shounen story I can remember -- is that the secondary hero actually delivered the death blow in one of their fights.

                Originally posted by Elfive View Post

                If you want to make a lot of kids in the most efficient manner
                There's not all that much about the Exalted that implies that efficiency was or could have been prioritized over some other things.

                Personally, I go with the idea that the very first Dragon Blooded were ten thousand individuals of an approximately even gender mix who managed to reach some sacred place (out of several million who heard the initial call and failed along the way), who by virtue of the nature of the journey had all come together and helped each other out long before making it there, where the Elemental Dragons revealed themselves and praised the strength of those who had made it and declared that they would be kin to them and blood to them for all time, and then they were Exalted. After that, little subgroups that had formed within them all travelled off in different directions to find their own way, but not before the whole lot of them affirmed their eternal kindred and they collectively pledged to meet again.

                A hundred years later, all of those groups were substantially larger, because human passions are what the are. Maybe they weren't as large as they could have been, but they were all pretty badass, so who cares. This was about the time when they started falling in with the Celestial Exalted.

                Not all of the original Ten Thousand lived to see each other again, but in the single battle in which all of the remaining ones came together and led the charge, it was a sight to behold.

                (Just to be clear, I mostly discount any references that explicit frame the Primordial War as having been a system where the Exalted were Chosen and then all immediately drawn into a kind of divine boot camp and then issued forth from an already established centralised and unified command structure as well. I imagine the circumstances of the War as a bit more mythic, although the Sidereals were always a fairly coherent host, and their actions helped coordinate distantly scattered Exalted of highly diverse technique.)

                                        You should want to play a Dragonblooded if stories centering on the Realm interest you.

                                        If you want to be a superhuman hero without the extra cultural baggage of people often thinking of you as possessed by Millennia old Demon-King.

                                        If running an empire sounds like fun, but you don't want to do all the work of building one from scratch.

                                        If you want to play up the drama of Responsibility and the Mandate of Kings. The Celestial Exalted may have more power, but these days when mortals are threatened by powers beyond them they will run to a Terrestrial rather than some Anathema.

                                        If political intrigue combined with a lineage-based power structure appeals to you, ala China, Egypt, or basically the entire Eastern Hemisphere up until WWI.

                                        If you want to roleplay what it's like to live in a world that tells you that you and your kin are infallible paragons of righteousness, yet you're daily witness to acts that contradict this, and one of your central conflicts consists of do you give into the culture of sycophancy or fight against it?

                                        And if the latter, will you continue to do so when that authority is the only thing standing between a safe and prosperous Satrapy and the carnage that comes from 300 years of simmering rebellious spirit?

                                        I think that's one of the central tenets of really interesting Dragonblooded games. The most righteous Dragonblooded are dedicated to a religion and philosophy that keeps people held down in misery*, but the most pragmatic (amoral) Dragonblood will happily abandon it, if it means their political advantage.

                                        So how do you save Creation while still staying true to yourself, and what ideals do you abandon or hold close in your pursuit of that end?

                                        Basically, there are a lot of interesting stories that only the Dragonblooded can tell. If one of those possible stories interest you, you should play a Dragonblooded.

                                        If not, there are plenty of people that are interested by them, and there are plenty of other Exalt stories to play out instead.

                                        *The Realm/Immaculate Order does lots of good things too! But, their Empire and their peace is paid for in the blood, sweat, and self-determination of the Satrapies, the costs shouldn't be shied away from anymore than the costs of empire building should be white-washed in a Celestial game.

                                        **This makes for really interesting mixed Exalt games too, so long as you flatten the Exp costs and make sure that the Dragonblooded has a different niche focus than the Celestial players.

                                                Originally posted by Valles View Post
                                                No, no I don't think it is. Even if I accepted your parallel, the difference in degree between the range of interesting play options cut off by blocking 'bile bending' and those involved in blocking interaction with physical elements is so great as to keep it from being a useful comparison.

                                                I have no objection to large parts, even a majority, of the charmset not involving overt manifestations. I do object to the thesis that the physical aspects of the meaning of, say, Air, should be sidelined and minimized in the fashion that they currently are - to the only region where physicality is allowed being stunts that require deliberately contradicting the established mechanical basis of the game.
                                                I'm not sure how many times it needs to be said that nobody is suggesting blocking DBs' interaction with physical elements, and the DBs have never been portrayed as lacking that interaction. It is increasingly difficult to take you seriously when you just keep polishing this chestnut over and over again. As far as I can tell our disagreement is entirely about degree, and about how those powers should be handled within the system.

                                                I mean, I just do not know where you are getting this. Even the one passage you cited as an example of someone making this argument wasn't, and I've never seen it myself. I can totally believe there's like one or two people in the fandom who object strongly to that Earth charm that raises fortifications, or the Archery charm that grows arrows out of a tree or shrub, or the Melee charm where you summon a sword made out of ice and hitting people with it chills them to the bone, or whatever. However, if those are actually the people you object to then I wish you would take it up with them.

                                                In the one I'm actually making, and taking your example of monetary Earth Aspects, I'd figure that animas would by default produce the commonest denominator of their elements - an Earth Aspect who glittered like a faceted constellation would be producing cubic zirconia or something else far more common than diamonds, and that the monetary value of what you could get out of charm manifestations would be linked to either other charms, more difficult gates like permanent essence, or, most likely, both.
                                                This isn't hugely bad, and I'd probably allow it as an unreliable cosmetic thing, but generally the way I would prefer to run it -- and I think the best compromise between elements -- is that the stuff that comes from anima flux is real but has a very short "half-life" and tends to dissolve into loose essence once you leave the area or tamp down your anima. This gives it a physicality without constraining your anima manifestation to "stuff that won't screw up the setting if every same-Aspect DB can do it on demand."

                                                The basic problem here is that one doesn't want people using their anima to accomplish this stuff except as a rare neat trick that isn't presumed commonplace, which fits nicely into the stunting model. Even if DBs can do that stuff, doing it through the anima is often not good for the anima. For instance, the Solar anima tends to bleach things, and I would let a Solar stunt bleaching out bloodstains with their anima to evade the notice of police, but I wuld not make this an on-tap "motes for bleach" effect. You buy a charm for that. If it's useful and reliable to use your anima to whip up a bunch of raw materials, or reseed an orchard, or conjure enough water for your group to survive in the desert, that' s warping what the anima is to the point where I think it's lost its appeal as a thing.
