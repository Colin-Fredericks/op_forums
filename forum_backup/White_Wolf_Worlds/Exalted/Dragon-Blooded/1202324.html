    Sell me on the Dragon-Blooded

    04-02-2018, 07:02 PM
    Ok, I'm not actually going to ask you to sell me on the Dragon-Blooded; I've been a big fan of DBs in general and the Realm in particular since 1E.

    What I am asking is for a bit of insight into DBs and dynasts before I make a decision about backing the kickstarter. I'll get right to my concern: that the Dragon-Blooded will be primarily defined by their utter inferiority (spiritually, martially, etc.) to the other exalts of the setting and that the Realm will be a baby-eating monster machine with no purpose or redeeming features other than to sit around being horrible and waiting for someone more powerful (a Solar, a Lunar, or whatever) to kick it over and make something better.

    There are themes about the Dragon-Blooded that I've always found very interesting... but it's hard for me to get into those themes, playing a heroic dynast or Immaculate monk, if the stuff that I don't personally enjoy all that much is at the forefront. I'm not saying that those things are bad per se, just that the aren't for me.

    I've follow the threads here about the Kickstarter, but I thought to start my own thread to get some perspective on the newest incarnation of the Dragon-Blooded.

    Thanks!

                          Originally posted by Equitable Remedy View Post
                          the Realm will be a baby-eating monster machine with no purpose or redeeming features other than to sit around being horrible
                          ​Actually, I can think of one way to respond to this that doesn't give away too much of what is in the book:

                          ​The book portrays some groups of the Dragon Blooded of the Realm as having sincere agendas and ideologies concerning the function of the Realm and their place therein. When I say "some groups", I'm not talking about a token good guy or two, just that it's not the focus of every corner of their society. It's a reasonably even mix.

                          ​The pursuit of these beliefs will lead to them doing some nice things, and some terrible things that they have a different perspective on. The potential for good is not incidental, even if it's not the one-and-only that can arise from them doing their thing.

                          ​It should also be noted that, conversely, there are aspects of the Realm's seedy underbelly that go a bit darker than they have before, but even the portrayal of that is handled with nuance and sophistication.

                          ​It's complex, but it's rich, and I would say that to dig into it in detail would be rewarding.

                                                  What this book seems to do well is focus a lot on the struggles of the dynasts, and the evils of a system rather than evils of the individual. Like for instance maybe you're really worried that a house Sesus legion is going to come storming into your home city and kill your whole family, if they can't be reasoned with and the civil war sparks hot. The thing is, you could really bolster your forces there with that big shipment of fire dust that your family managed to procure from a southern dust baron. It's your job to heroically go over there, see that the dust is loaded safely, battle off pirates who are debatably being paid off by house Sesus, and secure your family.

                                                  The thing is, when you get there, the shipment is short. Some horrible monstrosity from the Wyld physically grabbed the whole last caravan wagon and made off with it. Images flash through your head of your family being put to the sword by those thugs, if diplomacy fails, so you need to get that back. While you're in the satrapy though, you might notice the dust baron is seriously treating his people badly. I mean your family is paying out the nose and he's milking it for all it's worth, he's a dick but you get why. However nothing is stopping you from being the best guy ever and getting that firedust back, rooting out some of the corruption in the dust baron's organization to help take a little pressure off the little guy, and then making it back in time to throw some weight behind your threats that if Sesus sends a legion to your home, they're losing that legion.

                                                  The text kind of subtly implies that's most dynasts would just go there and make sure that's their family had absolutely everything it needed in the upcoming war, and that some might be bastards and drive the dust baron even harder to curry favour with their elders, others might be real true heroes, while others still might treat the whole thing as a vacation. Mostly some combination of all of those things and more will be true. You might even ask yourself if you could liberate the people from under the thumb of the dust baron, but that's damn hard, and if you don't get that dust somehow your family might pay with their lives. Or flip it, maybe you play the circle of DBs back on the isle carefully navigating the politics, trying desperately to hold off the war and clinging to a tiny sliver of hope that maybe there doesn't need to be one at all.

                                                  If it just showed you the realm's oppression it'd be a charicature of an empire who's only purpose in play is to tear it down and build a something better. It does a great job of bringing things down to a close, human level, where even if you can see the whole system and the problems with it, the things and ideals that matter to you are all in danger, and you're probably not going to be a pure perfect hero all the time but navigating that is half the fun.



                                                  As to DBs playing second banana to other exalts it's basically not there at all. Anathema are scary, but they are as much obstacles to be overcome as predators that stalk you in the night. This book actually makes me want to play a Solar again because it actually feels like the Scarlet Dynasty has this huge weight, vastness, power to it, whereas the lone anathema out there really have nothing. House Mnemon doesn't deal with a random Solar, this small section of a portion of an army does that. It makes those few Solars who survive and thrive feel more special and accomplished. Surviving the Realm is a feat man, and ever present is the knowledge that probably the only reason you did survive is because the entire Realm wasn't after you.

                                                  It also helps that that's basically the only time you're playing second banana. Other than your respected elders and fierce rivals the world is yours. You are utterly a class above. Where other women dream of seeing the ocean, or traveling across a mountain, you almost casually launch an expedition to the far reaches of the frontier to plumb ancient tombs and forgotten civilizations. Your concerns for food are how to feed your army, your concerns for dominance involve how far up the dynasty you can climb. You are at once incredibly individually powerful, and yet connected so deeply to something so much greater than yourself, struggling against others within it, as well as being bound by duty and the deepest kinship that any exalted experience.


                                                  TLDR;
                                                  I'm sorry this went on so long but it really is fantastic, I'm quite impressed and very excited to see more. Bringning the focus in tighter and making things more distinct really makes it feel more human and understandable. There's bad people, good people, most are a little of both, they're all in a shitty situation, and just maybe you and your friends have the power and wit to go out there and do something about it. Now go be a hero, whatever that means to you.
