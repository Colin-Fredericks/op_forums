                                Originally posted by Valles View Post
                                I think that Wulf's already covered it, frankly - DBs are described in their introduction and aspect fluff as being intimately tied to their elements, and then in practice they end up with 'phantasmal' special effects around far more generic abilities. That said, this isn't the point that has me exercised.
                                They are intimately tied to their elements, though, it's just that you don't consider "Fire Aspects embody the essence and nature of fire, and have charms which create fire or involve behaving like fire" as being "intimately tied to the element", even when there are a few more direct manifestations to go with it. I submit that this is an arbitrary distinction based on your personal image of what "elemental powers" should be like. It's like playing a game where the characters are based on the four humours, and then objecting that the Bile Aspect isn't mostly about playing bile-benders, as you'd been led to presume.

                                A substantial minority of DB powers are basically just "powers the Exalted need to have, and they're tied to this element because that's the element that matches the Ability". But that's by no means the overarching theme of their charmset: a lot of the powers that don't involve overt manifestations of the element, or that do but not to your satisfaction, are very clearly channelling the element in question.

                                What has me so incredulous is the way people seem to be completely ignoring the blatant self-contradiction involved in claiming that 'surfing on a self-created tidal wave' is both a difference in kind from 'filling a glass from your anima' or 'catching a waterskin by the water inside it', and somehow less disruptive. That, I don't understand.
                                There's some truth to this, but it's not the abject double standard you're making it out to be, I don't think. Broad utility powers are dicey because they're expansive, and they tend to distort the setting in the same way as other potent utilitarian technology (which is functionally what they are). They also call the setting to account -- if Earth aspects can easily create significant amounts of money, for instance, it's hard to justify the Dragon-Blooded considering that thing valuable. If you've decided in advance that you want the setting to be a certain way, the bits and bobs have to fall into line with that vision, or else you get the kind of situation we've sometimes had in the past where a common power trivializes a problem the setting presents as serious, and the answer to that is "eh, what can you do".

                                Using elemental powers to enhance things that Dragon-Blooded, and the Exalted in general, are already established as doing, presents fewer obstacles and therefore demands less care.

I do object to the thesis that the physical aspects of the meaning of, say, Air, should be sidelined and minimized in the fashion that they currently are - to the only region where physicality is allowed being stunts that require deliberately contradicting the established mechanical basis of the game.

                                    Originally posted by Gayo View Post
                                    There's some truth to this, but it's not the abject double standard you're making it out to be, I don't think.                                     I recall a point being made about how 'physical elemental manipulation in one charm' is a separate discussion from 'including physical elements in charms of the same scope as less physical charms'. If I wanted to make the former argument, I'd be starting a new thread about how one very flexible 48XP charm is a superior play experience to six 8XP charms that add up to about the same standard applications - as I haven't, so far.

                                    In the one I'm actually making, and taking your example of monetary Earth Aspects, I'd figure that animas would by default produce the commonest denominator of their elements - an Earth Aspect who glittered like a faceted constellation would be producing cubic zirconia or something else far more common than diamonds, and that the monetary value of what you could get out of charm manifestations would be linked to either other charms, more difficult gates like permanent essence, or, most likely, both.

                                    Given dev statements about how the biggest difficulty with charm design in the new system is coming up with ideas for what to do, I doubt that this represents an increase in complexity sufficient to outweigh the number of plot and play options it introduces.

                                        Basically I kind of treat it like this. Doing really cool stuff like pulling your anima in and encasing your body in diamond for the purpose of stunting a feat of strength to hold up a falling building is awesome, and it kind of feels lame if it's not actually happening. Likewise it feels lame if I have to stunt it as cubic zirconia or something. It would also really suck if I had to take a charm that let me manipulate my element in cool ways to be able to do that. I don't want to sink all my starting bonus points into something just to be able to do cool stuff with my element. On the other hand being able to use elemental manifestations for whatever the hell other than stunts, like a water aspect providing enough drinkable water for a village out in the wastes simply through having a water anima then that's too flexible to be a part of simply your anima.

                                        Kind of like that joke everyone makes about Glorious Solar Desk. If you can make a sword or bow and armor out of hard light, where exactly is the difficulty in making literally anything else out of hard light? Why can't GSS make a table shaped sword, or a canoe shaped sword and just use that for whatever purpose.
