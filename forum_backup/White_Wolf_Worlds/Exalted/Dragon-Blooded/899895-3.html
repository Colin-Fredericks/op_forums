                                                    Probably not.

                                                    Firstly, the dynasty has 10,000 Dragon-Blood, plus mortal members. The genetic pool is actually far bigger than what medieval monarchs were looking for. There's a reason that Henry VIII was forced by his dad to marry Catherine of Aragon, rather than just marry another Spanish princess: there wasn't one.
                                                    There was actually a point in the middle ages when a bunch of western monarchs married Byzantine Princesses, because there just wasn't any princesses in western Europe they were legally allowed to marry (due to Catholic rules about consanguanity).

                                                    Secondly, the dynasts are (probably) almost all descended from the Scarlet Empress by now, but they're not necessarily descended from a)her through many different lines, and b)other people who cross-over in the genetic record.
                                                    When the great-grandson of Tepet and the great-grandaughter of Mnemon marry, chances are reasonable that the Scarlet Empress is the only ancestor they share. It's possible they might share one or two others, but it's not very certain.
                                                    It's actually possible there are very old Dynasts who aren't descended from the Scarlet Empress. I mean, senior members of House Tepet might well be descended from Tepet's sister, brother, or cousin, a family of Dragonblood who all got promoted to Dynast when their relative married the Empress and got given his own House. I'd think by now almost all dynasts would be descended from the Scarlet Empress, but likely just the once.
                                                    Related to this, is the fact that there are not many inter-house marriages. Medieval aristocrats would often marry their cousin or second-cousin to keep property in the noble house, but it seems the Dynasty normally marries across Houses. This is probably partly because the male stays in his own House, so the Houses aren't losing people by marrying them off, just that they won't produce children for the house.

                                                    Thirdly, the Dynasty regularly gets infusions of peasant blood. Officially, the peasant who just exalted is a long-lost son of someone's dead brother, but really that's a legal fiction. He might marry another Dynastic Dragonblood if he's particularly heroic or lucky, but more likely he'll just marry a mortal Dynast. But that still infuses the Dynasty with outside blood.

                                                    Lastly, medieval aristocrats often married closely due to property rights. Since woman couldn't inherit in German Salic law, it was pretty common for a man to try to marry his daughter to his nephew (who was going to inherit the property), as I understand it. There were more general attempts to consolidate property by close marriages, and marriages to people far from the family could well mean the family would "lose" property.
                                                    But with dynastic inheritance, this seems to be less of an issue.
                                                    So there's not the incentive to marry closely, rather there's the incentive to marry into other Houses to secure diplomacy.
