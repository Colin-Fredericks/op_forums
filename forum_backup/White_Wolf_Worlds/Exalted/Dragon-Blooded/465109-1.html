    A Terrestrial might can use a Charm to bolster one or more allies traits equal to his own. However, if it is used on another Dragon-Blooded, he may be able to reflexively activate the Charm as well (whether or not he has it) for a different trait. It gives a sense of...their powers don't just add together, they multiply!

    And of course, during active attacks as well. Perhaps a Charm that as long as the Dragon-Blooded has an ally fighting beside him, he gains a boost to his Accuracy and Defense per ally, up to his Essence. If its a Dragon-Blooded ally, he also gains that Terrestrial's Anima surrounding his weapon, as per Dragon-Graced Weapon.

    And another thing that might be cool is that it doesn't have to be an ally. By that I mean, as long as another Dragon-Blooded is nearby, you gain the effect, as the blood reacts to blood. Perhaps.

    Not entirely certain, but Im certainly excited to find out!

                Given the fact that Charm trees are apparently immense now, there's no reason that all comers can't be satisfied here.

                I mean, I'm clearly not an expert in Charm design, but if I were gonna build Dragon-Blooded trees the way I'd do it in one of two ways: I'd either make the usual logical Charm trees that proceed from root to branch in an understandable fashion, with logical branches, and bolt the synergy Charms on in ways that make them entirely skippable if you want to do so, but easily takeable without many speedbumps if you didn't. This would provide flexibility for people who wanted to be all "I'm a Prince of the Earth, motherfuckers, not some soldier" and also people who wanted to be all "I am totally a soldier, all Princes of the Earth are soldiers, that's why we exist."

                The other way you could do it is have Charms pull double-duty, which might be a whole lot more interesting. "This Charm has Effect X if you use it when flying solo, but it also has the Synergy Keyword, when you use it in that way it has Effect Y, which is similar both mechanically and thematically to Effect X but is instead designed for teamwork purposes." Or something like that, anyway. Kind of like how they're projecting Getimian design, where Charms do different weird shit depending on what sort of Essence you dump in them.

                And of course there is always room for weird one-offs and suchly. The occasional Charm that's something like "this Charm is actually really bland, not bad per se but doesn't do anything special, but if you hook up with people who have the explicit complementary Charms to it (which are also actually kind of bland) you plug together like Voltron and are suddenly really fucking powerful." You wouldn't want to do that too often, because that specific example is very "you'd better be in an all-DB game with people who want to take the complementary Charms with you" but it would be a way to mix it up.

                "SEX NOVA is the kind of person who, after being chosen as the divine champion of the god of heroes, decided to call himself SEX NOVA."

                                        Originally posted by Erinys View Post
                                        Isn't there a Dragon-Blooded charm that gets more powerful when in the vicinity of the Anathema, so Wyld Hunts can use it as an especially "holy" weapon? Except it's actually a teamwork charm from the First Age to help Terrestrials work better in the proximity of their Celestial commanders.
                                        Defense-From-Anathema Method/Dragon's Parable Defense has a bit of a strange history to it. The original wording in 1e and 2e Dragon-Blooded simply stated that it worked against "Anathema," and didn't really go into any detail on what counted for that. When the Dragon-Blooded got the large errata overhaul that came with Thousand Correct Actions of the Upright Soldier, it got a clarification that explains that it worked against all creatures of darkness, plus Celestial Exalted, to cover Solar and Lunar Anathema. That was probably how most groups would run it, so it made sense, but it then had to explain why the holy protection charm would work against them. It attributed this to the Dragon-Blood tapping Gaia's primordial power and love as a shield. That introduces a lot of weird setting stuff (can I shut down Defense-From-Anathema by killing Gaia?) and also is pretty far away from the paradigm of how Terrestrial Charms work. So, when the Dreams of the First Age errata came around, it talked about how Defense-From-Anathema Method was known as Dragon's Parable Defense, like you described.

                                        That's sort of a twisted and tangled history, and I wouldn't want to use that kind of stopgap retcon as a foundation moving forward (even though I personally thought it was very clever).

                                            I want Charms that interact with the physicality of a Dragon-Blooded's Anima.                                             I want an Athletics Charm that benefits from being Earth-Aspected where the Earth Aspect literally walks up a stone staircase whose steps emerge from his Anima (increasing range or duration of an Athletics movement effect).

                                            I want a Melee Charm that benefits from being Water-Aspected where when an opponent parries the Water Aspect the Anima floods the opponent for knockdown.

                                            I want an Occult Charm that interacts with Spirits of living concepts (such as a social bonus) for being Wood-Aspected like a rabbit-spirit eating the Anima of a Wood Aspect as they converse.

                                                    Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post
                                                    Defense-From-Anathema Method/Dragon's Parable Defense has a bit of a strange history to it.
                                                    I would just like to chime in here and say that Defense-From-Anathema Method is one of my favorite Charms, and its history of one of the few genuinely good and delightful things to come out of DotFA.

                                                    It just works on a lot of levels. On the surface, it is an interesting and effective Charm that meshes well with the official ideology of the Realm and also stands to reason. Of course the Dragon-Blooded would have a widely-used Charm that makes it easier to kill Anathema, that's one of the key tenets their whole society is ordered around.

                                                    But then you dig a little bit deeper, and it unfolds like a blossom. If you actually look at the Charm, it doesn't really specify that it be invoked with killing intent or only in combat against an Anathema, does it? Plenty of Charms can have explicit social cues, mental states, and situations which are required for them to "fire;" that's usually the purview of social charms, but hacky hacky slash-slash Charms can have those as well. This one doesn't. Once you cut away all the social baggage from it, you realize you can use it in situations that have nothing to do with its ostensible raison d'etre.

                                                    And that's because it's an ancient First Age Charm and used to have entirely different social baggage! The Dragon-Blooded used to fight alongside the Anathema, slaying enemies of creation and serving in their retinues and acting as elite shock troops. One of the common images of a really serious First Age curbstomp has always, in my mind, involved a big ball of Dragon-Blooded with a delicious Celestial Exalt filling in the center. They fought alongside Anathema, and given the natural Dragon-Blooded tendencies towards synergy and teamwork, it makes total sense they'd develop a Charm that makes them more awesome and more likely to live when wading through a bunch of Fair Folk in the retinue of their crazy-ass Dawn Caste boss.

                                                    And then you get into the way that Charm was turned against the Solars, and the connotations involved in that inversion, and its this whole weird and awesome social history of the Dragon-Blooded Host all encapsulated in a single Charm.

                                                    I love that. I love that so much.

                                                    (Also, now I want to read a supplement called "A Social History of Charms." I'm sure someone in-setting wrote it.)
