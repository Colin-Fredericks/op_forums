            Originally posted by Sidmen View Post
            And yet, the deliberative meeting hall of the Old Realm had symbols float above your head to identify your charm usage. That's the only case where I've seen the actual books take a side on this old argument. Dev opinions, especially dev opinions from people that weren't present at the time of those books' writing are just that - opinions.

            Personally, I rather find it stupid that observation wouldn't have led the DBs to codify their magical charms over the past few centuries. It makes far more sense to me - especially when teaching people charms is a thing that happens. How on earth could you teach your students Jade Defense, having learned it from your teacher, and knowing that every other school also teaches a very similar technique without coming up with a name for it? Humans don't work like that - heck, we even came up with a name for flicking your wrist to deflect a blow whilst fencing; and the opening moves in chess are practically all categorized and named.

            In short, I don't buy that "she's just really good at ignoring my arguments" would be said, without immediately being followed by someone else going "yeah, probably has Jade Defense up". Substitute your own names if you want flavor, so your family might call it the Serenity of the Mountain Technique, or whatever.
            Yeah, in a structured culture that passes down knowledge of skills it is likely they will have a name they use for that skill so they can talk about it, for example: 'Hey, did you hear about how the son of Random Dynastic Rival was so incompetent that he managed to burn down their dojo trying to master elemental burst? What shame he has brought upon his house!' On the other hand an Exalt that more or less has to work things out on their own (like most solars and exigents) is only going to have an in setting name for their charms if they feel like it, the names in the core book will be purely for the benefit of the people playing the game so they know that the Dawn just popped out an essence fueled flurry attack.

                First off, let me say this to anyone reading this. I am not griping at any of the 3e Developers (3eD) -- please don't take anything that I'm writing in that vein. The below are just some simple desires, based off of things that I have seen in the previous editions of Exalted. I know that developing games (or writing anything for that matter) is not as easy as waving a wand and making it happen. I also know that you guys (and gals) work hard, and it is fully appreciated. Thanks.


                Originally posted by Tchokes View Post
                Okay, I have been meaning to write this for sometime now to express my worries about what I think are my biggest gripes about Exalted (and of course it is relative to my favorite ones, the Dragon-Blooded). This problem maybe something that appears exclusively with them because of a very simple reason: they are the only Exalts who exist in a complex, Exalt-filled society that has been running for centuries. When you have something like that, some things are bound to be different, and I don't think the past books have done an exceptional job at getting us inside a society that has demigods working for it left and right.
                Let us not forget about Lookshy. There are a few others (Maybe they will get expanded or new one's added?), but they tend to be smaller and ultimately they are not "Big Players" on the world stage (Ok...the Forest Witches might be, but they are just weird and would be considered Anathema as far as the rest of Creation is concerned...)

                Lookshy is a very well organized and populous nation. It's society is complex, and it's Dragon-Blooded numerous in comparison to their population: 1 DB per 100 mortals (At least according to the previous editions. Things may change in 3e, which is ok.) Not to mention the number of 1st Age Artifacts they have. (Again, this may change in 3e...)

                As for your concerns..."Sigh"...I'm with you on them.


                Originally posted by Tchokes View Post
                On Charms

                When RPing a Dragon-Blooded within the Realm one of my first doubts is if every Charm is 'catalogued' or if all Charms from the book are just the tip of the iceberg. What do I mean by that? The book has some 200+ Charms? If you are, lets say, a socialite in the Realm, you probably has memorized the ones that could threaten you in the social arena. Does that mean that when a Dragon-Blooded sees a jaded woman passing by they KNOW she has her Jade Defense up? Is it expected? Would using those Charms be expected, considered inpolite? Warlord's Convocation can be used on Dynasts aswell, it is a very powerful Charm. Is it well known? Do young Dragon-Blooded know that when they are called late at night to their general's quarters they are at risk of being turned into slaves?

                These problems arise fast in Dragon-Blooded and they pile up because they are a society, and a very old one at that. There are no more 'secret techniques' if all the Charms t heir kind possesses can be contained in a book. They would be rounded up already. People would know the taps to be sweetened before they even tasted anything in a party, and they would know that when your lover comes to you pissed as hell about something you didn't do that she was the target of Brother Against Brother. Or not? This is not a problem with the ressurging Solars, but it is a problem with the stabilished Dragon-Blooded, in my opinion. When you use Dragon Vortex Attack and shape it like a real Elemental Dragon with the Charm's expansions from the Upright Soldier, do people go "Oh! A Dragon Vortex Attack!" or do they go "AN ELEMENTAL DRAGON! BOW!". How well known are Charms, how much space for creativity do we have there?

                I would be interested in seeing how this society works. How alchemically improved are the lives of Dragon-Blooded, and from perfumes to narcotics to sexual activities, I need they should be expanded. Solars, Lunars, etc, all need the Charms and skills they have to survive. Dragon-Blooded had centuries to expand on combat-useless stuff, and that is a lot of their flavor, their frivolity and relaxed outlook on a world that is burning around them. I would like to see Charms based around carrying banners in the battlefield, architeture and buildings, cooking, sex, that others do not posses, in the Realm. If we are exploring the Realm, then it makes a lot of sense for us to see how possessing a society can affect demigods, what would they do with their power.

                I know most Charms are designed for real utility, but there should be a few more Charms for cosmetic effects, like manipulating elements and shaping them (something the Dragon-Blooded -still- lack!), from creating buildings and works of art, something in the Water Aspect Charms that could unpoison a water supply, and something in the Air Aspect that could affect the weather or cool the room. I would be interested in learning those things, and the last Realm book was not on par with my expecations.
                I agree that, among the Dragon-Blooded, Charms would be "known" -- perhaps individual Charm might be called by different names, but at least the more "common" ones would be the subject of study and, perhaps in some of the more risque parts of society, discussed or actually displayed in very "unique" ways (ie: Cynus parties with certain "Performance Charms").

                Like you I would like an expansion of the Charm Sets (You called them "Cosmetic effects," but a "Battlefield Banner" charm would be very useful if it rallied troops and relayed information without the enemy knowing. And don't discount any architecture Charms -- especially when it comes to building Manses). I want my Dragon-Bloods, both PCs and NPCs, to be able to do great things with their Charms, no matter their chosen field/skills.

                And while we are on the subject of Charms: Grievous Wound Alteration Energy converts Aggravated to Lethal; Wound-Closing Touch converts Lethal to Bashing; there isn't one that does anything for bashing. I get that Dragon-Blooded aren't "Healers"...but come on, they should be able to heal bashing in themselves or others. "Sigh"...I house-ruled this in my game (along with many other new Charms...), but it seemed an oddity to me.



                Originally posted by Tchokes View Post
                On The Imperial City

                It has been too long since I first started playing Exalted, and I would -really- like to see an unified idea for the Imperial City. How does teh Imperial Manse looks like? Have an artist draw that! How does the city itself looks like? How are the House Manses organized? How do they look? We need a map and interesting locales for the city, far more than it was presented in The Realm. This is the heart of any Dynast chronicle, even if t he players don't live there, that's how important this place is. It deserves a lot of treatement!

                How does the Onyx Curtain works? Do Dragon-Blooded show up to eat at restaurants there like in any normal city in our world? Is it by demand? Do they walk about to relax, or is the habit of walking carelessly with a lover something you only do in the Imperial Manse or outside the Imperial City? Are there theaters? Are the performances boosted by Charms? I have a hard time believing the Dragon-Blooded would book with months notice to see a play where mortals are protagonists, and their cousin with Performance Charms can out do them in a very dramatic way. How does it work? These questions can be expanded and many more can be asked for all the districts. Crimson Delights needs to be fleshed out, and so needs the Temple District. How harsh is the bureaucracy in the Scarlet Empire, is it so crippling that it can effect heroics?
                The Dragon-Blooded or Blessed Isle book(s) (Whatever eventually comes out) can have a short section on the Imperial City -- the book(s) can't cover everything.

                However, I would like to see the Imperial City have it's own book, complete with a detailed map, in-depth history, in-depth description of each section of the city, NPCs, etc. Yes, I am asking (and dreaming) for a lot.


                Originally posted by Tchokes View Post
                On NPCs

                If I could say anything, I would advise you guys to write-in more NPCs like the princess in Thorns and Wisdom Whisperer, people you want to see, flocking around the city. From that wacky, chimera-oriented armor designer (Thundersomething, I forget his name) and his rival, Nazegger, all people who have story hooks you can go with. Many NPCs in Exalted are protagonists, and while that's what I want in a novel, that's not what I want in my game. I need a supporting cast. So aside from social ingame mechanics, I would like to see more rumors about the city and it's denizens, people you read about and go 'I would like to meet this person and solve his problem!' and not 'I would like to meet this person and embark on his quest!', because in the end, the players are the ones who should be followed, not the other way around. I am far more interested, as a player and as a storyteller, in NPCs that I can use as support for my chronicle's most varied aspects, and small concepts can turn into very fun NPCs, like the young son of a General turned historian of war in Cathak that is the family disgrace, and sails off with the PCs in their intrepid new adventure searching for a Cathak artifact, long lost, in hopes his father will accept him! He is just the guy who knows a lot of history, but he has something he cannot achieve without the players (going out of the island would take convincing from the players to the NPC's House, resources to cross the Inner Sea, and a drive to go to adventures) and in the end his conquest is not overshadowing the players. You know you have made someone happy and you have improved the Dynasty a bit by 'healing' that small part of it, with the father accepting his son, and the House recovering an artifact. I would like to see more of that.


                I will probably think of more later! But thanks for hearing so far! Of course, as always, this is not an attempt at knocking at the old material, just some ideas on how I would like to see them expanded.
                Agreed on the NPCs. If they can't be full writeups then provide more info than a name. My desire would be to at the very least provide a quick blurb on NPCs: Name (DoB, DoE, DoD, Location, Interests) -- Shorthand: DoB = Date of Birth; DoE = Date of Exaltation; DoD = Date of Death; Location & Interests are self-explanatory. If desired a "Template" could be provided, similar to the one for Gods, with a "Ranking" system, so that Storytellers could quickly provide stats for the NPCs if they need too. This would keep the page-count down, while providing more world info. Just a thought.

                More on NPCs (And things in general, actually...): When mentioning things in the NPCs background/description it would help to know why they dislike someone. For example: In The Outcastes (I know...1e here, but there are similar things in 2e...), under the Forest Witches, Cevis Ghandarva's feelings "toward the Ledaal family, in particular, remain bitter." In the Outcaste this is the only mention of the Ledaal, with no other explanation of why. In MAP-DB, under the Forest Witches, it talks about how the Sidereal caught on to what Cevis Ghandarva was doing -- so I figured that the Ledaal flushed him out. But it took two different books, from two different editions to do this. "Sigh"



                More Thoughts:

                Consistency:
                One of the things that frustrates me is that there are inconsistencies between books. Not between editions -- that's going to happen. Between different books of the same edition. One book will mention something as happening on a particular date, and another will have it happening on another date. Another book will have slightly different events than another book. Now, none of these are "In Character" -- they are up-front, and "Out of Character" writings. I just ask for consistency, as much as humanly possible, between all the books (I know that this is difficult, and that I am asking a lot...)


                A Timeline:
                Please...A unified Timeline. It helps when people want to run a game set at some other point in the History of Exalted.


                The Blessed Isle
                I would like to see the Prefectures expanded upon, and the actual number written about expanded. It would be nice if the level of detail was expanded, with which House controls/influences each detailed (be explicit -- not coy here). An in-depth map of the Blessed Isle would be nice. Individual cities, with maps provided (one-page would be fine). A calendar, replete with Holidays - both secular and religious - would also be nice (though, that would be asking a lot...). The Thousand Scales: Expand & List the different portions of the Bureaucracy -- with their overlapping functions (A convoluted "map" of functions would be fun). The Legions: A list of which legions exist, where they are stationed, who the General's are, with a quick Blurp on them (and perhaps a write-up on the most important ones). The Immaculate Order: Show other places beyond the Palace Sublime. Primary Schools: List several, with a Blurb about each. (I could go on...)


                The Houses:
                They should get write-ups in whatever "Main Book" comes out (Dragon-Blood, Blessed-Isle, etc...), however I'd like to see a "House" book. Not sure if there should be a separate one for each House or one overarching "Houses of the Realm." Either way, subjects covered should be History, Movers/Shakers, Interests, Prominent NPCs, "Black Sheep," Family Trees, Unique "Powers" (Charms, Sorcery, Artifacts), Alliances, Enemies, House Secrets. Among other things.


                The Satrapies:
                Expanded upon, more added. Tell us who they are, what their culture is like, who lives there, what their interactions with the Realm and the surrounding region is like. What do they do? Provide NPCs, History, a compelling story for us.


                The Hundred Kingdoms:
                Yes, Virginia. I want a Hundred Kingdoms book. I want little ones, medium ones, big ones. I want pastorial, peaceful kingdoms. I want tyrannical overlords, and benevolent rulers. I want intrigue. I want...Adventure!


                Exalted:
                This is the most important thing for me. I want that excitement back. I want that Warm Fuzzy Glow that I had when I first picked up Exalted "Way Back When." Can I get that with 3e? Please?


                ~ N

                                I remember that the first edition Dragon Blooded book stated the charms were so cheap because they had been refined and retaught over many generations over and over. It seemed pretty clear that there was a limited number of "known" charms and I suppose they would be catalogued well. If I am misremembering, I apologize.

                                Personally I slightly prefer their charms to be something you could go read up on in a library. They would be called by the names in the book or ones similar to them, just as there would be dozens or even hundreds of entirely non-magical techniques taught to smiths or scribes with names ending in "Technique" or something like that.
                                Instead of calling it folding steel, it might be called "Folding Steel Technique" or "Binding the Metal Pages Treatment" or something like that. Such names were created for entirely non-magical procedures, to help the students memorize and remember every step. Just like such names make it (weirdly) easier for us to remember specific charm names.

                                So, to conclude: I think they'd be called by those names, and be research-able but would somewhat sink into the background of the conversation. Many Dynasts may have to go to the library to figure out which of "Dropping Snow Technique" and "Shaping Hand Style" was referring to a way to prepare eggs and which was a Craft charm.

                                Basically people proficient in something would recognize the names of the things they've either been studying or preparing to study. To everyone else, it's just professionals being pretentious.

                                                        Gotta say, as I have in previous threads like this, that the 'intuitive knowledge' aspect of 3e... and the removal of things like Charm manuals... is a huge mistake and is not authentic to the feel and atmosphere of previous editions.

                                                        I feel that the stimulationist argument of 'Oh, but this makes the world not make sense' is pretty weak as well. We never had any issues with logic embolisms while we were having great fun in the heavily wuxia-influenced world it portrays.

                                                        As I said to John in the first thread where he announced this rather impressive change, Ninja Scrolls was literally like THE movie that enabled me to put my first group together - 'Just like Ninja Scrolls!'. And the way they were trading techniques, discussing each others unique techniques, all of that was massively evocative and interesting.

                                                        Getting rid of that, rather than somehow embracing it and making it 'cool' in 3e, was a huge mistake that I will immediately fix with homebrew and retconning.
