    Dragon-Blooded design and charm space for 3E

    07-01-2015, 08:49 PM
    So for some time now I've been looking over the existing 2E and 2.5E Dragon-Blooded charm set and thinking of the possibility of moving it out of an ability bound system. Ever since I first had the chance to look at Infernals and how they work in 2E, both in structure and on a mechanical power level, I thought to myself 'This is exactly where I'd want Dragon-Blooded to be.' I think giving the Dragon-Blooded 6 charm cascades(one for each element and one for general effects that could suit any elemental flavor such as Elemental Bolt) better reinforces the idea that Aspect does not dictate role (like Castes do). It also frees Dragon-Blooded from being sold short as 'could have been Celestial's and allowing them to be more varied and interesting in how they approach a given situation.

    So my questions are as follows:

    To the developers: Will we see any changes to the Dragon-Blooded in regards to the abilities being tied to only one of the elements?

    To the community: Are there any other Dragon-Blooded fans interested in this sort of design space? Perhaps enough to join a combined homebrew effort?

                      Originally posted by Erinys View Post
                      They're actually going the opposite direction and making Infernals Ability-based. Putting Infernal charms into a small number of very large charm-trees apparently caused mechanical problems.
                      I actually thought Infernals sat in a realm comparable to Lunars in 2E in many ways. The mechanical problems of their broad charm structure was one of them, in addition to the issue of making Infernals into micro-Yozi. As a result, I have no complaints with ability based Infernals.

                      For Dragon-Blooded in particular, however, my aversion to ability based charm trees is due to several charms having multiple elemental options but still having surcharges based on the ability's aspect instead of the charm's. If out of aspect surcharges either go away (which would eliminate a lot of bean counting that only serves as a reminder of weakness), or become mitigated by say, a keyword for allowing a malleable charm aspect, it would go a long way toward dismissing my reservations. As it stands now though, having a Fire Elemental Bolt invoking a elemental surcharge to a Fire aspect for not being an Air Aspect, just seems silly to me.

                      If the writers allow some wiggle room in charm/ability aspect I'll be happy. More importantly though, I would be overjoyed if we could remove all blanket failure conditions from their charm space. It would be a shame to let them go another edition defined by their weakness instead of their uniqueness.

                                                  Part of my love for Dragonblooded comes from the idea that they have elemental kung fu in *every* Ability.

                                                  Which is why I like them having Ability based Charms, because that structure guarantees them that they get to have what is to mortal medicine as fire kung fu is to simple brawling. They are the guys who know in their soul the essence of flame, rock, sea, wind, creeping branch and choking vine, the ones in who this essence flows and they use it to perform feats of superhuman ability.

                                                  (Note the Immaculate Martial Arts fit into this as Dragonbloodedness squared - they're extra potent ultra concentrated essence of elemental kung fu, what the Dragonblooded whose very nature is elemental kung fu in everything would see as elemental kung fu.)

                                                          I definitely feel that each Aspect should have abilities related to it's nature. But I think that, as with all game design, it's all very well being clever with what your mechanics say about something, but they key aim for all gameplay is obviously to facilitate the game and give players options while retaining the focus on the milieu. So I'd expand each Aspect to cover a slightly broader base of abilities (what they are now, plus two or three more), then give picks, then give free picks. Because then you are giving people elemental flavour and also options, which seems like the best course. If that causes overlaps between the Aspects, that's not necessarily a bad thing!
