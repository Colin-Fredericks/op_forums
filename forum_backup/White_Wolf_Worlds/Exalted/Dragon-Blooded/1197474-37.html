                                            Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post

I assume they're automatically freed and then treated like a commoner Found Egg (Razor or Coin)... but I'm guessing the former owner is entitled to compensation?                                             Who would dare put a price on a mild inconvenience caused by a miraculous decree of the Elemental Dragons? Who would have the temerity to claim any ownership whatsoever upon the newest Prince of the Earth, even in their absence? And who would be foolish enough to view an Exalt as a lost slave instead of a potential adoptee (formal or informal)?

                                            The Scarlet Dynasty is legalistic, but their faith decrees that Exaltation as a Dragon-Blooded is an Elemental Dragon-backed statement about the Exalt's rightful place in society. It would be sacrilegious to make a claim as petty as slave ownership in the face of that.

                                            The really interesting question is: who does the slave owner have to bribe to claim that the new Exalt is part of their bloodline?

Or if they were Patrician owned, there might be the option of Dynastic adoption with the Patrician house being compensated.

                                                        So it's basically like in the United States, if one's slave is discovered to be somebody that was actually free, with corroborating documentation?

                                                        ​At least, that was the impression I got from 12 Years a Slave; that part of why Benedict Cumberbatch's character won't hear anything of Solomon Northup being a freeman is because he's still making payments for him, and when Solomon walks off with his friend carrying his identity papers, Michael Fassbender's character gets no compensation.

                                                        Originally posted by Dex Davican View Post
                                                        The really interesting question is: who does the slave owner have to bribe to claim that the new Exalt is part of their bloodline?
                                                        ​Hmm, I was wondering about the question of whether a Dynast would want to, but considering the barriers to adopting commoner or foreign outcastes, I suppose that they might actually be expected to. That and it represents the rare opportunity in which a Dynast or patrician might be the first one to note the person Exalting, and in circumstances private enough where it might not be reported to a prefectural official that could pass it to the Imperial Service*.

                                                        ​Buuuut it also does strike me as a circumstance that would go beyond just bribery and forging records. Isn't the social circle of a Dynast or patrician going to be well aware that they have not had a child for the past 9-20 years? Even if there's a claim that the child was kept completely isolated and home-schooled for that period, it seems to me that a lot of people who might try it will have been publically living in a manner that will indicate that such wasn't the case.

                                                        ​It seems like the kind of thing in which you'd need to pay for the silence of a lot of people, or else just rely on the hope that everybody in your social circle will politely ignore the fact that it's basically impossible for you to have had a child in that time. Or hell, what if you did?

                                                        ​Unless it's a question of claiming that they're part of your bloodline in the sense of being an Exalted child of your mortal fifth cousin six times removed that none of your friends have met but who very politely offered their one Exalted scion to a household better suited to handling their needs?

                                                        ​* Note to self on other circumstances in which that might happen: a Dynast on a hunting trip deep in the wilderness who happens upon some poaching peasant children, one of whom Exalts (raises ominous questions about what might be done with the other ones). A Dynast woman having been around the Threshold for long enough that when she happens upon some foreign outcaste, she can plausibly claim that the child had been hers and with her the whole time, and she's only just returning with them now that they've Exalted.
