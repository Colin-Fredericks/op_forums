    Dragon Bloods Work Better as a Party than Solars or the Inverse Cleric Problem

    03-12-2014, 01:14 PM


    The cleric dilemma in DnD is the mechanical fact that nearly every party needs a cleric, but not every group has a player that wants to play a cleric. Something of an inverse to this occurs in Solar games, but not as much in Dragon Blood games. I think that running Dragons Bloods works better than running Solar circles at the gaming table because the system is set up to favor Solar specialization at the cost of a smooth game whereas Dragon Bloods castes tend to be more balanced against each other. This premised on the notion that players want some equality of table time, or game attention, while playing the game, an idea that I take as granted for the purpose of this first post.

    As people are aware, the current system of rules are quite flawed. The system makes combat, which is probably the most system-heavy activity in the game, way too complicated. The result of this complication is that combat-focused characters and their players receive a greater amount of ST attention than other characters because the combat monkeys’ turns take so damn long to resolve. Furthermore, STs must gear their combat encounters generally towards the higher end of the character spectrum then towards the average character combat competency to ensure that a challenge is actually a challenge to the whole group. While a chain is only as durable as its weakest link, a Solar circle is generally as strong as is toughest Dawn. By itself, this observation is less than remarkable, but these problems are greatly aggregated by the specialization of Solar castes. Solar castes are strongly encouraged to specialize in narrow fields, include combat for the Dawn caste. As a result, Dawn caste characters and their players receive a significant and disproportionate amount of attention both during combat and during the planning of combat encounters. This negatively affects the enjoyment of the players of other castes who either must pay a premium in out of caste charms to be effective (that is, have an effect) in combat, or must endure playing second fiddle to Dawns for much of the game session. Even when the non-Dawn Solar invests in skills and charms sufficent to be a dynamo alone against mortal enemies, which will typically be the case, the non-Dawn is still likely to be a second stringer when fighting supernatural foes, fights that will likely be most of the fights rolled out in the game.

    In contrast to the Solars, Dragon Bloods do not have one particular caste that specializes in combat. Instead, the Dragon Blood castes each have a separate combat skill. Although not all the combat skills are equally effective, throwers are likely to do less damage than close-combat junkies, because no one caste has a total combat focus Dragon Blooded characters are more likely to be in closer pariaty to each other in terms of combat effectiveness. As such, Dragon Blood games are likely to develop a more equal share of table time between different characters and players. Of course there still be combat focused Dragon Blooded who will more effective than other Dragon Blooded but this will be less of a problem both because the Dragon Bloods will not be tied to a particular role by their caste (the “cleric problem” that someone needs to fill a role because the mechanics of the game demand it, rather than because that’s what the player wants his character to do) and because Dragon Blood combat charms develop more slowly than Solar ones. By this latter statement, I mean that DB combat charms do not escalate in power as quickly as Solar ones do and as such a DB journeyman of combat skills will relatively closer in combat ability to a DB master of combat skills than a Solar journeyman would be to a Solar master.

    Because of this, Dragon Blood groups will be better for players because we can expect Dragon Bloods to have greater equality in combat time and therefore play time in general.

    However, the analysis does not end there. As a follow up, it is reasonable to ask if the Solar game can be rehabilitated to ensure greater equality in game time. I submit it can through player education, ST oversight, and the development of a simplified combat system.

    The relationship between character mechanics and resulting table time are not intuitive. Solar players, particularly new Solar players, must understand how their mechanical choices may affect their table time. In so doing, Solar players should realize that the inherent specialization of their castes may have a negative effect on the game itself. Leveraging mechanical advantages in the game may have the unintended result of diminishing the fun in the game. The solution on the player side is for the Solar player to downplay specialization by having non-Dawns become more proficient in combat and Dawns branch out a bit into non-combat skills and such. Avowedly, the goal would not be to ensure that non-Dawns have equal combat proficiency with Dawns, but rather to ensure that non-Dawn characters are simply not totally and completely outshone by their Dawn comrades. If anything, the onus should be placed upon Dawn players to stretch their wings outside their assigned sphere both because the mechanical advantages of specializations are compelling and because the importance of combat will likely be self-evident to non-Dawns but it is possible that Dawns may equally understand why it is important that their characters spread out their abilities. In that regard, the importance of player to player communication in setting up characters cannot be understated because a Dawn player may be understandably wary of expanding into a field covered by another caste. A Dawn player would reasonably be upset if his character expanded into, say, Sail, only to discover that the Eclipse has focused on that.

    Storytellers also play an important role. The Storyteller should carefully govern character creation and development to ensure that the game runs well. I cannot stress enough that this sort of oversight must continue past character creation. It is quite possibly, indeed even likely, that a character’s relative combat table time may not be apparent until several sessions into a campaign.

    One final recommendation is that the combat system should be simplified and streamlined such that combat takes less time. This will free up more time for other aspects of role playing and make the table time of combat less important. As it currently stands, combat takes way, way too long even when there is a parity of combat skills between characters. Obviously the new system is being developed with this in mind.

    This concludes my primary analysis. I am sure a lot of people will point out points that require clarifications or raise alternative ideas.

    As a topic of discussion, I am interested if people have some idea on what proportion of combat charms should be in a Solar’s charm set, particularly at character creation. Is there some way to make a general assessment about what percentage a Dawn’s charm should be combat charms versus a non-Dawn’s percentage of combat charms?

      Personally I always found that the Dawns got the ass-end of the ability list.

      Generally speaking, of Archery, Martial Arts, Melee and Thrown you really only need 1. Outside of very specific encounters, you're probably not going to find too many situations of, "Sorry, but you don't have the right Combat Ability so you'll basically be kind of worthless this scene."

      So the Dawn has 4 combat Abilities, 3 of which are essentially redundant. Then he gets 5 Favored abilities to try and do everything else non-combat related.

      Meanwhile the other castes spend one Favored Ability on Melee/Martial Arts/Thrown/Archery, maybe another one on Dodge (allowing them to easily hold their own and compete with the Dawn in most combat situations) and then have plenty of options for non-combat situations.

      When I run Solar games it's very rare for someone to actually want to play a Dawn, simply because the only thing they do well is combat, and even then it's not going to be much better than another Caste who picks up a combat Ability.

                                  The focus on just one combat ability is an aggravating factor to the problems I'm discussing. That means more and more complicated charms and combos which in turn mean more table time for resolution of the fighter. Indeed, this is more of a problem for Solars because their charms are so much more powerful, both natively and how they grow in power relative to DB charms.

                                      As a side to the original post, you'll find the screen time/skill investment is proportional to the standard Physical/Social/Mental divide, which is inverse to the net impact on the environment/setting. or in other words, Mental focused character get the least "screen time" but when they do (Craft, sorcery, lore) they have the largest setting impact, making things that destroy cities, casting spells to destroy nations, creating new nations of whole cloth.                                                   Our group was talking about this recently, though from a different angle.

                                                  There are 25 Abilities. A single Solar gets to effectively favor* 10 of them. Throw out Abilities that don't apply to your game (Sail, sometimes Survival, sometimes Bureaucracy, etc.) and you're down to about 20-22 Abilities depending on Caste, etc. So right there -- bam! -- a single player is eating up half of the Abilities. Then throw in 3-4 more players and issues with character point-buy optimization theory crafting. Things get tight pretty quickly and the Charm picks become really important at differentiation.

                                                  Then introduce issues like; two players have Judge's Ear Technique, or Lock Opening Touch, or Hypnotic Tongue Technique, or Respect-Commanding Attitude. What do you do now? Take turns activating them and feeling useful?

                                                  Or the opposite problem where you have 4-5 players with widely different competencies. Good luck consistently getting through sessions where everyone feels useful.

                                                  * Sorta. You get where this is going, though.

                                                      I think the best way to look at this is to use the basketball team metaphor. Just because you have Michael Jordan on the team doesn't mean the rest of the team sits there with their thumbs up their asses, and good defense and teamwork can carry a team that lacks a reliable star player. It's okay if some people are better at combat than others as long as everybody has something to do and there are distinct roles to be played, not all of which necessarily require a mega-badass. In Legends of the Wulin, I played the weakest fighter in the party and was consistently useful in a support role, with the occasional personal score. Everyone has some baseline combat ability in that game, and I built for the role I wanted to take, but a lot of it was just that the game was designed to support that sort of play, which Exalted traditionally has not.

                                                      One of the things that makes DBs good is that their charm trees tend to have some combat applicability stuck in there regardless of what they are. This was an intentional choice to emphasize their martial bent, but it's a good principle in general for a game where combat and combat ability are taken for granted. I am curious to see if 3e dips its toe in those waters in the Solar charmset. D&D is actually also a good example of this methodology, particularly 4e.

                                                      Ultimately, though, this is the kind of thing that has to be addressed when you're designing your combat system, because you can only do so much to patch it with powers. If you need to keep 30% of your build choices sunk into combat stuff to remain functional in combat when you're partied up with two combat-heavy characters, that reflects a fundamental issue with the way the game was built.
