                Dragon-Blooded Charms take the Ability in question and puts it through the lens of the element, as the OP described. Sometimes, it's fairly obvious, like Thrown. Sometimes, somewhat less so, like Bureaucracy and water (which I feel is saying something like 'water is like coin and bureaucratic power in how it flows and shifts from person to person). Really, its an arbitrary choice based on slotting the Abilities into the five elements. And that's fine -- a lot of design comes down to arbitrary choices. Some people will key into them better than not.

                It's interesting looking back at the 1e ST guide, actually, and seeing how they were initially thinking of any Ability getting any element. Probably cut that idea due to word count, but it sounds like it's coming back in 3e; every Ability will have every element. Honestly, while it does take more word count, it's not that bad. I once started a project to do the same a few years back. A lot of Charms already there make more sense to me as different elements. Each element doesn't need to be equally represented in each Ability either; some Abilities may favor some elements over others, and that's fine.

                As for how the Charms in 2e represent Dragon-Blooded power? Dragon-Blooded are people of pan-elemental power. While they will favor one element over the rest due to their aspect, their aspect is somewhat more primal in nature than a celestial caste; aspect and the Abilities it's linked to will not match perfectly, what a particular Dragon-Blood peruses as their skills. So, you can easily have an Air swordsman who, while being Air-aspected, has learned a lot of Fire charms. And that's fine, since Dragon-Blooded are pan-elemental Exalted who draw their power from all the Dragons, not just one.

                    The Terrestrial Exalted are soldiers. All of them, every aspect. For the Solars, most of the fight-y bits were put into the Dawn Caste. For the Lunars, their warrior guys are the Full Moons. Sidereals are weird, but they DO have have a Battle Auspice.

                                                "What is a Dragon Blooded? A miserable little pile of elements! But enough talk... have at you!" - Lyta

                                                My group and I were never big fans of this aspect of Dragon Blooded. We all generally liked strong visual themes in our characters and tended to find Dragon Blooded all over the place. When I make a Wood Aspect, I don't really want to be shooting out fire or ice. I want my powers to reflect the fact that I'm Wood Aspect. So when I use a charm like Bellows-Pumping Stride, I don't want to leave flaming, fiery footprints in my wake. I'd much rather be leaving growths of flowers or grass with each step.

                                                So we ended up reskinning just about all the Dragon Blooded charms so that the visual effects of the charm reflect the Aspect of the user, because we thought that was a bit cooler.

                                                That worked well enough for us as a quick fix.

                                                    That's how I prefer DBs as well. I worked with a couple friends in making each Charmset kind of 'closed', having offense and defense and some social capability within all Aspects (like 1e Air signaled towards with Linguistics and Thrown); worked like a charm, and it is how I prefer to see the Dragon-Blooded. They should be pretty much Benders, as well, just more with specific techniques than as freeform (and it is arguable how alot of Avatar works, as well; the air-ball was Aang's technique, the earth-armor seemed to be Toph's signature, and so on.

Presence is not only because Fire is choleric; it certainly has presence, illuminating, catching the eye, and even going so far as to sear the vision of those who stare for too long. It would not fit in any other aspect. Same for... most others.

                                                    (Pretty much all of them but Earth have some good claim at Athletics and a couple others, however.)


                                                    As for the Children of Gaia thing, I think the thinking is that Third Circles makes them seem mundane, lesser, and being Children makes them the same ineffable sort of being as the Incarna, thus defusing precisely the sort of comment HK-87 made earlier. They are actual Exalted, from an actual powerful patron, not an Exalted-like thing made by lesser beings.
