    Originally posted by Colin_Fredericks View Post
    Torture in the real world provides the torturer only with whatever the victim thinks the torturer wants to hear. Shadow-Immolating Talon gets you the truth or the fact that your victim doesn't know. This isn't just fantasy-world torture, it's a description of the fantasy that torture proponents have about how torture works. Fast, effective, and without any serious risk of false information. That's why it bothers me. It's not the effects of the charm per se that are problematic, it's how close those effects are to real-world misconceptions that ruin lives.
    I just think this is a ship that's already sailed. If "torture doesn't work" isn't part of an RPG's core rules it's going to be effective because players can see the outcome of their dice rolls to do things like persuade people and figure out of they're lying. I guess the GM could ignore the dice rolls and have the information be false anyway but that's betraying the player's trust to make a statement.

                Originally posted by Colin_Fredericks View Post
                Shadow-Immolating Talon is a charm where you hurt someone for information. It instantly forces an honest confession out of someone to whom you deal health levels of deal damage, even if you kill them in the process. It even refunds the cost and doesn't need to be reset when your target doesn't know the answer. You can immediately try it again on your next target.
                I have access to the text.

                So first off I have an objection to your reading of it. The Charm triggers on a successful attack roll that has to beat Defense and Resolve. The wording is actually a bit unclear if you effectively need to beat both combined or separately. It says, "If the Dragon-Blood’s successes on her attack roll exceeds her target’s Resolve,..." which I would assume means successes after Defense subtracts from your rolled successes, but I can see an argument for rolled successes being need to compared to both independently. Regardless of how hard it really is to pull off though, it doesn't matter if you actually deal damage or not, just that you land a hit with enough successes. As far as I can tell, you can use Gambits as well, so you could Disarm or Grapple someone instead of dealing damage (making the Lasso of Truth image very valid).

                So, yes, a character that wants to torture people could use this Charm to make themselves "better" at it, but it can be used by people that aren't really into torture too.

                Your critique also doesn't separate physical violence influencing social interactions as a whole from torture. Most people would not define, "I demanded the truth and punched them once, then they told me the truth," as torture. Certainly morally questionable, but in most people's minds torture is more systematic; and doesn't even require physical violence. So there's still a lack of unpacking on this unless "any use of violence to obtain information in any way," is torture, but that's a hard definition to function with. Which creates a problem with this:

                Torture in the real world provides the torturer only with whatever the victim thinks the torturer wants to hear.
                This comes from studies of interrogation techniques (which I agree is a valid and important finding that people need to understand), which can last hours, or days, or weeks, or years. Nobody in the real world thinks you can walk in and slap someone and suddenly they'll tell you everything you want to know. People believe that a confession signed after six hours of psychological tricks played on a suspect that's been held without contact with a lawyer or anyone but police has validity; which is something that needs to be pushed back against. People think that you can waterboard someone three times a day, for a week, and they will tell you something useful; which is something that needs to be pushed back against.

                There's such a big disconnect between "I attack once and inflict a mind control effect on someone," and "systemic psychological manipulation," that you're not really addressing here.

                Shadow-Immolating Talon gets you the truth or the fact that your victim doesn't know.
                It tells the player that. I don't see where the text says the character knows that. If the Charm doesn't result in an answer to the question, the player might know the difference between not beating their Resolve, and the character simply not knowing, but how does the character actually know more than the Charm didn't work? The player might know the difference between it working properly, and a Defining Intimacy allowing the target to manipulate the answer, but the character doesn't know that. You seem to be translating the assumed mechanical transparency of Exalted with in-character knowledge.

                Fast, effective, and without any serious risk of false information.
                But there is a risk of false information. If your roll beats their Defense but not their Resolve, the Charm is spent but the target can answer however they want. If the target has a Defining Intimacy they can give you false information. This claim seems to ignore a fairly major set of conditions in the Charm text. A character having higher Resolve than Defense is a serious risk (especially in combat where they might be dealing with Onslaught penalties to their Defense but not Resolve), and lots of Defining Intimacies are a serious risk of getting in the way. Trying the "where is the Scarlet Empress" trick on someone that has a Defining Intimacy towards protecting the Realm's secrets whether they know about where she is or not can let them purposefully mislead your character and send them into all sorts of trouble that your character thinks is true because they trust the Charm.

                If something is so important that a character is tempted to use this Charm on random people, it seems strange that anyone that might know something wouldn't having a relevant Defining Intimacy to throw in the works.

                Running around punching a few thousand people to learn absolutely nothing undermines the idea that this Charm supports the idea that "fantasy torture always works," since... all you've found out is that a major mystery is a mystery because people don't know what the answers are. And you have a few thousand people now pissed at you for punching them (or the far worse backlash if you've been stabbing people over this). Your character is going to have a lot of social problems if that's what they decide to do when they get this Charm.

                Like I said, I don't think that whomever wrote this had this intention. I hope they change things before it goes into print.
                What, precisely, would you like to see changed in the Charm? It's a lot easier to get behind advocating this as a group - and thus more likely to succeed - if there's a positive ask rather than just, "fix this to my personal standards."

                ...or doesn't know, and you got some bonus dice and double 10s on damage for free.
                This again, seems to be a mistake in reading the rules. The character has to ask the question first, and if the target can't answer, the power goes off - so it 'works' but instantly refunds so there's no effect at all, including the bonuses to the attack. "If a character doesn’t know enough to give the Dragon-Blood an answer, the attack gains no benefits and the cost of this Charm is refunded." So, basically, the Charm protects you from wasting it on people that can't answer (since the player can't know that ahead of time).

                Edited to add: also this is not really what I came here to talk about in the first place...
                As noted, this is a pretty serious thing. That's why you care about it. Torture is a horrible thing, and we want the game to be responsible in how it handles it, so a claim of handling it irresponsibly is going to be a focal point even with other things said.

                As well there's some community context as well. During the 3e Kickstater with the previews for the different Exalted types, there was a lot of acrimony generated over a certain Abyssal Charm. Even after the developers acknowledge the wording was communicating their intent poorly and tried to clarify it, the game (which wasn't even close to out yet) had forum arguments constantly go into "the devs included a rape ghost Charm!" I at least perceive a desire to avoid repeating that; hence a lot fo the push back in this thread and my desire for a more thorough examination of your stance.

                                        People would have long since stopped using torture as a means to acquire information if it didn't work somewhat. The trouble is that you need to both break the will of the victim and do so in such a way that they know the only way to make the hurting stop is to tell the truth. On top of that, the torturer doesn't know whether if the torture has successfully extracted a piece of truth or if the victim has merely put together some plausible lie to try and make the pain end. Thus any information obtained under duress has to be collated with bits of verifiable information from other sources to see if the information fits with reality, potentially bits wrung from other torture victims This, of course, is complicated by all the other problems of questioning people, such as contaminating witnesses with outside information, determining if you have someone with information in the first place, etc.

                                        When it comes to Exalted, torture is part of the genre conventions. It's barbaric and savage. It's a staple of despotic overlords to have dungeons where they keep prisoners who they want to see suffer or who might still have useful information that must be extracted at the point of a hot iron. I would not be surprised if some Exalts have turned their talents towards such work.
