                        Now, for the last Ability, we have War.

                        I certainly like the name of Tactics Mean Everything. It adds bonus dice and rerolls 6s.

                        Excellence of the Dutiful General lets situational bonus dice on Strategic Maneuver rolls into successes that don’t count towards the dice cap. Tireless Footfalls Cadence enhances one’s army by letting the user ignore penalties for their fatigue or movement through difficult environments in Strategic Maneuver rolls, as well as fatigue-based penalties for the duration of the fight if their Maneuver beats that of an opposing general. That, on its own gives a nice picture of a basic advantage of the army led by Dragon Blooded, being able fast through marching longer and going via routes that other armies would need to go around.

                        Roaring Dragon Officer appears to have some incomplete editing. If I’m reading it correctly, it was originally written to reduce penalties for placing a command action in a flurry, and not penalizing Defense, with Earth Aura ignoring the penalties entirely. Given how the non-crossed out text states that the basic effect lets a command action be placed in a flurry (though not with an attack), I’m guessing that somewhere along the way it was recognised that command actions can’t be put in a flurry; Earth Aura now gives the capability that the basic Charm previously had.
                        Fire has a Charm with Blazing Courageous Swordsman Inspiration. I remember, ages ago, when there were descriptions of the problems with the mass combat system, how it emphasized being the general who led from the front, and how that worked well for Dragon Blooded but not for half of Solar concepts and basically none of Sidereals. Mind, considering how War was an Earth Aspect Ability, it arguably didn’t really fit for them either. Anyway, here’s a Fire Charm to adopt that quality, getting non-Charm exploding 10s on command rolls to give orders that use Charisma or Appearance. The Aura allows one to apply their bonus dice to the commanded battle group’s Willpower roll to prevent being routed.

                        Army-To-Mob Assault is a Water Charm that allows one to enhance a battle group under the Exalt’s command attacks another battle group, so that they can convert up to their Manipulation in bonus dice granted by commands into automatic successes, and adds to the difficulty to resist rout if the enhanced attack empties the enemy’s Magnitude (pop pop).

                        Through Wood they can use the Choking Weeds Tactic, the idea being that it controls special raids on the enemy’s logistics to weaken them and shore up your own side, mechanically conveyed in increasing the threshold of an opposing general when they’re contesting Strategic Manuevers, while allowing the winning Dragon Blood to benefit from a penalty to the enemy battle group’s Join Battle roll.

                        And Air gets Changing Winds Cunning, letting non-1 failed dice on Strategic Manuevers get rerolled, with an Essence 5 repurchase to let them spend Willpower after rolling a maneuver (and before rerolling the failures) to change the stratagem is they want it.

                        So, even before the Signatures, War gets a full spread of elements. That’s nice and all, but I do kind of hope that Earth is more prominent on the other side.

                        Well, to get there. First is Storm-Calling Strategos: create a magical stratagem to control weather to make enemies deal with storms and… this feels familiar, was this previewed? Well anyway, impose an environmental penalty on an enemy army’s physical actions, with a lesser penalty on singular enemies, and nothing to the Dragon Blood and their allies. Ah yes, I definitely remember this part; when the user’s Initiative is high enough, they can pay a bit to hit the other battle group with something like lightning or an avalanche as an environmental hazard.

                        Ah, here’s another return of an old classic as a Signature Charm: Ramparts of Obedient Earth. It’s been made a good deal more mechanically intricate and specific compared to how it used to be; for one thing, it specifically tied to commanding an army, since it can only be used while leading them through a stratagem. Join Battle can be rolled with Intelligence + War, with successes creating a pool of points to spend on shaping the battlefield. Sinkholes can be created to make difficult terrain adjacent to a battle group and force them to act last in the turn order for the first round of battle, with a point cost equal to the target’s Size. 4 points can be spent for creating barricades as an instantly successful Fortifications stratagem. 7 points can be used for sapping an enemy’s Fortificiations to negate their benefits, not need to treat the terrain as difficult, and reflexively advance one range band forward (as well as circumstantial benefits defined by the Storyteller). And a hefty 10 points can be used to reshape the entire battlefield; if the commander successfully enacts a Strategic Placement stratagem, they have freedom to add basically any earthen features to the field that they want, with the Storyteller adjudicating the particular effects of any given added feature, and the only limit being unable to directly harm enemies. This one I think is particularly good to systemize and focus (while still being able to do a lot); I think in the past it was a bit too broad, and could be use too easily to overwhelm singular opponents.

                        Deadly Wildfire Legion lets an Exalt expend their Fire Aura across their commanded battle group to add flame to the edge and tips of their weapons as they charge, adding non-Charm dice equal to Essence to the command action for orders, with the successes not only adding to their attack roll, but their damage as well. The group can finally take the action to fulfil the command immediately, rather than waiting for its place in the Initiative order. It can only be used once per scene, but gets reset by an allied battle group incapacitating a major enemy or routing a battle group.

                        Fog-of-War Misdirection is Mute, creating an eerie fogbank to lure and trap an enemy within, a magical stratagem to create that imposes a -2 penalty on vision-based rolls and attacks made from medium range or further, and a -1 penalty on checks for rout for battle groups caught within the fog itself (although also slightly penalising actions to target the ensnared group… wait, no, that’s a penalty on somebody trying to give them orders, that makes more sense). If the battle group does rout, the Exalt who made the forgets half its Size (but no more than their Essence) in Initiative. The fog can’t be dispersed through natural weather, but magical means are effective. While it can only be used once per story, it can be reset when one roleplays a scene to gain an advantage that adds dice to Strategic Maneuver actions through deception, subterfuge or treachery. For Water’s final Signature Charm of the game, it’s quite appropriately dickish.

                        And Wood gets Hidden Thorn Treachery, letting them spread out spies to become deeply embedded in enemy ranks. It creates a magical stratagem to have a traitor infiltrate enemy ranks, although only possible when the Dragon Blood already has a Strategic Manuever bonus from spies or traitors. If successful, they can reflexively reveal the agent’s presence at any valid point in combat (although the Storyteller can adjudicate impossible betrayals, barring an appropriate stunt). It also can’t be used on groups with Might 2 or higher or perfect morale. When the betrayal is declared, the battle group immediately falls apart from the reveal of a notable and high-ranking member as loyal to the enemy (unless that would be somebody narratively important and difficult to believe). If a commander manages to rally them, they can do… something, I need to reread the battle group rules to understand what this does. Something to do with only getting part of the force, while the rest goes to the Dragon Blood who used the Charm, while the group not being rallied means that they all go over.

                        Just a couple of pages left. Enfolded in the Dragon’s Wings is Earth, and allows one to fortify their group’s ranks and lines, rolling a special command actions reflexively for an allied battle group within short range that has been attacked, with every two successes adding to their Defense and soak. It can only be used once per scene, but reset by successfully rallying, and at Essence 5 its range can be extended to medium.

                        Fire gets a couple now, with Phantom Fire-Warrior Horde to expend a level of anima to create ranks of illusionary warriors to fill in gaps in a force, adding half their Essence to the Magnitude (pop pop) of a group when it’s rallied for numbers. The result of the rally roll can also be applied as a threaten action against all groups that see the illusory warriors come into existence, with those whose Resolve is beaten retreating until successfully ordered to do otherwise. Then it’s Indomitable Flame of Valor to ignite their courage, doubling 9s on rolls to rally or rally for numbers, and grants perfect morale for a round to a group with elite Drill; at Essence 5, 4 Initiative can be payed to extend the duration of the perfect moral to the full scene.

                        And the very last Charm is the Essence 4 sample, Earth Charm Blessed Dragon Champion. It’s another that requires a group led under a successful stratagem to use, after which 9s are doubled on all War rolls (the Charm lasts for a scene), and the user has increased Defense against battle groups. Non-Charm dice equal to the size of the largest battle group that the user commands to all attacks against enemy battle groups. So here, at the end, is one to let one basically wear their army.

                        I’m surprised at the lack of Charms to enhance artillery here, but I’d say if word count was limited, those would be among the lower priority. As a whole, War does very well despite the removal of Dragon Vortex Attack, having very effectively shifted the focus back to being an exceptional commander of troops.

                        Not much to say about the Earth Aspect as a whole. They’re ultimately… I’m not using a pun. The Abilities align well with the concept.

                        I can’t really say any more about the Charms as a whole that isn’t already evenly distributed. It’s a grand piece of work.

                        I’m ready for the final preview!

                        I have approximate knowledge of many things.

                                And now, the final preview has come out. It’s almost as long as the prior one, but should come in an easier to digest form.

                                First a chapter fiction, taking us back to the year of… the setting’s default year. Hmm, my initial impulse would be that it would depict the familiar scene of an adherent travelling to an Immaculate dojo in search of training, but the end of the first paragraph makes it more apparent that it’s somebody going off to deal with illicit worship.

                                It seems a bit on the nose to have a fertility god trying to compel an Immaculate monk (one born to House Cynis, no less). I do like the idea of the god being non-binary gendered. Plus, when the fighting starts, the little idea of a god who doesn’t need to breathe not really appreciating that until they begin suffocating.

                                Also a bit of affirmation about why one might oppose a corrupt god, both practical and rooted in Immaculate doctrine. I like some of the mystical elements of what the god employs to fight.
                                And there’s an interesting mix of ominousness and kindly instruction in the final line having the monk respond to “I yield” with “Good, but will you obey?”

                                I’m surprised that this didn’t feature Righteous River, although on the other hand she was rather prominent in the opening fiction. In any case, it’s a nice introduction for the Immaculate Dragon Styles.

                                On that note, into the chapter proper, and the introduction to those styles.

                                So it’s the same note on how the basic function of the styles was to afford a better means of beating the gods into line, with a bit of emphasis on how they arose from collaboration between Terrestrial martial artists and Sidereal instructors, a bit less one-sided. Now, what are some of the distinctions in how they work…

                                The styles have their aspects and benefit from Aura (with a note on how non-Terrestrials don’t get one), knew that already. Hmm, the demands of the styles mean that, besides Dragon Blooded, anybody who would be restricted by the Terrestrial keyword can’t learn the Charms. It gives Solars and Sidereals as examples, but that would surely also include Lunars and Abyssals and the like, only really leaving out Liminals, most small gods and probably more than a few Exigents.

                                They have no Terrestrial or Mastery keywords of their own, also known. Ohh, here’s something interesting; there were always references before to a benefit of getting deep into an Immaculate Style, but I could never fully parse them, and they seemed a bit dull. Whereas now, learning up to the Form Charm confers the benefit of being able to expend an Aura whose element corresponds with an advanced style while in the Form of any style, to ignore the Terrestrial keyword on all Martial Arts Charms for one tick. That’s a very interesting way to represent the unique benefits of learning these styles and how they align with the Dragon Blooded Essence, plus it plays nicely into the picture I had of Terrestrial limits depicting one straining their hardest and coming just short; that with the right boost, they can stretch a bit past that limit. Neat idea.

                                Given the strong ties between the styles and the Order, it’s good to have a sidebar elaborating on where and how they’re learned. I’m suddenly imagining a Dynast who went off to the Threshold to be taught in the dojo of a renegade, and how that might affect their reputation back home.

                                And then it’s into the styles… eh, they’re surely shorter and more accommodating than a full Ability cascade, and the introduction was short enough. I can squeeze Air Dragon Style in.
                                Teaches one to take on the qualities of the wind, utilises a lot of acrobatics (apparently more for the awareness it gives one of their own body, rather than manoeuvring in its own right), and mastery of breath control, with an emphasis on distant attacks. Fights with chops, spinning kicks and the chakram, compatible with light armour, and benefits from complementary Occult, Dodge and Stealth.

                                Air Dragon’s Sight improves spatial awareness through air sensitivity, ignoring half of one’s Perception in onslaught penalties or environmental penalties to Evasion, while surprise attacks inflict no penalty (ambushes still work). Being in Air Aura confers the first bonus that nobody else, even the Solars, can enjoy; being able to ignore one’s full Perception in those penalties.
                                Cloud-Treading Method lets the user ignore penalties for moving through difficult terrain, in concealment, and other environmental issues, as well as going across surfaces that would otherwise be unable to bear them (although not to stand still on them). Air Aura extends that to liquids, or even vapours besides the air; I’m suddenly picturing one being complemented by somebody shooting a lot of fire into a body of water to create a cloud of steam that can be ran across.

                                Wind Dragon Speed lets one make special hit-and-run strikes by flurrying a disengage action with an attack, without the usual penalties, with a disengage that effectively carries them out of range of all enemies (besides the crashed and the trivial) not causing Initiative loss. Air Aura allows a disengage to instead be flurried with an aim, but only for the benefit of Air Dragon attacks.

                                Breath-Seizing Technique is a much lengthier Charm than the rest. Let’s see… needs to have Initiative 12 and up, makes a decisive attack, and before rolling for damage they get to roll the attack roll’s successes against the target’s soak (with minimum damage) for withering damage. There are some caveats based on Overwhelming damage that I don’t quite understand on the face of it. The user doesn’t acquire any Initiative lost by the target, but does benefit from Initiative break for the decisive damage if the enemy is crashed. It also has some interaction with Lightning Strike Style that I would need to review. As a couple of final notes, it can lower the amount of time that somebody can effectively hold their breath, and has a compounded effect against spirits, causing them to lose motes as well as Initiative on the withering attack (although not working on things that categorically do no breathe, such as zombies and machines).

                                So it turns out that not every Dragon Style Charm benefits from Aura, meaning that there will be effects in which Solar and Terrestrial users will be equal.

                                Then there’s the Air Dragon Form. While in the form, withering chakram attacks treat their range as close for the Accuracy benefits, regardless of their range, and the user benefits from a bonus to Evasion, disengagement and Stealth. It can be activated reflexively when the fighter deals enough withering damage to place an opponent’s Initiative below their own.

                                Shroud of Unseen Winds allows one to benefit from a bonus to Stealth rolls, either in dice or successes depending on how high the user’s Initiative is. It also lets one stunt to be able to roll Stealth without a usual hiding place, with Air Aura letting one add successes without need for minimum Initiative (save not being crashed) and waves the need to stunt for concealment in the open.

                                Avenging Wind Strike is used to double 10s on a decisive damage roll, and with success can knock an enemy prone and back, with sufficient damage increasing the knockback distance from one range band to two. Air Aura lets them knock an opponent back two bands regardless of how little damage is done, but lets sufficient damage increase them to three bands.

                                Lightning Strike Style is used to extend the range of decisive attacks (to a maximum of long) and adds… I’m a bit unclear, I believe that it’s saying that Essence + Strength gets rolled, and the successes of that roll are… no, wait, it’s saying that extra successes on the attack roll up to a maximum of Essence + Strength get added as extra dice to the damage roll. The electrical discharge of the attack also allows it to ignore the Hardness of metal armour. Air Aura also lets it ignore the cover of metallic objects and structures, as the lightning is simply conducted straight through them, although full cover can give a benefit to their Defense.

                                Thunderclap Kata allows one to clap their hands to create a shockwave that forces everybody within medium range to make a Resistance roll against it as an environmental hazard to inflict bashing damage, which can also target dematerialized characters (and does worse damage to them), and has the cosmetic effect of being audible for miles. It can be used reflexively if one enters Air Dragon Form with at least 20 Initiative.

                                Tornado Offense Technique is a promising name. Ah, but it lets one (figuratively) become a tornado, rather then creating one. The fighter selects a range band within their weapon’s range, and delivers a decisive attack to everybody within it. It still requires aiming at a certain range, but it rolls a flat one-third of your Initiative in damage against every enemy that gets hit (which will be everybody with a Defense lower than the single decisive attack that you need to roll, so one should probably feel free to… do the Dragon Blooded have no Charm to add dice or successes to their general Martial Arts rolls?), in addition to extra dice equal to Perception for those who get the attack unexpectedly (and trivial characters just getting the full Initiative). Furthermore, Air Aura lets one add the number of enemies that were struck to their base Initiative after resetting, to a maximum of their Essence.

                                This one is pretty damn strong, arguably among the best multi-attack Charms in the game. It probably benefits best from a bit of manoeuvring and maybe some allies to herd as many opponents as possible into the same range band, but so long as there are more than three of them, it pays for itself, and even if there are three or fewer, having to only roll to hit once might make up for the reduced rolled damage. I mean... unless you’ve used one of Air Dragon’s other Charms to enhance stealth to conceal yourself before making the attack, meaning that you can add dice to every damage roll made against those opponents.

                                You know, I was gearing up to reserve judgement on the styles until I had seen all of them, and kind of leaning towards “they’re basically functional”, but this Charm here, and the manner in which it can synergize with others means I’m calling it early; Air Dragon Style alone has become truly impressive now, compared to how the Immaculate Styles were written in prior Editions that failed to capture my imagination.

                                And there are still a few to go. Wrathful Winds Kiai makes a gale-force shout, rolling Stamina + Martial Arts as an unblockable withering attack against everybody in a certain arc out to medium range, with no Accuracy bonus but a raw damage of Strength + the user’s Initiative, plus threshold successes on the roll, with a minimum of their Strength. Everybody that gets hit is knocked back and prone, and crashed enemies can’t make a movement action on their next turn. The user doesn’t gain any of the Initiative that enemies lost, and their own 10s on the damage roll lead to… sorry, thought of something, lead to them needing to roll a die of decisive bashing damage against themselves… or against the one that was hit? The latter would make more sense, but I think the wording could be cleared up. Spirits take decisive damage as a matter of course, and can be hit even when dematerialized. The Charm gets reset by incapacitating a threatening enemy.

                                Yeah, another synergy I realised in the Charm; the number of effects to knock people back, and render them prone. Jump about pushing people into position, pinning them down, and then throw your chakram to hit all of them with a devastating attack. Beautiful.

                                And last is Hurricane Combat Method, an actual Essence 5 Martial Arts Charm. It functions as a permanent enhancement to Air Dragon Form, letting one add a surcharge of 6 motes and one Willpower to enhance it, increasing the Evasion bonus and doubling 9s on Stealth and movement rolls. Once per round, they can pay five Initiative to reflexively make a withering or decisive attack that doesn’t count as their attack for the round, and can be used to clash. It’s a nice final benefit for the Style’s tactical approach.

                                So yeah, Air Dragon Style gets the ball rolling well and sets a very high bar. Good work there.

                                        Okay, I am surprisingly psyched to see the rest of the styles. Two more now, Earth and Fire.

                                        Earth Dragon is described as being about overwhelming force with meticulous deliberation, moving slowly and surely. Training involves hardening the body with things like beating up barrels of gravel and sleeping on beds of nails (incidentally, one thing that I did always like about the Immaculate styles were the descriptions for their training regimens). The result creates warriors who can withstand deadly blow and answer them back with tetsubos. The style’s unarmed strikes are said to be solid and powerful, often delivered with elbows and knees, as well as two-handed blows. It’s compatible with all armour, and is complementary not only with Occult (which can probably be safely assumed to be comprehensive to the styles) but Athletics for feats of strength.

                                        It starts with Stone Dragon’s Skin, letting one ignore half their Stamina in wound penalties to Parry, as well as adding to natural soak against withering attacks, and a bit of Hardness against decisive ones. It has reduced costs depending on how low the grade of one’s armour is, down to being completely unarmoured.

                                        Force of the Mountain lets one add dice to raw withering damage, or double 10s on decisive damage rolls, with Earth Aura increasing the amount that can be done for withering attacks. Stillness-of-Stone Atemi is used to increase the Overwhelming value of withering attacks, and gives the option to pay Initiative for each 10 that shows up on the damage roll to level a mobility penalty on the one who was hit until their next turn ends, or, if the attack crashed them, until they recover from crash. Earth Aura lets them also spend Initiative for rolled 9s.

                                        Unmoving Mountain Stance is used when the fighter is hit by an attack, but before it rolls damage, letting them reduce the Initiative that enemies gets from hitting them with withering attacks and… I think there’s an error there, since it says that it both does and doesn’t affect the Initiative that attackers gain (unless withering attacks gave default Initiative just for successfully landing the hit that I’ve forgotten about). It also makes Smash attacks or similar to shift them fail automatically unless it deals a minimum of withering or decisive damage (different values for each). Earth Aura adds to the Initiative denied to opponents, as well as how much damage needs to be done to knock them back.

                                        It comes to Earth Dragon Form, with which one becomes able to add half their Strength to attack rolls for Smash attacks or ones made on prone enemies, and adds full Strength to natural soak. The form can be reflexively entered when an enemy with lower Initiative manages to hit them.

                                        Ghost-Grounding Blow is a main attack for hitting spirits, used after crashing one with a withering attack to instantly force them to materialize and becomes incapable of dematerializing or using Hurry Home until a number of rounds equal to the user’s Strength have passed following the spirit recovering from crash.

                                        Following that is a lengthier one with Earthshaker Attack. When they have Initiative of 15 or higher, they can make a single decisive attack against all characters in short range, which is unblockable, undodgeable, and can’t be clashed, and requires those hit by it to roll Athletics to maintain their balance. Those who fail are knocked prone and suffer dice of Hardness ignoring bashing damage equal to the attacker’s Strength, and doesn’t reset the user to base Initiative. Battle groups that lack Might fail the roll automatically, and enemies that take at least 3 levels of damage are knocked back a range band, suffering damage equivalent to a short-range fall. Earth Aura allows one to divide their Initiative between opponents and add it to the Charm’s base damage, which will reset them to base. The Charm can only be used once per scene, and is reset by crashing an opponent while they’re prone.

                                        Earthshaker Attack isn’t quite the revelation that Tornado Offense Technique was, but it looks like an impressive Charm to strike multiple people in its own right.

                                        Shattering Fist Strike is used to enhance disarm gambits to actually break weapons with strikes, completely destroying mundane weapons and expending Earth Aura to make a gambit with the difficulty increased by the targeted Artifact’s rating to render it inoperable until repaired. I’m always going to be a fan of dramatically breaking magical weapons, so I like this Charm being here. It also lets one perform feats of demolition with double 9s and in the span of a miscellaneous action rather than necessarily longer.

                                        Following that is Weapon-Breaking Defense Technique, which allows a reflexive clash attack on a close range assault that applies the effect of the prior Charm, without counting as the combat action. Winning the clash adds the attack roll’s threshold successes to the Initiative roll. The Charm doesn’t function to clash unarmed or other Natural attacks. Being in Earth Aura allows one to clash Artifact weapons without needing to expend Aura, and the Defense can only be used once per scene unless reset with dealing 7 or more levels of decisive damage to an enemy.

                                        For their Essence 4 Charms, we first get Avalanche Method, which is rather costly, an Instant Charm that requires a whopping twenty motes and one Willpower. The Charm’s user needs at least 12 Initiative to use it, and the target must have a mobility penalty of at least -1 (I’m unsure if that includes penalties inflicted by Charms). The user gets to continuously make decisive attacks against an enemy until they’ve made a number equal to their mobility penalty, or miss, and each attack loses Initiative equal to the damage inflicted, only resetting to base when all attacks are made. If it’s used to land a smash attack, the enemy won’t be knocked back until the barrage is finished, with repeated smashes compounding how far back the character is flung, and being able to suffer falling damage if sent back to medium range or further. It also won’t free an enemy trapped by Hungry Earth Strike, while still being able to suffer falling damage from how far they might have been flung. Finally, being in Earth Aura allows one to use the Charm to its fullest even if an attack misses.

                                        I feel as though the Charm might as well be limited to hitting an enemy with a mobility penalty of at least -2, since it’s not really worth it to use the Charm to hit somebody once, but apart from that it looks like a solid Charm.

                                        Hungry Earth Strike was another good looking classic, standing out from the others for its evocative and readily comprehended effect. A withering attack (that must be either smashing or directed at somebody who is prone) can be made with double 8s on the damage roll, and if it succeeds in crashing the target (and they’re standing on rock or soil, or sent there by knockback), then the target gets sucked into the ground. While trapped, the character can’t take movement actions, suffers the penalties of being prone (which would include being vulnerable to several other Charms in this style), and each turn inflicts a die of Hardness-ignoring bashing damage. Spirits will suffer the effects of Ghost-Grounding Blow while they’re trapped, and anybody who is incapacitated in that state will die, being drawn into the earth. Buried characters can attempt to break free using an extended Strength + Athletics action, with the attacker’s Strength as the difficulty and their Essence + Strength as the goal number, with allies able to make their own rolls and adding their successes to the total towards the goal. Being in Earth Aura allows the user to bury an enemy without crashing them, so long as the attack reduces their Initiative below that of the user. It also needs to be reset, by incapacitating an enemy from a decisive attack made with 20+ Initiative.

                                        Last is Perfection of Earth Body, another enhancement to the Form Charm. Paying the same surcharge as Air Dragon Style had, the character becomes capable of adding their Stamina to withering or decisive damage for attacks to smash or against prone enemies, becomes immune to wound penalties, and the effects of crippling attacks. If the character is unarmoured, the soak bonus from the Form Charm is increased, and they gain Hardness.

                                        Earth Dragon Style doesn’t quite have the same apparent tactical hook that Air Dragon did, but it does have some noticeable synergies, and it comes together well.

                                        The styles are actually long enough on their own, so I’m going to post thoughts on them individually, but won’t be as long between them as I was with the Abilities.

                                                Fire Dragon Style, go!

                                                In that spirit, it’s description is shorter. A combination of unpredictable violence and self-discipline, trained by… huh, performing drills and practices in time to music, I think that’s new, they used to be repeatedly set on fire. They style is based on alternately reserving and unleashing force, unarmed attacks being rapid barrages of punches and chops and powerful kicks, also using short swords (traditionally paired), and compatible with light and medium armour. It has no other complementary Abilities.

                                                Flash-Fire Technique represents a finely honed combat instinct by being able to roll Join Battle twice (paying enhancement Charms separately for each), taking the higher result and letting them make a decisive attack on the first turn with extra successes on the attack roll (up to a limit of Dexterity) to the damage dice roll.

                                                Searing Edge Attack focuses killing intent through the strike in the form of painful heat, letting withering attacks cause those struck with them to lose additional Initiative, that the Exalt doesn’t gain.
                                                Flame-Flicker Stance is used to convert up to Essence 1s on an enemy’s attack roll into extra points on their Parry, with successfully blocked attacks from enemies with lower Initiative not inflicting onslaught penalties. Fire Aura removes the limits of how many 1s the Charm’s user can benefit from.

                                                Perfect Blazing Blow can double 9s on decisive attack rolls against enemies with lower Initiative, upgraded to double 8s against crashed opponents, and with Fire Aura letting each doubled success add a die to the damage roll.

                                                Now, what of Fire Dragon Form? Against enemies with lower Initiative, their attacks inflict onslaught before they’re rolled, and when the fighter successfully parries a decisive attack, it doubles the Initiative that the enemy loses. The Form can be reflexively adopted when the Exalt wins Join Battle.

                                                Yes, I’m seeing Fire Dragon’s distinct methodology here; certain benefits to Defense and withering attacks, building towards greater power on enemies with lower Initiative. It’s nuanced, but still very direct and straightforward.

                                                God-Immolating Strike is their spirit attacking Charm, letting a decisive attack deal aggravated damage to spirits and treating their Initiative as though it’s a number of points higher equal to Essence for the sake of the aforementioned Charm that function off of lower Initiative foes. When a spirit is damaged, its next turn has it roll… I’m not sure if that’s the user’s or targets Essence, but whichever one rolls that number for Hardness-ignoring aggravated damage (a number that dematerialized spirits add 2 to).

                                                With Essence-Igniting Nerve Strike, the fighter can light their foe’s Essence to burn them from within. A withering attack against a lower Initiative target; the Initiative reaped from the target is halved, but every 9 and 10 that show up on the damage rolls remove one or two motes, respectively (with the Charm’s user deciding if they’re personal or peripheral). If the enemy is crashed, the burning motes burst out of them, rolling one lethal die for every two motes, up to a maximum of the attacker’s Essence, and ignoring Hardness. If the user expends their Fire Aura, they get to reap the full Initiative from the successful attack.

                                                Overwhelming Fire Majesty Stance lets the Exalt really start to reap their patience, become wreathed in flame that imposes a penalty on enemy attacks on them, and causing those that do manage to succeed at close range to suffer a Hardness-ignoring die of lethal damage, although foes can pay Willpower to become immune to the attack penalty for the scene. Fire Aura allows the Exalt to waive the Charm’s Initiative cost and make it lose the Perilous keyword.

                                                Fiery Blade Attack is fairly self-explanatory; successful decisive attacks ignite a bonfire that burns the victim without fuel so long as they remain within it, where escape requires a disengage action (with a set difficulty of 2 if they’re not disengaging an actual enemy).

                                                Breath of the Fire Dragon makes an unblockable decisive attack based on Stamina + Martial Arts against everybody in a cone out to close range, plus an extra range band for each 10 Initiative (another example of reserving energy to be unleashed dramatically), to a maximum of medium range. It can also strike the dematerialized, and rolls a third of Initiative against everybody, that ignores Hardness. If anybody is hit, the attacker resets to base. Enemies that take sufficient damage ignite, suffering the user’s Essence in dice of lethal damage that ignore Hardness each turn until they put out; dematerialized spirits take aggravated damage and are made visible, unable to extinguish themselves unless they materialize. It can also light flammable scenery. It’s reset by gaining at least 15 Initiative on one tick.

                                                Smoldering Wound Attack is used when a decisive attack hits, but before its damage is rolled. Every 10 on the damage roll lets the attacker roll a die of unsoakable withering damage, adding to the fighter’s Initiative when they reset to base (unable to gain more than their Essence). If used on a crashed enemy, the additional rolled damage is also decisive, but still adds to the user’s Initiative. Being in Fire Aura allows one to use the Charm after the damage has been rolled (i.e. when the player knows how good of an effect it will have).

                                                Last is Consuming Might of the Fire Dragon, with which a martial artist can enhance Fire Dragon Form to let them treat their Initiative as though it is increased by a value equal to Wits for deciding turn order, as well as for the purpose of Charms based on having lower Initiative. The fighter’s withering attacks gain half their Strength as an Overwhelming value, and decisive attacks add one to their damage rolls. Enemies that hit them at close range take a die of lethal damage. As enemies are incapacitated, the Initiative, Overwhelming and decisive bonus, as well as the damage taken by attackers, is increased by one.

                                                Initiative has really done a lot for Fire Dragon, creating a metric by which the reserved and unleashed energy can really be measured, and thus giving the concept a lot more texture and verisimilitude. Its goals and synergies are even more readily grasped than Air Dragon, even if I think I still like that one a bit better. All in all, this is another nicely done one.

                                                        Now, to see what was done with Water Dragon Style. It’s used by monks, there can’t possibly be any duplicity, deception and manipulation here, right?! Just… making people drown in their own blood… well played Water, you magnificent bastard.

                                                        But yeah, the principle of the style is described as being in tune with the flow of things, rhythm, blood and Essence, so that they can redirect or impede those flows, with fluid defence and repeated and exploitative offence. Its unarmed attacks are based on swift sequences of punch, kick and claw strike, and uses tiger claws for weapons. It’s compatible with light and medium armour, and like Fire Dragon has no other complementary Abilities.

                                                        For a start they have Flowing Water Defense, letting a full defence be flurried with an attack without penalty, and Water Aura allowing successful attacks to refund the Initiative cost of full defence. Rippling Water Strike is conversely used to increase the Overwhelming value of a withering attack, and can use Dexterity in place of Strength for determining raw damage (which should have a nice reception ); if a foe is crashed with the attack, all the rest within close range take an onslaught penalty. Water Aura allows one to add their Dexterity to the usual raw damage calculation, rather than switch out Strength for it.

                                                        Speak of the devil, Drowning-in-Blood Technique; redirect somebody’s blood flow into their lungs. Of course Water would govern one of the more horrifying effects in the game (at least in concept and character). Decisive attacks double 10 on their damage rolls, and if they deal at least three levels, the choking on blood results in an increase wound penalty until the end of the scene or they crash the one that did it to them. Spirits are also susceptible to the technique through the flow of their Essence, and can have the wound penalty increasing effect stacked up to the user’s Essence. In their Water Aura, the martial artist adds half the foe’s wound penalty as bonus dice to the damage roll.

                                                        Shrugging Water-Dragon Escape gives double 9s on a Martial Arts based roll to resist grapples, disengage rolls, opposing enemy rushes, or rolling to escape constraints, with Aura providing a point of Initiative from success on the roll. Then it’s Water Dragon Form: bonus dice equal to a target’s wound penalty are given to attack rolls, and Stamina is regarded as double for soak calculations, with the capacity to enter the Form reflexively when enough decisive damage is inflicted on an enemy to increase their wound penalty.

                                                        Ah yes, an aspect of Water’s bastardry that I forgot to consider; theft. Theft-of-Essence Method can be used when an opponent is crashed to steal some of their motes (based partially on the wound penalty of the crashed enemy), adding them to the type of pool that they were taken from.

                                                        Hmm, Bottomless Depths Defense, previously one of the strongest defensive options available to Dragon Blooded. Now, they use it to roll their Essence + Stamina after being hit by an attack, and subtracting the result from damage successes, with a minimum of one removed even if no successes are rolled. Alternatively, Water Aura can be expended to negate all damage, so yeah, still one of the stronger defences. That’s offset by it only having a per-day use, unless reset by being hit with three decisive attacks without taking any damage. The only thing I regret is that it doesn’t have the flavour text of shunting that damage to the Elemental Dragon of Water anymore, but that’s a very minor quality.

                                                        Essence-Dousing Wave Attack is described as attacking with a dark, smothering Essence, that quenches the flame of an opponent’s battle prowess. Used when a decisive attack on a crashed enemy inflicts at least the target’s Essence in damage, then one of their ongoing Charms can be deactivated, so long as it belongs to a combat Ability or combat-based physical Attribute (I’m guessing the same basic parameter applies against the rare opponent with only Essence-based Charms). For an especially strong boost, Water Aura can be expended to use on an opponent that is not crashed.

                                                        Flow Reversal Strike is another one to interfere with somebody’s bodily fluids, reversing their course in the form of making an opponent roll Stamina + Resistance in response to being hit with a decisive attack, with a difficulty of the attack’s threshold successes, and where failure results in rolling extra decisive damage equal to the shortfall, and after the damage is inflicted, also inflicting withering damage equal to the current wound penalty (which the attacker does get). When used on spirits, their wound penalties will subtract successes, rather than dice, from the initial Resistance roll. It gets reset by crashing an enemy whose Initiative was higher than the Exalt’s before being attacked.

                                                        Hmm, we’ll be getting into some big ones now. Crashing Wave Style is this art’s access to multiple attacks; withering attacks can be made in sequence against a single enemy until an attack misses, the enemy is crashed, or they’ve inflicted enough of them equal to the target’s wound penalty plus one (when used against battle groups, it can keep going until it misses or depletes Magnitude (pop pop)). When in Water Aura an enemy being crashed is dealt dice of lethal damage equal to their onslaught penalty, ignoring Hardness. It can be reset by landing a decisive attack on a crashed enemy, and then building Initiative back up to 12 Initiative.

                                                        While Ghost-Restraining Whirlpool allows one to generate a maelstrom that draws in spirits; those within medium range cannot move away unless they disengage, nor can they use Hurry Home. Initiative lost by disengaging is transferred to the Exalt, as well as being able to steal two Initiative from each spirit that did not spend a turn moving towards the Exalt or being in close range of them. If the spirits are dematerialized, they’re even more susceptible, with the whole range of the Charm being difficult terrain, and suffering dice of Hardness-ignoring bashing damage if their Initiative is stolen. Water Aura extends the range to long, and the Charm can be activated reflexively when entering Water Dragon Form.

                                                        Finally there is Tsunami-Force Shout, which is not an enhancement to the Form Charm. Instead, it’s a special form of decisive attack; it requires at least 20 Initiative to use, and rolls an unblockable attack using Stamina + Martial Arts against all enemies in a cone out to medium range (although if the prior Charm is being used, the Shout extends through the entire whirlpool). The attack deals bashing damage to each enemy that is hit equal to the attack roll’s threshold successes multiplied by the struck person’s wound penalty, plus one, ignoring Hardness. Battle groups are considered to have a -4 wound penalty, and the attack doesn’t reset the Exalt’s Initiative to base (although as well as the cost in motes and Willpower, it has a rather hefty cost of 10 points anyway). An enemy damaged by the Shout will be knocked back and prone, although spirits that would be forced beyond the range of the Ghost-Restraining Whirlpool (which they cannot go beyond) will take damage as though they experienced a short fall. Enemies that take three or more damage will go back two bands and suffer short falling damage, unless it’s a spirit knocked into the boundary of the whirlpool, who takes it as a medium range fall. It can be reset by landing a decisive attack that resets Initiative, and then building back towards 20.

                                                        I’d say that Water Dragon is similar to Earth; less built around a specific internal tactical setup than Air and Fire, and more about applying conditions and exploiting circumstances, which I would say is very fitting to the concept. I’d also say that the quality of messing targets up painfully is applied more consistently throughout than the prior Editions.

                                                        On a final note before going off into Wood, I want to say that I especially like the many and varied ways that the styles can be particularly brutal towards spirits, while still short of having effects to permanently kill them. In general, it’s very effectively plays up the idea that an intended function of these styles was in dealing with unruly spirits, while it doesn’t interfere with my prior observation of generally lacking such power, and the dynamics that would present in the setting’s history; the plausibility that there are more than a few spirits that have been subjected to pain, humiliation and death from the Immaculate Dragon Styles, and can still be around in the Time of Tumult to be salty about it.

                                                            To finish off for the time being is Wood Dragon Style, the most mysterious of them. It can oversee cycles of life and death, as instructed through gruelling regimens of being brought to the brink of death, fasting to the body’s limit and consuming copious quantities of hallucinogenic and entheogenic drugs, bringing strange insights when they recover. They utilise pressure points and Essence flows to harm foes and heal friends, and we’re given a hint of their dread final power. The unarmed attack are described as precise finger jabs, as well as staves. The bow is still here, but with a twist; the martial artist is trained to wield it in close quarters like a staff, and while the style’s attacks don’t work through shots, the fighter doesn’t require a ready weapon action to switch between methods of using it. The style is only compatible with light armour, and is complemented by… Performance for distraction and misdirection. I would have sworn Medicine was in there, but have to remind myself that these Abilities aren’t often directly about what is used for the Style, but what helps them.

                                                            Wood Dragon Vitaliy strengthens the martial artist’s body, adding Martial Arts to their natural soak, and subtracting a die from decisive attacks, with the former bonus increased by Essence and the latter subtracting half the Essence in dice when in Aura. The Eyes of the Wood Dragon can instead see Essence paths through the body for precise strikes, adding Perception in raw damage to withering attacks, and letting a piercing attack by made, with Aura allowing the latter to ignore the usual Defense penalty.

                                                            Mind-Over-Body Meditation allows the Exalt to fix themselves up, rolling the lower of Essence or Stamina and applying the successes to heal non-aggravated damage, with a minimum of one, although it can only be used once per scene (no reset condition) and can’t be used when not fighting.

                                                            Soul-Marking Style is the start of messing with people’s souls, driving a thorn of Essence into them. If a decisive attack managed to deal damage with the Charm, then the fighter benefits from aiming for the remainder of the scene, so long as Wood Dragon attacks are used. Spirits hit with the thorn suffer a slight crippling penalty for the scene.

                                                            The Wood Dragon Form is activated by the Dragon Blood striking their own pressure points and chakras to align their Essence, gaining the lower of Essence or Stamina in temporary -1 health levels, that are kept for the scene even if the form is left, although when they fade at scene’s end the damage is transferred to the normal health track. It also allows for Mind-Over-Body Meditation to be reset by landing a decisive attack from at least 12 Initiative. Reflexive activation comes from using that Charm or similar effects to heal enough damage to reduce one’s wound penalty.

                                                            With Spirit-Wracking Method, withering attacks against spirits double 8s on the damage roll, but don’t grant the attacker the Initiative. They instead get to roll a die of Hardness-ignoring lethal damage for every 10 on the withering damage roll. A spirit crashed by the attack is wracked with agony, doubling wound penalties and the crippling penalty of Soul-Marking Style until they leave crash.

                                                            Death-Pattern-Sensing Attitude allows one to apply full defence against enemies hit with Soul-Marking, and ignores a penalty to Defence from their surprise attacks. If the fighter is ambushed by a marked person, a point of Willpower can be included in the Charm’s cost to be able to block the attack, although only with a Parry of half their Essence, which no effect can raise.
                                                            Spirit-Rending Technique allows one to tear apart unruly gods; a spirit that is Marked can be hit with a decisive attack with the value of Willpower added to the damage roll, dealing aggravated damage, with the attacker gaining motes equal to the spirit’s Essence if the attack incapacitates it.

                                                            Oh, Unbreakable Fascination Kata constitutes an instance in which Performance actually is helpful to the Charm. It is used to make a roll against enemies that can see the user, and any whose Resolve is beaten can neither attack on their next turn, nor move away from the object of fascination without successfully disengaging. The effect can be resisted by spending Willpower (if I’m reading correctly, it’s a little bit unclear), they’re immune to losing their attack for the scene, but still need to disengage to move away. Fascinated enemies that are Marked need to spend Willpower twice in resistance to gain that immunity.

                                                            Into the top level ones with Enthralling Blow Attack, which can be reflexively used upon activating the prior Charm to roll a single withering attack against all enemies in close range that were beguiled, even if they resisted with Willpower. Each hit enemy has a separate damage roll made, while only the highest yields points to the attacker, and damaged enemies can’t move on their next turn. If the enemy is crashed by the attack, then they experience the full effect of Unbreakable Fascination until they recover from crash, regardless of Willpower spent. Wood Aura enhances the Initiative reaped, allowing points equal to half of the user’s Essence to be taken from each person after the highest.

                                                            With Wood Dragon Succor (usable only with at least 12 Initiative), the mystic is able to lay hands on another character and rolls their Initiative, applying the successes (up to a maximum of the beneficiary’s Stamina + Resistance) to heal levels of non-aggravated damage, although the Exalt suffers one level of bashing damage for every two levels that they heal, which cannot be prevented. It resets the user to base Initiative, unless they can expend a Wood Aura to only need to lose Initiative equal to the healed health levels, down to a minimum of base. The Charm can only be used on the same person once per day.

                                                            And that brings us down to the infamous Soul Mastery. The Charm can only be used against opponents hit with Soul-Marking Style, allowing one to roll a gambit against a difficulty of the target’s Essence + Willpower, minus their wound penalty, down to a minimum of 6. If successful, the target is destroyed utterly; spirits are permanently destroyed, undead crumble to dust, and the living immediately die while their souls (mercifully) survive, immediately sent to Lethe. It cannot affect things that neither qualify as living nor dead, and can only be used once per scene. I’d say that this is about the best possible way that such an instant death effect could be utilised in this system, while still staying true to the essential concept of the Charm.

                                                            Wood Dragon came out well, managing to consolidate many of the themes and ideas from prior Editions while being less of a grab-bag of effects, and it sorted out the pinnacle Charm. Indeed, it should be evident that I think highly of what was achieved with all of the Immaculate Dragon Styles, and find their place in the lore and metagame to be secured.

                                                            Ahhh, it’s so late, but all I want to do is keep reading Charms, in contrast to the prior chapter. See, this is the problem with Third Edition Martial Arts; while they’re not necessarily short (if often a bit shorter than a given Ability), they’re immensely compelling to read due to the manner in which they internally synergize with one another, and build up towards dramatic effects. So I just want to keep consuming them.

                                                            Still, it’s probably for the best that I call it for the time-being, and the last of the Immaculate Styles seems like the best place to do so.
