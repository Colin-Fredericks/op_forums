                I have never found the "too great and vast for your tiny human perceptions" a particularly helpful handwave, because any attempt to write them is a human mind writing them. The Unconquered Sun himself is, at least to a degree, sapient in the normal way, otherwise how does he communicate with this Zeniths? Are we now to say that the Kukla possesses perceptions more vast, or a greater mind?

                Saying 'Kukla's being cannot be comprehended" feels cheap. If we want to think of GEDs as hurricanes, or volcanoes, then we need to essentially cross "sapient" off the list. Which is cool with me. Saying that there are certain forces in Creation that just are, that will wreak terrible destruction, and must sleep, is very evocative.

                I don't see it as 100% necessary though, especially in a game with a much smaller focus than 2nd.

The Maidens are "crazy" too, and for that matter, so is Luna! They're just human-shaped when they want to be so people give them a bye like they aren't some kind of inhuman monsters. (Also, you can sit them down for tea, and they'll probably listen.)

                    The Unconquered Sun is an engine of nigh-infinite divine fire too strong and too hot for Creation to actually bear. Yes, I would actually say he possesses a greater mind with vaster perceptions (and perceptions largely orthogonal to human ones) than most people, even most Solars, would be capable of. But I mean, even Solars can get to see the building blocks of what makes a person animate (at Essence 2, no less) or how compassionate they are or how far they'll go for their goals just by looking at 'em.

                    Heck, "that villain is crazy" is a stupid narrative kludge to fit any of their behavior into the confines of the story, but there's a wide gulf between "that villain's motives cannot be comprehended" and "that villain's priorities are deeply alien." You want Cenobites from the first couple of Hellraiser movies, as mad alien sensates, not the later sequels where they just murdered everyone.

                    You want a method to the madness, the Kukla as a world-destroying fishmalk is... kind of hilarious but no good for anyone.

                            Yet, the Incarnae, and most gods in general, are intended to be understandable by humans, and the gods are supposed to have relations with humans. So, while their perceptions and thoughts are different, they are sufficiently similar that they are useful to the story. Sol narratively, is derived from gods like Zeus, not gods like Cthulu. There is a conceit in mythology that says these incredibly powerful beings give a shit about little ole' us, and that we can actually deal with them, and even outsmart them or defeat them, at least for a time. Those myths are drawn upon heavily by Exalted.

                            Yeah, the Kukla is not a fishmalk; that ruins the whole thing. If he's more like a hurricane, or a super-volcano, an urge more than a person, then we are actually filling useful narrative space. The Kukla is not an enemy. The Kukla is not an ally. The Kukla is a giant plot device of doom.

                            Also, this conversation may be moot. I can't see much use to the devs in delving too deep into the GED end of things in a game largely centered on crumbling empires and desperate struggles for mortal power.

                            I do think "crazy" is tossed around a bit too much to justify evil. Course, Exalted leans on it pretty heavily (*looks at the Great Curse*).

                                I think gods should be comparable to humans in some broad strokes of their personality, emotional spectrum and relationships with one another (and the relatively small number of humans whom they wind up relating to), but are often attached to people in a general, abstract sense, can emotionally value a lot of things more than they do people, aren't necessarily interested in cultivating relationships or being part of a community or absorbing culture, and just don't get a lot of human needs and impulses.

                                Like, hardly any god has ever known hunger, even in the sense of the slight discomfort you feel when you've been too long between meals; that's a pretty serious departure from all humans, even the Exalted, right there (I don't think there's ever been a Resistance or Survival Charm published that helps with the issue of starving or dehydrating to death, as opposed to gathering food, and while it certainly fits within the range of what a Solar could achieve there are going to be a lot of players and characters who wouldn't bother with such a thing, so... good luck getting stuck in a barren cave).

                                Speaking of caves, consider the idea of a Solar who goes deep into one that nobody else has ever been in; if you encounter one of the native spirits of echos and darkness, he might have a form that very vaguely resembles you, will speak in a language that you can know (and probably with a dialect that is at least barely comprehensible), and might have some of the personality of a crotchety hermit who hates intrusions and company, but he's also a thing that has lived with contentment in the darkness for a million years, with a set of internal needs that is fulfilled by what could be found in such a place and a perception that sees what is around them in the necessary terms, and it's like... how do you relate to that? How does it relate to you? It's really really outside the range of anybody's experience.

                                You can talk to each other but, well, you can always talk to somebody who has experienced something so far outside the range of your own life that you just can't truly internalise or model what the experience of it really is, and depending on the people that difference can form a barrier against any form of more sophisticated relationship.

                                Also depends on how one thinks the powers gods have to be aware of their purview and domains works.
