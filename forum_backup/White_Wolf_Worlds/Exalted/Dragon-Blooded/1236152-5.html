                    Originally posted by aluminiumtrioxid View Post



                    This is the worst attitude to have in a party-based role-playing game. When everybody is special, nobody is.
                    Exactly. But it's what I'm used to.
                    Most (not all, but most) Solars turn up with Dex 4-5 and 5 in a combat ability. The difference of course, is the charms, but on a day-to-day level, you get exactly the thing you pointed out; it doesn't really feel you're that special when most of the rest of the party has a 5 in that ability (even if one character has 10 melee charms; after all, you've all got the same number of dice, because you all have excellencies).

                    4 of the 5 Solars in the game I play have Melee 5. None of them is supposed to be the world's greatest swordsman. But I mean, what are we going to do, not have Melee 5? How would Golden Hero get his demon-killing charms? How, more importantly, would the Adamant-circle sorceress and the Cult of the Illuminated preacher get Heavenly Guardian Defence? How would Fulgens the warlord get... actually I'm not sure he has any Melee charms beyond excellent strike. But, you know, what self-respecting warlord doesn't have Melee 5?
                    The only one who doesn't have a combat ability at 5 is the player who doesn't really like combat and wanted to be a 99% social character.

                    Admittedly, it's only combat abilities, I see plenty of characters who only have a few dots of social abilities.
                    And it's slightly better than it used to be, because everyone is fed up of the way it works like that (and I changed the character gen rules to discourage it). Yet they still do it a bit, because the rules reward you for doing it and punish you for not doing it.

                    Admittedly, I didn't really think of this until you guys started talking about it (I just put it down to optimisation), but I can see it. Decent charms not requiring 5 in abilities would definitely help.

                                I generally abide by a rule of "only one stat at 5, no ability above 3 unless it's a large part of my character's concept". Solars are actually amazing for this - you can throw your Charms around to the point of not having more than 3 in any single ability (including your Supernal), and just by virtue of the free excellencies and the strength of starting Charms, you'll still have a viable and fun to play character at the end.

                                Dragon-Blooded require a bit more finagling, but after playing them a bit (we've had like four sessions), I find that the combination of having to purchase excellencies and kinda crappy low-level Charms is weirdly helpful - you can use a good 40% of your starting Charms to start with a reliable broad expertise, and you'll still be able to meaningfully contribute in your secondary areas of focus while ceding the spotlight when it comes to challenges you're completely unsuited to. (Also, the Ability 1 prerequisite on excellencies means that you can get away with dropping a few in favor of cooler stuff in your primary area of expertise, then picking them up one by one in play on a "whenever needed" basis, because the training time is literally just "spend half an afternoon on this".)

                                                            Originally posted by aluminiumtrioxid View Post
                                                            And what does the Unconquered Sun represent?

                                                            (Hint: it's not "glowy stuff".)
                                                            Take a look at the high end solar charms for your answer.

                                                            But my point is that Solars are no more based on abilities than Dragon Bloods are. They're based on expressing the Power of the Unconquered Sun via their abilities.

                                                            Dragon Bloods are expressing the themes of their elemental blood expressed through their abilities.

                                                            I fully expect that Abyssals, Infernals, Sidereals, and Getimians will all have different ability minimums for roughly similar effects simply because each one is expressing different themes and those themes work better for different effects.

                                                            We could see infernals forge a weapon out of the air at melee 1 because it's easier to cause damage without external aid by channeling adjorjan's themes than it is for a solar to pull one out of pure excellence or a DB to create one out of elemental essence.

                                                            It's honestly pretty disingenuous to the writers to make these kind of comparisons. A better way of asking these questions would be to say: What level of mastery does it make sense for a Dragon Blood to pull a weapon out of thin air? Or: What level of mastery does it make sense for a Solar to pull a weapon out of thin air? Obviously the writers felt that a Dragon Blood had to master swordsmanship to a greater extent because that fits with the overarching themes of DB and their power structure as a whole. Whereas a solar swordsman of 'expert' level should be able to do so as well.
