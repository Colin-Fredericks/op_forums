    Originally posted by Blaque View Post
    I think an idea that the number today, as the recovered population post-Contagion Era, shows more to how potential badass it was in the past.
    I don't think it's reasonable to depict Creation as having recovered, even in human population, from the Contagion. 90% of all life died, followed by the majority of the plague survivors (due to Fair Folk.) That's ~95-99% of all animals, plants, elementals, gods, and humans. That must have caused famine, mass extinction, soil infertility, massive erosion, huge wildfires as the dead stuff dries up and burns, follow-up (mundane) disease problems as the dead bodies rot and foul the water, etc. 90% of all individual living creatures is the estimated death-toll for the mass extinction that ended the Cretaceous and killed the dinosaurs. After things like that, the real world doesn't recover (in biodiversity and ecosystem health) for millions of years.

    Now you have people who've been bombed back into the stone age (or at best the bronze age) and quite literally abandoned by Heaven, the Terrestrial gods are all fucked up, there are mutants and Fair Folk still running around killing people. The landscape is utterly denuded, much of it uninhabitable. Even after weeds and grass start to grow again (and it won't be like after a wildfire, because the Contagion would have killed off the seeds in the soil as well. It would be more like primary succession after a volcano covers everything in lava), I think it would take at least a few centuries just for most of the landscape to actually be able to support human life. The Realm got a head start in that maybe, but most of Creation realistically could not have had conditions in which human populations could grow for maybe the first half of the Age of Sorrows. After that people can start to spread out into the landscape and with the empty space, there will be some population growth. But it would be the population growth of a stone age or bronze age world that is still hugely lethal, not the exponential population increase of our modern industrial world. So maybe over the entire Age of Sorrows, the overall human population could have doubled just once.

    I doubt it's depicted this way in the books, but this is what I would consider believable, with most of the kingdoms and empires in the outer Directions being only a couple centuries old, and with soil fertility and living conditions still mostly very poor. 750 years is not enough for a species to recover from an apocalypse that extreme, especially not when most gods and Exalted refuse to actually do anything to rebuild.

    Maybe in the Blessed Isle with whatever it is they do force gods to cooperate and make the soil fertile and the weather not suck, the human population could have tripled or even quadrupled, if I'm generous. But even if the Realm was dedicated to breeding above all else, without a religion that encourages 1/3 of the Exalted to be celibate, they just couldn't have recovered the Dragon-Blooded host to their pre-Contagion population. It just isn't plausible to me at all.

    Which kind of goes with something I've thought about before, which is that the Contagion as depicted (at least in 1E-2E) is not only far in excess of what's necessary to set up a post-apocalypse setting. It actually makes the present-day setting's relative recovery implausible, and also sets up every NPC who supports the current status quo to be dumber than rocks.

                Even if you're going with an idea that Exalted were resistant to it (which isn't my understanding) for a population to grow they still need food, water, inhabitable land, shelter, infrastructure, etc. Even medicine, since the children are ordinary mortals until they exalt at whatever age, which means infant mortality is exactly as lethal as for any other humans, dependant on whatever public hygiene and medical care is available. And the Realm culture doesn't even encourage maximum-possible DB population growth.

                Also I forgot to mention that the Contagion would also have killed 95-99% of the stored seeds farmers had prepared for the next planting, and with famine looming people would tend to eat what was left, which may mean that most people lost the ability to farm altogether, and thus the landscape recovered slower than it could have with human help. Agriculture would then have to have spread slowly from surviving pockets, one crop from here and one from there... in other words I think that realistically we're talking "bombed back into the paleolithic", not the bronze age. And when you barely have enough to eat, do you bother teaching your kids how to make bronze or read? No, they're too busy looking for anything to eat. So technological knowledge would have to re-diffuse as well, or be re-invented from scratch.

                One other thing to consider regarding Shogunate numbers it that at that time, the Dragon-Blooded seem to have ruled more of the world than the Scarlet Empire does, and more directly too. That implies to me that their population was not just more spread out over the Directions, but also that it was larger, especially if (as I think) the mortal population was 10 to 100 times higher back then.

                In conclusion, I think my personal headcanon might become that the Contagion only killed humans and at most 70-80% of them, even counting those who died to the Balorian Crusade, which is still a huge apocalypse, but doesn't break the setting over its knee.

                        Wood Essence creates different premises for recovery of flora and microbes*.

                        Large sections of the devastated Creation subsequently disappeared.

                        90% of all life does not need to mean that each individual category of thing was reduced to 10%; it does not need to mean that stored seeds were reduced by that exact figure, assuming they were even targeted at all.

                        The Contagion was still threatening to the Exalted. Hell, it's the quintessentially threatening disease to the Exalted. A lot of Terrestrials could have caught it in the early stages just trying to cure it, before it became apparent that that was largely beyond them.

                        Spirits are not susceptible to disease.

                        The Dragon Blooded were probably greatly reduced in numbers; the Shogunate absolutely had more of them around than the Realm does, the depiction in the Aspect Books make no sense otherwise.

                        Depictions which, one should note, still portray the idea of about a hundred Exalted dying in a single battle to be an unprecedented disaster, even while assuming that a hundred Exalted being there in the first place wasn't a big deal in the least.

                        * Destroying 90% of most microbes won't stop them anyway. A lot of the good stuff has a doubling time of about twenty minutes; that means that in a single day, one cell becomes hundreds of trillions.

                                            On the scale of several millennia, yes. On the scale of a few centuries, it's wildly improbable to me. Not that there wouldn't be forests, wetlands, cities, etc. It is likely the Eastern lands are recovering faster. To one who doesn't know what the world used to be like, Creation's lands may appear lush and healthy and whole, the way that the second-growth brush and monocultures of exotic weeds (and piles of litter) in the park by my home looks normal to somebody who's never seen a healthy climax forest or intact wetland.

                                            But I can't conceive any way for the population levels of everything, including humans, to bounce back perfectly as if nothing had happened. Rats, dandelions, mosquitoes, bacteria (if they exist), in numbers at least (no clue if "genetic diversity" has any meaning in Creation). Not big creatures. Not the intricate webs of healthy, resilient ecosystems. Not humans or yeddim herds or tyrant lizards. This isn't a setting where the gods can magic entire thriving ecosystems or populous cities into existence from nothing. Nor do I get the impression many would try -- most gods seem to be too selfish to care about the state of Creation, and most Exalted are obsessed only with killing each other and/or getting rich.

                                            Exponential population growth in slow-breeding species (like humans or mammoths or whales) requires a big change in infant mortality, longevity, and/or age of first child-bearing. Without exponential growth, I don't think slow-breeders can restore a loss of 90% of the population in 7 centuries, especially not while their food source is also equally depopulated. In fact, if herbivores increase faster than their food source or predators can, they'll denude the landscape all over again.



                                            Also, I don't think it's entirely accurate to say that non-living systems in Creation were undamaged. The Fair Folk mangled the dragon-lines, the Terrestrial and Celestial bureaucracies are utterly dysfunctional and not doing their jobs, and there are shadowlands and Wyld zones all over. A state of nonstop warfare has its own devastating effect on the landscape.
