        Found what I was looking for in the Wyld Compass: The People of the Air number about 7000 in the far northern city of Sezakan. In addition to the God-Blooded among them, there are about a dozen DBs of Air and Fire aspect ruling in their Solar Council. Similarly, Seahome has more than a dozen DBs among its 6000 People of the Sea.

        Those numbers seem a bit low, esp considering that the Air Folk's Terrestrials descended from "several dozen Dragon-Blooded loyalists" and the Sea Folk have a policy of accepting female Lost Eggs who want to escape the patriarchal atmosphere of the West. I guess they all must have pretty low Breeding traits...

        Also, the tribe of beastmen I mentioned are the Blood Bats, who live in the jungles near Rathess, herding humans whose blood they drink. Interestingly, they have a twisted belief in the Unconquered Sun, that if he is not fed human blood every day, the sun will never set and will burn the world to ashes! So they are always on the hunt for sacrifices...There are an unspecified number of Terrestrials amongst them, who rule all the rest.

                Those statistics are calibrated for the sake of stories in general and player characters in particular, they don't reflect the reality of the setting.

                Originally posted by Seconis
                I know that it's 'not safe' for a female Dragonblood to give birth more than once every 5 years or so, but given that 300 is an average lifespan, the population of Dragonbloods in Creation should be exponentially higher than it currently is
                I'd say that the full length of their lifespan isn't entirely relevant, since I imagine that raising a Dynastic child is so effort intensive that they typically set aside long portions of their lives to dedicate entirely to that matter, and at all other times it's pushed aside. That, combined with the relative expense (not to mention the usual considerations for not having children constantly, combined with the implications of Dragon Blooded power) means that members of the Dynasty aren't really pushing to maximize their number of children in general.

                I'd also say that a not insignificant portion of Dynasts die before they have a chance to sire any children of their own. I mean, their schooling period alone... the House of Bells is a period of military training so intense that it would kill mortals, the Heptagram is four score teenagers all locked up together on an island to learn sorcery (and occasionally get used as guinea pigs by their teachers), and the Spiral Academy is where they go to learn cut-throat business practices and political corruption, I'd say a graduating class without at least three "unexplained" deaths or disappearances is considered a dull affair. Then, upon graduation, they go off to adventure through the dangerous Threshold for years or decades, from which I'd say many never return for the attraction of life free from Dynastic society alone.

                After that, you've got a singificant number who make a life long career of military service, on top of my expectation that any Dynasts who survive to retirement age having devoted some of their time in military endeavors (and a large proportion of the ones who don't make it to retirement age having died doing so).

                If we assume that the average Dynast has twenty children in their lifetime (most have a fair number fewer), I'd say that only maybe half the Dynasty has Breeding good enough for more than half their children to Exalt (with most of the rest being more like a third, with some fairly weak blooded who only get two or three floating around the periphery), and of those children who do Exalt maybe a third die before they have any children of their own, and another third die before they're halfway through their lives.

                If you're a Dynast who lives to retirement age, you've probably outlived all of your siblings, and in the majority of cases where you haven't, their own family lines have been fairly thinned out (which contributes to them being weak enough to not be murdered before they got to retirement). In general, I think Dynast families are a kind of pyramidal structure, where each generation consists of more people than the one above it since they haven't been around long enough to be killed yet.
