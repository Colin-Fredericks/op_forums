    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

    It's my view that if you're Exalted, no matter how shoddy your bloodline is, you're entitled to one Exalted child. Usually the eldest, just to account for the possibility of accidents or only wanting one child. Certainly, I can't recall any reference to a Terrestrial who didn't have a single Exalted child.

    This would allow an Outcaste to maintain an extremely thin line of descent down from themselves, but it's usually not sustainable in the long run; all it takes is one death and the Exalting blood is cut off (although your mortal children will have descendents carrying the blood, amongst whom one will eventually Exalt).
    I agree. Going by the crunch alone, a child of a Terrestrial without Breeding and the average mortal has a 30% chance of Exalting. This chance is doubled for two Exalts having a child together. Normally I don't like rolling dice for such things, but this probability feels about right to me.

                    I'm personally not up on the idea that being Exalted necessarily says anything about your breeding, and thus your odds of producing Exalted descendants. Like, I get more interest, not to mention sense of consistency from the writing, that sometimes people of the most exceptional breeding don't Exalt (but that exceptional breeding still matters for producing people who are likely to), and sometimes people with the tiniest trickle of Dragon Blood from a thousand years ago do, lacking any of the necessary factors for creating a decent nuclear family, let alone a sustainable Dynasty. In this, I feel that the Exaltation is preserved as something numinous and working on mythic logic, rather than something one can optimize like the size of cattle.

                    ​So yes, I'd feel that if you're an Outcaste, you're kind of like an extremely distant cousin who, by nigh inscrutable chance, managed to be interesting enough to be considered a member of the family, and apart from the courtesy of one Exalted kid, the rest of that line has the same infinitesimally smaller chance of Exalting that the Outcaste did.

You certainly wouldn't have mortal Realm patrician families; once your family marries enough unExalted Dynasts to produce the occasional Exalt, you have an Exalted bloodline that will never fade (barring unexpected disaster).

                        Narratively, you lose the trope of, "Alas for my people, as none of my children are fit to succeed me!" And that would be a damned shame.

                                            Well, that is somewhat true. I think that, as soldiers, they're practical enough to work alongside a Solar to defeat a Deathlord. Just because the Solar are body-snatching demons doesn't mean you should absolutely never team up with them to defeat a greater foe. Just don't expect them to invite the Solar round for tea afterwards.
                                            Certainly, you're right that they're used to teaming up with people who believe things that are opposed to their own beliefs. It's just that I think that an impression was given in 2nd ed, and I've heard this from some fans too, that they're totally cool with anathema, and that Solars can just go and hang out at Valkhawsen, which is very different and less believable than the 1st ed line that Minton quoted.

                                            Essentially, think of it as historical religions. They see them as religious enemies. 90% of the time, middle age Christians wouldn't ally with Muslims... but 10% of the time, screw the Byzantines, Muslims charge us less taxes. Or consider the 30 years war, where Catholic France allied with the Protestants because screw the Austrian Emperor, and Protestant Sweden allied with the Catholics because they wanted to conquer Denmark. The western Christian powers helped the Qing Dynasty crush the pseudo-Christian Taiping Heavenly Kingdom because it was dangerously socialist, and a corrupt Imperial monarchy is easier to exploit than a million fanatical peasants who believe they're ruled by Jesus Christ's younger brother. Or the way Christian Britain was allied with the Ottoman Muslims (until the slaughter of peasants in the Balkans got to much for them to ignore), because anti-Russian politics was considered more important than religion.
                                            And in the same way, Lookshy has teamed up many times with heathen god-worshippers and ghost-worshippers and so on to defeat their fellow Immaculates in the Realm (of course, that's reasonable because the enmity between different factions of the same religion can often eclipse what they feel about foreign religions, just because it's a more pressing problem, like the way Nestorian Christians used to fight for the Zorastrian Persians against Orthodox Christians in the Byzantine Empire, or for that matter the way today Sunni governments across the middle east receive American aid to combat Iran and it's Shi'ite or Alawite proxies like Hizb'allah and Bashir Assad).
                                            But that doesn't mean they're buddy-buddy with Anathema, they still dislike them, it's just that they haven't generally been as pressing an issue as, say, making sure everyone doesn't conquered by the Realm, or the Arzhecki, or recently Mask of Winters.

                                            Lookshy consider the Anathema evil, but they're the sorts of people who are willing to work with a lesser evil to defeat a greater evil.
                                            Whereas the Realm would never compromise (though that doesn't mean individual impious, ambitious DBs would never do so).
