                    Originally posted by Piff View Post
                    While the specific nature of Tut's handicaps is unknown to us, it is proven as of a few years ago that his parents were brother and sister, IIRC.
                    ​But that doesn't conclusively answer the question of whether or not any ailments he was afflicted with were a consequence of inbreeding.

                    ​Part of an issue with post hoc assessments of these kinds of monarchs is that they're a small sample size of very prominent figures, and I assert that there can be a tendency towards confirmation bias with them. We look at the examples of European monarchs in the modern era with mental illnesses or severe congenital disorders and scoff at it as the result of inbreeding, with minimal attempt to distinguish it from any other cause or source.

                    ​That's what I meant by popular narratives; not the case for marriage between close relatives in cases where it occurred, but the expectation that this led to severely disordered individuals.

                    ​That and what I perceive as the subject of inbreeding to start off from a position that associates it with subjects of derision, often involving monarchies or rural people in extreme poverty, the latter particularly often conjuring the image of horrifying deformities. That is another matter I am thinking of when I talk about narratives that obscure reality, even in cases where reality might be similar.
