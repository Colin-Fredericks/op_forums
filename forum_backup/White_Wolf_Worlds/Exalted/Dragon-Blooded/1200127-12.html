    #181
    04-15-2018, 08:17 AM
    All right, last Ability before the remaining seventeen come out on Tuesday. Investigation starts off with another straightforward success adder that also rerolls 6s, and advances into Tampering-Detection Technique; it makes nice use of the newly added case scene action by enhancing it to allow determination of concealed evidence, as well as having a unique action of observing an object and knowing whether or not it has been interfered with in some criminal manner recently.

    Permeating Insight also has a nice little combination of effects; it’s another that grants a non-Charm dice bonus in order to profile characters, and then grants an Investigation specialty on the person that you profiled that can last forever without needing a mote commitment. I’m getting this image of being able to develop a criminal nemesis on the fly.

    So Scent-of-Crime Method allows one to make an enhanced roll to realise that somebody in their presence has recently committed a crime, while actually figuring out who it was requires them to be profiled. I like how that’s useful, but still limited, and prompts further action. While further assisting those profile actions is Heart-Maze Navigation, allowing one to penalize a target’s Guile with Intimacies (and rapidly increase the speed of the action, if in Water Aura), which I find to be another nice instance of Water slipping between seams and cracks.

    Revelation-of-Associates Hunch is now a Fire Charm, and thus uses fire-based imagery not only as the basis of its concept of determining how many people were involved in a crime, but as the basis for how the Dragon Blood intuits it. There’s actually a nice form of connection there, that the number of criminals is metaphorically a fire casting off numerous embers that the Exalt can literally, physically feel.

    Bloodhound’s Nose Technique runs on the curious image of a Dragon Blood being able to actually pick up a criminal’s scent from a crime scene, and pursuing them through that. I’m going to go with the idea that such isn’t just a matter of Water or Wood power, but a bit of actual draconic imagery touching off of one of their Charms.

    There’s one more straightforward Charm to remove penalties, and then it’s into the Signatures. Warrant of Divine Safety actually looks remarkably powerful; you can bind somebody to their word (under the right circumstances, such as hospitality offered by somebody who is legally within a house) such that violations incur actually elemental phenomena manifesting to halt or punish the offenders. It only lasts a few hours, is confined to a certain space, and for entities of higher Essence than the user can “only” extend to giving heavy penalties or creating environmental hazards, but even within those confines it’s got clear benefits as to how it safeguards and what kinds of avenue it can open up. It’s a very interesting Charm, mystical, very overtly elemental, and with some grand story potential.

    Echoes Caught in Stone has a tough act to follow, but… it does all right. Its capacity to hear words that were spoken around a suspect object is limited, but I suspect that’s necessary to prevent it from being overpowered or bent towards purposes a bit too far afield from Investigation.

    Heh, Shadow-Immolating Talon has a neat effect in the form of enhancing an actual, physical attack with an armour-piercing question, that can compel the one struck by it to answer honestly and unequivocally (even if the attack means that they’re dying). The only recourse of the victim is if they have an opposing Defining Intimacy, and even then they only get to give an obscure or incomplete answer. It even has the benefit of not wasting the motes if the attack is made on somebody who couldn’t actually answer, although the attack is not enhanced either. As somebody whose world view is strongly influenced by the idea of incompleteness of information, this is a Charm that speaks to me; if an investigation should end in violence, the pursuer has recourse to ensuring that they actually do finally get an answer, rather than needing to kill the last person that could clarify what was even going on.

    Clear Water Prana is more straightforward compared to the other three, providing some strong benefits to casing a scene, being able to detect deliberately hidden items in that scene, and empowering them to contest magically enhanced concealment. Not much more to say about it than that.

    While Death-Unravelling Eye allows one to examine a corpse, even a heavily decayed one, without penalties related to the damage to the body, and being able to form a mental picture of it when fresh for the purposes of identification and further medical examination. Plus being able to tell the body’s process of decay, which can be used as a basis for its own deductions. I find it to be a good Investigation Charm for the manner in which it improves such queries with additional diagnostic tools and can provide additional direction to a story. It’s not an absolute thing, rather empowering the Exalt to ask questions and find answers that they might otherwise be incapable of.

    A Face in the Fog is another Mute Charm, allowing one to form a kind of visual silhouette of a criminal if one successfully cases a scene that somebody attempted to conceal. This is another one that feels as though it’s very good at driving stories, both for providing additional information to pursue and creating a capacity for a good “aha” moment when the quarry is actually discovered. I’ve got this mental picture of a Dragon Blood having investigated a case that went cold years ago, still having the mental imprint of the perpetrator’s image, and then at some point they happen to meet somebody and profile them as a matter of course, and are shocked to feel the murky image in their head suddenly come into focus to reveal the person in front of them.

    Falsehood-Unearthing Attitude is an Earth Charm, with the rather distinct effect of being able to determine Intimacies that a person would be willing to lie for the benefit of. Even apart from investigations, that’s an interesting possible avenue of information for an Exalt to have, whether it be informing their picture of that person’s character or having an idea of what kinds of things they would say that might be lies even before they say them (and thus, what to give particular scrutiny to).

    Foul Stench of Lies Discernment is finally the Dragon Blooded having their own version of the Charm to detect lies. It’s a bit less absolute than Judge’s Ear Technique and is another one tied into the profiling character action, which endears me a bit more to it.

    Scent of the Stranger’s Threshold is a nicely flavoured Wood Charm to determine whether or not a given individual is from round these parts. I like the way it always registers Fair Folk as non-natives, which seems like it would be lent to another nice moment of realisation as the Exalt looks more into them and finds a contradiction in the apparent fact that they’ve lived in the area for as long as anybody can remember. It also has an interesting synergy with Bloodhound’s Nose Technique, considering that telling whether or not a specific, known person is from a given area is a kind of inversion of the Charm’s normal effect.

    Heh, there are a few non-Water Charms concentrated more at the top. Homeward Trail Discovery Method is Air, and lets one build upon the prior Charm’s power to tell whether a target is from somewhere to be able to intuit when one sees their own actual home, as well as boosting actions to enter it through things like breaking down the door or picking its lock.

    Back to Water, and River-of-Memory Meditation acts like a kind of mental database, letting a character measure the details of a crime against people that have been recently profiled, allowing one to determine that somebody would have had motive (even if it doesn’t on its own reveal what that motive could have been), as well as whether or not the crime scene contains a particular identifying characteristic or calling card (including telling it apart from somebody copying it). This is an interesting one for the manner in which it can start investigation stories, or maybe kind of come in the middle of them. I’m seeing the Agatha Christie-esque story in which the first session of a story consists of introducing a cast of characters, many of whom the investigator is liable to profile, and then the second begins with an actual crime committed and the Exalt being able to narrow their list of suspects from the outset.

    Finally there’s an Essence 5 Charm in the form of Inescapable Wave Insight. This one provides a number of benefits if a character should have already uncovered a variable number of clues, to allow one to pursue the case to its conclusion: bonus dice are given to rolls to find and fight the one that they’re looking for, Charms to enhance the investigation have lower Willpower costs and… I presume they get the Mute keyword, but actually think the wording is a little bit unclear, and they can treat influence to get them to drop the investigation as unacceptable (as is anything to override their own judgement on what to do with their quarry, if they should be caught).

    I actually think that the Investigation Charms of Terrestrials are better than those of Solars in a few respects. Not stronger, but I feel as though these more effectively play into and enhance narratives of investigating a crime or pursuing a truth, with Charms more frequently based on improving one’s diagnostic arsenal and compelling one onto the next stage of pursuit, or into the next question, as well as some very distinct and attractive methods for the story to end.


    And that’s it for now. Come Tuesday, I look forward to starting off with the Larceny Charms that Dragon Blooded criminals might pit against these powers.

        All right, with seventeen Abilities to go through, I’m going to want to pick up the pace a bit to keep ahead of the next update.

        First off, for a reason I find hard to define, I like Underground River’s Flow as the name for the initial Larceny Charm; I guess it’s just because it’s a cute idea for crossing water imagery over with this Ability. Then there’s Flowing Body Disguise, the first appearance modifying Charm, with not only eliminating penalties for disguises to impersonate specific people or make significant alterations to appearance, but actually provides a non-Charm bonus success to impersonating a blood relative.

        There are a few straightforward Charms to enhance criminal or underhanded activity before Observer Awareness Method, allowing one to roll to determine that they’re being watched. Then there’s the first of the non-water Charms, Rose-Among-Thorns Distinction, which allows one to acquire a specialty in interaction with a specific criminal organisation and convince others that they belong to it. Following that is the Air Charm Whispering Thief Technique, allowing one to perform supernatural feats of ventriloquism to make their own voice sound from elsewhere, or allow them to speak without breaking their stealth.

        Back to Water with the Charm Artful Flowing Theft, which improves both attempts to steal and making it more difficult for the target to notice, limited to only being used once per scene. The Earth Charm Dragon’s Hidden Treasure was, I believe, among the previewed ones, allowing an Exalt to hide an object inside of structures of earth or stone. Face-Stealing Reflection is an interesting take on a familiar concept; it touches on a usual “read somebody’s mind to disguise as one they know” effect, but ties it to the lower power of the Dragon Blooded by making it dependent on a modified read intentions action, and bases it on revealing a Tie (and its nature) to somebody that the Dragon Blooded already knows well enough to try disguising as. I find this to be another good example of making the Dragon Blooded less powerful, but using a more interesting mechanical approach than just the weaker version of a Charm a Solar or Lunar might have.
        Bramble Purse Technique allows one to penalise attempts to pickpocket them, and inflicts injury upon those who fail to do so. Instant Disguise Prana allows one to more rapidly disguise themselves and do so without the need for any tools (although granting double 9s if they do, coming back to my preference for powers that encourage such). And Waters-of-Honesty Method allows one to make a read intentions roll to identify somebody engaged in cheating or criminal activity and what they’re doing (with even failure allowing one to intuit that something underhanded has taken place).

        The Signature Air Charm is Vault-Emptying Whirlwind Heist, which is their version of the “make retroactive changes as a representation of planning for contingencies” effect. Specifically, one needs to perform the Charm prior to the actual crime, making a roll and banking the successes to be spent on Charm bonuses to a number of helpful Abilities and various retroactive effects, ranging from being able to leave some kind of calling card to retroactively using the evidence concealing Charm.

        Dragon Snatches Jewel is their version of an enhanced disarm gambit (doubling 9s on the roll and refunding the Initiative cost if they succeed) that allows one to automatically attune a snatched Artifact weapon, or its hearthstone. It’s straightforward, but distinctly powerful for Terrestrials.

        Burning Sins Seduction allows one to make a specialised inspire roll to turn one against the law or stoke an attraction to criminality, although the player of the target is the one that decides what the provoked emotion is and the specific Intimacy that they’d be willing to break the law for (instantly forming a Minor one to fulfil that purpose if none of the others fit the bill).

        Flowing God-Dragon Stance is the first Larceny Charm with the Aura keyword, and is the uncommon Signature Charm with multiple prerequisites, allowing one to actually convert their body into liquid. In that form, they can naturally flow through any gaps that aren’t watertight, impossible to grapple without magic, makes one more difficult to damage, and improves stealth (especially in water). It has a failsafe against the Charm ending in a space too tight for the Exalt. It’s another fairly straightforward effect, but a rather dramatic and iconic one.

        And Terrifying Forest-Devil Mask was another previewed one, allowing one to don a mask that creates an iconic persona, acquiring additional specialties, an unchangeable Defining Intimacy appropriate to the persona, and can induce a Minor Tie to the persona in people who don’t see past the disguise. My thoughts on this are the same as when I first saw it; how it’s an interesting extension of a basic concept of the Ability, and a nice synthesis of Wood in its literal and metaphorical details.

        For a fun little countermeasure if somebody manages to see past a character’s disguise, there’s Vaporous Visage Evasion, allowing one to reflexively obscure their features with supernaturally generated mist. It breaks down the disguise for general onlookers, but allows one to conceal their identity against somebody that the disguise has already failed against.
        Incendiary Accusation Approach is a fun little comeuppance effect, building on the Charm to uncover cheating and the like by causing the unveiled person to mildly light on fire in a manner that reveals their wrongdoing to any onlookers.

        Investigation-Deflecting Current was already hinted at in the Signature Air Charm, and is elaborated as a Charm that specifically conceals evidence in a manner that implicates a particular person known to the Exalt. Characters who fail in their investigations find the false leads, while even the successes find both, and can’t tell the difference (with mechanics provided for how one can figure out which clues are false).

        Mischievous Wind Grasp provides one with the power to generate subtle wind currents for purposes of knocking around small objects in a manner that readies them to be picked up by the Charm’s user. That subtle Charm is appropriately followed up by Exploding Evidence Technique, which… is pretty self-explanatory (the only necessary elaboration being that it rigs the evidence to explode only if somebody tries, and fails, to case a scene imbued with the Essence).

        There’s another returning Charm with Window-In-The-Door Technique, allowing one to see through solid obstacles, and even use Mischievous Wind Grasp through them. I think it may be slightly more mechanically intricate than it used to be, but it’s a basically solid Charm.

        Finally there’s a couple of Essence 5 Charms. Expectation-Mirroring Stance allows one to build off of Face-Stealing Reflection, allowing one to instantaneously assume the appearance of somebody revealed by the prior Charm, even reaping additional motes for threshold successes on the initial read intentions roll. And Infallible Alibi Approach allows an Exalt to intuit evidence of their wrongdoing that they’ve put effort into concealing is being discovered, giving them an opportunity to remotely destroy the evidence, carrying the rather interesting cost (in addition to the motes) of placing an incriminating mark upon the user.

        The Larceny Charms look good overall, although they don’t quite have the wow factor for me that a few other Abilities have had. That might just be appropriate to the nature of this one, though, with all of the subtlety and misdirection.

                    Next up, Linguistics.

                    Heh, only one Charm in, and we’re at an out-of-Aspect element. Still, going by the name Cryptic Essence Cipher, it certainly sounds like one fitted to Water. It’s fairly straightforward mechanically, creating a code and designating who can read it. I do like the kinds of stories implied by having the option to make it decipherable to somebody sharing a Principle; the notion of writing an innocuous message that will convey a special meaning to somebody of a particular bent.

                    Water Charm Signature-Stealing Calligraphy improves one’s capacity to imitate others in writing, Fire Charm Fervor-Inciting Brushstrokes enhances a written inspire action, and Earth’s Tenacious Dragon Scholar improves Resolve against writing, so they’re fairly basic.

                    Back to Air is the return of the classic Wind-Carried Words Technique. It’s about the same as it ever was, being able to send your words a few miles away, although I believe that enhancements from improved Linguistics alone is new. Plus it’s got a rather decent difficulty calculation for what needs to be overcome to magically intercept a message.

                    I’ve often felt a bit iffy about Language-Learning Ritual, and… it’s all right. It’s limited enough to not completely overcome the language barrier at convenience, while still be useful enough to be worth using.

                    Enigma-Reading Eye is their straightforward code-breaking Charm, and then Thousand Tongues Meditation to offset some of the limits of Language-Learning.
                    Voices on the Wind reminds me of the Solar Charms to enhance hearing and touch, in a manner that let you listen in with things like a length of string or wire. It’s interesting to consider some ways in which the different power structure leads to some mildly similar effects being put under different umbrellas.

                    Hmm, I vaguely recall Speech Without Words as having had more general utility in the past… oh wait, it can still apply to people in general, just that one gets Sworn Kin added to the limit. I suspect that the detail of it relying on visual signals that can be noticed by others is also new. I need to look back and see if the Charm used to function outright as something like telepathy.
                    Back to some nasty Water in the form of Caustic Wit Invective to write messages that erode a target’s Intimacies, and then into the Signatures.

                    The effect of greatly speeding up writing provided by Flashing Saga Flourish is another example of something fairly basic but strong, with the per story limit giving an interesting sense of how discerning one has to be in what they choose to write.

                    Unshattered Diamond Parables has the very distinctive and unusual effect of being able to harness the theme or moral of a work that one has written as the equivalent of an Intimacy in a Decision point. That one seems to particularly have a strength in the form of incentivizing characters to continuously write, as well as the implications from their body of work; is it diverse in order to give them a base of pseudo-Intimacies that they would not naturally possess, or things that continuously reference and reinforce the character’s own beliefs, such that they can still call upon them even if the Intimacy in question has been used to bolster Resolve.

                    Fire is realised in Wildfire Words Technique, in the form of a Charm to empower attempts to spread one’s ideas through literature. I feel something for this Charm distinctly, as it reminds me of something that the previous developers once suggested as an idea of what it would be like to live somewhere ruled by the Dragon Blooded, that would still be fairly covert.

                    Heh, does it represent a bias on my part if I keep seeing Water as sneaky and underhanded? Anyway, Rewriting the Truth Technique is another Psyche Charm, allowing one to make a powerful written instil action to make somebody believe in something that is untrue. I take it that the Principle doesn’t need anything supporting or justifying it besides the writing itself, so it looks like it can be fairly strong, appropriately offset by being focused on an individual and only being usable once per story (with the very fitting reset condition of getting somebody to act on belief in something that is untrue in an extreme manner).

                    Oh, but Enthralling Lotus Calligraphy is another Psyche Charm, imbuing their writing with a draw to bring people to read the letter. I feel as though… oh, wait, it only works on the person who was intended to receive it. I’ve got to admit, I feel a bit underwhelmed by this one. It’s like, there are a lot of other Linguistics Charms that are operating on the assumption that people can be relied upon to read their letters, and here’s a power that only has an effect that is relevant on the assumption that somebody won’t do so? I don’t know, maybe it can be targeted at somebody that you know will be very adamant about not reading their letter, but otherwise?

                    Err, Incendiary Argument Approach for your supernaturally provocative writing. Poisoned Tongue Technique lets one disrupt the attempts of another to make social influence. And Reading the Unspoken Word gives one an intuition for what a given listener expects to hear, which can have benefits to impersonation or general conduct, either of which can provide non-Charm dice.
                    Dragon’s Voice Mastery is just a kind of general improvement to speaking actions that align with higher Principles. While Tantalizing Dragon’s Tongue is provocative in a rather different manner than Incendiary Argument.

                    Building off of Enthralling Lotus Calligraphy is Intoxicating Lotus Manuscript, which looks like a much more readily applied power; creating written works that are outright addictive to read. It’s certainly an interesting effect, although I can’t find much more to say about it.

                    Last of all is With One Mind. It’s possible that I conflated Speech Without Words with this, considering how it actually does create a telepathic bond. I’d say that before it was rather nebulous, so it benefits a lot from additions such as defend other and distract gambits.

                                All right, checking out Lore before calling it a night. This should be interesting, considering how in the past, it was home to some of the more overt elemental interactions.

                                The Excellency, Careful Insight-Gathering Study, obviously adds a benefit to Lore’s new hotness of introducing and challenging facts. Hmm, I was expecting that to be followed immediately by a tree expanding on improvements to fact actions, but instead we get an interesting effect of being able to use Lore to perform instil actions to make a person highly receptive to being taught by the Exalt. Conversely, Flawless Study Focus allows the character to engage in study that allows them to gain a temporary additional Lore specialty.

                                Now we get to the ones to connect with elements. Elemental Concentration Trance to allow one to briefly meditate upon a physical expression of an element, and the introduce facts related to subjects thematically connected to that element (such as masonry to Earth) using any of a variety of Abilities. I can’t quite recall how the Charm worked in prior Editions, but this is both an intriguing method of tying it to the Lore mechanic, and conveying the idea of drawing enlightenment and knowledge directly from the elements. Whereas Dragon-Kin Empowerment not only allows one to transfer some motes, but their own Elemental Aura (and, at a higher level, some of the anima) to other Dragon Blooded. It’s another nice example of the benefits to the Terrestrial Exalted working as teams.

                                That leads to that old favourite, Elemental Bolt Attack. The first thing that I notice is how its cost can now include a level of anima, which apart from any other mechanical considerations, just conveys a nice visual effect (especially if combining with a non-Aspect element). I’d say it has some decent damage, and some nice tactical variations between the elements.

                                Elemental Empowerment Meditation returns to allow characters to draw motes from the elements, necessarily offset by factors such as a potential cost in health, the motes being limited to Charms of the element that they were drawn from, and only being available once per day (with no reset condition).

                                That leads back into the Charms more directly associated with the Ability, starting with Sagacious Elder’s Instruction, allowing one to empower people that one has advised with an idea supported by a fact that the user introduced, by giving those persons an appropriate temporary specialty that can be applied to any Ability. It’s a handy little tool for characters in an instructing or supervisory role. Following that is Stern Tutor Discipline, a more straightforward Charm to help teach people things such as Attributes and Abilities, and ultimately magical powers.

                                Lightning Flash Inspiration allows the user to reroll non-1 failures on Lore rolls, and then it’s the Signatures. Fulminating Thunderhead Brilliance allows one to modify rolls to introduce facts, instead stating a goal that one seeks and rolling to have the Storyteller introduce or provide a fact that would assist in achieving that goal. I’ll have to see, but I think this might be a good Dragon Blooded variation on the Solar capacity to predict (or create) the future, both in its realisation of being less powerful while still driving stories forward, and the description of the mindset used when following lines of intuition to arrive at those conclusions.

                                Hoo boy, Truth-In-Stone Binding is wordy. Let me see… ah, it allows one to use Lore in a special gambit against Wyld creatures to instantly and indefinitely calcify them, with the only possibility for escape being if words on the resulting stony body being removed in some fashion. It can also be employed against Wyld landscape features, rendering them safe to people of Creation. It’s suggested that very powerful beings or phenomena can only be contained temporarily, but otherwise it’s a rather powerful Charm. Obviously a number of steps below what Solars can do with the Wyld, but far from insignificant, and a nice realisation of this power level and element covering this aspect of Lore. Possibly a bit too good, although I’ll need to check back on the gambit rules to fully figure it out.                                             So the Medicine Excellency provides an interesting suggestion of what a running theme might be, with its note on refining to perfection and how that lets filling your dice cap give you an extra success. Disease-Banishing Technique is another example of a Charm that grants somebody a specialty, Venom Expulsion Method lets you perform a rush job on treating poison, and Wound-Closing Technique is back as one to let you downgrade the severity of a patient’s injuries. That’s the basic trifecta, so let’s see where it goes from there.

                                            Ailment-Sensing Meditation is basic, but then there’s Nature’s Healing Bounty to serve as the Dragon Blooded version of the Charm to be divested of equipment by allowing one to improvise with credible and available sources of plants, or else use them to supplement one’s medical equipment.

                                            Constitution-Tempering Discipline lets one play with the new disease rules by converting the automatic botches they apply into straightforward dice penalties for patients that have already been treated with Infection-Banishing, albeit not indefinitely. Poisoner’s Deft Hand goes in on the other side of Wood, and I can alternately read that as the Exalt’s Essence enhancing the poison itself, or their enhanced medical knowledge allowing it to be applied with particular precision or potency.

                                            Death-Defying Endeavor is a rather striking name, although the actual effect is a straightforward dice trick. Grievous Wound Alteration Energy upgrades the previous healing Charm to allow it to treat aggravated damage. Aaand it’s at the Signatures already.

                                            Purity-of-Mind Method allows one to treat Flaws representing mental illness in a rather intricate manner. I appreciate how it can’t remove the illness completely, instead going for being able to reduce it to its least intense state as well as helping one cope with episodes. Magically removing mental illness has always been something that I found a bit iffy, both for it being too much of an easy way out and for the statement it made about what qualified as an ideal state of mind. I prefer the subject to be approached like this; a method of providing assistance, without trivialising the issue or invalidating the identity of a person for whom such matters are the default and the natural.

                                            Marmoreal Body Fortification is much simpler in comparison, with its power to reinforce the body of a patient to provide temporary extra health levels and occasional soak.

                                            Unbinding the Inner Flame looks like a neat method of briefly overclocking a person’s body, with a number of small, but key, bonuses to apply in combat, followed by a bit of a nasty crash.

                                            Body-Cleansing Ablution is a more subtle kind of enhancement than the previous, alleviating pain in the form of wound penalties (I presume it applies even to wounds that are inflicted after receiving the treatment) and providing a bit of assistance to treating disease and poison.

                                            While Rebirth of Flesh and Ivy allows one to fix a wounded person up with plant material sprouted from the anima, represented by being able to instantly heal damage levels, as well as being able to fix up forms of crippling damage that the patient could recover from (so splinting a broken arm is fine, but you can’t give them a prosthetic tree limb). The one who was healed in that manner can elect to incorporate memorials of it into their eventually healed body, and it has the nice reset condition of saving somebody that had been reduced to their Incapacitated health level.

I like the image that I get from that, of their Wood Essence partaking of the quality of diseases that they’ve managed to dominate, to eventually learn to inflict it upon others. At higher Essence, they can learn to extend that power to supernatural ailments (people had best be wary of veterans of war against the Bull of the North or Mask of Winters, lest they be given the dreaded bonus eruptus), although it can’t handle malady of the likes of the Great Contagion.

I suppose that this particular Ability would be one that would not accommodate quite as many deviations from its element. Anyway, Flesh-as-Stone Inurement is another type of Charm to alleviate wound penalties; possibly less potent than the Signature that did it, but with the additional application of being able to weaponise its… darn, I’m sure there’s an adjective related to morphine that I want to use, but it’s slipped from my mind… anyway, use that on an enemy in order to numb their limbs in a manner that penalises their use until one gets some bed rest. It almost looks like an example of pre-empting a manner in which some players might want to apply a power to numb the body, and so goes ahead and systemizes it directly.

                                                Now, to look for some enjoyable bits around Melee.

                                                Stoking Bonfire Style looks rather wordy for an Excellency Charm. It allows one to add bonuses to Melee rolls and increase their Parry, but has the additional benefit of having its cost lowered every time it is used against the same opponent after having been successful against them. I speculate that this is setting up how the Fire quality of Melee will work, with effects that can build up as they’re applied against opponents, evoking the image of a flame that is stoked to ever greater intensity.

                                                Next is Burning Fury Wreath to let one ignore points of Hardness in an enemy’s armour and Flame-Borne Interception, which allows one to increase their Parry by 1 each time they successfully block an attack for the duration of a round. Since it’s not referred to as a non-Charm bonus, that would still be limited to one’s dice cap, but considering how it costs 3 motes compared to the 2 motes per point that the Excellency costs, it can certainly be more efficient, if a bit of a gamble.

                                                Ah good, there’s Blinding Spark Distraction again to let one make blinding sparks from their weapon, this time working in clashes as well as parrying. Then a Water Charm, Graceful Flowing Defense (which sounds like a familiar name, which I wonder if it used to belong to another Ability) that allows the user to reduce penalties to their Parry, especially if they’re in Water Aura, a rather nice example of a stronger Dragon Blooded power that can be brought down a bit by using the Charms that surround it.

                                                Crimson Fang Bite provides a fairly straightforward bonus to damage rolls, and then the Wood Charm with the great name of Demon-Crushing Wolf Bite that allows one to increase the Overwhelming value of attacks and, in Aura, the raw damage as well.

                                                Then there’s Elemental Sheath, the rather curious Dragon Blooded variation on a Charm to banish a weapon Elsewhere; it only works on jade weapons, and is based on sending the weapon on through a local instance of an element (or a level of their anima), to be pulled later out of the same kind of substance. It’s an interesting kind of variation; it doesn’t necessarily change anything in the basic function of an Elsewhere storage Charm, but it plays into the nature of the Dragon Blooded and how they relate to the world.

                                                And considering that it’s followed by Dragon-Graced Weapon… yeah, I suspected it would work like this when I saw them alongside each other, and it’s actually a pretty damn cool image; that one enhances their weapon with elemental power not just by will, but by drawing the element along as the weapon is unsheathed from Elsewhere through it (although there’s also the option to simply form a regular weapon out of the element). As for the advantages provided by each element, they’re not massive or anything, but I imagine that some of them can synergize nicely with some weapons.

                                                There’s Crossfire Flash to enhance the Blinding Sparks into scorching flames that allows one to weaken an enemy’s roll in a clash, and Flame Warden Stance to allow one to apply a defend other action across a scene, with the added benefit of parrying effectively granting one a bonus die to attack rolls. I like how, thus far, so much of Fire Aspected Melee is defensive, if only for the subversion of what one might expect of those things crossed over.

                                                In that spirit is Blazing Interception, allowing one to apply a counterattack when they successfully defend another, with the added benefit if they’re in Fire Aura of letting the defended person make a distract gambit to benefit the counterattack. It actually gives an interesting image of what can make a Dragon Blooded weapon fighter dangerous; being in the middle of a group of allies to defend them, and building off of that as a whirring engine of deadly reprisal.

                                                Now, for the Signature Air Charm, Steel Tempest Strike. It’s not quite the kind of thing that the old developers suggested of an Air Melee Charm in that preview so many years ago; rather than shooting a blade of wind, it’s another example of using Air to carry the warrior into range of their target for a decisive attack, while still being allowed to attack again. Mind, I like that; it’s not as if Dragon Blooded lack for powers to shoot the elements, and the name gives me an image of somebody clean and quick in a manner that feels very much like becoming the wind yourself.

                                                Next along is Falling Mountain Fang, a rather different Charm for allowing one to use one of three unique gambits, all extending from a premise of increasing the weight of what is struck; one to make it necessary to use a feat of strength just to not drop your weapon (and levelling penalties even if successful to represent straining to carry and move with it), one that increases the mobility penalty of armour and makes one cost Initiative just to move (with a subsequent use intensifying the effect to the point where the armour’s wearer can’t even stand), and one to make less durable structures implode. It’s actually pretty damn cool; I often like the idea of Elemental Earth extending to gravitational effects, and the idea of knocking them into an enemy through one’s weapon seems… what am I looking for… a nicely blunt way of doing it. I don’t know, maybe its just because I find it reminiscent of Octavian’s attacks to cripple enemies by striking them to apply his oil, which I just like all on its own.

                                                And know there’s the Signature of Melee’s native element, Harnessed Firestorm Assault. While the name might imply an actual fire blast, this happens to be another metaphorical one, but in the form of another of those rare multi-attack Charms that does not evenly divide Initiative… at least on the surface. Hmm, the fact is that if one is limited to their Dexterity divided by two (rounded down) attacks, then the most that you’ll ever be able to use is two, so even though each attack has a standard of Initiative divided by two… it’s the same difference; you make two attacks, and they each get half of your Initiative, the same as just about any other multi-attack. Hell, if you don’t have a minimum of Dexterity 4, then it means that you’re spending 10 motes, 1 Willpower and expending your Fire Aura to make a single attack, which will use half of your Initiative. I mean, okay, if you have the right kind of specialty then you can add one more attack in there, but that still seems as though it only really has much value at Dexterity 5. I’ve… got to be honest here, I don’t think this is a very good Charm. It’s especially a pity, since it’s Fire and Melee, and they ought to go together like gangbusters.

                                                Well, moving on from that is Roaring River Slash, which uses the image of one’s blade being followed by a ribbon of cutting water to allow one to roll their decisive attack damage twice and combine the results, and this is more like it. I even like the image of how the water’s existence means that magically lit fire will be extinguished.

                                                Last is Aura of Grasping Branches, letting one adopt a defensive stance reminiscent of dense foliage, allowing them to ignore Onslaught penalties, albeit in an unusual manner; the Exalt is basically given a pool of penalty counters, that is distributed as one chooses across a round. Still, it’s a scene long Charm, the application is flexible, and Wood Aura allows one to not take onslaught penalties if an attack is successfully parried, so I’d say it’s a nice way of rounding out one’s defence.

                                                Moving on to Dragon Soul Burst, and there finally is something to channel fire into one’s attacks, specifically in the form of a special decisive attack that, while it does require aiming beforehand, has its own damage without needing to use Initiative (although it has that option in Fire Aura), ignores Hardness (meaning that it can be used against an armoured opponent even if the base damage is low), and has a chance of setting the target aflame. Unlike a lot of other Charms like this, it doesn’t even have a limited use. It’s not half bad as a proper fiery sword attack.

                                                Then there’s Smoldering Essence Attack, letting one not only increase the amount of Initiative lost on a withering attack, but setting them on a kind of spiritual fire that continues to burn away at their Initiative until a number of turns pass, or the ones so afflicted hits the Dragon Blood who did it to them with a withering attack. It’s another take on an element that I like; along with the physical and metaphorical, being able to apply the logic of the physical to the metaphorical.

                                                Burning Pinnacle Strike is used to reflexively launch an extra attack (withering or decisive) when an attack raises one’s Initiative to the top of the order, and the Balanced Wood Charm Root-and-Hand Merging lets one connect their hand to their weapon in a manner that improves one’s parry and makes it harder to disarm them.

                                                Leading up to the often contentious Portentous Comet Deflection. It’s fairly streamlined compared to prior Editions; rather than being a kind of semi-perfect defence, it lets the user reflexively clash an attack with a decisive attack, which may or may not count as one’s attack for the round if they have a Fire Aura to expend. I need to look back at the clash rules to have a good sense of how useful this is, but if nothing else, it fits in with the defensive aspect of Dragon Blooded Melee being more focused on responding offensively than something to simply provide an absolute defence would.

                                                From there we get Fire Incites Water to a Riot of Clouds, letting one combine power over fire and water to make a clash attack that, if successful, creates a cloud of steam that conveys some Initiative to the Exalt’s allies, and I presume creates a form of obscuring cover, although that isn’t systemically described here.

                                                And for the last two Charms, we get… ones that aren’t actually Fire. Mela’s Flashing Tongue is an Air Charm, that lets the user make repeated withering attacks on an opponent, the number equal to the user’s onslaught penalty, with Air Aura allowing one to make the last strike decisive (based on the image that the previous attacks built a static charge, that can be unleashed with the last).

                                                Last of all is Mirror-on-Water Focus, a Water Charm of all things at the pinnacle of Fire Aspected Melee. It can only be used if one’s anima is at the dim level (helped by the Charm itself being Mute), and provides a special kind of decisive counterattack that is rolled prior to the opponent making their own attack, only using half the Initiative for damage, but that applies the successes not only to health levels, but also to stripping successes from the ensuing attack. It can be reset, but if I’m reading it correctly, in a rather costly manner, needing to deal at least twenty withering damage in a clash attack generated by Fire Incites Water.

                                                Melee is an interesting mix of effects; rather than the straightforward attack options that one might expect from it and the element of Fire, it’s oriented around a lot of tricky tactical ideas, such as inflicting certain kinds of penalties on enemies or turning successful defence with it back around on opponents. Hell, it didn’t even have the multi-attack Charm from prior Editions that one of the Terrestrial quick characters from the core did (not a complaint, just an observation). It definitely feels very unique as an attack Ability, and considering how Archery and Brawl have already been seen as having more direct offensive power (and Dodge more direct defence, come to think of it), seems to have its own niche well guarded. Hell, I actually like the sense of its take on offensive fire that I come to in the end; it feels a bit less like wielding flame to blast and incinerate opponents, and more like being flame, something flickering, hard to grasp and often painful for those who do so, and harming more by sparking off embers or swaying a tongue into kindling then being a raging inferno.
