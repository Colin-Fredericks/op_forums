    Originally posted by Chausse View Post
    Is mote saving for rolls really worth it for craft ? I can't remember any time it was useful to track motes for crafting rolls
    There's less interaction between downtime and those charms than you think.

    Most are either committed or spent during the roll.
    Committed motes are a minor consideration, although committing too much means the character is spending himself dry all the time. Outside of peaceful times and places this may be a problem.

    Motes spent during the roll are critical for that day or three. It's a window of a few days where you are, essentially, on Deadline Mode. Burning yourself out to get the last touches done, concerned it may break apart.


    Either way both bring considerations that are relevant on camera, and bring considerations that are perennial in character. Most artisans will not start big demanding jobs if they can't have the peace of mind that they will be able to work mostly uninterruptedly. And interruptions may not be critical, but will there will be a sensible limitation anyway.

        Originally posted by LadyLens View Post
        Presumably, your character does not start craft projects in the midst of other time-critical activities.
        With charms like Craftsman Needs No Tools, and the general way the craft system works to incentivize crafting all the time, you're actually quite likely to be starting craft projects during time-critical activities. I already gave an example, upthread, of doing a major project to reinforce the gate of a besieged city just before the besiegers start battering at it, but I can think of many, many more examples. If the Circle is going to a fancy party in an hour but has nothing to wear, the Craft (Tailoring) specialist whips them all up some fancy formal wear. In a village under threat from floods, the Craft (Masonry) character builds a wall to divert the floodwaters at lightning speed. The Circle is ambushed in a teahouse by the Wyld Hunt, and their weapons are all somewhere else. The Craft (Weaponsmithing) person proceeds to cobble together tablelegs, sharp shards of pottery, and tablecloths as handle wrapping, and rapidly creates swords for everyone, so nobody has to deal with the improvised weapon penalty. And so on.

        And the Dragon-Blooded get craft speed enhancing charms too (Stone-Carving Fingers, for example), even if they're not as versatile or quite as fast as the Solar ones, so they can do similar sorts of craft tricks, and want to save motes.

                Originally posted by LadyLens View Post

                Presumably, your character does not start craft projects in the midst of other time-critical activities.
                "But that's exactly my point! Exactly my point" ~ Mouse

                Your character's knowledge of when they are in the midsts of a time-critical activity isn't perfect.

                You don't know if the Wyld Hunt has found you, or the Mask of Winters's deathknights are three days away, or if Raksi is about to unleash her latest horror on the world, or whatever you are using as plot. You know (maybe) that nothing is happening right now, but not that your schedule is free for the next two months.

                Obviously if the Wyld Hunt sets fire to your workshop, you know now isn't a great time. But how do you infallibly know when is a good time?

                The Empress is missing, the Deathlords walk Creation, the Solars have returned... if the ST asks "how do you spend the next six weeks", the characters have no way of knowing that they've got six hassle free weeks ahead.

                (I think the best way of doing it is probably for the ST to decide in secret how much downtime there's going to be, and have the PCs declare what they're doing on a week by week basis. But this almost deserves its own thread at this point.)

                                        Downtime is just time that no crisis (that can't be dealt with a simplified roll) happens. In my case, downtime is an accorded time that happens whenever the players complain loudly that they need more time to spend their exp and buy charms and skills.

                                        They usually do split their every day's time to do stuff, though. So, if they are crafting something, they spend eight hours every day on it (even adventure time), unless circumstances impede this. This applies to time spent training as well. The players have google sheets for every day, it's very organized. Some of them have invested heavily in the meditation charms to squeeze more time to build and grow (months sleeping only 5 minutes a day). They are the workaholic exalted. Fortunately, they have a handy lunar seneschal to offload the administration tasks, or else the kingdom would suffer.
