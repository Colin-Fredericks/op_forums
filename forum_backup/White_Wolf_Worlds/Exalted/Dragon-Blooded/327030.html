                              Re: Bending:

                              Q: Exalted 3rd Dragon-Blooded: can you speak to whether or not players will be able to evoke Avatar: TLA with the system as written? (nicodemus144)
                              A: Avatar evokes Dragon-Blooded. Just throwin' that out there. That said, I plan to introduce some more iconic elemental Charms to various abilities, and probably a new series of five (tentatively) DB-only martial arts. (John Mørke)
                              I love Avatar, but the original series is not exactly a sterling paragon of worldbuilding -- people are there for the characterization and voice performance and amazing fight choreography, not for the well-thought sociology on what societies run by supernatural martial artists look like. So I question the assertion that we should alter the Realm to make it more like the Fire Nation. The Realm is already an amazing fantasy society, and the ideas we have for making it even more amazing have not come from Avatar.

                              Also the observation that benders don't have much in common with DBs is a good one. Benders are people with supernatural martial arts, but they don't have dice-adders for mundane skills that push them above human competency in damn near every field of human endeavor; they also don't live to 250 years old as a matter of course. Those two things are going to -- and do -- have way more impact on how any society with them at its center look than their ability to snap their fingers and make fire. (Stephen Lea Sheppard)
                              [T]here will be martial arts and Evocations that let Dragon-Blooded have more flashy elemental powers. We are also spicing up and broadening their Charm set. But do not expect them to have a basic element-bending power or 2e Infernal style designs, because that misses the point of the humanity present in the Exalted. (John Mørke)

                                  I'm going to quote the best bit of advice ever given about Dragon-Blooded games because I think it can be extrapolated to some of the best advice about Exalted in general.

                                  Originally posted by Exalted: the Dragon-Blooded
                                  What Solar Exalted?
                                  Another valid approach is to ignore the Solar Exalted entirely, in many Dragon-Blooded stories they're unnecessary. The Realm is filled with many opportunities for adventure that have nothing to do with the Solars at all. Internecine conflict, religious debates and border clashes happen all the time - usually without the involvement of the Solars. You should never feel compelled to include any element of the setting that doesn't serve your purpose; if the addition of the Solars would upset your carefully laid series don't include them. It's just as reasonable to exclude them as it is to leave out the Guild, the Threshold or any element for which you have no use. Players usually introduce complications into stories on their own without the Storyteller's helping them. If the Solar Exalted would be another complication leave them be.

                                                      I guess I have a strange view on it, but the way I look at it there are two main reasons to play Terrestrials.

                                                      1. A lower power level game:
                                                      If you have ever tried to run an exalted game where the characters are stuck in prison or otherwise in a situation that limits them and tried to run it with Celestial exalts, it never lasts that long just due to the shear power level of the charms involved. You would have to use artifact bindings which block charm use and essence regeneration and after a certain point you can't say those are standard issue to all of Creations jails with a straight face. So there are just some types of games that you can only really play in the Exalted setting by using terrestrials.

                                                      2. You Want a Challenge:
                                                      It is inevitable that groups swap out players, old ones get different job hours or classes, so you bring in some new blood and teach them the game system. I find it works well to have the experienced players run Terrestrial characters and let the new players run celestial exalts. This gives the experienced players a change of pace, a bit of a challenge, and it forces them to use setting knowledge rather than say power level knowledge. With the new players the power level of their charms allows them to contribute to the game without needing to know much about it.


                                                      Admittedly this requires an ST who can work with these concepts to pull it off in a satisfying way. Such as keeping in mind that Terrestrials are the ones who can use their charms publically without getting a Wyld Hunt called on them.

                                                      Personally I had a lot of fun being the only Terrestrial in a mix group of Celestials because the character could be the public face of the group which allows for lots of role play and quick thinking moments. Sure you can't go toe to toe with the heavy hitters of the setting, but that never really bothered me. Terrestrials really should be played a lot like Xanatos from the Gargoyles series. He is much weaker than the Gargoyles, he heals much slower, he can't fly on his own, and he isn't that physically durable. But by using his wits and resources to their fullest, he can be the equal of the Gargoyles.

                                                      Then again a lot of players out there just don't have the interest in that kind of play style, they just want an "I win button" character build where you take a specific set of charms and turn their brains off since acting like a murder-hobo is their apex of fun.
