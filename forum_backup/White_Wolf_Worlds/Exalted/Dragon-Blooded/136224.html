                                      the stuff you're referencing comes from second edition, DarkLord. I'm pretty sure the Kukla didn't re-fertilize the world in its original writeup, it was just the apocalyptic disaster in physical form that remained in the reserves of Heaven for whenever a mad and world-shattering dragon would be preferable to the unthinkable cataclysm that necessitated its release. Given the general disdain a lot of this community seems to possess for large swaths of the 2e run, I expect in Third it won't be engraved into canon that the Elemental Dragons' deaths would be obviously and immediately deleterious to the stability of Creation and survival of its inhabitants.

                                      That said, SLS is very right inasmuch as what works best for your game may not be what works best for all games, and vice versa. Wrestling with titanic, violent expressions of nature in order to cripple the world seems like a perfectly cool idea, and you should feel free to run with it.

                                      EDIT: Also, destroying a jouten, IIRC, results in the destruction of the whole Primordial; destroying the Emerald Mother would also shatter the world-comet Gnosis, and if the Dragons are her souls, one assumes that they too would be destroyed. If one subscribes to the idea that Gaia or the Dragons are tied to Creation's geomancy or Essence flows, it would probably also cripple many manses in the process. Then again, it's apparently possible for souls to survive the death of their greater part and be absorbed into other Primordials; one of the Voice of the Magnus segments implied this. Also, since Elementals do not spring from her directly, one can assume they would continue to exist. Make of that what you will.

                                      FWIW, I seem to recall the Elemental Dragons of Fire and Earth slumbering at their respective poles, but don't quote me on that. If nothing else, it's a reasonable place to start looking.
