                Originally posted by Meianno Yuurei View Post
                I really don't see how the prophecy is harmed. "But Creation was almost destroyed, twice!!!!!"
                And yet against all odds it survives.
                Almost as if that was its fate..
                Both the Fair Folk and the Underworld (which was the origin of the Great Contagion) are outside of fate's purview. Creation survived that because of luck or, if you prefer, because that's just the way things happened, like in the real world. But it wasn't preserved by any sort of fate. That was literally the entire reason that "outside of fate" was created as a category. And there's important reasons it was set up that way...

                That's fine. I'm tired of the person who apparently started the "the Sids can't be shown to be right about anything" trend.
                Several things to keep in mind:

                1. The Sidereals don't have to be right all the time in order to be effective at what they're supposed to do. They just have to be right often enough for their choices to make sense and for their powers to be effective. Nobody else, note, gets to be right all the time - either in or outside the setting. The game absolutely supports the reading that the Primordial War was a mistake, that the way the Solars structured the First Age was a mistake, that the Shogunate and the Realm were mistakes, that everything the Lunars have done was a mistake. It doesn't force these, and I think the intended reading is more that they were imperfect and can be improved on, but they're clearly flawed. The Great Prophecy and the Usurpation are no different. They can be defensible, and reasonable, and something that makes sense in the backstory and supports the effectiveness of Sidereal powers, while also leaving open the reading that they were mistakes or that some element of them was fundamentally wrong. Nobody would expect Solar swordsmanship or management to be flawless, in the sense of never failing; it's silly to expect Sidereal prophecy to be flawless. It just has to be good enough for their actions to make sense.

                2. Part of the reason for this is because all splats are optional, and players are meant to be free to choose where they focus the game. The game does not require that the world be saved by Solars. It doesn't require that whatever solution players come up with resemble the Realm. Similarly, the game does not require that you follow Fate or conform to Sidereal prophecy. "Screw destiny" always has to be available as an option, which means "the Great Prophecy was flatly wrong" or "the Sidereals flatly misread it" have to be available as options. They're not appropriate for all games, but they have to be there, and the game has always been careful to leave them there. And I disagree with the assertion that this would negate the effectiveness or importance of Sidereals (just like "you can save the world without Solars" doesn't negate the importance of Solars) - going for that outcome is obviously not what you'd do if you wanted to make the Sidereal story front-and-center, but narratively it's completely defensible as long as the decisions Sidereals made made sense at the time; even faced with incontrovertible evidence that the Sidereal reading of the Great Prophecy was flatly, indisputably, completely wrong, Kejak can still credibly say "we knew that was a possibility, but the risk was too great." This is how people make hard decisions like that in the real world. More generally, the Sidereal theme is not and has never been about being perfect - about never getting it wrong. It's about having a layer of knowledge past what other people do, and making difficult decisions based on that knowledge. The fact that they sometimes get it wrong is an essential part of that story in the same way that eg. the fact that even the greatest Solar swordsman sometimes loses a fight and dies is a vital part of his story.

                3. It's not true that the one notable successful Sidereal act was the prophecy. Their most important historical act was the decision to enact the Usurpation. The game cannot and will not ever canonically tell you if this was the right decision or not (although individual games may of course weigh in on one side or the other), but it's the reality of that decision and its aftermath that defines the Sidereals, not "our prophecy is always right." They became the secret masters of the universe by having faith in their abilities and the willingness to seize power through violence in order to save the world and find the best path forwards. Whether they made the right choice or not is mostly immaterial at this point (as a modern-day young Sidereal, you didn't participate in that decision and can reasonably weigh in on either side.) The much more important thing is what that decision says about the Sidereals and the overarching themes it sets for them into the present day, ie. as a Sidereal, you are the person sitting on a secret throne in Heaven making vital decisions for everyone else.

                4. I sort of hinted at this above, but really... everyone in Exalted has fucked up. The game's past is not intended to be a manual for how to do things in the future. The Exalted and the gods wage war against their enemies at the dawn of time with glorious excess (and got cursed as a result); the First Age was a time of glorious madness when the Solars failed to rein in their own impulses or recognize what they were doing wrong; the Shogunate was an era of noble decline; the Realm was an exercise in glorious brutality, and the Lunars are... the Lunars, let's not even go there. Compared to these the Sidereals have largely come closest to constant success - they've mostly gotten what they wanted at each point in the timeline. But like everyone else, their past successes are qualified, and some of that qualification is in questions related to their hubris and whether they've really been making the right choices.

                5. If you make everything flawlessly governed by Fate, and just happening according to Fate, what happens to the heroic spirit? What does it say about the Empress (who is, after all, the greatest Dragon-Blooded hero), if her achievement wasn't entirely her own but was the fulfillment of some ancient Sidereal prophecy? Of course, this isn't an easy thing to answer - most stories that deal with fate have to grapple with what it means for free will, the heroic spirit and so on. But it's a complicated question, and the game wouldn't be served by saying "the Empress was just a tool of Fate" or whatever. The idea that the Empress genuinely saved Creation - that, if she had not done what she did, if she'd been a bit less heroic, everything might have died - is pretty important to her story and to the backstory of the Realm; narratively, that has at least as much heft as the Great Prophecy. She needs to have as much claim to saving Creation as Kejak Chejop. My point is that, for many players, "everything you've done has been ACCORDING TO THE PROPHECY" is going to be unfun and unsatisfying; and since the game does not revolve solely around Sidereals, it needs to provide support for such players, giving them escape hatches that they can use to thumb their nose at Fate.

                Of course, it's a tricky balancing act, because you don't want to risk damaging the grandeur or significance of Fate in games that do care about it (a problem that previous editions have sometimes grappled with.) But my point is, there are plenty of reasons to avoid making Sidereal readings of Fate flawless; and the Sidereal game doesn't depend on it being flawless, just on it being good enough that their actions are defensible.

                                                Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                                I think that it's all right for Sidereal prophecy to be fallible, but I think that, even with the Great Curse, there's an issue with the idea that they're widely unaware of a blindspot regarding things like the Underworld or Wyld.

                                                Mostly, I feel as though prophecies around the Usurpation should have been fairly focused on the subject of the Solars, rather than making promises about the distant future.
                                                Kinda. I think that the underlying presentation of the Fair Folk invasion and the Great Contagion as the Sidereals' great failure is unavoidable, though. There's more to the Usurpation than the Great Prophecy and the Usurpation itself - there's a core philosophy to the Bronze Faction that IMHO the books ought to go into more detail on.

                                                They believe that the ideal state of the world is essentially the best and the brightest Sidereals ruling the world from behind the scenes, using other Exalted as puppet kings. Whether the Great Prophecy encompassed those exact details isn't really relevant, because the core of Kejak Chejop's philosophy is essentially that only he (and the Sidereals) can be trusted to do what is necessary and that they were therefore justified in seizing control.

                                                In other words, rather than viewing the madness of the Solars, the Great Prophecy and the Usurpation as the big philosophical center of the Bronze Faction, I feel it's better to view them as the precipitating event that catalyzed a larger, more all-encompassing philosophy of Sidereal leadership.

                                                In particular, this explains the Sidereal attitude towards Lunars - yes, the Lunars are buttmad, but I don't feel it's credible for the game to try and argue that the failure to make peace with them for 1500 years is entirely on the Lunars. There needs to be some deeper political or philosophical disagreement for that to make sense, and the reality that the Bronze Faction would never, ever, ever accept anything remotely approximating equal power-sharing with the Lunars - and the Lunars are aware of this - explains a great deal. It's not necessarily as clear-cut as the Bronze Faction intentionally choosing war - they're not idiots - but the Usurpation was more than just a decision to kill the Solars; it was a decision to place absolute faith in Sidereal judgement and Sidereal prophecy, with all other Exalted rising or falling according to this. I feel the Bronze Faction is willing to kill and die for this... and I feel that what the Great Contagion and the Fair Folk invasion reveal is that (while they would never put it this way, since they feel that this principle is necessary for Creation's survival), they are willing to risk Creation itself.

                                                If the Sidereals had managed to make peace with the Lunars during the Shogunate, for instance, it is unlikely that the Fair Folk invasion would have succeeded, and it's possible even the Contagion could have been limited by their combined force. Huge amounts of energy and resources and time was wasted on that conflict. And I think the Sidereals thought they were genuinely making the right choice! They'd say that the Lunars were too dangerous, too unpredictable, too driven by their short-sighted rage or whatever, and that's not entirely wrong. But what the Sidereals failed to see was that they themselves were dangerous and unpredictable and driven by emotion as well. The conflict with the Lunars was at least partially a result of Sidereal intransigence.

                                                Or, to put it more simply, the severity of the damage done by the Great Contagion and the Fair Folk invasion is at least partially the fault of the Sidereals (it has to be, since they were in charge at the time), but the really serious problem isn't the Usurpation, it's their mismanagement in the ~750 years afterwards. Even presuming the Solars had to die, Creation could have been a lot more ready for those shocks than it was.

                                                Obviously the Lunars and the Dragon-Blooded also share some blame. Everyone screwed up at one point or another. I mean if you go back far enough it's the fault of the Solars for succumbing to madness and bringing things to that point, too. But my point is that it's more useful to look at it as a fundamental failure of the entire Bronze Faction philosophy, rather than a specific mechanical screw-up with their Fate powers or whatever. The Bronze Faction didn't just murder the Solars (which the prophecy said was necessary), they intentionally weakened the Dragon-Blooded and suppressed the Lunars, and as a result, when Creation faced a threat that was less suitable to being confronted by Sidereal power alone, it folded.

                                                It's not that they were unaware of the holes in their capabilities. They knew perfectly well. They chose to gamble that their power, combined with the diminished Dragon-Blooded, could protect Creation from anything unexpected that wandered in from outside its boundaries. And for 700 years they were right, so it wasn't a totally bad bet. But I feel that it wasn't entirely driven by reason and cold calculation, either, whatever Kejak might try to argue.
