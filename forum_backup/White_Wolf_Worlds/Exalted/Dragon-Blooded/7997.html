    Dragon-Blooded - My Worries About The Realm

    11-11-2013, 08:51 AM
    Okay, I have been meaning to write this for sometime now to express my worries about what I think are my biggest gripes about Exalted (and of course it is relative to my favorite ones, the Dragon-Blooded). This problem maybe something that appears exclusively with them because of a very simple reason: they are the only Exalts who exist in a complex, Exalt-filled society that has been running for centuries. When you have something like that, some things are bound to be different, and I don't think the past books have done an exceptional job at getting us inside a society that has demigods working for it left and right.


    On Charms

    When RPing a Dragon-Blooded within the Realm one of my first doubts is if every Charm is 'catalogued' or if all Charms from the book are just the tip of the iceberg. What do I mean by that? The book has some 200+ Charms? If you are, lets say, a socialite in the Realm, you probably has memorized the ones that could threaten you in the social arena. Does that mean that when a Dragon-Blooded sees a jaded woman passing by they KNOW she has her Jade Defense up? Is it expected? Would using those Charms be expected, considered inpolite? Warlord's Convocation can be used on Dynasts aswell, it is a very powerful Charm. Is it well known? Do young Dragon-Blooded know that when they are called late at night to their general's quarters they are at risk of being turned into slaves?

    These problems arise fast in Dragon-Blooded and they pile up because they are a society, and a very old one at that. There are no more 'secret techniques' if all the Charms t heir kind possesses can be contained in a book. They would be rounded up already. People would know the taps to be sweetened before they even tasted anything in a party, and they would know that when your lover comes to you pissed as hell about something you didn't do that she was the target of Brother Against Brother. Or not? This is not a problem with the ressurging Solars, but it is a problem with the stabilished Dragon-Blooded, in my opinion. When you use Dragon Vortex Attack and shape it like a real Elemental Dragon with the Charm's expansions from the Upright Soldier, do people go "Oh! A Dragon Vortex Attack!" or do they go "AN ELEMENTAL DRAGON! BOW!". How well known are Charms, how much space for creativity do we have there?

    I would be interested in seeing how this society works. How alchemically improved are the lives of Dragon-Blooded, and from perfumes to narcotics to sexual activities, I need they should be expanded. Solars, Lunars, etc, all need the Charms and skills they have to survive. Dragon-Blooded had centuries to expand on combat-useless stuff, and that is a lot of their flavor, their frivolity and relaxed outlook on a world that is burning around them. I would like to see Charms based around carrying banners in the battlefield, architeture and buildings, cooking, sex, that others do not posses, in the Realm. If we are exploring the Realm, then it makes a lot of sense for us to see how possessing a society can affect demigods, what would they do with their power.

    I know most Charms are designed for real utility, but there should be a few more Charms for cosmetic effects, like manipulating elements and shaping them (something the Dragon-Blooded -still- lack!), from creating buildings and works of art, something in the Water Aspect Charms that could unpoison a water supply, and something in the Air Aspect that could affect the weather or cool the room. I would be interested in learning those things, and the last Realm book was not on par with my expecations.


    On The Imperial City

    It has been too long since I first started playing Exalted, and I would -really- like to see an unified idea for the Imperial City. How does teh Imperial Manse looks like? Have an artist draw that! How does the city itself looks like? How are the House Manses organized? How do they look? We need a map and interesting locales for the city, far more than it was presented in The Realm. This is the heart of any Dynast chronicle, even if t he players don't live there, that's how important this place is. It deserves a lot of treatement!

    How does the Onyx Curtain works? Do Dragon-Blooded show up to eat at restaurants there like in any normal city in our world? Is it by demand? Do they walk about to relax, or is the habit of walking carelessly with a lover something you only do in the Imperial Manse or outside the Imperial City? Are there theaters? Are the performances boosted by Charms? I have a hard time believing the Dragon-Blooded would book with months notice to see a play where mortals are protagonists, and their cousin with Performance Charms can out do them in a very dramatic way. How does it work? These questions can be expanded and many more can be asked for all the districts. Crimson Delights needs to be fleshed out, and so needs the Temple District. How harsh is the bureaucracy in the Scarlet Empire, is it so crippling that it can effect heroics?


    On NPCs

    If I could say anything, I would advise you guys to write-in more NPCs like the princess in Thorns and Wisdom Whisperer, people you want to see, flocking around the city. From that wacky, chimera-oriented armor designer (Thundersomething, I forget his name) and his rival, Nazegger, all people who have story hooks you can go with. Many NPCs in Exalted are protagonists, and while that's what I want in a novel, that's not what I want in my game. I need a supporting cast. So aside from social ingame mechanics, I would like to see more rumors about the city and it's denizens, people you read about and go 'I would like to meet this person and solve his problem!' and not 'I would like to meet this person and embark on his quest!', because in the end, the players are the ones who should be followed, not the other way around. I am far more interested, as a player and as a storyteller, in NPCs that I can use as support for my chronicle's most varied aspects, and small concepts can turn into very fun NPCs, like the young son of a General turned historian of war in Cathak that is the family disgrace, and sails off with the PCs in their intrepid new adventure searching for a Cathak artifact, long lost, in hopes his father will accept him! He is just the guy who knows a lot of history, but he has something he cannot achieve without the players (going out of the island would take convincing from the players to the NPC's House, resources to cross the Inner Sea, and a drive to go to adventures) and in the end his conquest is not overshadowing the players. You know you have made someone happy and you have improved the Dynasty a bit by 'healing' that small part of it, with the father accepting his son, and the House recovering an artifact. I would like to see more of that.


    I will probably think of more later! But thanks for hearing so far! Of course, as always, this is not an attempt at knocking at the old material, just some ideas on how I would like to see them expanded.

    IA

                  Re: Usage of Charms/Categorization

                  My personal take on it comes from another WW game- I remember back in the day, one of the old V:tM books laid out the common etiquette that it was considered "vulgar" to use social disciplines in an obvious manner on other vampires unless there was a wide gap in station- i.e. the Prince using Dominate on an impertinent Neonate. Passive social boosters weren't just common, they were expected, but it was never couched in the name use of the powers. To translate this into DB society, something like Jade Defense wouldn't be called Jade Defense, it would be referred to in other ways, such as "Don't try and manipulate her, even with the graces of Heshiesh, she will not budge."

                  Incentive is not permission or justification.

                              The idea is that not everyone will think of every Charm -- or necessarily even any Charm, outside Martial Arts -- as a distinct thing or specific technique. This is not always the case -- it might be appropriate for Sidereals to view their Charms as distinct maneuvers, and particularly notable stuff like Deadly Beastman Transformation may be likewise recognized as distinct things -- but seems like a general rule. As above, Warlord's Convocation would probably just be spoken of as someone being incredibly persuasive, drawing on the dragons' blessing to gather devotees to her side.

                              Though he speaks of Solar Charms, the idea of power growing out from the character instead of an external thing seems broadly applicable:
                              Originally posted by John Mørke
                              I am saying Charms, as in Solar Charms, don't exist. They're descriptors for different characters.

                              Investigation is Sherlock Holmes, Athletics is Hercules and Jubei and Kenshin. Melee is also Jubei. The entire Night Caste set is Batman.

                              When you pick your Charms, you are picking traits you want to attach to your character in order to describe him. Every Charm in there is derived from a character in literature or myth or anime. You aren't using a power to make you Batman, you are using a power because you are Batman.

                              The first example is a guy who doesn't really deserve his power, because he didn't earn it, and the second is a guy from whom the power is derived. The essential difference undercuts several arguments posted earlier in the thread, and if you don't understand that, then you probably missed the point.

                              They aren't discrete techniques because they do not exist except for us to interface with them. Everyone who experiences such a thing in-game can experience them as a matter of absolutely no outside influence, including training.
