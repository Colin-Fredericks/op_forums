                        I think Exalted violates some genre conventions on that one. On a large scale, yes, the Solars are the underdogs. The Realm is the Empire, with all the territory, infrastructure, troops, etc. The Solars have themselves and whatever ragtag forces they put together. That largely follows standard Sci-Fi/Fantasy convention.

                        On the personal side, though, the dynamic is completely flipped. Solars are Darth Vader, and Dragon-Bloods are Luke. If you white-roomed the Vader/Luke fight (and we love our white rooms) Luke loses 99/100 times. He gets to win based on the story. Which is fine.

                        So, Exalted violates some expectations along the way, but the net result is two PC Exalts with a fun, dynamic play experience. Both are challenged, and both have numerous win and fail conditions.

                                                Originally posted by TheCountAlucard View Post
                                                From the perspective of the Dragon-Blooded, the Anathema are Heracles or Odysseus or Polyphemus or the Monkey King; it's preferable to employ some manner of unexpected tactic than to directly engage them in their area of greatest strength.

                                                As a reminder, Cthulhu (which is how it's spelled) was "solo'ed" by one guy (driving a steamship through his head).
                                                You say Cthulhu, I say Cthulu. Within the story, his name is unutterable by the human tongue. Feel better?

                                                As for the steamer line, yes, Alucard, I read the story, along with a great deal of Lovecraft's other work. You have to ground it within the time-frame it was written. We live in a world of mecha and Godzilla and Exalted. We live in a world where the power of the atomic weapon is conceptually easy to grasp. From the standpoint of the audience and the writer, the idea that anything could take that kind of impact to the face and live was existentially terrifying. Worse, that the thing had just woken up, and will come back. He's a creature for whom death is a temporary condition

                                                That's really the kind of threat the Anathema pose to the Dragon-Blooded. If caught while they are young, Anathema can be "killed," but they will return. If allowed to grow in power, they can become monstrously difficult to destroy. And their destruction may require the use of terrible First Age weapons, or tramp steamers (you don't generally need to ram a person with a ship to kill them).

                                                That isn't intended to oversell Celestials mechanically, but rather to emphasize how terrifying they are from the perspective of the Dragon-Blooded who fight them. A Dynast who faces off against a Lunar in the Threshold with his buddies is fighting the boogeyman, that thing he has been taught from birth to fear and hate. It's incredibly heroic and brave to stand and fight to the death rather than running. If one or two of his sworn brothers die, that's just the cost that was paid to keep Creation safe.
