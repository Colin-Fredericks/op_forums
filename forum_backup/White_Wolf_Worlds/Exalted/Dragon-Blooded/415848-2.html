    Sure, you can absolutely make Dragon-Bloods threatening. You don't even need to use martial arts to do it, though it helps.

    First, make sure you have a good grasp of their excellencies. With an applicable speciality, Speciality Focus and the First and Second excellencies, they add four dice and two successes for three motes, or one success for free. Optimized for dodge and with hearthstone bracers, an E5 DB has a DDV of 12 without spending a mote.

    Second, Defence From Anathema Method is a must. It has fewer prerequisites after errata, and all of them are pretty good, including the DB Shaping defence and perfect social defence. For 5m, 1wp, DfAM maxes out your DVs and MDVs for the scene whenever you are near Celestials. Passive DDV can raise to 15 for the scene.

    Third, External Penalties. With Thrown you have Elemental Armor Technique, which, when repurchased for the Earth and Fire versions, allows a scene long -3 external penalty against all attacks. Whirlwind Shield increases that by 3 against range. Over in Dodge, Elemental Defence Technique can inflict a further -2 penalty, but cannot be repurchased to select the effect, so it depends upon aspect.

    Fourth, the vortex. Dragon Vortex Attack is formidable on its own, but as an elemental hazard its penalties and damage can be resisted by Celestial Charms. However, upgraded with Wrath Directing Gesture, it can be used as a perfect defence for the whole group. Using the Sculpt and Shield functions, it can be reshaped reflexively to defend any ally within its radius for 11m. Provided Dragon-Bloods are flaring their animas (and of course they are) they are immune to the damage inflicted by this method of defence, so the only downside is that the Vortex itself is expensive to establish for the scene; 15m, 1wp.

    Fifth, Immaculate martial arts are formidable, but don't overlook the utility of Terrestrial Melee in offense. With full repurchases of Dragon-Graced Weapon and Dragon Blade Beneficence, the entire party can do a host of badtouch supplemental effects for free. Better yet, if you are fighting those pesky Infernals or Abyssals, everyone's damage becomes aggravated. If your opponent is unwise enough to neglect tick-long or longer penalty negation, a group attack enhanced by Threshing Floor Technique will rapidly drive their DVs into the ground.

    Sixth, With One Mind allows the group to reflexively Defend Other each member. A couple of members specced for high Parry DV and the Conflagration of Warding offer an additional layer of defence.

    Finally, cooperative Elemental Bolt is not to be underestimated. Combine a 50+ damage 30 attack dice death-sphere and have your Thrown specialist continue to use it every round for one mote a pop!

            Eh? You're presently 1/3 way in piling external/internal penalties compared to the post about the importance of penalties for DBs I made on the old forum. Such characters exist on a higher level in both pure power and, even more importantly, versatility and control.

            As I cannot link the old forum (archivation failed) I can instead remind you of this thread about Dragonblooded where we discussed Dragonblooded disfunction in 2/2.5 both in fluff and crunch. There I posted two characters I have used as Elder "DB" challenges to Celestial parties.

            The first one was from early in the edition, probably just after Scroll of Heroes and prior to the Dawn Solution or even splats like Oadenol's. So in the time of Core-perfects only, no tick/action-longs, and we as always used stunts in the way that eventually became the codified one: one per action, not one per attack/defense as the Chungians advocated which made the Solars even more ridiculous. So that character, an Earth Master/Hill-Crushing Hero mix, had accuracy and soak in the 80s, damage in the 60s, PDV of 30 or so, was facing relatively low-xp Solars who lacked plenty of powerful options even for the limited amount of splatbooks at the time...and was defeated.

            The other character is from the late 2.0 edition, is akuma/DB (so probably OK for the RotSE but you can drop the akuma stuff and focus on the DB goodies if you will). Just some of the things she had included:

            - Mantle of Elemental Fusion, fused with a Lesser Earth Dragon of the Quicksilver Queen stature. The main benefits derived are Essence Plethoras, Destiny Sponsorship inflicted 15 external penalty on attacks against Dodge DV, Invincible Symbol of Authority:Earth that denies/reduces earth and metal based attacks, including magic materials based on metals.

            - Transcendant Gaian Harmony:Air and Wood, inflicting 8 to 16 external penalties on certain attacks per the animas (She's Earth Aspect)

            - lesser penalty applicators like Consuming Might of the Fire Dragon and Flowing Water Defense (note that her stats presume a fight going on for some time so she has accumulated enough Water Style free motes)

            - Unchain the Dragon's Heart: probably won't come into play, but if it does it's a nasty surprise

            - Ordained Talons of Draconic Fury on the Eye of the Fire Dragon. That's the Rate 13 weapon line and the artifact itself does aggravated damage and charmbreaks Solars
            So, those who could at all engage her through her exceptional stealth/mobility were to face near full immunity to damage from magical materials (and a ward against Solar charm-magic weapons), DVs and penalties combining in the 50-100 range, the "best" of Cobra and Water Dragon and Joy in Adversity mote economy, high rate, high accuracy, aggravated charm-breaking attacks...and did she win against competent established Solars of just 5 Essence? No, she didn't. So the maximum system juice for Dragonblooded couldn't deal with Solars half-way to Essence 10, even when the Solars chose to engage instead of evading and annihilating everything Dragonblooded through Sorcery, Wyld Shaping, and minion-boosting.

                        Originally posted by Murcushio View Post
                        I think the analogy once made was that it's the difference between fighting 100 ants and 100 cats. You can't just SMOOSH the cats, so eventually they're probably going to win.
                        The funny thing is that the ants probably have a better chance at doing major damage. Kick over a bullet ant nest and they swarm you, they are small enough not to be noticed and can get inside your defenses.

                        Getting back to the matter at hand, there is a critical concept to understand, unless the ST and Players have agreed to play this sort of game, there is little chance of the Celestials being killed. Obviously there is always a chance that the player does something stupid and the dice fail them at ta critical moment, but it is unlikely. The terrestrials on the other hand are expected to die.

                        People talk about Celestial mote pool advantage all the time, but I rarely hear about anyone bringing out a couple dozen Terrestrials in Wood Dragon armor and laying down a mote suppression field so severe that nearly all essence use is brought to a halt, which when you think about it means there is remarkably little dice difference between the Celestials and the run of the mill mortal legion soldiers.

                        Hell you could make an argument that the Empress had a plan for Celestials attacking the Blessed Isle since she is a shogunate era officer with a very clear understanding of what Celestial Exalts are. Imagine if the Terrestrials and the Sidereals built stand alone devices to generate the Wood Dragon Armor field, considering that they were integrated into the armor they would be simple to make as stand alones. Of course I would do something evil like upgrade them so they are more powerful. They could have been sitting in an imperial armory for centuries just waiting for this day to come.

                        I guess it is like playing Evolve, the terrestrials are the hunters and the Celestials are similar to (or in the case of Lunars actually are) the monster. You win by team work, as opposed to just throwing massed numbers at the enemy.

                        Imagine setting wood dragon armor devices up to create a nullification arena, then you send the mortal troops in to it to fight the Celestial. While that is happening the Terrestrials and perhaps the random Sidereal are making sniper shots from outside the field when an opportunity presents itself. Or they could soften up the Celestial before sending in the mortals by using artillery of some type to deplete what few uses of defensive charms they could have.

                        That would be a starting point, next you start asking yourself about the war manses that dot the landscape of the Blessed Isle. They power the Imperial Defense Grid, but if you consider the fact that you still have points left over after all the Relays, you can still have a functional manse. So what sort of battlefield applications could these manses have? Integrated Greater Elemental Lens from the Dragonfly boat that allows Terrestrial attack charms to be absolutely terrifying makes for one hell of a artillery piece.

                        Alternatively the manse could have been designed as an aid station to heal wounded and depleted soldiers. With the right integrated artifacts and manse powers a terrestrial can be fully healed and completely mote recharged within moments of entering the manse. This means that your Terrestrials can tag team the Celestials by swapping out before they get mote tapped or too badly damaged.

                        Of course I would do things like rig the manse with mote recharge and the Greater Elemental Lens so the Terrestrials never run out of juice.

                        So I would suggest thinking along the lines of how to make the Celestials fight at a disadvantage at every turn.
