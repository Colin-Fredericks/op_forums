            On the subject of Mnemon negotiating with the Mask of Winters:

            ​In its own context, I actually liked that, and did not feel as though it compromised the character of Mnemon or the importance of the civil war. I kind of feel as though the Deathlords should more often be treated as people with whom one can conduct diplomatic relations with in a manner that doesn't constitute being corrupted by them, and even in cases where people can be ideologically opposed to them. I feel as though there is plenty of room in the setting for alliances that are in the short-term, between people that would otherwise be enemies, and where nobody has any illusions about how long it can last (although where the follow-up isn't necessarily another war; soft power has its applications). In the machinations of the Realm civil war, I think there definitely should be somebody who would want to court the ruler of Thorns for supplies, aid, intelligence, or keeping Lookshy off their back. There should also be people courting Lookshy for the same (with the added benefit of keeping the Mask of Winters off of them). And the other side of the table is measuring their own costs and benefits, and what powers in the Realm would be to their advantage.

            ​I don't think the title of Deathlord should be an obstacle to this, whether it be something understood as meaning a lord of the dead, and even if one recognises that they're talking to somebody who intends for the end of all life.

            ​The real world has had alliances between Nazis and communists, in spite of both having ideologies very openly demanding the destruction of the other.

            ​At the same time, I acknowledge that the surrounding context in which that chapter fiction first appeared was... I presume after the point where the publications started leaning towards the Deathlords being the all consuming Big Bads of the setting, which made an application of somebody pragmatically making a short-term alliance with one of them problematic. Not entirely irreconcilable, but weighted. I'm not really trying to argue here, just feel prompted to lay out my own thoughts.

            ​I look at Mnemon as she's written now as somebody whose convictions are of the kind in which even a pragmatic alliance with a Deathlord would be unconscionable. Mind, that's not the same as meaning that she wouldn't do it, just that I imagine it would be a harder sell to her, and an action that she would feel a measure of disgust about rather than "mwahaha, I am getting ahead and I'm going to screw over this bastard in the end". I don't strictly think that this shift in portrayal is redressing a short-coming in how she was written previously, I just happen to find it a lot more compelling.

            I have approximate knowledge of many things.
