        I suppose my first surprise is 'every game ends well before E3', which is not really my experience. (Then again, my primary mode of Exalted these days is online play.) But every group is different and I confess I do like giving DBs a one-ability pseudo-Supernal so they can play with one signature charm, so I can see how other folks would think differently.

        Honestly some of the complaints come off to me as "how dare Dragon-blooded be designed like Dragon-blooded!" (Like the Elemental Sheath thing. A charm that only works if... a Terrestrial is using an artifact weapon of the magical material they're most likely to possess and use, and they're the splat most likely to have the resources to have an artifact weapon in the first place, what with ruling much of the world.)

        To a certain degree Terrestrials get unfairly looked at because in every edition they come right after Solars, but the Dragon-blooded of this edition are not exactly "just above mortals". We already have that as a category: that's "mortal" sorcerers, people with various unique supernatural merits, thaumaturgists, and the like. Certainly, comparing them negatively to 2nd edition Dragon-blooded seems, at best, ludicrous to me. That was the edition that gave Terrestrials a perfect defense, in a combat system where perfect defenses were very important, and that perfect could not be used against anything you actually needed to use a perfect against.

        At least, not until the Thousand Correct Actions errata and other 2.5-era adjustments, but it was still a half-measure and at no point did they bother to make Terrestrials feel like more than Solars with shitty Excellency caps that damaged everything in their vicinity as a consequence of using their powers.

        There's room for wriggling and house rules, but in every way the 3e treatment seems vastly superior to previous iterations of the concept and works closer to their thematics, not just invoking elements in fluff but working them into every tree in effect, and giving Terrestrials things that they are better than Solars at doing... without damaging their role as the "individually weakest but most numerous type of Exalted". No matter how hard they train, no Solar is ever going to get the full effect of Immaculate Martial Arts. (On the other hand, they can come close, and the Solars are going to be superior at all other Martial Arts styles.)

        That doesn't mean that the criticisms raised in this thread are without merit (some I agree with to a less serious degree, like the signature charm thing), but this is the first time I'm excited about playing a DB since first edition. And First Edition was mostly because it was something different after playing nothing but corebook Solars.

I really don't want to come across as a dick, and I know how big long posts can make it seem like I'm being a dick, I just think that the effects of the DB charmset are actually not nearly as bad as they look at first glance.

                                    I've used the example of a Fire aspected brawler, using Earth aspected charms in the traditionally Water aspected brawl tree. I'll just do a quick re-hash of that, but basically it works out like this, as of essence 2.

                                    Balanced Charms:
                                    Become the Hammer
                                    Pounding Surf Style
                                    Water Dragon Coils
                                    Inescapable Whirlpool Hold

                                    Earth Charms:
                                    Crushing Avalanche Grasp
                                    Stone Fist Strike
                                    Rolling Boulder Blow


                                    That's a decent charm spread which will keep you in Earth Aura until you go bonfire as a fire aspect, which is something that you need to control. Which you can do as long as you do things like spend 9 motes* on your attacks, and carefully pick your defenses. For example use Resistance charms like Impervious Skin of Stone to defend yourself, or keep your dodge charm usage to only Threshold Warding Stance. It's not super easy, certainly, but you're a friggen Fire aspect using the Earth branch of the Water ability, you couldn't be more twisted up in your elements if you tried.

                                    Even if you get forced out of your Earth Aura though, that's okay. What you lose is the ability to ignore successful hits on your rounds of control, and 1 point of Overwhelming for withering attacks. What you gain is now you're free to use any water aspected charms that aren't balanced that you may have picked up, so it's not the end of the world. The thing is, that's as hard as it ever gets, because if you were, say, an Earth aspect, then no worries, bonfire never knocks you out of aura and you can just sit there in it using those charms forever.


                                    Also, when you read through the charms you get the impression that you're useless without Aura, but actually when you play through it you can annihilate demons without even touching the powerful aura effects. Using Aura is more like a special reward for people who want to really focus hard on one element. If Aura effects weren't powerful you'd just be handicaped in that endevour, they just help even things out. Bolded because those two sentences are more important than the previous two paragraphs. I'll explain below.


                                    The other thing you mention is that the power of DB charms is underwhelming. Which is true compared to Solar charms, but DBs are still badasses. Like a fairly optimized chargen DB brawler can easily defeat a Blood-Ape, who's a terrifying overmuscled supernatural demon from hell, with his bare hands. Use Hammering Wave Technique and Oaken Thew Exertion to make your hands deal +22 base damage, rerolling 6s because of the excellency, then next turn grapple him and slam him into oblivion. The ape stands next to no chance, and it's only a matter of possible lucky rolls that will even make this a fight.

                                    Spoiler tags hold a quick math justification for this.

                                    So, you've got like 5/2/5 for physical stats, lets say. Something you can super easily get off chargen if you want.

                                    You attack with a full excellency, which gives you 18 dice to hit his parry of 4, on average ~5 overflow successes.

                                    Baseline damage is +22 with those charms, plus the 5 from the overflow that's 27 damage against his soak of 7. You're rolling 20 dice, rerolling 6s. So like 11 average damage there.

                                    With his join battle of 6, that means you just buried him on the first round, snagging 17 initiative for yourself.

                                    He attacks back, using his incredibly powerful howl ability, getting about 4.5 successes. If you dodge it and use Flickering Candle Meditation you're going to put your evasion at like, 8, while giving him a penalty to attack. That's almost an evasion equal to his attack pool.

                                    Next turn you take your 25 initiative and proceed to end the blood ape by whatever means you deem most satisfying.


                                    For the enemies that the Exalted book presents to us, DBs are actually more than powerful enough to deal with them. They're still head and shoulders above almost any natural or supernatural threat that they may face, the only difference is that if they get surrounded and outnumbered or something they might be in trouble. That's a problem Solars don't tend to have in my experience, but I think it can lead to some good drama and better stories honestly.

                                    Anyway this has gone on too long... Is there any one area specifically where you think DBs are super weak, or over reliant on Aura? I covered Brawl up above but maybe Archery or something has more problems?



                                    *I know spending 9 motes technically does increase your anima by one level, but first you need to blow through all your personal, and then spend 3 turns getting to that point, at which point the fight will be over. I have maybe once in my whole life seen a third edition battle that lasted more than 5 rounds.
