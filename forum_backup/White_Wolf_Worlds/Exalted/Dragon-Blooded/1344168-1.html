                    The Elemental Dragons created Exalted.

                    They ought to be written up in a manner consistent with how one views beings of comparable power to the Celestial Incarnae, because that is the basic standard for being able to create Exalted.

                    It really always had been, it just never needed to be stated before. Now we've got an Edition that has introduced more Exalted and has the premise of new kinds being made in the course of a story, so the standard needs to be more explicit.

                    That also being a compelling argument for why they don't really function as Third Circle Demons (not powerful enough) or behemoths (not enough spiritual depth).

                    So that's your starting point.

                                                        It could make sense for them to have the same names; the original preachers/compilers/constructors of the Immaculate religion naming their (constructed) messiahs after the Elemental Dragons, as part of an attempt at legitimacy. Most new monotheistic religions still call their supreme deity "God", because it makes them look more mainstream.

                                                        If they're not, then that would presumably either be because a)Dragonblood scholars didn't know the names of the Elemental Dragons (possible but unlikely), b)the Elemental Dragons don't actually have names, c)the Sidereals thought it would irritate the real Elemental Dragons to use their names, so made up names for the Immaculate Dragons, d)their true names are considered too holy to be known by mortals or the unitiated, and only the Mouth of Peace and top Immaculates actually know their real names.

                                                        It could also be that it's just different languages. I say God, my Chinese friends say Shengdi, Syrian Christians say Maryah Alaha, in Hebrew it's YHWH, etc. Adonia, El, El Shadai, Jehovah, Allah. Which one is "right"? I don't think there really is a single "correct" one.

                                                        Maybe Hesiesh, Sextis Jylis, Mela, Daana'd and Pasiap are the old Earthtongue forms of the Dragons' names, and their real names (in Old Realm, I guess) are different.

                                                        In the game I run, where no PC has Occult or Lore 5 or a background at a famous university or something (the one mental character is a swamp shaman), and they mostly just speak Skytongue (the shaman also speaks Old Realm, the merchant also speaks Riverspeak and Guild Cant), the names of the Dragons are mostly confused and mixed. They've heard talk about Sutr, ruler of Muspelheim; is that Hesiesh? Is it not? They've heard that Jormungundr, the World Serpent, is causing earthquakes. Is that Pasiap? Gaia? Different gods, elementals, Raksha, locals, Dragonblood who may be Dynasts or from the Shogunate, etc, all use different and confusing terms. I only stuck with Mela for the Air Dragon, because I felt that being the Air Dragon, people in the North-North-East would have a more consistent way to talk about her than they would the Fire Dragon or Water Dragon.
                                                        Not, of course, that cultures present her consistently. The Raksha described her as the Dragon Queen of Wind and Frost, daughter of Jormungundr and Granddaughter of the giant Ymir, whose bones and blood make the earth. The Dragonblood who were the main antagonists for the first story arc talked about her as a (human-shaped) warrior-scholar. The peasantry consider her the Queen of the court of Winter Gods.
                                                        But they all called her Mela whether in Skytongue or Old Realm (partly so the PCs don't get confused; I want them to be a bit confused, but not too much!).

                                                        Now whether she calls herself Mela when the PCs meet her... I think even if she doesn't consider that her name (I imagine her actually using some extremely conceptual thing for her name, like a particular roar of Air, or maybe a string of complex syllables with deep meaning in Old Realm), if she's speaking (or roaring wind?) in Skytongue, she may answer to Mela because, well, that's what mortals call her. Maybe depends how irritatable and arrogant she's feeling.

                                                        Like, when I lived in China the second time, my teacher called me 欧子, and that's fine. My friend Liu has 3 names; his Mandarin name (Liu), his Manchurian name (Aisin Giorio), and his English name (Sherlock). No problem, no insult.
                                                        But then, we're not Dragons.
