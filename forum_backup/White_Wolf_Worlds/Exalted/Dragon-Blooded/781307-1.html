            Isator said it better than me.

            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
            Incidentally, the term "cannon fodder" often gets misused by people who aren't really aware of a prior tactical reality in which certain troops getting indiscriminately cut down by weapons fire was kind of an inescapable reality, and even then it refers mostly to an idea of either putting your least valuable troops at the front of the formation so that the ones killed in the opening volleys are a minimal loss, or the ineptitude of commanders advancing troops in maneuvers in which they'll be unnecessarily exposed to volleys for minimal gain.

            It does not refer to the basic, unfortunate reality that in heavy fighting, you are going to get a fair amount of troops get torn apart indiscriminately or that part of commanding a battle includes a willingness to order troops to their deaths.

            Which are things I would expect to be part of the assumptions and experience of the Dragon Blooded. Not always quite as enthusiastic as the aforementioned War Boys leaping into a literally explosive fray, but I would say that qualities including their numbers and means of replacement mean that in military matters, there can be an awareness and acceptance of the idea that being on the front lines means that somebody is sure to die, or that there are going to be operations in which one is asked to risk or give their life. I mean, there are surely any Exalted who are going to be capable of such sentiments, some Solar Exalt who would opt to stay in a mortally dangerous situation so that others might escape despite some quasi-rationalists insistence that their life is surely of greater value than that of any number of mortals and they should prioritize their own safety and future, but... again, it's a matter of assessing culture and attitude, even practical results.

            To be Terrestrial Exalted is not to be insignificant or interchangeable, but they can be many in a way that means the world doesn't stop turning and the fight doesn't stop raging if some should fall. Not because they were worthless, but because they know that there's a sibling or a child or a comrade waiting at their back to step into their place and continue forward. A sense of expandability not because some distant commander saw you only as a pawn to be moved or sacrificed at their will, but because you know your death won't be the end of things, and can even create openings to achieve things.

            You know, little things that place the Dragon Blooded in a context of agency and humanity and basic dignity, rather than a product in some manse fetishism.

            Also things that lend insight into the actual priority behind Dragon Blooded families; you're not making a horde, you're binding together partners and ensuring a line of succession.

            Referring to the idea of people as cannon fodder with sincerity and enthusiasm, well... if you've ever read any poetry by a World War I veteran in which the writer elaborates on their mildly exaggerated view of their armchair generals, you might have some indications as to their character.

                                            I wonder if the number of them might have come across worse when presented in terms of them being already organised into quasi-Great Houses. There were, what, five hundred of those? So you end up with family units consisting of about two thousand Exalted each?

                                            I dispute the point of them being nameless and indiscriminate (or at least of its significance, since that's really going to be the case anyway), but it feels a bit... heavy.

                                            At the same time... I think the presentation of a large number might create that sense of "Dragon Blooded are doing things everywhere", which rather than coming across as expendable makes them feel far too necessary and ubiquitous, at the same time as it doesn't actually hold up when the ratios are considered.

                                            These are all the kinds of things that make me view the Dragon Blooded of the First Age as having been more about expanding laterally; that it was better to be an individual and went off to form families with "locally" prominent figures to spread out dynasties that could produce one or two Exalted per generation to carry out the presence of the Realm, or for the powerful family lines to be based on ownership of significant rights and properties while also producing favoured children who could go off and join the central or patrolling institutions of the Realm, or attach themselves to the projects of the Solar Exalted.

                                            To the extent that such a thing is significant in games, I could put hints of it into First Age stories for Exalted characters to come across for the sake of contrasting eras or having insight into alternative social models.
