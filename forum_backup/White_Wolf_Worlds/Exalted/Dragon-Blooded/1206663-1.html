        Solar Exalted seem to have more Charms that hit (or can hit) single targets multiple times, whereas Dragon-Blooded seem to have more Charms that must hit multiple targets.

        Hammer on Iron Technique versus Fist-Spinning Maelstrom. Also, Hanging Tree Technique.

        Solar Brawl's Falling Hammer Strike allows a single Solar to keep stacking onslaught penalties on a target, as well as to preserve the penalties of Ox-Stunning Blow, and Ferocious Jab exploits onslaught penalty for greater damage.

        Meanwhile, Dragon-Blooded Brawl's Hammering Wave Technique is nowhere near as reliable as Falling Hammer Strike (though it at least boosts damage), so the Dragon-Blood needs to rely more on allies to impose onslaught penalties before she attacks. Pleasantly, Pounding Surf Style and Hammer Wave Technique lead upwards into other team-oriented Charms like Become the Wave and Swift-Striking Tide.


        The design intent seems to be making Dragon-Blooded effective team-players against single opponents, for players fighting a boss character, while also making high-level Dragon-Blooded effective boss characters against teams of player characters.

        Solar Exalted, on the other hand, have no particular weakness when working in a team, but their Charms are generally built to allow them to fight alone and win, as long as they are allowed to fight in their specialty. There's no particular emphasis about fighting single opponents or greater numbers, either.

                                I think there's a trade-off between potential for high-rated artifacts and cheapness of low-rated artifacts.
                                Imago-Hatching Realization costs 10 motes, 1 wp, and 5 gxp per use, +1 wxp if it's not the first use in the story. All my calculations that suggest it is the best signature involve using it on every roll (ie, 6 times, maybe +-1 once you get a second signature).
                                Blazing Dragon-Smith Arete costs 15 motes, 1 wp for the project. Also, its power is being brought down by the lowered terminus, but taking advantage of the lower terminus requires spending gxp for the roll, and paying for all your other charms again. Also, it gives +1 wxp for completing the project.
                                Eternal Omphalos Forge is somewhere in between, costing 15m, 1 wp, 15 gxp for the project, but increasing the terminus needs spending even more to take advantage of it (though you can alternately use it on some very easy project, just to gain a bunch of wp).                                 Ephemeral Form Composition is purely for efficiency, and doesn't grant power at all
                                Fortune-from-Flotsam Ingenuity isn't giving you any benefit to artifacts, instead giving speed on major projects.

                                            Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                            You don't want to talk to people defending the Charm... OK. But I think for the sake of good discussion, you should at least explain your claim to those of us confused by it.
                                            Sure, no problem.

                                            Shadow-Immolating Talon is a charm where you hurt someone for information. It instantly forces an honest confession out of someone to whom you deal health levels of deal damage, even if you kill them in the process. It even refunds the cost and doesn't need to be reset when your target doesn't know the answer. You can immediately try it again on your next target.

                                            Torture in the real world provides the torturer only with whatever the victim thinks the torturer wants to hear. Shadow-Immolating Talon gets you the truth or the fact that your victim doesn't know. This isn't just fantasy-world torture, it's a description of the fantasy that torture proponents have about how torture works. Fast, effective, and without any serious risk of false information. That's why it bothers me. It's not the effects of the charm per se that are problematic, it's how close those effects are to real-world misconceptions that ruin lives.

                                            Like I said, I don't think that whomever wrote this had this intention. I hope they change things before it goes into print.

                                            Tangential note: With the bonus effects and the refund, there's no reason an Investigation-heavy Dragon-Blooded wouldn't want to use this on every decisive attack they make, just to see if they can get some unexpected information out of their foes. If you yell "Where is the Scarlet Empress?" on every blow, your enemy either knows and tells you (OMGWTF hooray!), or doesn't know, and you got some bonus dice and double 10s on damage for free.

                                            Edited to add: also this is not really what I came here to talk about in the first place...
