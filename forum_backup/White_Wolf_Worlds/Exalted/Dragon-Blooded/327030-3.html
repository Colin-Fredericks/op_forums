        Now there is a lot to like about the dragon-blooded they allow stories of a scope just above the mortal to practically any problem with Essence and numbers. This also make them much easier for a GM to handle since most things challanging a human scaled up to 11 will work as a credible threat/challange to DB's where a solar need a supernatural challange at every corner to even notice it is there. They are more likely to actually do things that look like co-operation in combat as they are not as powerful and have more options to do so then solars and other exalts, who usually go at things alone or in small groups because it works. It is a lot easier to integrate them into a context as they are the only splat with an actual connection to the world that isn't 100% in the hands of the GM and player. Also anyone who looks at the Exalting will see that any splat is a wonderful antagonist splat but only the DB's are really an underdog allowing for a lot of room for being the heroes in a action/horror/thriller movie like story with Celestial antagonists.

        Now most of this is accompained by horrible charms that need a complete rework to actually be much fun or even thematically anything but a weak version of solars. In my last solar campaign using 2E rules the players where much more concerned being boxed in by multiple anima effects then actual attacks by DB's at least initially. I would not have been running a DB game as I am currently without rewriting the charms more or less entirely and introducing alternative paths to power.

        What the new DB book really needs is a good example of a few hundred DB's coming together with a couple of tens of thousands of mortal followers and stomping hard on something that is percieved as really dangerous, a deathlord or something and then for the book to allow that to happen mechanically.

                                                    Solars and Infernals both got multiple books with quality mechanics, and in the case of Infernals, the "big thing" for the edition, Devil Tigers. The Reclamation is presented as the thing happening right now in the setting, even getting a huge adventure book devoted to it, even if it was shite. Solars get things like cap bursters that let them play with toys that legitimately should be beyond the reach of any "normal" campaign because, well, what's the point of writing a hundred high Essence Charms for them if no one gets to use them?

                                                    Dragon-Blooded got shit mechanics, including the "fails against magic" clause, which is so nebulous as to make a lot of their Charms potentially useless depending on how you interpret it. They are disrespected in almost every mention, including the line about how gods view them as less than mortals, because at least mortals can get a "real" Exaltation. The Realm is also presented as a complete, toothless joke that any Solar should be able to conquer on a lazy Saturday when they're not feeling particularly ambitious.

                                                    Sidereals got absolutely catastrophic mechanics, basically a slapdash copy-paste from 1e that did nothing to adjust them to fit new mechanical conceits within the edition. The Bureau of Destinty is presented as completely incompotent, which then makes sense when they're casually wiped out in the aforementioned shite adventure because the writers clearly couldn't or were too lazy to account for their impact on the adventure as written. Garbage like Sidereals being executed by Heaven for anything short of high treason also makes its way into the setting.

                                                    Lunars get shit mechanics, but in contrast to Dragon-Blooded and Sidereals are let off easy setting-wise because other than Thousand Streams River, they're pretty much completely ignored.

                                                    Abyssals get shit mechanics (notice a pattern here?) and basically shoehorned into "Deathlord's bitch" or "Redemption" arcs.
