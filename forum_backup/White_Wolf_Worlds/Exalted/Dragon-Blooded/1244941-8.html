    Fitter Happier, I don't think you're wrong, though as we're on the whys I think you may be underestimating the Realm as empire of faith and ideology and as defence.

    Even if the Realm stood in a region to gain no profit, even should the entire Threshold not be profitable, my guess is they'd go out and conquer it because the Immaculate Philosophy presents the Perfected Hierarchy, with the Dragon blooded in command, as the appropriate spiritual condition of the world. If there were nothing to extract, they'd grumble and spend the revenues from their isle holdings to no material profit to uphold their belief.

    (This is not a "good" motivation really, its just not purely material. At the same time, its the ideology that does frame that quote by GCG about how (paraphrasing) Dynasts can be enormously brutal rulers who then go out and sacrifice themselves in battle for some uneconomically unimportant village, and that they often genuinely do aspire to being the enlightened samurai supermen that the Immaculate Philosophy says they are.)

    There's also the defense aspect; even if the Threshold were bleeding money from isle holdings, the Realm would run its empire because they are scared of not having a buffer of states between them and mortal kingdoms, the Lunar Exalted, dead and fae, and other powers (and for good reason).

    (Again not really "good" and self interest of a sort, but not material in the sense of motivated only by wealth).

    DrLoveMonkey, that could work, though "nation building" is hard and full of unintended consequence, as has been discovered, and I think most potential nations of Creation would be harder, and compromise with the powers about would change how that works out. But I guess would strike me as something more likely for powerful earth people transported to Creation (A Connecticut Yankee stuff) not something that people in the setting would immediately leap to, or evolve through experience, and less so a Solar Exalted with visions of the First Age in his head.

        Looking back at one of Dan Olson's videos about Man of Steel, I feel a need to remind myself that there's a certain necessary metatextual analysis of the apocalyptic scenario that the Realm was given in the first place, and how much the underlying premise of that might constitute an apologia for imperialism. It's like Lioness referenced a few posts back; the point that a good deal of fantasy and science fiction revolves around irreconcilably hostile enemies as a means of letting certain kinds of power fantasies play out without criticism.

        Or hell, even in-character, there might be somebody who has a basis for making a certain Mad Max Fury Road argument to the effect of "no, our broken down world does not in fact necessitate or benefit from this kind of militaristic authoritarian power structure, because that (or at least the premises and world view underlying it) is what got us into this mess in the first place".

        I wouldn't exactly call the Scarlet Empress evil, but for a character profile I'd at least go with the idea that her critical examination of the failure points of the Shogunate would have been rather limited.

            Originally posted by Verzio View Post
            Well, no, it's really not the same.

            Sure, there is a passing resemblance between "Our empire protects civilization from being destroyed by barbarians" and "Our empire protects humanity itself from being utterly annihilated", but they are substantially different, starting with the fact that the barbarians are part of humanity. When barbarians knock over empires, at worst you get a dark age, and if you don't happen to be one of the peasants killed in the war itself, it's not clear how much you actually suffer because "civilization" fell. I mean, art and literature suffer, sure. Urban life declines. Ways of life change. But for the rural peasant farmer, how much does one actually notice the difference between the Romans and the Franks? Because only insofar as Roman oppression is better than life under the Franks is the Roman oppression justified to the peasant.

            On the other hand, the Balorian Crusade, if successful, would have meant the extinction of humanity and the end of all Creation.

            So when you're considering putting down a peasant revolt that really was a justified reaction against the excesses of a satrap, the balance-points differ. "They're weakening the bulwark that allows art and literature and urban life to flourish, therefore we must put down the justified-in-itself revolt" is morally a very different proposition than, "They're weakening the bulwark that allows humanity, including themselves and their loved ones, to exist, therefore we must put down the justified-in-itself revolt."

            Now, that doesn't make the Realm's crimes not crimes, and it is a perfectly fair question how much the Realm actually protects existence from extinction. But "we protect humanity from nonexistence" is a far, far better argument for empire than "we protect civilization".
            It's actually worth noting, that in 3e at least, there probably isn't probably ever going to be, nor ever was going to be, another Balorian Crusade. It was a one-off event that likely Creation might just not see again. A big reason 3e is moving away from existential, world-ending plots like a second Baloriain Crusade is actually so that things like this well, do have ambiguity. The Realm ciould say they are the balwark agains the Fair Folk coming back, but to be fair, they never were going to come until Balor and they never will come again in the lifetime of an emprie.

            EDIT: Basically, giving the empire "No, really we're the necessary evil" is probably not good if you want to not seem to be indulging in imperial apoligia I htink.

                Originally posted by Verzio View Post
                On the other hand, the Balorian Crusade, if successful, would have meant the extinction of humanity and the end of all Creation.
                The Balorian Crusade was ended by the Realm Defense Grid. Nothing any Exalted outside the Empress and her companions did had anything to do with it; as far as we know, it would have gone down exactly the same way if she had been the only Dragon-Blood in existence.

                But much more importantly, the Realm Defense Grid continued to protect against Fair Folk incursions regardless of what else the Empress did. The argument that she provided some additional protection by immediately turning around and aiming it at the remnants of the Shogunate is flatly a lie (if anything, she left the Dragon-Blooded weaker in the immediate aftermath of that conflict.)

                It's not like you can really argue that the Fair Folk Invasion was a failure of the Shogunate, even. It's not at all clear that there's anything they could have done against the one-two punch of the Great Contagion and the Fair Folk invasion. And if the Realm Defense Grid broke down at any point in the Realm's history, followed by another Contagion + Fair Folk invasion, the Realm would have collapsed just as fast as the Shogunate did (in fact, probably faster.) Brutally murdering a bunch of Dragon-Blooded in order to form a new political order with her at the head didn't really make Creation any safer or stronger. All it accomplished was ensuring that its political order had a single point of failure and could easily fail catastrophically.

                The Empress could have chosen to maintain the Realm Defense Grid as a reclusive monk who used it only against dangers to Creation, for instance, refusing to get involved in any political struggles between nations. She could have recruited and trained a small force to back this up. She could even, you know, have taught someone else to use it, which would be pretty important if, say, she were to unexpectedly die or disappear or something. She did none of these things.

                (The last one in particular is probably her most glaring failure. But it's illustrative in that it shows how she was either primarily concerned with maintaining and extending her own power; or completely consumed with paranoia to the point where she didn't feel that anyone but her could be trusted to safeguard Creation's future.)

                        I do wonder if, in a world where we had to rewrite Exalted's base setting assumptions, I wouldn't do away with the RDG in favour of the Empress as more of a directly Shi Huang Di esque figure, putting together the strongest state among feuding Dragonblooded through radical reforms, harnessing the power of faith in the new Immaculate Philosophy as her state ideology and securing the backing of the Bronze Faction, then unifying the warring states. And make all this still very morally questionable as a long term path for the world and at the time.

                        Maybe even do away with the Balorian Crusade and Great Contagion as singular things, rather than the slow toll of disorder among the Dragonblooded and inadequacy to deal with smaller incursions and the gradual failure of First Age infrastructure in the absence of Solar geniuses. Plus attribute more loss since the First Age to Usurpation era destruction.

                        There are just so many questions that the apocalypse elements introduce - "How is this actually compatible with Sidereal astrology and the Great Prophecy?" being a recent one we've gone over and over - and the means that they are resolved are treated somewhat as an afterthought to the actual politics and central concerns of the setting - to exaggerate:"Oh hai guys, btw, the numberless Fair Folk could almost destroy the world, the Deathlords have super-bio weapons, the Sidereals could do nothing to plan for or prevent any of this, but it's all good because the Empress has a cache of nukes (which only very occasionally enter as a deus ex machina) and we're just not gonna talk about this too much in the actual business of the setting". The tools the Empress is described as using to build her Realm are not that integral to the means she used to gain it...

                        Either that or walk into these elements and yeah, work to more actively make maintaining a continued bulwark keeping the Fae divided, maintaining magical defenses of the Realm, managing plagues of magical origin, using magical weapons to maintain and hold power, worrying about the emergence of apocalyptic forces; all of this much more of a element of pre-occupation to the status quo powers of the setting, and something which they *do*.
