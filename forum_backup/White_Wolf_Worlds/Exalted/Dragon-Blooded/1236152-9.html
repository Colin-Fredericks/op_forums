    Originally posted by aluminiumtrioxid View Post


    they certainly are if you ignore native charms in favor of IMA and resonant evocations huehuehue

    (My low-effort trolling aside, I actually think there's an interesting dynamic in the decision to go hog wild with MA/Evocations versus investing in native Charms - the martial artist will definitely dominate during the 30 sessions of early game and probably for another story arc thereafter due to DXP pileup allowing an immediate completion of a MA style if training times are not strictly observed, but someone who uses their DXP to bump multiple abilities up to 5 and buys a crapton of sigs right out of the gate will be able to compete rather effectively.)
    Okay, but you can't buy Signature Charms out the gate. By the time you can buy Signature Charms, 4 out of 5 times the game is either over, or it's about to end. Every game I've ever seen, with a single exception, over five years of near-exclusively playing Exalted, ends before 150xp. Usually around the 100xp mark, or about 20 sessions. Whether group friction, or some new distraction came out, or the ST getting overwhelmed by the demands of the game. Games really don't last long, not unless you get a special group of PCs to keep everyone glued to it. That's why I hate Signature Charms, they're concentrating the coolest things Dragonblooded are capable of around the point games tend to collapse, and the effects of Aura mean that the early game is going to be miserable, as you need to work your way around your Charms shutting off whenever you need an effect to spike.

    The Solar Charmset, meanwhile, gives you Supernals, so you can play with cool effects (Not that I want DBs to have Supernals, but it's a mitigating factor for a real problem), and more importantly the Essence 1 game of the Solar is interesting and engaging, while the E1 Dragonblooded game is like..please god no. The fact that they start at E2 helps, but bought Excellencies damage your ability to reach the fun Charms, and again every single time I'm left throwing up my hands in frustration, buying a bunch of Excellencies, and resolving to write Jade Evocations that actually feel fun.

    And then I realize that my favorite Exalted splat has such a mess of a Charmset that I'm trying to find ways to avoid engaging with it. And I get really depressed and don't talk to anyone for awhile.

    It doesn't matter if the DB who works their way to signature Charms will be stronger than my Evocation using Martial Artist. He'll never have the chance to reach me.

                        You're absolutely correct. Designing a core functionality of a splat around an assumption that you will need 30 sessions to get there is absolutely ignorant of they way most games are played at the table. Even if you do get a game the group sticks with, scheduling issues will definitely crop up, meaning that the real-life time commitment before you actually get your cool toys is going to skyrocket. (I just tried to look up how long it took us to get to session 30 in the longest-running campaign our group played. Since we wrote session summaries on an ad-hoc basis, and individual summaries sometimes encompassed events from multiple sessions, I couldn't actually determine the exact cutoff point, but one thing is certain: finishing our first major story arc took us less than 20 sessions, which we took four years to play through. And that campaign started when some of us were still in the first few years of university, with practically unlimited free time.)

                        On the other hand, the 30 session requirement does seem to serve a design purpose: the game wants you to stock up on enough DXP to be able to buy up all the abilities you want to sig up to 5 while still having enough to cover your MA/Evocations/Sorcery needs. I strongly suggest bumping up DXP values or making up new categories you can earn DXP in if you lower the XP requirement for E3 (which you should do).

                                                    Originally posted by Sunder the Gold View Post
                                                    But every Signature Charm requires five dots in the Ability. Most Balanced Charms are around the 1-3 range.
                                                    Yeah I'm not a huge fan of that. Exalted as a whole has a weird design slant towards maximum abilities. Solar's Feats of Strength tree goes 3>5>5>5>5>5, the DB weapon summoning tree goes 1>3>5, the Solar medicine efficiency tree goes 1>5>5>5>5>5>5. I'm really not super happy with that as a whole. I think it's a poor design decision, but if you're going to be grabbing signature charms for abilities that you use most often, then you'd probably end up in the range of 4-5 for it anyway. Like if you're getting the only other dedicated water dodge charm Ebbing Tide Recedes, you've got dodge 4.

                                                    The nice thing about the dodge signatures, since dodge is a tree that doesn't have a lot of non-fire charms in it, almost all of them involve expending your aura to take effect. So if you take a bunch of non-balanced charms you can use them no problem after your second action.

                                                    For what it's worth the Unmoving Center of Enlightenment is a really good charm, even by signature standards. It can lock down an entire enemy force into doing nothing but trying and failing to run while you and your entire Hearth beat them down with impunity. It can even just end a combat against a single incredibly powerful creature unless they've got really great resolve or a damn good disengage pool. Like if some hotshot Solar swordsman relies on their parry and has 0 dodge, they're one good (Stamina + Dodge) roll away from just losing this combat to a single charm. Unless I'm reading it wrong again.
