                                              The latest thread summaries from RPG.net (and we owe Minton for that; I used to spend a lot of time there but not so much anymore and sifting those threads is a pain) have Holden saying that the plan at least was to have the first supplement come roaring out hot on the heels of the big release. So there's that.

                                              I think it might be time for some historical perspective on this, with the caveat that Onyx Path is not White Wolf and that this information is between ten and fifteen years old.

                                              Wikipedia has an excellently complete list of all Exalted publications here. Disregarding the Storyteller's Companions, lets see what the first two editions managed in their first two to three months of release, and how quick they got their first fatsplats out. I will summarize.

                                              The 1e Corebook dropped in August '01, at GenCon if I'm remembering properly. 1e actually managed to drop Scavenger Sons almost simultaneous with the Corebook and got Book of Three Circles and a Caste Book out within two months. After that things slowed down, likely due to holidays and due to the fact they were probably waiting to see if this new, untried property would catch on. (I also wonder how, if at all, 9/11 impacted the development cycle.) The Dragon-Blooded fatsplat took eight whole months to hit and people were really champing for it by the time it arrived.

                                              (Sidebar: 1e Dragon-Blooded was deeply infamous for having really, really shitty binding; many if not mos peoples copies fell apart on them to a greater or lesser extent.)

                                              So that was 1e.

                                              The 2e core hit in March of '06. Between then and July we got Wonders of the Lost Age, Compass: Scavenger Lands, and MoEP: Dragon-Blooded. The release curve generally looks smoother.

                                              For both editions they got their second fatsplat out a little over a year after the core, and in both cases it was Lunars.

                                              So that was the prior editions.

                                              What has me slightly perturbed is that, while those are very acceptable release schedules post-launch, and the overall schedules were deeply aggressive (there were long stretches of time where you could count on a new Exalted thinsplat every six weeks or so) the devs have talked a lot about how for the big books, especially in 2e, they were hampered by needing to pull the trigger on stuff that was obviously not ready to go out the door for scheduling reasons. And that was in the White Wolf days, when they had all the publishing muscle of a decade-old publishing house that was known for the wild success of its lines behind them. We now have none of that. This is good from the perspective that CCP is unlikely to ever demand Onyx Path kick something out the door to meet an arbitrary release date... but it also probably means that 3e is going to have a much less aggressive release schedule than both prior editions did, which means the big anchoring fatsplats seem likely to have long, long waits between them. I personally doubt we're going to see Lunars anytime before mid-2017, as they've got to get Dragon-Blooded AND Exigents out before'em, and Exigents are completely uncharted territory.

                                              It does mean when we do get them, they'll be done right, or at least if they're done badly it won't be for lack of time spent. But I'd prep for more of these long, agonizing waits.
