    Dragon-Blooded and Elemental Shintai

    02-02-2014, 02:17 AM
    As I understand it, the plan for 3e is to let give Infernals a build-your-own shintai that lets them reflect their personal Infernalness.

    Will Dragon-Blooded PCs have something akin to this? Instead of buying charms or elemental martial art techniques, could they have a sort of build-your-own elemental form?

    For example, my fire aspect DB could transform into a higher form with obsidian like skin, a corona of heat, the speed of a flickering flame, and a voice that melts brass. A water aspect DB could create a forum where they can flow between the cracks in wall, drown people within themselves, and no longer needs to breath air.

    Come and rock me Amadeus.

                                                          Originally posted by Blackwell View Post
                                                          Even if it's not natively supported, I think there's room for it. DBs have this interesting but rarely seen theme of... I don't know what to call it, maybe "evolvable"? I'm thinking of things like the Numina of the Mist, and to a lesser extent Dragons of a Different Color and Immaculate Grand Masters.
                                                          Also Lintha Ng Hut Dukantha and the seemingly really-popular-with-fans Saibok Gauto. There might be some more.

                                                          Dragonbloods seem like they have tended to get the broadest number of mentions of opportunity to transcend their nature. Transcending (and changing) paths are present in the splats who tend to be more denied the opportunity to make their own destiny on their merits (or lack thereof), or have limits to their destiny. (There's a dash of this in Sidereal Martial Arts as well, Infernals and Abyssals have sort of variants).

                                                          That's fine. Still I think a lot of that appeal for Dragonblooded will be less with Exigents around and so more scope for Celestial level power while playing to other themes (even if this a narrow slice of what Exigents are for), which the Devil Tigers made apparent was massively popular.

                                                          And with less of a build up of the Solar-Other gap, and less a gonzo high power focus, it probably will feel less like there idea that being a super-Dragonblooded is necessary to matter as an individual.

                                                          Also, on Exigents again, in a world where a lot more is possible as far as being an Exalt (there's all these weird guys around!), a Dragonblooded transcending its nature becomes less unusual, less threatening to the grand hierarchy of being (with Solars at its head, descending downwards through clear tiers) and so in a way (oddly?) less compelling to fans.

                                                          Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post
                                                          I think what you're looking for is an exigent of some element. DBs don't really do the kind of think you're talking about. They're more about viewing elements through the lens of abilities and then through another lens of martial application. Hense charms in the presence tree that are used to scream somebody's head off in combat.
                                                          Yeah, with the Dragonblooded, the main restraining factor for me with elemental evolution, aside from power level and all that, is that you want the Dragonblooded Host to feel like these fairly bloodied Spartan, samurai guys who train relentlessly hard at the skills of war, intrigue and empire, and to have that come through in their magic (yes, balanced with a dash of "unfairly advantaged beyond mortals").

                                                          Not so much these a magocracy whose power more springs forth from meditating upon and channeling the elements. Of course channeling the elements is important to them, but they're really not a magocracy of element-benders.

                                                          So they feel like their magic needs a "harder"(?) rooting in skill than the Infernals...
