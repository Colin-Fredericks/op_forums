    Originally posted by Metrophelean View Post
    1) It is theme. The Charms need to be named, so give them thematically appropriate, over-the-top titles to better flavor magic. It's much cooler than MELEE POWER 1, TIER 1. It was mentioned somewhere (though fuck if I can find the source) that the original Charm names were close to as godawful as that example. The choice was made to fix that, because boring.
    Originally posted by Tiresias View Post
    Legend has it that charms originally had really boring, self-evident names until Geoff Grabowski went and rewrote them to make them more interesting.
    My extensive research (visiting the Charms section of the What We Know Wiki) bears fruit!
    Originally posted by John Mørke
    Solar Charms originally had names like Extra Health Levels (Ox-Body Technique) and Multiple Attacks (Iron Whirlwind Attack), before Geoff went through and renamed them. He didn't change their functions, though. If you had Ox-Body Technique, you were playing a vital person with the constitution of an ox. If you had Iron Whirlwind Attack, you were just playing a guy who was really good at multiple attacks. Charms move in themed strings because they represent skill in certain areas. A character who is a walking blender is represented by putting 24 exp into the Multiple Attacks part of the tree; a guy who is about ki direction puts it into the Solar bolt part of the tree. Each part of the tree is a simplification; a list of purchases that help to depict a character's skill from the lower end to the upper end.
    Also,
    Originally posted by Metrophelean
    2) Legacy. It may have been decided in an earlier edition (in fact, there are references to 'scrolls' and 'books' of Charms in 2e -- but, like Reddit, I'm too lazy to source) that charms were very well categorized. I personally hate this. It's like talking in-character about health levels, or attributes "PRAY LISTEN, MORTAL. FOR MY CHARISMAS ARE RAD LARGE!"

        Although they have only been briefly mentioned by John, I think the "bloodline charms" will help a lot to depict the fact that DB society has been around for a long time and that their lineages have advanced in different ways.

I mean that throughout DB history, custom uses of essence (I mean creation of new charms) could have been discovered by different individuals who have passed them down only to their descendants.

        I really hope to see that some families are able to use cool charm trees and be distinguished for that. Just like Toph is famous for being the first earth bender to bend metal, so can DB families be recognized for such feats.

            To expand on what I said earlier: your character doesn't wake up one day and think "I really want to learn Warlord's Convocation! I can use it to influence people!" Rather, you, as the player, think "My character is a charismatic leader of men who can easily win the loyalties of people. To represent this mechanically, I will use the Warlord's Convocation Charm."

            I will also join Metrophelean and Gaius and say that, most of the time, I really dislike the idea of characters using Charms by name. A Dawn doesn't know he's using the Charm Fire and Stones Strike any more than a D&D fighter knows he's using the feat Power Attack. Though there are exceptions. For example, I figure that the ability to see Essence is probably called the All-Encompassing Sorcerer's Sight in some First Age texts. Actually, it probably has a bunch of other fancy names, too, because people in Creation like fancy names. Most of the time, though, people wouldn't actually use that name (even if they know about it): they're just going to say they can see Essence.

            This is also depends a lot on the type of being. Sidereals have more Charms that are flavorful to name in-charcter: I fully encourage a Sidereal to explain that he used the Earth and Sky Bargain to speed his recovery after a grave injury. A Jadeborn Warrior Caste who has fully mastered the Fivefold Embodiment of Jade should be able to brag about it. And an Alchemical would be downright disappointing if he couldn't point on his body and say "this is my Electrification Onslaught Dynamo, that one is my Momentum Redirection Pulse Injector..."

                    As many have mentioned, Charms are NOT spells like in Dungeons & Dragons.
                    Charms are like they are for the players benefits and because we need some mechanics to describe the characters, that's all.
                    In-Universe, they are application of Essence to various abilities the character ALREADY possess. That is why most charms have an Essence and a Skill requirement. You must ALREADY be a silver tongued charmer to learn how to use Essence to make your natural charm even better.

                    That is why the only things that could be recognized, in the core book of 2E, were Combos. Obvious charms were just an obvious use of Essence, ie. you could see the guy was using magic, but not what type, but Combos had names and special poses/gestures/moves that a rival could identify and then understand what the combo did.

                    However, Essence is definitely something that exist in-universe and is well understood, especially in the Realm. So while young dragon blooded might not be told directly "beware of the charm X", they would be aware to a point that people can do X, Y and Z through use of Essence.

                    The Devs have already stated about the new Dragon Blooded that we will see differences of note in the various Dynastic houses, maybe up to have exclusive charm lines common only to certain houses. Those would be the equivalent of secret training techniques that teach a dragon blooded to shape his Essence in certain specific ways and when united with mundane training, can obtain unique results.

                    This statement also bode well for having more details about the internal working of The Realm as a whole.

                            Well, a thesis that charms are personal feats of ability and Essence manipulation is as strong as easy it is to customise and create new charms. Because let's be honset here- if you can't create new charms or change existing ones in a meaningful way, then every character will have the same charms and that creates an impretion that they are indeed distinctive techniques that you can categorise and teach.

                            The alternative is making charms less numerous, but also broader and customizable visually so they can feel like they are different for each person. But this seems to be the opposite of what the Devs are doing with charms.

                            And then you have Dynastic Charms, Sorcerous Charms, Supernatural Martial Arts and a huge part of Sidreal Charms, which ARE distinctive techniques you can categorise and teach. We're left in this nebulous space where most of the charms are NOT distinctive and singular techniques, but the charmsets as a whole are not really well equiped to model every single possible power an individual can have and there are also charms that ARE distinctive and singular, but follow exactly the same rules as those that AREN'T. It's terribly confusing.

                            Personally, I like the idea that every charm is a power singular to the individual who developed it. But while making them so crunchy, you just can't make enough of them to account for every single ability a character might want to have, so there should be solid and easy guidelines to creating custom charms.

                                I definitely like the 'innate' conception of charms, but that doesn't entail that they can't be systematically characterized, named, whatever. Terms like "riposte" describe a fairly large category of physical movements, but it's a certainly an intelligible idea; it makes sense to give the whole family of things a name (in the same way that it might makes sense to name a charm). Similarly, we can (and the Greeks did) put all sorts of names to various sorts of moves that can be made in conversation or argumentation (RHETORIC in da house!), and that makes a lot of sense as well.

                                Having charm codices, primers and whatnot on the twists of Essence known to produce certain sorts of effects in the world, is, in my estimation, just too useful, too doable, and in man's nature to ignore. As I said, while I expect that such things will be "conflicting, politicized, argumentative, nonsensical, and fallacious," the Realm doesn't have to be comically inept about everything. There are too many examples of ancient societies, from Egypt to Greece to China, being extraordinarily thoughtful and precise in their conception and categorization of phenomena in the world (even which those societies did not understand the underlying facts of science of those phenomena) to make the 'innate' conception of charms outside the realm of codification.

                                            Originally posted by Equitable Remedy View Post
                                            Having charm codices, primers and whatnot on the twists of Essence known to produce certain sorts of effects in the world, is, in my estimation, just too useful, too doable, and in man's nature to ignore. As I said, while I expect that such things will be "conflicting, politicized, argumentative, nonsensical, and fallacious," the Realm doesn't have to be comically inept about everything. There are too many examples of ancient societies, from Egypt to Greece to China, being extraordinarily thoughtful and precise in their conception and categorization of phenomena in the world (even which those societies did not understand the underlying facts of science of those phenomena) to make the 'innate' conception of charms outside the realm of codification.
                                            I don't think the Realm is comically inept, at all. Given a society that raises its most physically, mentally and spiritually powerful citizens as the highest form of enlightenment, keeps their actions towards the much larger population of mortals mostly consequence-free, and exists in a constant state of civil cold war, it's still the highest functioning authority in Creation. The Realm is my single favorite part of the world's infrastructure.

                                            That said, every sorcerous, studious Dragon-Blooded philosopher with any interest in the codification of Charms is likely to be met with an equally sorcerous, studious Dragon-Blooded philosopher of another House. Charm codex after Charm codex would be met by refuting claims and special-interests-driven intrigue. Settling on a name would be all but impossible, given the power and importance of Charms like Wind-Carried Words and the imagery of Dragon-Graced Weapon. Who gets to decide? Are there religious connotations that might worry the Mouth of Peace? There's a lot going on, and given how our own real-world philosophers, religious mystics and men of science butt heads over dogma and evidence, it's not even a stretch to imagine a culture built on in-fighting and politics would do the same.

                                            But let me be totally clear: I like the idea that a magical society might try to codify the similar "powers" of its mightiest. I just don't dig that these codifications are presented as-is from the book in which we, the players, get them.

                                            I'm also not sure I buy the idea that the Charms as we're presented with them are exactly the same. Who's to say that Cynis Rokan and Mnemon Aedis, both Earth-Aspect Dragon-Blooded with grand goremauls and Dragon-Graced Weapon are, in-setting, using a similar power? I'll admit that second-edition leans that way, but that edition does codify its Charms. Charms are a thing that Exalted can study.

                                            But I'm getting dangerously close to a gamist/narrativist/simulationist argument, and no one wants or needs that.
