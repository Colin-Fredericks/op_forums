                Now, I’ve been waiting to see what would be done with Cynis.

                Right off the bat, there’s the good strong focus on them less as debauched, and more as masters of high society, and I can see interesting possibilities in the idea that they trade in specific and desirable slaves. Agriculture is a bit of a new focus for them (at least apart from drugs), but hardly out of place, and I was already on board with the idea that they have strong international trade links. In fact… yes, Cynis has always been Wood, but playing up the aspect of Wood as having a strong tendency to relationships even by the standards of Dragon Blooded melds nicely with the House.

                Still, they wouldn’t be Cynis if there wasn’t a bit of debauchery, although here I find the framing to focus less on that as something to judge, and more as an expression of a kind of libertarianism (which is rather ironic for the House tasked with trading in slaves), even before it gets into the idea that at least part of the reputation is an act to leave them underestimated and lax with their secrets.

                The House economics are about what one would expect, but for the very good addition of them as the foremost medical practitioners in the Dynasty. You know, between that and the stated proclivity for experimentation… okay, I’ve skipped ahead a bit to confirm that Bhagwai has not been reassigned to his mother’s House, and considering that he doesn’t seem to have been mentioned yet, he really ought to be. He fits with it a lot more closely, especially now, and considering the matrilineality, he really should have always been. My God, even the stated reason of “a child of two House founders would be too politically contentious”… that’s easier to do if you leave him with the mother. “Hey Cynis, who’s the father of that child you’re pregnant with?” “Some guy.” Anyway, rant over…

                Good contrast between their weaker overt military and their more subtle methods of infiltration and sabotage. I particularly like the idea of them being a kind of chemical weapons dealer, playing both sides of any covert conflict as suits them, to their compounding profit. Particularly with the idea that part of their capacity to engage in such is the nigh-unparalleled trade network.
                It's interesting that, as their enemies and allies go, the House has a stronger antagonism with Mnemon herself than her family, and that otherwise their most antagonistic relationship is with a House that they owe a lot of money to. I can see an interesting plot hook in which Cynis is forced to go a lot more open to protect itself, with the potential military support of Sesus (although I could personally see somebody championing courting more of Tepet).

                So I take it that all of the Houses are getting fancy headquarters now. Greyfalls looks to be more interesting, with the trade connections to some nifty new Eastern locales. I’m also guessing that part of the Realm’s motivation for empire now might be the need to control the manses to power the Defence Grid.

                Hmmm, I wonder if it might be a bit on the nose for Cynis to have died from a drug overdose, although I do like the idea that she was a great explorer and merchant, plus the notion that her crypt is an object of the family’s veneration. I can’t quite remember what the personalities of the Cynis triumvirate was before, but if it was different, then this distribution of skills and personality traits makes them feel like a good showing of the House capabilities and keeping them in the black.

                Ooohh, we’ve got a new candidate for the position of Empress, and one who plays into the possibility of the House developing its military fortunes if it needs to. I also like the idea of Laseral having the public persona of being a ruthless battle commander contrasted against a concealed genuine love with her husband.

                Hmm, was Cynis Umara in Masters of Jade? No, probably not, and even if she was this would be a significant revision; she probably just happens to be a very similar character. Aaaaand between Cynis Cerise and Cathak Elanda, I can only assume that the provision against the Dragon Blooded ever being born with birth defects or physiological disorders has just been dropped entirely. I approve.

                    Now, House Ledaal, one that was always more interesting in concept than execution. It’s starting off strong with language that conveys a sense of spiritual motive in their imperial mission…. contrasted by being the first seen that is divided against itself. The rise of Ledaal herself represents a fun, weird take on dynastic policies, as well as the mythmaking that inspires the House.

                    Ahh, and here’s the context behind the division in its leadership; the need to balance pushing for (and independently pursuing) responses to growing supernatural threats, and participation in the political game to actually maintain their constitution.

                    So, Ledaal still has Arjuf Dominion; I wonder if it’s still run by somebody who is in a relationship with her father. Hmm, didn’t Greyfalls used to belong to Ledaal? It would certainly fit in with the idea that they take a more direct hand in governing their satrapies.

                    That’s a nice middle ground between preceding depictions of military prowess; they’re not half bad, but they’ve got too many possible targets to focus military ventures on, and the heart of their power lacks security.

                    Jeez, Ragara sure has some problems making friends, eh? Whereas it’s nice to see a faction that actually does think that Mnemon is right for the job.
                    Oh wow; I wonder how many people are going to pursue that plot hook about Essence amplification. And it’s neat to use Ledaal’s particular hang-up to give it a unique relationship with an odd satrapy (or at least the shadowland that it borders and maintains contact with).

                    I think the scions of Ledaal are some particular standouts, with the slightly hypocritical matriarch and Rae’s particularly strong vendetta against Thorns, on top of the old favourite of Kebok Coren and her storm-demon lineage.

                    ​Oh, that was a lot shorter than it took to read and write. Perhaps I'll take a look at Mnemon a bit later.

                        Now, moving on to Mnemon, which is interesting for already having had a few references to at least its leading personage in the run up.

                        I had already suspected that there was going to be a stronger emphasis on piety in the House (not least since the Cynis write-up attributed it to Mnemon herself). An emphasis on architecture is interesting to me, because for the longest time, I was sure that Mnemon had been referenced somewhere as a literal architect by profession, and was very attached to the image, and yet it seemed to turn out to alternately be a misreading of her Nature or a complete fabrication on my part. Either way, it’s a role for them that satisfies me.
                        If I’m guessing right, then it would seem that the House’s perceived legitimacy for the succession, and power with which it backs it up, is coming from association with the Immaculate Order more than Mnemon having a sense of entitlement.

                        Hmm, I had always assumed that Cynis would be the one associated more with scandalous exoticism, so the idea that it’s a strong focus in Mnemon is both an interesting subversion of my expectations, and an amusing contrast considering the antipathy between those Houses.

                        And if I’m reading that line about snubbing other Houses correctly, they’ve got a curious predilection for insularity.

                        I see, if Mnemon’s backstory includes learning her skills while in hiding from Ragara, I’m guessing that whole line about the Empress promising to kill him if anything happened to his sister has been removed. Heh, I feel a surprising degree of sympathy for the idea of Mnemon falling into deeper debt to that brother’s scions.

                        And this is a very interesting change to Mnemon’s military predilictions, with the shift from “sure they could take on many other Houses alone” to “only recently acquired a large military, and has committed most to war with Perfect Soul”.

                        I was always fond of the more unpleasant Mnemon of Editions past, but I’m sold on this more measured revision of her.

                        My, but does Mnemon have by far the most elaborate inter-House relations thus far. I suppose that’s what comes from being run by one of the stronger candidates for the Throne. It’s also another strong extrapolation from the revision from them being a militarily strong House independently.

                        Whatever is changed in her, I do like that Mnemon retains a general dislike of V’Neef, even if the reasons have changed. In particular, for what they say about Mnemon; I’m liking this feel I’m getting of her as a powerful, respected, and intelligent figure but who lacks for social adeptness in a manner that makes it hard for her to be widely liked, and is a shortcoming in comparisons to the Empress. I’m getting this image of her as a kind of stern, slightly self-righteous nerd.

                        I kind of adore the idea of Mnemon renaming a city that she restored after herself, and the notion of her leaving dues to lost siblings…

                        Yeah, I’m, I’m completely sold. Mnemon is dead, long live Mnemon!

                        (Seriously, I am really surprised by just how much I like this shift)

                        Aaand we finally get Paragon in this Edition. I’m guessing that a more thorough exploration of its relationship with the Realm will be explored in the other book.

                        I like the idea of the inner circle; it represents that old idea of Mnemon exercising particular power over her House within this new context, with the added sense that the poor woman has some severe trust issues. Hmm, Caras Farim, I’m sure that person was in prior Editions…

                        Ohoho I’m looking forward to seeing Mnemon’s dedicated profile.

                        Ah well, I guess it makes sense that most of what there is has already been said. I suppose that’s what you get when the founder is the still-living matriarch. Still, as a final cherry on the Sundae, I do like the idea that she has a tendency to austerity and a dislike of sycophants.

                        Ohoho, I just had a thought: previous Editions Mnemon was the Cersei Lannister of the books, while Third Edition Mnemon is the Cersei of the tv show. 😉

                        And we’re continuing with fun altered family conventions and endearing Mnemon characteristics with her having brought in a sister as an adopted daughter, who’s a cool freaky occultist to boot!

                        I think Pallan may be the first strong example of the place of sons in the matrilineal system of the Dynasty; his values as a member of Mnemon give him traits that are attractive to be drawn into another House, while also constituting a form of soft power that can be projected into them. That would have been good even without crossing his stars with a Ragara. I believe that I had anticipated the notion that a man impregnating another woman would have controversy if his procreative Essence is considered to be his wife’s property.

                        And it’s all topped off nicely with somebody who takes the House’s tendency towards spirituality in a very different direction.

                        So yeah, House Mnemon: A+ right there.
