                        There are a couple of reasons Terrestrials are my favourite host of Exalts in 2e. Remember that this is the perspective of one who has never actually played a Terrestrial, and only ever a handful games of Exalted in any capacity, though.

                        They are fun to write Charms for. I always thought Solar Charms were a bit boring to write, because they were often in the vein of "you are immune to this" or "you can parry anything". Terrestrial Charms (disregarding the elemental imagery which is neat but not essential to me) need to be weaker than that, and the easiest way to do that, besides having smaller numbers, was to restrict when the Charm could be used. Those clauses served as a neat way of forming a statement on the Terrestrials and their society, and made it feel like the powers they used reproduced that society. It made you feel involved in their culture much more than writing for Solars did.

                        Naturally, there's a lot to be said against Charms having center stage and that we should focus on characters instead. I that's certainly right, but there's a difference between focusing on characters and making Charms boring or non-thematical. I hope the Terrestrials get to keep their restrictive conditions on their Charms in 3e. They need them to make their society make sense.

                        I also like their underdog perspective. It's really strange, because from the start, Solars were supposed to be the underdogs, having just come out of the Jade Prison to suddenly find themselves faced with century-old empire bent on their extermination. I suppose it's a testament to how well 2e represented the value of infrastructure that Solars still came out feeling like the stronger party. In 3e this might change. Instead, Terrestrials kind of feel like hopped-up footsoldiers in the face of their returning general. And well... I kind of like that, actually.

                        And I must say I never once heard anyone call a Solar an "underdog". Sure, there are accounts of Wyld Hunt threatening them, but then it's just a matter of not being strong enough yet and when you are you'll be fine. There's no bloody edge of desperation with Solars. The setting doesn't really let them prove themselves to show their worth, it kind of presents them as inherently worthy and that these people, while currently stronger than you, are actually your lessers and will fall in time. For a Terrestrial, or even a group of Terrestrials, that edge is there. Fighting a Solar is a matter of life and death. The setting does not assure them they will eventually win, in fact it goes to lengths to show why you may not. There are real things at stake here; your life, your friends' lives, maybe even your whole culture. There's defiance in their eyes, which just isn't there in the Solar's.

                        To clarify, I've no idea how this feels in actual play. Solars may in fact be proper underdogs. My views are based on how the books and the boards talks about them. A player behind the wheel might help.

                                Dragon-Blooded are about tradition, and duty, and the glory of working for something greater than yourself- a Realm that is wide and powerful and intriguing and lovely, even as it is crumbling into dust. A lot of character arcs in fantasy are about sticking it to the Man, about being new and unique and- let's face it- more important, somehow, than other people.
                                I understand why those plots are valid, but sometimes I don't want to read about the village of Berk being changed forever. Sometimes I don't want the heroes to tear down what already exists in pursuit of a Glorious Destiny.
                                Sometimes I want to read about characters who know the world they live in, not simply explore it or conquer it or remember it from thousands of years ago. Sometimes I want to play as Mulan, and find new ways to honour old responsibilities. Sometimes I want to feel that familial pride, that stability, secondhand.
                                That's why I like the Dragon-Blooded.

                                                        I see a lot of reasons why people like Dynasts, which is fine, but nothing specific to Dragonblooded.

                                                        I like Dragonblooded because of their thematics of their power. They are Princes of the Earth. Their power either comes from or is inspired by the 5 Terrestrial Dragons and the Pillars of the Elements that keep Creation anchored in reality. Without them, Creation would likely dissipate. They are figuratively the Exalted of Creation.

                                                        Their power is not given down from on high, it is passed by blood and lineage. Strong traditions run with blood. Blood and lineage are very mortal conceits. This grounds them in mortality and the people. This also sets them above people. The power is passed by lineage, which ties you to something strong. Not just anyone can be them, whereas a Solar is chosen from on high, but anyone could be chosen. They are figuratively the Exalted of the People, those Above and Below others.

                                                        Be they Dynasts or Scavenger Lords they are many, they are powerful, they are deeply tied to Creation in a way no other splat is.
