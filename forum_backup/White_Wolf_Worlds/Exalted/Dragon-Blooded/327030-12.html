    I'd say, regarding DB anima effects, that it's a question of what you want to be "generic" powers that all DBs of a particular aspect has, vs. more specific and unique powers honed through charms.

    Dawns being scary in combat or Full Moons being strong are so central to what they do that it makes sense for it to be a generic "everyone gets this" anima power. Bringing meaningful, lasting water or growth to a wasteland is, in my opinion, a more unique sort of power -- it's not something that every single DB of the right aspect should get automatically. Don't get me wrong, I'm fine with DBs being able to do it somehow, I just don't think it's so central to what they are that every DB should be able to do it inherently with no training.

    Being "the Wood Aspect who can make plants thrive even in a desert" should be a meaningful statement and something that makes your Wood Aspect DB a bit unique; it shouldn't be something people generically assume is true about you just because you're a Wood Aspect.

                    I don't think it's quite this extreme, but that's a bit closer. It's more like, OK, in the Taoist five elements or the European four humours theory, the elements are a way of conceptualizing the balance of someone's health and character. So being brash, flamboyant, extroverted, dynamic, bright, etc are properties associated with fire, and fire aspects aren't necessarily like that, but they have a greater propensity for those traits in the balance, and have an easier time developing in that direction. But it's sort of like being tall or having an outgoing personality, rather than a rigid thing. Your Aspect predisposes you to certain things, and you may emulate it much more overtly in the manifestation of your charms (i.e. even a fickle Earth Aspect may be steadfast in the context of their Integrity and Resistance powers), but there's meant to be a fair bit of freedom for departure from that norm individually.

                    The extra step that Exalted takes here, beyond just conceptualizing these groups as "clans" with distinctive profiles, is that when you're a Fire Aspect, you have those properties because you are full of the essence of fire, and because that connection is not simply a metaphor in the setting, it also gives you an affinity with actual fire. But because Exalted tends to put a lot of focus on character individuality, it tries not to overplay the "actual fire affinity" part or disconnect it overmuch from the character's actions, because it makes your abilities feel less self-expressive and self-derived, so it needs to be balanced out by things that are extremely self-expressive and self-derived to get the tone right.

                                        Shintai is a Yozi word!

                                        But as far as broad "embody my element" charms go...

                                        Physically transforming into a body made of an element for an extended period of time strikes me as more Lunar or 2e-Infernal than Dragon-Blooded. That doesn't mean they can't get it, but it's something that doesn't fit very well in an Ability-based charmset; and it doesn't strike me as essential to them. (For example, at its most basic -- scenelong body of air. Athletics, Dodge, Stealth, or Larceny? If it were realistically powerful, would be one of the best tricks DBs were likely to have in any of those categories, which causes a problem if you put it in any one of them.)

                                        But it's not just a mechanical concern; I don't feel it entirely fits them thematically, either. The DB charmset has generally been about specializing in a particular talent and using that as a focus for their elemental powers, not about going for 2e-Infernal-style unified physical transcendence in a particular theme. Momentarily turning into a body of air as a specific trick to dodge an attack makes sense; broad "I can do everything that you could justify fitting into air-transformation" doesn't.

                                        I mean, elder DBs can definitely achieve that broad mastery of their element, but they do it by mastering many many charms across many different abilities, not by learning one "master of air" charm. An elder air-aspect's mastery of his element comes from the countless different things he has learned throughout his life rather than from one cheap trick.
