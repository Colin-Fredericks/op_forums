    Depends on the Exaltation, and then on the individual Exalted.

    Alchemicals obviously have highly codified and extremely Charm manuals. Sidereals might as well, given the nature of their Charms and the Martial Arts motif.

    Solars? I don't see why they would, and it certainly wouldn't matter if they did, since the knowledge would be long lost in the forgotten ruins of the First Age. Even then, it can go either way. I can play a Solar Himura Kenshin who has very specific charm techniques and combinations, but I can also play Solar Hercules, whose magic powers never get more specific than "really strong dude."

    Dragon-Blooded are going to be even more varied. Between bloodline-specific charms and the teachings of ancient Immaculate Masters, there's plenty of room for rare Charms that express an extremely advanced mastery of Essence, but some of the most basic charms aren't going to be worth codifying, both because they're so prevalent that they're normalized within Dragon-Blooded society and because they're not much more impressive than what mortals can do.

        I think low-level Charms will be an even mix: Ox-Body will probably not be specificelly codified other than "use Essence to strengthen your body young one" while Elemental Bolt and Wind Carrying Words (or whatever it's called) would be more codified: they are extremely useful and versatile, so getting them down to textbook level is probably needed. Of course the big question is whether Dragongraced Weapon/Arrow is taught as one Charm or a different one... for Fire aspects it maybe about honing your passion, while for Earth aspects it's about wielding your stoicness with your weapon or whatever...

        Higher level Charms are taught more on a master-apprentice level imho, so there's even more variance there, especially since for example Jaded X (forgot its name) can be taught as "learn how to focus your negative energies into an impenetrable shield against or manipulation" or "remember the most boring moment of your life... now wrap some Essence around that", so I don't think that's exactly codified.

                    As good a solution as any, right? But... honestly, why worry about it too much? As we always say, story over narrative. We don't really need to justify the fact that charms have changed over the centuries in the fiction but haven't any mechanical adjustments currently ; if that DOES bother us, we can always homebrew that.

                    Solar charm 'techniques' and manuals enable cool characters. They let characters talk about interesting things and add distinctive, fun shit to the titles they claim ('I developed MAJESTIC THUNDER BLAM!'). I've literally had dozens of PCs do this. I've had PCs describe sections of downtime where they retreated from foes as getting into contact with ancient memories and learning lost techniques of Solar-Fu. I've gotten huge mileage out of Solars finding old charm manuals from past incarnations.

                    Hell, the Invisible Fortress had a few of these!

                    This change in 3e bugs me for these reasons. It seems like the 'story first' philosophy isn't being used here which is frustrating. I get the simulationist arguments against it, why it 'doesn't make sense', I just don't care and when I didn't care previously it didn't impede my fun or suspension of disbelief in any real measurable way.

                                Originally posted by MissMaddy View Post
                                This change in 3e bugs me for these reasons. It seems like the 'story first' philosophy isn't being used here which is frustrating. I get the simulationist arguments against it, why it 'doesn't make sense', I just don't care and when I didn't care previously it didn't impede my fun or suspension of disbelief in any real measurable way.
                                My perspective on it is that if it isn't handled very, very carefully, "Solar charms are discrete objects* which can be codified in manuals" can imply that charms are a function of the Solar Exaltation rather than a function of the Solar Exalted, which shifts the focus of the game from the characters to their powers, which in turn encourages discussions of "Solar shards," "supermotes," "divine power batteries," "cosmic lottery" and a heap of other irrelevant fanon, which ultimately results in the Solar Exalted being displaced and overshadowed by their own Exaltation. From a design standpoint, "Yurgen Kaneko shot a Sidereal in the head with an arrow from a thousand miles away" is much more interesting and relevant "Yurgen Kaneko killed a Sidereal from a thousand miles away with Accuracy Without Distance and the 1st Archery Excellency."

                                The idea works for Alchemicals because it's part of their design philosophy and they run with, but I'm not sure if it would work nearly as well for the other Exalted.

                                The players can have manuals of Solar charms, but I don't think the developers should be the ones to introduce the idea.

                                *Not necessarily a physical object, but an "object of study."

                                    Originally posted by The MG View Post
                                    Take heart, for it's like 90% probable that the "no Charm manuals" thing will take the shape of not mentioning Charm manuals rather than blanket prohibitions of the concept.
                                    This has been my takeaway, too. Toward the end of the lengthy conversation in which this whole thing first arose with John (starts relatively early in here with Hippokrene's comment), he explicitly said this wasn't something that especially bothered him. And from a little later:
                                    Originally posted by John Mørke
                                    Whether you see it your way or mine won't change how the Charms work mechanically.

                                    But a Solar who just Exalted in Nexus can complete the entire Dawn Charmset without ever meeting another Solar, without knowing what the Solars were/are, and without any exposure to First Age lore or past lives.

                                    The Charm set is not external, waiting for him to discover it. So he has no idea what the names of any of these Charms are. He exists in a complete vacuum, and the game supports this—if he is a Dawn, he needs no training. If he can learn the Charms without training, and can learn the Charms without knowing their names, and is not automatically Exalted with a full codex of Charm names (which he is not) then you might conclude that the "Charms" (he may not even think of them as Charms) he has learned have completely different names and methods and pathos from the exact (mechanically) same Charms of another Solar, regardless that these two Solars were modeled by the same Charm set, the same mechanics, the same names.

                                    IE, the names of Charms, and even Charms themselves don't qualify as anything important or crucial or necessary. They are just character descriptors.
                                    There's nothing wrong with the concept of a character who gains a particular Charm and the player fluffs it as a technique learned from a manual or legendary scroll (with/without the same name as the Charm). There's nothing wrong with the concept of a character who gains the same Charm and the player fluffs it as a different technique said character has devised (with/without the same name as the Charm). There's nothing wrong with the concept of a character who gains the exact same Charm and the player fluffs it not as an explicit technique at all, simply a mark of the character's refined skill/capabilities/whatever. It's entirely possible for all of a given character's various Charms to each be fluffed in entirely different ways.

                                    Saying all Charms are very definitely discrete things in-setting is more limiting to possible stories than just letting the Charms be names and mechanics that can indeed be that, or can be whatever interpretation a given player/table wants them to be. Saying in the book that they are explicitly not discrete things in-setting would likewise step on this.
