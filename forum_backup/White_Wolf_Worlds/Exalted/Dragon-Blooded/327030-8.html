                                        Originally posted by Valles View Post
                                        ...I don't see how having 'elemental' abilities can have any other point. If we're not allowed to actually use the tools we're given, why play the game at all?
                                        Well, remember, in this case we're just talking about your unique anima expression. Compare it to a Twilight, say -- I might have my anima consist of fluttering pages containing secret knowledge, which is cool. But then turning around and saying "all right, I glance at those fluttering pages and learn this secret from them" is not cool (except maybe as a stunt to justify a Lore roll I could make anyway.) Players shouldn't get disproportionate rewards for how they describe their anima, or you're both devaluing any charms that do similar things and encouraging them to chose their anima tactically.

                                        That said, remember that the things you want to do aren't necessarily impossible; you just need a charm for them. Bringing forth water or lush greenery in the desert is a logical thing for some DB charms of the appropriate aspects to do.

                                        Originally posted by wulf View Post
                                        It's lines like this that have led me to believe that the Dragon-Blooded actually used elemental powers rather than powers with elemental themes.
                                        They do use elemental powers, really. It's just that their elemental powers are primarily built around this concept of elemental swords-and-sandal heroes. (People usually describe it as "elemental supersoldiers", but supersoldiers makes them sound like space marines or something.) They channel elemental power through their talents.

                                        On the other hand, you shouldn't give up completely on what you want. 3e is still going to have them be Ability-based, but it's also going to allow you to make powers of all five elements in every Ability, which opens up a lot more room for powers -- for example, above, you asked about using your power over Earth to dig a tunnel, and I mentioned that that's something you could probably do in 2e because it fits into Craft and matches their other Craft charms; but in 2e, you couldn't have them use Craft to create a wall of flames. (At least, not easily -- you could probably create a 'raise earthworks' charm and then have an option to invoke it with other elements, but it would be clunky.)

                                        In 3e you'll probably be able to do something like that a lot more easily. But the game is still built to have you specialize along the lines defined by your mortal skills -- you can't easily be a generic "master of all things fire", since DBs are something more specific than that.

                                            Originally posted by wulf View Post
                                            Maybe that's why I've never really been interested in playing the DBs, really. Because, to me, that's boring. This also explains why I like playing Mage so much wherein I can be an enlightened mortal whose power can generate, manipulate or snuff out raw elemental essence (among other things) to virtually any effect I can imagine.

                                            Essentially, the DBs are similar to the Solar Exalted but instead of having sunlight and other solar iconography in their aesthetics, it's elements. Which is a bit of a tease, for me. It's dangling the imagery and potential for element-shaping in front of my face but then saying, "No, no. They don't actually work like that." Their outward presentation has always spoken to me of elemental demi-gods, not demi-gods whose cosmetic expressions are elementally-themed. You're correct, I've misunderstood them entirely and now that I have had it cleared up for me I can honestly say that I want nothing to do with Dragon-Blooded in my Creation. For the purposes of my games, I'll replace them entirely with god-blooded or enlightened mortals.
                                            Have you read the 1st Edition Dragonblooded Aspect books?

                                            The Dragonblooded don't manipulate the elements the way that, ahem, some other fantasy setting does, but I wouldn't go far enough to say that Solar>Sun = same as Dragonblooded>Elements.

                                            For the Dragonblooded their elemental aspect is as much a psychology and philosophy (I doubt there's any difference between the two in Creation) as it is an "expression". Fire aspect Dragonblooded are taught the value of restraint and discipline lest their burning passions destroy themselves and others (which, I'll admit, is a little reminiscent of that other fantasy setting) Earth Aspects are encouraged to change and grow, Water to conserve their energy for the perfect strike.*

                                            They're nuanced characters, and the elements reflect and refract the way the see Creation and themselves, not just some collection of powers. Some of them believe themselves to be inherently more enlightened* than mortals, and that their element-founded mindset is above mortal thinking in the same way that mortal thinking is above animals. Almost all of them believe they know what's best for Creation (generally, the Dragonblooded). Although they stride across Creation as the Princes of the Earth they have passions and appetites that cannot be matched by mortal individuals.

                                            Before you throw them out, I urge you to take a second look at them knowing that they're emphatically -not- elemental benders, nor element themed minor Solars, and see how you like them as they are.

                                            (*Keeping in mind that in setting, enlightenment runs closer to 1970's Communist China Party Doctrine than actual enlightenment as wisdom)

                                                Every Dragon-Blood belongs to one of five elemental aspects that correspond to the Elemental Dragons of Air, Earth, Fire, Water and Wood.                                                 Nothing impacts the life of a Terrestrial Exalt so much as the Elemental Dragon whose power she channels.

                                                - Manual of Exalted Power: The Dragon-Blooded, page 126


                                                In one sense, this is hyperbole. In another sense, this is more a case of bad translation from 1E to 2E. MoEP thinks that the Dragonblooded are humans* with elemental powers.

                                                They're not. Not anymore than Solar are humans* who happen to cop the powers of The Unconquered Sun.

                                                The Dragonblooded do not manipulate their element, they are their element; the essence of that element is entwined with how they think, the foods they favor, who they find attractive, which skills they naturally excel at. A Fire Aspect who activates Comet Deflecting Technique is burning with the grace of fire as she interposes her weapon to block an attack, the Air aspects voice is the flowing wind travelling miles distant to send a dire warning or a chilling secret.

                                                The Dragonblooded's elemental aspect is incredibly important. I wouldn't go so far as to say it's the 'most important thing' (because who the hell knows what makes us people?) but I would say it's a hell of an important thing. The elemental aesthetic of the Dragonblood is not there to lead one to think of individuals who manipulate their element (though some charms may express themselves this way), the elemental aesthetic is there to reinforce the influence that the Dragonblooded's aspect has on who they are as an individual.

                                                *Of course Dragonblooded and Solar are humans. They get lonely-hungry-horny-sleepy-giddy, but they tend to do it in extreme ways. And an Exalt that controls their passions is showing an amount of self-restraint that's mind-boggling. But one should be reminded that the Exalted are not merely people with powers**

                                                **Or maybe they are, and people just tend to act extravagant and crazy when they have incredible power. It's kind of one of the big questions of Exalted. It's probably ineffable.

                                                        I think this is a somewhat artificial distinction, and not one implied by the passages you cited, but running with it, they have always had some of both, albeit not as many of the former as might be ideal (largely a function of the fact that their charmset was small, and there are a lot of meat-and-potatoes powers that "bending" doesn't cover). But even having said all that, it doesn't really have anything to do with whether they have generalized "bending" or not. Dragon-Blooded with nothing but physical, literalist element manipulation would still have it divided into narrowly-focused ability charms.

But I think this is mostly assumptions you brought to the table yourself, because it's not really even in the passages you cited specifically.

                                                        There's nothing wrong with being mistaken, especially in a game like Exalted which has subtle and convoluted presentation and is usually pitched poorly. But it's not a failing of the game that it happened, either.
