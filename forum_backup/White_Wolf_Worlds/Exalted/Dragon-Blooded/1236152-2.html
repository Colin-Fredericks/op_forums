                                            My concerns with WFHW all stem from excellencies in some way. Purchased excellencies are the big one, not for balance concerns, but because they make Dragon-Bloods a lot more boring. The problem with purchased excellencies was always that it's a giant sucking fun tax. Charms are what Exalted revolves around, they define your characters and employing them in fun and interesting ways is the big mechanical draw of the system, so having to spend a big chunk of your sorely limited charm picks on the boringest charms in the game feels pretty crappy. It's especially bad if you want to be more of a generalist character who's competent in multiple fields. When you're playing a Solar, you can be pretty damn solid at a whole bunch of different abilities just by allocating dots in the right places and leaning on your free excellencies in fields where you haven't bought charms, but DBs trying to scatter out dots and excellencies to the point where they're good at combat, social intrigue and one or two other things can easily end up spending half their starting charms on just excellencies. Which is further exacerbated by the fact that of your remaining charm picks, about half again will probably be spent on the fairly bland entry charms that DBs get - and to be fair, a lot of Solar trees start off with pretty bland effects too, but proportionately they eat up much less of your character.

                                            Now, yeah, you can get around all of that by just focusing harder on one thing. If all your want your DB to do is be Sword Guy, having to spend one fifteenth of your starting charms on a melee excellency is inconsequential. But a) without supernal, DBs aren't really set up to mono-focus like that, and more to the point b) that's really boring. Or at least, it is for me. I don't want to be pushed into making hyper specialised characters who kick ass at their one job and then go get pizza once the combat ends and the super specialised social person takes over for the talky scene. So I'm being hemmed in by the crushing tides of boring. On one side, I could play the all-rounder characters I enjoy and not have a good time because I've got maybe three charms total that have interesting, narrative-changing effects, or on the other I could play a one-note character who works better mechanically but who I don't enjoy on a fundamental level. And it's not like any of this is some big important power tier thing that needs to be in the game, nothing in the setting breaks if DBs can be adequate generalists.

                                            The other big problem with excellencies, which this apparently all stemmed from, is that they're just plain better than a lot of DB charms. This is the big objection I have to the arguments that DB charms are fine power-wise, and we're just too used to Solar charms to properly judge them. Yes, DBs are a couple 'tiers' below Solars, but the weak DB charms aren't bad because they're worse than Solar charms. They're bad because they're worse than excellencies. If every Exalt is going to have excellencies, there should never be any Exalted charm that is weaker than one, because then what is the point of that charm even existing? And there are quite a lot of effects throughout the DB charmset that are, if not objectively worse than excellencies because they're giving bonuses to different things, overpriced enough that I'd pretty much never want to spend those motes on such a weak effect when I could instead be converting those motes to dice on a one for one basis. And this sucks, because excellencies are fundamentally boring. They're a useful way to demonstrate Exalted superiority, but if the design of the game is pushing you to spend your limited resource of cool points on adding numbers instead of using your unique, fun abilities that change how you interact with the world, that design is failing.

                                            So then to balance out excellencies with these overly weak charms, excellencies were made purchased instead of free. Then to make excellencies feel like less of a tax, free perks were added onto the excellencies. But this hasn't addressed the problem at all. Actually, it's done the exact opposite and made the problem twice as bad. Not only are there a bunch of charms that are weak in comparison to excellencies, but it's now actually a competition where it wasn't before. With free excellencies, you pick up 15 proper, interesting charms at chargen, and while some of them are weak, maybe you'll end up seeing situations where you can justify using them. They're on your sheet, after all, might as well look for a chance to make use of them. With purchased excellencies? You're going to spend a bunch of those 15 charm picks on excellencies. They're the better charms. Which means that you won't even have the weaker charms sitting on your sheet, waiting for a chance to employ them. You're a walking collection of dice adders, who doesn't even have the option of using the interesting but weak charms. And then to make matters even worse, excellencies gained their little dice tricks and such, which makes them even stronger in comparison to other charms, so now the pool of proper, interesting charms that are both using, and worth buying in the first place, grows even smaller. DBs are pushed so hard towards just adding dice and never interacting with the fun side of the game. And that's not an issue of 'power tiers' or whatever, it's just bad gameplay.

                                            My homebrew: Abyssals, Infernals, Dragon Kings, martial arts.

                                                        Originally posted by Lanaya View Post
                                                        The other big problem with excellencies, which this apparently all stemmed from, is that they're just plain better than a lot of DB charms.                                                         ^Basically this.

                                                        I don't dislike the Dragon-Blooded charmset. Some elements I'm outright happy with.

                                                        But their design is very much slanted towards a linear progression of increasingly powerful effects instead of the cool helix structure of the Solar set, where you had very strong entry-level Charms, followed by more situational picks, followed by those sexy E3 power spikes. Take Melee, for example - as a Solar, five Charms in melee make you King Shit of Fuck Mountain: you pick Excellent Strike for what is essentially a permanent accuracy boost that grants you mote efficiency superiority against an opponent who can match you dice for dice, Dipping Swallow Defense so you'll never ever have to worry about penalties in your life, One Weapon Two Blows and Peony Blossom Technique for action economy superiority, and Call the Blade so you're never caught with your pants down if someone disarms you. Note that none of these Charms have a prerequisite higher than 3 points in Melee.

                                                        What can a Dragon-Blooded get?

                                                        The set starts out promisingly enough - Stoking Bonfire Style effectively rolls the function of Excellent Strike into the excellency itself, while being both weaker and more situational than its Solar equivalent (which it should be). Cool, what do I do to get something similar to DSD? I apparently need to pick Flame-Borne Interception, an effect that... matches my onslaught with an equivalent Charm bonus but only as long as I keep successfully parrying? Placing a "win more" effect like this at the basis of the weakest Exalt type's Melee tree is... certainly a decision. But once I do have FBI, I can actually grab Graceful Flowing Defense, which is actually a very well-designed Charm for the splat's power level. This, by itself, is a microcosm of the design difference between the Solar and the Dragon-Blooded set - we have the effect that gives a powerful basic capability hidden behind a more situational pick. The issue is the same with the set's equivalent of Fire and Stones Strike - the Dragon-Blooded version is split between two inferior Charms (Crimson Fang Bite and Demon-Crushing Wolf Bite, the former of which costs Willpower and the latter comes with an inconvenient Aura setup which, again, I personally like*), but those Charms, again, are locked away behind the ridiculously situational effect of being able to ignore a few points of Hardness. Then, predictably, we don't get anything like OWTB and PBT (Dragon-Blooded don't get to break the action economy over their knees willy-nilly, something we've known since we've seen the core's Terrestrial-keyworded effects), followed by what feels like an absolute slap in the face, which is their equivalent of Call the Blade: Dragon-Graced Weapon. Taken in isolation, it would be a fine enough Charm - it is more expensive than Call the Blade, but it comes with a neat bonus effect (sometimes even a multiple choice option for said bonus effect, depending on the battlefield setup) and it never, ever fails. However, it has a prerequisite of 5 dots in Melee, and another Charm that might very well be completely useless to you, depending on whether you happen to have an artifact weapon that is made of jade. Amazing. I need to be a master swordsman just to avoid being completely shut down by any schmuck who can succeed on a Disarm gambit, and even if I am, I also need to pick a dead Charm for the privilege.

                                                        This is generally a theme with the way the Dragon-Blooded charmset is put together: it discourages dipping by forcing you to pick a few Charms with effects that range from situational to useless for every worthwhile effect you might actually be interested in. The problem is not actually that you have to purchase excellencies, it's the fact that the designers decided to forego the basic structure of the Solar charmset in favor of a more linear power progression for dubious gain.


                                                        *Can we talk about how cool Demon-Crushing Wolf Bite is, designwise? Fire and Stones Strike's power lies in the fact that it adds post-soak damage dice, something these Charms don't do... but DCWB elegantly side-steps the issue by adding its bonus to Overwhelming, which works out to be effectively same in a lot of cases where you suddenly need a bunch of post-soak damage dice; then it adds some extra power to the effect while driving player behavior/build diversity by allowing a more generalized use of the Charm in an atypical Aura state; then it takes a lot of power away relatively to FSS by making the Charm's cost fixed instead of variable on a 1-for-1 basis, forcing players to either build specifically to maximize its benefit (which, funnily enough, takes them in a direction where they will benefit more from other Wood Charms as well), or to effectively waste motes.
