    Many people in the setting will argue whether the Usurpation was wrong, or the Great Uprising was right, and whether that means the Solars or Dragonblood of today are the heroes or villains.

    But they're all wrong. Because a war that happened 1500 years doesn't tell you who is good or bad today. I don't consider your average Dane a bad guy because 1300 years ago the Danish ravaged my country's farms and monasteries. You couldn't tell who were the good guys and bad guys in the Balkan wars of the 90s based on the activities of the Ustashe in the 40s.
    I went to a museum in Harbin, China, about the atrocities carried out by Japanese scientists, like live vivisections and infecting people with anthrax. But, while China today likes to invoke these things in their disputes with Japan, I can't say that Japanese atrocities in the 30s and 40s means that China is right and Japan is wrong in today's disputes.

    The only people you could really judge on the Usurpation are the Sidereal and Lunar elders who actually lived through it, like Kejak, Leviathan, Raksi, etc.

    Now, the Scarlet Dynasty is one of the setting's villains. But it's one of the more morally ambiguous ones. They do a lot of evil stuff, but they also do some good stuff. And in a giant Empire, not everyone is exactly the same amount of bad. There are people who want to reform the Dynasty, there are monks who try to battle its excesses, etc. And there are also decadent and corrupt slavers, murderous fanatics, etc. And a lot of just regular people who are trying to get on with their lives and don't really think about the systemic inequalities their society is built on.

    Essentially, the Realm is based on the Chinese Empire, the Roman Empire, and the British Empire. And these giant states are ambiguous. My opinion of them is quite negative, but they have plenty of defenders, and they did do some good stuff. I can't deny the Romans taught the Britons how to write, even if they did enslave and brutalise the Britons and wipe out their history and culture.
    I remember when I went to Kenya, people in Nairobi thought the British Empire was awful, whereas people in Mombasa had a much more positive attitude.
    I knew a guy from a tribe in the mountains of Myanmar, who told me how great Britain was because a British missionary taught his people writing, medicine, Christianity, and that chopping people's heads off to decorate your village is not cool. But I'm sure people in lowland Myanmar have a very different attitude.
    I had a Zambian friend who believed that God allowed the British to conquer Zambia in order to bring them Christianity, which he saw as worth the colonialism. Not that he wanted to be ruled by Britain again, but he didn't see the Empire as entirely a bad thing. I did wonder if his grandparents had the same attitude though.

    Abyssals, Infernals and Raksha are a much more straight-forward evil than the Dynasty. While there may be individual anti-heroes (we have a Raksha in our party, she doesn't really get morality, but she likes hanging around with the PCs and having adventures, and we had a rogue Infernal before), overall they're very definitely the bad guys.
    Whereas the Scarlet Dynasty are much more complex. You can't really reform the Neverborn, but reforming the Realm might be possible. An Abyssal may not care if an army of savage barbarians kill everyone in your city, but you may be able to convince a nearby group of Dynasts to save people. House Ledaal is willing to put its efforts towards battling the Abyssals. And while being ruled by the Realm means heavy taxes, it's better than being genocided by Abyssals.

    Overall, the point is that the Dynasty is bad, but not as bad as the setting's other main foes.

    Now... are the Solars good? Several people have mentioned that Solars are more likely than Dynasts to come from oppressed and marginalised people. So they're more likely to be fighting for a better world. But power corrupts, and Solars have a lot of power. So I might say, overall, Solars are probably better than Dragonblood, but it's very much an inidividual thing.

    And this is what it often comes to. I might say, for example, that in WW2 the Brits, French, Russians, Americans, Chinese, etc, were the good guys, and the Germans, Italians, Japanese, Croats, etc, were the bad guys. But that's an overall picture. There were French fascist collaborators. There were Italians who fought Fascism. There were Germans who resisted Nazism. There were plenty of people who went along with their country, but if they were in a different country would have gone along with them as well. Stalin fought the Nazis (eventually) but was definitely a bad guy.
    Good and evil are often quite individual. And so I don't think you can say "The Dragonblood" or "The Solars" (or "The Sidereals" or "The Lunars") are the good or bad guys. Pelleps Delled is a bad guy, but I'm sure lots of people in the North think Yurgen Kaneko is way worse. (Not to mention judging whether people are good or bad is simplistic anyway).
    Even within one party of PCs, some of the characters are better or worse than others.

    In the game I play, for example, I'd say:
    Loyal Fox, Eclipse - Good, if bitchy
    Reborn in Shining Light, Zenith - Good, if foolish and sometimes unwilling to restrain his friends
    Golden Hero, Night - He's a liar and an assassin. Anti-hero at best.
    Queen Enhenduanna, Eclipse - Gives people prosperity+cool magic powers. Uses magic mind-control to enforce loyalty on nobles and officers.
    Fulgens, Dawn - Evil. Definitely evil. Inching towards Hitler-esque levels of evil.

    Really, you can't just judge them as Solars, you have to judge them as individuals.


    Two last points:
    There are loads of Dragonblood in the world who have little to do with the Realm. You can hardly blame Lookshyans who defend the River Province from the Realm, for the Realm.
    No-one knows about the Great Curse (except maybe Jupiter and Lytek, and they're not telling). Which is something that's probably quite important in judging the morality of Solars past and present (if a Solar turns a thousand beautiful people into statues to decorate their manor, is that the Solar's fault, or the Neverborn who cursed her with madness?), but no one can do.

That is to say, the Realm hypes itself, and I would argue for people to apply critical thinking that keeps them from buying into that hype.

            I don't apply those standards to the characters; for there to be fictional characters within the setting, from multiple angles, who buy into the Realm's hype (even if their attitude on the Realm itself is to take over in that role for it) is not only to be expected, but something that I'm not inclined to write them off for and pass judgement on. That's partially my perspective on pre-modern history, and it's particularly how I approach a lot of fiction, because fiction affords me that level of detachment. It provides an environment in which to explore certain ideas and scenarios and perspectives without real world consequences, so long as I give consideration for how such is influencing me.

            So while I would find the principle of the Realm's imperialism to be bad with little to no ambiguity, I think there's a reasonable place to approach and discuss it from its own side of things, that has more complexity than just indulging in playing the bad guys or being one of the lone good cops in a corrupt system.

Representative institutions were not born out of some sort of wise choices by the philosophers.

                                                The story I usually encounter is that China largely opted out of this path, for a lower taxed, less regulating and less interfering centralized state that did not require "compromise" to the authority of the Confucian elite via representative politics, albeit even the merit based promotion of that elite itself offered some form of representation within government for the Chinese class of gentry. China avoided representation in government by having a relatively smaller, more minimal state. And the Confucian bureaucracy moralized this as the correct path, by forming an official ideology that money should be in society with producers (that, is they favoured money being with "the individual" rather than taxed by "the state").
