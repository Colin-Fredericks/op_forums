                                        Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz View Post
                                        Yes... but tbh, Lunars are probably the main obstacle on the way of Fae raiders, if only because they're closer.
                                        There's about 400 Lunars at maximum at any given point in time. Going off the map, the border of Creation (to the extent that there is a border rather than a gradual shift, but I'm working with what I've got here) is about 56,000 miles in total. Despite those 400 Lunars generally being pushed to the edges of the world, they are demonstrably not all hanging out right on the edge of Creation - of all the established Lunar dominions, the closest to Creation's border is the Caul, which is 1800 miles away from the edge of the world. And that's a lot of those less-than-400 Lunars all staying in one place, actively fighting people who aren't the Fair Folk and therefore not doing anything to sail around searching for invaders from the Wyld trying to slip past their continent.

                                        The point here being that it would be incredibly generous to assume that there's one Lunar for every 560 miles of Creation's border, given how few there are in total and how many of the few we have are way deeper into the world than that. Moreover, those handful of Lunars who are way out on the edges of the world are going to be clumping together into circles at a significant frequency, so we'd very frequently be finding thousands of miles of Creation's border without a single Lunar anywhere to be seen. And in the really rare case that a bunch of fae raiders do have the tremendous misfortune of bumping into one of that scant handful of Lunars, there isn't even a particular reason to assume they'd fight. I'm not saying they wouldn't, or that it would be unreasonable of them to do battle, but if I'm fighting an endless guerilla war against the mightiest empire in Creation and I see an army of Wyld-born monsters marching towards said empire, I'm hardly going to risk my troops and my life in an attempt to stop them.

                                        So yes, sometimes raksha will try to invade Creation and be stopped by a Lunar, but it never made any sense for that to be marketed as a big Thing that Lunars do even in second edition where that was designed to be their Thing, and it makes absolutely no sense in third. The odds of your Wyld-spawned hordes being stopped by a heroic Lunar who just happened to be there when they invaded aren't meaningfully bigger than the odds of them being stopped by, like, a Liminal who was just wandering through on the way to hunt down a hungry ghost, and they're going to be much, much smaller than the odds that a band of spirits or particularly plucky mortals work together to defend their domain. And all of those things are less than the odds that the Realm, the ten thousand dragons who rule the world and the disciplined, well-equipped legions who serve them, will be the ones who end up stopping the march of the Fair Folk. Not because they're the good guys or because empires are awesome and happy places, but because they have the raw power required to do it, the numbers required to cover all that territory and the motivation to protect the lands they're currently plundering from other marauding parties.

                                                        Originally posted by Epee102 View Post
                                                        Its a really really really common one though. I mean, not in the specific, but a good number of empires justify their use of force as being the light of civilization against barbarism.
                                                        Well, no, it's really not the same.

                                                        Sure, there is a passing resemblance between "Our empire protects civilization from being destroyed by barbarians" and "Our empire protects humanity itself from being utterly annihilated", but they are substantially different, starting with the fact that the barbarians are part of humanity. When barbarians knock over empires, at worst you get a dark age, and if you don't happen to be one of the peasants killed in the war itself, it's not clear how much you actually suffer because "civilization" fell. I mean, art and literature suffer, sure. Urban life declines. Ways of life change. But for the rural peasant farmer, how much does one actually notice the difference between the Romans and the Franks? Because only insofar as Roman oppression is better than life under the Franks is the Roman oppression justified to the peasant.

                                                        On the other hand, the Balorian Crusade, if successful, would have meant the extinction of humanity and the end of all Creation.

                                                        So when you're considering putting down a peasant revolt that really was a justified reaction against the excesses of a satrap, the balance-points differ. "They're weakening the bulwark that allows art and literature and urban life to flourish, therefore we must put down the justified-in-itself revolt" is morally a very different proposition than, "They're weakening the bulwark that allows humanity, including themselves and their loved ones, to exist, therefore we must put down the justified-in-itself revolt."

                                                        Now, that doesn't make the Realm's crimes not crimes, and it is a perfectly fair question how much the Realm actually protects existence from extinction. But "we protect humanity from nonexistence" is a far, far better argument for empire than "we protect civilization".
