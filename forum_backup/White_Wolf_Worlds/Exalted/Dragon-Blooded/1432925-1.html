        Selective Conception is not an out-of-character meta thing, though. It's a Merit in which the character has the actual trait of controlling their conception deliberately.

        Questions of how pregnancy is handled can reasonably differ between players, but even in a game where everybody is in a place of "this happens only if we want it to", there's additional narrative space for a character having actively switched it on.

        That's how you get stories such as Dune, where there's an apparent political flex and impending succession crisis to the emperor's Bene Gesserit consort having given him only daughters.

        For Well Bred, I take issue with phrasing it as spending Merit points on children while also recognising the social influence elements of it. You're spending points for the dice bonuses representing the regard people will place on your established capacity to provide Exalted children.

        That gives it tighter narrative focus than the Breeding Background, which provided larger mote pools and eventually access to additional Charms. Exclusivity producing Dragon Blooded who were actually more powerful, there's some eugenics stuff to get up in arms about (not to mention a Background that ends up feeling kind of mandatory but not actually very interesting).

        The Well Bred Merit, you buy that if you want to engage with the intrigue of people pursuing you as a marriage prospect because of the promise of your children. Or if you want the advantage if that's going to be part of the game anyway.

        Heck, take the Merit and then demand that none of your children Exalt. That's part of how it can work in the setting!

                Feudalist propaganda around a blood-based ruling class of 'betters', supremacist thinking of 'the chosen race', and the nepotism accompanying each are, to modern meritocratic thinking, abhorrent. The arranged marriages and the wasting of human talent for the accident of their birth, doubly so. To have a mechanic such as Well-Bred whiffs of the widespread glorification of oppressors.

                This, in certain society-changing campaigns, is a challenge to the players to imagine something better. Do you promote people based on their ability to recall the words of a certain philosopher in written exams? Do you allow the most ruthless exploiters of labor to accrue the capital they will use to buy and market their own positions of privilege? Do you allow the abbots of the Immaculate Order or Cult of the Illuminated to take the levels of state power and assert a theocracy? Keep in mind that the Dragon-Blooded will still, mechanically, best mortals in these selection mechanisms (Celestial Exalted, better still). Because the world of Exalted is intentionally biased, lacking in fairness, unapologetically hierarchical.

                On a more personal basis, being uncomfortable about your peers or elders having an interest, let alone voicing it to you, about your own procreation decisions can, indeed, be stressful. But telling off or besting that NPC is the catharsis such tension sets up. In that way, Exalted can be the healthy practice of setting and keeping boundaries.

                    Some of those are things where a direct measurable competition isn't really the most sensible way for them to work, and some are where there are enough configurations of people that it becomes perfectly suitable to have places where an Exalt of particular skills and inclination interacts with several mortals who are better at certain things.

                    Or good at things complementary to the Exalt.

                    Or less good at things but are compelling to an Exalt, in ways that can make Exalted power serve agendas not their own.

                    The Touman Clans have a Lunar who does not lead them (even though she probably has leadership skills), and is in a position where she might do things that serve the mortal leader rather than herself.

                    Even in the Scarlet Dynasty, this nuance can be found. Prominently in House Nellens, but even elsewhere there can be a mortal better at something important than a Dragon Blood, or where the nature of their relationship complicates the power dynamic (even if their society functions on an assumption of Exalted at the top).
