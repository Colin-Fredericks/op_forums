            Originally posted by The Wizard of Oz
            Oh, and Mnemon absolutely knows about Sidereals, which is why Kejak doesn't want her on the throne.
            As I recall, the issue is that Mnemon doesn't know the whole story, and Chejop and other Sidereals are reluctant to fill her in on any more of it. I think this is supposed to be an issue they have with any Dynasts who know about Sidereals; nobody is supposed to have ever known as much about it as the Empress (for instance, Mnemon knows that there are Sidereals and knows that the other Exalted aren't genuinely soul-eating demon sorcerer things, but I don't think she's supposed to be aware that the entire Immaculate Philosophy is a fabrication of the Sidereals, which could be a problem), and there are a few problems with having somebody else know as much (which range from the possibility that it will undermine their ability to work in the system, since people who have grown up in the Realm probably can't appreciate the context before it that made the system seem necessary, to the idea that the Sidereals would prefer somebody who could be more easily directed than the Empress was). Other than that, I think a lot of people interact with Sidereals the way that characters in The Lord of the Rings interact with Gandalf; they're not generally very curious about where he came from as opposed to what he's doing at the moment, and they're typically all right (if a bit cordially frustrated) with the idea that any questions about such will be met with cryptic or obstinate responses. (I think a lot of audience members are like this as well; as far as I know, the general moviegoing public took the idea of Gandalf as a largely uncategorized thing and that whole matter of returning from the dead at face value)

                    Originally posted by Erinys View Post
                    Why?

                    A DB in Heaven can easily find out the Immaculate faith is a bunch of lies. And find out about the Sidereals. That is a serious problem, because not all of them will be OK with living out lies and being used by the Sidereals.
                    I mean, it's terribly boring as a blanket policy statement, for one thing. And it's a pretty lazy policy, for another. It's an example of something that is really heavy-handed and shuts down stories.

                    I think it's more interesting to want to manipulate, explain away, rationalize, and justify it to any DB you see. Present them with the factual choice that if they go home and tell everyone, who will actually believe them? They'll be called a heretic and killed. And if they are believed by some few, what will it accomplish rather than push the Sick Man that is the Realm right over the precipice due to anarchy in its top rank? Or get you labeled "dangerous to the ongoing order of the Realm" and either exiled or given a truly shit position as far away from anything important as possible to keep you quiet? Or say you actually agree with that, then go home and double cross the agreement - that kicks off additional plots.

                    That is a choice and the seed of a story. "lol teacup ninja leaps forth from your bowl of pho at a canal-side noodle shop and kills u with ur own chopstix bc your punk ass shouldn't be here, gg, reroll a new char" is not.

                    Of course Sids can kill DBs - and probably without too much difficulty, given the right agent or just the right martial arts. How exactly is that interesting? What value does that add to the setting?

                                                    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                                    Other than that, I think a lot of people interact with Sidereals the way that characters in The Lord of the Rings interact with Gandalf; they're not generally very curious about where he came from as opposed to what he's doing at the moment, and they're typically all right (if a bit cordially frustrated) with the idea that any questions about such will be met with cryptic or obstinate responses.                                                     I don't know. In LotR, wizards were an established thing -- people had sayings and stories about them. They might not know everything about the technical details, but when Gandalf appeared and started throwing around magic, they'd say "oh, it's a wizard, everyone knows what a wizard is."

                                                    For the audience it's much the same. Gandalf has a long white beard, a pointy hat, a robe, and a staff. Of course he can use magic. Why wouldn't he be able to? What else is there to explain? Pointy hat. Beard. QED.

                                                    Sidereals are in a more awkward position because there's no easy explanation for the powers they wield, no easy cultural narrative they can slip into to make people accept them without question. They can pass themselves off as mortal sorcerers, perhaps, or (in 3e) as Exigents, but these wouldn't have worked as well in 2e, and anyone who witnesses Sidereal powers too frequently or used by multiple different Sidereals is going to start to get suspicious.

But as Exalted hosts, the Sidereals and Dragon-Blooded play the role in the setting of defending and benefitting from the status quo, which means that as groups they include lots of people, and policies, that condone and/or encourage what the status quo is and does -- much of which is quite awful. Maintaining an empire and slave trade requires routine acts of extreme brutality and cruelty.                                                             Individual characters can act against the grain, but part of being a member of groups that have this narrative role is that they will be connected with, involved in, complicit with, or asked to participate in "asshole" activities. Whether they believe it's justified or not, how they go about their business in that context, and what goals they have, that looks to me like part of roleplaying in the context of that narrative role in the Age of Sorrows.
