    Some questions on DB charms and character building

    04-28-2020, 09:40 PM
    Hey gang,
    At long last I found an Exalted game! And a DB game, my favorite splat that doesn't rhyme with "Midereal" . I've been lurking around the forums, there is a ton of great advice, and I have some questions I'm hoping people have insight on.

    The Aura system is interesting. I think for combat charms my plan is to stick in aspect (that gives me an excuse to do IMA) so Aura doesn't trip me up, at least for my first character. But I'm wondering if people find Aura is as much of a consideration when it comes to the social side of charms? I know that's an really broad question. For instance, my vague plan is to head maybe five or more charms into socialize, at the very least get up to Auspicious First Meeting Attitude. I'm not seeing too many charms in socialize with aura effects, and so it doesn't seem like there is a big difference between a fire aspect using those charms versus water or air. Am I correct? If so, is this notably less true in other trees?

    Also, for combat capability my plan was to at least take the IMA up to the form charm. If I'm a Fire aspect, should I grab some dodge charms as well? If I'm Air, should I supplement the style with thrown and/or stealth charms (or maybe heavenly typhoon evocations)? Speaking of Air, I was also looking at water dragon and eventually (prob after chargen?) some ebon shadow. Would both that and Air end up making a more ninja-like character? What would the differences be?

    Lastly, I kinda love that the righteous devil form charm makes it work with wood dragon. It gives me the image of a (to put it mildly) unorthodox dancing immaculate cowboy monk. Do those styles actually support each other well?

      Air Dragon

      Air Dragon is one of the most alter-ability dependent styles in the game, but that's not necessarily a bad thing. With it you really want to grab Dodge, Athletics and Stealth as abilities, and maybe a few charms in them. If you have Heirs to the Shogunate there's some particularly solid new Air based Dodge, Athletics and Stealth charms in there. If you're getting Heavenly Typhoon I'd also invest in Occult. Thrown charms are not necessary but if you've got the ability dots and charms to spare some of them can be fun, but you can't use them in conjunction with your MA.

      The main strategies of the style involve Thrown's strengths, namely getting into Close range with archers, and staying at Short range against everyone else, being exactly where they don't want you. It's also exceptional anywhere you can take advantage of terrain elevation or difficult terrain, and of course stealth attacks. It's a lot of investment, but for that you'll get a good combat style, the ability to run up smoke, vanish into thin air, and annihilate spirits, as well as having both a good doge and parry score in the event of unblockable/undodgeable attacks.

      You might think about gettting Midnight Thorn with this one, it's a jade chain shirt from Arms.

      Earth Dragon

      Not much to say with this except it's really solid and also really straight forward. Just hit things, often with smashing attacks, and soak damage. Heavy armor is the best choice for combat, but the mobility penalty can sometimes suck, so going unarmored with resistance charms isn't the worst thing in the world. Also a style that taking three dex and maxing out strength and stamina might not be the worst idea, depending on your campaign. Maybe grab Faith's Pillar or Fist of Titans. Mela's Coil is also a good armor. In fact it's probably the best armor in the game.

      Fire Dragon

      Doesn't actually blend that well with dodge, most of its charms specify you need to parry with them. Definitely pair it with a strong Awareness though, and if you're into mixing styles it goes pretty amazing with Steel Devil, depending on your campaign. It doesn't work very well against enemies that have really high defense, and penalty ignoring charms, and high soak, for example. You might want to pair with Smiling Razors, if you do though get Stealth as well as Larceny its evocations are based around smokey sneak attacks and infiltration. Likewise Midnight Thorn.

      I'd also watch out with it. Like a lot of Fire charms in general, the trick with Fire Dragon is actually knowing when to burn out, and when to simmer. With it's Join Battle boosting charm and it's other charms that add bonuses to decisive attacks and reflexive activations for going first, a possible strategy is to just blow 30+ motes in the very first turn of combat and just end something, but then that's it, your mote pool is cold ash after that.

      Water Dragon

      Not much to say on this either, it's just solid and pretty straightforward. The only thing is it deals in a lot of wound penalty stuff, so the best strategy with it is probably not to wait until you have 40 initiative and destroy something, but more to give them death by degrees as they drown. It combos well with ally attacks that give enhanced wound penalties. It can pair well with Tiger Style if you want to be aggressive, or Ebon Shadow if you want to be a ninja.

      Vengeant, the razor claws, are cool, but very...angry. For an angry Iselsi murderer though that's a good thing.

      Wood Dragon

      Kind of a super weird style. If you're going to be really social with this one grabbing the Red Jade Powerbow Heartsong might be a good fit, also getting it with archery. It doesn't pair especially well with Righteous Devil, but like Archery it will let you attack at range and fight at close range if you want to. It also just pairs well with Performance anyway as some of the attacks use it. Also if you get this style pump your Stamina and get ox-bodies, and maybe some healing magic as well. It's a really rugged style for that reason.

      It also pairs well with White Reaper style, which can also use staves. Archery/RD style complement it by giving you variety, WR style just adds more effects onto what you were already doing.

      If you're looking for artifacts the Daring Venture V'Neef family one is pretty good. You can't use a full longbow while mounted so Hundred Rivers is just a combat companion or non-combat mount if you wanted to use Archery, otherwise you can just ride him around using the bow as a staff and dismounting to shoot if you ever need to. Get survival with it though.

      The other ones you might use are Calumny, the Green Jade Wrackstaff of house Cynis, which is awesome to use for a social character because it steals secrets from people. Or you can use Sekhem, the Red Jade Dragon Sigh Wand, it doesn't give much synergy but it is cool and its bayonet counts as an artifact as well.

          But I'm wondering if people find Aura is as much of a consideration when it comes to the social side of charms?
          I find it's very manageable.

          NPCs have Resolve scores of 1-5. You can't convince anyone without an Intimacy, so you're looking at 0-8 before Charms, Willpower and Stunts. 0-17 worst case. But the fact is, most NPCs have 2-3 Resolve, eating a -1 from an Intimacy, and you're throwing at least a 2-die stunt. Even as a Dragon-Blooded, you have to pretty much trip over and swallow your tongue to fail social rolls (unless you're trying something crazy like convincing Ahlat to destroy Harborhead -- I mapped out how to do that with a Solar in another thread).

          As you basically don't need Charms at all, Aura isn't pivotal. Charms are gravy.

          Exalted gives you stats for the typical farmer or shop keeper. Resolve 1, Guile 1. It gives you stats for an exotic and successful caravan merchant - 4. It gives you stats for one of the most powerful gods in Creation 5/4.

          Take Ahlat. Resolve 5. Have an adversial ST who says Ahlat views all Dragon-Blooded as cowards and traitors. Resolve 9.

          Let's say you want him to bless your army so Harbourhead isn't wiped out by Alchemicals. Resolve 7. You make a speach, referencing your love of the city asking the god to set aside your differences and win glory on the battlefield in defense of all Creation; +1 Success and +2 dice. You spend Willpower; +1 Success. 4m Excellency adds exploding 10s and two more Successes. You now only need to roll 3 successes, which should be easily doable on Charisma 3 Presence 4 plus two from the Stunt (84% certainty before the exploding 10s).

          Yes, Ahlat could Stunt/Willpower that up to 5-7 successes needed. But equally you could use the Air Anima power, have Charisma/Presence 5, Specailty, and tag the "War is the Crucible of Heroes" Intimacy. (If both sides go All In then, keeping the PC at a Level 2 Stunt, it's Resolve 12, dropped to 9 with 5 automatic successes, so trying for 4 successes on 17 dice with exploding 10s... 97% certainty of success.)

          So long as you're trying to do reasonable things, you won't really need Charms like Hot Blooded Ardor -- let alone the awesome Aura effects of Hot Blooded Ardor.

                      I really need to make a thread about this...

                      Exalted has two "modes". The Default mode sees you create characters like the example in the Corebook and the pregens in Tomb of Dreams. The Optimized mode sees you make your characters as powerful as possible.

                      Default, I see no reason why the social MAs shouldn't be fine.

                      Crane Style: The big hit is Fluttering Cry of Warning. If you have a way to reliably Defend Other with your Marital Arts Parry then this'll work on am Optimized DB.

                      Silver Voiced Nightingale Style: Voice of the Nightbird's Mastery effect is huge. Losing that sucks. Reasonable synergy with Crane.

                      Righteous Devil Style: Reasonable synergy with Wood Dragon and White Reaper.

                      Black Claw Style: This really suffers for a DB PC. Lots of effects capped by Essence. Multitarget dropped to single target. No Solar Performance Charms to help. It's fine if you're playing Default. OK for a Water Dragon Immaculate.

                      Dreaming Pearl Courtesan: 2/3rds of these Charms have either (or both of) the Terrestrial or Mastery keywords. But... you can start with the Form Charm as a DB. Which means free Artifact weapon and armor. Great for Water Aspects with Water Dragon Style. Reasonable synergy with Air Dragon Style. This is the style you want to have Immaculate Martial Arts with.

                      White Veil Style: If it existed, it would be the defining social MA. It would be disappointingly far better for Solars. Its Charms would be steeply priced for the size of Dragon-Blooded mote pools. A dedicated DB martial artist would find this combos well with the other social MAs but essentially no other style, except maybe Ebon Shadow.

                          @JohnDoe244 and DrLoveMonkey will give you better advice than I can, but just wanted to mention that you don’t necessarily have to rely on MA to leverage your social stats in combat with DBs if you don’t want to, it’s something they’ve historically done well with native charms.

                          Wary Yellow Dog Attitude lets you use Socialise for JB, Fearsome Dragon Presence gives your opponent a -2 penalty for a tick, Performance has things like Vibrating Strings Defence and Petal-Strewn Pavane, the Integrity Excellency lets you ignore wound penalties, and so on.

                          Once you hit Essence 3 and beyond and have access to things like Signature Charms, Aura of Invulnerability and Three-String Sword Prana, your ‘capability ceiling’ is maybe (to my eyes at least) higher than for a lot of the social MAs for DBs, and you’re less restricted in terms of armour and weapon combos with your primary MA/combat ability.

                          That said, social MA styles are fun and characterful as hell (John's breakdown of them above is great), so you’ll hopefully have a blast if you go down that route. I personally really wanna play a DB Stealth/Larceny/White Veil master at some point.

Maybe lean into that by leaving Athletics at 1 and boost it cheaply later as it's favoured?

                                                          2. You're very wilderness orientated but have a dot of mundane followers, what's your thinking, are they just typical Dynast servants, or something more? If you've got an idea then great, but I'd be tempted to ditch for something you can use all the time and isn't going to slow you down as you plunge into the wilderness.

                                                          3. Random question: Is it a game in the West as you're taking Seatongue? If you're going to be on a ship lots is a massive wolf going to be as useful as you'd like? Disregard if you're, say, going to be doing loads of guerilla type stuff on Caul or the like...
