    Originally posted by The Soulforger's Flame View Post
    I have two questions:
    1. Is it okay/expected to have multiple Ties to a character based on conflicting feelings? If you were trying to ruin a person's positive view of another character, would you simultaneously try to lower the character's positive tie while also building up a new negative tie, or would you instead lower their positive tie to transform it into a negative tie?

    In our game, there are two PCs who are in love with each other but one is deservedly distrustful of the other. We've been running that he has a Defining Tie (Love) and also a Minor Tie (Distrust), which conflict with each other in certain situations, and that tends to work out, but I was curious what the expectation was. (I imagine things would start falling apart if you had multiple positive or multiple negative ties for one person).
    You are playing correctly. (Well mutual Defining Ties of Love probably represents a deeply unhealthy co-dependent relationship, but as you're playing great heroes of myth, I'ma assume this is intentional.) You (usually) take the highest supporting and highest opposing Intimacy for each roll, so multiple Intimacies for the same person isn't complicated.

    Originally posted by Core Rulebook, page 224
    CONTRADICTORY INTIMACIES

    Can a character have contradictory Intimacies? Can you love someone, and also fear them, or even hate them? Can you admire the Scarlet Empress and hate the Realm she’s built? Can you trust Panther (a former gladiator) as a friend, while distrusting gladiators and other men who live by violence?

    Absolutely! Characters frequently hold contradictory feelings close to their hearts—it’s part of the complexity of good drama. In the case of contradictory Intimacies and social influence, these conflicting Intimacies act upon a character’s Resolve just the same as in any other situation where influence is supported by one Intimacy and opposed by another.
    As for ruining someone's positive view of someone else, either approach listed works. It depends on what you're trying to achieve. You could try to complete erode the positive Intimacy, or you could try to throw in a conflicting Intimacy straight away.

    Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post
    It's doable, but for purpose of Intimacies you pick at character creation, it might be simplest to take one Tie whose emotional context encompasses the conflicting feelings.
    Also a great solution. One of the PCs in my game has a Tie of Ambilivant Love/Hate for her mother. But a lot of canon NPCs have ties of mixed emotional context (like Cynis Cogen's tie of Troubled Loyalty to his House).

                The merits are pretty balanced for just being able to take them at cost, but roleplaying concerns should always be taken into account, especially when thinking about merits. If you take Cult 5, you're not really allowed to get pissy because your character is a magnet for Wyld Hunts and constantly have to deal with your worshippers everywhere you go. Cult makes you the object of worship for a direction spanning religion, it's not just a free Willpower battery.

                Likewise Deep Breath Cultivation lets you meditate for an hour and completely refill your motepool, and adds Stamina to her hourly mote recovery, but that incredible power comes from having a crystal pure soul and immaculately clean body. If you don't have that, where's it coming from? That's a question that should have an answer, and probably not one that's gamed to minimize interaction. Like "There was an air spirit who I made a deal with to avenge her against a secret society that killed her friend, so she sacrificed herself to give me the power of super-essence respiration, but then I just didn't care to follow through, and the society collapsed anyway and everyone in it is dead, and they didn't know about me making this deal in the first place and I don't want to hear anything about them showing up in this game."

                                                    I'm usually willing to let Exalts' special benefits get around the limitations on spells (a Fire Aspect sorceress, for example, could hit an enemy with Flight of the Brilliant Raptor at short range to herself, and use her anima ability to ignore being engulfed in the bonfire). But Impervious Sphere of Water is doing more than just putting people inside it underwater. The sphere is impervious, it's right there in the name. Note the difficulty of moving in or out of it: requiring a (Strength + Athletics) roll is much more difficult than simply entering a body of water. Due to this, I wouldn't allow Fang-of-the-Depths Draw to ignore the Impervious Sphere, no.
