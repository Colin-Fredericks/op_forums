        I think that the root cause of a lot of these problems is the conceit in earlier books that "mote expenditure causes errors in the Loom." Seriously, that was just not a good idea at all. Even beyond the whole question of "How many motes does it take to break reality?" the deeper issue is that the gods who are supposed to be managing shit have to spend motes to do that management. A good of rain and storms doesn't have any innate ability to adjust rain and storms independent of his charms, and those are going to cost motes. The implication that managing reality actively breaks it in other parts is just silly.

        Even if you say that this is only true of Exalts and not of gods... Well, okay. WHY? "Because something amazing happaned" is insufficient explanation for me. I'd rather just do away with mote expenditure causing errors entirely. That way I can say "Essence 'spending' is an act of supernal will and a minor miracle occurs, just like when you pray to the God of Bountiful Harvests and get one."

                The real problem with developer/author claims that one can't apply the scientific method to Essence (and the given example about it) are thus:

                1) You're taking the player-base from a setting where it was explicitly true that it COULD be measured to a setting where it's explicitly true that it CANNOT, claiming it's the "same world" so to speak, and then not explaining in any terms how this is actually better, as opposed to simply different. Rather, what you might term as better is seen as something of an authorial conceit. This can be summed up in the statement "It clashes with what we know about the setting". Sorcery (as players currently understand it) requires research and trial-and-error testing. Wherever results are repeatable enough to produce trial and error, the scientific method can be applied.

                2) Wherever a two discreet uses of an effect produce the same result using the same fuel, so to speak, the scientific method applies. Saying that a character cannot determine that two characters using "Glorious Solar Attack Alpha" use the same amount of mystical prowess to achieve the same results is an insult to the idea of the intelligent character. Again, this relies on the player bases current understanding of charms. It may well be the current development paradigm that no two uses of a charm can be identical, or that no two characters can have identical expressions of the same charm. If that's the case, though, that should be a sidebar. This can be summed up as "Two characters both pay three motes for effect A. How does someone who can see mystical energy not notice this?"

                3) In reference to understanding particles and love, the author example only holds water if the character doing the math has no method to manipulate said particles. This actually strikes me as literally a perfect example of how Three-Fold Binding of the Heart might have been invented. Since it, as last printed, literally forced people to fall in love with you. There was also a Sidereal charm that was pretty infamous for destroying love. Of course, that one was VERY mystical, and involved removing someone's name from a list. But that's cause and effect. SO you CAN apply the scientific method to it. It just very well may be that your scientific answer is "This list controls the potential to be loved. Things not on this list cannot be loved."


                Now, I am totally down with the answer that even if you COULD somehow detect these things, the technology, hell, even the foundational principles for said technology, might not exist. It might even be that the mathematics to quantify it outside of certain very specific scenarios and methods, such as Sorcerous Workings, might not exist. I think what's REALLY going on here is a misunderstanding of what the scientific method IS. I expect that rational observation of phenomena in Creation leads one to mystical and transcendent answers. When you apply the scientific method in Creation, you cannot get answers that are useful to people who do not exist in certain transcendent states. I also expect it tends to break down around the works of the Exalted and the more powerful gods. Just because I honestly don't believe most higher mathematics properly describe the functions of creation.

                Rational observation that produces repeatable results is impossible to avoid in the context of a game system. Impossible. That's sort of how systems work. Okay it's exactly how systems work. BUT it doesn't mean those observations always provide data that can explain things. Exaltation being a miracle is cool. Exigence being a thing makes it an observable and variable phenomena. This means that a lot of miracles in the setting are phrased in ways that imply one can, with the proper senses, observe them, codify the differences, note the similarities, and basically explain how it all seems to occur. Know how something happens and why it happens are different, though. I think that's the hang up here.

                So lets just put this on the table bluntly. The Scientific Method won't provide useful answers because the authors are going to go out of their way to do their best to make sure it doesn't. Take that as you like it, but understand their rational in doing so. It IS a somewhat sensible claim.

                Authors, Devs, etc: Rationalism and the Scientific Method not existing as workable concepts is probably hard for most people to wrap their head around. It might be better to work from the angle "These things are different". Rationalism in any setting where miracles and gods are demonstrable is going to include those things. It's only rational to pray to a river god for his good will when he keeps drowning your kids if you don't.

                                            It seems to me there are obvious ways in which scientific rationalism could be productively applied to Exalted - almost certainly there exist curious people who have tried to record e.g. how often a very weak spell (which is a discrete named thing in-setting across editions, while Charms have been changing in that respect) could be cast over time or between Exalts of different development of their personal Essence. I'd be astounded if Exalts with access to the fruits of these individuals' efforts (Sidereals, people with First Age memories, occultists trained at the Heptagram) didn't understand in-setting that the prayers of a cult dedicated to an Exalt restored their Essence over the course of the day.

                                            At the same time, I am totally comfortable with the term "mote" being an out-of-setting term only, because a mote is not like a calorie. A calorie is the amount of energy that raises the temperature of one gram of water by one degree Celsius at one atmosphere of pressure. Motes oscillate in their energy-efficiency, and often have effects that simply cannot be compared in terms of energy. Empirical inquiry is never going to lead you to a systemic understanding of what a single mote is able to accomplish, because the answer to that question might variously be "15 watts of work for ten seconds," "true love for a year and a day," and "the color blue."

                                                    Originally posted by Side Item View Post
                                                    Just so I'm clear, what specific parts of Essence do you want to be examined? I can see Twilight Sally studying dragon lines and trying to see how Lunar essence can be better harnessed by a manse, but when it comes to system abstractions like mote counts I don't see how it makes sense for her to Scientific Method her way to realizing that, for instance, she gains 5 motes of Essence every round she spends in combat.
                                                    Generally speaking, it should be possible to know that Spirit A has a bigger pool of Essence than Spirit B, and that Solar A has a bigger Essence Pool than Solar B. It should even be possible, using the correct charms, to calculate how large someone's Essence pool is, how many motes it costs to fuel a given charm, and therefore how often someone can use a charm.

                                                    And let's be clear - you don't even really need the concept of motes to calculate Essence pools.

                                                    I mean, I can easily imagine Sally Twilight in the First Age making an Artifact box, which you can channel Essence into.

                                                    "Oh, look. I just filled 5 of these boxes with my Essence, and I can't fill a 6th one. That must mean I have 5 boxes worth of Essence. Dawn Caste Dave over there was able to fill 7 boxes worth of Essence."

                                                    And, rather than calculating Essence pools by motes (which are an abstraction), they can calculate Essence pools by "boxes" (out of game, each of which house 10 motes of Essence), which are discrete things in the game. So even if Sally doesn't know she has 50 motes, and indeed the word mote doesn't exist, she does know she has 5 boxes of Essence, Dave has 7 boxes of Essence, and she can begin performing calculations.

                                                    And if that doesn't work, imagine them making identical artifacts and seeing how many of these one person can attune to before they can't attune to anymore (as their Essence pool is now empty).

                                                    I can also imagine similar scenarios where Sally Twilight teaches Dave the same charm she knows, and then the two use the charm the same number of times to calculate Essence. If the charm is a 1 mote cost charm (the smallest amount of Essence you can spend) then they can get a rough count of how many motes (or boxes or artifacts attuned) they each possess.

                                                    This doesn't seem like rocket science to and it does feel like the sort of thing that genius Exalted in the First Age, in their quest to understand Exaltation and their own powers might have done. "Even though Sally and Dave are both using the same charm, why can Dave use that charm more than Sally can? We know Charms run off of Essence. So maybe he has more Essence? Let's find out."

                                                    Adding more mysteries to the game is fine and all, but this one seems like a mystery for the sake of being a mystery at the risk of ruining my verisimilitude. I just have a lot of difficulty imagining that the Exalted of the First Age didn't engage in comprehensive and detailed examinations of Essence and were never able to come to even a basic understanding of something like an approximation of the size of their own Essence pool.

                                                            Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post
                                                            It can be! It totally can be. It's just that empirical inquiry isn't a very useful or productive way to experience it. That's why All-Encompassing Sorcerer's Sight is an Occult Charm, a description and magnification of your ability to see and experience the transcendental aspects of reality, instead of being a Lore Charm or an Awareness Charm.
                                                            Mm. The majority of the Charms dealing with "Essence" as a thing are in Lore, though, to the point where by and large "Essence" as a concept is a Lore thing rather than an Occult thing, and that seems to have been intentionally carried forward.

                                                            Originally posted by Robert Vance View Post
                                                            I get that this is a hard sell, especially since I think at least the vast majority of this forum subscribes pretty whole-heartedly to scientific rationalism, or something like it. I mean, I come from a faith that has a long tradition of interrogating and questioning the divine and am an atheist on top of that, so I get the resistance to "Essence is mysterious and ineffable." But Creation is a world in which spirituality and spiritual experience are major, indispensable elements of reality.
                                                            I have to say, I really feel like this is at least as much the game's doing as the community's. On the whole, it's been more consistent about presenting Essence as conforming to scientific rationalism than not, it put nearly all the Essence powers in the "science" ability, it emphasized the technological aspects of Essence use (e.g. things like automatic lights that turn on when you spend Essence) and presented devices analogous to modern technology that run on "essence", it often depicted savants as being analogous to modern scientists, and -- subtly but I think most importantly -- it framed "motes" as an in-setting construct and encouraged people to think about their mechanical resources in that light. Compare D&D or Vampire, which typically refer to such things as "points" or "levels" even when they are broadly analogous to setting elements, to emphasize that they're mechanical abstractions. This isn't just line drift -- it reached new heights of ostentation with DotFA, but it's been that way since early 1e.

                                                            Edited to add: I went back to the books to make sure I wasn't talking out of my ass, and there in the glossary -- 1e defines "mote" as "a unit used by sorcerers and savants to measure Essence", while 2e defines it as "The smallest divisible unit of Essence in the parlance of savants and sorcerers". And the game seems to be actively encouraging a simulationist link between motes-as-setting-construct and the resource you spend, given the number of powers that cost 1 mote but could easily be free. So...I think that's where that came from, more or less?
