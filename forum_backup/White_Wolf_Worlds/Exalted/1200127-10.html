    Ah good, our next part is relatively early.

    I’ve already seen the chapter fiction with Arada, since it was what came with the House Tepet preview. Suffice to say, it’s a nice little account of the Exalted at war, between elements like the weird magics that they employ and the brutal battles. Plus the nice account of a higher Essence Dragon Blood, with the aid of a Hearth mate, putting down a Solar (who still hardly seems weak in the example).

    So, the character creation chapter; should be fairly boilerplate.

    Aspect Abilities are the same as they ever were, but I already knew that. What’s really interesting is that they now get a full five Favoured Abilities. I was wondering if they would do that; in discussions about the Dragon Blooded in the past, it was noted that their Aspect Abilities were already diverse that even with the three they used to have, you could get a fairly diverse array of starting characters. But now, between the additional Abilities and the manner in which Charms are going to work, with every Ability having elements and its Signature, you have latitude to build any Aspect virtually any way that you want. I think that’s a very good change, as I know people have had strong character concepts but struggled with which Aspect to give on the grounds of how to get the Abilities that they want.

    I also already knew about the additional specialities you can get based on the character’s background. I find something perversely amusing in how education at Pasiap’s Stair would give one specialties in Athletics and Resistance.

    I’d also say that additional dots in certain key Merits is a good and balanced way to represent the additional power that comes from a privileged upbringing.

    Aaand that’s the chapter done. I like how, even in something as simple as character creation, there was a standout detail.

    Next!

    Now a chapter fiction from the perspective of lost egg Kingfisher Swift, and her adopted patrician family. I like the perspective on a family member growing old before your eyes.

    Oh no, her career is over?! Those goddamn Cathaks. {shakes fist}

    I like how so many of the chapter fictions are offering a different cultural perspective on the place of Dragon Blooded in generally, and the Realm specifically. In particular, Swift’s sense of disillusionment in the Realm after having been brought in with the message of a lost egg’s prospects, and the manner in which she and her niece/household matriarch are discussing their own opportunities in the face of coming war, with innuendo and implication. It also inspires a neat little plot hook, of Dynasts going to war and being shocked by an uprising of patricians backed by outcastes in key strategic locations. I’ve often thought about how the Realm could resemble the Chinese Warring States, with many of the larger kingdoms often fighting over control of small but important states lying in between them, some of which had assets to maintain a measure of independence. I never considered the Imperial City itself in that role, somewhere between a haven of power for some strong patrician families and holdout of loyalism to the Scarlet Empress.

    Now, let’s see if I can quickly get through what new tricks the Dragon Blooded get.

    The distributions of existing Merits is all familiar and basic. I expected that Prasadi Dragon Blooded to be able to get Cult, what with being gods, and I quite appreciate a breakdown of what levels of Resources actually mean to the Dragon Blooded, especially how little to none can represent being severely indebted.

    Now, what’s new…

    Ohohoho God, a Merit to represent their fancy titles or nicknames, such as the Roseblack. That is priceless (and I mean that in the best manner possible). That’s the kind of thing that I like specifically for my own fascination with names, and I think being able to occasionally upgrade stunts based off of it is a fine mechanical realisation.

    I’m guessing that the Well-Bred Merit is at least partially covering the old Breeding Background, although if it’s still exclusive to Dragon Blooded… So yeah, like they used to say, the slightly more abstract notion of improved odds for children to Exalt, and the firmer one of getting a tasty automatic success in bargaining rolls in which having a good pedigree is a farctor. Useful, but not with the sense of being mandatory that the old Background’s extra motes was.

    Oh, is that the extent of them? Well, I must admit to being a little bit disappointed that there never was a Breeding Merit for mortals. Ah well, let’s see about these flaws.

    Or rather, flaw singular. An inversion of the Merit for the likes of outcastes and leftover children makes sense, and can probably add a nice sense of texture to possible social interactions in the Realm, in return for the bonus xp.

    I was wondering if there would be a discussion on children Exalting in the absence of a Storytelling chapter, and a sidebar right next to the associated Merits is surely the best place. Good good; in place of a dice roll, providing a general ratio (something that I’ve always wanted) as a guideline for Storytellers. I presume that this discussion of ratios to pedigrees is assuming that one is effectively husbanding their progenitive Essence; so if you’re a pair of Dynasts with some mortals or leftover children in your family history, even if you maximize progenitive Essence in every child, odds of it taking are about fifty-fifty, whereas if your bloodline is consistently strong across generations, you can expect it of the majority of your children. But since even the best odds can fail, and with the manner in which statistics works, there are still some outlying cases of deviations from that standard; a high pedigree couple might improbably produce numerous mortals, while a lower one might have a few extra. It’s a good, solid picture of the Dynasty.

    Next up are the Aspect write-ups; I’ll be looking forward to these in the full book, as I expect the art pieces to really make them pop. For now, I’ll mostly be interested in discussion of things such as Aspect markings and anima powers, since I expect the main body of the write-ups themselves to be fairly familiar.

    Yeah, the description of the Air Aspect is fairly standard. I’ve actually got my pdf of the First Edition book to compare on a few notes, and I observe that one thing that’s a bit different from them is a more detailed perspective on the place an Aspect within a Hearth, so that’s nice; generate some of the dynamics they could have.

    Uh-huh, Aspect markings of pale skin, cool feels and smells and the occasional visual effect of wind or electricity. And the anima… wait, are they doing the same thing as the Solars, where the anima powers are described in detail a bit further on? Yeah, I suddenly remembered that was what they did before just before I got to this part, and I can see here they they’re described, but without the mechanical breakdowns. I’ll save comment on those until I see them in their full forms. For the moment, I like the touch of the light of which even a Dragon Blood’s anima is composed resembling light with a particular association of the element, such as reflecting off of ice.

    With that, there isn’t much to say about the other Aspects, beyond a general endorsement of some of the variety in their markings and character in their animas.

    Oh, I also like the revised take on exactly what the basis of their Aspect Abilities is, particularly the Fire one with the idea of excelling at things that draw attention and give them unrestrained access.

    Okay, now we’re on to the anima banner. There’s the breakdown of levels, something that I think they didn’t often have before. Anima flux seems to not quite be the great danger that it used to be, at least unintentionally. It still has the notion of causing possible scenery damage (with the caveat of being dramatically appropriate), and the amusing element of creating a preference for hardier homes, but I think it’s dropped the element in which it makes them widely averse to riding animals (although I’ll reserve that until I see the Ride Charms, unless somebody in the know wishes to clarify).

    Now the anima powers, which have interactions with Elemental Aura of Charms. I like the detail of being able to spend a mote to have something like a minor, dramatic manifestation of your anima for purposes of convenience; it’s actually the kind of thing I would have house-ruled before, given the opportunity. There’s something a bit wrong if you can’t, by default, snap a finger at your pipe to light it when you’re a Fire Aspect (or Air, with a spark of electricity).

    So, the powers; I’m particularly interested to see the part about drawing upon one’s passions and the like. Air gets to make high leaps vertically and horizontally and be resistant to falling damage, generate a slight environmental penalty on ranged attacks, and occasionally add extra dice to mental and social actions in upholding a Principle. Earth gets to boost soak and Hardness and Defence against certain Earth-themed attacks, a slight resistance to pain and physical debility, and a knockback effect to their Anima flux. Fire gets immunity to natural fire and improved soak against fire attacks, improved damage to their anima flux, and can occasionally get dice from emotional Intimacies to boost actions upholding them. Water lets you walk across the surface of water and improves mobility in difficult terrain (nice sense of their adaptability there), breath and and act within water without difficulty, and boost effects to get away from enemies and keep from being grappled. And Wood gets immunity to mundane poison from plants and improved resistance to all other poison and disease, can reduce penalties to Evasion and movement actions, and their Anima flux can inflict poison of their own.

    I think these additions to the anima powers are reasonably solid; they’re low key as is appropriate to Dragon Blooded, but still useful and convey some nice character. The ones that give some dice bonuses also have interesting implications; between their non-Charm dice and Dragon Blooded being able to add their specialties to their dice cap, I can see some circumstances in which one can gain a rather decent bonus to a dice roll. Not with the consistency of a Solar, but if applied in the right moment…

    And the character advancement. As I recall, there was going to be something in that for their expression of the Great Curse…

    The Expression Bonus is about what it was with Solars, building off of one’s Intimacies and Flaws, whereas the Role/Aspect Bonus… Ah, right, the Great Curse is an additional criteria for earning the experience, along with ceding the spotlight and expressing one’s Aspect. I take it that their Curse will be described in a bit. The Aspect roles that can be rewarded are all what one could derive from the Aspect write-ups.

    Hmm, a reiteration of training times is a curious thing, since it seems about the same as in the core rules to me. The principle of raising Essence is the same, although I understand that the numbers are a bit different, and I’ll be interested to compare them a bit later on.

    And finally, the Dragon Blooded Great Curse. The much weaker effect than on the Celestials manifests through lacking a Limit Track, instead being a more subtle influence on behaviour that is incentivized through awarding experience points if it enhances the game. I find that to be a stronger expression of the principle than before, where it was about acting in a deleterious manner if you ran out of Willpower.

    Air Aspects are Cursed with a potential for bad reactions to the world falling short of their ideals, become so caught up in ideals that they ignore the well-being of others, or become self-aggrandizing to the point of being touchy or arrogant. Earth can have a bad attitude with regards to how they defend their traditions, become intractable in upholding them, or violent or morose in response to insult or challenge of their validity. Fire can become dangerously excessive or overwhelmingly driven by extreme emotions with regards to how they relate to others or perceive themselves. Water can make perseverance into something that loses perspective or becomes extreme in its methods, as well as loosening moral standards or becoming caught up in unnecessarily circuitous planning. And Wood might make one hedonistic or thrill-seeking followed by significant emotional lows, or overbearing in relationships with others.
    It’s an interesting take on the Curse, where the emotions and impulses are all the kinds of thing that a Dragon Blood may already have, but the benefits create an incentive to play them up at dramatic or inopportune moments that emphasize them as something that can undermine the Exalted.

    I think the Traits chapter is nice and lean now; it trimmed away a lot of unnecessary benefits to the Dragon Blooded, while seasoning a bit with some nice anima powers and incentives to play into the characterisation of Aspects.

    I’ll get a start on Charms later, although for today I’ll probably just stick to the introductory parts and actually look at the Abilities over the next few days.

            All right, a start on the Charms chapter. Beginning with a chapter fiction featuring Mnemon! Don’t let me down now.

            Ah, and she’s in Jiara. I’m not usually a fan of that idea of immortals becoming bored with the world, but it’s fitting to her particular traits; plus, it reaches my soft spot for recognition of behavioural patterns in the aggregate. In keeping with Mnemon’s stronger Immaculate leanings now, I like the manner in which she expresses contempt for grandiose mortal architecture… oh, that covers her other element as well, delightful! I also appreciate this idea that she’s here to personally direct operations.


            Aaaand she’s thinking about how having her personal guard keep a respectful distance leaves her “alone as she ever was”, and my heart’s breaking.

            I like the description of her mystically taking in the city, an-oh yes, kung fu fight with Novia Claro!

            I’ve got to say, I like the idea of Mnemon being a bit light-hearted in battle, even when she’s slashed to ribbons. That and the demon-summoning; she wouldn’t be her without it.

            As a chapter fiction, it doesn’t necessarily present the powers of the Dragon Blooded much more than several of the others. But it features Mnemon being haughty and kicking ass, and it’s hard for me to not like such a thing.

            Now, on to the actual powers… well, the outline.

            A fairly concise reiteration of the basic point of what Charms are, with a reference to their perception in the context of a big society of Exalted.

            Something that I had caught a passing glimpse of elsewhere; Dragon Blooded ultimately did not get free Excellencies, instead having to buy them as separate Charms. I saw Holden begin to say something about playtesting, so I presume it was found that access to such power for the Dragon Blooded was too unbalancing. Mind, I imagine that the non-Charm dice that they can occasionally get from their anima can mitigate that a bit.

            Elemental Aura was introduced months back, but if there’s anything more mechanically detailed here… Ah, the manner in which Aura manifested in Anima wasn’t described before. The Fire Aspect… heh, speaking of Avatar influences. Overall, I like the notion of their animas being able to transform a bit like that, as I feel it ties into the organic and cyclical elemental themes.
            Right, so Aura is activated by spending a short period using elementally pure powers, lasting either for a scene, or until another element is wielded. A bonfire anima prevents any alternate element’s Aura, to the point where it automatically shifts an extant Aura. So that would mean that if you enter an Aura other than your native element, using enough Essence will cut it off. That answers a question I had before about whether there would be anything giving one’s native element prominence.

            There’s an even mix of effects there, Charms that can only be used in Aura, Charms made stronger, ones using it as fuel and others being tied to its duration. Ah, and there’s a helpful keyword to give some Charms that don’t mess up the Aura, as well as elementally neutral Charms. And finally, non-Dragon Blooded powers have no interaction, regardless of their own elemental nature, with the exception of the Immaculate Dragon Styles.

            Signature Charms were also introduced, but now there’s the interesting detail of Essence 5 letting a Dragon Blood to learn their Aspect element’s Charm in their Aspect and Favoured Abilities. So there’s another thing to give that element centrality, albeit in a nicely soft manner that doesn’t undermine the potential elemental diversity of the Dragon Blooded.

            Keywords… most have already been introduced, one for each element, Aura for the ones that require that, Balanced for what doesn’t mess it up… oh, the detail of a mix of Balanced Charms letting you enter any of the Auras is neat. There’s a keyword to denote Excellency Charms, and the Signatures.

            Ah yes, of course there would be Charms with multiple elements, Elemental Bolt and the like.

            And that’s the end of the introduction. Good foundations here, and I’m looking forward to seeing how they play out in the actual Charms tomorrow.

                        First observation: I think that Unobstructed Hunter’s Aim may have more keywords than any previous Charm that wasn’t in Martial Arts.

                        Second observation: it’s interesting for Charms in the same cascade to have different elements. I suppose that’s the only way that they could be managed without making each Ability massively bloated. Besides, the whole thing already has a sense of elemental integration.

                        Oh, Unobstructed Hunter’s Bow is the Excellency; I wouldn’t have expected those to be elemental. But it’s also Balanced, so you don’t need to mess up your non-Wood Auras in order to add dice to Archery attacks. And it’s spruced up a bit with being able to ignore a sensory penalty.

                        I like how Harvest of the Hunter interacts with the mechanics for ammunition checks; it makes the basic ammo producing Charm a bit more interesting, on top of the image of drawing them from surrounding plant life.

                        I like the way the Charms can be elemental, while the actual effects don’t carry elemental manifestations for two reasons: one is that it emphasizes that for the Dragon Blooded, the elements can be things more metaphorical as well as inherent to the basic way of doing things, and the other is that it overlays a significant tactical element to their use of Charms. Being able to reflexively reload a flame weapon is simple enough, but what if it’s going to break your Aura?

                        Still, it’s nice that they do get more overt manifestations, and I like the expression of Wood themes in the entangling vines that eat undead.

                        Oh, they got Arbor Sentinel Technique into the book after all. I wonder what changed for Vance to be satisfied by it.

                        Drawing Lightning Style has a nice little effect, and I like the visuals of drawing lightning into an arrow.

                        Ahh, the signature Charms. Horizon-Spanning Arc is interesting to me because it seems to play off that theme of long-ranged support that was stated as part of why Archery is a Wood Aspect Ability. Plus it’s another nice example of something understated, but pretty powerful.

                        I like the way that Earth’s Judgement Awakened realises the idea that the counterattack is powered by one’s personality by how the counterattack rolls Presence.

                        Blazing Phoenix Pinion… fairly straightforward. Exploding arrow. It’s a bit odd to have a description of people being set on fire here rather than in the core, but I guess that while there are still effects there, it’s not as prominent as it is with the Dragon Blooded.

                        Fang-of-the-Depths Draw adds a nice element over the natural Water Aspect capacity to move in water (even if it’s not limited to them). I also like the dynamic in the concealment effects, with the image of a Terrestrial switching back and forth between attacking from the water and popping out to stab somebody, before just falling back into the water.

                        And Heartbeats before Death is a nice, straightforward Charm for rapid and precise loosing.

                        Hmm, clashing attacks really work well with Dragonfly Finds Mate.

                        I don’t really get why Salamander Swallows Flame doesn’t have the Wood Keyword, but I am loving the image of a Dragon Blood catching a Solar’s Blazing Bolt, an Abyssals Crypt Bolt, or an Infernal’s Green Nimbus, and shooting it back at them.

                        Now, I don’t remember if Sparrow Dives Hawk was ever a thing before, and I am definitely not going to trawl through old Charm chapters, but from apart from the additional power it provides, that name is inspired.

                        Huh; not very many Essence 4 Charms, and we go very quickly to Essence 5. What have they got there…

                        Seven-Year Swarm Volley seems like a good example of the kind of Charm that has a good, strong teamwork element, and definitely the kind of thing that should be limited by needing the Aura and only getting one shot per scene. And Arrow Rain Tempest… heh, I love how that name is not metaphorical, as a way of realising a powerful Water based Archery attack. Plus how it’s pretty powerful, even in comparison to some Solar effects, but for the limits in use and Aura.

                        The Archery Charms are a good showcase. There’s a nice mix of effects being metaphorically and literally elemental, how even the metaphorical ones have their obvious strengths, and a good sense of what Aura does for the ebb and flow of how Dragon Blooded powers are employed. It also makes good use of the limited charge thing that the core introduced; how they can get stronger powers than they used to, but they have to be used very carefully.

                                        Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                        So I looked into what Holden said specifically; apparently when people got Excellencies automatically, playtesters too frequently relied on that alone, and it made the Dragon Blooded feel more like cut rate Solars. There was an adjustment with the intention of Dragon Blooded wheeling and dealing to bring their powers to bear with maximized strength.
                                        I'm not sure how it'll work in play or not, but the Charms thus far released are DEFINITELY in this vein, and it's very very interesting to me.

                                        Like... previously, the Dragon-Blooded were sort of "worse Solars" when it came to the charmset. The things they had going for them were hyper-efficient Excellencies, a smattering of synergy Charms, and at the meta level the fact that they had access to all sorts of resources via chargen that Solars usually did not. But mechanically speaking they operated a LOT like Solars, just with the top end removed; no or highly limited perfects, a smaller mote pool, Charms that did basically the same stuff but were strictly not as good, etc.

                                        (My personal table experience was that starting DBs were a LOT like starting Solars, and then the Solars would gain access to their top end and the Dragon-Blooded were like "... oh. Right. I can't... I can't do that." Others mileage may vary.)

                                        Well, one look at this Charmset says that this has changed radically. They are still, strictly speaking "worse than" Solars, for a given value of "worse," but they are emphatically not "worse Solars."

                                        What's been done, and someone correct me if I'm wrong here, is that they've been given a very powerful but also very limited toolkit, with internal synergies AND internal anti-synergies. The hyper-efficient Excellencies have gone away and been replaced with what looks like either "flexible" Excellencies (dice-adder OR more expensive success adder) or "bonus" Excellencies (dice adder AND special sauce.) The Charms do very very potent things but require setup, forethought, and the proper circumstances to be fully exploited; where a Solar would just brute force their way through by being so fucking amazing that they can't be stopped, a Dragon-Blood needs the right tool for the job and the right circumstances to use that tool... but if that applies, their raw heroism and elemental power often carries the day. There's definitely wheeling and dealing involved.

                                        There also seems to be a big re-emphasis on their elemental nature and their ties to Creation.
