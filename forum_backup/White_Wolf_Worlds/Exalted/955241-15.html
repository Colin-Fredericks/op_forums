        Free Him! 5
        Play Offense: 1

        As the dozens of raitons not tearing into your windfall stare at you, you raise their tangled compatriot and hold it out in front of you. It squawks and struggles, and tries to peck at your hand.
        “Stop it, you stupid little shit,” you mutter, “I’m trying to help you.”

        The raiton stops squawking, and cocks its head. It stops its thrashing. It’s…it’s honestly a little creepy, you think. You know they’re smart birds, but it’s almost like it can actually understand you.

        You raise your knife. The quietly watching flock doesn’t like that! Several of them spread their wings to look bigger, and hiss at you.

        “It’s okay,” you tell them, trying to be as soothing as you can. “Look. I’m just cutting him loose.”

        Your knife is new, and sharp. Your twine is old, and cheap. Cutting the raiton loose is easy, taking only a couple of quick cuts before the big black bird is free. It sits there for a moment, head cocked, watching you…then hops out of your hand and flutters away into the sky, ignoring the food all the other raitons are squabbling over.

        The silent watchers watch it go, then look back at the meat scattered over the rooftop…then back skyward. One by one, they take off to follow it. The raitons scavenging over the rest of the roof ignore them, continuing to croak and shriek at each other as they fight over your things…but then the ones that have taken to the air circle back around and call to them, and they too leave the roof and take to the sky.

        The great black flock wings its way off southward, and you are left alone on the roof with your admittedly rather pecked-over prize.

        Quickly, not wanting to lose the daylight, you gather everything on the roof and toss it into your bag. Then you take out the small bottle of vinegar you brought with you and pour it over your back. You wince as the cold liquid hits the cuts on your back-you can’t see how bad it is back there, but it stings something awful. You’ll look at the wound again when you get back to your apartment.

        Slowly and carefully, you descend the drainpipe to street level. The world is dark gray and the sky overhead is a deep violet, with an evening star or two starting to poke their way through the omnipresent Nexus smog. You give a long and wary stare to the alley you last saw the Raptor Cat limping down…but there’s no sign of the beast, other than a scraping of fur and a little blood on the cobblestones where it hit the ground. After a moment of thought, you scrape this up with your knife as best you can and put it in one of your jars. Between River Dragon blubber and Raptor Cat fur, you might be able to come up with some sort of stinky ointment that’ll scare away animals. You won’t have much use for it, there aren’t a lot of inhuman predators roaming the streets of Nexus…but you might be able to sell it. There are lots of pack animals in town, and the power to spook them and cause wagons to go off course or overturn is something people might readily pay for…

        Anyway, that’s something for when you get home.
        *****

        It’s a long walk home to your new apartment, and a stressful one-you know the area around Nighthammer pretty well, but as you’re heading north through Nightside to your new neighborhood the territory gets less and less familiar. You don’t know which alleys are safe shortcuts and which ones are meeting places for people you don’t want to run into. You’re not sure which areas of the night markets are “protected” by street gangs who will want to charge you for passing through their territory. You’re forced to stick to main thoroughfares and take slower paths than you normally would. It takes a little over an hour to get back to the Blackwall neighborhood. It’s…a lot less homey at night. There’s garbage in streets and mean looking cats-and every time you hear a rustle in an alley and look down it to meet a pair of gleaming reflective cat-eyes, you find adrenaline surging through your body as your hands tighten on the handle of your knife. That Raptor Cat is still out there somewhere, licking its wounds.

        The figures lurking on stoops during the day are now laughing in alleyways, drinking and sometimes yelling at each other. The rooftop gardens aren’t visible in the dark, and the only lights are from the windows of the apartment buildings-the streets are dark and menacing. Somewhere a block or so away you can hear loud sounds of merriment and cruel laughter-Blackwall is only a few minutes from the canal, and you remember there’s a popular tavern there where some of the boatmen and dockworkers unwind.

        You’re only accosted once or twice, but you must cut a pretty imposing figure, carrying a big knife and a heavy and- despite your best efforts- bloodstained bag, with torn clothes. Most people leave you alone, and the ones who don’t are drunk enough it’s easy to evade them.

        Finally, you arrive home. You dash up the creaky stairs, turn your key in the lock, and enter your apartment-and jump as White Moth shrieks in terror at the sight of you, throwing a piece of chicken on a stick at you.

        You close and lock the door as she calms down. You can’t help smiling a little. Having people be afraid of you, having people want to get out of your way instead of seeing you as prey…that feels good.

        “It’s just me, Moth,” you sigh. “You can relax.”

        “Merciful Ancestors, Tavi.” She gasps. “What happened to you?”

        “What do you mean?”

        “Well, you’re wearing completely different clothes, and they’re all ripped up, and you have cuts and scratches all over your face and hands, and you’re carrying a giant knife, and you stink like dead fish, and…is that bag dripping blood?”

        You toss the bag onto your new counter.

        “Fuck.” You mutter. “That was a brand new bag…Moth, do you know any good ways to get bloodstains out?”

        “Lemon and table salt. Gods, what happened to your back?!”

        “I, uh. I fought a bunch of raitons. And a Raptor Cat.”

        White Moth looks at you like you’ve sprouted wings and tentacles. “You…fought a Raptor Cat.”

        “Yeah. There’s been a rumor going around for a while that there’s one loose in the city, living in the roofways. Some Merchant Prince’s pet that got loose? Turns out it’s true. Thing kind of came after me.”

        Moth rolls off the bedroll where she had been eating, and starts digging through cupboards, and she comes out with…bandages and several bottles of ointment? You can’t help being surprised, she doesn’t seem practical-minded enough to have medical supplies readily available.

        “Here, Tavi. We’re going to want to disinfect those right away or you’re going to get sick. Raptor Cats are like those big lizards from the Southeast-they’ll kill you right away if they can, but if they can’t they’ll just try to injure you. Their claws are filthy, so the wounds usually get infected…and then they just follow you from a distance until you get too sick to fight.”

        “I already put some vinegar on them,” you say, “But yeah, it can’t hurt…so wait, how do you know so much about Raptor Cats?”

        “I read,” Moth says, rolling her eyes as she gently turns you around to get a better look at your back.

        “You, uh…you say this thing is gonna be following me around now?”

        “Well…you said you fought it. Did you give it a good scare?”

        “I think so. Knocked it off a roof. I think it has a broken leg now.”

        “I guess we can hope that’ll do the trick. But be on your guard. They’re really good at tracking by scent, and they’re ambush predators. They’re…mostly nocturnal, if I’m remembering right.”

        “What’s nocturnal?”

        “They come out at night, and sleep through most of the day. I would avoid going out much at night for a few days, if I were you.”

        “…okay. OW!”

        Moth pats your shoulder. “Sorry. This ointment will sting really badly, but it’ll make sure you heal up really well. No dashing scars for you, young man.”

        “Too bad,” you chuckle. “Being able to say ‘I got this one fighting a giant panther-snake monster’ is some nice bragging rights.”

        “Yeah, I suppose a lot of girls would go for that…though personally I prefer a boy who knows how to chant in the language of the Old Realm and can recite genealogies from memory.”

        “…I have the weirdest roommate,” you declare, wincing as she rubs the ointment into your cuts.

        “Hey, I’m not the one out fighting raptor cats and bringing home bags full of human hands.”

        “What!? That’s not full of human hands!”

        “No? I just sort of assumed. What’s in there then?”

        “…none of your business!”

        “Is it a head?”

        “It’s not a head. It’s just a fish and a bunch of meats I tried to smoke, and apparently I didn’t do a very good job…and managed to attract a Raptor Cat with the smell.”

        “Well, they’re all yours then. I hate fish. Here, take off your shirt.” Moth tugs at your new outfit. “I’ll need to wrap this bandage up over your shoulder and around your chest.”

        “You, uh…you have to?”

        “Otherwise it’s going to fall off. I’m going to want to get some around your waist as well, just to hold these dressings in place…Don’t worry, I won’t tell anyone how scrawny you are.”

        You’re…uncomfortable disrobing in front of people. The only ones who know you’re a girl are Aunt Dwi and a couple of her people at the Flaming Peacock. Even Rat doesn’t know, and he’s the closest thing you have to a best friend. You don’t know White Moth very well yet, and you’re pretty sure that she’s a bit…well, disorganized and irresponsible and scatter-brained. You’re not sure you want her knowing your secret. She seems like the sort of person who might tell people without even meaning to tell anyone. On the other hand, she does seem to know what she’s doing with these bandages and disinfectants, and you don’t much relish the thought of getting infected wounds. Especially if there’s a Raptor Cat out there waiting for you to get weak before it shows up for Round Two.

        What should you do?

                                            Do the Bandages Yourself 1
                                            Let Moth In On Your Secret: 5

                                            “Fine,” you tell Moth. “Here, I’ll take my shirt off so you can do the bandages. But…listen. Don’t tell anybody.”

                                            “Don’t tell anybody about wha-oh.” She nods as you take off your shirt. “You, um. You’re a girl?
                                            ”
                                            “I…yeah. But nobody knows that, Moth. I want to keep it that way?”

                                            She seems confused. “How come?”

                                            “The only thing worse than being a boy alone on the streets is being a girl alone on the streets.” You shake your head. “It’s dangerous.”

                                            Moth nods. “I think I get it…but how long do you plan to keep up the act? I mean, you are really very masculine-no offense? But surely you can’t pretend forever?”

                                            “Well…I don’t know. I mean, I hear if you don’t eat well it can delay puberty, so…”

                                            “That’s only in cases of extreme malnutrition, Tavi.” Moth frowns. “I mean…you’re way too skinny…have you been deliberately starving yourself?!”

                                            “What? No!” You find yourself actually chuckling. “No, I eat everything I can, whenever I can!”

                                            “Then the gods blessed you with a much better metabolism than mine,” she sighs.

                                            “Metabowhat?”

                                            “Metabolism. It’s, um…it’s the speed at which your body burns food. People like me with slow metabolisms don’t have to eat much and we still put on fat.”

                                            “I’m pretty sure I’m not skinny because of a…metabolism. Pretty sure I’m skinny because I usually don’t have money for food.”

                                            “Oh. Oh! I’m so sorry!”

                                            “…for what?”

                                            “I didn’t mean to be insensitive…”

                                            “You mean, like, for a job?” You finish up with the ointment and put it away, and walk across the room to grab another outfit to put on.

                                            “Yeah.”

                                            “I’m…well, I’m hoping I’m an apprentice canal boatman? We’ll see, it depends on if my teacher was serious about teaching me or not. He agreed to it last night, but I haven’t actually talked to him since, and it was kind of a weird night.”

                                            “I see. I know the feeling. My teacher sometimes makes me feel more like a servant than an apprentice. Always running around town fetching ingredients and stirring cauldrons and drawing eyes on pieces of obsidian with white chalk…there’s a lot less one on one instruction time than I’d like. But you learn by doing, you know?”

                                            “Making this fishhook into a needle. I’m going to sew up that torn shirt.”

                                            “You know how to sew?” She seems impressed. “I’d have just thrown it out.”

                                            “Yeah. I just bought that shirt. I’m going to damn well get some use out of it.”

                                            “I’d offer you one of my needles, but they’re not much good for sewing. No eyes…I guess they’re pins, really?”

                                            “What are they for?” You get your twine and sit cross-legged next to her on the bedroll.

                                            “Acupuncture. They’re good for clearing my mind if I want to meditate.”

                                            “That’s the thing where you stick pins in yourself?”

                                            “I mostly stick them in my feet. It helps me think.”

                                            “Yeah, yeah…listen, Tavi. You said part of me staying here was I had to teach you to read, right?”

                                            “I did.”

We have to write them over and over again, so you’ll learn them pretty quickly.”

                                            “They’re kind of a long prayer strip. When we’re done, they’ll say, ‘In the name of the Dual Monarchy, in the name of Setesh, in the name of Saturn who brings to each their appointed Ending, I do bind thee, O Spirit of the Departed, and seal thee within this, thy prison, until these words fade and their power is broken. By the power of the Lords of the Dead, Be It So!’…we’re going to have to use some fancy ink I bought. It’s made from the aqueous humors of dead jailers.”

                                            “…what’s an Aqueous Humor?”

                                            “They’re for binding angry ghosts so they can’t hurt anyone. You trap the ghost in a clay pot or a statue, and wrap prayer strips all around it.”
