    Tomb of the Spiral Key- My first Exalted Session! [Actual Play]

    04-30-2018, 09:45 AM
    Hello! I finally got a chance to actually play! Last week my friends came by and we spent a day making characters and picking charms. Today we started a prologue for 3 of the 5 characters who we free. I posted the Outline earlier, and we followed it down to number 12, meeting the Abyssal Exalted. First the characters, then the session review, and then my thoughts on actual play.

    1) The Characters: We have a cool trio. They are
    The Captain (Eclipse, Sail). Has a boat and a Crew of ~80 good men. Exalted upon punching the eye out of a Kraken. married into the Peleps family before exaltation, and his Father-in-Law, a Peleps Admiral turned a blind eye on his anathema s-i-l in exchange for a) saving his life and boat, and b)using the newfound family anathema to Privateer for the Peleps family and sabotage other Great Houses. Favors Brawl and Single Point style.

    The Scholar (Twilight, Occult). Has a Tiger-Sized Otter called Meat Bun. Exalted upon reading the Broken Winged Crane and not going insane (although it cost him a lot of memories "quarantined" away). Has 2 melee charms and ISoB along with Death of Obsidian Butterflies. Generally focused on curse-breaking and traveling, he learned how to fight because the roads are never too safe for travellers. Did I mention he has a tiger-sized Otter?

    The Atheist (Twilight, Occult). Is a sorcerer, Exalted upon meeting every god in Great Forks after a 50 year pilgrimage. Refuses to believe that gods and sorcery are real, and that there must be a naturalistic explanation for occult phenomena. Is a giant walking contradiction, which feeds into her limit and mania flaws. Her Initiation is Scarred by Nightmares, except its about the dissonance of her beliefs and the state of Creation's Metaphysics. Uses a clockwork crossbow.

    The three all started on the same island called The Maiden's Isle, which is a decent sized island with a pop of 2000 or so. It has a miraculous source of pure water that never spoils, and the trees there grow strong and tall, and twice as fast as usual. It has a small mine as well, making it a natural hub of ship repair and taking on provisions. The Scholar is there under contract to break a curse on a shipwright's daughter, the Captain is there to offload his grey-market goods, and the Atheist is there because her transport was damaged in a storm.

    The Session
    The Captain and his men go to the Isle's premier inn to get their customary first drink, where the Scholar is currently residing. It is the night of the Festival to the Maiden, so both are sticking around town to have a drink and relax before departing. The Captain's men freak out seeing a giant otter hooked up by the horses, and one of them takes a dare to touch it. He gets his arm broken for his troubles (Side note, this sailor's name is Ja Ni. He'll be important later). The Captain and Scholar meet, and resolve things over a drink. The Atheist is overwhelmed with Scientific Curiosity™ for the otter and wants to vivisect it. She splits off from them after being told no.

    At the Festival, things proceed smoothly and the three are separate until an unseen arrow ends the life of the High Priest right before the Isle's divine patron, the Isle Maiden finishes manifesting. Raiders attack the town square, butchering innocents and pushing to the spring in the center of the square. The Captain leaps 50 feet right on to the Altar of the spring, openly showing his power and daring any to attack him. He has twenty men with him, and they form a disciplined line separating the raiders from the civilians. The Scholar leaps down from his viewing balcony, his straight sword landing straight through a bandit. The Atheist watches this and readies her crossbow- she's not personally invested yet. The Captain has taken a few raiders out with his fists, but he wants to set an example, so he declares a grapple (yes, against a Battlegroup!). Effortlessly grabbing the first poor bastard in front of him, he uses Increasing Strength Exercise (and Mighty Thews) to rip the man in half, and then throw his corpse at the others. This works, but the man screams in terror as his belly is tore open and his spine is pulled until it rips apart. (So at this point, the BG had already moved from size 3 to size 2 and passed their morale test to resist breaking up. This made them retest at +1 difficulty, and they failed). The Raiders closest to the Captain experience primal fear, and they throw down arms as quickly as possible but run into the reinforcements still pressing on. The scholar kicks a spear sideways at a few raiders trying to rush him, then slices through their necks as they recoil from the impact, and he catches the spear midair to throw at one of the fleeing raiders, which kills him. The Atheist takes a chance to shoot down one of the giant oil + coal braziers, send fifty plus pounds of stone and flame down in the middle of the retreating raiders, sowing chaos, confusion, and panic.

    The head of the Raiders, a large man called Black Tooth, rallies his men and pushes forward, only for the Captain to slice his arm off in a single decisive clash (Literally a Decisive Clash using Single Point). The raiders book it, and are chased out by the Captain's Crew. Things calm down a bit, plot and RP happens. Black Tooth gives away his medallion to the Captain, and it has a strange spiral emblem. He mumbles about the Key before passing out. Oh, and his bloody is weird syrupy sludge so he doesn't bleed to death. A captured raider gives up the plan: they were supposed to knock over a festival for it's easy pickings, and then flee. He has no idea about this Key business at all, but he reveals that Black Tooth was defeated in combat last week by a man dressed all in black with a white mask, who then made some kind of deal with Black Tooth. The Captain press gangs him into service (it was that or he could stand trial. he chose press ganging). The Mayor sends the power trio to check on the Maiden's Grotto, where she actually lives up the mountain. He's worried that the raiders will attack her or that she'll take this as an affront (she is very proud and easily offended) and either way will cut off the pure water that the isle depends on.

    The Trio, some sailors, a chatty local, and a burly dockworker make the trek inland and up the mountain. The top is a weird mess of labyrinthine passages and natural cliff formations. The chatty local says that the way forward is only open to the pure of heart and blah blah blah, recanting local legends. The Scholar uses All Encompassing Sorcerer's Sight to follow a channel of water essence through the paths, and the trio eventually find a dead end. they use their Investigations skills and uncover a way to drop the wall. Inside they find the Maiden's Grotto, where the Maiden entrusts them with the Spiral Key. It was given to her millennia ago, by the Captain himself (well, his previous incarnation). In the light of the Grotto, the caste marks of three light up by the Maiden's presence, and each are definitively revealed to each other (only the Captain has shown his castemark in the festival battle). She senses the town is being attacked again (She can project herself to any of the blessed springs on the island), and the trio rush to its defense. The Atheist calls up Stormwind Rider and ferries the three to the town instantly, while a river forms ex nihilo and a black jade boat appears to ferry back the others.

    The Trio finds a major wall has crumbled, and the undead are pouring into the city. Hundreds of them. Before they intervene, however, they see a band of 5 zombies dressed in robes and brandishing actual weapons moving toward an unbroken section of the wall. Sensing something afoot, they leap down and intervene. We now have our first combat against Solo Opponents. (So technically, 5 zombies is textbook "this should be a battlegroup", which i told the players. This was our tutorial combat against regulars though). Side note: zombies have a LOT of health, 0x2, -1x3, -2x3, I. The Captain delays his action, the Atheist shoots one with a bow, and the Scholar (who has ISoB up already) begins shaping motes for DoOB. Since his Initiation is Broken Wing Crane, he uses the "stored motes and Stunts" ritual of the Talisman of 10k Eyes. The Scholar does really well on this shaping action, and as blood pours out of the Crane and into his shaping hand he is able to pull of Obsidian Butterflies on the first turn. It deals 5 damage to each zombie, a very very powerful opening gambit (not mechanical gambit, figure of speech). The Captain grapples one, and then throws him up into the air and let's gravity do the rest. the Atheist shoots one "where there is a shard of Obsidian lodged in it's head", braining it. The fight starts to wrap up, but not before the Scholar and the Captain perform a double gambit to simultaneously punch in the head of one zombie from both sides, which succeeds only for the Scholar to botch his Resistance roll to undead stench (seriously, three 1's and no successes on 5 dice!), so he falls hands and knees to the ground retching up the undead brain bits that got in his mouth. Meat Bun (the Otter) shows up by now and starts trying to lick him clean, only for it to also double over a retch, so now that's going on. Since the last zombie (initiative crashed) is trying to pull itself up from the ground by grabbing it's pointy-end-in-the-ground sword, the Atheist decides the best kill shot (from Init. 3) would be to stunt an arrow off of the back of the bronze'd puker, ricocheting it up into the brain of the zombie from below. This works (it had 3 health levels left and was crashed, plus its the last zombie. I fiat'd that a single point of damage would kill it).

    The trio pull themselves together (mostly the Scholar and the Otter), and they find a weird clockwork ticking box with a glass cylinder on it, and some noxious looking chemicals swirling in it. figuring its some kind of bomb, they throw it at the zombie horde! only it's not a bomb, its a defiled cloud that produces shadowlands taint. Well, at least it didn't go off wherever the zombie crew wanted it to go off. The Trio easily jump the walls (by jumping, Stormwind Ridering, and Otterriding) and head to the palisade of the desperate townsfolk. The Captain's first mate, Ja Ni (get it?) is there with the mayor. As the trio land in the defended side of the palisade, a black-cloaked figure in a white mask jumps from the shadows and executes the mayor. He then effortlessly defeats Ja Ni, impaling him on two swords and then using a hip toss movement to flip around Ja Ni and transfer that energy into a throw, sending the body sliding off of the 2 swords and careening towards the Captain, where it comes to an undignified, rolling stop. Meat Bun recognizes the man from early and makes a soft cry.

    That's where we finished for tonight.


    Impressions
    1) Even for a forum junkie, it was tough keeping track of all the rules for withering, decisive, battlegroups, and then we took a 10 minute "how the hell does grapple work" break. This was gettin easier by the end of the night though.

    2) Grapple is crazy good, considering 99% of everyone else can't grapple well.

    3) Even against zombies, combat took longer than I thought it would in terms of rounds. Against a ragtag BG and the basic zombie, it was still 1 withering attack with charms to jump up initiative, and then one decisive to deal about 4ish points of damage.

    4) Sorcery is rad as F***. Also, having Stormwind Rider was like playing Fallout or Mass Effect and having a perk show up on the dialogue wheel. DoOB did 5 damage right off the bat too, I shudder to think what ordinary battlegroups will do against that level of power

    5) Do you only get ONE of the Shaping Rituals? I thought you got all of them for your chosen path, but it seems like you just pick one you like.

    6) Some rituals are way, way better than others. The 10k Talisman i mentioned gives you a ritual to add Stunt Rating + 2 motes, which is easy to get, and also lets you store up to ten motes in it. That was enough with a good roll to cast the 15 sm DoOB at someone on the enemies at the 1st round of combat. One of the other ones listed in the "miscellaneous rituals" section lets you make an Occult and Int roll and store it up... once per story. How are those things on the same planet?

    7) Not that Zombies are tough, but a standard zombie was literally incapable of successfully attacking any of the players. Now, the Scholar and the Captain were Dex 4/5 and Ability 5, so that's top end of combat ability, but that meant that with a standard medium weapon they had parry 6; and 5 dice attack of a zombie would only get through with an obscenely lucky roll- more successes than dice. A basic bandit enemy is the same. Maybe I should have been working on onslaught more.

    8) Its a little unclear where the Familiar 2 and Familiar 3 line is. F3 is Tyrant Lizard and Explicitly Supernatural Abilities familiar, where as "An otter, but the size of a tiger" seems like a good F2. But also just a regular horse is an F2. You can buy a horse though, more or less out of pocket, which seems like it shouldnt be in the same category as "Rajah from Aladdin", or a horse/lion combo.

    9) Range Bands are like a real thing huh. Quite a few times getting to medium was a consideration for the Captain. I'm also not sure how far you can move without using a Combat action for it. I settled on "One range band and an attack, or 2 ranges and no attack". Where are the rules for that?

    10) The archer had to Ready and Aim the crossbow at medium range, which took a lot of time. Sure, the attack was rad, but it seemed like a big investment of turns. 2 turns prep and one turn to attack is how long a combat sorcery should probably take. it was made from relative safety though, so there's that.

    11) The Otter was the unofficial Mascot of the night. Everyone loves him.

    12) Having an animal that is basically "A tiger, but also even better in water" is really useful The West

    13) I was surprised how un-minmaxy my players were once we got to making a real character. Their merits are all built around their backstories and thematic, but their aren't synergistic with their other powers. It might ruffle some feathers to say this (blah blah Storm Wind Fallacy), but i really feel it made these characters a lot better to interact with. Of course the crazy lady who spent 50 years walking the wilds on a pilgrimage would have Iron Stomach, Endure Elements, and the Survive type merits. The Scholar has dips into melee and Danger Sense because he's a wanderer whose been attacked dozens of times. The Crew of the Captain were used as a BG once, but mostly the performed routine work and served as grunts. He didn't even use all of them. Also, he uses Brawl and Single Point (to effectively play as a cross between Luffy and Mugen) because he thinks that would be fun, not because it's mechanically optimal.

    15) Players will come up with the most insane, crazy ideas that would never have occurred to me ever.

    16) Its really rewarding when a player looks at their charms for an out of combat scenario and says "Hey, would this help"?

    17) It's a lot of effort to work in meaningful noncombat encounters, especially avoiding the "Adventure Game One Solution" syndrome of doing Exactly What The ST Wants. It's Very Very Rewarding though.

    18) the players are 10+years RPing level, Once or Twice level, and First Time TTRPGing level, and you'd be hard pressed to have guessed who was who.

    Thank you all for reading, and I'll post again after our session in May!

    Please be warned: this is not champagne, this is most likely a duck.
    -Chausse

      Sorcerous Overlord, you’ve got a lot there but somethings immediately jumped out, I’m going to number them in relation to each impression point

      2) yes grapple is crazy effective, and can essentially lock down an opponent and render them near impotent imo.

      3) keep in mind that bgs don’t operate with having flowing initiative. Instead, withering attacks by characters deal damage directly to the track of a battlegroup,

      4) Sorcery is indeed awesome

      5) Each circle of sorcery you select ONE of the shaping rituals within the set of shaping rituals for a particular tradition (ie you don’t get both the stunt and the commitment rituals, you only get one). Each circle you get another which can come from the same tradition or a new one.

      7)! See point about withering attacks
