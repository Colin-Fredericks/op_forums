    Remember, Solars reshape shit and derail plots and that's OK! If they're steamrolling almost everything, well...they're Solars. That's their schtick. If it didn't take an epic effort to seriously stymie them, the setting wouldn't make sense.
    While it is certainly true that the Solar exalted are "big damn heroes" of the highest degree, my zenith caste has yet to take damage five sessions in and he makes a point of being at the front of every combat. Ideally I'd like my players to feel a sense of accomplishment, which requires that they both affect major change AND feel as if their victories were hard-won.     Has anyone had a chance to test abilities/charms that allow for reflexive decisive attacks?         Here's a non-combat tip: if you plan to make mysteries a primary element of your adventure, don't reduce solving them down to one Intelligence + Investigation roll with an obscene difficulty. Instead, break one investigation down into many smaller rolls to find clues and let your players draw their own conclusions.

        Example: instead of one roll to determine who killed the local seneschal, players might roll to establish whether the attacker used magic, what the murder weapon was, if they had help, or if the attack took place elsewhere. Encourage out of character guessing backed up by rolls and storytelling until the player can put the clues together themselves for a more satisfying win.

                                    I generally, in 2e, just went around the rules. Needed Fey to be bad ass? Have a fey noble swordsman with Jade artifact sword, and an Illusion/real/camouflage/smoke/dancing cloak that -10 dice for all the above stuff coming into to play as defense. They would have to activate perfect sight, look through illusions, ignore cover, and ignore defend other. I also had most Fey warriors in Behemoth guises, shaped liked D and D dragons, with the foot soldier goblins exactly like the Draconians from Dragonlance.

                                    Want stuff to be climactic? Build your NPC like PCs, with escape routes and underhanded traps, like burning sandstorms that earth/fire DBs and elementals are immune to, but do 2B or 2L, plus an environmental penalty. Or have Bowman 500 bowman, in 5 battle groups of a hundred, on horses ride around the PCs, at long Range( Parthians used this to good effect against roman legions under Crasus at the Battle of Carhae). Or have have Firwands with bayonets set up in 2 BC, where one will step back, while another step forward, so they can regroups( ie make a Morale roll to heal/recover). Or even 5 BCs of 5 Super elite Mortals each with excellent weapons or access to MA charms( Enlightened 2e Mortals or Godblooded).

                                        As for battlegroups, the ones without commanders die only slightly slower than individual characters with the same stats, get ganged up on by the entire party and end up becoming giant sacks of initiative for my players to steal before using on individual actors of actual note.                                                             How'd they do?

                                                            To be honest, I tend to just try to run whatever fight I think is reasonable fluff-wise, rather than what I think will challenge the PCs in the fight.

                                                            So, for example, I statted up a really badass elite Wyld Hunt, which turned up after 20 sessions of them just fighting non-Exalts (because Exalts aren't that common, and it took time for the Wyld Hunt to hear enough about where/who they are).
                                                            The Wyld Hunt was hiding in a hidden cellar under a shrine, ready to ambush them at a feast. I wasn't sure if the PCs would discover the ambush or not: as it was, they did, because they realised their Dragonblood allies were up to something (that being that they'd betrayed them to the Wyld Hunt), thanks to good Read Intentions rolls, and then found them with the Lunar's Perception charms, and so they unleashed the Magma Kraken, which of course is pretty devastating against five people in a basement not expecting a Magma Kraken.
                                                            Next session, they may well fight half a Wyld Hunt, who are pretty injured. Probably not a very challenging fight, but that's the reward for discovering the ambush.

                                                            I think the thing is, my players tend to either not enjoy combats as much as I, or enjoy them for half an hour or so, and then get bored (as do I: actually, I try to design foes that will hit hard but not last that long, so the fight doesn't drag on too badly). So, I don't really run a very heavy combat game. Mostly the combats are there because I feel they ought to be there, for story reasons.

                                                            If the players loved combat, I'd probably do a lot more and go out of my way to challenge the players.

                                                            Also, I guess if they were Essence 1. They recently hit Essence 5, so it seems infeasible that they'd run into something very challenging every 2-3 sessions. But a lot more things are challenging for Essence 1 characters.
