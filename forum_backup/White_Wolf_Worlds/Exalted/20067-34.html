    Originally posted by MrBridger View Post
    I'm pretty new to Storytelling games like Exalted; I've been interested in them for some time, but I've only ever played D&D and Pathfinder. In those games, it's pretty easy to get a balanced combat encounter because you have the party's level on one hand and the enemies' Challenge Ratings on the other. If they're the same, the monsters are as strong as the party. Storytelling games don't have that. So how do you make sure, when setting up a group of people for the PCs to fight, that they aren't going to walk over/get walked over by them? Do you just go through and compare every relevant stat and Charm, or is there a shorter way?
    What do you do for social combat?
    I'm not sure about 1st Ed, and nobody actually knows for certain with 3rd Ed, but in the 2nd Ed Core book there is a 4 page spread, starting on pg 247, that while not-great, is about the only really written way of guesstimating encounters. As with everything else, it is abstracted to the hilt, but it gives a relative look at hazard levels based on attack dice need by the players, attack dice available to the enemy, and the defenses available to both sides. That same abstracted system can basically be used to roughly calculate social combat too.

    Thus, an Abyssal enemy has stats XYZ, and an unaugmented die pool of A, an unaugmented defense of B, and some Charm that they ALWAYS activate that boosts pool A or defense B. With these effectively static numbers, you can guess, but an educated guess, that they are maybe a threat to your 0 XP perfect circle, not much of a threat to your 50 XP circle, and a laughable not-quite-extra-speed-bump for your 400 XP circle.

    Far from perfect, but also doesn't require you to go, "This Charm does this, and that Charm does that, and they can be stacked like this, but not while using this other Charm over here, etc." for hours and hours.

                                    It's more that in realms of existence without a Loom of Fate to impose the conditions of causality on the world, things tend to operate under rules imposed by the strongest thing around.

                                    Note, the Underworld does have a Fate-equivalent in the Calendar of Setesh - it's just not connected to the Loom of Fate, so if you want to check someone's destiny while he's in the Underworld, you have to travel to the Underworld and look into its sullen skies.

                                    Malfeas has no fate; things happen according to the will of the Yozis, and this means that sometimes every direction is "up," or that you can go through the door on one building and appear in another building tens of miles away.

                                    The Wyld has no fate; the deeper you go into it, the less tenuous the grasp the idea of "cause and effect" have, save for the areas where powerful Raksha and the like impose their wills on the raw chaos.

                                        Originally posted by nalak42 View Post
                                        Abyssals and infernals are outside of fate.
                                        Abyssals are not automatically Outside of Fate. That only happens due to certain charms. However, on exaltation, when they pledge to the Neverborn, their original fate strand corrodes and snaps and in heaven's record they are listed among dead. Their dark fate occludes them from astrological detection in Creation (though not while in the Underworld), but they are otherwise normally affected by Fate effects.

                                        Infernals, on the other claw, do 'naturally' exists OoF even in Creation, and must use a charm, Loom Snarling Deception, to conceal their OoF nature with a fictional fate (which can explicitly include their original identity).

                                        Out of Fate is the status of entities from outside of Creation that are powerful enough to avoid being woven into the Loom, or to creatures tied to direct extensions of their will. These include:
                                        • Gaia, and her Third and Second Circle Kami
                                        • Autochthon, his Divine Ministers, and the Alchemical Exalted (should a return happen)
                                        • The Yozii, their Third and Second Circle Demons, and the Infernal Exalted (both ken).
                                        • The Neverborn, their Hekatonkhires, and the Deathlords.
                                        • Behemoths
                                        • Unshaped, Noble Rakasta, and creatures native to the deeper Wyld (such as gryphons).


                                        Lesser entities from outside of Creation cannot avoid being entangled by the Loom when they enter its purview; such as ghosts, hobgoblins and creatures of the nearer Wyld (including wyld mutants), First Circle Demons, or Deathknights (unless using avatar-keyed charms).
