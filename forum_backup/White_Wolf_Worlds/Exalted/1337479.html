    Lets talk ghosts

    09-23-2019, 08:55 AM
    I'm planning my plot and the PCs will be going up against everyone's go-to villain, Mask of Winters. To learn more about him I'm going to have them go after one of his ambassadors to the cities near Thorns. Now the Deathlords have complex organisations to administer their realms, run everything and so on and almost all of these will be ghosts since, by and large, their empire exist in the Underworld. I'm plotting that Masks ambassador will be a ghost, some ancient and cruel murdered princess. Now, many of the ghosts we have information for tend to return to haunt the living because of things left undone or the horror of their death. But a ghost that awoke in the Underworld, centuries ago, and has now travelled to creation through a shadowland and, for all intents and purposes, has a day job, how should she function?

    I'm guessing she would be corporeal, or at least have the ability to be so. I'm also guessing she'd be just as sentient, wilful and ambitious in death as in life.

    If she is "killed", would her body discorporate? Could she have returned and taken possession of her original corpse?

    Mask would almost certainly have ensured her absolute loyalty via necromancy, but does it seem reasonable that she would be fairly weakly controlled so as to give her maximum flexibility as an ambassador, rather than tightly controlled which would render her little more than a messenger?

    I'm interested in any thoughts you might have around this.

          Experienced and powerful ghosts can become solid for a scene in normal Creation. It's difficult even for them.

          More commonly (though still not something most ghosts can do) is to possess a corpse with the charm Nemissary's Ride. Generally a human, but animals are possible.
          Inanimate objects can also be possessed by some ghosts, for example a suit of armour.
          And very powerful ghosts might even learn to possess a living body, though this is very difficult.

          Originally posted by gribblethemunchkin
          Mask would almost certainly have ensured her absolute loyalty via necromancy, but does it seem reasonable that she would be fairly weakly controlled so as to give her maximum flexibility as an ambassador, rather than tightly controlled which would render her little more than a messenger?
          So, basically while it doesn't have rules yet, Summon Ghost used to work similarly to Summon Demon. So if that stays the same (I imagine it will), it gives her a Defining Intimacy of loyalty to Mask of Winters. But she's not a puppet, she's just a loyal servant, who can still do things the way she thinks best (and may have other Definining Intimacies as strong as the one to Mask).

                          Originally posted by gribblethemunchkin View Post
                          Now, many of the ghosts we have information for tend to return to haunt the living because of things left undone or the horror of their death.
                          I know that Roll of Glorious Divinities II describes Passions in terms of being pieces of unfinished business, but I feel as though that's utilizing a very common (almost cliché) term around ghost lore without fully integrating with all aspects of their depiction in the setting, or even in other parts of the same book.

                          For example, I don't think it's entirely reflective of things underlying ancestor cults and the ghosts that tend to go off to join them, to say nothing of the Skullstone Archipelago. Those are cases where one's death is supposed to be something where one is liberated from the hardships and constraints of living and enjoy the powers and luxuries that come from being one of the honoured dead. I mean, when we get things like a child carving a small wooden horse to be buried with his father, where it will manifest as a magnificent stallion for his father to ride, is that a matter of unfinished business, or wanting to start existing with far greater benefits? I suppose that technically speaking "my life wasn't nice enough" qualifies as an unfinished business, but it's a bit nebulous compared to how the term is usually used.

                          Originally posted by gribblethemunchkin
                          But a ghost that awoke in the Underworld, centuries ago, and has now travelled to creation through a shadowland and, for all intents and purposes, has a day job, how should she function?
                          For the time being until such time as we get a fully realised update of them for the new Edition, I have a notion that ghosts are initially carried by attachment to and memories of their lives, which can be sustained by interactions with and sustenance from those things as they are in the living world. Ancestor cults might have the easiest time of it, providing rites and offerings over a long period of time to directly vital energy to nourish their deathly forms. Haunting also serves as a means of remembering and being remembered.

                          Sooner or later, those connections to the living world fade; things change too far from how they were when you were alive, or you stop having descendants around to honour you. As that happens many ghosts will either stop having anything to hold off the call of Lethe, or else become little more than shallow echoes; the only alternative when life can't provide enough sustenance is to lean more deeply into death. That can often take the form of more overtly expressing and embodying the form or circumstances of one's own death, the emotions of it becoming a lot more prominent and things relating to it forming the basis of one's personality. Even deeper and more dangerous than that is where one internalizes more and more of the iconography of the Underworld into oneself, becoming less and less the memory of a human being and more an inhabitant of that bleak landscape.

                          Either of those things might include a hefty dose of sheer resentment for those that are still alive, and for the moment I think that's an impulse that many Deathlords effectively appeal to and mobilise.

                          So I could say that the ghost of a cruel and murdered princess might serve under the Mask of Winters long after her own life or the kingdom she governed are all but forgotten for reasons including bitterness at all of those secure, contented still living princesses running around up there, and partially because such a powerful overlord provides great opportunities to develop and expand one's own prospects while inhabiting the Underworld.

                          I have approximate knowledge of many things.
                          Write up as I play Xenoblade Chronicles.

                                  Isator Levi - Thats some great reasoning and I'm gleefully pinching it for my game.

                                  I think I overlooked Nemissaries when I wrote this post and it occurs to me that that's probably what this ambassador should be, possessing a custom made corpse for ambassadorial purposes.

She might have a lesser nemisarry as a bodyguard in a battle ready corpse body.

                                  I like the idea that her connections to the world of the living have long since expired and she has thrown herself into the whole "dead and loving it" direction, integrating the symbology of death into her outfits, persona and so on not because its a uniform or a whim but because that immersion is how she sees herself and the strength of her self image is what allows her to resist lethe. e.g. she self identifies not as a person who has died, but as a ghost, a mover and shaker in the realm of the dead.

                                  Her intimacies must reflect this and their strength is what sustains her so they should be fairly obvious and strong.
