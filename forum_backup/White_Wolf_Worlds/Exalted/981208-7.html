                            Originally posted by Baaldam View Post
                            Well, considering in its original context "barbarian" has more to do with cultural arrogance and mass slavery apologism - too things the Realm is infamous for, incidentally - and some of the "barbarous" societies contemporary to the greeks were as complex and rich, if not more so in the persians, egyptians or mesopotamian city-states cases, as the hellenic, i would say that is quite acceptable. Not to mention the Delzahn, Halta and the Haslanti also have lunar patrons and are far from backward.
                            The main thing to consider on this is in Holden's commentary on Lunar Barbarism. The tl;dr:
                            1e went heavily for what he defines as 'ideological barbarism' in Lunars - the fantasy image of a barbarian as a violent and uncivilized person who prides himself on ithose qualities, going further than 'we live away from the dominant civilization and are sneered at by them', and all the way into "civilization is soft and weak, we like wearing itchy dirty furs and wiping with poison ivy and wondering why our butts hurt, and then rolling a big city for riches before burning it to the ground and laughing all BARBARBARBARBAR".

                            2e shied away from that but had them only tangentially connected to what he defined as 'political barbarism'; they live and work with far-afield outlanders of tribes and cultures that are opposed to the dominant civilization not so much because they believe it to be superior [aside from trying to make it superior to overthrow the Realm, but this is because they want to overthrow the Dragon-Blooded, not because they have a philosophical objection to running water and advanced metallurgy] but because it's what they have available. But as a reaction to 1e's ideological barbarian being near-universally despised, they lost a lot of the stylistic barbarian.

                            3e is allegedly going for a combination of stylistic and political. They supposedly don't consider themselves in the context of 'person from tribe' for long, simply because Exaltation tends to make you get a bigger picture [if nothing else, you'll probably be 'biggest guy in entire region around tribe'], but dress according to the cultures they're working with, ICly because it makes it easier to accept them and not see them as an unwanted foreign influence, and OOCly because that was the aesthetic the developers wanted to go with for Lunars as a primary course. So the NPC Lunars we see will wear mongolian-styled furs or Apache style feathered war bonnets instead of silk kimono or Daogong-era cheongsam or whatnot, but they aren't all 'tear apart the interior plumbing barbarbar' like 1e. It's more 'kick out the Dragon-Blooded so we can get revenge and have the benefits of infrastructure'.

                            This information is of course about two or three years old at this point, I think? But it's what we know so far at least.

                                                    Generally speaking, the "Lunars don't have many artifacts, and haven't been able to make artifacts much" thing is an intentional aspect of the way the game is set up. Artifacts are supposed to be rare, and having the bulk of them is meant to be a defining feature of the DB place in the setting. Some Lunars still manage to craft things, yes, but inventing an elaborate explanation for how they could have crafted large numbers of artifacts is self-defeating - the things you're trying to explain away were intentionally put there specifically to make it credible that Lunars have made few artifacts.

                                                    Nothing stops you from making a Lunar-made artifact and giving it a backstory about how they put it together; in fact, the difficulty the setting imposes on that sort of thing only makes the ones they do make more special. The Wyld Hunt isn't omnipresent, and Creation is huge; you don't really need a complicated explanation for how a Lunar slipped through the net long enough to build something cool. It's just something that is (or at least was) very difficult and dangerous for them.

                                                    But "oh Lunars can just poop out a special off-the-grid stabilized Wyld bubble whenever they want" is missing the point. The setting heavily depends on the fact that DBs, Sidereals, and Lunars have not, generally speaking, been able to produce many world-shaking wonders over the past 1500 years. That cannot change without making the entire setting collapse.

                                                    (On top of this, consider that "structural" explanations for why Sidereals and Lunars didn't - Sidereals haven't had the time, Lunars haven't had the resources and have been on the run - allow Sidereals and Lunars to have decent crafting charms with the setting explanation that they haven't been using them too much for the past 1500 years because of those things. If you remove that setting explanation, the only way to avoid a setting comically awash in world-shaking wonders is to nerf Sidereal and Lunar crafting into the ground. It's much better to say eg. "yes, a Sidereal or Lunar can build those things if they have the time and resources, but up until now, they haven't been able to for various reasons that are, to some extent, less applicable now" so PCs who play those splats can still dip their toes into bigger crafting projects if they want.)

                                                            Lunars actually can do the top-tier workings. It's not a rigid thing like that. (In fact, even DBs can do all but the very top-tier Ambition 3 Solar workings.)

                                                            But "once in a lifetime event" sells it short. I think Hatewheel said something about the transition from 2e to 3e about how, in 2e, the game and the fanbase in particular mostly fixated on the Exalted version of Sephiroth - the most over-the-top ridiculously powerful optimal character who achieves everything in their specialty they could conceivably do, and many things outside it.

                                                            Most Exalted are not like that. The hypothetical Exalt who has every charm in their area of expertise rarely exists. Most Exalted never do the absolute limit of what the rules allow them to do. The Exalted are masterful, but they are not literally perfect. They make mistakes. They have limitations. Sometimes they're the peerless conquering hero, and sometimes they're Achilles in his tent, or Odysseus fucking up by pointlessly offending the gods. Even most Dawns do not actually devote their entire lives to a perfect flawless training regimen - they have loves, they have other goals and responsibilities, and these things conflict and keep most Exalted from reaching their hypothetical potential. (In fact, I'd probably argue that no Exalt has yet reached their "full potential", to the extent that that's a thing, though some have come closer than others.)

                                                            The same applies to the Lunar Exalted. They can perform Solar-tier Ambition 3 workings, or make even the greatest N/A artifacts. Most of them have not. It isn't a once-in-a-lifetime thing, it's a it-may-have-happened-a-few-times-ever thing. I mean, Solar-tier Ambition 3 workings are of course unthinkably rare even for Solars; we know of maybe one that happened, ever. It's likely that the vast majority of Twilights in the First Age never even attempted one.

                                                            But that gets into what I'm saying above, in that the game relies more on "here are the practical reasons why this doesn't happen" rather than "it is flatly impossible to do this unless you have a Tier 3 Exaltation" or something like that. I mean - the practical barriers are higher for Lunars than Solars; Lunars need more means to make up for their disadvantages in attempting Solar workings, and almost certainly have far worse crafting charms if they want to attempt N/A artifacts. But it's not impossible, and part of the reason the game can say that is because the setting is set up in a way that provides an explanation for why most Lunars haven't been able to reach that theoretical pinnacle, especially recently.

                                                            Because if they did reach it, even once in a lifetime, the setting would look very different. (And it would also somewhat defeat the purpose of having such a pinnacle by making it seem boring and 'expected' of all Exalted. Again, even most Twilights probably never attempt the top tier of Sorcerous Workings. It's supposed to be a big deal.)

                                                            (By "world-shaking", though, I mean "capable of shaking the entire world", ie. an N/A artifact of such power that it makes everyone everywhere sit up and take notice. Lunars are theoretically capable of making artifacts on that level, but it's very possible that none have been made by them in the past 1500 years, and at most it would be just a few, as needed for individual stories.)
