    Who has had a devil tiger, metropoli/patropoli, or abyssal redemption in a game?

    11-29-2020, 02:55 PM
    (oh god please give me more characters in the title please)

    These three seemingly disparate things all have a certain commonality to me.

    Firstly, they are super cool as fuck and awesome.

    Second, though, they are such extreme things that require a herculean effort and journey to the point where balancing that particular PC with the rest of the party that it feels like it'd be nearly impossible for the others players to not feel a little left out unless they all did this (and that would require such a ridiculously long game that I'm not sure it's feasible.

    Like, an Infernal who became a Devil Tiger has to invent an entire soul hierachy and their own charms and isolate themselves for a long while, and the narrative playing-out of the event would have to be quite long and detailed in a way the other PC's cannot really influence. Likewise, an Alchemical becoming a city requires the player and the ST to actually create Municipal Charms and the other PC's would quite literally be playing inside that PC, and while that would make for some fascinating roleplaying with a good group, it would feel quite weird (a game where all the Alchemical PC's became cities and they basically started playing Civilization would be...fascinating and very weird).

    But hey maybe these really aren't things for PC's to do--patropoli and metropoli perhaps exist mainly to be cool bits of Autochthonia for human-scale PC's to play around in, not really a goal for PC's to do themselves. A lot of things in Exalted that are described mechanically don't actually seem intended for PC's to really use, just to ground everything in the same rules the players use and to give ST's mechanical ideas for their NPC's and settings and the like.

    (a more small-scale example of this kind of thing is Solars or Abyssals learning SMA--something that requires immense investment to even be able to do, but also a long-ass journey to even figure out they can do it, find a mentor, and then actually learn it, while their circlemates cannot really do anything, unless one of them happens to be a Sid who can teach them but that's highly unlikely due to you know Arcane Fate and being shackled to the bureau of destiny and all that)

      I agree about Devil Tigers and Metropoli/Patropoli, but Abyssal redemption feels much less how you're describing than the other ones.

      Like, a redeemed Abyssal is not that different as a character - I don't think they're supposed to have any powers that Abyssals and Solars don't already have. So unlike the other ones, when the quest is over, they can keep playing with the other PCs as before.

      For the actual redemption process, my understanding is that it's entirely up to the Storyteller what that would entail. Typically I would hope the Storyteller to put challenges in the way of redemption that either require all the PCs working together to defeat, or require specialties of some of the other PCs. (Maybe the Circle's Loremaster knows of a genius First Age Solar whose theories that it may be possible to modify an Exaltation were considered heretical and dangerous by the other Solars, and whose notes, preserved in an undisturbed tomb, may be helpful. Maybe it requires bargaining with powerful supernatural entities, who may have their own demands. Probably it requires recovering your Monstrance of Celestial Portion, which is presumably quite heavily guarded. I think there's been previous forum threads you can look to for ideas...)

      They should be pretty happy working on this unless they have their own character goals, and if they do they can each help each other with their personal goals.

      Why is a Sidereal unlikely to be in a Circle with a Solar or an Abyssal? And similarly, isn't a Sidereal who knows how to teach SMA likely to have a mission from the Bureau of Destiny or their own personal agenda which is so dangerous they fear to undertake it, which they would much prefer sending a Solar or Abyssal to do, where a team of Exalts would be even better, and likely to be willing to engage in some quid pro quo?

      Or said more plainly, we can try to form intricate, multilayered plots, but I don't think any of us are above including "The NPC will give you what you seek if you complete one (1) quest" if it means all the players get to have fun helping one player with their goals.

          My Infernal, The Prince of Iron and Fire, became a Devil Tiger who ended up learning Patropolis Charms. That was a game that lasted over a decade IRL and had a pretty big time-skip IC. So... yeah.

          We basically had a First Age Solar (not really, but basically), a Redeemed Abyssal (which, I agree with habitableexoplanet, really wasn't that big a deal), my Infernal, a non-Redeemed Abyssal (honestly, way more broken than the Redeemed one) and a "Regular" Essence 10 Dawn.

          The fact that, in the end, my body was the new heaven and the other characters lived inside me (and that I had Seven Demonic Souls and Charms the size of warehouses) was waaaay less disruptive to the game than when Sweet (our regular Solar Dawn) responded to the Ebon Dragon breaking out of hell by grappling his three-mile-long body and just throwing him back inside. Compared to what the Solars were doing at Essence 10 with their native charms, be growing my own 3CDs instead of just summoning existing ones was not a significant disruption to the game.

          Was getting there disruptive? Not really.

          We blue booked a lot (I think I still have my blue book actually...) and had a generous time-skip. It's just like... what else were you going to do in 2E?

          "Okay we're going to spend the next five sessions venturing into the Underworld to steal your Monsterance, then it's off to heaven to try and convince someone to teach us Sidereal Martial Arts!"

          You weren't doing that instead of playing the main-plot. That was the main-plot.

          Because "wipe out this Wyld Hunt" or "cure this incurable plague" or "wipe out poverty in Nexus" was like a five-minute precursor to the Real Action(TM). You needed players who wanted to do crazy stuff like this, otherwise, your play-session would be sitting around for an hour wondering what you should do like those vultures from Disney's Jungle Book. I mean, five sessions to steal a Monstrance includes four sessions of beating on the Deathlords (bless you secret weaknesses). You based a story around redeeming the Abyssal, because the next story would be about teaching the Dawn SMA, and the story after that would be the Alchemical recovering the Eye of Autochthon, and the story after that would be... whatever epic quest the next PC wanted to do.

              I ran an Infernals game that went through Devil Tiger status. What you need is players who are willing to tinker with the system and a willingess to retcon stuff that just doesn't work out. Generally we would make one charm at a time or an individual demon as plots came up. It requires a decent amount of trust and transparency between the player and GM which can be hard with the more "The game as reality" perspectives but i didn't find it particularly impossible.
