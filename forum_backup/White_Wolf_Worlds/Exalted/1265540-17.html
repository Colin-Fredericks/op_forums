                    Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post

                    It still seems functionally identical to sorcery in general, but with shaping rituals that the authorities don't like. Any Exalted, hence, could be witches; it doesn't seem a thing that can be used to define any one type of hero.
                    First, you have to remember that it's about broad themes, rather than very exact definitions. And as I tried to show, "witch-yness" is not purely about any one thing.

                    It's not just magic the authorities don't like. That's just part of it.

                    On the subject of witches, it's not like one day the governments of Europe made a list of "bad magic and good magic" or a rule that "boys can do magic, girls can't." (In fact, while two-thirds of witch executions during the Witch craze, 1 third were still men.)

                    Rather certain kinds of magic, such as those involving curses, spirits and nature, were seen as dangerously pagan, and so became frowned on. This mixed with sexism, and the fact that women had a bigger role in magic in pagan societies (the Vikings thought magic was for girls; sometimes in their myths the other gods make fun of Odin because he's god of magic. What a girl!), as well as classism, meant it became associated with women, especially rural ones, as well as magicians who didn't attend university (no one accuses John Dee of being a witch).


                    "Witchy" is a reasonable way to view Lunar magic. It's not a 100% dividing line between Lunars and other things. It's just a large part of the inspiration behind their mysticism, compared to Sidereal astrology, Getimian yin/yang alchemy (maybe?), or Dragonblood elementalism.


                    To take a different tack, as Isator said, others can turn into animals. That doesn't stop it being a major Lunar theme.

                        Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post
                        It's also not like the other Exalted that the Immaculates hate don't hit the "witch" buttons in strong thematic ways in their own respective rights.                         Eh........................
                        No, I don't think so.

                        The point of Solars isn't that they take inspiration from the monsters, witches, dark magicians, etc, of human myth.

                        Lunars are raging bestial cannibal predators who wield dark magic, seduce mortals, and wish to tear down civilisation. The deconstruction, is that actually they're kind of presented as the good guys (with exceptions who are pretty stereotypical villains), because are great Empires actually good?

                        Solars are shining heroes empowered by God to save the world. They're the rightful Kings. They've been falsely accused of being demons, but they're not. They don't command the power of Hell*, they don't gain power by consuming their foes.
                        The deconstruction is that the Kings aren't quite rightful, that being a shining hero doesn't prove you're good, and when the rightful Kings ruled they went mad with power.

                        Immaculates may consider the Solars unclean monsters, but the theme is that they're not really, they're heroes.
                        The theme of Lunars is that they are monsters, but why does being a monster mean you're evil?

                        *Except when they do... because Solomon does. They do it not by foul pacts, but because they have God's authority over demons.

                        (Before anyone points out that the above doesn't fit 100% of characters, I'm talking about overall themes.)

                        As Thesaurus referenced, Lunars are the Nemean Lion or the Hydra.
                        Exalted asks why the Nemean Lion and the Hydra are the bad guys; aren't they just defending their territory? Aren't they just trying to eat?

                        Solars are King David... but didn't he totally murder a guy? Is he really a hero?* They're Hercules... but is Hercules the hero? He went mad and killed his family. Odysseus is a Solar. Why is he the hero when he travelled to a foreign city and helped destroy it just to help salve a King's ego?
                        I mean, maybe they are the hero. Exalted asks the question, but you can still decide they are.
                        And you can decide that Lunars really are the monster if you want.

                        But even if you decide Solars and Lunars are both heroes, or they're both monsters, that doesn't mean they're the same. Exalted asks the question about both, but it's not the same starting point.

                        *To be fair to King David, the question is asked of him, and he realises he's not the hero he thought he was. Not all ancient stories are stereotypical.
