                                                    The mechanics have always, always, always said that features like that are adjusted into something that is fitting for the alternative shape. Your songbird has the tail of a songbird, which might be unusually speckled.


                                                    Originally posted by Roadie
                                                    I would actually find it really cool if Lunars had to actively do something to build up their stronger forms out of mutation points, put some investment into disguise attempts and Survival to not instantly stick out with inaccurate animal behavior, had to practice with specific forms to avoid being subtly 'off' in ways that raise peoples' hackles, or whatever.
                                                    So you're saying that you want the Lunars as a baseline to be distinctly bad at their essential, iconic capability?

                                                    Never mind that, on this occasion, a Lunar making a really bad job of acting in-character has explicitly been made a basis for observers to get decent bonuses to their rolls to see through them. That is more than they got in Second Edition; in Second Edition, a person had to be specifically on the lookout for Lunars or shapeshifters, for which they might get as many as a whole two points to their Mental Defence Value.

                                                    Oh yeah, that was another thing about how it worked; you didn't roll for observation against a flat penalty, the Tell was treated as a social attack with a flat value of 12 successes, meaning that anybody with up to a Mental Defence Value of eleven straight up couldn't see it. If you had prior familiarity with what a given Lunar's Tell was, and were specifically on the lookout for Lunars, you generously only needed to have a Mental Defence Value of 8.


                                                    Originally posted by Roadie
                                                    I don't understand why it's just been carried across as-is for multiple editions.
                                                    Well, for one thing, there's the literary background that every single respondent has referred to, to which you've replied nothing. Then there's the fact that describing it as being carried along as-is is not accurate, as the mechanical implementation goes. As I understand it, First Edition didn't model the question of seeing the Tell and making the connection at all, and Second Edition used the system I described above, in which the requirements are fairly high, there's not really any direct action on anybody's part, and nothing comes from how the Lunar acts.

                                                    Here you have a system in which the integrity of a Lunar's most basic magical disguise is something that can be severely compromised by such things as having openly been a Lunar in a different form in front of the observer in the past, making a poor job of playing who or what you're pretending to be, and being up against somebody canny and observant enough to read your intentions or profile you and realise that you're probably not who you say you are.

                                                    The Tell as it currently is has some back and forth, affords more dynamic agency to an observer, and can actually add a bit of tension.
