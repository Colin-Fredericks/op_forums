    Happy that the Moon isn't Evil; it doesn't need to be evil to drive monsters mad

    04-02-2020, 03:09 PM
    In previous Editions, Lunars would gain Limit by looking at the full moon the first time in a night. Because Werewolves.


    In some fiction, monsters loathe the presence of the sun because it is the celestial body of Good, and monsters adore the presence of the moon because it is the celestial body of Evil. After all, does not the moon rule the night?

    No, actually. The moon rises during the day, as well. The moon isn’t dark on nights of the new moon; the moon is completely absent because it was out during the day, barely visible in the sun’s light.

    Furthermore, as the sun is associated with gold, the moon is associated with silver. But it is silver, not gold, that has a historical reputation as the bane of all evils: poisons, diseases, and monsters. [In Exalted, both orichalcum and moonsilver have this potential for "holiness".]

    In Exalted, the moon as a protective entity is realized in that it interferes with demon-summoning. I don't recall what sort of effect the lunar phases has on the Wyld in Third Edition.

    But if you still want the full moon to be a night where some monsters go nuts, I can offer an alternative explanation for why nocturnal monsters are more active and violent on nights of the full moon. An explanation which doesn’t suggest the moon is something evil which empowers monsters.


    The moon, after all, reflects the light of the sun that these creatures hate so much. For nocturnal monsters which live exclusively at night (or at least without the sun), they don’t need the light of the moon to see, and would in fact be most comfortable on nights of the new moon when humans are most blind.

    So the full moon might actually be driving nocturnal monsters crazy because it represents an invasion of their safe darkness by the sun they hate so much. Their prey is given more light to spot them, and they feel the sting of the sun even at night.

    Being driven crazy, they become more active and frantic where otherwise they might be calm and calculated. They become sloppier, more prone to revealing themselves and leaving evidence and witnesses, rather than quietly disappearing carefully targeted victims.


    An acquaintance of mine adds:

    [To add to this,] notions that the moon inspires madness of any sort (i.e. “lunacy”) is mostly because of the astrological/theological notions that the moon exerts a powerful influence on intuition, instinct, and the subconscious; it speaks to the parts of you aren’t usually consciously aware of, and that can just as easily bring inspiration, insight, and bursts of creativity and innovation. In that sense, one could say in more than one way that the moon brings to light all that the sun can’t reach, both in the night and in the mind.

    Heck, one could say that anyone suffering “lunacy” isn’t being driven mad, but just having it driven into the open for all to see, much like nocturnal monsters aren’t being empowered and emboldened by the moon but forced into a frenzy.

      Aren't those explanations largely similar to the 2e canon from the Lunar Manual (henceforth, but not, the Moonual)? The full moon, per the description of the Caste, was noted as a stabilizing force that pulled Creation away from the Wyld so it wouldn't completely dissolve into chaos; Glories and later material leaned heavily on Luna luring in monsters with lower-light phases to hunt and kill them, or as mockery and provocation. 1e could come off as a bit more dickish, since the interpretation could be that Luna pulled on the "ocean of the Wyld" to move it closer, but it was also something that brought predictability to an otherwise unpredictable font of chaos.

      Tengentially, now that I'm looking at them years later, the aspects of Luna from 2e just seem to be "Luna as the Maidens of Destiny". The Bloody Huntress is Martian Luna, the Silver-Horned Watcher is Saturnian Luna (perhaps more in choice of reaping imagery than other concerns), the Two-Faced Bride is Luna as a kind of Aphroditus figure, and hence Venus, the Walker at the Crossroads is Jovian Luna, and the White Navigator is Mercurial Luna.           I idly came up with a metaphysical whosawhatsit a few years back for lunacy and its association with the moon. Nothing too deep, and mainly for my own meta-philosophical musings

          Luna is against the Wyld, as Sol is against the Creatures of Darkness. She even "borrows" His light to do so. But, where the Sun burns away the Darkness, the Moon..... takes it, makes the darkness part of itself. The Moon is associated with darkness through its phases, waxing and waning.

          So, again, Luna is against the Wyld..... but Luna is also of the Wyld. Two sides of the same coin. She antagonizes it, sets Herself against it, but where Sol would burn it away in righteous wrath, She mocks it, uses it against itself. Drives monsters and men mad so they might more easily be made low.

          So, as Luna rises full in the night sky, She reflects the Wyld. Either the Wyld itself, or Her own shifting nature
