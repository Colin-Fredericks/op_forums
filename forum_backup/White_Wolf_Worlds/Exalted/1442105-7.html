            What are the Lintha really, dilluted inheritors of a pre-human legacy like Jadeborn or Dragon Kings, persevering descendants of a lineage of demon-blooded favored by Kimbery since the Time Before, a cesspool of multiple legacies with dreams of grandeur and the pedigree of polluted water?

            Yes

            The Lintha of the Time of Tumult are unlike the Lintha from before Dukantha, who are unlike the Lintha from before Contagion, who are unlike the Lintha of the First Age and who might be looked askance upon by their alien forebears - delve deep enough into their mausoleums of salt & brine preserved carcasses enfolded in seaweed wrappings and one may find finery and forms ever more alien, some like pelagothropes or beastfolk, others with markings of the blood of dragons or elementals, beings with an almost idealized cast closer to the greatest of Jadeborn than any man or woman born from a human womb, but also older and stranger beings, between reptilian or piscine, more akin to homage or mockeries of Mosok & their brethren and older more eldritch besides.

            Because the Lintha are the beloved of the Sea that Went Against the Flame, they ebb and flow, they corrode and mix up with whatever crosses their path, in an endless cycle of acretion and infusion, condemned to tides of repetitive transformation that yet produces utterly different beings.

            The ghosts of the Lintha are more aware of these layers of difference as the ancestors of certain epochs serve as mediators between themselves and layers of older ancestors deeper in the Sea of Shadows than them, who themselves will sometimes comment or hint of even more ancient and alien beings on greater depths that communicate in ways no human-like throat could even vocalize, above or below water, though only a very few of them moved to Bluehaven with the other, "younger" ancestors, their time already past in an era when Ng Ooro was a great land that might as well be a continent.

            But the seas one constant is the tides of change. That which flows may ebb, low tide gives way to high tide. That which is buried may be uncovered when the currents are right. Who's to say what form, old or new, will the Lintha take as the tides of war churn Creation asunder?

                                There's an entire small 'conspiracy' of gods that are reaching down from heaven into Creation, contacting sorcerers, thaumaturges, and god-blooded. What are they doing? What's their plan?

                                What are they trying to do?

                                Well, its all relatively benign. In the Balorian Crusade and the Great Contagion, many gods lost their jobs as their portfolios were wrecked. The god of artificial flight got off easy, since at least someone knows about balloons. But some gods weren't okay. Their entire nations were destroyed, the species they were in charge of were rendered extinct, or the cultural activity they presided over disappeared with the culture as they fell beneath the twin catalysms.

                                And so these gods try to regain their status and their power, or at least some of them by trying to revive, or replace, what they had lost. Thaumaturges use selective breeding and spells to try and convert wastelands back into the verdant forests and fields they once were. Sorcerers are paid in Ambrosia and Quintessence to create entire new species or restore long destroyed records. God-blooded are hired as champions and promoters of various causes to one nifty festival to a particular tasty treat.

                                    Once upon a time between plagues and wars, a person lost its arm. No one alive knows its name, face or gender anymore, only that in a place of magic that will change with the telling, that person found the secrets ways of sorcery and adopted a claw of the wood dragon in its stead. Arm, sorcerous blessing and artifact weapon all in one, the sorcerer or sorceress would delve deeply in its essence, producing many wonders and miracles through evocations drawn from its whorls & grains. So much so that when one day that magician died, the Claw would live on as an artifact on its own, a heirloom and phylactery of sorts, preserving a shade of that sorcerer as a spirit of sorts within its slumbering branches.

                                    Its fate is the source of countless, sometimes contradicting tales.

                                    In one, that Wood Dragon Claw would become the royal relic of a city-state or country the sorcerer founded, guiding - or possessing - its successors across the generations.

                                    According to others, each man or woman to succeed the founder would also learn to master the original spell - and with each ruler's passing, another living Wood Dragon Claw would come to be in the kingdom, until the palace's garden was surrounded by sorcerous shrubs that await the fateful time to awaken and be wielded once again by champions.

                                    On another, the sorcerous artifact would denude itself of all its leaves, that would spread into the four winds in the form of parchments telling of its story and the mysteries of its unique path into a sort of immortality or godhood perhaps. Texts that would spring to life a hundred heretic societies across the Dreaming Sea and beyond.

                                    And then there are those that speak of that arm-sized branch turning into a full tree, a sapling that would grow mighty in worship of its legend through generations and centuries into Moloch, a giant of ironwood, a dormant colossus that will turn into a living, breathing, moving xoanon, a warstrider of jade-hard wood of motives as mysterious as the nature that leads it to take people as riders or offerings from time to time.

                                    Which of these tales are true, if any of them, is hard to say unless one meets any of those esoteric things - and then there are those rare, bold if not mad few that would dare say all of them are, in some form or another. But if so, who can say for sure what purpose would serve such a garden of Wood Dragon Claws and the even stranger ascended colossus of lumber that indirectly cultivated all of them?
