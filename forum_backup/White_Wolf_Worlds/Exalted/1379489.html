    [Ex3] Tales of the Lost Ages - campaign [Actual Play] and reflections

    04-08-2020, 01:16 PM
    After loooong time (almost as long as solars were stuck in their prison) my wife finally agreed to play Exalted after she got burnt years ago (tl;dr we played with friends and the game went into comedy and it was a mess). It's her first serious time with Exalted and my first time really running 3ed. I'm opening this thread to use as a place to note and reflect on how the campaign is going, ask questions and hear your comments.

    Basic Premises:
    1. Due to the inherent complexity of the system, especially charms, we decided to start playing as heroic mortals. After playing the first 2 scenes of the prologue I'm happy with that decision
    2. We have kids so we don't have much time to play. That means we play on average 1.5 - 2.5h at time 2-3 times a week. It should amount to a regular one session per week just broken down into smaller pieces. That means that I want to make every scene count.
    3. The game should progress from heroic mortal to solar at some point. Not sure yet when, but probably at the end of first story/story arc. My plan as a Storyteller is that we're both comfortable with most of the game mechanics before that happens.

    Character:
    Mei (surname missing yet) - a First Age archeologist from a minor Scavenger Lands noble family [created using heroic mortal rules from p.125 in Ex.3 Corebook].

    Attributes:
    Strength 2 Charisma 2 Intelligence 3
    Dexterity 3 Manipulation 3 Wits 3
    Stamina 2 Appearance 3 Perception 3

    Abilities: Athletics 3, Awareness (danger sense) 3, Beaurocracy 1, Dodge 2, Integrity 2, Investigation (deduction) 3, Larceny 1,

    Merits: Ambidextrous, Language 1 (Old Realm), Enhanced Sense (Sight)m Resources 2, Quick Draw 1, Linguistics 2, Lore (First Age) 4, Melee (katana) 3, Occult 2, Presence 2, Resistance 2, Ride 1, Socialize 3, Survival 2

    Willpower: 5
    Essence: 1

    Combat ( Wit = withering, Dec = decisive, DMG = damage, OV = Overwhelming)
    Katana: Wit 9 / Dec 7, DMG 11, OV 2, Lethal, Balanced, Melee
    Whip: Wit 11 / Dec 7, DMG 9, Bashing, Melee, Flexible, Grappling, Reaching
    Join Battle: 6
    Soak/Hardness: 5 (Buff jacket) / 0
    Parry 5 Evasion 3

    Social Influence:
    Resolve 3 Guile 3
    Intimacies: Gathering Knowledge (defining), restore good name of the family (major), love to unknowm places (minor), find Tomb of the Golden King(minor), undefined minor intimacy


    We managed to create the character we're both satisfied with (looking at statistics and concept it's quite obviously a Twilight Caste) and set the overall theme of the campaign. If you haven't noticed it will be archeomancy and the first age artifacts search. Lots of Indiana Jones to come. I'm still thinking about a big exalted story arc, but for now, I'm focusing on fleshing out the heroic mortal part of the game.

    The Story so far
    Story I: In Search of a Golden King Tomb
    Scene 1: A new job
    The game starts in the city of Great Forks where Mei has a house. We meet the character on an idle day where her reading of a book is interrupted by her maid saying that she has an important guest. The maid adds that it's some kind of priest but she doesn't know which god. Mei meets with the priest (with a bodyguard) who explains that he came to hire her to find a tomb and retrieve a special stone from it, which would benefit their temple very much. He presents her with a first age scroll tube with seal already broken that contains a note written during the Shogunate Era speaking about the Tomb of the Golden King. After a brief conversation, setting of dates, etc. Mei decides to accept the job and agrees to meet with the priest tomorrow to discuss the details of the expedition. The priest leaves her with few obols of jade as a first installment of her pay and entrusts her the scroll to study over the night. After some formal goodbyes, the priest leaves and Mei makes sure he is not followed before returning home to study the scroll.


    Scene 2: Burglar?!
    Mei walks into the study room just in time to find the safe where she left the scroll open with a stranger standing in front of it. Without thinking she grabs a nearby sword from the wall and attacks him shouting. A fight ensues where the PC quickly gets massive advantage and goes for a decisive attack with 13:1 initiative. She manages to deal 4L damage and the ninja-burglar runs away without grabbing the scroll. Mei follows him to the rooftop and beyond to the street where she loses the sight of the ninja in the dense, evening crowd. Irritated she comes back to the house screaming at the house guard for not noticing the ninja and firing him on the spot.


    Scene 3: A Long Night
    Mei screams a bit at her servants but eventually sits down to study the scroll and makes notes of the important bits, including the waypoints to the Tomb. As a result of a successful Lore test, the player adds that the Tomb is was an exalted (probably solar). She orders the guard (who didn't react to screaming on him) to guard the now damaged safe and goes to sleep with the scroll with her. The night goes uneventful.


    Storyteller's comments and reflections
    Game Session 1:

    Story and World:
    • OMG I forgot to create the name of the priest, the religion or the god. I just said that it's a popular cult in Great Forks of a God/Spirit in Terrestrial Beaurocracy, mostly worshipped by local middle-class people. Halp?
    • Portraying the Creation is more complicated then I thought it would be. I'm doing it as a generic mix of eastern fantasy based on China and SE Asia but I'm not sure if that's the right direction. Is that normal?
    • I feel like the GReat Forks is more like one of the big religious cities in India (Kolkata or Varanasi) then Chinese so the mix of how I imagine the city starts to be really crazy. Does anyone have any good advice on that?
    Game mechanics:
    • The first attempt at combat was pretty nice. We absolutely need to add stunts to the rules list next time.
    • Where are chase rules? Am I just blind or there are no chase rules in EX3?
    • The fact-adding through Lore checks is great. I have a feeling we will be using it often.
    • We managed to learn(-ish) simple tests, contested tests, and basic combat mechanics. So far so good, the idea of withering and decisive attacks is not very intuitive for my player but I guess that just needs some time.
    General:
    • I think I'm going to push this story arc into a race between the player and BBEG and a young dragon-blooded should fit nicely to this role. I'm just in two minds should he be backed by the Realm or by the Immaculate order (I know it can be both but one needs to be dominating).
    • My player is new to Exalted and I haven't played for a while as well. I'm going to prepare tons of short (and sometimes probably longer) materials for her in-character journal (like in computer RPGs) to help her keep the whole game lore and be able to return to the stuff she needs. Are there any good tools for that?
    More to come soon (tm).

                  Game Session 2 AKA the Ultimate Disaster


                  The Story so Far
                  Scene 4: Visit to the Temple
                  In the morning Mei sends an errands boy to announce her coming to the temple soon. She also sends her assistant to book the trip east on the river and spread the rumors that she's going to visit family home for a funeral. She then goes to visit the priest of the Gold-Paved Road Cult who visited her the other day. The conversation in the temple is rather short and sweet. Mei accepts the job (taking Minor Intimacy 'Find the Tomb of Golden King' without any rolls) and has a brief conversation about who may want to thwart the efforts. The priest kindly points out to the Realm saying that it's either the Immaculate Order or The All-Seeing-Eye. After the conversation, Mei returns home only to find two Immaculate Monks on her courtyard. Instead of confronting them for what are they doing, she decides to leave it as it is and withdraws unnoticed and goes for lunch.


                  Downtime 1: Preparing for the travel. Not much to mention here.

                  Scene 5: In the Port
                  Mei and her assistant get ready to embark on the ship that will carry them east on the evening of the same day when an Earth Aspect Dragon-Blooded interrupts. He is dressed in a white jade lamel armour and wields Titan Fists Goremaul (cf. Arms of the Chosen) and rather imposing. The says 'I know that you met the Gold-Paved Road Priest. We can do it nicely or painfully - the choice is yours. Give me what he gave you and no one will get hurt.' and this is where things got off-tangent game-wise


                  Storyteller's Comments and Reflections
                  I'm not sure if it was a good idea to play yesterday, to begin with. I was not in a great mood, highly irritated by the kids before going to beds, and my wife was clearly off as well. She played doing game to avoid any plot and game lore - immaculate monks? I go the other direction. Helpful priest, who wants to do an info dump? I ask him nothing. The cherry on top was her reaction to the appearance of the DB - 'I don't know. I can't do anything. I will not tell you what I do cause I can't do anything'. It ended with a quite intense argument and some personal blaming which is never a great way to finish the day. In retrospect, I have no clue what I could have done better, except of keeping my cool - I've been gradually dropping snippets of game lore to my wife, we had some casual conversations about the game setting, I even allowed for a Lore roll when she declared an (IMO) cocky and stupid declaration just after DB appeared to remind her some lore and allow her to change it in time. Still, I got accused of leaving her with no choice, not allowing her to do anything and expecting god knows what from her. I'm giving some time for the emotions to cool down before trying to resolve this issue.

                  A legit setting question: What is a DB status in the setting? I get that dynasts are princes and princesses in the Realm but what about other places. When we have a Dynast in say Great Forks, like in my game. What can such DB reasonably expect from a normal mortal? from an unordinary mortal (say local noble)? I do have some problems balancing the social impact and expectations when DBs and mortals interact. Any clues and ideas?

                      Dragon-Blooded are basically foreign nobility.

                      What would you do for Prince William? What would your city's governor do?

                      Technically, a Realm Dragon-Blooded has very little authority in Great Forks. They might be seen as a celebrity, or an enlightened bohivista, or a folk hero -- a living legend straight out of a story book.

                      Now, to address your problem.

                      Thinking you can't do anything and giving up is exactly how 99.99% of Creation's mortals would react when put in that situation. 0.009% of people would stand up to the Dragon-Blooded. 0.0001% of people will succeed when they stand up to the Dragon-Blooded.

                      This isn't a problem with your scenario. Your scenario is fine. It's a problem with player expectations.

                      Mei is the hero. She's going to win. The people who become Solars win by being audacious and taking on impossible odds.

                      Katniss Evergreen isn't a divine superhero blessed by the gods. Countless Tributes reaped from her districts went before her and died. But we don't hear their stories. We hear the story of the person who wins against all the odds.
