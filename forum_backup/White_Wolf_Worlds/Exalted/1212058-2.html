    re: Zenith Smite Solars
    I didn't mean the Zenith anima ability - but you basically hit it with the "trope codifier".

    The Zenith thought all Solars adhered to the "lawful, good, golden, nice" trope - therefore he was upset at the non-lawful, non-good, non-golden, non-nine characters existed. He thought they were sabotaging the game - so in a fit of Limit Break, he bashed a circle-mates brains out.

    As you note, D&D Races & Classes are trope codifiers - so a zealous Paladin, a Tiefling Warlock and cuthroat Rogue might dislike each other IC, but OOC know what to accept (and give each other proper berth to avoid triggering each other).


    re: Species/Aliens vs Races vs Culture

    A huge issue seems to be the terminology.

    A species is what I think of sci-fi aliens and Exalted extinct "races" - biologically unrelated to one another. This is why "aliens with funny foreheads" fall flat - it breaks the immersion when most of us think alien-life will be utterly inhuman (which is why later sci-fi came up with the idea of progenitor-races "seeding intelligent life", thus explaining us all as modified-animals)

    A culture is the pure fluff behavioral stuff. There is no "American military race", but there is "American military culture". There is "gun culture", "sub-cultures", etc. The cities of New York, Los Angeles and Miami all have different cultures.

    A race is both. It's not blood alone, it's not culture alone.

    And remember that the books used the Wyld to simulate evolution (ie, totem spirits of endangered animal species braving the Wyld hoping that their progeny might acquire a mutation or two that will allow them to adapt and survive).



    I see a lot of.... American unease with "race" in these posts - and I understand the unease if your conception of race is reduced to "white, black", etc. But I disagree - and hope the convo remains healthy.

    Your skin color is not your race.

    It's impossible to "embrace diversity" by denying we are diverse.

    Turks don't confuse themselves with Armenians. The Japanese don't confuse themselves with Koreans or Chinese (and even Japanese-Americans are "Gaijin"). I can't imagine a Navajo, a Seminole and a Sioux think they're all "the same".

    In Spain, Basques don't confuse themselves with Catalans, nor Madrileños with Gallegos, nor Asturians with Canarians. (and these aren't just provincial names like "New Yorker" or "Californian" - there are physical and social traits associated with each). I have a friend whose family is Canarian - and I could tell by the shape of his face. My ethnonym (Gallego) is used as a slur by Madrileños and yet we take that as a point of pride (and would view their ethnonym as the insult).

    Several of my friends took DNA tests (and the Spanish percentages were never "generic Spanish" nor "generic European" nor "generic White" - you can see the region). Same applies for our Latin American roots (you can't confuse a Puerto Rican, Mexican, Peruvian or Columbian "native" traces - they're distinct). We can see the differences even if Americans just see "brown". I certainly don't need to worry about my gaming-group reducing the planet to 4-5 color-coded "races" (we're all migrants of mixed heritage).

    Originally posted by Epee102 View Post
    But if I were porting it over Tameriel,I'd drop most of the racial bonuses.     You say "speech and debate classes" because that implies that these are cultural differences. They're not.

    (In ESO) Bretons don't have mandatory "magic classes" - they're part elven. In Exalted terms, they're part God-Blooded. It doesn't matter if you want to be a farmer, a wizard, a bard or a warrior - these things have nothing to do with your heritage.

    (In RL) the Bajau Laut (Philipines, Malaysia, Indonesia) were part of articles that came out a week ago - confirming they are genetically adapted to the water (50% larger spleens vs their non-aquatic neighbors). The Moken (West Coast of Thailand) have swimmers who can see twice as far and stay twice as long underwater. The are biologically and culturally aquatic. This is awesome. This is a point of pride. In Exalted & Real Life terms, they have a mutation and no amount of swim classes will enlarge your spleen to let you free dive 200ft like they do.

    (In ESO) Nords don't have "nature survival & combat classes". Their pre-history mentions Atmora freezing over (so they either had a mutation to thrive in the cold - or died). Then they had to fight the Falmer to live in Skyrim (so they either had another mutation that gave a bonus to combat - or died). "Resist Frost" and "Battle Frenzy" aren't cultural artifacts - they're natural selection killing off whoever doesn't breed true. Nature & history don't care if you want to be a bard or a farmer - you are descended from frost-warriors - so you're a frost-warrior-turned-bard or a frost-warrior-turned-farmer, etc.

    (In RL) the Inuit have mutations (that 100% of their population has) that only 15% of Han Chinese and 2% of Europeans have - which help them thrive off their raw high-meat, high-fat diets. Conversely, the Havasupai (in Arizona) grew sick from European foods. Denying biological differences resulted in suffering - as the Havasupai lost teeth, became diabetic and a half-dozen other diseases.

    The Bantu & Benin (sickle-cell anemia to resist Malaria); the Kalenjin & other Nilotic peoples are runners (people have copied their diets, altitude & other practices - but you can't copy biomechanics); even Europeans (digesting milk & alcohol - and 1% being HIV immune due to plagues).

    All of these are awesome - and none of them "cultural".

    I understand American concerns (I think) -     but none of these differences claim "superiority". Twenty different LATAM countries can see the differences in one another (and have rivalries) without anyone claiming to be a master race.

    In Spain, the same applies to regions - the Catalans are industrious/dour economical/stingy; the Gallegos are blunt/curt dumb/honest brutes/strong (Orcs!); the Madrileños think they brought "civilization" unto everyone else (snooty/high-class, manipulative/leaders); etc - everyone likes to pretend they're better, but it's on part with a Dwarf, an Orc and a High Elf forming a group (they're still a group - Galician-Orcs aren't trying to wage genocide on High-Elven-Madrileños, nor are Catalan-Dwarves trying to replace Basque-Druids).

    (In ESO) Imperials I'd liken to Roman or Latin people (their obvious inspiration). The Romans were great at assimilating "European barbarians" and the Spanish strove to emulate the model (intermixing and creating a dozen syncretic cultures).

    I never took "speech classes" to learn the differences - I'm not especially social - but I'd slap myself if I ever committed some of the faux pas I've seen Anglo-American tourists commit. You grow up surrounded by different peoples and need to learn the nuances. Meeting "Middle Easterners" was new for me in College, but they have a lot of similarities (even our mother tongues share links in syntax and vocabulary).

    And English-speakers find words offensive - Spanish-speakers listen to tone. You might not understand Japanese or Arabic words but you can always pick up tone.

    It does not stretch my imagination that a race (the Imperials) living in a central-location surrounded by multiple races will naturally have more nuance for cultural differences - that the more isolated or war-like Nords, Reguards and Bretons don't. Imperials aren't "silver tongued" because they took classes - they're "silver tongued" because everyone else talks like an idiot with foreigners. The Altmer don't seem to notice their eugenicism terrifies others - the Bosmer come across like cannibals - the Dunmer religion shocks tourists - Redguards & Nords are too busy picking fights to learn social charms. The Imperial gift is being less assholes than the isolated bumpkins that surround them.


    re: Race as Trope Markers

    I really like the 3E Lintha clarification you folks point to - and by extension, all this mulling on Elder Scrolls races. I'm liking this "nationalistic racism" and "propaganda" angle a lot. Less mechanical difference (except maybe a few mutations). No need for "Orcs, Dwarves, Elves" with unique origin stories - when "humans adapted to different environments" are fitting (and they don't need to live in the far-flung corners of the Bordermarches for this benefit).

    The Snake People in An-Teng are a race. No one thinks of them as "Beastmen" - they're seen a distinct local race.

    The Lintha are a race. No one thinks about magic-mechanics of being altered-humans or not. A couple aquatic mutations could suffice for humans to make up completely fictitious histories for this "magical" race.

    The Sijanese? Are a race. Without making them all ghost-blooded, it'd suffice to give 1-2 mutations or merits that might be shadowland related (representing a lifetime among the dead that no mere tourist or migrant will acquire overnight).

    The people of Great Forks? Are a race. Without making them all god-blooded, it'd suffice to give 1-2 mutations or merits in the vein of Bretons (from Elder Scrolls).

    They're all human.

    But one could easily "build racial tropes" (the way Orcs, Dwarves, Elves, etc have them) representing stereotypes (that have grains of truth in them).
