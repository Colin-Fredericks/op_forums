                                Well, to discuss the Bureaucracy side of the question here...

                                One of my characters was a 'typical' filthy-rich (Resources 5) Bureacracy-Supernal Eclipse, and he was more or less the linchpin of the group. Owned his own island in the West, and a lot of plots revolved around his island and his fleet. (He had both Merchant and non-Merchant Bureaucracy Charms.)

                                Anyway, from a plot perspective, Bureaucracy is not generally an in-your-face Ability, and shares an 'indirect approach' with Socialize and other Abilities. Bureaucracy is not the brute force of Presence or even Performance, but it is all about setting things up socially--particularly on a large scale--so that the menu of options available to you is more favorable than the menu of options available to your opponent. Someone trying to bribe someone else? Offer a larger bribe. Can't? Wreak havoc with the briber's bank accounts so that he cannot make good on the bribe.

                                One recent (non-Solar) example is the political infighting among the Dynasty between Sesus and Tepet to make the war against the Bull as excruciating as possible. In general, the larger your undertaking in Creation, the more important Bureacracy (and its Charms) can be. If you plan on taking on the Dynasty, fomenting ongoing paralysis in their bureaucracy will slow any possible response to your actions.

                                A good 'character concept' for a traveling Bureaucrat besides troubleshooter--which is still tied to a single organization, generally--is an Ambassador. You'd probably want Socialize and Linguistics as well, but Bureaucracy is crucial for managing things. Oh, I'm in treaty negotiations and there's a third party also angling for the same treaty? Sorry if your messages all go awry (Indolent Official Charm) while mine have no problem (Speed the Wheels). The speed advantage involved with such things cannot be understated, and if you want to RP the negotiations rather than the messages going back and forth, it's unlikely that there will be Essence users you're using as couriers.

                                I think a good TV example of a Solar Eclipse that uses Bureaucracy well is Col. Hogan from Hogan's Heroes (I am currently rewatching the show). Sure, he's a fast-talking action con artist, but he can be seen as having both positive Bureaucracy (just check out the extensiveness of his underground system and how he processes prisoners 'like clockwork') and negative Bureaucracy (Stalag 13 never runs well) aspects.

                                                Originally posted by Elfive View Post
                                                Honestly this was one of the useful things about being able to buy a speciality three times in 2e. You could have a character that was really good at one aspect of an ability but only mediocre at anything else.

                                                Why did they change that, anyway?
                                                Well, the main reason was that optimized characters always went Attribute 5 + Ability 5 + Specialty 3. Because you could only have three Specialties, every character had only one or maybe two closely related specialties - swords and fighting with two weapons, for example. This base of 13 dice contributed to number bloat and so on.

                                                ​If you want to bring back the old stacking specialties without breaking the game in half, you need just a few things. First, every Specialty beyond the first needs to be more expensive in a manner similar to how normal Ability dots work. I'd recommend trying to price it so that it's about half the cost of an Ability dot. Second, the maximum number of dice you can get from Ability + Specialties is 6. Put those two house rules in place and you should be able to have eight Specialties in one Ability without any problem.
