        Originally posted by Shaddar View Post
        A working for each effect.
        That's more reasonable, but I'd say that some of these may not fit as Terrestrial Workings.

        Originally posted by Shaddar View Post
        What different types of immortalities there are?
        Lots of kinds, unsurprisingly. The word gets thrown around often in fiction and myth.

        For instance, the Aesir of Norse mythology would not age as long as they ate the golden apples and would still die if they were killed.

        The Primordials and Titans of Greek mythology were ageless and unkillable, but could still become trapped or sustain injuries in a reasonably permanent fashion, so it'd really suck for one if he or she got chopped to pieces or imprisoned.

        Other characters have been called immortal, but are simply really long-lived or regenerate perfectly from injury, or both (Wolverine from Marvel comics). Some simply will not die until some condition is met (Juggernaut from Exalted is a fine example, but so too is Dorian Gray!). Some can die or be killed after a fashion, but have it not be permanent for them (Jesus, Mario, or the Doctor from Doctor Who). Others can go dormant indefinitely as long as they're not destroyed (Vampires from the World of Darkness). Some just have a really long lifespan, such that a human can be born and die without ever seeing them age (Dragon-Blooded, most other Exalts, those who eat the Peaches of Immortality in heaven's orchards in The Journey to the West).

        Basically, something's "immortal" if it doesn't die in the same fashion we do.

        You can't use sorcery to give yourself "perfect" immortality, which is to say, no matter how good your magic is, you won't last forever.
