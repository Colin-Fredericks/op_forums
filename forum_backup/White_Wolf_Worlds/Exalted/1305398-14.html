    Nice fight!

    I have to say I don't fully agree with the choices tactically though. Against an opponent who has more than 3 initiative launching a decisive attack is almost always a god awful idea. Giving your opponent two turns in a row when you're at 3 initiative and they already have close to a dangerous amount is very bad business. If you're sitting at short range from an enemy that's got 7i just wither them and move back, then they can't get to you next turn no matter what, and that gives you an information advantage.

    See, when somebody moves from medium to short with you and rushes, you will know at the end of that turn if they'll be able to get to you next turn. If the rush fails you know you're safe, you can attack and move back freely, if it succeeds, you know that nothing you do can keep you from getting caught next turn, so you can take a full defense or something.

    Air Dragon is an extremely high finesse style, it might be the most high finesse style in the game even, but it actually does work.


    Also I'm a bit iffy on having 5 Air Dragon with a specialty and 4 Dodge with no specialty. At that point your Evasion isn't even as good as your Parry, and your form is just playing catchup with your stats. And for anyone who's reading this thinking "Well you can't just have every ability and attribute at 5!" of course you can't. You can't say "What are you going to do against a master poisoner?" and get a response of "Well I'll just have max (Stamina + Resistance) and full poison charms!" That's silly, you could sit there all day coming up with enemies and new charms and abilities that you'd need to counter them. You'd need every Athletics, Awareness and Resistance charm as just a start.

    What you can say though is "What does my combat style do best?" For example Fire Melee loves to clash, if you can give yourself some kind of clash bonus, you can expect you'll be using it. Silver Voiced Nightingale has Performance based attack supplements, adds Performance to damage, and adds Performance to Evasion. Which is double important because it means you really want Performance and Dodge, and that isn't just pulling stats that you need out of nowhere. "What does a SVN stylist use against a master poisoner?" dodge "What about a deadly mobile archer?" dodge "What about a master of melee?" dodge.

    What you can't say though, is that every enemy will have good mobility pools and charms. Some will, and those guys can be a pretty big problem, but the ones who don't? An Air Immaculate is their worst nightmare. That won't always be the case, certainly, but hopefully that's why you didn't piss off everyone else at your table to the point where they want to leave your character for dead.


    And again, again, I don't think Air Dragon is some crazy OP style and is way better than Earth Dragon. I think Earth Dragon probably IS better in most situations. Air Dragon requires a lot more investment into other abilities, Dodge, yes, but also Athletics and Stealth. Of course if you already wanted to take Stealth and stuff then it's no big deal, if you didn't it's a big extra cost. Earth Dragon doesn't give a fuck, it just needs Earth Dragon, it doesn't even needs Resistance, it just fits thematically. It doesn't require any finesse either, just sit there with a ton of soak and hit things. Still don't decisive anyone who's got more than 3i, if you can help it, but it's very straightforward.

    It is a totally viable combat option though, and even more importantly it's fun. Avenging Wind Strike, Tornado Offense Technique. Fun as hell, man.

        As an aside separate from the whole Air Dragon thing, since this is an arena thread and this line showed up in the mission statement:

        Originally posted by DrLoveMonkey View Post
        LLLLLET'S GET READY TO RUMBLEEEEE!!!
        What it is:
        • A place to gain some insight into how Exalted fights might go in particular situations

        I think it might be a good idea to talk about the whole attacking people who haven't been crashed yet a bit more.

        The way exalted combat works, how it was designed to work, is that people start the fight, battle for initiative, and then end it with one decisive blow. That's why they're called decisive attacks.

        Launching decisive attacks are incredibly dangerous, for lots of reasons. One is that they're just lower accuracy attacks with any weapon other than a mundane heavy, or an Archery weapon in close range. So when you launch them at an enemy with equal skill, and if they have motes equal mote investment, your chances of hitting are 50/50, and that's not great. Not only that but the consequences for missing are punishing, in most cases losing 3i for your trouble.

        It's worse than that though, because if you hit it makes you really vulnerable to anyone else. If there is any enemy who isn't crashed, or at 1i-2i they're going to act after you in the round, and then when the round resets they're going to act before you. If there are multiple enemies that haven't gone yet, they all get to act twice. That's really bad, because you're only 3 damage away from being crashed, which would be bad enough if it didn't give your opponent 5 extra initiative to kill you with, which they can now use immediately in a decisive attack that gets an onslaught bonus with themselves.

        Sometimes you do have to worry about crashed opponents though. If they're crashed, but only by a little, like at -1, then when you attack they could very easily crash you, getting up to initiative 8 at bare minimum, and possibly even more. That's really not good because then they get to go again, and if they're smart they're actually just going to wither you again. The reason it's dumb to just decisive right off of 8, unless you're very sure you can kill your opponent, is Initiative Shift.

        Initiative shift can only be triggered while you're in crash, and crash the person who crashed you. It refreshes your turn, immediately, and resets all your movement, and gives you a bunch of initiative on top of whatever you rolled. At the very least you reset to base and then roll your Jon Battle pool, adding it to base, and at the most you exploded an enemy and jumped up to like 10 initiative, and then roll your Join Battle and add it anyway. Then you get to act again because your turn reset. Then the round resets and you act again for the third time in a row.

        So if there is somebody on the field that you've crashed, you'd better make sure that if you decisive them that they'll either be dead, or at least out of crash by the time their turn comes along. Battlefield awareness, in this way, is very very important. If you think you probably have enough initiative to kill somebody that you crashed, don't attack. Only do it when you're sure you have enough. Like three times as many dice as they have levels left.

        OR there's another thing you can do, which is at the very least wait until your enemy is about to reset. If they've been crashed for two rounds already, then on the beginning of their next turn they reset to base. Since it's at the beginning of their turn they don't get to act before they reset, and thus can't benefit from initiative shift. That's another tactic.


        Thankfully, in 95% of fights that I've seen in Exalted, the players aren't alone. So here's another thing you can do. If you want to decisive and there's somebody around with more initiative than your reset, make sure you have a friend with lots of initiative still in the fight. And when I say lots I mean like, 18 or so. Enough that when the guy crashes you jumps up 10 initiative, he still won't be going first next round, that way your friend can wither him down and hopefully bring him out of deadly range.

        Incidentally, I've seen this kind of thing happen to a lot of antagonists in my games. They roll an amazing join battle, the players progressively whittle them down, not in one shot, but each one attacking and bringing him lower and lower in the turn order, bringing them under other PCs who then proceed to attack and bring them lower than OTHER PCs, until at last the bad guy is at the bottom. Then the round resets and everyone acts again, attacking over and over again.


        So anyway, hopefully that helps some people out there with strategy, and might show Storytellers out there why it can be a good idea to have lesser foes in with your big bad guy. It might seem like a bad idea because it feels like they'll just be initiative batteries for the players, but they can be dangerous. If a player crashes one for some cheap initiative, and then decisive attacks the big bad, or a different minion without realizing that the minion they crashed is 3 damage away from getting an insane combo. If it's a damn Blood Ape or something, and you're a cruel ST with no regard for your players, that could easily be 6 attacks in a row.

                    The combat:


                    Both of them toss 4m on their JB, going for dice over successes in the hopes of making a big bank with immediate killing potential.

                    Air Dragon: 10S for 13i
                    Earth Dragon: 4S for 7i

                    Air Dragon tosses a decisive chakram at Earth with a 5m excellency under the justification that it doesn't have to kill, just wound badly enough to ruin her chances of ever catching up: 10d base +2 stunt +5 excellency for 17 dice. Earth Dragon blows 4m 1WP on bringing her Parry up to 8.

                    Air Dragon rolls 10 successes. 13 damage dice turn into 8 damage; Earth Dragon is on her -4 health level. Air Dragon moves out to medium range.

                    Earth Dragon moves into short range and attempts a rush, blowing 3m on her anima power to ignore a point of wound penalty, another 4m on the excellency, and a WP for 4 dice and 3 AS. Air Dragon opposes with a full excellency, rolling 11 dice with 2 AS. Earth Dragon has 4 successes, Air Dragon gathers 10. Rush fails, but the anima power allows Earth Dragon to enter Earth Aura.

                    DING DONG NEXT TURN, regen 5.

                    Turn 2
                    Earth Dragon at 7i, Air Dragon at 3i.

                    ...I shouldn't have wasted those 4 motes - it's Earth Dragon's turn and she can just reflexively move into close range. She goes all-out: 5m excellency + 3m Force of the Mountain +2m Stillness-of-Stone Atemi +1WP on an elbow strike. She is now at bonfire, rolling 17 dice +1 AS. Her opponent spends 4m on a full excellency and a WP, bringing her Evasion up to 9.

                    Earth Dragon hits with 13 successes - base damage 12 +5 FoM +4 threshold is 21 raw damage, -8 is 13 post-soak. She rolls 9 successes, crashing the Air Immaculate, gaining 15i and spending 2 of it on raising her foe's mobility penalty by 2.

                    Air Dragon disengages with Wind Dragon's Speed and 2 auto-successes, rolling 9 dice. Earth Dragon tosses another 7m 1WP into opposing her, secure in the knowledge that if she manages to keep her around, she can reliably execute her next turn. Air Dragon rolls 4 successes plus 2 for a total of 6; Earth Dragon's dice explode into multiple 10s, yielding 6 successes total plus her 3 AS, bringing her up to 9. Air Dragon fails to disengage, but still has an attack to make.

                    She needs her motes for dodging and disengaging, so she will only spend 1m on her attack, rolling 18 dice against Earth Dragon's Parry of 1. 12 successes, 11 goes into damage, bringing her up to 24. Earth Dragon has 17 soak at this point; she spends 9 of her remaining 10m on activating Earth Dragon Form and bringing it up to 22. Air Dragon is reduced to Overwhelming; 3 dice yield a single success.

                    DING DONG NEXT TURN, regen 5

                    Turn 3
                    Earth Dragon at 19i, Air Dragon at -4 and 1 turn into her crash.

                    Earth Dragon attempts a smash with FoM +2 excellency for 11d +1 WP. Air Dragon defends with 4m 1wp, bringing her Evasion up to 9. Earth Dragon rolls exactly 9 successes on the attack, bringing him up to 10; 17 dice of damage roll 11 successes with the double 10s and the rerolls. Air Dragon is dead.


                    ...Yyyyeah this is about what I expected. Admittedly, Earth Dragon got hideously lucky more than a couple times there, but then again, so did Air with her alpha strike.
                    Air Dragon would probably have takent hat one if they didn't try that decisive attack on turn one. Going below the Earth Dragon in Initiative allowed them to take two consecutive actions, thus allowing them to close the gap without any roll (without even a flurry, really). Which is the one thing you cannot let a heavy hitting style do as Air Dragon.

                    Anyway the issue with Air Dragon I think is the following:

                    - While its Charms are powerful, it encourages a fighting style that is neither fun to fight against nor to fight with yourself (this is the main point)

                    - Due to all its best Charms being about keeping your distance (along with some utility like Dodge penalty negation and Stealth boosts) it doesn't go very well with any other style. Even leaving aside its incompability weapon wise (unarmed is a thing, especially when it comes to decisive attacks), there just isn't very much in there that any traditional close combat martial art needs. Closest is probably Ebon Shadow Style. This is a slight issue in so far as it means that Air Aspects who want to go for a Martial Arts Master build have basically a huge Charm tax on getting that tasty option of ignoring the Terrestrial keyword without any gain.

                    -It doesn't feel like a Martial Art. Most of its stuff are elementally themed tricks that enable movement, dodging, stealth etc. Later on you have some genuine elemental martial attacks, but those are not available to most characters and it feels like it lacks some sort of signature workhorse Charm that gives the style identity. I guess Breath Seizing Method is that workhorse, but it is almost comically bad (even after they buffed it, at that).

                    Fire Dragon on the other hand I view less critical. It's a win more tree, but Exalts do a lot of winning, so often times it won't feel bad to play with it, even though nobody'd pick it in a PvP league or whatever. (Also slight note: When you use Glory Overwhelming Flash Fire Technique suddenly becomes a rather terrifying Charm if you're willing to take the fever)

                                                            Tried my own build idea (Laughing Monster Lunar vs a couple of Immaculates), turns out that when you need to take "-1 Defense and give 1i to your opponent every round" or else find a way around an opponent who can easily outmaneuver you while he deals with your friend 1v1, all while making sure NEVER to end up next to said friend AND taking negative Intimacies against him, it ends up shredding just about any duo, not just Immaculates, though they get hit extra hard if they don't have an Air Dragon Stylist around (which can... poke at you and will more than likely be the one targetted first, so even then). Pretty nasty build but it takes investment to pull off. Mainly, you need six Laughing Monster Charms, at least 2-3 Dexterity Defense Charms, preferably 1-3 offensive Dexterity Charms though I didn't use those in this test (I just noticed they would have helped the Lunar), Perception Join Battle boosters, and a bit of good luck.

                                                            It's basically an alpha strike build but whose initiative gets subtracted by 2 then divided by two (but doubles 10s and adds +Intimacy dice) and rolled against two peeps, who then disrupt each other a ton and feed you initiative for free, and then when one of them attacks you you just wombo-combo Agile Beast Defense, Fleet Gazelle and Furiously Stalling Destiny along with a stunt (7m 1wp total) to make them miss pretty easily, then use Friendship-Dissolving Poison (you won't crash yourself since you gained 1i from Agile Beast) to redirect the attack against the other opponent and make them hate each other and even fight each other. Unitary Forge Being makes that even easier to accomplish if you can pull it off later on, but they'd need a bunch of Ox Bodies to survive that many Decisive attacks anyway.
