      Significant improvements in 3e, from my perspective [incomplete list, just things that pop into my head]:

      Internal scaling for Solar charms. Solars actually feel like they have room in all abilities, but especially noncombat ones, between dabbler and master [Stealth in 2e core book was four charms. FOUR. Dodge was about as long.] Also, they don't immediately [or in a lot of/most cases, ever] just jump to "you never need to roll again unless you're opposing another Solaroid", unlike 2e.

      Combat. Combat in 2e was very much rocket tag. Every single attack was targetting your health levels, which in theory makes for gripping exciting combat but in practice means the only winning strategy [barring regeneration Lunars or the like] was just to never get hit, which meant combat was a long series of ineffectual swats depleting an invisible mote counter until one side ran out of motes and abruptly died from the next attack. 3e's split between initiative and health level attacks, withering and decisive damage, allows for far better combats that feel like you're making ground, like you can actually tell the swing of the battle beyond your own mote count. Also, the former perfect defenses are properly priced from the beginning, so they're there primarily as a "oh shit" button and a "I don't die to chump-slaying death beams" instead of "spam cheaper perfect, win". Overall 3e combat is more engaging and doesn't have the perpetual feel of "I could deploy my ultimate combo, BUT it'd probably be perfected and leave me at a disadvantage, so I guess it's back to jabbing".

      Martial Arts: Martial Arts in 2e felt like kind of a waste; if you were a Solar pursuing them, outside of Solar Hero Style and its' infinite Solar-tiered expansions [which would become Brawl charms], they were [barring standout combos or individual charms that were obscenely overpowered compared to the rest] inferior to what you could get for being a Solar. So they felt neat, but hamstrung. Mastery keyword means that martial arts can be used by Solars to stand shoulder to shoulder with native charms [in their narrower focused areas] while not making them a One True Build for everyone else. Which leads to...

      Non-Solar optimism: While we don't know too much about non-Solars, we can make some inferences, chief among them the relative disparity between basic Martial Arts charms, Mastery, and Terrestrial. And in my estimation, the disparity between Mastery and Terrestrial is about what I'd have expected to exist between Solar and Celestial in 2e; if martial arts are any indication at all, non-Solars will be a more significant player mechanically, making a confrontation with an experienced Lunar or Sidereal much less of a foregone conclusion and pointless waste of time to play out, and making being the non-Solar in a group far less of a "Scrappy Doo" position.

      Social system: Oh my god it actually works beyond "OBEY ME *hammers until out of willpower, forces mind control*", and is cool and encourages actual interaction and figuring out of hooks to ingratiate yourself with the NPC. It feels better, it's more fun in play, and mostly pleasing in a thematic rather than utilitarian sense, the "scene-long perfect" of joining battle is gone.

              I am by no means the best candidate to tell you what to play. Only you can decide that really. I have however been playing Exalted since 1st edition. My thoughts are as follows:

              1E: Was a good game all around. It's lore/narrative are really what drew me to Exalted all those years ago. It had it's fair share of mechanical issues as well (Lunar's were broke even then). I feel it's problems though were known well enough that simple house rules could keep the game going. However, that's just my opinion and I'm sure many others disagreed. Regardless, good games almost always develop enough cracks that a 2.0 reboot is required. So I wasn't upset when 2E was announced as a project.

              2E: Well it was a bit rocky even at the start. Even in the beginning the "paranoia combat" became a known issue. What really killed it though was that each supplement added more mechanics that were either broken to begin with, or were broken by later supplements. A lot of this was laid at the feet of different writers each having conflicting points of view, and very little play test and oversight of what was being sent to the press. The end result being a game that was very unforgiving and punishing to new players, and required a lot of ST mediation to keep from breaking even with experienced players. I believe the majority of us weren't surprised or terribly upset with the announcement of 3E.

              3E: So far, I'm enjoying 3E a lot. I really like the new combat system, as well as all the sorcery/artifact widgets. The few lore changes have been quite inspiring as well. It has some issues to work out, but nothing I've seen so far is so broken that it's un-fixable. Even the Craft system can be greatly improved with a few tweaks as several people have displayed in other posts. So I'm going to stay positive about the few wrinkles we've found. Who knows, maybe the next few supplements will add to the existing rules and iron out those pesky wrinkles we've identified!

              TLDR:

              1E: great 1st gen game with some well documented cracks
              2E: so bloated and broke that entire books could be dedicated to errata and not fix it
              3E: new and refreshing, but Exalted is a huge setting and future products will either make or break this edition

                                                          Something that worked for my group, and quite a few others from what I've read, is starting with a mortals game. Have your players roll up heroic mortals and play through the game for several sessions doing combat, social influence, battle groups, crafting (if anyone wants to), sorcery, etc. without any charms or mote pools.

                                                          If everyone has a solid understanding of the game, it's a lot easier to later incorporate charms, because all they do is break the rules of one of those systems in their own individual way. They'll have a much better sense of what it means to, say, "double post-soak damage dice" if they've already done some combat. I think it's possible to take in all of the rules, from the base systems through all of the charms, just by reading them, but it's probably a lot easier and smoother to build off of a foundation.
