    Gonna go ahead and spoiler my reply to Sorcerous Overlord , though I hope I'm not boring the rest of y'all to tears...
    Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post
    There's no mechanical incentive for either of these abilities…
    Sure there is. Unexpected attacks penalize the enemy’s defense by 2; likewise, a successful Disarm prevents the defender from being able to attack with their weapon, absent some means to retrieve it.

    Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post
    …and there's nothing stopping an Ebon Shadower from disarming you and giving you a stern talking to.
    His lack of Presence dots might make his argument less compelling.

    Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post
    Yes, you absolutely can have them act in different ways, but that's making a personal choice about how they act, it's not different martial art.
    I disagree; we have different Martial Arts because each is intended to have a central theme that it follows, even before Charms.

    Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post
    I'm really curious bout why you skipped Melee, Archery, and Brawl only to state the absolute obvious.
    Mostly because if I included the whole list, it would add to what was already a lengthy post.

    Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post
    But going back to your example about Crane and ES, a Greek Hoplite throwing a spear from the shield wall is different from the Ninja throwing a kunai and Viking tossing a hatchet, but no one is suggestion[sic] that really there's 3 different kinds of Thrown, even though mechanically there's only one.
    I’d suggest it. I argued for it years back. A lack of discrete widgets should not render your Ability dots flavorless. If your character learned Medicine dots from Skullstone's necrosurgery college, it should be a different experience than learning them via the drug operations in Great Forks. You should be excellent at amputating limbs, versus being amazing at treating addiction.

    Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post
    Are those different characters, philosophies, and styles? absolutely. Does it matter in game? not really.
    Again, I disagree.

    Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post
    I think you mean something like Craft: Swords and Craft: Ships are two different things?
    That’s part of it.

    Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post
    I'm not sure "how you came to have them" has any effect in game.
    There’s a bevy of Merits you can take for sorcery, defined by “how you came to have them.” None learns The Burning Name studying under Mara.

    Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post
    Is there a difference between a DaVinci type swordsmith who is a self-taught genius vs a protege of Alveua, who is also a swordsmith? they both hammer out swords.
    The Da Vinci type doesn’t hammer his swords out of people.

    Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post
    Even then, it doesn't match Martial Arts because there's only one set of Lore charms with a restriction on how you use them, which is the polar opposite of 10+ MAs with mechanical incentives to use different combat philosophies.
    It’s still a closer match than Melee.

    Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post
    In Universe, Tiger Style and Ebon Shadow are Supernatural. They aren't just another type of kickboxing.
    You say that like it can’t be both.

    Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post
    Mortals, with perhaps a handful of rare exceptions, literally cannot do them.
    Lacking the enlightenment to draw out magical techniques from understanding of the mortal skill, is not lacking the capacity to understand the mortal skill.

    Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post
    As discussed early, I don't think so on a meaningful level. The boxer boxes, the Tae Kwon Doer kicks. Maybe one takes a full defense, or a rush, or a whatever, but there's really not much for them to do in Exalted beyond roll with a specialty and stunt.
    Consider looking past dice, at the underlying philosophies.

    Originally posted by Sorcerous Overlord View Post
    There's no mechanic for the boxer to close inside of kicking range staying low and pummeling the gut of the kicker, just as there's no mechanic that gives the kicker advantage for footsies and zoning out the boxer when they try to close distance.
    It’s below the typical level of system resolution; that doesn’t mean it must be ignored. An ST is encouraged to award or penalize where necessary, after all; and if I was running a mortal game, that’s exactly what I’d be doing for the boxer who can’t escape the kicker (and for the kicker when the boxer has outwaited his opponent’s stamina and starts his own flurry of blows).

    I actually did that when I was running Exalts; Seamstress, a necrosurgeon, had a comatose patient. Pitiless Triage Judgment informed her that a blow to the head had cracked his skull, and that the inflammation in his brain (and a piece of skull not being where it should!) was what was keeping him under. When she was asked to suggest a medicine to reduce the swelling in his brain, I assigned her a dice penalty to the roll; as someone who learned her trade cutting on corpses, this was outside her wheelhouse.

    (He recovered, by the way. There was a Guild factor handy with a whole wagon full of drugs.)

                A Melee knife-fighter has the same accuracy, damage and Parry as an unarmed Brawler (after stunting).

                Straight swords and slashing swords have the same stats.

                Heck, if you pick up the broken leg of a barstool during a bar-room brawl the your damage, accuracy and Parry doesn't change from simply being unarmed because clubs have the same stats as fists.

                Exalted isn't really about having that high a level of system granularity.

                I don't think complaining that Ebon Shadow and Crane styles are too similar is a valid complaint if they're also identical to Brawl and Melee.

                I mean, I get Sorcerous Overlord wants a higher level of granularity across the board, but I don't see an advantage to doing it that way. In D&D you roll your D20, add your modifiers and compare to AC. I don't want to go back to pages of THAC0 style charts to cross reference what body part I hit when using a round-house kick against a boxer.

                                                Originally posted by Katamari_Naneke View Post
                                                Do mortal martial artists play any significant role in your stories?
                                                The trouble I have is that there isn't a lot of mechanical difference between them. I can sort of dummy it up a bit by giving them relevant specialties (i.e. Ebon Shadow Stylists get a MA specialty in "surprise attacks," White Reaper Stylists get one in "while outnumbered") but I doubt player characters would even know the difference. Maybe I could make up some custom merits, or figure out some way to give them very limited access to charms? Do any of you have better ideas?
                                                In Second Edition Martial Artists and Martial Art schools played a really big role in my games due to the way that Awakened Mortal Martial Artists were sort of a head above normal mortal warriors. My players tended to spend a lot of time figuring out ways to get their mortal followers trained (either as Martial Artists, Sorcerers or Thaumatuges), coming up with martial art styles and Charms for them and whatnot.

                                                I've noticed in Third Edition, without the mechanical distinction, my players don't really care all that much and will o just have their mortal followers default to generic Melee or Archery or whatever rather than Martial Arts. Even the one player who specifically wanted a group of Ninja dudes as followers still build them with melee and thrown rather than Martial Arts. Players still take Martial Arts for themselves, but as something they would focus on for NPC followers and whatnot, it's kind of vanished.

                                                From the GM side, when Martial Art schools or organizations pop up, they tend to fill the same role as any other local political faction (like a religious order, or the local thieves guild, merchant guild or what have you). Overall, Martial Arts as a whole definitely doesn't play as large of a role in my games these days as they used to.
