                                                Originally posted by wonderandawe View Post
                                                This is an interesting moral dilemma. Should an Exalt be judged based on the actions of their previous incarnation? Should an entire Exalted host be judged by the actions of a few (or most) members? The Bronze faction Sidereals of the Usurpation certainly believed so.

                                                The first question is explored a bit by a Deep Space Nine episode.
                                                I don't know that the Bronze Faction is "judging" the modern Solars for the crimes their past incarnations committed. Rather, they held a Prophecy that said the only way, 100% to save the world without the risk of its destruction, would be to murder every Solar and Lunar and continue to murder them forever. If you ever stopped doing that, then there would be a risk that the world would be destroyed. The Bronze Faction felt that they had the duty to Creation to do shoulder that burden and engage in the Usurpation, even though presumably many members were friends or lovers of the Solar and Lunar Exalted. They were simply the faction that put their "job" ahead of their personal feelings and idealistic thinking.

                                                When the Bronze Faction leads a Wyld Hunt against a Solar, they probably just think it's bad luck on the part of the mortal who received a Solar Exaltation - sucks to be them, now they have to be killed even though they probably didn't do anything to deserve death.

                                                That said, Solars being held accountable for the actions of their past incarnations or, on the flip side, holding others accountable for the actions they took against that Solar's past incarnation, can absolutely make good plot hooks. A Solar can walk into a Fair Folk Court and, by virtue of his past life, demand the fealty of the gathered Fair Folk due to an oath they swore, but at the same time, the Solar may have to deal with people who are unhappy with actions his past incarnation took - it's basically a two way street.

                                                            Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                                                            I would argue that the entire premise of Exalted, the Usurpation and almost the entirety of the Sidereal player experience are ruined if the takeaway is, "The Great Prophecy was never accurate or credible to begin with and the Sidereal were complete, blithering morons for following it." Really, when the Sidereal's were on par with the Three Stooges in concerns of the Prophecy, and the Prophecy and subsequent Usurpation is supposed to be the cornerstone of Exalted's setting, that doesn't really set a good tone for the game.
                                                            Well, indeed, the cornerstone of the Usurpation is the Great Curse, and that is not meant to set a good tone.

                                                            In EX2, the solar (and lunar) exalts' Great Curse manifested as the Flawed Virtues which drove them to increasingly irrational actions, which prompted the sidereal exalts to gather together to contemplate their options. However, the sidereal exalts were themselves subject to their own version of the Great Curse: Flawed Fates, which fated them to advocate or adopt unwise actions and then rationalise this as their own meritorious choice. Their tendency to Limit Break, of course, increased when they gathered together in numbers and this was explicitly presented as the reason so many advocated for the bronze solution rather than the gold.

                                                            The Great Curse is still with us in EX3, though with somewhat improved playability for solars. I rather hope sidereal exalts get a much more clear Great Curse mechanic in EX3.]
