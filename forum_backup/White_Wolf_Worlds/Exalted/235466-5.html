        I wonder if it might not be a mistake to put in too many implications that there's something sinister and mysterious about the longevity and vitality of characters like Mnemon and the Scarlet Empress. For one thing, the tone and details of the setting feel like something that supports the idea that, if you're sufficiently wealthy and well connected, and have good fortune in the genes department, it's not especially difficult to access resources that can extend your lifespan. I'd also think that, even if it's not directly detailed, it wouldn't be difficult to figure out how to make a sorcerous project to aid in such. Mnemon and the Empress are well endowed with all such things to among the highest levels*, and it isn't really a big deal, it's just part of the general strangeness.

        For Cainan, I'd say he's in the genetic bad luck and not entirely healthy lifestyle department, and is insufficiently motivated to acquire the resources to live notably longer. Sidereals look on this, and mostly just shrug at what a shame it is; it would be convenient if he was going to live longer, but not to a degree sufficient to invest a lot of effort towards preserving him. A Peach of Immortality is actually the last thing you want to put in that situation, or any other for that matter; they're such a valuable commodity in Yu-Shan that their acquisition and transportation is not only extremely expensive, but a security nightmare, and they're so beyond valuable in Creation that hardly any figure they could save will provide a contribution sufficient to outweigh the likely degree of violence over possession of one if word of them should get out. Heck, if you offered Cainan a Peach of Immortality, and he fully understood what it was, there's a good chance he'd either hoard it against some future possibilities (which is fairly likely to end with him being murdered by a retainer or family member in the course of them stealing it), or sell it for the sake of resources he can use towards certain more immediate gratifications (which then means you've let a Peach of Immortality out to cause chaos in the world). The fact of their value and their rarity alone is the kind of thing that will often create an aversion to eating one immediately just for the sake of having it around, even if this isn't rational (although, on the other hand, there's something to be said for holding off on it for as long as possible for the sake of extending your life to the fullest degree).

        That's not to say that your characters can't engage in the expense and undertake the risks (which are reasonably manageable within the setting) to actually acquire, transport and consume the things, but it's part of why people generally wouldn't try in the setting. It's really the sort of audacious thing that can be left open for PCs who want to take a crack at it, rather than something just assumed to be lined up and easy outside of the circles of the game.

        * I imagine that for a lot of other people, the options within reach might not be quite so dignified. Ragara is almost twice as old as Mnemon is, but whatever his methods and advantages, they don't quite seem to have kept him in the best condition, although that might just be bad luck or some differences in lifestyle. I imagine there are a fair number of Dynasts who succumb to old age even when they could make a crack at living a lot longer because they don't care for the alteration in how they'd leave the world.

                                                    So yeah. Bhagwei. Dude's a poorly written jerk and I hate his guts and don't remotely get what you guys see in him.

                                                    Granted, I mostly hate his guts because of his actions as master of the Heptagram. But taking a step back here, let's consider how stupid his story in Aspect Book Wood is on every level. The secret incest baby thing comes off like the writer just discovered Game of Thrones for the first time and was feeling all edgy and hardcore (this is early White Wolf after all ) The most baffling part however is how completely irrelevant it is to Bhagwei's story. It's basically there to give him a claim to the throne... so he can ignore it and be all 'I'm too good for secular power'. You really didn't need incest to give the guy who personally trained almost every active sorcerer in the Realm a claim to the throne. So yeah, not only incest, but stupid pointless incest there to make us be more disgusted at the Dynasty. Yaaaay.

                                                    (It's small potatoes compared to that and what comes next, but the Empress killing her newly healed lover is also very stupid. I get that it's supposed to be subversive, remind us 'oh yeah, the dynasty are the bad guys' but it didn't have that vibe to me at all. It just felt stupid, a capricious act to try and shock me, the reader.)

                                                    So yeah. Following that, we get the high crimes stuff. Ragara Bhagwei, dominie of the Heptagram, takes advantage of his position to initiate a sexual relationship with a student, Tepet Oshina. Due to the course of these 'experiments', she is killed.

                                                    *breath*

                                                    You guys are gonna fan for this? Really?

                                                    Because that's my biggest issue with the point. Yes, it's sodding tasteless in isolation. But the presentation of it is what runs beyond irksome into frothing anger for me. The book seemingly, bafflingly doesn't regard this as a villain moment. There's a sidebar encouraging PCs to continue these experiments. It's one rejoinder is that PCs " should opt to begin their experiments with bisclaverets or neomah as guinea pigs instead of the daughter of a powerful Great House!" The tone reads as comedic to me - as if Bhagwei doing this to the daughter of a Great House was the one mistake he made. Bhagwei is not an obviously unreliable narrator like Deled, he's presented as more detached and analytical, and he's given an excuse that Oshina hadn't done the preparations he'd asked of her. Further the text sets her up with mild slutshaming, Bhagwei noting her "appetite for rich, old Exalts near death from old age". Painting her as a gold digger to try and ensure the reader isn't sympathetic to her fate.

                                                    The whole passage isn't written like the act of a villain, it's just 'a thing that happened'. And yeah, that's kinda opinion. But look at the OP.

                                                    Ragara Bhagwei knows the ways to sex so powerful, it makes his Dragon-Blooded partners explode. He usually refrains.
                                                    And I'm not trying to get at Omicron here, just use their words for illustrative effect. That's absolutely how this bit was written. It's not given gravity akin to Deled's fanaticism, or Lahor's brutality. Bhagwei is presented as a 'good Dynast', fighting Abyssals and being aloof from politics. .

                                                    Oh, and lest we forget, he then proceeds to skin a little girl and wear her as magic clothing.

                                                    ... I'd be lying if I said that disgusts me as much as I find it... grimly hilarious, in the 'wise old sorcerer' writeup. It definitely helps me not be able to take him seriously as any kind of positive, goodly mentor figure? I'd rather use Sesus Chenow in that role - at least I can try and argue old age mellowed him out a bit.
