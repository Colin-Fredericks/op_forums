            Originally posted by Morangias View Post

            In a perfect world, a Solar wouldn't feel like he got the whole world handed on a silver platter without even asking
            In particular, I feel that it ultimately robs a certain measure of credibility from Solars if we assume that, even if their most meaningful challenges come from the moral implications of what they set out and manage to do, their ultimate peril from making the "wrong" choice is that they'll have to live with their sadfeels. It doesn't really feel true to the setting if Solars are presented as insulated from the possibility that their choices can actually make people mad enough to want to kill them, and that such people can actually succeed.

            That's what I feel when I see anything that objects to different priorities of Solar power on the grounds that Solars should be powerful enough that they get to treat the world as a moral puzzle they have to try and solve.

            It's why I prefer proposed Third Edition Taboo-Inflicting Diatribe to the Second Edition one; the Third Edition one still allows for and implies the possibility that if, say, I use it to try and direct Sova away from cultural hatred of Yugash, that individuals within Sova who, for whatever personal or political reasons (like being a reactionary or having actually been witness to the desecration of the Ixut soulgems) have a direct sense of hatred towards Yugash, might still engage in things to try and redirect Sova, including targeting the actual Solar, recognised as a source of the change and designated an unwelcome interloper.

            To the extent that fighting behemoths might be part of this, well... most of the time, it probably isn't, the behemoth is just a big monster for you to be impressive about fighting (though part of being impressive is having a meaningful possibility of danger). Still, there might be some other practical or moral considerations, whether it's the behemoth being a blight on certain people and facing meaningful peril for their sake, or else the behemoth is just sport but you might wonder if risking your life that others might depend on is a prudent course of action.

            But it's mostly about the other stuff.

            Originally posted by Morangias
            a DB would feel like an elemental badass and a worthy heir to the titan-slaying army of ancient myths, all the while retaining a palpable difference in raw power between the two and all the nuances this adds to their respective stories.
            Yes, lately when I think of the differences, I think somewhat of the idea of Dragon Blooded being less about struggling almost to the point of futility (if ever so valiant!), and more that, considering their limitations, they need a bit more set up and support and suitable environment (with an eye towards their powers having means to grant them some things). Not quite Janest fighting with incredible might if she's in a wheat field, not quite the Sidereal who keeps up arrangements that let their arcane powers actually apply to the fullest; in short, not quite the idea that a Water Aspect who fights underwater has power close to that of a Celestial. But more, if the ground is the sort that the Terrestrial can move in with good facility, and they've got a few traps prepared, and are quick and clever, they can apply themselves well. Dragon Blooded may be less powerful than many Exalts, but asymmetric warfare is a thing that exists.

            That feels like a closer approach to John's sentiments of "Exalts defined by what they can't do" then the idea that Terrestrials approach what they can't do as some noble struggle.

            I have approximate knowledge of many things.

                I also like the idea that the current setting gives a Dragon-Blood a lot of his power as well, not just his Exaltation. At least in 2e, Terrestrials are given a specific ability spread in addition to their basic points, making them generally competent in many areas. Namely war. And since Dragon-Bloods work together, each of them with knowledge of combat tactics, it's an extremely effective interplay between them. Any of them can call out and begin coordinating their attacks together, and their different elemental advantages give them greater insight into any battle in which they are working together.

                As well, a Dynast's schooling, as said previously, gives them a general competence in many other areas. I've been in many campaigns as Celestial Exalts and everyone in the group almost universally never takes War, Socialize, Ride or Archery. Then I'll play a Dynast in the mixed group and it seems like I'm weak at first...except for the fact I'm the only one there that doesn't act like a Barbarian with no table manners, who can competently command an army, who can ride a horse, and who can actually use those big ballista weapons while besieged. A dragon-blood may not pack as big of a mystical punch, but a lot of the time, he is the most USEFUL bastard in the party. :.P
