        Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
        ... Has this question come up before? It feels very familiar, and contentious...
        It hasn't come up from me, but I'd be surprised if nobody else has had the same question!

        It came up for me, since my group has been playtesting through various parts of the book. We'd been doing generic-difficulty rolls to know-stuff and make-stuff when it came up, but got to a part where we started trying to figure out how to do it for reals.

        This was actually a simplified version of the question. The rest of it starts getting into why we'd need to have recursive color-coded craft XP (an idea which doesn't appear anywhere else) to add things to the setting, but with Lore you can change the setting somewhat instantly. Should we be able to build frameworks of hidden knowledge with Lore XP? Could I add a system where my Loremaster Player-Character savant adds a series of clues to the setting (silver Lore XP) leading to ruins and temples (each generating gold Lore XP) and finally leading to the completed Artifact I've wanted all along, or the sorcerous working canned and ready to go (just add water!), or the hidden weakness of the Chronicle villain? Also yes, I probably got the colors all out of order.

        That said, contention is not anywhere on the menu of questions as I intend them. But, if I happen to elicit tales of exciting-at-the-table Lore and Craft gameplay, I'm definitely all ears!

        If I can pull off an Ex3 game, it will be for a full table of probably 5 players plus me - that's a lot to be on top of all the time, behind the screen.

        Thanks!
