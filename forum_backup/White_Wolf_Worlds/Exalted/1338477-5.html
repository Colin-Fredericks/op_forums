            Originally posted by Grod_the_giant View Post
            Would the onslaught penalty replace the usual bonus to attack, or would they stack?
            Originally posted by Chausse View Post
            I think they stack, presented as it it, which is kinda very strong. Maybe the onslaught should only be applied after the attack.
            The intent was that both the onslaught penalty and and the normal bonus for Size would apply. I don't like the onslaught penalty only applying after the attack, because it still means that a battlegroup on its own is probably not going to hit, since the onslaught penalty will usually go away before they get a chance to attack again. The combination does make battlegroups overall more powerful, but I'm pretty much okay with that result - while large battlegroups are currently dangerous, small ones, I find, tend to be less dangerous than if you just ran the combatants individually, and I think this compensates for that.

            That said, if you don't want to make battlegroups overall more powerful, dropping the bonus to hit from Size would probably be the most logical. That would, however, mean that if a Size 5 battlegroup attacked 6 or more characters in Close range of one another, their numbers would provide absolutely no benefit, which also seems off. Perhaps as a compromise, subtract the onslaught penalty being inflicted from the battlegroup's bonus to attack from Size? That makes the calculation a bit more complex, of course, but not cripplingly so, I think.

                        Edit : I would also be tempted to make Hold the line cause an actual Withering Clash between the contestants rather than a special opposed roll, although that's more of a personal preference, the opposed roll is probably a safer bet balance-wise.
                        I considered just making it a clash, but decided against it for a couple of reasons. First, clashes are already pretty potent - being able to use your combat skill against the enemy, instead of your Defense, when your weapon skill isn't affected by onslaught penalties or other defense-only modifiers, and when it's probably your best pool anyway, is quite good. Adding the ability to prevent enemy movement on top of that just seemed overpowered. Second, I think that making this mechanically distinct from a clash opens up some interesting potential charm design space. I wouldn't mind seeing a few Solar Melee charms devoted to the idea of being an immovable object.

                            So Kelly Pedersen, you writing a new move has inspired me, and since there is another topic where I posted about my difficulties balancing "intermediate" encounters to make them interesting, I decided to write down some ideas that came to my mind.

                            Here is the link to the document I just wrote.

                            https://docs.google.com/document/d/1...it?usp=sharing

                            Please, do not hesitate to make suggestions of you had the patience to read the whole of it. I must also excuse my English, I know I don't do too many grammar mistakes but my writing does not feel very English because of my native language and this is quite a long document.

                            I'd also like to add some clarification : This may not appear clearly, but one purpose of these rules is to allow not for Fight Scenes only to have more tools that the ST can use, but rather to allow for making more easily engaging Action scenes where multiple interesting stakes can happen at once and ask for the players to come with creative solutions and unexpected development, which I feel is lacking in the action scenes at the moment in the game, because the ST has to invent all of them and be very inventive all the time without written support that facilitates his decisions.

                            Here is its content, as of tonight :

                            Introduction

                            So, your players decided to do something you didn’t prepare for (the usual), and you thus have no idea what to do. You foolishly said that there is certainly a quite powerful god/kingdom/exigent west of here, and suddenly you find yourself saying “Here you arrive at the gate of the palace/temple, and you see the guards and commanders preparing to fight you because reasons”. But deep down, you know you got no NPCs prepared and no idea on how to handle what’s next, but you do know that what’s going to happen is all your NPC’s are going to get crashed or dead, with no opportunity to do anything interesting.

                            Sometimes it’s fine, because your characters are so much stronger than anything else, and you don’t really care about it, and you want it to go quick. But sometimes you are frustrated and you would have liked this to be at least interesting or challenging, even if ultimately you know your PCs are going to win, but you don’t know how to do this. The purpose of this homebrew is to help you achieve this.

                            What this homebrew must achieve :
                            • Simplicity : It must not add complicated things to keep track of for the ST, who is certainly in this situation because he has already lots of work or few guidelines
                            • Abstraction : It must add tools that can be easily abstracted and re-used for the ST in different situation to add a variety of content
                            • Fun : It must produce scenes or situations that the ST and players had fun participating
                            • Coherence : It must allow the ST and the players to play their characters in a way that seems in line with the rest of the mechanics and fluff of the game
                            What this homebrew is not intended for :
                            • Fighting some random thugs no one has any interest in. Just roll (Attribute + Ability) and be done with it.
                            • Not resolve any final big battle against extremely powerful adversaries. Some ideas here might be useful for this kind of battle, but it’s not its primary purpose.
                            • A way to cheat out the existing mechanics of the game. If any point in this homebrew can be used for shenanigans not present in the Core books, you should prioritize using the Core rules.
                            • Balance. The purpose of this guide is to artificially help you raise the stakes of certain scenes. It will propose solutions that are not balanced, and you should not consider to use them in other places.
                            TL;DR

                            To sum it up, the purpose of this homebrew is to give easy access tool for STs who didn’t have time to prepare encounters for NPCs they think should be meaningful or impressive, but do not know how to achieve it in a reasonable time of preparation.

                            The actual content

                            These rules are explicitly designed to be used only for the NPCs and the STs. Let the players use them for their party at your own risk.

                            Set-up and join battle

                            At the beginning of the encounter, chose a Flagbearer. The Flagbearer is usually the antagonist NPC who is in charge where the PCs are intervening and will oppose them, controlling the armies, directing lieutenants, wielding authority from his Evil Master etc … He will usually be Exalted, or supernaturally powerful in some way, but you didn’t have enough time to flesh it out.

                            The Flagbearer starts the fight rolling his usual initiative, and add another 5. So he rolls Join Battle with (Wits + Awareness) + 3 (Rules from the Core) + 5. The Flagbearer first action CANNOT be a decisive attack, else he forfeits 5 initiative. The Flagbearer then generates 3 initiative per round. This initiative is meant to be used by the Flagbearer to create dramatic circumstances that will create fun situations for the players to resolve. Trying to shenanigan this initiative in order to just do mass decisive damage to the PCs should be considered an act of bad faith, and you should be ashamed of it.

                            Putting down the Flagbearer in some way is usually the objective of the PCs, and the fight should be considered almost done when this is achieved, except if another rule explicitly says so.

                            TL;DR
                            Set your most important NPC as a Flagbearer, roll his initiative, and add another 5. Don’t start with a decisive attack with him. The Flagbearer gains 3 initiative on each subsequent round for free. If the Flagbearer is beaten, the fight should usually end.

                            Charms

                            Keyword

                            But : Many Charms will involve your Flagbearer or its allies doing something, and your PCs will often react saying “But my elemental is supposed to be blocking the pathway with his powers” or something along those lines. Whenever a Charm has this keyword, the player with the most interesting argument gain Initiative, from 1 if his argument is not very strong to 5 if his argument is very strong (Basically, the cost of the merit usually associated is a good scale). Can be gained only once per use of the Charm. This should act as a reward for the player when he doesn’t already mechanically benefit from his resource, not a way for him to benefit from something already mechanically useful.

                            Forward, guards !
                            Cost : 3i.
                            Type : Supplemental.
                            Keyword : But

                            The Flagbearer designates a place that is at least at Medium (Long would be better) Range, and holds some sort of strategic, symbolic or magical importance. While travelling toward this place, the Flagbearer (and him alone) gains +3 soak and 2 Hardness per unit and per size of battlegroup accompanying him (ie being in Close Range or making sense to accompany him or protecting him through the use of some magical powers). For example, if he had a Battlegroup of Size 3 and a familiar with him, he would gain 12 soak and 8 Hardness). Should the road toward the objective be blocked definitively (like really definitively), the effect of this Charms end.
                            This Charm can be used when Join Battle is rolled.

                            Notes
                            The evident purpose of this Charm is to help the ST protect the Flagbearer while he attains a place that hold scenaristical importance, like the temple of a friendly god that owes a debt to him, or secret room filled with traps that will protect him. It also prevents the players going full stupid on their main target and force them to confront the other forces present. It also can make for some interesting way the players can try to block the enemy.

                            This shall not fall into their hands, flee !
                            Cost : 3i
                            Type : Reflexive
                            Keyword : But.

                            The Flagbearer designates a close NPC or bystander that somehow recognizes his authority, set his initiative to the lowest of (his own initiative or 15), and entrust him with whatever MacGuffin (or keys of the safe, or the plans leading to the object) the players were looking for. The NPC, now called the Runner, then start to flee on its turn, gaining the following advantage :
                            • If using combat movement, the Runner first reflexively moves a range band that doesn’t count as his move action for the round (Illustrate with the Flagbearer’s powers giving him some boost)
                            • If not using combat movement, the Runner starts with 5 successes on anyone else trying to contest him (Illustrate with the Flagbearer’s powers giving him some boost)
                            • If the Runner was nameless, his pools for running are set as those of a professional runner : He rolls 7 dices for most athletical challenge for escaping.
                            • The Flagbearer or any close lieutenant close to him can help the Runner escape by reflexively spending 3 initiative to double his next roll. Do not abuse it, it’s meant to make things more cool and tensed by illustrating cool powers of the NPCs when player characters are closing on the Runner and give them a challenge, not a free easy escape for the Runner with the object the players are desperately needing.
                            • The Runner cannot use a decisive attack while running away, except if he is totally cornered and has absolutely no way to escape. If he is a non combattant, he shouldn’t be allowed to make a decisive attack at all. Should the Runner use a decisive attack, he loses the control of the Macguffin, that ideally goes close to the nearest precipice or equivalent.
                            Notes
                            The evident purpose of this Charm is to give some sort of chase scene, and allows the athletics characters a chance to illustrate themselves by doing a dynamic chase. It also allows to illustrate the coolness and flexibility of the antagonist NPCs by finding imaginative ways his powers and armies can interact in a chase scene.
                            Do not use this Charm if the players have no way to chase someone, except if you consider they have failed to catch the Macguffin for long enough and deserve to lose its immediate track.

                            You think you can come past my armies ?
                            Cost : 3i
                            Type : Reflexive
                            Keyword : But.

                            The Flagbearer does not fight alone, and he will not be the first to fall. Upon being attacked, he can reflexively be the target of a Defend Other action for this attack by any close battlegroup he commands or any fidel lieutenant close to him. Only usable once for each lieutenant and each battle group size.
                            Should the attacking character still successfully hit him through the Defending character, the ST can decide to exceptionally protect the Flagbearer but at a great cost :
                            • On a decisive attack, the Defending Character that intercepts the blow takes double damage (as if by Thunderbolt Attack Prana, but not cumulable with it).
                            • On a withering attack, the attacking character can choose any ally in the fight. This ally will gain the same amount of initiative he does after successfully resolving the attack against the Flagbearer, but the Flagbearer does not lose any initiative as a direct result of the attack.
                            Will you stop me, or save the children ?
                            Cost : 5i
                            Type : Reflexive
                            Keyword : But.

                            The luck every villain gets when the hero finally close on him. The Flagbearer designates something a PC care for deeply or has a positive intimacy toward. The designated object or person find itself in a terrible situation, usually through the confusion of the fight : the close walls start crumbling around it and threaten to smash it, a close magical relic activates and starts contaminating it with Wyld influence or Strange powers, a group of innocent bystanders get thrown in the middle of the confusion need protection else their fate sealed, etc …

                            Notes
                            The evident purpose of this Charm is to divert a PC that has a lethal opportunity on the Flagbearer or an important lieutenant using the famous trope of “What is the most important to you ?”, and allows the PC to illustrate himself with cool powers to save the day : Maybe he can Occult away the magic relic, use a Charm to stop the Wyld influence or bump the innocent victim out of harm’s way. This Charm does not need the bad guy to know the intimacy of the PC : To him, it’s just a fortunate turn of event.

                            There are forces in this world that are beyond your comprehension !
                            Cost : 10i
                            Type : Simple.
                            Keyword : But.

                            When in a place of power (eg the heart of a manse) or theatrically impressing, the Flagbearer reveals he prepared for this day since a long time ago. Bound by a sworn pact, a promise of power, or a costly purchase, a huge figure comes to aid the Flagbearer, called the Titan. The Titan should be in the theme of where the action is taking place, and the one summoning it. The Titan emerges at close or short range from the Flagbearer, with 10 initiative. He does not act that turn, but is considered having used a Protect Other action on the Flagbearer that lasts until his next turn.
                            His stats should include the following parameters :
                            • The Titan is usually legendary size, with the associated benefits
                            • The Titan health points should average (4*Number of PC units actively fighting)
                            • The Titan will rarely fight to death, but should the players utterly defeat him, they should earn some sort of advantage for doing so (A god rival of the one defeated will contact them and give them a reward, the beating heart of an elemental is a precious magical component the players were looking for, etc … Do not hesitate to make use of State a Fact to earn the reward by one of the players to highlight them)
                            • The Titan’s attack and damage stats should threaten at least one fighting player (ie : Attack is (Defense of one player)*2 and Base damage is (Soak of a player +3))
                            • The Titan’s soak should not be higher that the base damage of the strongest player character in the group
                            • The Titan CANNOT kill any player character not their very close ally, but he can greatly injure them. Anyone put down to 0 hp by The Titan can usually be stabilized by a medicine roll that takes into account the situation the players are in.
                            • The Titan is usually slow, constrained, or very proud. Upon crashing or defeating 1-3 player controlled units (1 if there are few, 3 if there are a lot of them), The Titan consider its job done, and will usually start leaving without demanding its rest, making it expressely clear to the players that he is no longer a threat and should not be pursued if they are in a bad shape.
                            Notes
                            The evident purpose of this Charm is to surprise the players with some cool huge stuff to kill or confront, and give a chance for the Big Game Hunter players to illustrate himself. Do not use this Charm if the players are in a bad shape already. I implemented some safeguards so he doesn’t become stupidly too lethal if for some reason mistakes were made.

                            Personal notes

                            Here is my non exhaustive list of personal notes, advices and things I would like to upgrade.

                            Notes
                            • Many of the ideas I have are certainly inspirations from recent games like DD that gives epic monsters special moves in their lairs, as well as the last post from Kelly Pedersen about making a new move
                            • I am really open for criticism. Really. But don’t come to explain to me that Exalted is already fine as it is, because that’s not really the point of this homebrew.
                              The "But" Keyword idea came to my mind when speaking to DrLoveMonkey in another thread about not using the retainers/allies of your PCs when they were important for a fight but not enough to actually include them in the mechanics of the fight, so you just put them in the background. I think it's a not too bad solution to use them in a satisfactory way
                            Advices
                            • Don’t use it when you have an encounter you think is already balanced
                            • Don’t abuse it. Don’t let your players abuse it.
                            Things I would like to upgrade
                            • I’d certainly add a Charm about improving a lieutenant in some form or another
                            • I’d certainly add a Charm about passing the Flagbearer title to someone if the current one became irrelevant
