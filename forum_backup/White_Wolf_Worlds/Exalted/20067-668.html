    Originally posted by Chausse View Post
    1) Suppose a character with 2 intimacies A and B. Suppose someone is trying to instill/persuade him using intimacy A. Does intimacy A lower the resolve of the defending character because the action itself is using intimacy A against him, or would the persuader need to use another intimacy (for example B) to benefit lowering the resolve of the defender ?
    You'd use Intimacy A in most cases here. Intimacy B would only come into play, usually, if the target could claim it opposed your influence, and thus gained its bonus to resolve, which could partially or completely cancel the penalty that Intimacy A created.

    Originally posted by Chausse
    2) How do you handle characters trying to threaten battle groups. I found myself against a player rolling like 20 successes on a Presence roll to try to scare a battle group while fighting, he had some good anima vibe that made the description cool, but then how do you resolve it normally ? Should it trigger a normal rout check ? A rout check with difficulty the roll of the threaten action ?
    I would handle this as a Presence roll vs. the battlegroup's average Resolve, attempting to inspire an emotion of "panic" or "fear". I'd definitely apply the penalty for group size, and, in fact, I might add an additional penalty equal to the group's Size. I'd also add +1 to the group's Resolve for Average Drill, and +2 for Elite. If the roll was successful, the unit would experience the emotion, which would add its usual modifier to checks - if the character who provoked the fear then attacked the group and did enough damage to cause a rout check, for example, the rout check would be penalized by -3 (for a major intimacy, the standard strength of an inspired emotion), and if a commander tried to order a unit to attack the source of the fear, they'd take the same penalty. But I wouldn't have it provoke an instant rout check. That seems like the domain of a charm to me.

    Originally posted by Chausse
    3) How do you handle threatening the familiar of a Solar with Spirit-tied pet, or some summoned creatures like Demons and Elementals, who have a Defining tie of Loyalty toward their master they cannot act against and that can't be lowered ? Specifically for example the Spirit-tied Pet Charm of Solar states that "No influence of any kind can make it act against this intimacy", the threaten action is clearly a social influence, does it means all Solar familiars are immuned threaten action ? Kinda the same goes for demons and elementals
    I wouldn't say creatures with such unbreakable intimacies are impossible to threaten - such a threat simply can't make it act against its master. But you can scare beings into doing stuff against their master's wishes. For example, with a familiar, a successful threaten could convince the creature to leave you alone, even if its master told it to attack you. Or a creature that the master was riding on might actually run away from you, despite the master commanding it to return, because it sees itself as "rescuing" its master from the scary person. Remember, the imposed Intimacy in all the cases we're talking about is one of loyalty, which is not necessarily the same as obedience. It means that the creature won't directly harm the master (or, at least, do know knowingly), and will try to do what's good for the master as the creature perceives it, not automatically slavishly follow orders.

        Originally posted by armyofwhispers View Post

        I dunno, I think there`s room for stunting in such a way that you get around the flaw by compensating for it. I think that should qualify for Solar XP. It`s still effecting gameplay by forcing the character to think around it.
        Eh. Given the book example of a character who isn't being limited by a Flaw is a blind Solar using Awareness charms to overcome it, I don't think asking for a stunt is all that onerous. Charms have actual in-game costs, whereas a stunt doesn't, and, in fact, it's giving a bonus, since it's cancelling both the penalty for lacking a limb, and still giving its normal bonus to boot.

        That said, to maintain consistency, I suppose I should revise my earlier answer - I would allow someone to stunt a one-armed grapple to avoid the penalty. It's just, if you do that, you won't get the Solar XP for the Amputee flaw.

                                            Originally posted by Chausse View Post
                                            And another question !

                                            It is written that siege weapons can do feats of demolition, with a perception/intelligence + sail/war roll that allows for a whole new range of 15+ strength checks (So I guess with 30 successes at maximum ?).

                                            However, there are not many charms in sail/war to augment your total successes, and I believe statistically even the best Solar or specialized Exalted will not be able to achieve these feats. Does anyone share another analysis than mine or could highlights few spots on this situation ?

                                            There's a few things to consider here. First, bear in mind that feats of demolition don't necessarily have to be accomplished all in one roll - they can be treated as extended actions. In particular, anything listed on the Feats of Strength table as "slowly do this thing" or "do this over time" is probably an extended action.

                                            Second, the rules for feats of strength add bonus successes when using an appropriate tool, which I'd definitely say a siege weapon counts as (Exalted Core, p. 229)

                                            Third, the Bombard tag adds +4 dice to feats of destruction (Arms of the Chosen, p. 127)

                                            Finally, though it's not particularly clear, I would add a siege weapon's Accuracy to its pool for feats of demolition.

                                            So, putting all that together, let's look at an example: the feats of strength table lists "slowly bash through the wall of a fortress" as requiring minimum Str 10, and 15 successes. We'll give the crew of the weapon a reasonable pool of 6 - professionals, though hardly masters. They're using a catapult, so add +4 dice. And they're firing at their weapon's ideal range, Medium, for another +4 dice from Accuracy. So they've got a pool of 14. On average, that's 7 successes per roll, plus one bonus success for using an appropriate tool. On extended feats of demolition, I treat the Str minimum as the target number for each roll, meaning an average roll for these catapulters isn't making it, but they don't have to get too much above average to make it. It will probably take 30-40 catapult shots to break down a fortress wall. That may seem like a lot, but bear in mind that's happening in combat time, so it's probably 2-3 minutes of "real time".

                                            The numbers get even better once you start taking Exalted skill levels into account. An Exalted siege engineer with a base (Attribute + Ability) total of 8 would have a pool of 16 to start with, and she could add 5 dice in Excellency every turn and still break even on motes, taking her up to 21. That's 11.5 successes per shot on average, 2.5 successes per shot towards the goal number, so it should only take 6 shots to take down that wall.

                                            And, of course, you can always come up with custom charms to help with this kind of stuff too. I could see Solar War having a charm or two to enhance siege weapon feats of demolition. It's just a niche thing, so probably not worth the wordcount. Maybe if we ever get a book devoted to war and grand scale strategy, they could appear there.
