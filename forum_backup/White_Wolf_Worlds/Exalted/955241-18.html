                                                    Sacrificial Magic 6
                                                    River Travel 3
                                                    Business Management 3
                                                    Craft: Charms and Talismans 2
                                                    Presence: Inspirational Speeches 3
                                                    City Politics 1
                                                    Navigation: 1
                                                    Ruthless Lethality: 1
                                                    Organized Crime: 2
                                                    Falconry 6+ 5 Vote Terrible Pun Bonus to Break The Tie=11

                                                    For the record, I’m definitely using the “just add up all the votes and don’t worry about preference” method. If you tell me your first, second, and third choice…I just count that as one vote for each, like in the linked video. I’m lazy, you guys. Also, in the event of a tie, I’m totally just going with whoever makes the best pun. Raiton the money indeed!

                                                    You consider a number of grandiose ambitions-sailing away, or joining a caravan, or becoming a crime lord, or becoming a sorcerer…but the more you think about it, the more you start to think these dreams aren’t realistic. Nobody’s going to take a kid your age seriously, and the more people you have to get close with the more you’ll have to worry about people figuring out your gender, which you’d still rather keep a secret…

                                                    Animals, though…raptor cats and raitons don’t care whether you’re male or female. They don’t care whether you’re an adult or a kid. They can’t lie to you or cheat you or steal from you the way people can. As friends and minions go…well, they’re a lot more trustworthy than a human criminal organization would be! You have a slight and unusual advantage, and you decide that since you have some time to spare, you’re going to see how far it can get you.

                                                    Your decision made, you tidy up your dishes, lighting one of Moth’s many candles for light as the evening deepens. You’re determined to keep the apartment clean, if only to provide Moth with a good role model. Once you’re done cleaning up after dinner, you decide to start preparing for the future-and as is your old habit, food is at the forefront of your mind. You take a few minutes to peel your eggs. You put four of them aside for breakfast tomorrow, and then stack the rest of them in your clay jug and fill it with vinegar and a couple dried peppers. You put the jar on the stovetop until the vinegar starts to boil, then apply some dripping wax from the candle to the hot clay rim before putting the top on tight so that wax seeps up around the edge of the lid. Hopefully that seal will hold as it cools, and the eggs will pickle and be good for a few months.

                                                    After about an hour of fruitlessly trying to read one of Moth’s books by candlelight, picking out and copying the words you recognize and giving yourself a headache trying to fill in the words in between, you disrobe and take a look at your bandages. Moth did good work. You poke them gingerly-the scratches from the Raptor Cat are still sore, but you don’t feel any swelling or any sign of infection. You probably ought to remove the bandages and clean the wounds again…but you’re not sure you could re-apply the dressings without assistance from Moth, and she still hasn’t gotten home yet. Concluding that they’ll probably be okay at leat until tomorrow, you leave them on, blow out the candle, and crawl into the bedroll.
                                                    ***



                                                    You dream of soaring high over Nexus by night. You have great black wings, and the feel of the wind streaming over your feathers is rapturous. You turn northward, winging your way out beyond the city walls. Rice paddies and herds of goats slumbering on terraced hills pass below you, the moon glinting on puddles and night-dew when it slips briefly out from behind the clouds. You glance up at the handful of stars visible through that gap, reorient yourself North, and continue flying…but as you go the wind begins to pick up and you see distant thunderheads flickering on the Northern horizon. Not willing to chance a storm, you turn back home towards Nexus, but the wind is no longer with you, and heavy rain begins to fall, drenching your feathers and weighing you down…and then something huge moves through the flickering clouds overhead, a great slithering dragon-shape surrounding by howling winds…and then it ROARS, with a voice like thunder, and-

                                                    You jolt awake, heart pounding, to the sound of pots and pans clattering, instinctively reaching your hand towards the knife you usually keep tied to your arm but which you decided to sleep without. For a moment your terror deepens as you realize you’re unarmed…but then as you awaken further, you orient yourself-you’re in your apartment, and the dark figure moving pots around is White Moth. You’re safe.

                                                    “Hey,” Moth says softly. “Sorry to wake you up. I didn’t mean to drop that.”

                                                    It’s raining outside. You can hear the dull rumble of rain on the roof, and you see that Moth has closed the shutters in the window.

                                                    You groan. “It’s alright…gods, you’re back late.”

                                                    “Yeah.” She shrugs. “It’s, uh…it’s been a very educational evening.” Something in Moth’s voice seems a little shaken. She puts a pot of water on the stove, stirs up the low coals and adds some more charcoal, and slumps against the wall, exhausted. Her normally wild and tangled hair is wet and flat over her face and shoulders.

                                                    “Long day?”

                                                    “Yeah. Not as long as it’ll be for the Master of Nine Black Chains, though. I finished my part so he let me go home, but he won’t be able to stop working until at least sunrise tomorrow…and probably he’ll keep going until about half an hour before sunset.”

                                                    “What’s he doing? Catching ghosts?”

                                                    “Yeah.” Moth nods. “I caught a couple myself. Like I said. Very educational.”

                                                    “You…um. You didn’t bring them home with you, did you?”

                                                    “Ha! No. The Master needed them for guides.”

                                                    “Where’s he going?”

                                                    “Someplace I’m not ready to follow him. Not yet.” She sighs. “I know he’s from Sijan, but the dead around here aren’t always as cooperative. I really hope he’s okay.”

                                                    There’s a flicker of lightning from the window, and for a moment the room is stark white except for Moth, who is silhouetted in black except for white bandages on her left hand and arm, and the red of blood on both hands.

                                                    Then it’s dark again.

                                                    “I hope he’s okay too,” you declare softly. “Moth, are you alright?”

                                                    She fumbles with the bag of rice a little, opening it and scooping out some to pour into the pot of water on the stove. Then she sits down on the bedroll next to you. You can smell her sweat and a cloying acrid scent like herbs…and also an old earthy rotting sort of smell, and blood.

                                                    The thunder follows several long seconds after the flash, a long low rumble that rattles the dishes in the cupboard.

                                                    “I…I’ll be fine, Tavi.” She sighs. “Just…it’s been a hard day. If the Master hadn’t been there I think I probably would have died. I’ve never…I’ve always seen ghosts and stuff, ever since I was little, but they’ve always been…wispy. This is the first time they’ve been able to touch me.”

                                                    She holds up her bandaged arm. “The Master says I should consider studying some sort of athletics or martial arts or dance or something. I was too slow today.”
                                                    “…a ghost did that to you?”

                                                    “Yeah. I mean, I cut my hand for an offering to bring it close, but it, uh, wanted more than I offered I guess.” She shrugs. “Next time I do this sorta thing I’ll weave together some warding prayer strips to make armor, I think. I had a knife coated with salt, but I was too slow to do anything with it.”

                                                    “…is that why you’re so good with bandages?”

                                                    “What?”

                                                    “Cutting yourself for ghosts?”

                                                    “Oh. No, I don’t usually do that. The Master does it all the time, though. His blood is apparently delicious. So I’ve gotten pretty good at bandaging him up. He heals pretty quick, usually.”
                                                    You shake your head. “This all sounds pretty dangerous, Moth.”

                                                    “Well, yeah. That’s why most people don’t do it. But if you pull it off, you can do something most people can’t, and that’s power.” Moth shrugs again. “I decided a long time ago that I was going to take the little gift the gods gave me and use it to become as powerful as I could. When I die, I don’t want to be some sad little ghost whining about all the things she should have done with her life, I want to be rich and powerful in a fancy mausoleum with lots of servants and I want people to remember me after I’m gone.”

                                                    You prop yourself up on your elbow. “Moth, you… want… to be a ghost?”

                                                    “Well, not anytime soon! But we all have to die sometime, right? So I’m gonna plan for it.”

                                                    “But…you…shouldn’t you think about moving on to your next life instead?”

                                                    Moth gives you a strange look. Or maybe her face just looks strange because it’s dark.

                                                    “Tavi, the Dragons don’t usually look too fondly on necromancers.”

                                                    “…I guess not.”

                                                    “Listen, you go back to sleep. Will it bother you if I light a candle and read a bit while I eat?”

                                                    “I’ll be fine.”

                                                    You snuggle into the bedroll, and hear the flare of a match, and the darkness of your closed eyes goes deep red with candlelight. You worry about Moth and the fate of her soul for a little bit, and then drift off to sleep to the sound of pages turning.

                                                    You don’t have any more dreams.
                                                    ***



                                                    You awaken early again the next morning. Rain is still pounding dully against the roof, and Moth has curled up around you on the bedroll, snoring gently. She must have washed and changed before bed; the bandage on her left hand is clean and white instead of dripping red like it was last night. Her arm is wrapped around you, so you have a pretty good vantage point.
                                                    You enjoy just relaxing and being warm for a little while, but you grow bored after a few minutes. It’s clear you’re not falling back asleep…and you have to pee. You gingerly wriggle out from under Moth’s arm and get up, making your way over to the chamber pot. Once you’ve finished your business you empty it out the window and rinse it out with rainwater-might as well take advantage of the pouring rain while you have it.

                                                    Your little ginger root seems to be enjoying the wet weather, it’s pushed out a small leaf overnight. The raitons don’t appear to have left any gifts on your windowsill this morning; you expect they’re all sheltering someplace against the rain.

                                                    As you’re about to close the shutters to stop the damp getting in, your expectations are proved wrong: a big black raiton laboriously flaps its way through the gloom, alighting on your windowsill. You notice it’s carrying a shiny button in its beak. It places the button on the windowsill, nudges it once towards you, then rustles its feathers, preening, as you stare at it.

                                                    “Good morning,” you murmur softly. Then, an idea occurring to you, you back slowly away and grab one of the boiled eggs you were saving for breakfast. You return to the window and slowly hold the egg out to the raiton.

                                                    It looks at your hand…then at your face. After several seconds, it snaps forward suddenly, and it takes all your self-composure not to flinch away from the flash of little needle-sharp teeth as it digs into the egg. You hold your hand flat, the way you’ve heard one is supposed to feed horses or goats without getting bitten, but that doesn’t work very well for sharp beaks and you get poked a couple of times, though nothing that breaks the skin.

                                                    The raiton eats about half the egg before just picking up the rest and swallowing it whole, its head tilted back and bobbing up and down comically. Then it’s staring at you again, considering. It croaks, looks up, and flutters away above the window. Then it flutters back down, perching on the windowsill and cocking its head to peer at you quizzically. It repeats this pantomime several times, flying up and returning, as though it expects you to come out the window and follow it skyward.

                                                    “Sorry, friend,” you whisper. “I can’t fly.”

                                                    Not seeming to understand you, it again flutters out and up into the rain, cawing, and you can’t help the impression that the bird thinks you’re not particularly bright.

                                                    “Shush, you’ll wake Moth,” you mutter. “Fine. I’ll follow you. Just be quiet!”

                                                    You close the shutters, put on your shoes, throw on a baggy old shirt you don’t mind getting wet, and grab your key. You and Moth are on the third floor, which is the topmost floor of your building. Some of the buildings in nicer parts of Nexus are old First Age structures with dozens of stories, but out here in the Blackwater District it’s mostly big squat square tenement buildings.

                                                    It’s a short walk down the hall to the stairs, and then a short walk up to the rooftop. Gleaming Smile’s rooftop is wide and gently sloped so the water runs away from the center of the building and out towards either side. The tiles are rough clay, and many of them are cracked. It’s not very steep, it’s almost flat. Even if it wasn’t easy to walk on, there’s a short railing around the eaves on all sides. A big round iron grill rusts under an awning next to the door going downstairs and inside, and there’s a long wooden table set out and tall poles with ropes strung between them for hanging paper lanterns. You expect that during the summer months, the tenants probably come up here to cook and spend time with each other…and to have somebody to eat with after dark.
                                                    Right now, it’s bleak and gray and blurry in the unrelenting rain. Somewhere in the East, you’re pretty sure the Sun is rising behind the clouds, because the gray is lighter than it was from your window downstairs. The awning is pouring a small waterfall out onto the roof, and tiny rivers run between the clay tiles, forming puddles and pools before draining off the eaves. There are no paper lanterns hanging on the rope between the poles this morning.

                                                    There are, however, five puffy and unhappy looking raitons, dripping wet and obviously in a foul mood, because one of them hisses at you as you step out onto the roof. The others follow suit, glaring at you and extending their wings to look larger and more threatening. The one from your window downstairs flaps up over the lip of the roof and circles overhead, cawing several times. You recognize it by the yellow bits of yolk dusting its beak and the feathers of its head. The others watch it, then gradually they seem to calm, though they still eye you warily.

                                                    The raiton above circles several times, then lands next to you, looks up at you expectantly, and hops away towards the others. Passing under its perched companions, it hops under the table and out of sight…and you hear a hacking gurgle. Slowly following the bird (instantly regretting it as soon as you come out from under the awning into the cold rain), you lower your body and turn your head sideways.

                                                    It is vomiting up the boiled egg you gave it! You feel briefly insulted! Raitons eat corpses and eyeballs and garbage! More than that, it’s throwing away FOOD!

                                                    Your anger fades quickly, though, as you notice another shape under the table. A wet feathered clump, it trembles and extends a beak to peck at the pile of egg the other raiton has deposited. Where the other raitons are constantly preening, this one appears scruffy, and you notice several places where feathers are missing and you can see dull gray and black scales underneath. You suspect it’s injured.

                                                    The raiton that delivered the egg hops out from under the table and looks up at you again quizzically as its bedraggled fellow slowly consumes the egg.

                                                    You turn your back on it and go back inside, shutting the door behind you.

                                                    Two minutes later, you return with another boiled egg and a fistful of dried fish. Again, most of the raitons hiss and puff up at your approach, but this time they shut up when the sick one under the table croaks quietly. They watch you silently as you crawl under the table and deposit another boiled egg and some dried fish in front of it. The injured bird doesn’t even bother acting afraid of you, it just starts eating. This close, you realize that it smells strange-not quite like urine, but like some of the chemicals they sometimes cart in over at the foundry. And on closer examination, its feathers aren’t just missing in places-the feathers around those places are singed, as though the feathers were burned away. It also still has a bit of twine around its left foot. This is the raiton that found your bundle hanging in the smokestack and got all tangled up in your twine trying to get it out. The one you saved from the Raptor Cat, the one that saved you from the Raptor cat with its distracting Raptor Cat impression.

                                                    “What happened to you, huh?” You wonder aloud.

                                                    You hear scuffling and cawing, and as you crawl out from under the table, you find the rest of the raitons (except for the one with yolk on its beak) have fallen from their perches and are croaking and flopping on their backs. Several of them are hopping with wings outstretched at an odd angle, pretending to have broken wings. One is huddled in a feathery ball, making a piteous gargling sound.

                                                    They have figured out that you give free food to sick raitons. You can’t help but burst out laughing, rolling onto your back on the cold wet roof as the rain streams down your face. You’ve done the same routine to get pity handouts, especially during the colder months. You remember Rat even got you a set of crutches from someplace. Seeing such foreboding creatures, symbolic of the finality of death and ill omens, flapping about in the hopes of a free meal…you don’t stop chuckling for a while.

                                                    You hand out pieces of dried fish to the rest of the raitons, and they instantly stop playacting and wolf it down as fast as they can. The quicker ones even have the nerve to sidle up to the slower ones and try to snatch food from them. Nobody is going after the big pile you gave the injured raiton under the table, though.

                                                    You remember Moth’s talk last night, and you resolve not to waste the opportunity the Gods have given you. You hold some dried fish in your clenched fist, and hold it out. A couple of the raitons peck at it, smelling the food but not sure how to get at it. One of them nips at you, and you bap it on the beak, sending it croaking and cawing and scurrying away. The others also move back…but quickly return.

                                                    Eventually, by trial and error, one of them figures out what you want, hopping up onto your wrist. You open your fist slightly and let it get a few bites of fish, before shaking your arm to send it flapping away. Then you hold out your fist again.

                                                    They learn quickly. Soon, the idea that landing on an outstretched fist results in a treat has spread to the lot of them, and they start squabbling with each other to perch on your arm when you hold it out. The squabbling escalates into a chaotic aerial battle full of squawks and warbles and wet flapping wings, before a low, quiet hiss cuts the whole thing short.
                                                    The sick raiton has finished its meal and emerged from under the table, and it’s hissing at the rest of them. Looking positively sheepish, they flutter away from you and from each other to alight once more on the rope around the rooftop picnic area, desperately attempting to regain a sense of dignity.

                                                    You kneel next to the sick raiton and hold out your fist. It looks at the fist, then cocks its head sideways to look at you with one eye. Another raiton croaks, and the sick one squawks back, and there’s a raucous chorus of harsh cries as they all express their opinions on whatever is being discussed…and then the sick one hops up onto your outstretched fist. You unclench your fist to allow it to eat some dried fish, then stand up. The bird stays steady on your hand.

                                                    Abruptly all the rest of the raitons take to the air, cawing and croaking and circling overhead before winging away through the rain to the south. The one on your hand calls after them, and extends its wings a bit…but doesn’t fly after them. It turns to look at you again, and then pecks your hand once, hard.

                                                    You bap it on the beak with your free hand, and it hisses.

                                                    You bap it on the beak again, and it shuts up.
                                                    ***



                                                    “Wha…what’s that smell?” Moth asks, stirring in her bedroll and yawning. It’s a couple hours before noon. It’s still drizzling gently outside. “Ugh, it’s like something died…”

                                                    “Sorry about that.” You gesture to the scruffy raiton perched on the wooden chair next to the stove. “He does smell a bit rank.”

                                                    Moth rubs her eyes. “Tavi…why…why is there a raiton?”

                                                    “He’s hurt. I’m taking care of him. Going to see if I can maybe teach him a few tricks.”

                                                    “…can one of those tricks be baths? It’s like…wet feathers and piss and…ozone?”

                                                    “What’s ozone?”

                                                    “It’s air that’s heavily charged with Essence. You sometimes smell it near a Dragon Line coming down from the North, or where lightning has struck.”

                                                    “Lightning, huh? That’d explain the singe marks? Did you get struck by lightning, little guy?”

                                                    “If it got struck by lightning, it’d be cooked.” Moth blearily pushes off her blanket and sits up. “Look, um. I’m fine with a pet raiton, he looks really neat and it kind of works with my Princess of Shadows thing…”

                                                    “I thought it was Umbral Infinitudes?”

                                                    “Shush. But if he’s gonna crap in the apartment and smell bad, I have concerns.”

                                                    “You. Have concerns about how the apartment smells.”

                                                    “Listen, it’s fine if they’re MY smells, Tavi. I don’t notice those. And you don’t really smell, but that bird-“

                                                    “Alright, I’ll keep him clean.”

                                                    “What’s his name?” Moth picks her nose, looks at the resulting bit of snot on her finger impassively, then reaches out to wipe it on the underside of the chair.

                                                    “He doesn’t have one?”

                                                    “Hm. You should let me pick a name for him!”

                                                    “Oh gods.”

                                                    “He can be…The Ill Omened Harbinger! Ooh, no, we should call him Midnight Lightning, since he’s black and he smells like lightning…oh, wait, wait. How do you say Midnight Lightning in Skytongue...what’s midnight…lightning’s Kaummulak-“

                                                    “Usi!” croaks the raiton. “Kaummulak Omajualuk. Nimak.”

                                                    “…Tavi? Why does your raiton speak Skytongue?”

                                                    “What’s Skytongue?”

                                                    “It’s a language they speak way up North. I only know a few words, I don’t even know how their sentence structure works…” Moth puts her hand on her chest. “Atik…White Moth.”

                                                    Moth extends her hand to the raiton, and it cocks its head, then speaks, very slowly, in a low croaking voice. “Atik…Chupong. Nakuk AngajukKak.”

                                                    Moth's eyes widen in excitement. “I think his name is Chupong Nakuk Angajukkak?”

                                                    “Chupong,” the raiton corrects her. “Atik. Chupong.”

                                                    “Um. Moth? Do you happen to have a book that says what words mean in Skytongue?” You appear to have brought home a talking raiton.

                                                    “Yeah, it’s…just a minute.” Moth starts rummaging around her disorganized piles of books. “Do you suppose he used to be somebody’s pet, and learned words? Raitons can be very clever.”

                                                    “Maybe…”

                                                    “Pitsik? Ak Pitsik.” The raiton, seeming to be enjoying the attention, flaps its wings and pecks at your hand.

                                                    “I think Pitsik means he wants more fish,” you sigh and grab some from the bag. You hold it out, fist clenched. “Pitsik?”

                                                    “Pitsik!” The raiton bobs its head and pecks at your hand. You don’t open it.

                                                    “Chupong.” You say its name and the bird goes still and quiet. “Come.” You hold out your fist again, and the raiton hops onto your wrist. Now that it's done the trick you give it some fish. “Come. Pitsik.”

                                                    The raiton scarfs down the dried fish. “Come. Pitsik,” it agrees, and continues to sit on your wrist as it preens its feathers.

                                                    “That was so cool!” Moth declares. “You got him on your hand!”

                                                    “Yeah. Any luck with that book?”

                                                    “Book? Oh! Yes, one second…” She goes back to rummaging, then finds the book she’s looking for and flips it open. “Here it is! A Guildsman’s Guide to Gethamane, complete with Riverspeak to Skytongue Dictionary…hm…Pitsik means dried fish.”

                                                    “What’s Chupong mean?”

                                                    “…it’s not in here. And it’s weird, Chupong doesn’t sound like Skytongue…but what was it? Nakuk Anchorjack?”

                                                    “Nakuk AngajukKak!” The raiton croaks at her.

                                                    “Nakuk….nakuk…okay, that means strong, or…faultless? Says it’s used to describe goods of high quality. And Anga, anga….oh, here it is. It means war-leader or chieftain or daimyo. There’s a note saying he’s the guy you want to barter with.”

                                                    “So he’s…Chupong the Flawless Daimyo?”

                                                    “I mean, I can’t really speak Skytongue, and this book’s intended less as a grammatical guide and more as a guide to not getting cheated by whoever you hire as a translator…but maybe Perfected Daimyo Chupong would sound better in Riverspeak?”

                                                    The raiton is looking back and forth between the two of you intently as you keep saying its name. You hold it out in front of your face. “Uh, Moth? How do you say ‘name,’ again?”

                                                    “Atik.”

                                                    “Okay, then. Atik…Tavi. Tavi Nakuk AnjajukKak.”

                                                    The raven hops off your hand and back onto the chair, its body shaking slightly at it croaks repeatedly. Laughter sounds the same in any language.

                                                    “…did you just try to convince it that you were Perfected Daimyo Tavi?”

                                                    “It was worth a shot.” You scowl. “Didn’t want to get outranked by a damn raiton.” Moth snorts and bursts out laughing, and this sends the raiton into further levels of hilarity. It warbles in amusement. Eventually, once they both get tired of it and shut up, you sigh. “Look, we should see if we can find out more…how do you say, ‘what’ in Skytongue?”

                                                    “Uh….Suna?”

                                                    You gently touch the bird’s scorched feathers. “Suna?”

                                                    Chupong…you’re starting to think of him by his name, now…looks up at you. “Nimak. Kaummulak Omajualuk.”

                                                    Moth runs her finger down the pages, searching… “Nimak…that’s, um…Hurt? Injury? And Kaummulak is lightning, I know that one…Omaj, Omaj…Omajualuk! Oh.”

                                                    “What?”

                                                    “Omajualuk is…monster.”

                                                    You close your eyes and take a deep breath. Moth keeps talking.

                                                    “So…he got hurt by a…lightning monster? But-“

                                                    “Moth, how do you say Snake? Big flying lightning snake?”

                                                    “Let’s see…Kullugiak?”

                                                    “Kullugiak! Kaummulak Kullugiak Omajualuk!” The raiton is excited again.

                                                    You sit down.

                                                    You had a dream last night about flying north, and a thunderstorm, and at the end before you woke up there was a huge thing slithering through the clouds overhead…this is too weird.
                                                    ***



                                                    You spend the rest of the morning with Chupong and White Moth, going through her Riverspeak to Skytongue dictionary. The raiton learns quickly once you repeat words to it. Pitsik is Fish. AnjajukKak is Daimyo. Omajualuk is Monster.

                                                    It becomes slowly and haltingly clear that Chupong is grateful to you for fighting the cat-monster and saving him, and that the other raitons you met today are Chupong’s…the translation is a little unclear, but whatever Guildsman wrote the book about travelling in the North said to think of them as deeply loyal bodyguards. Chupong is the son of…the best analog you can get is “Grand Sky King,” and he has come south to…conquer and prove himself in battle, if Moth’s translation is right. Those are tasks which you’re not really sure how a raiton would go about accomplishing. Chupong had hoped to slay the…lightning monster…in the storm, but was defeated without ever even getting close to it.

                                                    Moth has no idea why you dreamed about what Chupong was doing last night, and Chupong either doesn’t know or doesn’t understand the translation-the Guild Dictionary is very specific on fractions and percentages, but there’s not much room devoted to dreams or descriptive language, and anyway you and Moth have no idea how grammar works in Skytongue-which becomes crystal clear after the raiton starts using full sentences, then switches back to short one-word bursts in apparent disappointment at your lack of fluency.

                                                    White Moth’s theory is that the raiton is part spirit, perhaps the mortal offspring of a raiton god and a normal raiton, and she’s equally fascinated by your dreaming about Chupong’s adventures and jealous of the fact that you found him first. The bird, for his part, seems to like you-he feels comfortable resting on your wrist, or on your shoulder while you sit by the stove, though now that he’s figured out the word for fish he won’t shut up about it.

                                                    You find Moth’s dictionary just as fascinating as the magic raiton, and honestly a lot less unnerving. If only there was a Riverspeak to Pictures dictionary…you ask, but Moth says she doesn’t know of any books like that.

                                                    The morning turns into the afternoon, and Moth leaves you to go check on the Master of Nine Black Chains. You stay, and keep teaching the Raiton words.

                                                    “Rice.”
                                                    “Egg.”
                                                    “No.”
                                                    “Chair.”
                                                    “Fire.”
                                                    “Coin.”
                                                    “NO!”
                                                    “Hair.”
                                                    “Come.”
                                                    “Sit.”
                                                    “Stay.”

                                                    You’re pretty sure you learn a few curse words in Skytongue when you take the egg away from him, but over the course of the day the two of you develop an understanding, and eventually the bird even lets you scrub him with some warm water and a brush to clean his feathers. He helps, preening and preening even after you’re finished.
                                                    ***



                                                    The day turns into night. Moth comes back, reporting that her Master is tired and a little anemic but otherwise unharmed, and is now resting safely. The three of you spend your evening with the dictionary, you writing out words for things in the apartment and saying them out loud for the raiton, and the raiton repeating them. Eventually he starts bringing you the things-chopsticks, books, the corner of the blanket.

                                                    The next day your learning continues. You take Chupong up on the roof now that it’s no longer raining, and he hops around under the cloudy sky, not yet ready to take wing. Moth comes up with you, and you continue your lessons. You teach Chupong to bring you coins, and reward him with bits of boiled egg. Chupong teaches you to get a firm grasp on the coins first-once he gets the egg he’ll hop away without letting go of the quarter-dinar bit of silver if you let him. He has trouble figuring out that you don’t want him defecating in the apartment, and he refuses to go out the window the way you were trying to teach him…but after observing you do so, he starts using the chamber pot.

                                                    A day turns into a week. Chupong’s injuries are starting to heal, and his feathers are starting to come back in, though he looks scruffy. He’s getting basic two word combinations down: “Tavi NO!” and “Fish me!” are his favorites, though he’s also fond of abruptly screeching “DEATH FIRE!” without any warning. Moth has found you a picture book meant to teach rich children to read. It has lots of words in Riverspeak, along with pictures. You and the raiton both study it with deep interest. You copy the letters onto parchment. The raiton tries to copy you and draw them with his beak, but he’s clumsy. Moth is both amused and impressed by how quickly both of you are learning. The Master of Nine Black Chains recovers from his exertions (a trip to the Underworld, Moth tells you), and Moth goes back to the University of Nexus every day to assist him. Apparently things are going well at the University; the Master was able to gather the ingredients in the Underworld he needed and selling a couple of angry ghost in clay jugs to unscrupulous Guildsmen will pay for the rest of them. Moth says the Master is getting ready for a big ritual on the night of the New Moon.

                                                    A week turns into a month. Resplendent Water ends, and Descending Water begins. You talk to Gleaming Smile, and pay him the two months’ back rent Moth owes him-you’d rather he not send the Iron League after your reading teacher. He agrees to let you rent the roof, and set up a little garden (as well as a couple of wood-and-wicker structures to grow beans on with little bars in them for the raitons to perch). You’ll just add it onto what Moth owes you when she gets her stipend from her mother at the start of Ascending Earth next month. Chupong has healed enough that he can fly again. The rest of the raitons, his “bodyguards,” are learning a lot of the same tricks that he is. You start getting bits of silver dropped on your windowsill in the mornings instead of buttons and ribbons and dead lizard bits. The others aren’t as clever as Chupong, but they fly out over the city and then croak and caw at their Daimyo and then he croaks back to you: “Cat Monster. East,” or “Red wagon, South.” You’re well on your way to using them as spies, even if the communication is still pretty imprecise. You’ve noticed there are several flocks of raitons in the city, and yours are a little bit bigger than the average bird-the others make way for them in the sky, and the big Northern birds tend to get first pickings of any decent scavenging due to their superior size and strength. Chupong has told you their names: Gerel and Tsetseg, always squabbling with one another. Erdene, who flies oddly due to one bad eye. Rishi, the only one who refuses fish and prefers eggs. Tara, chubby and extremely aggressive, prone to biting your fingers and requiring a much firmer hand than the others when it comes to beak-bopping. Prem, Chupong’s mate, the one who led you to him when he was injured. After a month working with them, it’s clear they’re all a bunch of thieving scoundrels who can’t be trusted for a minute…but they seem to listen to Chupong, and Chupong likes you.

                                                    You like him too. Sometimes, when you dream, you see the world through his eyes. Under Moth’s guidance, you’ve been meditating, trying to do the same trick when you’re awake. It’s even worked a couple of times. Moth seems very excited by all this, and refers to Chupong as your “familiar.” Chupong seems unfazed, and describes your relationship with some Skytongue word that’s not in White Moth’s book that sounds a lot like gargling rocks. Your wounds have healed, and so have your Raiton’s.

                                                    You have a flock of vaguely obedient spies who are….reasonably reliable, but a bit bad at reporting what they’ve seen. They’ve all mastered the “come,” command, though, so you can pull the trick where you raise both arms and seven raitons land on you at once, or rise up to circle menacingly overhead. Between that and teaching Chupong to say, “Kneel, mortal,” you’re pretty sure you’re capable of intimidating most street thugs you run across, though you’re not eager to test it in action.

                                                    Paying rent and stocking up on groceries has reminded you, though, that you’re still losing money every month-albeit less now that the raitons are bringing you spare change they find. As Moth put it, the gods have given you a gift. Now it’s time to turn that gift into power!

                                                    What should you do:

                                                    Start Your Own Gang: Go see if you can round up Rat and maybe some of his friends from the Sluice Gang and use your new feathered friends to look imposing enough to get put in charge.

                                                    Join A Gang: You’re not dangerous, not yet, not really. But you can be useful. You should use your raitons to gather some intel about how valuable goods are getting moved and see if you can get into a real gang like the Leopard Gang by offering that intel (and the promise of more) to their leadership. Membership would give you protection from other ruffians as well as a cut of the earnings…and with your brain, those earnings would grow.

                                                    Find a Crew: Nexus is nice…but where having a bunch of raitons spying for you would really shine is out on the water. There are boats up and down the river laden with all manner of valuables, and river pirates would love to have somebody on board with talking birds who could scout out cargo ships to see if they were worth going after…and cargo ships might pay good money to have somebody on board who could scout upriver for pirates.

                                                    Serve the Master: White Moth’s teacher, the Master of Nine Black Chains, is obviously a skilled occultist. You’re feeling in a bit over your head here-Chupong is obviously magic, which is usually dangerous. You’re concerned that if the bird is part god like Moth says, feeding it fish might count as prayer outside of an Immaculate feast day…and more importantly, if you’re getting dragged into the world of gods and spirits you want to be prepared. Anybody who can survive overnight in the Underworld is worth learning from…but Moth has made it clear that if you want to learn from the Master, you’ll need to work for the Master, and he obviously has some kind of major plan in the works that may be dangerous for a relative amateur to join partway through.

                                                    Renegotiate with Fifteen Fingers Liu: Now that you’ve got flying scouts, you can probably set up a better arrangement with the Chrysanthemum Abacus Merchants. Perhaps they could use your help tracking down debtors without your having to risk actually going out and meeting those debtors in person, for example. You’re pretty sure the birds (or at least Chupong) can recognize and follow different people based on a good picture. And the Chrysanthemum Abacus Merchants have a trading house and offices in other cities, they might like to have fast-winged messengers to carry news and more up to date pricing information to their caravans than their competition is able to manage.

                                                    Enter the Service of the Midnight Queen: The Council of Entities has spies all over Nexus. They have eyes and ears everywhere…but as far as you know, those spies don’t include the city’s raitons. You should try and get assigned duties working for the Council’s intelligence network. The pay would be decent and perhaps more importantly…if you’re on the Council’s business, people are going to be a lot more reluctant to mess with you.

                                                    Start a Business: Three words-Aerial Dumpling Deliveries! Or maybe you should start that pickling business you’ve been mulling over, you could deliver pickles by air!

                                                    Start Smuggling: Three words-Aerial Drug Deliveries! Everything’s legal in Nexus, but there are folks who would pay you good money to smuggle illegal drugs to other places like Lookshy or Thorns or Calin. And Aunt Dwi can probably hook you up with those folks.

                                                    Go Hunting: With raitons acting as your hunting dogs, you’ve been keeping tabs on that Raptor Cat. You know what areas of the city it’s been prowling, and you’re pretty sure you could set a trap and drive the cat into it. The creature would be very profitable to sell…or if you could chain it up and train the thing, it might be helpful in ways raitons aren’t.

                                                    Plan a Heist: Your raitons are clever, and Chupong is able to identify people or objects from pictures. You should pick something small and valuable and see if you can get into the jewel-thief game.

                                                    Start a Cult: Why lead a street gang when you can be worshipped as a god? You have a bunch of raitons doing your bidding, and one of them might even actually be a god. Or maybe you should claim to be the high priest of a god? If he's actually a divine being like Moth thinks he is, Chupong might get more out of the prayers than you would. The Dragons wouldn’t approve, but you remember how lavishly appointed their temple was. People would just give you free money in the form of donations if they worship at your temple, especially if you were able to pull off a miracle or three. You have enough money to buy a small lot in the Temple District and set up a nice looking shrine to draw people in, but that would drain a lot of your resources.
